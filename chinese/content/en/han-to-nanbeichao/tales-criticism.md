---
title: "Tales and criticism"
---

The closing chapter sets out three new inventions of the Six Dynasties: **opening a file on ghosts and spirits (tales of the strange, *zhiguai*), sketching the famous gentlemen (anecdotes of people, *zhiren*), and opening court on literature (criticism)**. The three share one spirit of the age: **when the study of the Classics no longer had the sole supply of truth, people were in a hurry to write everything down — including dreams, gossip, and opinions about poetry — to sort it into kinds, and to set the order of seats.**

## Three inventions {#def-faming}

::: definition Tales of the strange: inventing a ghost that does not deceive {#def-zhiguai}
**The genre's own confession**: the preface to Gan Bao's 干宝 *Soushen ji* 搜神记 ('Records of the Search for Spirits') states of its own accord that the materials were **“考先志于载籍，收遗逸于当时”** ('examining earlier records among the documents, and gathering what had been left out in his own time') (Our translation.) — three buckets: fragmentary scrolls, oral accounts, and what was learned by asking about; **he himself was a historian (Director of the Secretariat, concurrently in charge of the compilation office)**, and so what is written on the birth certificate of the tale of the strange is: **'a record', not 'a composition'** — **to write of ghosts and spirits and yet call one's own brush the historian's brush: this is the first licence that *xiaoshuo* ('fiction; the minor talk of the old catalogues') begged and obtained in the shadow of the Histories**. The question of household registration: the *Han zhi* defines *xiaoshuo* as **“街谈巷语，道听途说者之所造也”** ('the talk of the streets and alleys, made by those who pass on what they hear by the road') (Our translation.), and quotes an old saying, **“小道，必有可观者焉；致远恐泥，是以君子不为也”** ('the minor way is sure to have something in it worth looking at; carried far, one fears it will stick in the mud, and therefore the gentleman does not practise it') (Our translation.) — ***xiaoshuo*'s registration was a temporary registration from the day it took up residence in the city; and a temporary registration means precisely this: there are no old rules, and new rules grow as they please** (the 'Jingji zhi' of the *Book of Sui* places writings of ghosts, spirits and anomalies under the Masters, which is precisely the Wei–Jin edition in which this charter was carried out). **A sample dissection** ('The Tomb of the Three Kings', 《三王墓》): Mei Jianchi 眉间尺 avenges his father (Ganjiang 干将 and Moye 莫邪 forged swords for the king of Chu and were killed); the king posts a heavy reward for the purchase of his head; **a knight-errant from the mountains names his terms: Chi's head and the sword as the betrothal gifts, and he will go in his place**; the head is boiled for three days and does not rot, and the stranger asks the king to come to the pit — the three kings are then buried in one tomb. **The point for narratology: the head is a pass, and trust is settled on the spot by delivery of the 'head'; the supernatural, of the kind that leaps and stamps in the boiling broth, serves only as glue for the plot, and the motives are entirely human — the ethic of revenge outweighs the normative roles of ruler and subject. The recipe of the best kind of tale of the strange: a costume of ghosts and monsters, a core of legal principle**. What follows: the *Soushen houji* 搜神后记 ('Further Records of the Search for Spirits') **is traditionally attributed to Tao Yuanming 陶渊明 — even a ghost story had to rush to register the trademark of a famous gentleman**; Liu Xiaobiao's 刘孝标 commentary on the *Shishuo* quotes tales of the strange widely — **in the Six Dynasties, tales of the strange and anecdotes of people were footnotes to each other, and both belonged to one 'recording machine'**.
:::

::: definition Anecdotes of people: the literary version of the nine-rank system {#def-zhiren}
***A New Account of Tales of the World* (*Shishuo xinyu*)**: gathered by Liu Yiqing 刘义庆, Prince of Linchuan of the Song, with a commentary by Liu Xiaobiao of the Liang — **the text now transmitted has thirty-six categories: Virtuous Conduct, Speech, Affairs of Government and Letters are the first four, while Unrestrained Abandon, Aloof Arrogance, Bold Candour and Infatuation are placed towards the end**. **The first four categories are copied from the ranked subjects of the Confucian school, while the later categories take 'crossing the line' as *fengliu* 风流 ('stylish distinction') — the split between the head and the tail of the critical system is precisely the fault line of the Wei–Jin 'rediscovery of the person'**. **Scale**: more than a thousand items, **each of thirty to fifty characters — the ceiling of the Chinese sketch as a form**. Two cases beyond appeal: Xie An 谢安 receives the dispatch of victory at the Huai and the Fei, **“了无喜色，围棋如故”** ('not the least look of joy, and the *weiqi* went on as before') (Our translation.); a guest asks, and **he replies unhurriedly, “小儿辈大破贼”** ('the youngsters have thoroughly smashed the bandits') (Our translation.) — **the utmost restraint of language**; **“过户限，心喜甚，不觉屐齿之折”** ('once he had crossed the threshold, his heart was greatly pleased, and before he knew it the teeth of his clogs had broken') (Our translation.) — **the utmost leaking of a secret by a detail**. Within a single piece, ritual propriety and the body conspire to finish acting out the truth of *yaliang* 雅量 ('cultivated equanimity'): **the narratology of the *Shishuo* = restraint + the giveaway**. Commentary: **Liu Xiaobiao's commentary cites several hundred books, and lost works are preserved by means of it — the weight, as historical material, of a commentary that bites back at the main text; the same age and the same method as Pei Songzhi's 裴松之 *Sanguo zhi zhu* 三国志注 ('Commentary on the Records of the Three Kingdoms'): history and *xiaoshuo* share the technique of commentaries and subcommentaries**. **Lineage**: the *Book of Documents* has its *Yu* ('Sayings') and its *Zhi* ('Records'), and ancient books of the 'sayings' class are devoted to recording admirable words — **anecdotes of people are the sayings-class come back to life; the two great trunks of *xiaoshuo* (tales of the strange = the remainder of history; anecdotes of people = the remainder of sayings) both grew up wearing the open-crotch trousers of the histories**.
:::

::: definition Criticism: literature opens court {#def-piping}
A legislative chain of a hundred years: ***Dianlun*, 'Lun wen' 典论·论文 ('Authoritative Discourses', 'Discourse on Literature') (the licence) → 'Wen fu' 文赋 ('Rhapsody on Literature') (the scope of the business) → 'Wenzhang liubie lun' 文章流别论 ('Treatise on the Branches of Literary Writing') (a sense of genealogy) → *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (*Wenxin diaolong*) (the constitution) → *Shipin* 诗品 ('Grades of Poetry') (a compilation of decided cases) → *Wenxuan* 文选 ('Selections of Refined Literature') (the full-text library of the cases)**. ***The Literary Mind***, in fifty chapters: **the three chapters “本乎道，师乎圣，体乎经”** ('rooted in the Way, taking the sages as one's teachers, and taking one's form from the Classics') (Our translation.) **establish the constitution, and the two chapters “酌乎纬、变乎骚”** ('drawing on the apocrypha, and changing from the *Sao*') (Our translation.) **leave an exit for reform**; **the forty-character operating standard of the discussion of genres: “原始以表末，释名以章义，选文以定篇，敷理以举统”** ('trace the origin so as to set out the end; explain the name so as to make the meaning plain; select writings so as to fix the pieces; set forth the principles so as to raise the governing thread') (Our translation.) — **for each kind of genre, one general history + a definition + criticism of selected writings + the essentials of writing: the complete embryo of the handbook format, more than a thousand years earlier than Western textbooks of the same kind**. *Grades of Poetry*: more than one hundred and twenty persons, **divided into three grades, with sources and streams attached** — **the literary version of the law for grading men for office in nine ranks; its greatest invention is the very fact that 'criticism can be made into a book'**: the pleasure of reading poetry and reading a written judgment out of it, **aesthetic dictatorship and democracy sharing one bed inside 'grading'**; both in its lifetime and after its death it quarrelled the world of letters upside down — **to be reviled is precisely the evidence that criticism had obtained a public standing**: Xiao Tong's 萧统 compiling the *Wenxuan*, and Liu Xie's 刘勰 writing *The Literary Mind*, seated at the same table with it, form the three legs of the literary thought of the Qi and the Liang (the Cao father and sons and the 'four friends', and the masters of the Qi and the Liang, none of them submitting to any other; the two biographies in the *Book of Wei* and the *Book of Liang* show, each in the other, that clamour). **The golden rule of criticism: to talk about poetry was the safest public speech of the time — the flourishing of literary theory is the photographic negative of the climate of speech**.
:::

::: example A paired reading of two pieces: boiling broth and the board {#ex-duidu}
A paired reading of the two pieces. 'The Tomb of the Three Kings' (*Soushen ji*, juan 11) = **three beats, 'commission — verification — execution'**: the knight-errant takes 'obtaining the head and the sword' as the condition of the contract, the head that will not rot when boiled is the plot's delay, and the king comes to the pit and the three heads are buried together. Xie An, in *Shishuo*, 'Cultivated Equanimity', hearing of the victory at the Huai and the Fei = **two beats, 'press — leak'**: the *weiqi* goes on as before, and the words of the reply are flat (the press); crossing the threshold, he breaks the teeth of his clogs (the leak — the received text reads “屐齿”; the editions differ slightly, and the plot is locked). Please write, for each, a 'forensic report on the genre' of 100 characters: **what social function does each of these two narrative grammars serve**?
::: solution
**Report on 'The Tomb of the Three Kings'**: **the primitive specimen of contract narrative**: the knight-errant's commission requires the 'head' as security — **in a society where the court cannot be trusted, trust must be settled on the spot with the flesh**; the head that will not rot when boiled is **the crack the plot leaves for the delay**, so that the step 'the king comes to look in person' can happen — **the supernatural is in charge of the physics, ethics is in charge of the motive, and the division of labour in the tale of the strange takes its settled form here**; downstream it runs straight to the system of the Tang tale (*chuanqi*) *Gujian ji* 古剑记 ('Record of an Ancient Sword'), and to Lu Xun's 'Forging the Swords' (**the 'man in black', Yanzhiaozhe 宴之敖者, is that very knight-errant — Mei Jianchi's head is exchanged for an upgraded version of “头也给你”** ('I give you the head as well') (Our translation.): **professionalisation, with revenge as the trade**). **Report on 'Cultivated Equanimity'**: **an advertisement for the aristocratic personality**: its function is to establish '**jurisdiction over the emotions**' as the standard for admission to the class — **the game may be lost, but the face must not change; the body's giveaway (the clog-teeth) is reported by the author on his own initiative, and this on the contrary reinforces the narrative: restraint that can give itself away is worth more than restraint that does not**. Taking the two together: **tales of the strange taught Chinese stories 'how to make them up', and anecdotes of people taught Chinese literature 'how to look at a person' — the two starting lines of fiction and of prose, and the Six Dynasties fired one shot on each**.
:::

::: intuition Criticism is literature's parliament {#int-yihui}
Imagine the literature of the Six Dynasties as a new state: the *Dianlun* is a **declaration of independence**, composition is the **economy**, the *Wenxuan* is a **survey of the territory**, *The Literary Mind* is **the constitution and administrative law**, and *Grades of Poetry* is **the collected judgments of the supreme court** — **when does a literary community begin to write a constitution? The answer is usually: when it no longer needs to explain itself by survival**. So the 'self-consciousness of literature' (Lu Xun's topic: the age of Cao Pi 曹丕) is not an instant of awakening; **it is institutions taking shape: there is an anthology (who counts as one of us), there is a history (where we come from), there is grading (who is first now), there is the rhapsody (how to write) — only a 'four-haves' youth can be said to be self-conscious**. This also explains why the explosion of criticism comes in the Qi and the Liang: **the politics of the great clans was saturated, and genius involuted onto the critics' benches — if one could not write the 'Two Capitals' (*Liang du* 两都), one could write the rules for criticising the 'Two Capitals'; Jian'an issued literature its licence, the Six Dynasties gave literature a consultative assembly, and the term of the parliament will have its vote of confidence cast by the High Tang of the next lesson (the Tang)**.
:::

::: history The ledger of the three houses' legacy {#sec-sanjia}
**Tang**: **the Tang tale exchanged the 'historian's brush' of the tale of the strange for a 'literary brush' — the first time in the history of fiction that it openly admitted it was making things up** (pieces of the kind of 'The Ancient Mirror' (*Gujing* 古镜) and 'Inside the Pillow' (*Zhenzhong* 枕中) still borrow a shell of the numinous and the strange, while the narrator has already taken a stand both in front of the stage and behind it); the *Shishuo* was recognised by the notebook literature (*biji*) of the Song and the Ming as its grandfather. **Song**: **the *Taiping guangji* 太平广记 ('Broad Records of the Taiping Era'), in four hundred juan, gathers them by category, and the tales of the strange of the Han, the Wei and the Six Dynasties are roughly complete by means of it — the people of the Song used a library to preserve the night-talk of earlier ages**; the system of Song editions of the *Shishuo* was established (Wang Zao's 汪藻 *Shishuo xulu* 世说叙录 ('A Record of the Transmission of the *Shishuo*') notes its circulation), and it was spared the scattering and loss of **“书经三写，鲁鱼亥豕”** ('a book, once it has been copied three times, confuses *lu* with *yu* and *hai* with *shi*') (Our translation.). ***The Literary Mind* in the Tang**: encyclopaedias (*leishu*) quote it, and historians commend it (Liu Zhi 刘知 records the particulars — **people of the Tang already granted it authority, and yet its readers were very few**). **Yuan and Ming**: ***The Literary Mind* becomes prominent when it reaches the Yuan and the Ming — printed editions, commentary by marking (*pingdian*; Yang Shen 杨慎 and others), and a place in the grove of 'combined printings': a classic of criticism, too, has to manage to be born in time for the window of print**; **Feng Menglong 冯梦龙 compiled the *Gujin tangai* 古今谭概 ('A Conspectus of Talk Ancient and Modern') and the *Xiaofu* 笑府 ('Treasury of Laughs') — the *Shishuo* moving downmarket: fine phrases become jokes, and the appraisal of persons (*pinzao*) becomes a comic bit**. **Qing**: **the *General Catalogue of the Four Treasuries* (*Siku zongmu* 四库总目) honours *The Literary Mind* with the settled verdict along the line of “文家之楷” ('a model for writers'); Ji Yun's 纪昀 edition with critical marks drew it into the queue of reference books for composition in the examinations — the worldly peak of canonisation**; collation and the recovery of lost texts take up the relay. **The modern period**: **Lu Xun's *Zhongguo xiaoshuo shilüe* 中国小说史略 ('A Brief History of Chinese Fiction') establishes the headings 'ghosts, spirits and the strange' and 'recording speech (anecdotes of people)', and the history of fiction is thereby set up as a subject; *Wei Jin fengdu ji wenzhang yu yao ji jiu zhi guanxi* 魏晋风度及文章与药及酒之关系 ('The Wei–Jin Manner and the Relation of Writing to Drugs and to Wine') reads the *Shishuo* as 'a document of life' — '*Shishuo* studies' became a learning that custom had settled; *The Literary Mind* enters the textbooks of 'literary theory': criticism of criticism, ancient and modern at one and the same exchange rate**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading this lesson
**Retroactively recognising tales of the strange as the modern 'fiction'** (**in the *Han zhi*, '*xiaoshuo*' is the last of the ten schools, a 'minor way' — people of the Six Dynasties who wrote of the strange did not call it composition; to carry them in a sedan chair under 'the ancient short stories of our country' is to change the ancients' profession for them**); **taking the *Shishuo* in whole as historical material** (**it is 'a beauty-contest album of the conduct of famous gentlemen' — one and the same event may come out of political propaganda; Liu Xiaobiao's commentary often dismantles the stage itself — reading the main text against the commentary is the minimum threshold**); **reading *The Literary Mind* as an 'introduction to literary theory'** (**it is a general history of genres + a guide to writing + a philosophy of literature; it does not have the modern skeleton of four sections, 'general introduction — genres — composition — criticism', and the modern table of contents was pieced together by later people**); **forgetting that the three inventions share one root** (**ghosts and spirits, famous gentlemen, and works are all processed on the same machine of 'record — classify — grade': the genius of the Six Dynasties is filing, not inspiration — bibliography and tales of the strange are the same age, and this is not a coincidence**).
:::

::: exercise Liu Xiaobiao takes up his post {level=1}
Choose for yourself a short item of the *Shishuo* that really exists (one each from Virtuous Conduct and from Unrestrained Abandon), and **write two notes in imitation of Liu Xiaobiao's commentary**: (1) **a source note** (the person's record of offices, and further evidence for the event; reference works may be consulted); (2) **a note correcting an error, or a supplementary note** (point out a contradiction, or cite a variant text). Attach an explanation of 50 characters: will your note **reinforce** the main text, or **dismantle** it?
::: solution
Marking: the language of the commentary form is in order (format at the level of “某某书曰／按／《晋阳秋》曰” ('Such-and-such a book says / I note / the *Jin yangqiu* says')); **if you dismantle the stage, you must dismantle it with evidence** (“按：此人是时已死，不得与某共事” ('I note: this man was already dead at that time, and could not have served together with so-and-so') (Our translation.) is a famous move of Liu Xiaobiao's — **the code of honour of the commentary: do not cite material from outside the *Shishuo* that cannot be checked**); the explanation hits the mark: the credit of a collection of anecdotes of people comes from 'every item has a source', and the commentary is **an auditor carried on one's person**.

::: summary
Settling the account at the close: tales of the strange (**a licence for the historian's brush + 'a costume of ghosts and monsters, a core of legal principle' — downstream to *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio* (*Liaozhai zhiyi*) and *Old Tales Retold* (*Gushi xinbian* 故事新编)**), anecdotes of people (**the thirty-six categories split at head and tail = the fault line of Wei–Jin thought; the sketch-grammar of 'restraint + the giveaway'**), criticism (**from the licence to the parliament: the anthology settles the territory, grading sets the order of seats, and *The Literary Mind* establishes the constitution**). This lesson is also the settling of the whole course: **from 'at root, not a single character' to 'at root, there is a book' — the empire of the Han rhapsody, the persons of the *Records of the Grand Historian*, the street of the *yuefu* (Music Bureau poems; ballads), the namelessness of the old poems, the licence of Jian'an, the mask of Zhengshi, the demotion of field and garden, the independence of landscape, the two shores of the folk songs, until the Six Dynasties filed them, classified them and graded them — the self-consciousness of literature is not a slogan; it is a whole set of institutions**. Take this set of institutions on into the Tang: over there, poetry will for the first time have no need to explain why it exists.
:::
