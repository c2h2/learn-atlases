---
title: "Sima Qian and the *Shiji*"
---

The rhapsody (*fu*) of the previous lesson speaks for the empire; the man of this lesson **speaks for the losers** — and what he tells is the earliest, and also the best, set of stories of 'people' in the whole of China. The one hundred and thirty chapters of the *Records of the Grand Historian* (*Shiji* 《史记》) are a **maze built out of five buildings**: if you lose your way in any one chapter, that is by design — the way out is in another chapter. This chapter hands you the blueprint.

## The five forms and the chronological limits {#def-wuti}

::: definition The architecture of the one hundred and thirty chapters {#def-jianzhu}
**Five building types**: twelve **Basic Annals** (*benji* 本纪; annals of the Son of Heaven — **the title deed to time**), ten **Tables** (*biao* 表; **a high-end spreadsheet from two thousand years ago**: years, people and states crammed onto a single sheet of paper, **and those who work on the *Shiji* spend half a lifetime studying the row-and-column grammar of the tables**), eight **Treatises** (*shu* 书; rites, music, pitch-pipes, the calendar, astronomy, the five phases, water, and the **Balanced Standard = an economic special issue** — eight white papers from the department of culture and institutions), thirty **Hereditary Houses** (*shijia* 世家; the feudal lords and **Confucius** — **Confucius, who held no noble rank, taking a house in the aristocratic quarter is the most famous overstep of authority by the Grand Historian, Taishigong 太史公**), seventy **Biographies** (*liezhuan* 列传; **a long gallery of group portraits** running from assassins to wandering knights to merchants, the last chapter, the 'Preface of the Grand Historian' (*Taishigong zixu* 《太史公自序》), = the architect's signature). **Three oversteps**: ① **Xiang Yu 项羽 was not the Son of Heaven, yet he occupies a Basic Annals** (“**政由羽出，号为霸王**” — 'Government issued from Yu; he was styled Hegemon-King.' (Our translation.) — **recognising power as it actually was, and not minding the title**); ② **Chen She 陈涉 was not a feudal lord, yet he enters the Hereditary Houses** (“**首事**”, 'first to rise', against the Qin — **the rebel is handed the aristocrat's title deed**); ③ **the 'Biographies of the Money-makers' (*Huozhi liezhuan* 《货殖列传》) writes biographies for merchants** (“**天下熙熙，皆为利来**” — 'All the world bustles; all come for profit.' (Our translation.) — **Sima Qian 司马迁 took in everyone the histories had refused**). **Chronological limits of a general history**: from the Yellow Emperor at the start down to the Taichu era — **the first Chinese general history to 'tell it from the beginning'**; and 'from the beginning' is itself a stance: **by way of a common ancestry in the Five Emperors, the various peoples are entered in one and the same family register, and the realm of the Western Han acquires, on bamboo slips, a unity at the level of a genealogy**.
:::

::: definition The three-piece kit of *Shiji* brushwork {#def-bifa}
**One: scene and dialogue** (the song at Gaixia 垓下, the dance at Hongmen 鸿门, the farewell at the Yi River 易水 — **the Grand Historian swapped the historian's brush for the blocking of a play**; he will even **write the source of the material into the main text**: the 'Biographies of the Assassins' states outright “**公孙季功、董生为夏无且道如此**” — 'Gongsun Jigong and Dong Sheng told it thus for Xia Wuju.' (Our translation.) — **oral history from a memorial gathering goes straight into the main text: a historiographical self-awareness that traces the source to the person**). **Two: the method of mutual illumination** (*hujian fa* 互见法) (one person's truth is taken apart and stored in pieces: Xiang Yu's heroic domination sits in the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu'; his harshness, ferocity, cruelty and rage are scattered through other men's biographies — **in the 'Basic Annals of Gaozu', Liu Bang 刘邦 is wise, divine and martial; in the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu' he abandons his children and shoves the carriage** — **the main biography gives him his dignity, the other biographies leak what is underneath: the method of mutual illumination = a multi-window browser opened for a complicated person**). **Three: a judgement that does not judge** (the “**太史公曰**” ('The Grand Historian says') at the end of a chapter is the only formal commentator's chair — but the real verdict is in the **arrangement**: who enters the Basic Annals and who the Biographies, who shares a biography with whom, the order of the biographies, a line of someone else's poem quoted in the appraisal — **structure is values; a name-list is a manifesto**; “**不载于某书**” ('not recorded in a given book') and “**载于某书**” ('recorded in a given book') sound equally loud — the same method as “**沉默比选文更响**” in the previous chapter's anthology, 'silence is louder than the texts selected' (Our translation.)).
:::

::: example A three-act play: the last forty lines of the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu' {#ex-xiangyu}
Gaixia: **hearing the songs of Chu in the night** → “**力拔山兮气盖世，时不利兮骓不逝**” — 'My strength could uproot a mountain, ah, my spirit covered the age; the times are against me, ah, Zhui will not go.' (Our translation.) → **tears fell in several lines; those about him all wept, and none could look up**; the swift fights at Dongcheng 东城: “**此天之亡我，非战之罪也**” — 'It is Heaven that destroys me; it is not the fault of my fighting.' (Our translation.) → three swift fights in which he himself demonstrates “天之亡我” ('Heaven destroys me'); the Wu River 乌江: **the station headman had a boat ready** → “**纵江东父兄怜而王我，我何面目见之**” — 'Even if the fathers and brothers east of the River, out of pity, made me king, what face would I have to see them?' (Our translation.) → “**吾知公长者，吾骑此马五岁...不忍杀，以赐公**” — 'I know you are a man of worth. I have ridden this horse for five years... I cannot bear to kill it, and I give it to you.' (Our translation.) → **and so he cut his own throat**; the Grand Historian says: he cites the rumour “**舜目重瞳**” ('Shun had double pupils') — “**羽亦起陇亩之中...分裂天下而封王侯，政由羽出，号为霸王，位虽不终，近古以来未尝有也**” — 'Yu too rose from among the fields and acres... he split the realm and enfeoffed kings and marquises; government issued from Yu, he was styled Hegemon-King, and though his place did not last, there had been nothing like it since recent antiquity.' (Our translation.) — but immediately afterwards he criticises “**自矜功伐，奋其私智**” — 'He prided himself on his merit and his exploits, and gave free rein to his private cleverness.' (Our translation.), **returning “天也” ('It was Heaven.') on the spot as goods sent back**: “**身死东城，尚不觉寤而不自责，过矣**”……“**岂不谬哉**”! — 'He died at Dongcheng and still did not wake to it or blame himself: that was a fault... How could this not be absurd!' (Our translation.) Question: is the Grand Historian's 'final score' for Xiang Yu in the appraisal?
::: solution
**The score is not in the appraisal at all — it is in the assignment of form**: the specification of a Basic Annals (the treatment given to an emperor), the **length** of the Basic Annals (the heroes of the Biographies all cross the stage inside Xiang Yu's biography), and **the largest quota of lines in the spotlight of the whole performance** — **the star rating in the *Shiji* is decided by the type of building: to be housed in a Basic Annals is to recognise, after the fact, the Hegemon-King as the chairman of the age**. The appraisal is only **a verbal deduction of marks after the event**: it both writes his death with a full measure of poetry (the song of 'Oh, Yu' (*Yu xi* 虞兮) **is the first heroic death-song in Chinese literature** — the three-piece set of song + beauty + famous horse: **the Grand Historian issued the losers a full set of lyrical licences**), and in the seams between the characters it carries out the historian's duty (“岂不谬哉” — 'How could this not be absurd!' (Our translation.)). **Literature finds no crime; history finds a logical fault; the method of mutual illumination sentences him to observation for life** — three courts for one and the same person. **This is precisely the structural reason the *Shiji* becomes literature while not ceasing to be history**: it does not resolve the contradiction; it **typesets the contradiction**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The person you read is several chapters pieced together {#int-pin}
The *Shiji* taught the Chinese reader one thing: **no biography is true unless you have read the whole family bucket of what mutually illuminates him** — Liu Bang in the 'Basic Annals of Gaozu' is the founding protagonist who is “**仁而爱人，喜施**” — 'humane and loving towards others, and fond of giving' (Our translation.); in the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu' he is **the child-flinging father who, when the army is beaten, “推堕孝惠、鲁元车下”** — 'shoved Xiaohui and Luyuan down from the carriage' (Our translation.); in the 'Hereditary House of the Marquis of Liu' he is rebuffed to his face by Zhang Liang 张良 with “**不可**” ('It will not do.') — **the same crown shows different cracks in different windows**. This 'method of reading a person through many windows' has long been the instinct with which modern people handle information (one person: a CV / a friends' circle / a former colleague's assessment) — **Sima Qian was merely the earliest to refuse a single-source narrative**. One layer deeper: he does not even exempt the 'narrator' — **that sentence of self-statement, “究天人之际”** — 'to investigate the juncture of Heaven and man' (Our translation.) — **in the 'Letter in Reply to Ren An' (*Bao Ren An shu* 《报任安书》), admits that what the historian has to deal with is the 'juncture'** (between Heaven and man, between fact and judgement, between love and hate), **not either side, 'Heaven' or 'man'** — the greatness of the *Shiji* does not lie in its being objective, but in its **making the insecurity of its subjectivity public the whole way through**.
:::

::: history The transmission and the uses of one book {#sec-liu}
**Hidden and shown**: after the book was done it was **kept in the house, and “后世稍得见”** — 'later generations gradually came to see it' (Our translation.) — **“成一家之言”** — 'to form the words of one family' (Our translation.) — **was at first the words of one room**; **in the time of Emperor Xuan, his daughter's son Yang Yun 杨恽 submitted a memorial so that it would be heard, and only then was the *Shiji* made public** — **a private history of one family had to wait, and only a tie through the imperial in-laws (*waiqi* 外戚) brought the publication it was waiting for**; **that letter in which he speaks his own mind, the 'Letter in Reply to Ren An', survives today in full because the *Selections of Refined Literature* (*Wenxuan* 《文选》) preserved it** — **a private letter depends on an anthology to be transmitted: black humour in the study of texts**; **the three commentaries** (*sanjia zhu* 三家注) (in the Liu Song, **Pei Yin 裴骃's *Collected Explanations* (*Jijie* 集解)**; in the Tang, **Sima Zhen 司马贞's *Suoyin* (索引)**; in the Tang, **Zhang Shoujie 张守节's *Correct Meanings* (*Zhengyi* 正义)** — **a three-storey flat for one book**, [[classical-chinese-2/exegesis]] in its on-site version); **the age of critical marking**: **the Tang–Song *guwen* movement promoted the *Shiji* from 'history' to 'literature'** — promoted it from 'history' to 'literature' — Han Yu 韩愈's '*qi*', Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修's 'spirit and manner of the Six Ones', both came out through the *Shiji*'s gate; 'prose takes the *Shiji* as its ancestor' became the composition password of the age of the civil-service examinations; **Zhang Xuecheng 章学诚's 'the Six Classics are all history'** lifted the Histories division into the parlour of the Classics division; **Lu Xun 鲁迅's “史家之绝唱，无韵之《离骚》”** — 'the historian's unsurpassed song, an *Encountering Sorrow* without rhyme' (Our translation.) — (*An Outline of Han Literary History*, 《汉文学史纲要》) — **one advertising line has ruled the literary standing of the *Shiji* for a hundred years** (an unsurpassed song = a eulogy for **history and literature thereafter hardly sharing one body again**, and also the speech that awards it the prize); **the present**: **the *Shiji* has entered full-text search and 'databases of the method of mutual illumination'** — **turning 'read the family bucket' from a virtue into one click**.
:::

::: warning Four pits in reading history
**Taking the drama as the historical source** (the feel of a camera shot in Gaixia's “莫能仰视” ('none could look up') comes from **oral telling and imagination** — **asking 'might it be true?' is more professional than asking 'was it recorded?'**); **judging the person from a single chapter** (read only the 'Basic Annals of Gaozu' and you worship Liu Bang; read only the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu' and you sympathise with Xiang Yu — **the method of mutual illumination has already warned you: a person is compounded of many chapters**); **skipping the Tables and the Treatises** (“文学青年跳过律书平准书” — 'literary youth skip the Treatise on the Pitch-pipes and the Treatise on the Balanced Standard' (Our translation.) — **the 'Treatise on the Balanced Standard' is the medical report on Han finances; if you do not understand it you cannot read the foreign wars of Emperor Wu's reign, or the 'Biographies of the Harsh Officials'** — **literariness grows on a foundation of economy and institutions**); **taking 'The Grand Historian says' for a scoring machine** (appraisals often hold **restraint, irony, and what could not be helped** — the appraisal in the 'Biographies of the Xiongnu', on Emperor Wu's campaigns against the Xiongnu, offers only a faint word of criticism — the oblique brush of “在位之臣知今上意” — 'The ministers then in office knew the present sovereign's intent.' (Our translation.) — **before reading an appraisal, first ask: could he say this straight out?**).
:::

::: exercise A mutual-illumination jigsaw {level=1}
Make a '**Liu Bang jigsaw**': from the 'Basic Annals of Gaozu', the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu', the 'Hereditary House of the Marquis of Liu' and the 'Hereditary House of Chancellor Xiao', take one or two key entries from each (for example **分一杯羹、数项王十罪、踞洗见郦生、推堕子女车下** — 'sharing a cup of stew', 'counting out ten crimes of the King of Xiang', 'receiving Master Li while squatting at his wash', 'shoving son and daughter down from the carriage' — the place each comes from must be checked entry by entry), make a four-column comparison table, and write one observation of a 'difference between windows'.
::: solution
Marking: **zero mismatches between entry and chapter title** (assign “分一杯羹” ('sharing a cup of stew') to Xiang Yu and you are out — **the first hands-on lesson in the method of mutual illumination: do not take someone else's window for his face**); the observation sentence must bring out the **pragmatics** (the same rascally deed is “豁达权变” ('open-hearted and ready to adapt') in the Basic Annals, and “慢而侮人” ('arrogant, and insulting to others') in the biography over there — **narrative stance = choice of wording**); a conclusion that 'it will not piece together' is allowed — **not piecing together is a report that passes** (a person is a process, not a jigsaw).
:::

::: exercise Write a verdict for the name-list {level=2}
The first of the Biographies in the *Shiji* is **Bo Yi 伯夷**; the last two are 'Wandering Knights' and 'Money-makers' — some people say, on this basis, that Sima Qian '**first stood those who had starved to death at the very front, and at the end let those who made money and those who broke the prohibitions close the bill**'. Write **the affirmative** (the arrangement reveals a subtle meaning) and **the negative** (over-reading), 150 characters each, and explain: **what sort of external evidence could let one side win** (generic conventions of the same age / the author's self-statement elsewhere / a change in chapter order between editions; take one of these and follow it through).
::: solution
Marking: the affirmative's strongest card = **the 'Letter in Reply to Ren An', “述历黄帝以来”** — 'I narrate the course from the Yellow Emperor on' (Our translation.) — **plus the preface's own account of the order of the Biographies** (“**扶义倜傥，不令己失时，立功名于天下，作七十列传**” — 'For those who upheld the right and stood out from the crowd, who did not let themselves miss the time, and who established merit and a name in the world, I made the seventy Biographies.' (Our translation.) — the preface does indeed state a motive for the ordering); the negative's strongest card = **the ten Tables have a fixed logic of lineage, and the chapter order may have been arranged by later ages** (the doubt resting on this: **the 'Preface of the Grand Historian' is itself a text that has been copied**); the condition for winning must be something one can carry out (**thinking as the study of editions does: find the earliest catalogue record and check the chapter order against it**); the marker does not look at the stance, but at **an awareness of how strong the evidence is**.
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the *Shiji* is the ancestor of fiction {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters on the four legacies the *Shiji* handed to later fiction: **scene and dialogue, type-characters (wandering knights / harsh officials / flatterers and favourites), a narrator's personality in the manner of 'The Grand Historian says', and the blood-kinship of the name 'chuanqi'** (the '传' of the Tang tale, *chuanqi* 传奇, is the '传' of *liezhuan* 列传, the Biographies); and, taking one chapter of *Dream of the Red Chamber* (for example the way **You Sanjie 尤三姐 or Qingwen 晴雯** is written) or one martial-arts novel as the case, show that one of these legacies is still at work.
::: solution
Marking points: of the four legacies, at least **two must be nailed down** (the kinship of the character '传': ***liezhuan* → *chuanqi*; even the name is the heir**); the case must be **specific down to the sentence** (a character's entrance in the manner of “那妇人睁着眼” — 'that woman, with her eyes wide open' (Our translation.) — = the **method of opening the likeness** in a *Shiji* biography — how the Basic Annals and the Biographies '**set the make-up at the opening**' for a person); set the narrator's personality against the '**笔者按**' ('note by the present writer') of present-day web fiction — **intrusive narration has never died; it has only changed its ID**; if the conclusion grants that '**the divorce of history and literature was completed in the Tang, but the child went to literature**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The maze blueprint, account settled: the five forms (**building type is the star rating**), three oversteps (Confucius, Xiang Yu, the money-makers — **everyone the histories had refused was taken in**), the three-piece kit of brushwork (**scenes that trace the source to a person, the many windows of mutual illumination, arrangement's judgement that does not judge**), transmission (kept at home — imperial in-laws — the three commentaries — the *guwen* movement — the 'unsurpassed song' remark — the database). The method in one sentence: **to read the *Shiji*, first recognise the building, then piece the jigsaw, then ask 'could he say this straight out?'**. The next chapter leaves the court halls for the lanes: Han *yuefu* (Music Bureau ballads) — the street handed the microphone to the tongues of ordinary people.
:::
