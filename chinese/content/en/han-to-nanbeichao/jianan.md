---
title: "Jian’an literature"
---

In AD 196, Emperor Xian of the Han 汉献帝 was carried off to Xudu 许都 — **the last scrap of the Han dynasty's dignity moved house, yet literature, in the new landlord's courtyard, entered its golden age early**. The Three Caos 三曹 and the Seven Masters 七子 gathered at Ye 邺城 and did something no one had done before: **they made the writing of poetry an identity, made appraisal a form of social life, and made “writing does not perish” (文章不朽) an official slogan**. This chapter is about that “literary self-awareness in an age of chaos”.

## The Three Caos {#def-sanzao}

::: definition Cao Cao: new wine in an old bottle {#def-caocao}
**The hijacking of old *yuefu* titles** (*yuefu*: Music Bureau poems; ballads): every famous piece by Cao Cao 曹操 is an **old Han *yuefu* title** — 《薤露》 (*Xielu*, ‘Dew on the Shallots’) and 《蒿里》 (*Haoli*, ‘the land of the dead’) (**dirges written on the politics of the day**: “**白骨露于野，千里无鸡鸣。生民百遗一，念之断人肠**” (‘White bones lie exposed in the wild; for a thousand *li* no cock crows. Of the living people, one in a hundred is left; to think of it tears the bowels.’ (Our translation.) A *li* 里 is the traditional measure of distance; its length changed from age to age.), which he himself marks “**歌以咏志**” (‘I sing to give voice to my resolve.’ (Our translation.)) — **using a song sung for the dead to autopsy the age**. **Later ages simply called 《薤露》 and 《蒿里》 “a true record of the end of the Han” — the name *shīshǐ* 诗史 (‘poet-historian’) was one the men of the Tang all yielded to Du Fu 杜甫, and Cao Cao was the man who handed his paper in early**); 《短歌行》 (*Duan ge xing*, ‘Short Song’) — **“对酒当歌，人生几何” (‘Wine before me, I should sing; how long is a life?’ (Our translation.)) “月明星稀，乌鹊南飞” (‘The moon is bright, the stars are few; crows and magpies fly south.’ (Our translation.))** — **set lines of the *Book of Songs* taken apart and fitted together again at will (“青青子衿” (‘Green, green, your collar.’ (Our translation.)) and “呦呦鹿鸣，食野之苹” (‘*Youyou* cry the deer, feeding on the artemisia of the wild.’ (Our translation.)) carried in unchanged), the vocabulary of canonical learning commandeered for private lyric feeling for the first time**; 《步出夏门行·龟虽寿》 (*Bu chu Xia men xing*, ‘Walking Out of the Xia Gate’; *Gui sui shou*, ‘Though the Tortoise Lives Long’) — “**老骥伏枥，志在千里；烈士暮年，壮心不已**” (‘An old steed stoops in its stall, its will set on a thousand *li*; a man of ardent purpose in his evening years, his bold heart does not cease.’ (Our translation.)) — **written as pension insurance for himself at fifty-three: a lifespan “does not rest only with Heaven”, resentencing the doctrine of a fate decreed by Heaven as a doctrine of struggle**. Liu Xie 刘勰 fixed his judgement of the poetry in the two words “**古直**” (‘archaic and direct’) alone; Zhong Rong 钟嵘 placed Cao Cao in the lower grade, yet allowed that he “**甚有悲凉之句**” (‘does have lines of deep desolation.’ (Our translation.)) — **the mismatch of grade and famous line is the most famous scene of a miscarriage of justice in the *Shi pin* 诗品 (‘Gradings of Poetry’)**); **《让县自明本志令》** (*Rang xian zi ming ben zhi ling*, ‘Order Yielding the Counties to Make My Original Aims Plain’) — “**设使国家无有孤，不知当几人称帝，几人称王**” (‘Suppose the state had not had me: I do not know how many men would have called themselves emperor, how many would have called themselves king.’ (Our translation.)) — **the summit of colloquial political autobiography: taking the credit with no ornament is itself a famous scene in literary history**.
:::

::: definition Cao Pi: the critic-emperor {#def-caopi}
**《典论·论文》** (*Dian lun* 典论, ‘Authoritative Discourses’, the essay ‘On Literature’) — **the first monograph of Chinese literary criticism**. Four sentences changed the whole pattern. **① “盖文章，经国之大业，不朽之盛事”** (‘For writing is a great enterprise in the ordering of the state, a splendid affair that does not perish.’ (Our translation.)) — **lifting the rhapsody (*fu*) up from “a boy's carving of insects” (Yang Xiong's 扬雄 words of regret, [[classical-chinese-2/parallel-prose-fu]]) and setting it right as a second system for establishing deeds: Emperor Wen 文帝 (Cao Pi) himself issued literature its licence**; **② “文以气为主，气之清浊有体，不可力强而致”** (‘In writing, *qi* (vital energy) is the master; whether *qi* is clear or turbid has its own constitution, and cannot be reached by force of effort.’ (Our translation.)) — **a theory of genius: style is fixed by innate gift, as voices differ, “虽在父兄，不能以移子弟”** (‘Even with a father or an elder brother, it cannot be passed on to the sons and younger brothers.’ (Our translation.)) (a sentence at which even Cao Pi himself admits defeat); **③ “奏议宜雅，书论宜理，铭诔尚实，诗赋欲丽”** (‘Memorials and deliberations should be dignified; letters and discourses should be well reasoned; inscriptions and threnodies value what is solid; poetry and rhapsody desire beauty.’ (Our translation.)) — **four classes and eight forms; the four characters “诗赋欲丽” write “beauty” formally into the KPI of literary genres — a declaration of independence for lyric literature**; **④ “文人相轻，自古而然”** (‘Men of letters belittle one another; it has been so since ancient times.’ (Our translation.)) — **the first sentence of a sociology of literature in China: he understood it better than anyone, because he was the host of the salon**. 《燕歌行》 (*Yan ge xing*, ‘Song of Yan’) — “**秋风萧瑟天气凉，草木摇落露为霜，群燕辞归鹄南翔**” (‘The autumn wind is bleak and soughing, the weather cold; grasses and trees shake and fall, the dew becomes frost; the swallows in flocks take their leave and return, the swans fly south.’ (Our translation.)) — **the first complete seven-character poem that survives, rhyming in every line, yet in the voice of a longing wife beyond the frontier**: **an emperor writing a longing wife is both an experiment in form and a political persona**.
:::

::: definition Cao Zhi: bone and *qi* singularly high {#def-cao}
**The line between the earlier and later halves of his life = the greatest specimen of a turn in Jian’an 建安 literature**: in the earlier period, 《白马篇》 (*Bai ma pian*, ‘The White Horse’) — “**捐躯赴国难，视死忽如归**” (‘I give my body and go to the kingdom's crisis, and look on death as lightly as a return home.’ (Our translation.)), **the figure of the knight-errant youth = the standard identity photograph of how Jian’an scholar-gentlemen imagined themselves**; the later period (he failed in the contest to be named heir, and was moved again and again from fief to fief) — **“骨气奇高，词采华茂，情兼雅怨，体被文质”** (‘Bone and *qi* are singularly high; the colour of the diction is splendidly rich; feeling joins the elegant and the resentful; the form is clothed in both pattern and substance.’ (Our translation.)) (the top grade in Zhong Rong's *Shi pin*, almost the full-marks template set for the five-character line): 《赠白马王彪》 (*Zeng Baima wang Biao*, ‘Presented to Biao, Prince of Baima’) — “**心悲动我神，弃置莫复陈**” (‘Grief at heart stirs my spirit; set it aside, and state it no more.’ (Our translation.)) — **the three characters “莫复陈” (‘state it no more’) weigh the whole poem down: everything that could be stated has been cut; what the cutting leaves is the wrong**; 《洛神赋》 (*Luo shen fu*, ‘Rhapsody on the Goddess of the Luo’) — “**凌波微步，罗袜生尘**” (‘She treads the waves with minute steps; her gauze stockings raise a dust.’ (Our translation.)) — **the last spray of the motif, in the *Songs of Chu*, of love between a human and a spirit, and also a perfect landing of the “rhapsody” as a short lyric piece** (the great department store of [[han-to-nanbeichao/han-fu]] is here dismantled into a crystal exhibition hall). **With Cao Zhi, Jian’an five-character verse completes “from song to reading”**: the voice of *yuefu* → the literary tone of the *Nineteen Old Poems* → **Cao Zhi's parallelism, ornate diction and design of the piece — the five-character line formally acquires its own household register as written literature**.
:::

::: example The salon at Ye: the invention of composing together on one topic {#ex-yexia}
In the Jian’an years, Cao Pi and Cao Zhi roamed the Western Garden 西园 together with Wang Can 王粲, Chen Lin 陈琳, Liu Zhen 刘桢, Ying Yang 应玚 and Xu Gan 徐干, **the same evening, the same topic, each writing his own** (group pieces on shared topics survive, among them the 《公宴诗》 (*Gong yan shi*, ‘Poems of the Ducal Banquet’)). Cao Pi's 《与吴质书》 (*Yu Wu Zhi shu*, ‘Letter to Wu Zhi’ 吴质) mourns that salon and comments on the dead friends one by one: “**徐干时有齐气……应玚和而不壮，刘桢壮而不密**” (‘Xu Gan at times has the *qi* of Qi… Ying Yang is harmonious but not robust; Liu Zhen is robust but not close-knit.’ (Our translation.)) — **one shortcoming apiece: it is “men of letters belittle one another” operated in reverse, criticism in the form of mourning**. Questions: ① What does composing together on the same topic mean for the evolution of genres? ② Taken together, what new profession did the *Dian lun* and the ‘Letter to Wu Zhi’ invent?
::: solution
① **The same topic = a literary experiment with the variables controlled**: topic, occasion and the constraints of prosody are all the same, **and high and low stand plain at once — poetry has “horizontal evaluation” for the first time**. The style-labels of the Seven Masters (“forceful” 遒, “close-knit” 密, “the *qi* of Qi” 齐气) **were read off precisely in this writing side by side** — **the salon is both a unit of production and a generator of criticism**. The pattern of complimentary reply in the ducal-banquet poems also produced the great tradition of **presentation-and-reply poetry** (from then on a poem could be “sent to someone”; half the social life of Tang poetry starts work from here). ② **Critic + literary historian**: Cao Pi turned appraisal from “judging people at the month's beginning” (character assessment in the manner of Xu Shao 许劭) into **the judging of writing** — **a transplanting of *pǐnzǎo* 品藻 (‘the appraisal of persons’): first judging people, then judging writing; this is the gene of Six Dynasties criticism** (the *Shi pin* of [[han-to-nanbeichao/tales-criticism]] waits at the finish to take this baton). And his declaration of “a splendid affair that does not perish” **turned the ten years he held power into the window in which literature was approved as a project**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Why an age of chaos produced *fenggu* {#int-fenggu}
The shape which “the Jian’an spirit” (*Jian’an fenggu*; *fenggu* 风骨, ‘wind and bone’; vigour of feeling and structure) took in its own time is Liu Xie's eight characters: “**慷慨以任气，磊落以使才**” (‘Impassioned, they give rein to *qi*; open and bold, they put their talent to use.’ (Our translation.)) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, ‘Elucidating Poetry’, 《文心·明诗》) — **the age pushed “life is short” and “achievement is great” to the limit at the same time, and the amplitude of the poetry was pulled to the full**. In the *Nineteen Old Poems* the scholar-gentlemen sigh towards the white poplars (“**驱车上东门**”, ‘I drive my carriage out through the Upper East Gate.’ (Our translation.)); **in Cao Cao's mouth the same theme of morning dew is rewritten as “the bold heart does not cease” — the same despair: the Nineteen use it to brew wine, Jian’an uses it to whet a knife**. What is called *fenggu* was recognised again and again after the fact by later ages (Chen Zi'ang 陈子昂, Yin Fan 殷璠 and Wang Shizhen 王士禛 each had a version of his own), but three things in the core can be set on the same scale: **the weight of feeling (not moaning where there is no illness), the bone-strength of the language (not a piling-up of ornament), the sense of being on the spot in one's own age (not hiding away in the study)**. **The counter-intuitive point is this: Jian’an's “literary self-awareness” was not cultivated by keeping clear of the age of chaos — it was precisely forced out by that chaos. A lifespan was uncertain, achievement stood before the eyes, and writing was the only thing one could carry away**.
:::

::: history A movement of retrospective recognition {#sec-zhuiren}
**Its own time (196–232)**: the salon at Ye — Cao Pi's *Dian lun* — Cao Zhi's later period — **the set of three, creation, criticism and the model, arrived together (the chain of evidence for literary self-awareness closed while they were still alive)**; **Eastern Jin to the Southern Dynasties**: Xie Lingyun 谢灵运, “**天下才一石，曹子建独得八斗**” (‘Of the talent under Heaven there is one *dan*; Cao Zijian alone obtained eight *dou*.’ (Our translation.) One *dan* 石 contained ten *dou* 斗.) (as quoted in 《释常谈》 (*Shi chang tan*, ‘Explanations of Common Sayings’) — **“eight *dou*” has from then on been the superlative of the Chinese language**) — **Cao Zhi was lifted out from among the Seven Masters and set up alone as the ancestor of “bone and *qi*”**; Liu Xie's ‘Elucidating Poetry’ opened a file on Jian’an, “**暨建安之初，五言雄赡… 慷慨以任气**” (‘Come the beginning of Jian’an, five-character verse was bold and ample… impassioned, they gave rein to *qi*.’ (Our translation.)); Zhong Rong's *Shi pin* places Cao Zhi in the top grade, and **in the table of sources and streams a great many poets are “其源出于陈思”** (‘his source issues from Chensi.’ (Our translation.)) (Prince Chensi 陈思王 is Cao Zhi) — **for the first time in the history of criticism, someone became other people's “source of household registration”**; **Tang**: **Chen Zi'ang's 《与东方公书》 (*Yu Dongfang gong shu*, ‘Letter to Lord Dongfang’): “汉魏风骨，晋宋莫传”** (‘The *fenggu* of the Han and the Wei, the Jin and the Liu-Song did not pass on.’ (Our translation.)) — **“Jian’an *fenggu*” left the factory as a weapon: it was used to strike the target of the palace style (*gōngtǐ* 宫体) of Qi and Liang**; Yin Fan's 《河岳英灵集》 (*He yue ying ling ji*, ‘The Heroic Spirits of the Rivers and Marchmounts’) went on under the same banner; **Song**: **men of the Song began to reverse the verdict, and would not let it rest: some critics pointed out that within Cao Zhi's “bone and *qi*” there was an admixture of affected *qi* — the concept of *fenggu* took a recoil for the first time**; **Ming**: the Former and Latter Seven Masters read “**Han and Wei ancient-style poetry**” straight through as one body — **Jian’an was absorbed into the larger archaist signboard, “Han and Wei”**; **Qing**: Wang Shizhen picked out “***fenggu***” in particular as one core of his discussion of poetry — **the concept completed its independence: Jian’an withdrew onto the shelf, and *fenggu* became a brand name**; **today**: “Jian’an *fenggu*” enters the dictionaries — **a retrospective recognition lasting one thousand eight hundred years sealed the poetic exchanges of three politicians and a crowd of aides as the gold standard of Chinese poetry**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading Jian’an
**Crying “poet-historian” too early** (Cao Cao's 《薤露》 and 《蒿里》 have the spirit of a true record; **“poet-historian” is a title conferred by later ages** — before adding “history” to any poet, first ask: does it record, or is it being used to prove something?); **treating the Three Caos as pure men of letters** (**Cao Cao was a military commander, Cao Pi an emperor, Cao Zhi a political failure — the whole tension of Jian’an literature comes from the scene of power**; to read 《赠白马王彪》 (‘Presented to Biao, Prince of Baima’) as “talent that found no use” is to read it smaller than it is — **it is a prisoner's statement of appeal**); **taking “Jian’an *fenggu*” for an objective standard** (it is a **weapon manufactured** by Chen Zi'ang and his like — the pleasure of the history of literary ideas: first the target, then the signboard); **ignoring the displaced origins of the Seven Masters** (Wang Can depended on Liu Biao 刘表; Cai Yan 蔡琰 was lost among the Xiongnu; Chen Lin changed masters and wrote a proclamation — **“impassioned, giving rein to *qi*” has its materials science: *qi* comes out of suffering**).
:::

::: exercise Horizontal evaluation {level=1}
An experiment on the same topic. Imitating the salon at Ye, with a friend (or playing both parts yourself), each of you, on **the same topic** (for example “a convenience store late at night”), writes a new poem or a piece of doggerel of no more than eight lines; then **comment on each other in the sentence-pattern of the *Dian lun***: each person one sentence of the form “each of A and B has a shortcoming” (for example “X is robust but not close-knit; Y is harmonious but not robust”).
::: solution
Marking: the sentences of mutual comment **must be neatly parallel, and the shortcomings must differ** (**the etiquette of evaluation on the same topic: the dispraise must not be dispraise of the same cut**). Attach an observation of one hundred characters: **under the same topic, can you see the two writers' *qi*?** — a slowness of the “Qi *qi*” kind, a rush of the “untrammelled *qi*” kind — **only when you translate that vocabulary of *xuánxué* 玄学 (‘mysterious learning’) into linguistic features that can be checked (rhythm, the density of allusion, habits of ending) have you really read Cao Pi**.
:::

::: exercise A verdict for a tragedy {level=2}
Two places in Cai Yan's 《悲愤诗》 (‘Poem of Grief and Indignation’): “**马边悬男头，马后载妇女**” (‘At the horses' sides hang men's heads; behind the horses they carry the women.’ (Our translation.)) (the column of those driven off as captives); “**儿前抱我颈，问母欲何之**” (‘My child comes forward and clasps my neck, and asks his mother where she means to go.’ (Our translation.)) (the moment of returning to the Han and parting from her son). Choose either place and write a verdict of 200 characters — **by what device the “truth” of this poem is made to stand** (set it against “tracing the source to a person” in Chapter 2, and against “facelessness” in the previous chapter).
::: solution
For reference: it rests on **bodily detail in the first person** (the child's question is the most cutting — **the privacy of a detail is proportional to the cost of inventing it: one can invent “白骨露于野” (‘white bones lie exposed in the wild’ (Our translation.)), one cannot invent “儿前抱我颈” (‘my child comes forward and clasps my neck’ (Our translation.))**) + **the credit which the autobiographical genre endorses** (the poem's title is itself a declaration: I have been through this — **the ‘Poem of Grief and Indignation’ is the extreme form of “tracing the source to a person”: the source is the author's own body**). Marking: credit for being able to point out **the solitary-peak standing, in literary history, of narratives of women's trauma** (epic before this is all from a male point of view — **China's longest narrative poem, *The Peacock Flies Southeast* (《孔雀东南飞》), writes of institutions devouring people; the ‘Poem of Grief and Indignation’ writes of war devouring people; and both come from / stay pressed against the victim's own voice** — **the twin masterpieces of Han and Wei narrative poetry happen, both of them, to be the testimony of people at the margin**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the bill for literary self-awareness {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**Jian’an is the age of literature's self-awareness (Lu Xun 鲁迅, ‘The Wei–Jin Manner and Writing, and Their Relation to Drugs and Wine’, 《魏晋风度及文章与药及酒之关系》)**”. You must deal with three bodies of material: **Cao Pi's declaration of “a splendid affair that does not perish”** (the official text of self-awareness); **Cao Zhi's belittling of himself over “rhapsodies as a minor path” in his 《与杨德祖书》 (*Yu Yang Dezu shu*, ‘Letter to Yang Dezu’ 杨德祖)** (“**岂徒以翰墨为勋绩、辞赋为君子哉**” (‘How could one merely take brush and ink for one's meritorious deeds, and rhapsodies for the making of a gentleman?’ (Our translation.)) — **what he longed for was to establish deeds**); **the actual mode of production in the salon at Ye** (courtesy exchanges, shared topics, appraisal). Argue: **what, exactly, is the “self-awareness” aware of — that literature has value, or that literature has a cost?** And pick out one **conceptual fault** in the “theory of self-awareness” (for example: letting a declaration stand in for the relations of production / letting an elite sample stand in for the whole picture of the age).
::: solution
Marking points: the contradictory materials are used to the full (**the elder brother says literature is a great enterprise that does not perish; the younger brother says rhapsodies are a minor path, and his will is to establish deeds — of the parties to the “age of self-awareness”, one is talking the price up and one is talking it down: self-awareness ≠ narcissism**); the argument from “cost” holds together (**literature's independence = it begins to have a market price of its own, and Cao Zhi's discontent is precisely a by-product of that price — once “man of letters” becomes an identity, it can be used to demote someone**: Cao Cao used literary men as his aides, Cao Pi set store by himself through his writing, and the label “man of letters” led Cao Zhi wrong for a lifetime — **to invent the identity is to invent the cage**); to hit even one conceptual fault is already good (**Lu Xun's lecture had, in the first place, the context of the occasion; if “self-awareness” is defined as “knowing that one is doing literature”, then Sima Qian 司马迁 was more “self-aware” than Cao Pi — what Jian’an invented was not self-awareness, but the institutionalisation of self-awareness: the three-in-one of the group, of appraisal, and of transmission to later ages**); one whose conclusion dares the other side (“Jian’an was not an awakening; it was **going public** — for the first time literature had an exchange, a rating agency and shareholders”) receives an A.
:::

::: summary
Closing the accounts of the salon and the hall: Cao Cao (**old bottles filled with new wine; a dirge as autopsy; morning dew rewritten as a doctrine of struggle**); Cao Pi (**a declaration in four sentences — status, genius, the sorting of genres, mutual contempt — the emperor issues literature its licence**; the first performance of the seven-character line, in 《燕歌行》 (‘Song of Yan’)); Cao Zhi (**from song to reading: the five-character line obtains a household register as written literature; “莫复陈” (‘state it no more’) = what the cutting leaves is the wrong**); at Ye (**composing together on the same topic = horizontal evaluation; criticism is born with the salon**); *fenggu* (**in its own time it is “impassioned, giving rein to *qi*”; afterwards it is a weapon — one thousand eight hundred years of retrospective recognition**). One sentence of method: **first ask from which place in power a work comes, and only then speak of style** — every Jian’an line of “人生几何” (‘how long is a life?’) is a prince of the borderlands and an exiled scholar-gentleman speaking at the same time. The next chapter, between *xuanxue* and poison: Zhengshi 正始 literature, and how Ruan Ji's 阮籍 eighty-two “anonymous poems” wrote what could not be said into the most obscure greatness in China.
:::
