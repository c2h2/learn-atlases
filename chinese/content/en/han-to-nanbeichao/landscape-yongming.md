title: "Landscape poetry and the Yongming style"
---

The previous chapter's closing argument said: *xuanyan* 玄言 ('metaphysical discourse') had unloaded the task of symbolising, and only then did scenery have a chance to be nothing but scenery. This lesson, this chapter, delivers the goods — **two things made Chinese poetry slough off its old body and change its bones: in subject-matter, landscape took the householder's seat; in sound, poetry was fitted with a metronome**. The former is surnamed Xie (two people); the latter is surnamed Shen.

## Xie Lingyun 谢灵运: writing landscape as a survey report {#def-xielingyun}

::: definition The scenes of Yongjia and 'spring grass on the pond' {#def-daxie}
**The persona**: the Xie were a lineage of the highest rank — **Lingyun inherited the title Duke of Kangle; he considered that on his ability he ought to take part in confidential affairs of state, yet he lost the contest with the powerful of the new dynasty**: **a display, out of place, of the previous dynasty's aristocracy in the present one**. **Sent out as governor and still unable to have his way, he then roamed at will**: climbing mountains, felling trees, opening roads through the hills, **chiselling mountains and crossing ravines from the southern hills of Shining 始宁 straight through to Linhai 临海; a governor taking his retinue into the mountains was like a survey party — a local official's report alarmed the court, which took them for "山贼" ('mountain bandits')** (his biography in the *Song shu* 宋书 ('Book of Song') — **the first time "landscape" entered the official histories in the form of social news: the man who wrote landscape himself became the scandal of landscape**). **The case of the famous lines**: "**池塘生春草，园柳变鸣禽**" ('Spring grass grows on the pond; the garden willows change their singing birds.' (Our translation.)) — the *Shipin* 诗品 ('Gradings of Poets') records that he dreamed of his younger clansman Huilian 惠连, and "**此语有神助，非吾语也**" ('These words had divine aid; they are not my words.' (Our translation.)) — **later readers exposed the secret of Heaven: all common characters, zero allusion, the antithesis not putting its strength into it — the "wonder" lies precisely in "not being brilliant": when everyone else is polishing the words, not polishing became the highest polish** (**he is the other half of the subject of "庄老告退，山水方滋"** ('Zhuangzi and Laozi take their leave; landscape then flourishes.' (Our translation.)): **Lingyun's "clarity" (*qing* 清) is the washing-off of the printing ink of metaphysical discourse, so that the scenery makes its entrance still carrying its dampness**). **The structural specimen**: his landscape poems have a standard three sections — **record the track of the outing → describe the look of things → haul one metaphysical discourse on to close**: **when landscape poetry was born it was still wrapped in the swaddling clothes of metaphysical discourse** — cutting the umbilical cord was a matter for later generations (Xie Tiao 谢朓, He Xun 何逊, and the people of the Tang queued up to carry it out). **"Formal likeness" takes up its post**: the chapter 'Colours of Things' in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (*Wenxin diaolong*) delivers its official assessment of the age: "**自近代以来，文贵形似，窥情风景之上，钻貌草木之中**" ('From recent times on, writing has prized formal likeness, peering into feeling upon the scenery and boring into appearance amid the grasses and trees.' (Our translation.)) — **depiction itself became the value** — *bide* 比德 ('matching virtue to things'; 仁者乐山, 'The benevolent delight in mountains.' (Our translation.)) retires from the post, **and landscape is promoted from a moral metaphor to an object of the senses**.
:::

::: definition Xie Tiao and 'becoming the short poem' {#def-xietiao}
"**Xiao Xie**" (Xie Tiao), set against "**Da Xie**": **cut away the profusion and clarify the bone** — Da Xie is a long survey report; Xiao Xie is a **postcard: a chosen view instead of the panorama, atmosphere instead of process**: "**余霞散成绮，澄江静如练**" ('The last of the glow disperses into figured silk; the clear river lies still as plain silk.' (Our translation.)) ('晚登三山还望京邑', 'Climbing the Three Mountains in the Evening and Looking Back at the Capital') — **stacking the Yangtze as two kinds of textile: a metaphor on a cosmic scale, paired with a calm at degree zero; Li Bai 李白 in his whole life never walked out of this couplet ("解道澄江净如练，令人长忆谢玄晖", 'I have come to understand "the clear river, pure as plain silk"; it makes one long remember Xie Xuanhui.' (Our translation.))**. "**好诗圆美流转如弹丸**" ('A good poem is round and lovely, turning and flowing like a pellet.' (Our translation.)) (the *Nan shi* 南史 ('History of the Southern Dynasties'), Wang Yun 王筠 quoting Shen Yue 沈约) — **the aesthetics of poetry for the first time listed "smoothness" itself as a standard: a turn from "is the content right?" to "is the mouthfeel good?"**. *The Literary Mind* draws up the period's general ledger: "**宋初文咏，体有因革，庄老告退，而山水方滋**" ('In the literary songs of the early Song, the style had both inheritance and change; Zhuangzi and Laozi took their leave, and landscape then flourished.' (Our translation.)) — **note the wording: 告退 / 方滋, took their leave / then flourished — not a revolution, a change of shift**; and the **Yongming style**'s name in its own day was "**新变**" ('new change') — **the first time in the history of Chinese literary theory that "new change" was not a charge but a model number**.
:::

## The four tones and the eight faults: invention of the metronome {#def-shenglv}

::: definition The Yongming study of prosody {#def-yongming}
**Technical background**: Zhou Yong 周颙 **was skilled at discerning sounds and rhymes**, and with Shen Yue and others the theory of the "**four tones**" arose — **the level, rising, departing and entering tones (the entering tone is also called the checked tone)** became poetry's four pitch-gears (**the four tones ≠ level and oblique; but "level" stands on its own, while "rising, departing and entering" were later packed together as "oblique" — the level and oblique tones (also called level and deflected) of recent-style poetry are the four tones' consolidated statement**, and the [[poetry-metrics]] factory takes its material on here). **Three causes**: ***zhuandu* 转读 ('the modulated chanting of Buddhist scriptures')** (a craze for research into the pitch of Sanskrit; monks and literati in the same hall), **the natural piling-up of rhythm in the parallel lines of rhapsody** (the antithesis of [[classical-chinese-2/parallel-prose-fu]] is the forerunner of prosody: **once the number of characters is aligned, the next step is to align pitch**), **the great-clan salon turned into a competition** (even a talent of eight *dou* needs a new racetrack). **The programme** (Shen Yue, 'Discussion appended to the Biography of Xie Lingyun' in the *Song shu*): "**欲使宫羽相变，低昂互节；若前有浮声，则后须切响。一简之内，音韵尽殊；两句之中，轻重悉异**" ('One would have the notes *gong* and *yu* alternate, the low and the high setting the measure for each other; if a floating sound comes first, a clipped sound must follow. Within a single line, the sounds and rhymes are all distinct; within two lines, the light and the heavy are in every case different.' (Our translation.)) — **four sentences = an upgrade of poetry from "rhyming by nature" to "scored the whole way through"**. **The eight faults**: *pingtou* 平头 ('level head'), *shangwei* 上尾 ('raised tail'), *fengyao* 蜂腰 ('wasp's waist'), *hexi* 鹤膝 ('crane's knee'), *dayun* 大韵 ('major rhyme'), *xiaoyun* 小韵 ('minor rhyme'), *pangniu* 旁纽 ('side initial'), *zhengniu* 正纽 ('direct initial') — **all of them cast as "prohibitions": they only say which combinations of sound are not allowed to appear, and they do not give you a ready-made template**; **a prohibitive rule is a sampler**: **cut, from every possible tone-sequence of the ten characters in two lines, the part that "sounds harsh", and the remaining feasible region will grow a shape of itself — that shape is the later regulated line**.
:::

::: example How subtraction grows into addition: the birth of the regulated line {#ex-jianfa}
Using this lesson's mechanism, complete a problem in arithmetic. One five-character couplet is ten characters, and each character takes one of two settings, level or oblique (a simplified model): ① the total number of combinations? ② If one forbids "five characters running oblique in the upper line, and five characters running level in the lower line" (of the crane's-knee / one-sided-withering kind), ③ and further forbids the two lines having the same tone in corresponding positions (of the level-head kind), ④ and further forbids the last character of an even line from dragging on in the same tone as anything other than the rhyme-words of the whole piece (of the raised-tail kind) — what does the remaining "easy-on-the-ear region" look like? For comparison: what genre is this?
::: solution
① **2 to the 10th power = 1024** kinds; after ②–④ have been subtracted one after another, **the high-frequency zone of what survives is highly concentrated**: one or two patterns keep reappearing, the upper line "**仄仄平平仄**", the lower line "**平平仄仄平**" — **because, under the three constraints "禁相邻同调" ('forbid adjacent characters in the same tone'), "禁结尾撞车" ('forbid the endings colliding') and "禁头把齐平" ('forbid the heads being levelled flush'), they are the only largest family of solutions that can be satisfied all at once**. **Conclusion: the regulated poem (*lüshi*) is not something anyone invented; it is the fixed point that the eight faults' subtraction subtracts its way to** — Shen Yue and his company thought they were drawing up a blacklist, **and the day the blacklist is exhaustively listed is the moment the metre develops** (so both sentences are right, "the Yongming style failed to become regulated verse" and "the Yongming style invented recent-style poetry": **a failed concrete list, a successful abstract algorithm**). Marking: working out the order of magnitude, plus saying "fixed point", is full marks.
:::

::: intuition The politics of sound {#int-shengyin}
Why did the metronome appear in precisely this hundred years? **Because poetry's "audience" had changed**: Han *yuefu* (Music Bureau poems; ballads) was sung to the marketplace and the court; Jian'an was for the wine feast; **Yongming was for the study and the desk — when poetry turned from "sung for people to hear" to "read for the eye to hear", sound went from being the carrier to being an object under management** (**metre is the grammar of silent reading: the announcer in your head who sounds the words for you is the commissioning party of the Yongming style**). The people who pushed the four-tone theory were mostly **friends of the Buddhist order** — **a Chinese historical phonology that came from the sound of chanting scriptures drove Chinese from "a natural sound" to "a sound held up to reflection"** ([[phonology/why-phonology]]: the birth of historical phonology is of one source with poetry: **first poetry's question, and only then the rhyme dictionary's answer**); and **the "fussiness" of the eight faults** is precisely their historical merit: **only when the rules are fine to the point of absurdity can the poets cry out in pain together, and the scale of that cry (which faults must be avoided, which may be relaxed) is the true body of later regulated verse** — **metre is not a genius's design; it is the crystallisation of complaint**.
:::

::: history From 'new change' to 'Shen and Song' {#sec-xinbian}
**483–493**, Yongming: the Prince of Jingling's **Western Residence** — **Shen Yue, Xie Tiao, Wang Rong 王融, Xiao Yan 萧衍, Xiao Chen 萧琛, Ren Fang 任昉 and Lu Chui 陆倕, the "Eight Friends": the first time in the history of the salon that prosody was taken as the club's articles**; **the 490s**, Zhong Rong 钟嵘, in the 'Preface' to the *Shipin*, mocks the doctrine of the faults of sound for making writing "**文多拘忌，伤其真美**" ('full of constraints and taboos, injuring its true beauty' (Our translation.)) — **a bad review from a contemporary is the hardest exhibit: the illness really did exist, and the medicine really was bitter**; **Liang**: palace-style poetry (*gongti shi*; Emperor Jianwen 简文帝) — **prosody and amorous feeling merge: the new change turned to probing downwards in subject-matter, supplying examples for the "amorous" category of regulated verse**; Chen and Sui: **Yin Keng 阴铿 and He Xun, along the line of "清省" ('clear and spare') — the embryo of the five-character regulated poem took shape in the short poems of the south**; **the Northern Dynasties**: **Yu Xin 庾信 went north (555) — a fusion experiment in "南方温婉＋北方刚劲" ('southern gentleness + northern vigour'), fuelled by exile**; **the early Tang**: **Shen Quanqi 沈佺期 and Song Zhiwen 宋之问, "回忌声病，约句准篇" ('avoiding the faults of sound, regulating the line and standardising the piece' (Our translation.)) — regulated verse took its settled form, and the medal of merit was issued to those who summed it up**; **the High Tang**: **Du Fu 杜甫, "晚节渐于诗律细" ('In my late years I gradually grow finer in the rules of poetry.' (Our translation.)), and a specimen from the opposing side is also on the scene: Li Bai resisted regulated verse with ancient-style verse — **the ruling power of metre can be proved from the very existence of its opponents**; **later ages**: **"文变染乎世情" ('Literary change is dyed by the feeling of the age.' (Our translation.)) (*The Literary Mind*) — from landscape to prosody, the whole of this unit is an instance of "the age's feeling changed, and literature had no choice but to"**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading this lesson
**Scolding the eight faults as "formalism"** (**what they forbid is "being awkward in the mouth" — in an age when no one had yet reflected on the sound of Chinese, legislating mouthfeel was a scientific experiment, not a fetish for the palace style**); **reading "池塘生春草" ('Spring grass grows on the pond.' (Our translation.)) as a miracle** (**the theory of divine aid is the poet's own public relations**: the condition of this line's success is precisely that it is "learnable" — what one learns is "not polishing", and the precondition of "not polishing" is having polished to the very top); **supposing that landscape = nature** (**landscape poetry is a compound of the great clans' way of going on outings, the manor economy, and the taste for "clarity": looking at mountains is an act of consumption entering the poem — Lingyun's "survey party" is the material basis; "nature" has always been an invented way of looking**); **using how finished Tang poetry is to sneer at the Yongming half-finished product** (**the arrogance of an evolutionary point of view: the eight faults' "fussy failure" is a necessary stage in the making of regulated verse — you cannot sneer at an embryo for having no lungs**).
:::

::: exercise A defence of the spring grass {level=1}
"池塘生春草" ('Spring grass grows on the pond.' (Our translation.)) has been sung to the skies down the ages, but a Tang writer of a later generation sneered: "此吾家儿童之语也" ('These are the words of a child of my own house.' (Our translation.)). **Write a defence of 150 characters for this line** (restricted to this lesson's two weapons: "the polish of not polishing" and "common characters, zero allusion, zero antithesis"), **then use another 50 characters to rebut it on behalf of "a child's words"**.
::: solution
Marking: the defence must land on **contextual value** (in the market of "俪采百字之偶" ('ornate parallelism running to a hundred characters' (Our translation.)), plain description is differentiated pricing — **half a line's meaning sits on the price-list of its age**, which is the thinking about editions in [[classical-chinese-2/commentaries]], transferred to another use); the rebuttal must also stand: **"not polishing" can be imitated without end; once it becomes a set routine, "pond and spring grass" drops back to "a child's words" — greatness is a conspiracy between the age and the line, and one side does not carry the can alone**.
:::
:::

::: exercise A check-up for faults of sound {level=2}
Provide a five-character couplet of your own making (for example "**竹喧归浣女，莲动下渔舟**" ('Bamboo murmurs and the washerwomen go home; lotuses stir and a fishing boat comes down.' (Our translation.)) — this is a ready-made regulated line by Wang Wei 王维; this question requires you to **compose for yourself** one couplet that conforms as a regulated line, plus one couplet that deliberately commits "level head"): mark the four tones of each character (a rough marking on the system of the *Guangyun* 广韵 ('Broad Rhymes') is enough; see [[phonology/rhyme-tables]]), point out where the "fault" is and why it is awkward to read aloud, and read it to a companion as a blind test.
::: solution
Marking: **the four-tone marking and the description of position must be checkable** (level head = **the first character of the upper and lower lines (or the first two characters) belongs to the same tone category** — for example if both lines open with "仄仄", top-heavy, and the role played is the same); if the blind test **does not hear it**, one must honestly write "this fault may be relaxed" — **the historical rate at which the eight faults were weeded out is itself one of the correct answers to this question** (an honesty on the level of Shen Yue's own admission, "**达大理者，今或未能**" ('As for those who reach the great principle, perhaps none today is yet able.' (Our translation.)): the inventor of the rules may not himself have fulfilled them all — **the spirit of a set of general notes: own the account first, then legislate**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the ledger of form {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, arguing that "**Yongming prosody was a failed piece of legislation and a successful experiment**". You must draw on at least three of: **"writing prizes formal likeness" in *The Literary Mind*** (subject-matter becomes independent), **Zhong Rong's bad review** (a contemporary audit), **the mechanism "the regulated line is the fixed point of the prohibitive method"**, **the downstream of Yu Xin or of the early Tang**; and give one present-day analogy (choose any one of **a programming language's deprecation list / hitting the beat of the BGM in a short video / the orthography of school-rule style**).
::: solution
Points for marking: "failure" and "success" must be **kept in separate accounts** (failure = as a compulsory norm, the eight faults were **too fussy to be carried out, and already rotten by the Qi and Liang**; success = **setting sound up as an object that can be discussed, so that from then on Chinese poetry had an acoustics**); the mechanism sentence can be restated (the fixed point of subtraction — **legislation failed, sampling succeeded**); the analogy lines up (**hitting the beat = the entertainment version of raised tail: miss it and things are "awkward" — the feeling of awkwardness is a collation note in the public's hands**); if the conclusion can say "**form is the most honest content: it records an age's demands on the body (enunciation, breathing, silent reading)**", that is an A — and it stands with the intuition section's "the announcer is the commissioning party" as each other's statement of account.
:::

::: summary
The lesson of changing the bones closes its books: Da Xie (**landscape enters as a "survey report", born wrapped in the swaddling clothes of metaphysical discourse, "writing prizes formal likeness" — matching virtue to things retires from the post, and the senses take up the post**), Xiao Xie (**the turn into the postcard, plus the "pellet" standard of mouthfeel**), Yongming (**four tones = four gears; level and oblique = a consolidated statement; the eight faults = a sampler of prohibitive rules**), Yu Xin (**the exile experiment of a north-and-south fusion** — the goods prepared for the next chapter and the northern folk songs, and for Tang poetry). The method in one sentence: **ask of a poem "who is in charge of the sound, and to whom does the subject-matter give its account?" — these two questions are the whole answer of the Six Dynasties**. The next chapter goes over to the other bank: pastoral songs at the foot of the Great Wall, and love songs on the Qinhuai River — a divided world is still divided in the songs, but both kinds of vocal style are already prepared to be taken into Tang poetry.
:::
