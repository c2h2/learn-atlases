title: "Tao Yuanming"
---

The previous chapter ends on the words “痛感出院，下一站风景” — 'the sense of pain is discharged from hospital; the next stop is scenery' (Our translation.) — but the first spokesman for that scenery **does not write scenery at all**: he writes grass, he writes beans, he writes poverty, he writes death. Tao Yuanming 陶渊明 is the greatest **experiment in demotion** in the history of Chinese literature: he demotes the subject-matter to farm work, demotes the language to speech, demotes the stance to “欲辨已忘言” — 'I would draw the distinction, but have already forgotten the words' (Our translation.) — and then this “降” ('demotion') became a height that later ages have looked up to for one thousand six hundred years.

## One man, three ledgers {#def-sanzhang}

::: definition The ledger of taking office, and 'The Return' {#def-chushi}
**The accounts**: parents old and the family poor; he began as provincial libationer (州祭酒), and resigned; afterwards he served on the staff of Huan Xuan 桓玄 and as aide (参军) to Liu Jingxuan 刘敬宣, and ended as magistrate of Pengze 彭泽 — **in and out several times over ten-odd years** (**the preface to *Guiqu laixi ci* 归去来兮辞 ('The Return') states his motive in his own words: “质性自然，非矫厉所得；饥冻虽切，违己交病” — 'My inborn quality is to be unforced; it is not got by bending and driving. Hunger and cold press hard, yet to go against myself brings illness in turn.' (Our translation.) — **the four characters “违己交病” ('to go against myself brings illness in turn') are the general thesis of the whole man: hunger is even more bearable than going against himself**); **the affair of “不为五斗米” ('not for five pecks of rice')**: the 'Biographies of Recluses' (隐逸传) in the *Song shu* 宋书 ('Book of Song') record that he sighed “我不能为五斗米向乡里小儿折腰” — 'I cannot, for five pecks of rice, bow to a youngster of my own village.' (Our translation.) — and that very day untied his seal-ribbon — **the story is so good that everyone adds a scene: the episode in which the commandery sends a “督邮” ('commandery inspector') to the county is given in the *Jin shu* 晋书 ('Book of Jin') as “乡里小人” ('a petty man of the locality'); the discrepancies of detail are precisely the growth rings of the legend as it grows**); **'The Return'**: the preface = **a resignation manual** (“悟已往之不谏，知来者之可追。实迷途其未远，觉今是而昨非” — 'I realise that what is past cannot be remonstrated with, and I know that what is to come may still be pursued. Truly the lost road has not taken me far; I wake to find today right and yesterday wrong.' (Our translation.) — **using the words of Jie Yu 接舆, the madman of Chu, in the *Analects*, “往者不可谏” — 'what has gone by cannot be remonstrated with' (Our translation.), to grant himself an amnesty**), the main text = **a montage of going home**: “三径就荒，松菊犹存” — 'The three paths have gone to waste; pine and chrysanthemum are still there.' (Our translation.) “云无心以出岫，鸟倦飞而知还” — 'The cloud, with no intent, issues from the peak; the bird, tired of flying, knows to return.' (Our translation.) — **the two zero-degree images “云” ('cloud') and “鸟” ('bird') have from then on been held on contract by the Chinese language: on the table of lyric verbs in Chinese landscape poetry, the first page is the one he signed** (the close of [[han-to-nanbeichao/zhengshi-jin]] says “我将在风景里重新署名” — 'I shall sign my name again inside the scenery.' (Our translation.), and the signature is in these two lines).
:::

::: definition The labour ledger: the true face of field-and-garden poetry {#def-laodong}
**Field-and-garden poetry (*tianyuan shi* 田园诗) is not a landscape painting; it is a labour log**: “种豆南山下，草盛豆苗稀。晨兴理荒秽，带月荷锄归” — 'I sow beans at the foot of the southern hill; the weeds grow thick, the bean shoots are sparse. At dawn I rise and clear the wild waste; I come home, hoe on my shoulder, bringing the moon with me.' (Our translation.) — **the yield is written into the poem (sparse!); this is the first time in the history of Chinese poetry that someone admits he is no good at farming**; “道狭草木长，夕露沾我衣。衣沾不足惜，但使愿无违” — 'The lane is narrow, grasses and trees grow tall; the evening dew soaks my clothes. Clothes soaked are not worth a regret, if only the wish is not crossed.' (Our translation.) — **the conclusion of the labour is not a harvest; it is the conservation of “愿” ('the wish')**. **Poverty, too, must be entered in the ledger**: “夏日长抱饥，寒夜无被眠。造夕思鸡鸣，及晨愿乌迁” ('A poem of resentment, in the Chu mode, shown to Registrar Pang and Administrator Deng', *Yuan shi Chu diao shi Pang zhubu Deng zhizhong* 怨诗楚调示庞主簿邓治中) — 'Through the long summer I clasp my hunger; on cold nights I sleep with no quilt. Come evening, I think of the cock crow; come morning, I wish the crow would move on.' (Our translation.) — **so hungry that he longs for dawn in order to get through the night, and longs for dark in order to hide from the cold: the symmetry of the rhetoric and the asymmetry of the life**; the 'Biography of Master Five Willows' (*Wuliu xiansheng zhuan* 五柳先生传) — “闲静少言，不慕荣利。好读书，不求甚解；每有会意，便欣然忘食” + “环堵萧然，不蔽风日；短褐穿结，箪瓢屡空，晏如也” — 'Quiet and still, of few words, he does not covet glory or profit. He likes reading, and does not seek a laboured understanding; whenever he meets the sense, he is so glad he forgets to eat.' + 'Bare walls stand desolate, and keep out neither wind nor sun; his short coarse coat is worn through and knotted, basket and gourd are often empty, and he is at ease.' (Our translation.) — **the self-portrait is carried so far that “穷” ('poverty') is painted into a copyright image: 先生不知何许人也 — 'Of the gentleman, one does not know where he is from.' (Our translation.) — the first sentence about the protagonist of the biography cancels the household registration** (the biographical version of the “无面” ('faceless') technique of [[han-to-nanbeichao/nineteen-old-poems]]).
:::

::: definition The ledger of life and death, and the true meaning {#def-shengsi}
**Three sets, the 'Dirges' (*Wange ci* 挽歌辞) and the 'Sacrificial Address to Myself' (*Ziji wen* 自祭文): he holds his own funeral in advance** — “死去何所道，托体同山阿” — 'Once dead, what is there to say? Entrust the body to the same fold of the hill.' (Our translation.) (death is written as a geological event: **the remains are donated to the body of the hill; the hill owes no debt of human feeling**); the 'Sacrificial Address to Myself' closes with “人生实难，死如之何” — 'To live is truly hard; of death, what then?' (Our translation.) — **he grants the difficulty of living, and puts no question to death: this is the ultimate technique of a person without religion** (compare the *Nineteen Old Poems*, “服食求神仙，多为药所误” — 'Dosing themselves to seek the immortals, they are mostly misled by the drugs.' (Our translation.) — **Tao Yuanming throws out completely the false prescription that the Han had already pricked through, and he writes no new one**); “纵浪大化中，不喜亦不惧。应尽便须尽，无复独多虑” — 'Drift loose in the Great Change; neither rejoice nor fear. When it ought to end, then let it end; do not, on your own, go on fretting.' (Our translation.) (the three poems 'Body, Shadow and Spirit', *Xing ying shen* 形影神, **set up a debate of their own: body, shadow and spirit, three selves, hold a meeting** — **the best solution in Chinese philosophical poetry: let the self fight a civil war, then let “神” ('spirit') urge a peace — the conclusion is *wěiyùn* 委运 ('entrusting oneself to whatever comes'): go with the current, but do not concede defeat**). **The ledger of “真意” ('true meaning')**: “采菊东篱下，悠然见南山。山气日夕佳，飞鸟相与还。此中有真意，欲辨已忘言” — 'I pluck chrysanthemums beneath the eastern hedge, and at my ease catch sight of the southern hill. The mountain air is fair at the close of day; birds in flight return together. In this there is a true meaning; I would draw the distinction, but have already forgotten the words.' (Our translation.) — **the site of the experiment, from the *Zhuangzi*, in “得意而忘言” — 'once you have got the meaning, you forget the words' (Our translation.) (the debate on words and meaning went round for a hundred years, and the case was closed at last inside one little field-and-garden poem: the shelf-life of truth runs out at language)**; and, in the same collection, **'An Account of Wine' (*Shu jiu* 述酒)**, which annotated editions of the Song determine to be, written in cryptic language, **the news of Liu Yu's 刘裕禅代 ('taking the throne by a ceded abdication')** — **“忘言” ('forgetting words') is a technique, and “要说” ('having to speak') is a technique too: the same man commands, at the same time, silence and cipher**.
:::

::: example Dossier of the contradictions {#ex-maodun}
Set out four pairs of materials. A: “猛志逸四海，骛翮思远翥” — 'A fierce resolve runs beyond the four seas; racing quills long to soar afar.' (Our translation.) ('Miscellaneous Poems', *Za shi* 杂诗) / “少时壮且厉，抚剑独行游” — 'When young, sturdy and stern; hand on sword, he wandered alone.' (Our translation.) ('In Imitation of the Ancients', *Ni gu* 拟古); B: “采菊东篱下，悠然见南山” — 'I pluck chrysanthemums beneath the eastern hedge, and at my ease catch sight of the southern hill.' (Our translation.); C: 'Singing of Jing Ke' (*Yong Jing Ke* 咏荆轲), “惜哉剑术疏，奇功遂不成” — 'A pity: his swordsmanship was slack, and so the extraordinary feat was not achieved.' (Our translation.); D: the dirges and the sacrificial address to himself. The question: **who made the Tao Yuanming of *jìngmù* 静穆 ('serene stillness')? How do the four pairs of materials coexist?**
::: solution
**The technical name for their coexistence: what the preface to 'The Return' calls “质性自然” ('an inborn quality that is unforced') — his highest self-defence is not morality but material: the natural = not pruned**. Item by item: **A/B are not a change from before to after; they are two tenses** — “猛志” ('fierce resolve') is **the present continuous of memory** (the pieces that sing of “猛志” were mostly written after the return to the fields: returning to the fields did not kill the young man; it only gave him a holiday, only gave him a holiday); **C proves “题材解禁” ('the ban on subject-matter lifted')**: he writes Jing Ke 荆轲 so that every stroke of the knife draws blood — **“静穆” ('serene stillness') is the anthologies' cut: field-and-garden poems are only about three tenths of the Tao collection; the rest is singing of history, the account of the wine, reproaching his sons, dirges — later readers cut away two thirds**; **D is the arbitration court of the contradiction**: honesty about death makes every stance (fierce resolve / quiet withdrawal) legitimate at once — **a man who knows he is going to die puts on neither bustle nor quiet**. Zhu Guangqian 朱光潜 says that Tao is the utmost of “静穆” ('serene stillness'); Lu Xun 鲁迅 answers, “陶潜正因为并非‘浑身静穆’，所以他伟大” — 'Tao Qian is great precisely because he is not "serene stillness from head to foot".' (Our translation.) (*Ti weiding cao* 题未定草, 'Drafts with Titles Not Yet Fixed') — **in this debate of the 1930s both sides cited the evidence rightly: serene stillness is true, and 金刚怒目 ('the angry glare of the guardian king') is also true; the only thing wrong is the single-choice question**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The Peach Blossom Spring: reclusion from time itself {#int-taohua}
The setting of 'An Account of the Peach Blossom Spring, together with a Poem' (*Taohuayuan ji bing shi* 桃花源记并诗) is worth watching frame by frame. **The entrance** (**“晋太元中，武陵人捕鱼为业” — 'In the Taiyuan era of the Jin, a man of Wuling made fishing his living.' (Our translation.) — an opening shot in the manner of history and biography, disguising fiction as an interview**; “忘路之远近，忽逢桃花林” — 'He forgot how far the road had gone, and suddenly came on a grove of peach blossom.' (Our translation.) — **losing the way is the only ticket**); **the village** (“自云先世避秦时乱……不知有汉，无论魏晋” — 'They said themselves that their forebears had fled the troubles of the Qin time … they did not know of the Han, let alone Wei or Jin.' (Our translation.) — **what they hid from was not the mountains but the dynasties: the definition of utopia = reclusion from time itself**; “黄发垂髫，并怡然自乐” — 'The yellow-haired and the little ones with hanging hair were all, of themselves, glad and at ease.' (Our translation.); “其中往来种作，男女衣着，悉如外人” — 'Those who came and went at their planting, and the clothing of the men and women, were in every case like people outside.' (Our translation.) — **note: the first identity of the people of the Peach Spring is farmer — Tao Yuanming's paradise has no men of letters, and they do not even write poems**); **the ending, three in a row** (“此中人语云：不足为外人道也” — 'The people of this place said: it is not worth telling to those outside.' (Our translation.) — **the caution is itself a curse**; “处处志之……遂迷，不复得路” — 'He marked it at every point … then he was lost, and did not find the road again.' (Our translation.) — **the marks fail: the sure defeat of institutionalised way-finding (“志”, 'to mark') by accident (“忘路”, 'forgetting the road')**); **Liu Ziji 刘子骥** (“欣然规往，未果，寻病终。后遂无问津者” — 'Gladly he planned to go; it came to nothing; soon he fell ill and died. After that there was no one who asked the way.' (Our translation.) — **writing a real famous man onto the list of failures = using reality to stamp a seal on the fiction: even the man most famous for seeking out recluses cannot find it — from then on the story is immune to every empirical test**). **The accompanying poem closes on “嬴氏乱天纪，贤者避其世”** — 'The Ying clan disordered the order of Heaven; worthy men withdrew from their age.' (Our translation.), **writing the political index onto the front of the myth** — **the record stores it, the poem states it: prose is in charge of the mystery, verse of the political argument**.
:::

::: history One thousand six hundred years of posthumous elevation {#sec-zhuifeng}
**The death knell first sounds**: Yan Yanzhi 颜延之, 'Dirge for the Summoned Scholar Tao' (*Tao zhengshi lei* 陶征士诔) — **in the official eulogy written by a friend, Tao's poetry is almost at zero: at the time his identity was *chǔshì* 处士 ('a scholar dwelling at home'), not poet**; **Xiao Tong 萧统** compiled the *Collected Works of Tao Yuanming* (*Tao Yuanming ji* 陶渊明集) and wrote a preface — “有能观其文，恬退之节亮乎；味其怀，贞慎之诚昭乎” — 'If one can observe his writings, how the integrity of his quiet withdrawal shines; if one tastes what he held within, how the sincerity of his firmness and care is bright.' (Our translation.): **a crown prince of the Liang made a moral textbook of him, and in passing preserved the works**; **Zhong Rong 钟嵘, in *Shi pin* 诗品 ('Gradings of Poetry'), middle grade, calling him “古今隐逸诗人之宗”** ('the ancestor of reclusive poets ancient and modern') — **a famous case in the history of labels: middle grade + “隐逸” ('reclusion') = a great name, but with a ceiling**; **Tang**: **a relatively silent Tang — Wang Ji 王绩 studied the man; Meng Haoran 孟浩然 and Wang Wei 王维, along the one road of landscape and field-and-garden, carried his subject-matter off between them; the standing of the man himself is not conspicuous** (**Du Fu 杜甫, the one eye that knew the pearl: “焉得思如陶谢手” — 'How am I to get thought like the hands of Tao and Xie?' (Our translation.) — Tao and Xie named in one breath; but what the mainstream of the High Tang bought was the account of “诗到王维” ('when poetry comes to Wang Wei')**); **Song**: **a general mobilisation to reverse the verdict — Su Shi 苏轼 matched Tao all his life, and matched him poem by poem to the end (beginning from 'Twenty Poems Matching Tao's "Drinking Wine"', *He Tao yinjiu ershi shou* 和陶饮酒二十首, and, demoted to Lingnan 岭南, he still matched the complete collection to the last) — the largest act of homage in the history of Chinese literature**; **the Song were agreed that beneath Tao's *píngdàn* 平淡 ('the plain and bland') a great force lay hidden — Su Shi's eight characters, “质而实绮，癯而实腴” — 'Plain, yet in truth ornate; gaunt, yet in truth full.' (Our translation.), translate “降” ('the lowering') back into “高” ('the high')**; and from Huang Tingjian 黄庭坚 and Xin Qiji 辛弃疾 on down, Gong Zizhen 龚自珍 with “陶诗韩笔两嶙峋” — 'Tao's poems and Han's brush, the two of them craggy.' (Our translation.), each leading a squad of the “怒目队” ('angry-glare brigade'); **Yuan**: **Yuan**: stories of Tao Yuanming become a master-motif of the variety play (*zaju*) ('Entering the Peach Spring by Mistake', *Wu ru taoyuan* 误入桃源, and others) — “桃源” ('the Peach Spring') turns from a poem into a unit of space on the Chinese stage**; **the present**: **Lu Xun, in 'The Manner of the Wei and the Jin, and the Relation of Writing to Drugs and Wine' (*Wei Jin fengdu ji wenzhang yu yao ji jiu zhi guanxi* 魏晋风度及文章与药及酒之关系), pulls Tao back to the site of 稼穑 ('sowing and reaping'): “陶潜正因为并非浑身静穆，所以他伟大” — 'Tao Qian is great precisely because he is not serene stillness from head to foot.' (Our translation.) — **the retrial of the twentieth century: it opposes both the verdict of “封建士大夫的消极” ('the passivity of the feudal scholar-official') and the amnesty-and-enlistment of “静穆” ('serene stillness')**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading Tao
**Reading field and garden as a homestay** (“采菊” ('plucking chrysanthemums') is not a lifestyle blogger — **his fields were truly poor, truly hungry, truly badly farmed: the first layer that romanticising deletes is the “草盛豆苗稀” ('the weeds grow thick, the bean shoots are sparse') that he himself wrote in**); **taking “不求甚解” ('not seeking a laboured understanding') as an attitude to study** (the words that follow this sentence in the 'Biography of Master Five Willows' are “每有会意，便欣然忘食” — 'whenever he meets the sense, he is so glad he forgets to eat' (Our translation.) — **“不求甚解” is aimed at the study of chapters and sentences; what it maintains is “会意” ('meeting the sense'): what it deletes is exegesis (glossing), what it keeps is resonance**); **on a single-choice question, choosing serene stillness or the angry glare** (Lu Xun has already ruled: **“局部的特别放大” ('a special enlargement of one part') will in every case produce a false Tao Yuanming**); **taking the Peach Spring as a social programme** (**the ending of the Account is “遂迷，不复得路” — 'then he was lost, and did not find the road again' (Our translation.) — the design of the Peach Spring is precisely that it cannot be found again: it is a coordinate system for criticism, not a drawing one can build from**: treat it as a planning map, and it has already failed from the moment the fisherman “处处志之” ('marked it at every point')).
:::

::: exercise Reading aloud in three tenses {level=1}
Read together the third of 'Returning to Dwell in Gardens and Fields' (*Gui yuantian ju* 归园田居) and, from the 'Miscellaneous Poems', “猛志逸四海” — 'a fierce resolve runs beyond the four seas' (Our translation.), and fill in a **tense table**: which poem uses the present of the body, which uses the future of the wish, and which is the completion of recollection? Then copy out “衣沾不足惜，但使愿无违”, and mark what the character “愿” is rhymed with in this poem.
::: solution
Marking: the tense table is right (草盛苗稀 = **the present tense of labour under way**; 猛志 = **a future tense that was never made good**; 抚剑独行游 = **the perfect tense of looking back**); the analysis of “愿” is in place: **“愿无违” is in the same rhyme as “归” and “衣” — the wish is held in the rhyme by “归” ('return'): returning to the fields is the rhyme-word of the wish**; if the student can admit that the three poems **refute one another** and are all honest, that is a pass.

::: exercise Short essay: the invention of the classic {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters. Taking the reception history of Tao Yuanming as the case, argue that “伟大诗人是作品与追封的乘积，缺一为零” — '**a great poet is the product of the work and the posthumous elevation; miss either one, and the product is zero**' (Our translation.). You must go by way of this material: **Yan Yanzhi's dirge does not set store by his poetry → Zhong Rong's middle grade → the silence of the Tang → the great heat of the Song (Su Shi matching Tao, Zhu Xi's settled judgement) → Lu Xun's retrial**; and choose for yourself one contemporary case of “posthumous elevation” (**the market myth of Van Gogh, the publication of Kafka, Jay Chou 周杰伦 entering the textbooks** — any one) and line the mechanisms up. Answer: **of the “Tao Yuanming” we read today, how many parts are the man of 405, and how many parts the man of 1086?**
::: solution
Marking points: the chain is complete, and **each link explains the demand side** (**what the Song needed: an idol of “平淡” ('the plain and bland') able to stand against the tradition of *qǐmǐ* 绮靡 ('ornate and soft'), plus an identity-template for those who had lost out in factional strife — Su Shi's matching Tao is not a book review; it is claiming kinship**); the contemporary analogy lines up (**the work's “原始发行量” ('original print run') is small; “追封” ('posthumous elevation') decides the reprint and the price**); the question of “几分” ('how many parts') **gives a proportion and states the grounds** (model answer: the words and sentences of 'The Return' are the year 405; the man who is “平淡而近自然” ('plain, and close to the natural') is the year 1086 — **a text has an author; a classic does not; a classic is the new copy that each age saves onto the old file** — whoever can write a sentence of this form receives an A, and completes this lesson).

::: summary
Report on the demotion experiment: office (**“违己交病” ('to go against himself brings illness in turn'): hunger is bearable by comparison with going against himself; the resignation letter rewrites the *Analects***), ploughing (**the yield enters the poem + poverty enters the picture: the biographical version of the “无面” ('faceless') technique**), death (**the obituary issued in advance, “托体同山阿” — 'entrust the body to the same fold of the hill' (Our translation.) — the remains are donated to the body of the hill; the hill owes no debt of human feeling; body, shadow and spirit set the self to civil war**), the Peach Spring (**reclusion from time + the seal of Liu Ziji: that it cannot be got is the whole of its meaning**), posthumous elevation (**progenitor of the middle grade → an icon of the Song → Lu Xun's retrial: the classic = the work × the posthumous elevation**). The method, in one sentence: **in reading Tao Yuanming, first ask “这句是谁给他加的光” — 'who added this light to the line for him?' (Our translation.); read any classic on the same principle**.
