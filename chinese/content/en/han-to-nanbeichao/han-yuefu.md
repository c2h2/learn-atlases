---
title: "Han ballads"
---

The rhapsody (*fu*) is the court's loudspeaker; *yuefu* (Music Bureau poems; ballads) is **the microphone handed in from the street**. In the second century BC the Han set up a state institution of music, and its tasks included collecting folk songs — **for the first time in the history of Chinese literature, oral composition from the lanes and alleys was taken into the archives by the machinery of the state** — and so there came to be a body of poems in which those who speak are no longer scholar-officials, but forsaken wives, soldiers, the father of a sick wife, and an abused orphan.

## The institution and the titles {#sec-jigou}

::: definition *Yuefu*: a state that gathered songs, and titles carried on {#def-jigou}
**The institution.** "**乐府**" (*yuefu*) was originally the name of a government office (the Qin already had a Director of the Music Bureau, a 乐府令; **Emperor Wu (武帝) established its function of gathering songs** — the 'Treatise on Rites and Music' in the *Book of Han*: **“至武帝定郊祀之礼……乃立乐府，采诗夜诵：赵、代、秦、楚之讴”** ('When it came to Emperor Wu settling the rites of the suburban sacrifices … he thereupon established the Music Bureau, gathered songs and had them recited by night: the ballads of Zhao, Dai, Qin and Chu.' (Our translation.)) — **the state set up an archives section for "the people's voice"**); the Commandant for Harmonising the Pitch (协律都尉), **Li Yannian 李延年** (singer, and author, of the song "**北方有佳人**" ('There is a beauty in the north') — **the favoured consort's elder brother advanced himself by song: the institution's first staff were court singers**); **the master ledger in the *Han zhi* 《汉志》**: the twelve characters "**感于哀乐，缘事而发**" ('Moved by grief and joy, they arise from events.' (Our translation.)) — **the programme of Han *yuefu*: first the impact of an event, then the output of a poem** (set against the mild footnote in the *Book of Songs*, “发乎情” ('issuing from feeling') — ***yuefu* pushed the character “事” ('event') to the front of the stage**). **The system of titles.** Guo Maoqian 郭茂倩 of the Song, in 《乐府诗集》 (*Yuefu shiji*, *Collected Yuefu Poems*), divided *yuefu* into twelve categories, among them **suburban and temple, drum-and-wind, *xianghe* ('singing in response'), *qingshang* ('clear *shang*') and miscellaneous tunes** — **the "title" one reads today is the number under which the musicians filed it**: 《陌上桑》 ('Mulberries by the Path') was originally titled 《**艳歌罗敷行**》 ('The *yan* song: ballad of Luofu') (“艳” = the prelude section of a *xianghe* piece — **what remained of a tune-title became the poem's title**); **the "three modes" of *xianghe* song** (level, clear and *se*: 平、清、瑟) are **labels of pitch** — the poem is a song-text, **melody first and text afterwards**. Later ages' "**imitation *yuefu***" (拟乐府): Cao Cao 曹操, in 《短歌行》 ('Short Song'), uses a Han title to write of the events of his own time — **new wine in an old bottle, and the trademark on the bottle may not be changed**; by the Tang, "**即事名篇，无复依傍**" ('Name the piece from the event, and depend on nothing further.' (Our translation.)) (Du Fu 杜甫's 《悲陈陶》 ('Lament at Chentao') and other such pieces, and the new-title *yuefu* of Yuan and Bai 元白 — **smash the bottle and write directly** — this debt is not paid off until the course on Tang literature).
:::

::: definition The speaking voice: how Han *yuefu* writes speech {#def-shengkou}
**① Coming straight to the point** (《东门行》 ('Ballad of the East Gate'): "**盎中无斗米储，还视架上无悬衣**" ('In the jar there is not a *dou* of rice stored; I turn and look — on the rack hangs no garment.' (Our translation.)) — **the opening is a ledger in the red**; 《上山采蘼芜》 ('Up the mountain to pick *miwu*'): "**上山采蘼芜，下山逢故夫**" ('Up the mountain to pick *miwu*; down the mountain, I meet my former husband.' (Our translation.)) — **a showdown over getting back together; the first ten characters open the soap opera**). **② Driven by dialogue** (in 《东门行》 the wife clutches her husband's clothes and, weeping, remonstrates; he answers **“咄！行！吾去为迟！”** ('Hey! I am going! I am late to leave!' (Our translation.)) — **the single character “咄” keeps, for the whole poem, the feel of a live recording**; the questions and answers of the Prefect (使君) and Luofu (罗敷) in 《陌上桑》 = **the skeleton of the whole poem; the narrator hardly comes on**). **③ Apostrophe and oath** (《上邪》 ('O Heaven'): "**上邪！我欲与君相知，长命无绝衰**" ('O Heaven! I wish to be joined with you in love, long in life, never cut off, never fading.' (Our translation.)) — **an oath that brings a suit before Heaven**). **④ Indictment in small detail** (《孤儿行》 ('Ballad of the Orphan') goes through **the melon-cart overturning, hands worn to pestle-stumps, a face thick with grime** — a stream of details piled into a child-labour timesheet — not one word of abuse, only a running account submitted; the figures are the prosecution's statement). **⑤ The rhapsody's method enters the poem** (Luofu boasting of her husband in 《陌上桑》: **东方千余骑，夫婿居上头** ('To the east, a thousand riders and more; my husband is at their head.' (Our translation.)) — **a catalogue of showing off wealth, arranged by direction, by colour, by speed of promotion = the shelves of the grand rhapsody carried into a folk song**; **“青丝系马尾，黄金络马头”** ('Dark silk ties the horse's tail; yellow gold nets the horse's head.' (Our translation.)): **writing the hardness of a marriage by the brightness of property**) — **elaborate setting-forth is the blood plasma that *yuefu* and the rhapsody share: the word-hoard of Shanglin Park (上林苑) and Luofu's husband are two products of the same rhetorical industry**.
:::

::: example 《孔雀东南飞》: a case of dying for love in a society of filial sons {#ex-kongque}
The whole poem is more than seventeen hundred characters, **the first long narrative poem in Chinese literature** (it first appears in 《玉台新咏》 (*Yutai xinyong*, *New Songs of the Jade Terrace*), under the title 《古诗为焦仲卿妻作》 (*An Old Poem Written for the Wife of Jiao Zhongqing*); **the short preface before the poem declares the facts of the case: “汉末建安中，庐江府小吏焦仲卿妻刘氏，为仲卿母所遣，自誓不嫁……二人皆投水而死。时人伤之，为诗云尔。”** ('In Jian'an, at the end of the Han, Liu, the wife of Jiao Zhongqing, a minor clerk of Lujiang Prefecture, was sent away by Zhongqing's mother and swore she would not marry again … The two of them both threw themselves into the water and died. People of the time grieved for them, and so a poem was made.' (Our translation.))). Please take apart four workstations: ① the preface's "**时人伤之**" ('people of the time grieved for them') — **the tone of a newspaper editorial**, issuing a stamp of "real" for the whole poem; ② Lanzhi's (兰芝) account of herself, "**十三能织素…十六诵诗书**" ('At thirteen I could weave plain silk … at sixteen I recited the *Songs* and the *Documents*.' (Our translation.)) — **self-proof in the form of an inventory of betrothal gifts**; ③ the words of blessing with which Lanzhi takes leave of her mother-in-law, "**受母钱帛多，不堪母驱使。今日还家去，念母劳家里**" ('Much of Mother's money and silk I have received; I have not been equal to Mother's sending me about. Today I go back home; I think of Mother labouring in the house.' (Our translation.)) — **an expulsion order that complies, character by character**; ④ burial together and the change into birds, "**东西植松柏…中有双飞鸟，自名为鸳鸯**" ('East and west they plant pine and cypress … in the midst is a pair of birds in flight, naming themselves mandarin ducks.' (Our translation.)) — **a mythic patch where the narrative fails**.
::: solution
① The preface = **the anti-forgery label of reportage** (“时人伤之” hangs the poem on the hook of the news — **the same lineage as the *Records of the Grand Historian* tracing a source to a named person**: the "sense of the real" in Chinese narrative literature is endorsed by **a chain of retelling**). ② The self-account = **self-proof in the form of a CV inventory** (everything she is worth is priced by **hours of work and accomplishments** — **yet the reason for sending her away has nothing to do with anything she was trained in: what the mother wants is not a "worthy wife" but a "compliant wife"; the three characters “自专由”** ('acting of her own accord') **are the mother-in-law's only charge — Lanzhi's tragedy is not unfilial conduct; it is that she matches every textbook perfectly, while the examination question is changed at the last moment to obedience**). ③ The words of leave-taking = **a nail-house of ritual law** (every character accords with ritual, accords with it to the point of **being impossible to answer** — **the highest grade of the art of yielding: use the other party's rules to nail the other party to the pillory**). ④ The change into birds = **an "otherwise dealt with", in the sense of narratology** (the line of reality runs into a dead end all the way: repudiation → coercion → dying for love — **after both are dead a reunion is required, and folk narrative reaches across and borrows the transformation motif of the *Songs of Chu*; note that the poem never announces that the two "become married"; it only gives a pair of mandarin ducks: the institution wins, and love, even in the form of its victory, can only be an escape**). **The structure of the whole poem = a textbook of the double bind: Jiao Zhongqing 焦仲卿 loves his wife, but "filial piety" requires him to obey his mother; Lanzhi keeps faith, but "ritual" requires her to obey her elder brother — every virtue is the noose of another virtue**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Narrative's first mouthful of milk {#int-xushu}
The Chinese lyric tradition is highly developed, **yet narrative literature, all of it, drinks *yuefu*'s milk**: 《为焦仲卿妻作》 (*Written for Jiao Zhongqing's Wife*) gives the full template of long narrative — **linear time + advance by dialogue + key objects (the reed and the rock, and, in the careful dressing, “妾有绣腰襦” ('I have an embroidered waist-jacket.' (Our translation.))) + type-characters (the wicked mother-in-law, the coward, the brutal elder brother, the resolute wife)**; 《陌上桑》 broke the ground for the light comedy of "**writing conflict in dialogue, writing refusal in ornate exaggeration**" — **Luofu's victory is a victory of rhetoric: she does not fight a duel; she stages a more lavish imagining for the other party to see** (the method of Shanglin Park used for the first time in self-defence). Even the basic annals and the biographies of the *Records of the Grand Historian* are of the same age as *yuefu* — **one and the same narrative industry of "scene + dialogue + type": the histories take it to write of people, the folk songs take it to tell of events** (the three-act play of [[han-to-nanbeichao/shiji]] claims kinship here). One test is enough: **before the Han, literature said "I am sad"; with Han *yuefu*, literature began to say "that morning, he took up his sword" — from emotion to scene is Chinese literature's coming-of-age rite**.
:::

::: history The institution died; the poems lived {#sec-jiang}
**The beginning of the second century BC**: the 'Treatise on Rites and Music', “乃立乐府” ('thereupon established the Music Bureau') — **gathering songs and reciting them by night opens for business**; **6 BC** (after Emperor Ai (哀帝) came to the throne) **the officials of the Music Bureau are dismissed — an administrative act of “放郑声”** ('banishing the sounds of Zheng'); **the system of gathering songs dies for the first time**; **the Eastern Han** sets it up again, **collecting and literati imitation side by side**: Xin Yannian 辛延年's 《羽林郎》 ('The Feathered-Forest Gentleman') — **and 《焦仲卿妻》 (*Jiao Zhongqing's Wife*) was made by "people of the time" and transmitted after Jian'an — a long folk poem enters the literati's copying and passing-on in the status of the "nameless"**; **Wei and Jin**: **the lyrics of the three *qingshang* modes were rewritten by Cao Cao and his sons as political lyric** (《**短歌行**》 and 《**步出夏门行**》 ('Short Song', 'Walking Out through Xia Gate') — **a Han folk song's bottle, filled with Lord Cao's wine: imitation *yuefu* becomes literature of the first rank for the first time**); **Song, Qi, Liang and Chen**: the Southern Dynasties' **"Wu songs and Western tunes"** (the 《子夜歌》 ('Midnight Songs') system — **the institution for gathering songs is set up again; this time the "street" is the Qinhuai River at Jiankang**: [[han-to-nanbeichao/folk-songs]]) + **the 'Treatise on Music' in the *Book of Song* records the Han tunes** — **documentary study of musical institutions starts work: when the tunes have died, the sleeve notes become official history**; in the Liang, **Zhijiang 智匠's 《古今乐录》** (*Gujin yuelu*, *A Record of Music Ancient and Modern*) — **though the original book is lost, the reconstructed editions preserve, for each *yuefu*, the information on how it was performed**; **the Tang**: **Du Fu's new-title *yuefu*, “即事名篇”** ('naming the piece from the event'), Yuan Jie 元结's 《舂陵行》 ('Ballad of Chongling'), **the new-*yuefu* movement of Yuan and Bai** (“**文章合为时而著，歌诗合为事而作**” ('Prose should be written for the time; songs and poems should be made for events.' (Our translation.)) — **Bai Juyi 白居易 translates “感于哀乐缘事而发”** ('Moved by grief and joy, they arise from events.' (Our translation.)) **into a fighting programme: a definition from two hundred years before becomes a slogan of reform**); **the Song**: **Guo Maoqian's 《乐府诗集》**, one hundred juan — **the master ledger of every *yuefu* title**; **the Qing**: **Shen Deqian 沈德潜's 《古诗源》 (*Gushi yuan*, *The Source of Old Poems*) crowns the volume with Han *yuefu* — the *gediao* school ('style and tone') "takes the people of Han as its father", even though a few forged old poems are mixed into the book**; **today**: Han *yuefu* enters secondary-school textbooks and *guofeng* songs — **the closed loop of “采诗夜诵”** ('gathering songs and reciting them by night'): **street → court → textbook → the street again**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading *yuefu*
**Treating the song-words as pure poems** (“艳”, “趋” and “乱” are **terms of musical form**, not of the literary sense — to read *yuefu*, first accept that it **is song**: some "leaps" are in fact **repetition and a change of head**); **romanticising "the folk"** (collected and taken into the mode = **the folk after the court musicians have mixed it** — "original ecology" is an imagination of later ages; the pain in 《孤儿行》 is real, but **the microphone, too, was handed over by the state**); **reading 《陌上桑》 as a lesson in morals** (the reading as "a hymn to chastity and resolve" misses the poem's **comic apparatus**: Luofu deceives, the Prefect is taken in, the listeners smile in recognition — **this is a comic turn, not a chastity arch**); **reading 《孔雀东南飞》 (*Southeast Flies the Peacock*) as a struggle between mother-in-law and daughter-in-law** (a theory of villains saves too much trouble — **the poem's real opponent is the institution: everyone acts "correctly" by his or her own ritual law, and then the whole company dies: tragedy in its highest specification**).
:::

::: exercise The child-labour counting-house {level=1}
Read 《孤儿行》, set out a **timesheet** (one or two entries each for the tasks, the journey there and back, injuries on the surface of the body, and dress through the four seasons), then write 100 characters: why is "submitting a running account" more powerful than crying straight out to Heaven and beating the earth?
::: solution
Marking: the accounts **have a source in the wording, line by line** (for example "**南到江口**" ('south to the mouth of the river'), "**树下收瓜**" ('gathering melons under the trees'); **if an entry is wrong, this question collapses**); the analysis of the mechanism is in place: **figures do not draw the conclusion for the reader; they force the reader to draw it — the highest form of indictment is to make the jury speak**; anyone who can point out the structural reversal (**the call to the dead father at the end of the piece = the ledger suddenly acquiring a voice that weeps: enter the accounts first, weep afterwards, and only then is the weeping credible**) does better.
:::

::: exercise The shot analyst {level=2}
《陌上桑》 writes Luofu's beauty entirely through **onlookers losing their composure** (the traveller sets down his load, the youth takes off his cap, the ploughman forgets his plough — the passage "**行者见罗敷，下担捋髭须**" ('A traveller sees Luofu, sets down his load and strokes his moustache and beard.' (Our translation.))): draw a **diagram of camera positions** (who looks, at what, and how long the reaction lasts), and explain why **the poet does not describe her face**; finally imitate it in a passage of 80 characters, "writing a person through reactions" (the auntie at the canteen window, or a classmate asleep on the underground, will both do).
::: solution
Marking: the diagram of positions should set out **the economics of sidelong description** (the intensity of the beauty = the **sum and the steps** of the onlookers' loss of composure; not writing the face = **handing the power to set the price to the whole house, and never being caught out — imagination is always more splendid than fine-brush painting**); point out that "the Prefect's loss of composure = the starting gun of the narrative" (**the conflict is launched by "looking" — this poem is a case, two thousand years ago, of "the politics of the gaze"**; an answer may move up a level to the ornate exaggeration of [[rhetoric/choosing-words]]); the imitation **must have a staircase that descends or ascends** (laying it out flat does no good).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: how an institution manufactures martyrs {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, with 《孔雀东南飞》 as the principal case, arguing "**the poem has no bad people, only an institution in which everyone is 'correct'**": take apart the "grounds in ritual law" of the four parties — Mother Jiao (焦母; a widowed mother's property in the feelings of an only son), Elder Brother Liu (刘兄; **a householder's authority to marry off and sell a younger sister**), Zhongqing (the mutual exclusion of a filial son's duty to obey and a husband's duty), Lanzhi (**the more she has been taught, the less she has to bargain with**) — and show **why dying for love is the only executable option inside this system**; and, with one modern family event (pressure to marry / bride-price / a school-district flat — choose any), make **an analogy of mechanism that sticks on no label**.
::: solution
Marking points: the four parties' grounds in ritual law **each cite a line of the poem as evidence** (Mother Jiao: "**吾意久怀忿，汝岂得自由**" ('Long has my mind harboured anger; how should you come by freedom?' (Our translation.)) — the right to define freedom is in her hands; Elder Brother Liu: "**先嫁得府吏，后嫁得郎君，否泰如天地，足以荣汝身**" ('Marrying first, you got a commandery clerk; marrying next, you would get a young lord. Misfortune and fortune stand as far apart as heaven and earth; enough to bring honour on you.' (Our translation.)) — pressing her with profit; Lanzhi: "**黄泉下相见，勿违今日言**" ('We shall meet beneath the Yellow Springs; do not go against what is said today.' (Our translation.)) — the oath is issued by the woman: **her only power to legislate for her own life is to decide the manner of her death**); the deduction of the "only option" is complete (repudiation → return → coercion → **the institution welds shut every lawful exit, and the unlawful exits come in a pair**); the modern analogy **lines up the mechanism only, not the intensity** (pressure to marry = a mild version of property in the feelings — **distinguish the "spectrum of oppression" before you analogise, or the essay first commits a moral misdemeanour**); if the conclusion lands on "**the right feeling in reading this poem is not hatred of the mother-in-law, but investigating the institution**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The microphone lesson settles its accounts: the institution (**gathering songs and reciting them by night = an archives section for the people's voice**; Emperor Ai's abolition = the first death), the five-piece set of the voice (coming straight to the point, driven by dialogue, apostrophe, indictment by running account, the rhapsody's method entering the poem — **the word-hoard of Shanglin Park and Luofu's husband belong to one and the same rhetorical industry**), 《孔雀东南飞》 (**a textbook of the double bind + the "otherwise dealt with" of the change into birds**), the afterlife (imitation *yuefu* — new *yuefu* — the textbook — the street again). The method in one sentence: **Han *yuefu* is the starting point of "event-driven" literature — asking "what event forced this sentence out" comes closer to its mode of production than asking "is this sentence beautiful"**. In the next chapter, a literatus's hand takes the microphone: the *Nineteen Old Poems* — how the nameless become the most famous of all.
:::
