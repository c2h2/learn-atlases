---
title: "From the Zhengshi era to the Jin"
---

The men of letters of Jian'an wrote their poems beside power; over the next hundred years, **the writing of poetry itself became a high-risk occupation**. After the coup at the Gaoping Mausoleum in 249, the side your name was signed on was the house whose ghost you were — and so poetry learned three things: **to play mute (Ruan Ji 阮籍), to commit suicide by inventory (Ji Kang 嵇康), and to take the discourse of principle as a refuge (*xuányán* 玄言, 'metaphysical verse')**. This chapter passes through three layers of mist, to find the face beneath each one.

## The Zhengshi era: the invention of the mask {#sec-zhengshi}

::: definition Dark Learning (*xuánxué* 玄学), pure conversation, and the two, Ji and Ruan {#def-zhengshi}
**The backdrop.** He Yan 何晏's commentary on the *Analects*, and Wang Bi 王弼's commentaries on the *Laozi* and the *Book of Changes* — **“以无为本” ('taking non-being as the root') took the cosmology of classical scholarship apart into an ontology, and for the first time the literati discussed, as a company, "existence itself"**; **pure conversation** (*qīngtán* 清谈) = a philosophical contest with a script, a judge and an audience (**“谈” ('talk') is a performance: a feather fan, a deer's-tail whisk, the whole company won over — the salon was upgraded from the poetry gatherings at Ye to a ministry of ends**). **Ruan Ji's eighty-two poems, *Yonghuai* 咏怀 ('Singing my feelings'): an engineering science of obscurity** — comparison and evocation, myth, roaming-immortal verse and meditation on the past come on shift by shift; Liu Xie 刘勰's three-character verdict, “**阮旨遥深**” ('Ruan's purport is remote and deep'): **every poem looks as if it pointed somewhere, and every poem refuses to be pinned down** — this is **a lyric protocol invented in an age when informing cost nothing: if the author does not sign, the writing cannot be produced in court as proof** (Sima Zhao 司马昭 sought a marriage for his son, and Ruan Ji **stayed drunk for sixty days on end** — **refusing the alliance with his body, and refusing commentary with his poetry, are one and the same technique**.) **Ji Kang: writing a personality out as a legal instrument** — “**非汤武而薄周孔**” ('rejecting Tang and Wu, and slighting the Duke of Zhou and Confucius') (Our translation.) ('Letter Breaking Off Relations with Shan Juyuan' 山巨源) — **on the surface he expels the teaching of names (*míngjiào* 名教); what he actually expels is the Sima house's shop-sign, “以孝治天下” ('governing the realm by filial piety')** (**the Sima clan took the realm by the ritual and legal procedure of “禅让” ('yielding the throne', *shànràng*); they could least bear anyone pressing them for the original documents of loyalty and filial piety**); the letter lists “**七不堪二不可**” ('seven things unbearable and two things that will not be done') — **difficulty getting up, documents that weary the body, dressing in mourning to condole: refusing office by an inventory of daily life is the first time "personality" makes a complete entrance as a protagonist of Chinese literature**; put to death in the Eastern Market, **《广陵散》于今绝矣** ('*Guangling san* is, from this day, cut off') (Our translation.) — **a guillotine speech that picks one tune before death: by the time the legend had accreted, layer on layer, as far as *A New Account of Tales of the World*, the man was already more famous than the sources**.
:::

::: definition The Taikang era: the two hands of Lu Ji and Zuo Si {#def-taikang}
**The era-name of Emperor Wu of Jin, Taikang (280–289): unification was brief, and family rank endured.** *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, judging the age: “**缛旨星稠，繁文绮合……采缛于正始，力柔于建安**” ('Ornate in purport, dense as stars; elaborate in writing, joined like patterned silk … colour more profuse than the Zhengshi, force softer than Jian'an.') (Our translation.) — **the big bang of rhetoric and the ebbing of bone-force (*gǔlì* 骨力) happened together**. **Lu Ji 陆机: the founder of formalism** — a scion of a fallen state, from Wu commandery, who entered Luoyang; the *Rhapsody on Literature* is **China's first psychology of composition** (“**遵四时以叹逝，瞻万物而思纷**” ('One follows the four seasons and sighs over what passes; one looks out on the ten thousand things and thoughts teem.') (Our translation.)), and yet **the six characters “诗缘情而绮靡” ('Poetry follows from feeling, and is gorgeously delicate.') (Our translation.) are the real nuclear bomb: they expand “赋欲丽” ('the rhapsody should be beautiful') (Cao Pi 曹丕) into a formal definition of poetry — feeling plus a sense of beauty, a coup against the official line of “言志” ('expressing intent')** (the licence of [[han-to-nanbeichao/jianan]], once in his hands, was rewritten as a scope of business); ***Imitations of Ancient Poems*, twelve pieces: the *Nineteen Old Poems* used as a firing range for antithesis and prosody — from here "imitation" becomes the serious training of Chinese literature (the *ni*, the *xiao*, the *fang* and the cento: a whole set of genres of "playing in concert with the ancients" starts work with Lu Ji)**. **Zuo Si 左思: striking the other way** — eight poems, *Singing of History*: “**世胄蹑高位，英俊沉下僚。地势使之然，由来非一朝**” ('Heirs of the great houses step into the high offices; the outstanding sink among the junior clerks. The lie of the ground makes it so; it did not begin with a single reign.') (Our translation.) — **the form of singing history (*yǒngshǐ* 咏史) is settled from this point: history is not a store of lessons; it is social-media commentary on the present**; the *Rhapsody on the Three Capitals*, ten years in the conceiving, **paper dear in Luoyang** — **the measure of a hit in the age of copying by hand: the price of paper is the print run**; Zhong Rong 钟嵘's top grade praises him, “**文典以怨，颇为精切，得讽谕之致**” ('The writing is classical and yet resentful, quite exact, and it catches the manner of indirect remonstrance.') (Our translation.) — **“左思风力” ('Zuo Si's wind-force') later became a synonym for “风力” ('wind-force', *fēnglì*): in an age of form, backbone (*gǔqì* 骨气) made its name by a posture of opposition**.
:::

::: definition The Eastern Jin: metaphysical verse and its cracks {#def-xuanyan}
**The mainstream.** *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Times in Sequence': “**自中朝贵玄，江左称盛；因谈馀风，遂成俗好；是以诗必柱下之旨归，赋乃漆园之义疏**” ('Once the central court prized the mysterious, east of the River it was called the full flourishing; carried on the after-wind of talk, it became what custom loved; and so a poem had to come home to the purport of the Pillar Clerk (Laozi), and a rhapsody was a commentary on the meaning of the Lacquer Garden (Zhuangzi).') (Our translation.) — **poetry became footnotes to the *Daodejing*: the only time in the history of Chinese poetry when "philosophy beat poetry"**. But it has to be read along three cracks. ① **Guo Pu's 郭璞 poems of roaming with immortals** — the “游仙” ('roaming immortal') form, in his hands, “**坎壈咏怀，非列仙之趣**” ('singing of hardship and of one's feelings; not the delight of the ranked immortals') (Our translation.) (the gist of *Grades of Poetry*): **the immortals' country is the mental map of literati in exile; the reverse of a taste for the ranked immortals is inflation on solid ground**; ② **the Orchid Pavilion (353)** — Wang Xizhi's 王羲之 'Orchid Pavilion Preface', “**一死生为虚诞，齐彭殇为妄作**” ('To treat death and life as one is hollow nonsense; to set Pengzu level with a child who dies young is a wild fabrication.') (Our translation.) — **a falling-out, inside Dark Learning, with Dark Learning: the medicine of “齐物” ('equalising things', *qíwù*) is knocked to the ground, and the sense of pain comes back on**; the poems gathered at the elegant assembly (pieces by Sun Chuo 孙绰, Xie Hun 谢混 and others) are still speaking principle, **but the reasons are said for the landscape to hear: inside the language of principle, for the first time, a speaking part is left for the scenery** — **metaphysical verse is not the enemy of landscape; it is the obstetrics of landscape**; ③ **Zhi Dun 支遁** — reading 'Free and Easy Wandering' by Buddhist principle, **“新理层出，群贤莫之折”** ('New principles came forth layer on layer, and none of the worthies could refute them.') (Our translation.) (*A New Account of Tales of the World*, 'Literature') — **Prajñā studies (*bōrě xué* 般若学) pushed onto the floor of pure conversation: the next baton of Dark Learning was handed to Buddhist learning, and poetry's next subject (dwelling in the mountains, climbing to a view, woods and springs inside the sound of sutra-lectures) changed its patron too**.
:::

::: example A set of false name-cards: the five-character exchanges of Li Ling and Su Wu {#ex-lingwu}
*Selections of Refined Literature*, under 'miscellaneous poems', includes **several pieces exchanged between Li Ling 李陵 and Su Wu 苏武** — “**携手上河梁，游子暮何之**” ('Hand in hand, up onto the river bridge; the wanderer at dusk, where is he bound?') (Our translation.), **narrating a parting of the living in 99 BC, and yet in fully mature five-character verse**; **that they are spurious is the received view of modern scholarship**. The question: **what does a forgery rest on, that it should stand? The credit of which age is it passing itself off as?**
::: solution
**Three lines for judging a forgery (a general review of this lesson's method):** **① dating by language** (how mature the five-character line is, and the late features of the vocabulary — **the age of the stock of metaphors and set phrases cannot be hidden**; the literary version of cross-line verification in the manner of Yan Ruoqu 阎若璩, the four-line encirclement of [[classical-chinese-2/ancient-culture]]); **② no foothold in the record** (the *Book of Han* records Ling's story and no poems; the doings of the exchange are not in the histories — **an answering exchange where history has no such event = a plot in the manner of fiction**); **③ the genre dated to the wrong age** (the 'miscellaneous poems' format of presentation and reply belongs to the end of the Han and after the Wei — **only after Jian'an is there the literary habit of "sending a poem to a friend"**; composing together on one shared topic, in the previous chapter, can serve as the counter-proof). **What it passes itself off as is the credit of "the Han" — and credit was precisely what the world of letters then most lacked**: five-character verse needed a glorious ancestor (**the "old poems" have no names, just right for issuing identity cards to the nameless**). **The two ledgers of literary history:** **the value of the forgeries is not reduced — they are the finished goods of "lyric behind a mask": later people put on Li Ling's face to weep their own partings. Imitation (*nǐ* 拟, Lu Ji), borrowed attribution (*tuō* 托, the old *yuefu*), and the proxy voice (*dài* 代, the form of speaking for another) are of one family: the subject does not dare to sign, and so invents a role**. The shape of the concluding sentence: **these are not Li Ling's poems; they are the one face shared by countless people parted, between Han and Wei — to carry a dating as far as an author is news; to carry a dating as far as a kind of person, that is literary history**.
:::

::: intuition A hundred years, four ways of handling the "I" {#int-siZhong}
Run the camera fast: **Jian'an (196–220): the "I" comes on** — “老骥伏枥” ('an old steed stoops in the stable') (Our translation.) declares its great ambition in public; **Zhengshi (240–254): the "I" wears a mask** — Ruan Ji's "I" changes costume through eighty-two dreams, and does not sign one of them; **Taikang (280–289): the "I" changes into work clothes** — Lu Ji covers the wound of a fallen state with precise rhetoric, and hands personality over to formal pattern; **Eastern Jin (317–405): the "I" goes into hospital** — metaphysical verse puts emotion in the keeping of "principle", and poetry becomes a sanatorium. **The four responses are the forms of one and the same personality under different pressures**: as the pressure rises, lyric retreats from the public square into a closed room, from the signature into singing one's feelings, from language into silence — **but mark the last leap: on the day at the Orchid Pavilion, treating death and life as one was declared hollow nonsense, the sense of pain was discharged, and the next stop is landscape ([[han-to-nanbeichao/landscape-yongming]]): the "I" will sign its name again inside the scenery**.
:::

::: history From pure conversation to expounding the scriptures {#sec-qingtan}
**249**, the coup at the Gaoping Mausoleum — the famous scholars cut by half, and pure conversation moves house (**from talk of political argument towards talk of ontology: a safe topic is the thermometer of the age**); **262**, Ji Kang put to death in the Eastern Market, and ***A New Account of Tales of the World* accretes, layer on layer, into a myth of the "metallic personality"** (**the quick wit of the visit to Zhong Hui 钟会, “何所闻而来，何所见而去” ('What did you hear, that you came? What did you see, that you go?') (Our translation.), writes the death penalty out in advance as performance art**); **around 289**, Lu Ji enters Luoyang — **drawn into the turmoil of the Eight Princes, and on the point of execution he sighs “华亭鹤唳，岂可复闻乎” ('The cranes crying at Huating — shall I ever hear them again?') (Our translation.) — the illusion of southern gentlemen going north is settled, the bill paid, by a death sentence**; **around 300**, Zuo Si's *Rhapsody on the Three Capitals*, **paper dear in Luoyang** — **a miracle of circulation in the age of copying by hand, the rhapsody's last time at the top of the stream**; **311**, the Yongjia disorders — **Liu Kun 刘琨 holding Bingzhou alone; of his poems two survive, and both are blood: “何意百炼刚，化为绕指柔” ('How could I have thought that hardness tempered a hundred times would turn to a softness wound round the finger?') (Our translation.) ('Presented Again to Lu Chen' 卢谌) — the last torch of *fēnggǔ* 风骨 ('wind and bone'; vigour of feeling and structure) inside a besieged city in the north**; **353**, the Orchid Pavilion — **the purification rite + poems + preface + calligraphy: one elegant gathering becomes, for a thousand years after, the template of "literati activity"** (the five changes in the make-up of the book, in [[classical-chinese-2/books-editions]], here acquire a home ground); **the 360s**, Zhi Dun reads the *Zhuangzi* by way of the Buddha — **for the first time a *śramaṇa* (*shāmén* 沙门) sits down in the judge's seat of pure conversation**; **405**, Tao Yuanming 陶渊明 resigns Pengze — **the next opponent of metaphysical verse is not landscape, but "the genuine" (the next chapter)**; **from the Liang and Chen on:** ***The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* is completed, and closes the coffin on the whole of Wei and Jin — the ten characters cited above, “采缛于正始，力柔于建安” ('colour more profuse than the Zhengshi, force softer than Jian'an') (Our translation.) — the history of criticism writes a hundred years into ten characters, and from then on anyone who wants to quarrel can quarrel only inside the frame of those ten characters**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading the Wei and Jin
**Reading metaphysical poems, without exception, as "a step backwards"** (**it is philosophy beating poetry, and it is also the necessary labour pains of poetry making way for landscape** — before you mark it, first ask what function it serves); **taking “归趣难求” ('the drift it comes home to is hard to find') for a failure of understanding** (**more than one meaning is a function, not a malfunction** — Ruan Ji's safety depends exactly on never being pinned down; **every time the school of pinning-down wins the feat of one poem, it loses an age**); **using the spurious as history** (the Li Ling poems cannot be used to prove that the Western Han had five-character verse — **but they prove perfectly that people of the fifth century needed Li Ling**); **reading the gorgeously delicate (*qǐmǐ* 绮靡) as decadence** (**a sense of form is an extension of the storehouse of rhetoric**: without the Taikang "gorgeously delicate", Yongming prosody would have had no material — see the account in [[han-to-nanbeichao/landscape-yongming]]).
:::

::: exercise Deciphering the *Yonghuai* {level=1}
Choose the first of the *Yonghuai* (“夜中不能寐”, 'In the night I cannot sleep.') (Our translation.) and any one poem that carries myth (of your own choosing), and make a **two-column commentary**: the left column, "can be pinned down" (the words, and where the allusions come from); the right column, "cannot be pinned down" (who is meant? what event?). Then write 80 characters: **is the blank in the right column a defect, or is it a structure?**
::: solution
Marking: the left column **traces allusions to their sources** (“**薄帷鉴明月，清风吹我襟**” ('The thin curtain mirrors the bright moon; a clear wind blows my lapel.') (Our translation.) is plain description, and needs no allusion; where the *Zhuangzi* or the *Sao* is drawn on, give the title of the piece); the right column leaves its blank honestly; a conclusion that reaches this level counts as excellent: **the right column is by design — the *yonghuai* form = a lawful format left for what cannot be said (the two characters “咏怀” are "report only the feeling, not the facts of the case" — the title itself is a disclaimer)**.
:::

::: exercise A brief to reverse the case against metaphysical verse {level=2}
The materials of the argument may only be quotations that can be checked — *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*: “**诗必柱下之旨归，赋乃漆园之义疏**” ('A poem had to come home to the purport of the Pillar Clerk (Laozi), and a rhapsody was a commentary on the meaning of the Lacquer Garden (Zhuangzi).') (Our translation.). Write a reversal of 250 characters: **defend metaphysical verse as "the obstetrics of landscape poetry" and as "a test ground for decompressing language"**, and use no more than one term from this lesson and one contemporary analogy (**conceptual music / minimalism / the age of the podcast** — choose one).
::: solution
Marking: **an argument from the mechanism** (the language of principle has scenery **step down** from its post as "a tool of analogy with virtue" — **a mountain no longer has to symbolise humaneness, and water no longer has to symbolise rightness: once the task of symbolising is lifted, scenery has a chance to be only scenery** — this step is the precondition of landscape standing on its own); the term is called on accurately (“采缛于正始，力柔于建安” ('colour more profuse than the Zhengshi, force softer than Jian'an') (Our translation.) is a verdict, and it is also a periodisation); the contemporary analogy lines up by function (minimalist music **yields** melody's place to timbre — metaphysical verse **yields** the image's place to the concept, and **the yielding itself is the agenda of the next revolution**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: forgeries, masks and anonymity {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, threading together the three cases of this lesson: **the spuriousness of the Li Ling exchanges**, **the concealment of Ruan Ji's *Yonghuai***, **the imitation in Lu Ji's imitations of the ancients**. Argue: "**When the cost of signing a name is too high, or when credit is not enough, literature develops a set of identity techniques.**" You must give each of the three cases its own name for the identity technique (**borrowed name / obscurity / imitation**), one mechanism in common, and one contemporary counterpart (**posting from an alt account / AI generation / anonymous submission** — choose one); and answer: **does literary history need forgeries?**
::: solution
Points for marking: each of the three techniques has a definition in one sentence (**borrowed name = borrowing an ancient's credit; obscurity = deleting one's own signature; imitation = installing the ancients as guardians**); the mechanism in common: **identity is genre — the "author" in literary history is sometimes a configuration of form**; the contemporary analogy does not go hunting after the curious (**an alt account = the "borrowed name" of the present: anonymity makes honesty possible — just as “阮旨遥深” ('Ruan's purport is remote and deep') makes it possible to write one's feelings out**); the answer to "does it need them?": if one can answer "**need its being spurious, not its being a fraud**" (the forged work is the testimony of an age; the act of forging is a stain on publication — **keep separate accounts**), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Closing the books on the underground period: the Zhengshi (**the mask: the anonymity protocol of singing one's feelings, plus the inventory-personality of the letter that breaks off relations — “非汤武薄周孔” ('rejecting Tang and Wu and slighting the Duke of Zhou and Confucius') strikes at the shop-sign, not at the classics**), the Taikang (**work clothes: the reform of the scope of business in “缘情绮靡” ('following from feeling, gorgeously delicate'), plus imitation as training; Zuo Si makes his name by a posture of opposition**), the Eastern Jin (**the sanatorium: philosophy beats poetry, but three cracks — roaming with immortals, the Orchid Pavilion, Zhi Dun — are just handing the key to landscape**); the Li Ling case (**to carry a dating as far as an author is news; to carry a dating as far as a kind of person is literary history**). One sentence of method: **first ask whether this poem dares to sign its name, then ask whether it wants to be famous — the whole secret of the Wei and Jin is in the manner of the signature**. Next chapter: a man who really did hang up the seal — Tao Yuanming, the quietest revolution in the history of Chinese literature.
:::
