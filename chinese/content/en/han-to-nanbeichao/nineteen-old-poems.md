---
title: "The *Nineteen Old Poems*"
---

The most honoured set of poems in the history of Chinese poetry, **with the author column entirely blank**. They were not originally "a set" — not until the *Wenxuan* 文选 (*Selections of Refined Literature*) fixed nineteen anonymous five-character poems on the same page of its table of contents was “古诗十九首” born as a name, the *Nineteen Old Poems*. This chapter treats two things: **how they were selected, and why anonymity became, instead, the highest grade of signature**.

## The birth of a set of poems {#def-chusheng}

::: definition Where the "nineteen" of the Nineteen comes from {#def-shijiu}
**Raw material**: a batch of literati five-character poems circulating in Luoyang in the late Eastern Han (**about the second century of the common era**). “古诗” was in the first place **a relative label of period** (for people of the Southern Dynasties, a Han poem simply was an "old" poem). The *Wenxuan* gathered anonymous old pieces of mixed origin and titled them “古诗” ('old poems'). **Bound into a set**: under the category “杂诗” ('miscellaneous poems') in the *Wenxuan* of Xiao Tong 萧统, nineteen poems were selected, and he **invented for himself the collective title “古诗十九首” — from then on the nineteen share a single life**. The order was arranged by the anthologist (**the opening piece, “行行重行行” ('On and on, again on and on.' (Our translation.)), starts on the momentum of reduplicated words, and the last several pieces, “驱车上东门” ('I drive my carriage to the Upper East Gate.' (Our translation.)), “驱车上东门” ('I drive my carriage to the Upper East Gate.'), and “出郭门直视” ('I go out by the outer gate and look straight ahead.' (Our translation.)), weep one after another over the burial ground —** an arc is edited that walks from parting to death; it is curation, not a genealogy). **The bankruptcy of the Mei Cheng theory**: a commentary on the *Wenxuan* cites an old account, “或云枚乘” ('Some say Mei Cheng.' (Our translation.)) — Mei Cheng 枚乘 was a man of the Wen–Jing period, and yet the poems contain “**驱车上东门**” (a city gate of Luoyang) and “**游戏宛与洛**” ('At play in Wan and Luo.' (Our translation.)): **the place names give the secret away, and Liu Xie 刘勰 bluntly condemns the theory as “疏” ('careless')**. **A lesson in the fixing of names**: **we think we are reading "a collection of Han poems"; in fact we are reading one page of the *Wenxuan*'s table of contents — the "set" in a set of poems is the fingerprint of an anthology** ([[classical-chinese-2/commentaries]] the three checks have their first practical here).
:::

::: definition Twin themes and three prescriptions {#def-yaofang}
**Twin themes: space and time**. **Parting** — the lyric protagonists of the Nineteen are **two kinds of people in motion**: the gentleman who travels out to seek office (“**还顾望旧乡，长路漫浩浩**” — 'I turn my head and look back towards my old home; the long road stretches vast and vast.' (Our translation.)) and the wife who waits for him (“**荡子行不归，空床难独守**” — 'The wanderer goes and does not return; an empty bed is hard to keep alone.' (Our translation.)): **the empire's transport network, and the movement of men through the selection of officials, leave in the poems a pair of shadows set at an equal distance — on the great roads of Luoyang, half the going is people, half the going is waiting**. **Time** — “**生年不满百，常怀千岁忧**” ('The years of a life do not fill a hundred, yet one always harbours cares of a thousand years.' (Our translation.)), “**人生非金石，岂能长寿考**” ('A human life is not metal or stone; how can it last into a great old age?' (Our translation.)); the scenery is fixed: “**驱车上东门，遥望郭北墓**” ('I drive my carriage to the Upper East Gate and gaze afar at the tombs north of the outer wall.' (Our translation.)) — **the white poplars of Luoyang's burial ground are the fixed set-dressing of the Nineteen**.
Faced with “年命如朝露” ('one's allotted years are like the dew at morning.' (Our translation.)), the Nineteen prescribe three medicines. **① Establishing one's affairs** — “**何不策高足，先据要路津**” ('Why not whip on a swift horse, and be first to seize the ford on the road that matters?' (Our translation.)): **the sense of competition is written in plain words onto the seizure of the road to office; it is a growth ring of the Eastern Han's society on the move**. **② Seeking pleasure** — “**荡涤放情志，何为自结束**” ('Wash it away, and let feeling and will run free; why bind oneself?' (Our translation.)), and the immortal Wangzi Qiao 王子乔 is no match for **fine wine in a golden goblet** (the lines above “服食求神仙” are precisely a self-defence of “**极宴娱心意**” — 'Feast to the utmost, and delight the heart.' (Our translation.) — **pleasure is written as the speech for the defence against death**). **③ Seeking immortality** — and then **puncturing it with their own hand**: “**服食求神仙，多为药所误**” ('Ingesting elixirs in search of gods and immortals, one is mostly misled by the drugs.' (Our translation.)) — **even the placebo fails, and so the first two medicines show their bitter taste as well**: establishing one's affairs may not be won, pleasure is of no help, and the immortals are a fraud — **the three medicines pull the stage from under one another, and the dregs that remain are the “意” ('intent') of the Nineteen: the lucidity of going on living inside what does not work**. This undermining of itself is where they are more honest than philosophy.
:::

::: definition Crowned among the old: how anonymity does the crowning {#def-jiamian}
**The "old" of the tone**: the Nineteen are **the coming-of-age rite of the five-character line** (the folk songs of [[han-to-nanbeichao/han-yuefu]] gave the skeleton, and **the Nineteen tuned it into a literati vocal style**: no allusion, no calling-out, no laying-out at length — the settled verdict of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* 《文心雕龙》 is “**婉转附物，怊怅切情，实五言之冠冕也**” — 'They wind about and cling to things; forlorn, they cut to the feeling. Truly they are the crown of five-character verse.' (Our translation.) — **“冠冕” ('crown') = from then on five-character verse had an official size**). **The aesthetic dividend of "no face"**: the *Shipin* 诗品 (*Grades of Poetry*) of Zhong Rong 钟嵘 places them in the **top grade** and prices them at “**一字千金**” ('a thousand pieces of gold for a single character.' (Our translation.)) — **because no author can be ascertained, the reader cannot use a "life" to dilute the lines**: the “君” of “**思君令人老**” ('Thinking of you makes one old.' (Our translation.)) can be a husband, a lord, an ideal, the emperor — **a vacuum of background, an overload of meaning: this is the privilege of the faceless face**. **The triple jump of the coronation**: taken into the *Wenxuan* (**into the textbook**) → ranked by *Wenxin* and the *Shipin* (**into criticism**) → **after the Tang, “古诗” ('old poems') became a coordinate of genre that could be imitated and compared — nameless poems gave birth to a crowd of famous children**.
:::

::: example 'Far, far the Oxherd Star': the rhetoric of one river {#ex-qianniu}
“迢迢牵牛星，皎皎河汉女。纤纤擢素手，札札弄机杼。终日不成章，泣涕零如雨。河汉清且浅，相去复几许？盈盈一水间，脉脉不得语。” Far, far, the Oxherd Star; bright, bright, the woman of the River of Stars. Slender, slender, she lifts her white hands; clack, clack, she plies loom and shuttle. All day she completes no patterned cloth; weeping, her tears fall like rain. The River of Stars is clear and shallow; how far apart, after all, are they? Between them, one brimming stretch of water; they gaze, full of feeling, and cannot speak. (Our translation.) Take it apart at four workstations: ① **reduplicated words as a camera push**; ② **the *Book of Songs* thread in “终日不成章”** (“**虽则七襄，不成报章**” — 'Though she moves through seven stages, she completes no patterned cloth in return.' (Our translation.) — the poem ‘大东’, 'The Great East'); ③ **the paradox of “清且浅”** ('clear, and shallow'); ④ **the close on “不得语”** ('cannot speak').
::: solution
① **Camera-work through six groups of reduplicated words**: “迢迢” and “皎皎” are **a long shot of the starry sky**; “纤纤” and “札札” cut to **a close-up of the loom and its soundtrack**; “盈盈” returns to **a medium shot of the river**; “脉脉” **stops on a face that does not speak** — **the earliest complete push-in script in Chinese, with distance measured out in reduplicated words**. ② “不成章” connects with ‘大东’ in the Minor Odes, 《小雅·大东》 — **in that Western Zhou poem of the *Book of Songs*, the Oxherd and the Weaving Girl were already “七襄” ('seven stages') and yet “不成报章” ('completing no patterned cloth in return'): the Nineteen rewrite the grievance of the stars of the Classic as the grievance of a household** (**the Oxherd and the Weaving Girl change from "constellations put under levies and labour service" into "a husband and wife separated by a river": the turning of myth into the human realm happens in the Eastern Han**; the Milky Way = the prototype of institutional separation: **“盈盈一水” ('one brimming stretch of water') is from then on the shortest "ends of the earth" in the Chinese language**). ③ **“清且浅” is sophistry on a cosmic scale** — **the shallower the river, the less reason for the barrier: a physical obstacle was never the point; an authorisation that forbids the crossing is. The rhetorical question “相去复几许” ('And how far apart are they, after all?') = an accusation dressed up as arithmetic**. ④ **“不得语”**: the only action in the whole poem is that there is no action — **weeping like rain, and yet unable to speak; tears are the bankruptcy report of language** (set beside 《上邪》, which calls on Heaven and swears an oath: **the folk voice of the Han *yuefu* (Music Bureau poems) is “上邪！” ('Heaven above!' (Our translation.)); the literati voice of the Nineteen is “脉脉不得语” ('gazing, full of feeling, and unable to speak') — the same sense of wrong, one pronouncing sentence, one swallowing the sound: a difference of voice is a difference of class**).
:::

::: intuition Why the five-character line is the "default vocal style" {#int-wuyan}
An experiment this lesson can run: **compress the Nineteen into four-character lines** (“行行重行行” → “行行日远” ('on and on, farther by the day')?) — **the meaning is kept, the sigh is gone: the extra character the five-character line has is exactly the volume of a sigh**.
:::

::: history The victory of the nameless {#sec-shengli}
**Late Eastern Han to Wei and Jin**: old poems circulated in the passing-on of copies and at banquet gatherings; the "Mei Cheng" theory cited in the commentary on the *Wenxuan* keeps the dispute on the record. **Six Dynasties**: the *Wenxuan* binds them into a set — **the set is born**; *Wenxin*'s “**五言冠冕**” ('the crown of five-character verse') and the top grade in the *Shipin* — **the world of criticism gives its ruling on the faceless one: it is the ancestor of all five-character verse**. **Tang**: **the "Han" in the “汉魏风骨” of Chen Zi'ang 陈子昂, the *fenggu* of Han and Wei ('wind and bone'; vigour of feeling and structure), has as its main force exactly this batch of old poems and *yuefu* — the "old" in the slogan of returning to the ancient is goods supplied by the nameless**. **Song**: **Yan Yu 严羽's *Canglang shihua* 沧浪诗话 (*Canglang's Remarks on Poetry*) ranks “古诗” first among the genres — the anthology becomes the household register of poetics**. **Ming**: on the ledgers of the archaist school, the Nineteen are the creditor at the head of the list for “**五言古诗之祖**” ('the ancestor of five-character ancient-style verse'). **Qing**: **the *Gushi yuan* 古诗源 (*The Source of Old Poems*) of Shen Deqian 沈德潜 opens the volume with the Nineteen — the generation-character "ancestor" is capped**. **Modern times**: **Zhu Ziqing 朱自清's *Shi yan zhi bian* 诗言志辨 (*An Examination of "Poetry Expresses Intent"*) and Ma Maoyuan 马茂元's *Gushi shijiu shou tansuo* 古诗十九首探索 (*An Exploration of the Nineteen Old Poems*) — a scholarly homecoming: "no face" is restored to the job-seeking young men of Eastern Han Luoyang and to wives in empty rooms (an author is conjectured for the first time: not an individual, but a kind of person — the disappointed gentleman)** — **the victory of the nameless is completed here: they do not need an author; what they need is history**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading old poems
**Treating the "set" as a boy band** (the order and the arc are both the anthology's arrangement — **to read from "parting" through to "death" is the *Wenxuan*'s curation**); **nailing down who “君” is** (the “君” of “**思君令人老**” has at least four readings, and the poem does not declare itself — **the "suspension of *bi* and *xing*" (*bi* and *xing*: comparison and evocation), between expressing feeling and writing on an object, is precisely its ecological niche**; forcing an index thins the poem into a slip asking for leave); **supposing that "nameless = plain"** (**plainness is an effect, not a cost**: what Liu Xie praises is exactly the precision of “婉转附物” — **not working at carving is craftsmanship reversed at the highest grade**); **using a High Tang standard to complain that it has no miracle** (the force of the Nineteen lies in **there being no event**: no war, no strange encounter, only the seasons and the waiting — **they prove that a lyric poem can live without relying on a story**).
:::

::: exercise The binder {level=1}
A rearrangement experiment: choose for yourself **six poems** from the Nineteen, arrange them into a set in an order you think reasonable, and write a "curatorial preface" of 150 characters (imitating the logic of the *Wenxuan*: either along a curve of mood, or by day and night, or by a man's and a woman's points of view). Then set it beside the original order, and state **one place where the original order is more accomplished than yours (or falls short of yours)**.
::: solution
Marking: **the choice of the six and their ordering must be run through by one and the same logic** (for example, spring → autumn → the grave → awakening: one road of "seeing time"); in the preface, **at least two lines of verse are quoted as labels on the exhibits** (to cite a text is to use the method); the comparison must be honest — **the arc of the original order is "from walking to lying down" (opening on the momentum of reduplicated words, closing on weeping at the graves in succession): if you can point out this layer, you pass**.
:::

::: exercise A letter home from the wanderer's wife {level=2}
Choose any one poem and rewrite it as a letter home of 300 characters, **in the first person of the one who waits**: **the only nouns you may use as images are nouns that appear in that poem's text**, though you may use modern Chinese and a present-day setting. Attach a self-assessment of 50 characters: which image was hardest to carry across, and why?
::: solution
Marking: **no outside help for the images** (“白露”, 'white dew', can be carried onto the windowsill of a rented room; “**胡马依北风，越鸟巢南枝**” — 'The Hu horse keeps by the north wind; the Yue bird nests on a southern branch.' (Our translation.) — can be carried across as **two metro cards, for different cities, in a mobile phone** — **what is transplanted is the mechanism (an instinctive orientation), not the object**); the emotion stays in the "swallowed voice" gear (**no wailing to Heaven and beating the ground — the Nineteen's sense of measure is the hidden point this question tests**); the self-assessment names the difficulty = “**不得语**” is the hardest: the genre of the letter home is by nature one that wants speech, while the force of the original poem is precisely that it cannot be said — **the tug of war between genre and the original text is the whole of this question**.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: namelessness as the extreme of poetry {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, arguing that "**a blank in the author column is the greatest literary institution of the Nineteen**". You must call on at least three historical facts: **the bankruptcy of the Mei Cheng theory** (internal evidence from place names), **Zhong Rong's “一字千金”** ('a thousand pieces of gold for a single character'), and **the need, in a "return to the ancient" of Chen Zi'ang's kind, for an "old" that is nameless**. Compare one contemporary textual phenomenon (choose any one of **an unsigned pop song in a white-noise music library / the "is there a source?" of talk passed around online / the restoration of a silent film with no subtitles**, and align the mechanisms). Finally answer: if archaeology dug up the exact authors of the Nineteen, **would they be downgraded**?
::: solution
Points for marking: the historical facts are used accurately (**internal evidence from place names killing the Mei Cheng theory = the reappearance, in literary history, of cross-line verification of the 'Yaodian' 尧典 ('Canon of Yao') kind — a pass both in the common-sense lesson and in the commentary lesson**; “一字千金” is **the price a reader sets on facelessness**; a return to the ancient needs the nameless — **where there is a name there is that person, and that person is that person's limit, so the nameless "old" is a line of credit with no limit on it**); the comparison aligns the mechanisms (restoring a silent film with no subtitles ≈ lines of verse with no background — **the disappearance of context is at once a loss and a liberation**); the answer on "downgrading" must give **two kinds of value** (documentary value goes up: author, date and occasion all delivered; **"faceless value" goes down: the privilege of suspension is void** — **to be able to tell the two values apart is an A**; if you ask back “我们真的愿意知道作者吗” ('Do we really want to know the author?' (Our translation.)) and give your reasons, mark it excellent).
:::

::: summary
Closing the account of the birth certificate and the coronation: the binding (**the "set" is the fingerprint of the *Wenxuan***; the Mei Cheng theory is pierced through by place names), the twin themes (**parting × time**: the wanderer and the longing wife = the obverse and reverse shadows of the empire's transport network; **three prescriptions, and the undermining of itself**), the vocal style (**the cruising speed of the five-character line: the extra character is the volume of a sigh**; “白露是闹钟” ('white dew is an alarm clock.' (Our translation.)), “衣带日已缓” ('Day by day my belt grows loose.' (Our translation.)): the body reports the longing of itself), the coronation (**the faceless face: a vacuum of background, an overload of meaning**; those who imitate the "old" fill the boxes — **nameless poems gave birth to famous children**). One sentence of method: **when you read the two characters “古诗”, first ask who called it old — "old" is a retrospective recognition, not a property**. The next chapter enters Ye 邺: how the Jian'an 建安 literati, in the intervals of the wars, for the first time spoke out loud of “文章不朽” ('Literary writing is imperishable.' (Our translation.)).
:::
