---
title: "Southern and Northern folk songs"
---

In the same hundred years, each bank of the Yangtze had a vocal style of its own: **the south is love among bamboo and silk; the north is life and death in the wind and sand**. Stranger still, the "love-words" of both sides never say the thing straight — the Southern Dynasties use **homophones** ("莲" is "怜", "丝" is "思"), the Northern Dynasties use **distance** (to go once is to be gone to Yanmen or the Congling). This chapter issues you two dictionaries, and a mirror besides: **these "voices of the folk" — through whose collecting and rewriting did they pass**.

## The Southern Dynasties: a love-cipher of homophones {#def-nan}

::: definition Wu songs and Western songs {#def-wuge}
**Two great regions of production**: **Wu songs** (吴歌) — the music of household use in the Wu-speaking region centred on Jiankang 建康 (the 'Treatise on Music' of the *Book of Song* records, outside the various categories of *xianghe* 相和, the old heading "Wu songs" — **popular music enters the music treatise of an official history as an established institution**); **Western songs** (西曲) — songs of the river-merchants' post roads through Jing, Ying, Fan and Deng (Xiangyang and Jiangling) (**"west" is a direction as reckoned by people in Jiankang: upstream along the river, merchants prize profit and take parting lightly — so Western songs are mostly merchant-wives seeing people off and welcoming them, while Wu songs are mostly spring longing in the inner rooms**). **The "Ziye" trademark**: the 'Treatise on Music' of the *Old Book of Tang* adopts the account of "a woman of the Jin named Ziye" — **all that is certain today is that it is the foremost great tune of the Wu songs, and the *Ziye Songs of the Four Seasons* (《子夜四时歌》) are its "suite of the four seasons"**. **A three-piece cipher set**: **莲 = 怜 ('to love'), 丝 = 思 ('to long for'), 藕 = 偶 ('a mate')** — and one further term of address: **"欢" = the beloved** (**the moment the word "欢" is spoken in a song, the scene of love is constituted — this is the first-person dictionary of Southern Dynasties folk song**). "**春林花多媚，春鸟意多哀**" ("In the spring woods the flowers are full of charm; the spring birds' feelings are full of sorrow." (Our translation.)) ('Spring Song' in the *Ziye Songs of the Four Seasons* — **the folk-song version of writing sorrow through a joyful scene: the myriad things have all become accomplices in a lost love**).
:::

::: definition The chain-link art of 'Song of Xizhou' {#def-xizhou}
The **ceiling** of Southern Dynasties *yuefu* (Music Bureau poems; ballads). First strip the technique completely bare: **① the hook-lock of carried-over words** ("**单衫杏子红，双鬓鸦雏色**" "An unlined jacket, apricot-red; the hair at the two temples, the colour of a young crow." (Our translation.) "**栏杆十二曲，垂手明如玉**" "The railing has twelve bends; the lowered hands are bright as jade." (Our translation.) "**莲子青如水，置莲怀袖中**" "Lotus seeds green as water; she places the lotus within breast and sleeve." (Our translation.) — **莲 = 怜, 清 = 情 ('feeling'), 莲心 = 怜中心 ('love at the heart'): the homophone here is not an ornament, it is a load-bearing wall — the character "情" is not permitted to appear anywhere in the piece, and the whole "莲" clan does the work in its place**). **② the seasons' turn on duty** (plum blossom plucked and sent north of the River; the shrike sees summer off; the *wujiu* 乌臼 falls to autumn; the green *huai* sways into spring — the seasonal signs of the four seasons come on shift one by one — **a year of longing clocks in by a year of seasonal signs: the time-structure of the *Ziye Songs of the Four Seasons* is enlarged into a single song**). **③ the dream's travel permit** ("**南风知我意，吹梦到西洲**" "The south wind knows my mind, and blows the dream to Xizhou." (Our translation.) — **the line of the real makes no progress at any point along the way (gazing, going upstairs, hands lowered, going out of the gate); the only journey that succeeds is the dream: the most civilised sentence of complaint in the Southern Dynasties**). **The problem of how the text was fixed**: the form of this piece is close to the Southern Dynasties ditties of the Jiangling area; **it was sung from mouth to mouth, and it was also polished by literati — inside *yuefu*, "the folk" and "the literati" have always been in collusion, not in opposition** ([[han-to-nanbeichao/han-yuefu]] an extreme case of the "mixing desk" principle).
:::

## The Northern Dynasties: the vocal style of wind and sand {#def-bei}

::: definition Northern songs within drum, horn and transverse pipe {#def-beige}
**The channel of preservation is bitterly ironic**: the household heading under which Northern Dynasties folk songs have come down is the *Liang Songs for Drum, Horn and Transverse Pipe* (《**梁鼓角横吹曲**》) — **the military-music archive of the Liang, a southern dynasty, kept the north's songs for it** (the regimes of south and north copied one another's playlists: music was the only visa-free diplomacy in an age of disorder); the master ledger of the *Yuefu shiji* (*Collection of Yuefu Poetry*, 《乐府诗集》) preserves sixty-odd pieces, **chiefly written out in Chinese, with words in phonetic transcription kept here and there — of which the two characters "可汗" matter most: in the *Ballad of Mulan* (《木兰诗》), "昨夜见军帖，可汗大点兵"** ("Last night I saw the army notice; the khan is calling a great muster." (Our translation.)) — **the khan who pops out of the army notice is the badge of identity of Northern Dynasties folk song: a story of loyalty and filial piety about a woman of a Han house, its household register filed under the conscription system of the northern Asian steppe**. **Three vocal styles**: **the man on campaign** ("**陇头流水，鸣声幽咽。遥望秦川，肝肠断绝**" "At Longtou the water flows, its sound a choked sobbing. Gazing far off to Qinchuan, liver and bowels are severed." (Our translation.) — the 'Longtou Songs' (《陇头歌辞》): an acoustic montage that writes homesickness in the sound of water; the Long mountains are the northerner's "Yangguan"); **the knight-errant** ("**男儿欲作健，结伴不须多**" "A man who would be a brave needs no large company." (Our translation.) — the 'Qiyu Songs' (《企喻歌》)); **the sky's dome from horseback** (the 'Chile Song' (《**敕勒歌**》): "**天似穹庐，笼盖四野。天苍苍，野茫茫，风吹草低见牛羊**" "The sky is like a domed tent, covering the wilds on every side. The sky is a deep blue, the wilds stretch without end; the wind blows, the grass bends low, and oxen and sheep come into view." (Our translation.) — **it was originally a song in a northern tongue; translated into Chinese, it became instead the shortest epic in Chinese poetry: the *History of the Northern Dynasties* records its grieving song at an army feast — a work that translation has kept safe sometimes lives longer than the original text**). **The *Ballad of Mulan*: the summation of northern song** — "**不闻机杼声，惟闻女叹息**" ("No sound of loom or shuttle is heard; one hears only a girl sighing." (Our translation.)), "**万里赴戎机，关山度若飞。朔气传金柝，寒光照铁衣**" ("Over ten thousand *li* she goes to the business of war; passes and mountains are crossed as if in flight. The northern air carries the metal night-clapper; cold light shines on iron armour." (Our translation.)), and at the close "**雄兔脚扑朔，雌兔眼迷离；双兔傍地走，安能辨我是雄雌**" ("The male hare's feet kick and scuffle; the female hare's eyes are misted and blurred. Two hares run side by side along the ground; how can you tell whether I am male or female?" (Our translation.)) — **the plainness of the Northern Dynasties and the parallel couplets of the Southern Dynasties are joined in a single poem; together with 'The Peacock Flies South-east' (《孔雀东南飞》) it is called one of the twin peaks of narrative *yuefu*: one south and one north, one death and one life**.
:::

::: example An experiment in paired sounds: one and the same "grief", two grammars {#ex-duiyin}
Southern song outsources the feeling of sorrow: "**春林花多媚，春鸟意多哀**" ("In the spring woods the flowers are full of charm; the spring birds' feelings are full of sorrow." (Our translation.)) (the bird sorrows in her place); northern song reports the viscera direct: "**遥望秦川，肝肠断绝**" ("Gazing far off to Qinchuan, liver and bowels are severed." (Our translation.)). Set the south's reticence once more — "**南风知我意，吹梦到西洲**" ("The south wind knows my mind, and blows the dream to Xizhou." (Our translation.)) — against the north's frankness: the 'Song of Breaking Off Willow Branches' (《折杨柳枝歌》), "**门前一株枣，岁岁不知老。阿婆不嫁女，那得孙儿抱**" ("Before the gate, one jujube tree; year after year it knows nothing of growing old. If Granny will not marry off her daughter, how is she to hold a grandchild?" (Our translation.)) (a Northern Dynasties song pressing for marriage: the mother's point of view, plain-spoken to the point of coarseness, and coarse to the point of frankness). The question: **the rhetorical strategy, and the social soil, of each of the two grammars**.
::: solution
**The south: an outsourcing system** — feeling cannot be spoken (the code of rites + salon culture), and so: ① **things (the lotus, silk thread) do the work in its place**; ② **bird, moon and wind fill the post** ("春鸟意多哀" "the spring birds' feelings are full of sorrow" (Our translation.); "明月照楼" "the bright moon shines on the tower" (Our translation.); "南风知我意" "the south wind knows my mind" (Our translation.) — **feeling is moved out of the subject item by item; the poem reads like scenery, but it is a medical case-file**) — the soil: **a mixed quarter where marketplace and court live together** (Wu songs and Western songs were collected and edited by the music offices of the capital cities; **the listeners ran from boat-women to the likes of Xiao Yan 萧衍**: refinement is what a mixed clientele requires). **The north: an implosion system** — feeling is said direct ("肝肠断绝" "liver and bowels are severed" (Our translation.)), and the matter is asked direct ("阿婆不嫁女，那得孙儿抱" "If Granny will not marry off her daughter, how is she to hold a grandchild?" (Our translation.) — **marriage is written up as pressure on the stores: the humour of northern song is a counting-house humour**); the soil: **a society of war and resettled people** — campaign and garrison, herding, peoples living mixed together; the walls of language are thinner than in the Southern Dynasties; **the character "欢" appears far less often in northern song than in southern song: northern song does not deal in love; what it deals in is parting itself**. The marking sentence for the volume bound as one: **Southern Dynasties folk songs teach you "how to say love so that it is not said straight"; Northern Dynasties folk songs teach you "how to say bitterness straight"** — Tang poetry incorporates both grammars into its ranks: Wang Wei 王维 learns the former, Wang Changling 王昌龄 the latter.
:::

::: intuition Whose people, and whose in-between, in "folk" {#int-minjian}
The mirror one must look into before reading the folk songs of the Southern and Northern Dynasties: **every piece called "folk" has passed through three stages of official-document travel** — ① **collecting** (the ears of the music offices and of the capital cities sieve first: whatever is "full-flavoured" enough is what gets taken in); ② **writing down** (from song to text: **noting the sounds, cutting the long to fit the short, deleting the vulgar and keeping the elegant — the *yuefu* institution is a mixing desk**; [[han-to-nanbeichao/han-yuefu]] "the microphone, too, is one the state hands over" (Our translation.) holds with double force here: **this time the person who passes the microphone is still a southern court, and the ones who sing are northerners**); ③ **filing by class** (Guo Maoqian 郭茂倩 files them by **instrument — occasion — region**: **the pigeonholes of the archive decide the order in which we "hear" today**). The conclusion is not "it is all fake", but this: **a folk song is a compound of folk material and an official format — its "truth" lies in the first-handness of the feeling; its "beauty" lies in the secondariness of the collecting**. The method of studying it changes with that: **do not ask only "what did the common people think?"; ask at the same time "who, at what time, for the sake of what, kept which lines?"**.
:::

::: history Two banks of one river {#sec-liangge}
**Han and Wei**: the skeleton of the old *yuefu* is still there (the *xianghe* song lyrics); **Eastern Jin — Song — Qi**: **Wu songs and Western songs flourish greatly in the capital cities**; the 'Treatise on Music' of the *Book of Song* opens a file — **outside music of the "elegant" kind, popular music for the first time enters the music treatise of an official history as an established institution**; **Liang and Chen**: **the court imitates them in great quantity** (Emperor Jianwen of the Liang and the princes exchanging verses — **the "tender charm" of the Wu songs links with the "amorous" of palace style: for the last batch of fine pieces among Southern Dynasties folk songs, the client is the crown prince**); **the north**: **northern songs enter the Liang** (drum, horn and transverse pipe: **the "imported playlist" of the Liang military band became the only home of Northern Dynasties folk songs**); **Northern Wei — Zhou — Sui**: **mutual translation between the music of south and north speeds up** — the Sui establishes the **seven-part music and the nine-part music**: **Hu music and popular music are shelved in separate cabinets within the state's ritual music, and the Tang "ten-part music" inherits the arrangement** (**from folk song to banquets for state guests: the musicians were the true united front from the Southern and Northern Dynasties through to the Sui and Tang**); **Tang**: **imitations go on without a break, while original making stops** — **Li Bai 李白, 'Ziye Songs of Wu' (《子夜吴歌》), "长安一片月，万户捣衣声"** ("Over Chang'an a sheet of moonlight; from ten thousand households, the sound of beating cloth." (Our translation.)): **Tang writers took the tone of the Wu songs back to write Chang'an: the imitator is more "self-aware" than the original maker**; **Song**: **Guo Maoqian's master ledger**; **Ming**: **Feng Menglong 冯梦龙 compiles the *Mountain Songs* (《山歌》)** — **folk songs in the new Wu of the Three Wu; the system of homophones and puns revives just as it was ("石" in 石头, and "丝" — a Ming copy of the medieval cipher); he wrote a preface for the mountain songs and published them, openly taking the vulgar to be the true — late-Ming publishers reproduce the function of the "*yuefu*": the voice of the folk once again needs the sponsorship of men with examination honours**; **Qing to the present**: *xintianyou* 信天游, *nanwu* songs 南无歌, and the "*caifeng*" movement — **from the collecting of songs to field recording:** collecting always comes before preserving; this iron rule has not changed from the 'Han Treatise' (《汉志》) down to today (compare: the perspective of "register" in [[rhetoric/registers]] — folk song = the marketplace register transcribed by an official register).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading folk songs
**Taking the folk song for something "purely of the folk"** (the three stages of official-document travel: a compound is not a counterfeit — **but do not take it as first-hand material for a social survey either**: "阿婆不嫁女" ("If Granny will not marry off her daughter" (Our translation.)) is a line of song, not a household register); **taking the homophone for a game with words** (**莲 = 怜 is not a rhetorical Easter egg; it is a necessity of speech in a society under the code of rites: the cipher itself is the content — it records which things cannot be said straight**); **judging better and worse by "plainness"** (the chain structure of 'Song of Xizhou' is as fine as a Swiss watch — **the "plainness" of the folk is often the invisibility cloak of craft**); **making the opposition of south and north absolute** (the *Ballad of Mulan*, in a single poem, is already a hybrid: **the borderland is what is normal; both Jiangnan and the grasslands are a capital's prejudice**).
:::

::: exercise Compiling the cipher {level=1}
Decipher and annotate a stretch of lyrics from the "莲" clan (choose several lines yourself from the *Ziye* songs or from 'Song of Xizhou'), and set out **three columns**: the literal (the image) / the homophone (the feeling) / a full translation of the line's sense; then **compose one of your own**, a four-line "new Wu song", using at least two modern "homophones or puns" (**Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, being online, dropping offline — any of these can be a "丝"**).
::: solution
Marking: the three columns must not cross into one another's places (**the translated sense must allow one to work back from it: if the homophone is removed, the line collapses at once; the cipher is a load-bearing wall — whoever marks it "decoration" loses marks**); the line the composition must clear: **the pun has to carry pain** ("蓝牙断开" ("Bluetooth disconnected") has to be able to bear "绝弦" ("a string snapped") before it truly inherits the 莲/怜 system — **the life in a pun = the degree to which a thing cannot be said straight**).
:::

::: exercise A comparison table of vocal styles {level=2}
Using the *Ballad of Mulan* and 'Song of Xizhou', fill in a **six-dimension comparison table** (scene / manner of going / time-structure / strategy of expressing feeling / perspective of gender / the action at the end), and write a concluding sentence: both poems write of "waiting and going far" — why are the ways they handle it so contrary?
::: solution
For reference: **the action at the end is the key evidence — 'Song of Xizhou' stops at the dream ("吹梦到西洲" "blows the dream to Xizhou" (Our translation.)), the *Ballad of Mulan* stops at telling-apart (the playful cheek of "安能辨我是雄雌" "how can you tell whether I am male or female?" (Our translation.)): one hands what cannot be solved over to the south wind; the other laughs, and takes the question of gender as spoils of war**; the concluding sentence hits the mark when it says: **southern song handles "not to be had"; northern song handles "gone, and back again" — a difference of structure = a difference of way of life (the scattering of a city of trade, versus the distant expedition of a society under conscription)**; if the table is filled from impression alone, with no line quoted, it does not pass.

::: exercise A short essay: who is singing in the people's stead {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim that "**the folk songs of the Southern and Northern Dynasties are a specimen of China's first 'popular-music industry'**". You must draw on at least three of: **the capital-city market for Wu songs and Western songs**, **imitation at the Liang court**, **the cross-border archiving of drum, horn and transverse pipe**, **the late-Ming restart in Feng Menglong's *Mountain Songs***; discuss how the four rings **collecting — adapting — releasing — nostalgia** are opened again in every generation; and give one contemporary case (any one of **a short-video cover of a viral hit / a sample in "world music" / a band's arrangement of an old tune**).
::: solution
Marking points: all four rings present, and each with a **subject** (**at every ring ask "who pays?": the music offices of the capital cities, the crown prince, the military band, the bookseller — the "folk" is the view from the top of this food chain**); the mechanism of "reopening" clearly set out (**every generation needs a "true voice" — but the "true voice" is always invented anew: Feng Menglong's preface, the exchange of verses between Emperor Wu of the Liang and his sons, and today's "original-ecology" stage are three skins of the same act**); the case lined up (a cover of a viral hit = the Douyin version of imitating *yuefu* — **new wine in old bottles always works, because the bottle is the channel, and the wine is the demand**); if the conclusion writes "**the 'folk' is not a source; it is a radio station each generation builds for itself — the frequency is always on, and the station logo changes in turn**", that is an A.

::: summary
Two dictionaries and one mirror: the south (**莲 for 怜 and 丝 for 思, plus the dictionary of the character "欢", plus the outsourcing grammar: scenery is a medical case-file**), the north (**the khan's household register, the implosion grammar of liver and bowels severed, tunes translated at the army feast — translation outlives the original text**), the mirror (**three stages of official-document travel: the first-handness of the feeling + the secondariness of the collecting = the folk song, this compound**), the river (**the united front of musicians and imitations flows into the Tang: Wang Wei takes the south, Wang Changling takes the north**). The method in one sentence: **when you hear "the voice of the people", first find the hand that passed the microphone**. The next chapter brings things to a close: the fiction of tales of the strange (*zhiguai*), the lingering savour of anecdotes of people (*zhiren*), and the criticism of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* and of the *Shipin* (《诗品》, 'Gradings of Poetry') — the Six Dynasties fitted literature with a filing cabinet, a mirror and a written judgement.
:::
