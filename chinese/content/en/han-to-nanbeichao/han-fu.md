---
title: "The Han rhapsody"
---

The empire needed a genre worthy of itself: **the words had to be many, the things had to be complete, and the momentum had to spread off the page**. The Han rhapsody (*fu* 赋; also rhyme-prose, poetic exposition) is the delivery note for that demand. It cuts out the lyric organs of the *Songs of Chu* and fits in their place a set of **encyclopaedic lungs**: three thousand characters on one breath, and it does not draw another. This chapter teaches you both to stroll through this giant mall and to see clearly why its fire exit ('讽', *feng*, 'indirect admonition') is always locked.

## The genealogy of the rhapsody {#def-zupu}

::: definition From the *sao* style to the giant rhapsody {#def-santi}
**One name, two ancestors**: the basic meaning of '赋' is **to spread** (to set the matter out at length) — Liu Xi 刘熙: '赋，敷也，敷陈其志' ('赋 means 敷: to spread forth one's intent.') (Our translation.) — one thing and two names; its **biological father is the *Songs of Chu*** (the shape of the line, the character 兮, the fragrant herbs, the shamanic air — [[pre-qin-literature/songs-of-chu]]), and its **institutional father is the 'Rhapsodies' of Xunzi 荀子, *Fu pian* 《赋篇》** ('object-verse in the manner of riddles'; **the one who named it was Xun Kuang 荀况 himself** — the five pieces in the 'Rhapsodies', '礼' ('Ritual'), '知' ('Knowledge'), '云' ('Clouds'), '蚕' ('the Silkworm') and '针' ('the Needle'), **the first genre in history to be named with the word '赋'**). **Four stages of evolution**: ① **the *sao*-style rhapsody** (early Han: Jia Yi 贾谊, *Diao Qu Yuan fu* 《吊屈原赋》 ('Rhapsody Mourning Qu Yuan') and *Funiao fu* 《鵩鸟赋》 ('Rhapsody on the Owl') — **exile literature in the Chu voice**: the demoted Grand Tutor to the King of Changsha crosses the river of Qu Yuan 屈原 once more, on the original pattern); ② **the grand rhapsody takes its settled form** (Mei Sheng 枚乘, *Qi fa* 《七发》 ('Seven Stimuli'): **eight sections of question-and-challenge; seven matters all fail to cure the illness of the '太子' ('crown prince'); at the end '要言妙道' ('essential words and the wondrous Way') works at a single needle-prick — from then on 'seven' is another name for the genre**; Liu Xie 刘勰 calls it '**the head of the rhapsody**' — **the mall of the grand rhapsody, the skeleton of question-and-challenge, and the business model of encouraging a hundred are all delivered in this one piece**); ③ **the grand rhapsody at its height** (Sima Xiangru 司马相如, *Zixu* 《子虚》 ('Sir Vacuous'), *Shanglin* 《上林》 ('The Shanglin Park') and *Daren* 《大人》 ('The Great Man') — **the show flat of the empire is completed**; Yang Xiong 扬雄's 'four great rhapsodies', *Ganquan* 《甘泉》 ('Sweet Springs'), *Hedong* 《河东》 ('East of the River'), *Yulie* 《羽猎》 ('The Plume Hunt') and *Changyang* 《长杨》 ('Tall Poplars') — **a high-definition broadcast of the emperor's progress**; Ban Gu 班固, *Liang du* 《两都》 ('The Two Capitals'), and Zhang Heng 张衡, *Er jing* 《二京》 ('The Two Metropolises') — **a competitive pitch between capitals: to write a rhapsody = to bid for a city**); ④ **the turn to lyric** (the later Eastern Han: Zhang Heng's *Guitian fu* 《归田赋》 ('Rhapsody on Returning to the Fields') sounds the first note of fields and gardens; Wang Can 王粲, *Denglou fu* 《登楼赋》 ('Rhapsody on Climbing the Tower'), and Mi Heng 祢衡, *Yingwu fu* 《鹦鹉赋》 ('Rhapsody on the Parrot') — **the great mall breaks up the partnership, and the rhapsody withdraws to the study: giving form to things yields its place to writing intent**). The short rhapsody and the parallel rhapsody of the Six Dynasties take the baton — **[[classical-chinese-2/parallel-prose-fu]] has already set out the definition '铺采摘文，体物写志' ('Spread the colours and cull the patterned words; give form to things and write intent.') (Our translation.); this chapter walks the first half of its life**.
:::

::: definition The three-layer construction of the grand rhapsody {#def-gouzao}
**First layer, the question-and-challenge arrangement**: *Zixu fu* 《子虚赋》 ('Rhapsody of Sir Vacuous') has three false name-cards — **Zi Xu, envoy of Chu; Master Wu You, a man of Qi; and Lord Wang Shi** — **all three persons are fictional, and the name is the thesis of the essay** (子虚＝子虚, the name *is* 'empty, sir'; 乌有＝乌有, the name *is* 'there is none'; 亡是公＝无此人, 'there is no such person' — **to write a piece of criticism in personal names is the earliest metafictional device in Chinese literature**): Zi Xu boasts of Chu, Wu You boasts of Qi, and Lord Wang Shi **pins the two men's home places to the ground and spreads them flat**, and at the end offers 'admonition' on the Son of Heaven's behalf — **the structure is the political argument: local showing-off is co-opted by the centre's flaunting of wealth**. Second layer, **the catalogue-learning of display**: the database of the Shanglin rhapsody — mountains have a word-bank for mountains, waters a word-bank for waters, and fruit trees a shelf for fruit trees ('Seven Stimuli' had already shown the way: food and drink, carriages and horses, music and sensual beauty, hunting, watching the bore — **an exposition with one hall to each show**; linked-radical characters, with '**金、玉、草、虫、鱼、龙**' ('metal, jade, grass, insect, fish, dragon') and the components running in rows — **fireworks of the shapes of Chinese characters: beyond the meaning, the face of the writing itself is the display frontage**). Third layer, **the moral receipt**: 曲终奏雅 ('when the tune ends, the refined note is played'). The formula of closure: after the boast has been driven to its limit, **the protagonist suddenly awakes, stops the hunt / reduces the parks / shares the profit with officers and people** — Liu Xie sums this up as '**劝百而讽一**' ('a hundred encouragements and one admonition') — **a hundred points of lavish display buy one point of remonstrance; the exchange rate was wrong from the start**.
:::

::: example A page of the Shanglin rhapsody: fireworks of the written face {#ex-shanglin}
Set the questions from material this lesson has already established: ① How does the three-peak structure of Zi Xu — Wu You — Lord Wang Shi complete the ladder of power, 'locality → centre → Son of Heaven'? ② What force as historical fact has the line in the rhapsody '**发仓廪以赈穷民，开田畴与百姓同之**' ('Open the granaries to relieve the poor, and open the fields to share them with the common people.') (Our translation.) (is it a policy of Emperor Wu of Han, or a fiction of the rhapsody)? ③ If the moral receipt is deleted, **does the political meaning of this rhapsody reverse**?
::: solution
① Three peaks = three steps: **the lords boasting against one another is a leftover of the Warring States (the tone of Zi Xu and Wu You is like diplomatic language); Lord Wang Shi's steamrolling of them is the new grammar of the empire — literary structure copies political integration exactly**; a method of close reading: **mark each false name-card with its 'speaking time' — the number of characters in the display is the quota of the right to speak**. ② Relieving the poor, and sharing with the common people, is **the rhapsody's sentence-form of what ought to be, not a record of events** — in the Basic Annals of Emperor Wu in the *Records of the Grand Historian* and the *Book of Han* there is no matching edict that lines up with this passage of 'opening Shanglin' — **before you treat literature as a historical source, first check whether it is a fixed fitting of the genre** (the lesson: the moral receipt **belongs to the rhapsody, not to the court**). ③ It would reverse: **display without a receipt is incitement; only with a receipt is it called 'admonition'** — the rhapsody's qualification to count as politically safe rests entirely on this tail; but readers only clip the tail and do not read the main text (**in the copying and transmission of every age, what is excerpted and passed on is always the passage of display**), so '劝百讽一' ('a hundred encouragements and one admonition') is not a failure; it is **the normal operating condition of this genre**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The psychology of wealth-flaunting literature {#int-xuanfu}
The grand rhapsody is **humankind's earliest large-scale brand promotional film**: Emperor Wu of Han read *Daren fu* 《大人赋》 ('Rhapsody on the Great Man'), and the *Records of the Grand Historian* records: '**天子大悦，飘飘然有凌云气，游天地之间意**' ('The Son of Heaven was greatly delighted; he felt himself floating, with an air of rising above the clouds and a mind to wander between heaven and earth.') (Our translation.). **The Son of Heaven read a rhapsody of his own empire and read it as an advertisement for cultivating immortality — the client himself is the first viewer**. On this basis one can take apart the three beats of wealth-flaunting literature: **one, aestheticise volume itself** (the count of characters, the count of things, the count of place-names = the dashboard of national strength); **two, turn the reader into a tourist** (the rhapsody does not ask you to believe; it only asks you to **browse**); **three, use the moral receipt to issue a duty-free certificate for the pleasure** ('admonition' = a one-stop service that lets the emperor **both have his pleasure and examine himself**). These three beats reappear, unchanged, in today's **city image-films, financial reports and annual galas of the big firms** — so the Han rhapsody is not a dead genre; **it is the first-edition source code of that ancient genre, the 'promotional film', and the comments are still written just the same**.
:::

::: history The rhapsody at court, and the ebb {#sec-ting}
**Early Han**: the vassal kings kept the rhapsody — **around the Rebellion of the Seven Kingdoms of Wu and Chu (154 BC), the Liang Garden of King Xiao of Liang was the first headquarters of a rhapsody school, and Mei Sheng's 'Seven Stimuli' is a product of the Liang Garden**; Jia Yi, in his person as a demoted official, wrote the dynasty a *sao*-style memorandum; **Emperor Wu**: **the Music Bureau's collecting of songs and the awaiting-summons for rhapsody hung out their signs in the same year — the court bought, at one stroke, 'the voice of the street' and 'the rhetoric of the empire'** (**the Shanglin rhapsodies were offered to a court that was then opening the frontiers; the genre and the dynastic fortune expanded their capacity in step**); Emperor Xuan defended the rhapsody, and set it alongside '贤于倡优' ('worthier than the actors and jesters') — **the court acknowledged it as entertainment of a higher grade**; **Emperor Cheng** decreed a search for lost books, and **Yang Xiong came to be known by his rhapsodies; '天子令尚书赐笔札'** ('The Son of Heaven ordered the Masters of Writing to grant him brush and slips.') (Our translation.) — **the emperor issuing manuscript paper to a man of letters: the news of the highest treatment a Han writer received**; when Wang Mang 王莽 usurped the Han, **Yang Xiong was summoned in his old age to receive the canons**, and he had always kept himself in the loneliness of his declining years — **the compiling of *Fangyan* 《方言》 ('Regional Speech') and the lawsuit of the Tianlu Pavilion became a famous unresolved case of 'the scholar and a change of dynasty'** — **the age of the grand rhapsody settled its bill together with the imperial finances**; **Eastern Han**: a competitive pitch between capitals ('The Two Capitals', 'The Two Metropolises'), and the Hongdu Gate School selecting men for office by painting, calligraphy and rhapsody (Emperor Ling — **the rhapsody entered the examination system for the first time, though orthodoxy looked askance**); **Jian'an**: the writers at Ye, **Wang Can's 'Rhapsody on Climbing the Tower' and Cao Zhi 曹植's *Luoshen fu* 《洛神赋》 ('Rhapsody on the Goddess of the Luo') — the frame of the grand rhapsody was taken down, and a small house for lyric feeling put up instead**; **Six Dynasties**: the rhapsody and parallel prose flow together ([[classical-chinese-2/parallel-prose-fu]]), and **Liu Xie's *Quan fu* 《诠赋》 ('Elucidating the Rhapsody') closes the coffin: '铺采摘文，体物写志'** ('Spread the colours and cull the patterned words; give form to things and write intent.') (Our translation.) — **the epitaph is written more presentably than the dead**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading the rhapsody
**Taking a memorised word-bank for understanding** (the hard characters of the grand rhapsody are **a threshold designed on purpose** — the function of rare linked-radical characters is 'grandeur', not 'accuracy'; **read the structure first, then look the characters up**); **taking remonstrance for historical fact** ('sharing it with the common people' is a fitting of the genre, not a record of policy — already judged in example ② above); **sentencing the grand rhapsody to death by the standard of lyric feeling** ('the Han rhapsody has no feeling' — **it was never developed for this lyrical system of yours**; men of the Six Dynasties disliked its '铺张扬厉' ('spread out and driven to the limit'), and men of the Song disliked the regulated rhapsody as '俳' ('a showman's turn'); every age changes the verdict by its own taste — a great part of the history of literary criticism is **a record of verdicts overturned by taste**); **forgetting that the rhapsody is recited to the ear** ('**不歌而诵谓之赋**' — 'What is not sung, but recited, is called *fu*.' (Our translation.) — Ban Gu's *Han zhi* 《汉志》 ('Han Treatise'): the rhapsody is the script of **an art of recitation**; silent reading throws away half its energy; only the emperor who read it in silence turned the rhapsody into literature).
:::

::: exercise Surveying the structure {level=1}
Read 《七发》 (an extract will do). For the eight parts, draw a **circuit diagram of the question-and-challenge** (who falls ill, who writes the prescription, and why the first seven prescriptions do not work), and answer in 100 characters: **why must '要言妙道' ('essential words and the wondrous Way') be placed in the last prescription, and what would follow if it came one prescription earlier or one later?**
::: solution
For marking: **the sensory upgrade-sequence of the seven prescriptions** (music — food and drink — carriages and horses — sightseeing — hunting — watching the bore — essential words: **the stimulus is raised step by step, and this is exactly the circulation route through the show flat of the grand rhapsody**); the account of the last prescription is on target: **the illness of the body is an illness of rhetoric, and only speech can cure it — the prince '据几而起' ('rose, leaning on the armrest') — the recovery happens in the instant of 'hearing': in the worldview of the rhapsody, listening is treatment is teaching**; being able to connect this with one's own experience (being cured by an advertisement's 'last sentence of values') earns extra marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Writing the exposition {level=2}
Imitating the shelf-modules of 《七发》 and 《上林》, write, for your school or your city, a **'wealth-flaunting rhapsody' of three hundred characters**: one frame of question-and-challenge (two who boast of their home places + one who lowers the dimension), one list of linked radicals or of things of a kind (a canteen menu, or a ranking of full-sugar milk teas, will both do), and close with **one moral receipt**. Self-assessment: can your receipt hold the main text back?
::: solution
For marking: **the list must have 'gone up a dimension'** (ordered by quantity, rarity or degree of absurdity — **catalogue-learning is a verb, not a noun**); the speech of the one who lowers the dimension must be **more overbearing than both sides added together** (otherwise the structure collapses); the feebleness of the receipt must be written honestly (**'劝百讽一' ('a hundred encouragements and one admonition') is the exchange rate you yourself have judged**); if the list reads like an annual report — congratulations, **you are already put through to the rhapsodists of the Han**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the political economy of the grand rhapsody {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: 'The grand rhapsody is a **three-party transaction among the court, the writer and the reader** — the court buys display frontage, the writer buys a post of awaiting summons, the reader buys a spectacle; remonstrance is the exemption clause of the contract.' You must draw on at least three historical facts: Emperor Wu reading 《大人赋》 and being greatly pleased; Yang Xiong's regret in '雕虫篆刻，壮夫不为' ('Carving insects and engraving seals: a proper man does not do it.') (Our translation.) ([[classical-chinese-2/parallel-prose-fu]] has already shown it; quote it straight here); the Liang Garden and Emperor Ling's Hongdu Gate School (**the rhapsody's system of patronage and its function as a way into office**). And analyse one modern text of the same kind (any one of a city image-film, a financial-report annual meeting, or an open world in a game).
::: solution
Marking points: the facts are accurate (**Emperor Wu's great pleasure shows that the client took the bait in person; Yang Xiong's regret is the author's later claim against the terms of the transaction; the Hongdu Gate School = the rhapsody's institutional victory and its failure of taste, happening at the same time**); the model of the 'three-party transaction' must be tested by **at least one counter-example** (for instance, no one carried out the Shanglin sentence on relieving the poor — **the exemption clause has never paid out on a claim**); the modern analogy aligns the mechanisms (an open-world game: the map's **collection rate** = the present-day reincarnation of catalogue-learning; '本作纯属虚构' ('This work is entirely a fiction.') (Our translation.) = the moral receipt); if the conclusion can admit that '**the point of the satirical clause is not that it takes effect, but that it makes the whole text legitimate**', that is an A — this sentence was paid in advance in example ② of this lesson, and the essay must pay the balance.
:::

::: summary
Checking out of the mall tour: genealogy (a *Songs of Chu* father + a Xunzi-rhapsody father; *sao* style — grand rhapsody — lyric, four stages), construction (**the question-and-challenge frame of three false name-cards, the fireworks of linked-radical characters, the moral receipt**), psychology (the three beats of the promotional film: **volume aestheticised, the reader turned tourist, pleasure made duty-free**), fate (headquarters at the Liang Garden — awaiting summons at court — sitting the examinations at Hongdu — the building taken down at Ye — the coffin closed in the *Wenxin* 《文心》). One sentence of method: **in reading a rhapsody, first read the structure of its contract, then read its ornament of words; ask 'whom was the admonition given to?', then ask 'where did the money come from?'**. The next chapter leaves the mall and enters the hall of history: how Sima Qian 司马迁 built, on bamboo slips, a labyrinth of persons that not even emperors can walk out of.
