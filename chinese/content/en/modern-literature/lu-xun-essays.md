---
title: "Lu Xun’s essays and prose poems"
---

The previous chapter took apart the machine that tells other people's stories; this chapter goes into two rooms: one facing the street (***zawen*** (polemical essay) — the javelin and the scalpel), one facing inward (***Wild Grass*** — operating on oneself). The two rooms share one set of wiring: **to take a stand never exempts one from self-interrogation**.

## The first room: *zawen* {#def-zawen}

::: definition The business licence of a genre in breach {#def-yingye}
The household registration of "*zawen*" was a hard case from the start (the live case file of Chapter 8's study of genres: **an "occasional piece" (随意文) that is neither poem nor prose nor fiction — both its self-deprecation ("杂", 'miscellaneous') and its disguise ("报屁股", 'the newspaper's backside') come from the system of newspaper columns**: in 1918 the "**随感录**" ('Random jottings') column in *New Youth* (《新青年》) opened its doors = the delivery room of the modern *zawen* (short, fast, to a deadline, facing the newspaper's readers — **form is decided by the layout: one issue, one encounter, forces out a build of "a thousand characters and a thousand peaks"**); the self-translation "**且介亭**" (cutting "租界", 'concession', in half — **the garret of the semi-concession: the geographical nameplate of *zawen*: form, the concession and censorship, the three of them, keep office at one desk**). The functional definition is taken from Lu Xun's own account: "**对于有害的事物，立刻给以反响或抗争，是感应的神经，攻守的手足**" ('Towards things that do harm, give an immediate response or resistance: it is the nerve of sensation, the hands and feet of attack and defence.') (Our translation.) (preface to *Qiejie ting zawen*, 《且介亭杂文》) — ***zawen* = the reflex arc of literature: it does not produce the digestion of a long work; it produces a spasm on the scale of milliseconds; its "shortness" is not contempt, it is the physical requirement of the speed of nerve conduction**.
:::

::: definition Three moves {#def-zhaoshu}
**① Quotation marks as an instrument of torture** — 《“友邦惊诧”论》 ('On the "shock" of the friendly powers'): a production line of quotation marks on the pattern of "**友邦人士，莫名之‘惊诧’，中国的人民，反在‘屠刀’之下**" ('Gentlemen of the friendly powers, a "shock" that cannot be named; the people of China, by contrast, under the "butcher's knife".') (Our translation.) (the celebrated lines in the piece are a run of quotation-mark blows of the sort “友邦人士，从此‘惊诧’了” ('The gentlemen of the friendly powers have, from this point, been "shocked".') (Our translation.)) — **to put quotation marks on a citation = to put handcuffs on a stance: the whole piece does not refute the reasons for "shock"; it only exhibits the timetable of "shock" (what it is today, what it is tomorrow), and lets the chain of evidence walk itself to the verdict** (the economics of satire: do not pay the wages of argument; let the other side's self-contradiction sell itself out); **② Ethnography of a low corpus** — 《论“他妈的！”》 ('On "他妈的!"') enters from the distribution density of the national curse (an evidential inquiry into "**中国的国骂**" ('the national curse of China') — **begin the archaeology of a culture from the most vulgar corpus: the system of rank has left a complete fossil in the generational grammar of swear-words: one obscenity reads out a history of the lineage**); **③ Paradoxical titles** — 《**为了忘却的记念**》 ('Remembrance in order to forget'; the five martyrs of the League of Left-Wing Writers) — **"为了忘却" ('in order to forget') and "记念" ('remembrance') pull the stage out from under each other: rhetoric under the censorship system — mechanism: the title is a case: the authorities confiscate the right to "记得" ('remember'), so the piece calls itself a recording done in order to forget (the grammar of saying the opposite: internalise the prohibition as one's own motive, so that the prohibition has nowhere to take hold)**; 《记念刘和珍君》 ('In memory of Liu Hezhen') is the frontal-assault version: the whole text saws between "speaking and not being able to speak" (the repetition of “我说” ('I say') and “我无话可说” ('I have nothing I can say') — **the structure of a mourning piece = the record of language being loaded and unloaded in the face of mourning**.
:::

## The second room: *Wild Grass* {#def-yecao}

::: definition The black room of expressionism {#def-heiwu}
The opening of 《野草·题辞》 (the 'Inscription' to *Wild Grass*) is already the final hearing of Chapter 4's debate on words and meaning: "**当我沉默着的时候，我觉得充实；我将开口，同时感到空虚**" ('When I am silent, I feel full; I am about to open my mouth, and at the same moment I feel empty.') (Our translation.) — **fullness belongs to silence, emptiness to opening one's mouth: expression is judged a loss-making business (the old clause "言不尽意", 'words do not exhaust meaning', grows a modern neurosis here)**; and the whole of *Wild Grass* is **an account book that opens for business knowing the loss** — **the hedge between "辞的不动" ('the words will not move') and "不得不作" ('I cannot but write') produces the form: the prose poem = a voucher paying the interest on a breach (not rhyming does not give it freedom; breaking into lines does not give it the identity of prose — in breach at both ends, a tax refund at both ends)**.
:::

::: definition Four one-acts: the rhetoric of choice {#def-dumuju}
The genre of *Wild Grass* is **the inner one-act** (the person is cut into props, which come on stage and cross-examine one another) — **《过客》** ('The passer-by'): a nameless stranger (the name is borrowed; he is called, in a "客客气气" way — 'guest-politely' — a "过客", 'passer-by', as he crosses the wilds; a girl gives cloth, an old man urges “回转去罢” ('Do turn back.') (Our translation.), and the only reply is "**但我总须到前方去**" ('But I must still go on to what is ahead.') (Our translation.) — **he still walks, "不知道前面是坟" ('not knowing that ahead is the grave'): choice comes before knowledge (year one of existentialism in China: no guarantee stood by the light, only the reverse of "不得不停下", 'having no choice but to stop')**; **《死火》** ('The dead fire'): "I" finds a frozen flame in an ice valley, carries it out of the valley, and it **at last burns out and dies** — **death by freezing or death by burning: of the two bad options, the only property "I" seizes is the act of "choosing"** (**mechanism: a literary specimen of the dilemma (aporia) — of the two bad options, dying frozen and dying burnt, the only thing "I" can carry away is the proof of "having chosen": Lu Xun does not give a way out; he dissects the word "way out"**); **《影的告别》** ('The shadow's farewell'): "**我不过一个影，要别你而沉没在黑暗里了。然而黑暗又会吞并我，然而光明又会使我消失**" ('I am only a shadow, about to leave you and sink into the darkness. Yet the darkness will swallow me, and yet the light will make me disappear.') (Our translation.) — **the shadow has no position open at either the bright end or the dark end: to wander so is not to hesitate; it is the only honest balance after the books are cleared** ("**我不愿彷徨于明暗之间**" ('I am not willing to wander between light and dark.') (Our translation.) — **to choose, between light and dark, "to be sunk by the darkness" = to complete a last declaration by cancelling the self**); **《风筝》** ('The kite'): an elder brother destroys a kite his younger brother had secretly made; years later he repents and asks forgiveness, "**然而他是没有怨恨的——然而我的惩罚终于没有**" ('Yet there was no resentment in him — and yet my punishment, in the end, there was none.') (Our translation.) — **a bad debt in the economy of repentance: if the victim does not keep the books, the perpetrator's debt stays on the account for ever — when forgiveness is absent, guilt becomes a life sentence ("a creditor who does not forgive": Lu Xun sentences himself to life, and lets the reader off without a ticket**).
:::

::: example The array of nothing: 'Such a fighter' × 'Revenge' {#ex-wuwu}
《这样的战士》 ('Such a fighter'): "**他走进无物之阵，所遇见的都对他一一点头……然而只不过是无物之阵**" ('He walks into the array of nothing; everyone he meets nods to him, one by one… and yet it is only an array of nothing.') (Our translation.) — the enemy wears the coat of "**好**" ('good') (“好青年”, 'good youth'; “好文艺”, 'good literature and art'; “好国粹”, 'good national essence'); the fighter raises his spear, and **when the other falls he still looks "good"**; 《复仇》 ('Revenge'): two people, naked and holding knives, stand in the open wild; “路人” ('passers-by') come, layer on layer, to savour the sight — “以“无所为而看”对“看”本身罢课：他们“以自居于无所有、空虚之中，来‘让’看客无戏可看” ('By "looking while doing nothing", they strike against "looking" itself: they "take their stand in having nothing and in emptiness, so as to 'let' the spectators have no play to watch."') (Our translation.) (the gist). Please: ① Is "无物之阵" ('the array of nothing') the same phenomenon as Chapter 1's "a blacklist does not produce works" (hint: **the enemy's invisibility = the informalisation of the rules — the object of satire has no substance, because oppression is carried out by consensus and respectability**); ② at the level of the device, the "refusal to be looked at" in 'Revenge' = a reverse copy of 《示众》 ('Shown to the crowd') (set the two out in a list); ③ adjudicate: **which phrase stays closer to the text, "反抗绝望" ('resisting despair') or "绝望的抗战" ('despair's war of resistance')** (“绝望之为虚妄，正与希望相同” ('That despair is illusory is exactly the same as that hope is.') (Our translation.) — 《希望》 ('Hope'), quoting Petőfi).
::: solution
① **The same kind of informal power**: what the Eight Don'ts bans is the writing; what the array of nothing dodges is the name — **the enemy's disguise as "good" = censorship needs no decree; taste and respectability are the enforcers: the fighter's spear therefore cannot find a lawful place to land (to strike "good" = to strike the consensus = to make oneself a madman — the adult version of the madman's case)**; ② the comparison (《示众》: **looking = the privilege of consuming other people's pain; the crowd of onlookers produces the event**; 《复仇》: **the non-cooperation strike of those being looked at = taking back the right to define the "event" — no struggling, no bleeding, no satisfaction; mechanism: defaulting on an expectation = taking the knife to the auditorium; the same people are demoted, across the two works, from protagonists to a laughing-stock — Lu Xun writes the reader into the queue as well: when you reach "the passers-by", check where you yourself are standing**); ③ the adjudication (**"resisting despair" leans to a psychological epic (the inspirational edition of existentialism), and is often emptied out into a slogan; "despair's war of resistance" keeps the tactics: the fighter raises the spear knowing there is nothing there — the form of the struggle itself becomes testimony, and victory is deleted from the contract: at the end of 'Such a fighter' the fighter ages and weakens, and dies inside the array of nothing — death does not change the formation, but death makes the formation show itself: at the close he “身中泥镞，一手还高举着没有投出的矛” ('clay arrowheads in his body, one hand still holding high the spear he has not thrown') (Our translation.) — the posture is the legacy**).
:::

::: intuition Two brush-edges, one liver {#int-yifu}
*Zawen* faces outward (naming names, auditing the books, putting quotation marks to the torture); *Wild Grass* faces inward (cutting sections, self-trial, deregistering the self) — **two facings of one and the same principle**: every form Lu Xun uses shares one anti-hypocrisy clause: **rhetoric may not pardon the speaker** (in the *zawen*, "I" always occupies one place on the list of those indicted; in *Wild Grass*, even the body and the shadow of "I" each appear in court — "**我的确时时解剖别人，然而更多的是更无情面地解剖我自己**" ('I do indeed dissect others all the time; yet even more, and with still less regard for face, I dissect myself.') (Our translation.) (《写在〈坟〉后面》, 'Written behind *The Grave*'): this sentence is not modesty; it is institutional design: self-interrogation is the collateral of the *zawen*'s credibility (one dares to put quotation marks on others only by daring to put them on oneself first)**). A warning added for this lesson**: this sentence-form is often quoted as evidence of "greatness" — **this section registers it only as a device: the collateral mechanism explains why Lu Xun's *zawen* dares to use quotation marks, and why his satire is immune to the backlash of "你行你上" ('if you can do it, you step up and do it'): because he had already done it, already stepped up, already paid**.
:::

::: history A history of the dagger's two uses {#sec-liangyong}
**1918**, Random Jottings (**the column system opens for business**); **1925–1926**, the golden years of pen-wars in the supplements (the weekly 《语丝》 (*Yusi*) — **the *Yusi* manner of “任意而谈，无所顾忌” ('talk as one pleases, with nothing held back') (Our translation.):** the periodical = an incubator of form (Chapter 8: genre is the grammar of collective memory; the Yusi Society = a club whose badge is the *zawen*), and “语丝体” ('the *Yusi* style') takes its name from this); **1928–1935**, the controversy over revolutionary literature and the "free men" (**the *zawen* becomes a weapon of the line: Lu Xun's later *zawen* opens fire at full force — and at the same time draws the charge of "extremism": extremism is a side-effect of the reflex arc, not a moral defect**); **1932–1934**, the **struggle between genres**, the humour and leisure of the "*Lunyu* school" against left-wing *zawen* (**Lin Yutang runs 《论语》 (*Lunyu*) and 《人间世》 (*Renjian shi*, 'This human world'): competition in the same trade between "using humour as a weapon" and "using a weapon as a weapon" — a commercial war of tea and dagger: two styles of subjectivity (Chapter 8: style is a choice function: humour chooses "the freedom of looking on", *zawen* chooses "the liability of stepping in"**); **the 1940s**, the *zawen* storm at Yan'an (**the debate over "exposure and praise" — for the first time the javelin is required to apply for a permit to carry a gun (the live case file of Chapter 9's history of effects)**); **1961–62**, 《燕山夜话》 (*Yanshan yehua*, 'Evening talks at Yanshan') and 《三家村札记》 (*Sanjiacun zaji*, 'Notes from Three-Family Village') (**a brief revival of *zawen* in the years of the planned economy, and its tragic calling-in: the fate of the columnists becomes the last footnote to *zawen***); **the 1980s–90s**, a revival of *zawen* (**the recovery of the press = the recovery of form: the informal essay, the *zawen* and topical commentary expand in successive waves — Chapter 1's institutional account: the rise and fall of a form follows the budget of the page**); **now**, **microblog form and the thousand-character commentary = *zawen* kept alive by the algorithm (**the repost count = the new reading of "response or resistance"; the hot search = a real-time survey map of the array of nothing: the instrument of torture that is quotation marks evolves into the emoji — the carrier frequency of satire is tuned up with the medium; the reflex arc does not recognise its master, but it recognises the stimulus).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading
**"*Zawen* = swearing at people"** (**a physiological misreading of the reflex arc: swearing looks at emotion; *zawen* looks at the chain of evidence and the device: miss any one of the three tools — quotation marks, evidential research, the paradoxical title — and it degenerates into letting off steam**); **treating *Wild Grass* as a set of riddle-faces** (**guessing, sentence by sentence, the political charade of "the shadow = who, the dead fire = what": the habit of *suoyin* (ferreting out hidden referents) returns (Chapter 9's report, column by column: the rate at which a metaphor is cashed is fixed by the community; a single solution confiscates the whole book) — what the one-act performs is the dilemma of a structure, not a cipher of someone's whereabouts**); **the repeat offence of turning Lu Xun into quotable lines** (**the day “当我沉默着的时候” ('When I am silent') is printed on a bookmark is the day the hedge between "full / empty" is flattened: what the extracted saying deletes is the cost of the next sentence**); **waving the charge of "extremism" through without inspection** (**to call Lu Xun extreme, one must first point to what ought to have been left unsaid: the side-effect of the reflex arc may be discussed, but "extremism" is not itself a refutation — quote the text first, then bring values in**).
:::

::: exercise Quotation-mark exercise {level=1}
From the latest round of public quarrelling, take three slogans that call themselves respectable (choose any three kinds from “为你好” ('for your own good'), “格局” ('breadth of outlook'), “情绪稳定” ('emotional stability')), and **write a 50-character "exhibition-style" short comment on each**: do not refute their reasons; only timetable them (who quoted what, of what matter, and when — **let the timetable walk itself to the verdict**); then judge one another's work, and whose "quotation marks" are placed most accurately.
::: solution
Marking: **zero refutation by adjective** (the discipline of the exhibition style); **a timetable that is real and can be checked** (three columns: date, occasion, speaker — **the baseline of the *zawen*'s chain of evidence**); the most accurate placement = the one that lets the slogan's self-contradiction develop itself (the semantic gap in “为你好” between urging a donation and urging overtime — **the lease of the same quotation marks in different sentences**).
:::

::: exercise Adapting the one-act {level=2}
Rewrite 《过客》 or 《风筝》 as **a three-minute contemporary short play** (keep the structural dilemma; psychological description and narration are forbidden — **only dialogue and stage directions are allowed**); attach 80 characters on what "the ice valley / the open wild" has been changed into in your version.
::: solution
Marking: **the dilemma undamaged** (the options must still all be bad, and there must be no way back — **to change it into "someone wakes him to the truth" is a breach: the road Lu Xun does not give, you may not build in private**); the stage directions carry the device (**cloth, grave, kite = the testimonial function of the props**); the conversion is honest (on the scale of "the ice valley = the office block of overtime" — **the conversion must keep the science of temperature: death by freezing = being used up, death by burning = resigning the title and the post, two kinds of bad — keep the sole property-right in "choosing"**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: collateral and the reflex arc {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters assessing "**Lu Xun made *zawen* a kind of credit instrument: behind every quotation mark fired at someone else there is a page of one's own account paid in first — the principal of satire is one's own unease**". You must draw on at least five things: **the definition of the reflex arc**, **quotation marks as an instrument of torture**, **the paradoxical title**, **the dilemma of the one-act**, **the loss-making opening for business in the 'Inscription' to *Wild Grass***; and adjudicate: **whether "everyone has quotation marks", in the age of social connection, has realised a democratisation of Lu Xun's *zawen* — or has only copied its vocal cords**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the five mechanisms (**the reflex arc = a spasm on the scale of milliseconds, and a chain of evidence; quotation marks = a citation in handcuffs, the collateral mechanism making it immune to "你行你上"; the paradoxical title = the prohibition internalised; the dilemma = the rhetoric of choice (the act of choosing is the only property); opening at a loss = the account book of expressionism**); the adjudication (**two lines of reference: the democratisation argument — a repost is a column, a hot search is the real-time survey of the array of nothing: everyone raising a spear fits the handing down of the "reflex arc"; the vocal-cord-copying argument — Lu Xun's firepower and his bill to himself are settled on separate accounts; online satire almost entirely only exports and does not pay in: quotation marks without collateral are a tattoo, not handcuffs; and the "array of nothing" becomes, inside the algorithm, an echo chamber — the spear swings at comrades on the same ground, and the formation never shows itself**); if the conclusion writes "**the lifespan of *zawen* does not depend on how sharp the dagger is; it depends on whether the person holding the knife also counts himself on the list of wounds: those who copy the vocal cords are many, those who pay in the bill are few — the reflex arc is still the public body's, only the report of pain is often forged**", that is an A.

::: summary
The two rooms, filed: facing the street (**the reflex arc: three tools, quotation marks, ethnography and the paradoxical title; shortness is a requirement of speed**), facing inward (**the account book that opens at a loss: four one-acts perform the last property, which is "choosing"**), the circuit (**rhetoric may not pardon the speaker: self-interrogation is the collateral of credibility**), and posterity (**the commercial war of tea and dagger, the permit to carry a gun, life-support by the hot search**). The method in one sentence: **audit the author's bill first, then listen to his verdict**. Of the chapters on form, two remain: poetry and the essay — the next chapter looks at how the new literature settles that old account of regulated metre which it had itself written off.
:::
