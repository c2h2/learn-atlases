---
title: "The literary revolution"
---

The first lesson starts from a **change of dynasty**: around 1917, a group of people announced the **abdication of the written language**. What this chapter follows is not 'the new is necessarily good', but **how a coup in the language became a set of institutions, and the new problems literature was left with after the victory**.

## Definition: two revolutions are one resignation {#def-gaige}

::: definition Changing the language = changing the regime {#def-zhengquan}
An event log: **1917.1** Hu Shi 胡适 (Hu Shih), 'Wenxue gailiang chuyi' 《文学改良刍议》 ('A Preliminary Discussion of Literary Reform'), in *New Youth* 《新青年》 — **the eight don'ts** (八不主义) (what is said must have substance; do not imitate the ancients; do not moan without being ill; be sure to get rid of hackneyed airs and stock phrases; do not use allusions; do not insist on parallelism (parallel couplet); do not avoid common characters and colloquial expressions… **a 'refined list of negative clauses'**); **1917.2** Chen Duxiu 陈独秀, 'Wenxue geming lun' 《文学革命论》 ('On the Literary Revolution') — **the three isms** (三大主义) (overthrow aristocratic / classical / mountain-and-forest literature, and build a literature of the common people / of realism / of society — **what Hu Shi opened was a proposal for revision; what Chen Duxiu issued was a revolutionary wanted notice: one essay speaks of 'reform', one of 'revolution', the same year and the same journal — the birth certificate of the new literature is stamped with two kinds of official seal: the mild technical faction and the hard line-faction; for the ten years after that, this pair of partners bit each other all the way and divided the spoils all the way**). **Theoretical gauge-reading (the map of four elements in Lesson 15)**: what the Literary Revolution first took the life of was **the medium between 'world' and 'work'** — **the vernacular (*baihua* 白话) was not a new cart for old goods; it reset the right of way: the rules of who is literate, who runs a newspaper, and who gets into the textbook were rewritten in full**; **'unity of speech and writing'** (言文一致) (speech and writing made one) **in passing cancelled a privilege, two thousand years old, in a 'second language'** — **Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan* 文言) had been the official interface shared by every dialect region (the household-registration system of the 'two births' in Lesson 1); the vernacular's coming out on top was the interface open-sourced: everyone could connect, and everyone, equally, no longer owed it rent**.
:::

::: definition The study of institutional victory {#def-zhidu}
Histories of ideas are fond of speaking of 'the victory of thought'; what institutional history reckons is another set of books: **in 1920 the Ministry of Education issued a general order that the Chinese textbooks of the national schools were to switch to vernacular prose** (语体文) — **a single official sheet did what *New Youth* could not do in ten years: a four-in-one change of track in teaching materials, teaching staff, examinations and printing (the same logic as 'the fixing of the forms of Chinese characters in 1955': the endgame of an event in language has always been a stamp at the administrative window**); **print capital** (the textbooks of the Commercial Press (商务印书馆) and the redesign of *Xiaoshuo yuebao* 《小说月报》 ('Fiction Monthly') — **the publishing market needed a new product 'understandable once one knows the characters': the vernacular was the first literary interface aimed at incremental users**); **the trinity of students returning from abroad + university chairs + the editorial offices of supplements** (**the earliest 'establishment' of the new literature: the journal is the party, the society is the party branch, the polemic is the parliament — the Republican branch of the literary field of Chapter 2 opened for business**). **This lesson's verdict: the victory of the vernacular was not 'easy to understand' defeating 'elegance'; it was** a new infrastructure defeating the old infrastructure — **once the infrastructure is built, the outcome is locked fast: thereafter those who write Classical Chinese may still be masters, but they are no longer reimbursed by any institution**.
:::

::: example The eight don'ts = the photographic negative of level and oblique {#ex-fupian}
Set the 'eight don'ts' side by side with the prohibitive rules of the regulated poem (*lüshi* 律诗) (the drawings of Lessons 13–14 are called back in at this moment): the regulated poem = **a positive construction drawing** (where level, where oblique, where it pairs — executable rules of generation); the eight don'ts = **a negative blacklist** (one is only permitted not to do, and is not told what to do — an 'ethics of prohibitions': one can say 'allusion is not allowed', and cannot say 'what ought to be written'). Please: ① translate 'do not imitate the ancients / be sure to get rid of hackneyed airs and stock phrases' into the 'substitutability test' of Lesson 1, and show that **the two prohibitions are in fact readings on the same instrument**; ② find the structural weak spot of the eight don'ts — **a negative list does not produce works** (use this textbook's old formula: the problem of free verse has never been that it does not rhyme, but who decides what is rhymed); ③ verify the law that 'a blacklist becomes a catchphrase': **the hackneyed phrases the new literature produced within ten years** (an inflation of apostrophe (呼告) on the pattern of '啊！' ('Ah!') and '——哦，我的 X！' ('— Oh, my X!'); the chain of 的 in Europeanised long sentences). For each, give one example sentence you can think of, and explain **why the corpse of a prohibition is the thing most easily turned into a stock phrase**.
::: solution
① **The same instrument**: 'do not imitate the ancients' = running a substitution test on set phrases (**swap out 月落乌啼 ('the moon sets, crows cry') and see whether information is lost: if nothing is lost, it is a stock phrase**); 'be sure to get rid of hackneyed airs and stock phrases' = the version of the test that carries it out — **Hu Shi does not write a prescription; he only issues a thermometer: this is where, in theory, he is more 'modern' than Chen Duxiu (an operable clause vs a declaration of position)**. ② **The weak spot, entered**: a blacklist is a clearing procedure that is **necessary but not sufficient** (**cleaned out is not the same as built up: the eight don'ts cannot govern structure, point of view or rhythm — precisely the parts the new literature of the 1920s lacked most, hence the awkward run of years of 'vernacular fluent, and the fiction still not fit to look at' (the critics of the time, a standing account)**); 'what to do' has to be imported separately (**fiction, spoken drama, the prose poem — forms an 'ism' cannot give, to be supplied by translation — see the next section**). ③ **The law**: **a stock phrase = a rule already disarmed** (the regulated poem's 月 ('moon') and 愁 ('sorrow') are the residue after a rule has worn down; the vernacular's 啊 ('ah') and 心弦 ('heart-strings') are the same: apostrophe is the wholesaling of the 'vacant post' in Chapter 6 — **too many posts, and everyone has a vacancy to sit in, so the seat itself is worth nothing: the term in economics for a hackneyed phrase is 'sign inflation'**); the Europeanised chain of 的, for its part, is **a grammatical tax on the interface** (new vocabulary calls for new clauses, and clauses call for markers — the over-construction of 的: a creative disorder, a grade above the hackneyed phrase, and very soon a hackneyed phrase itself).
:::
:::

::: definition An equipment list of what was brought in {#def-jinkou}
The new literature's stock of forms is **an overseas-shopping list**: **fiction** (Liang Qichao's 梁启超 'new fiction' and Lin Shu's 林纾 **relay translation** had already paved the way; '**欲新一国之民，不可不先新一国之小说**' — '**If one wishes to make new the people of a country, one cannot but first make new the fiction of that country.**' (Our translation.) — Liang Qichao, 《译林序》 ('Preface to *Yilin*', 'Forest of Translations') — **fiction was appointed as the Ministry of Education and not as the Ministry of Entertainment: a re-signing, in 1902, of the 'carrier clause' of Chapter 1**); **spoken drama** (*huaju* 话剧) (**the triple jump from 'civilised drama' (文明戏) to 'aimei drama' (爱美剧, a transliteration of amateur) to professional spoken drama: a genre 'with no classics to rebel against' — the easiest thing to revolutionise: zero stock = zero burden (set beside the lesson on the song lyric (*ci* 词): a new genre always sprouts where an old genre is saturated)**); **free verse** (Whitman brought in by translation — **'the great liberation of poetic form': the problem of metre was temporarily written off rather than solved: the write-off ran for ten years, and in 1926 the Crescent school (新月派) came to the door to collect the bill (Chapter 4)**); **the prose poem** (the line of *Wild Grass* 《野草》 — **'having no form' is also the invention of a form**); **realism** (写实主义) (the line from Zola to Ibsen — **'problem fiction' (问题小说): marriage, education, peasants and workers — the first time literature took 'a hearing on social problems' as its job description (the Republican shift-rota of the firelight in Chapter 2)**). **The key correction: this is not a one-way history of 'Western influence'; it is 'crisis procurement': the old theme-bank (Lesson 14's 'the theme-bank exhausted') + anxiety that the country would perish = a procurement priority decided by survival rather than by taste — which is why realism and problem fiction were chosen, and the sonnet was not: form, too, sets store by emergency aid**.
:::

::: history Ten years from rebellion to the collection {#sec-guanzang}
**1902** Liang Qichao's revolution in the world of fiction (**martial law first, revolution afterwards: fiction's post was changed first, and the post of language afterwards — the order shows that the Literary Revolution was not 'thought arrived first', but 'function arrived first'**); **1915** *New Youth* is founded (**the venue and the microphone are in place**); **1917** the Preliminary Discussion and the essay on the Revolution (**this chapter's home ground**); **1918** 'A Madman's Diary' (《狂人日记》) (**the vernacular comes on with its foremost representative work: the first exhibit of revolutionary literature is already a masterpiece — 'the starting point is the peak' is luck and is also a curse: those who come after start out facing the ceiling**); **1919** the May Fourth Movement (**the student movement pushed the literary issue up onto the municipal front page: the 'infrastructure of public opinion' in *Meizhou pinglun* 《每周评论》 ('Weekly Review') and in the supplements of the papers**); **1920** the general order of the Ministry of Education (**administrative ratification after the fact — the institutional turning point of this chapter**); **1921–22** the Literary Research Association (文学研究会) and the Creation Society (创造社) are founded (**from coterie journals to schools as institutions — the two shop-signs, 'for life' and 'for art': two rounds of bidding, in the Republic, on the 'autonomy' clause of Chapter 2**); **mid-1920s** polemics become the normal state ('Problems and Isms' and 'science and metaphysics' spill over into the literary world: literary journals also run forums for thought — the supplement is the public sphere (Habermas's Shanghai branch)); **1925, the National Language (*guoyu* 国语) Unification Movement deepens — the vernacular turns from 'a literary language' into the full-strength version of 'a national language'**; **a look back in the 1930s**: the new literature begins **to be turned into literary history** (stocktakings, of every kind, of 'the new literature of the past twenty years' (**a revolution retires into bibliography: an advance performance, in the Republic, of Lesson 15's 'the way a school is laid off is by becoming common knowledge'**) — **both sides in the quarrel, in those years, between *Xinchao* 《新潮》 ('New Tide') and *Xueheng* 《学衡》 ('The Critical Review') are now alike taken into vernacular textbooks: the victor amnesties both sides of the civil war as notes**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in the revolutionary narrative
**The lie that 'Classical Chinese is already dead'** (**the Tongguang style (同光体), the Xueheng school and the Jiayin style (甲寅体) of Zhang Shiba 章士钯 stayed active into the 1930s; old-style verse went on being produced under the brushes of Lu Xun 鲁迅, Yu Dafu 郁达夫 and Mao Zedong 毛泽东 — on this lesson's count: Classical Chinese was not killed; it was stopped from being reimbursed: the organ survives, the medical insurance is void**); **argument in place of the work** (**reading 'A Madman's Diary' as an 'anti-feudal manifesto' and passing over the narrative device of the 'persecution maniac' (迫害狂) — the double frame of a preface in Classical Chinese and a diary in the vernacular is precisely the explosion, inside the text, of 'the coup in the medium' in section 1 of this chapter: 'a frame in Classical Chinese = the cover of the case notes; a vernacular main text = the patient's own account: a normal person's format wrapping a stretch of mad speech: form is the verdict'**); **the simplification 'the restore-the-ancients party = reactionary'** (**the Babbitt (白璧德) humanism of the Xueheng school is another route of modernity, not a fossil: a revolutionary narrative needs enemies; the history of scholarship needs the court to sit again**); **the illusion of 'success in one go'** (**in the market in circulation in the 1920s, the sales of the Mandarin Duck and Butterfly school (鸳鸯蝴蝶派) crushed the new literature: the victory of the new literature depended on later ratification by textbooks and by literary history — the bestseller list and the literary-history list have always kept separate accounts: the victor's own time is often the bestseller year of those who were not chosen**).
:::

::: exercise An experiment with a negative list {level=1}
Write an informal essay of 150 characters, **strictly carrying out a modern version of the 'eight don'ts'** (no stock phrases, no moaning without an illness, no allusion, no apostrophe, no three-layer chain of 的, no 仿佛 ('as if') or 宛如 ('just like')); then **rewrite it as your own penalty**: you may use only the forbidden means above to write on the same subject — and compare the two versions' ratio of 'quantity of information / quantity of posture'.
::: solution
Marking: **zero hits on forbidden items** (a chain of 的 counts at three or above — **the executability of a prohibition is precisely Hu Shi's hidden legacy**); **self-estimate the quantity of posture** (once apostrophes of the 啊 ('ah') sort are deleted clean, the facts and judgements that remain are the quantity of information — **the laboratory version of 'do not imitate the ancients': first see how much 'like other people' has been deleted**); the reflection must point out **the legitimate uses of the second version** (parody, the inertia of lyric expression, the revival of the spoken language — **the reverse list of the blacklist is half the stock of rhetoric: in 1926 the Crescent school staged a restoration of 'musicality' on the strength of this half-sheet of the list**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Dissecting a text in two written languages {level=2}
Reread the 'preface + main text' of 'A Madman's Diary': list **the 'narrative contract' of the preface in Classical Chinese and of the vernacular diary, each on its own** (who is guaranteeing what to whom), and answer: **why turning the diary into the 'vernacular' and then wrapping a layer of 'Classical Chinese' round it is fiercer than the pure vernacular** (hint: the three seats set apart in Chapter 5 + the double frame in Chapter 4).
::: solution
Marking: comparison of the contracts (**the preface = the medical / documentary guarantee of a normal person arranging the madman's case notes for him; the diary = first-hand perception with no guarantee put up — 'the two layers of contract pull the platform from under each other: no sooner have you trusted the cover than the contents overthrow the patient; no sooner have you trusted the contents than the cover says he is ill'**); the degree of fierceness (**'救救孩子' ('Save the children.' (Our translation.)) is shouted out from inside the case notes: the legitimacy of the shout has already been judged by the cover to be sick speech — the reader is forced to examine himself: by which contract am I reading? — in the instant of choosing a contract, you have already stepped onto one side of 吃人 ('eating people'), or onto the other: the coup in the medium is folded into the binding of a single book**).
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: what new problems did literature gain after the victory {level=3}
Write 600 to 1,000 characters, assessing '**a revolution in language is a change of regime, and a change of regime never solves literary problems; it only changes the registry of "what counts as a literary problem"**'. You must call on: **the two official seals**, **the theory of institutional reimbursement**, **crisis procurement**, **a blacklist does not produce works**, **the curse of 'the starting point is the peak'** — at least five; and give a ruling: **'does the vernacular owe the old vernacular fiction (*Water Margin* 《水浒》, *Dream of the Red Chamber* 《红楼》) a payment of child maintenance' — the new literature cursed the old literature while financing itself upon it; on whose books does this sum sit**.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**seals = the division of labour between the line and technique; reimbursement = infrastructure locking the outcome fast; procurement = forms ranked by emergency aid; the blacklist = a clearing procedure does not generate structure; the curse of the peak = those who come after start out against the ceiling**); the ruling on child maintenance (**two ledgers: the Hu Shi line — *Water Margin* and *Dream of the Red Chamber* were raised to 'the orthodox line of national-language literature': a genealogy of the vernacular is a financing report the new literature wrote for itself ('pledge the classical as security for a new debt: use the deposit of *Dream of the Red Chamber* to open an account for the vernacular'); the counter-line — once the old vernacular's standard of 'living literature' is in place, every work in Classical Chinese from the Ming and Qing downwards goes automatically onto the roll of inferior students: the child maintenance is in fact deducted from another batch of the inheritance (nine-tenths of the disputes over taxonomy in Chapter 1 are disputes in the study of resources — a replay in the Republic)**); if the conclusion can be written '**the revolution solves "who speaks", and does not solve "what is said": the registry once changed, the queue is lined up as before — only the people jumping it have changed their accent**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Resignation and the founding of the regime, filed: regime (**the medium is the right of way: the interface open-sourced, the rent void**), seals (**the symbiotic infighting of the technical faction and the line faction**), reimbursement (**the official paper of 1920: infrastructure locks the outcome fast**), procurement (**a stock of forms ranked by crisis: not choosing the sonnet was not a mistake; it was the logic of the emergency department**), the curse (**the starting point is the peak + a blacklist does not produce works — the next chapter looks at how the first batch of people set to work under the ceiling, and how a man called Lu Xun exchanged the curse for a key**).
:::
