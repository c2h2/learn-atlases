---
title: "The left-wing movement"
---

The sampling contest of the previous chapter changed tracks in 1930: **fiction no longer asked only "what sort of thing can be written", and began to ask "for whom to write, whose word to follow, and how much the party dues come to"** — this chapter records the whole process by which the literary field was reorganised by politics: **from debate to association, from association to discipline, from discipline to dissolution — a complete organisational life cycle, run through in twenty years**.

## First stop: the debate of 1928 {#def-1928}

::: definition Overthrowing "revolutionary literature" {#def-lunzheng}
**The revolutionary-literature debate of 1928–1929**: one camp, the Creation Society (创造社) and the Sun Society (太阳社), advocated "revolutionary literature" and turned its attack on Lu Xun 鲁迅 and the Literary Research Association (文学研究会) (**the form of words in the charges: "fallen behind" (落伍), "hesitating" (徬徨), "compromising" (妥协) — the first "re-checking of qualifications" inside the new literature: the veteran founders who had started the venture as partners were audited by their partners (Chapter 2: the valuation war at the exchange — "revolution" became the new currency of account, and every old account was revalued)**). **Lu Xun's three briefs in reply**: ① **splitting the accounts of performance and of practice** — to be "**left-wing**" is something one ought to do, not something one **hangs up** (the position, as commonly stated, of essays of the *Qiejieting zawen* 且介亭杂文 kind on the "left wing" (*“左翼”……*): **a political identity is not an insurance policy for a literary form; hanging out a signboard is not performing the contract**); ② **the satirical scene of the "revolutionary coffee shop"** — **'The Revolutionary Coffee Shop' (《革命咖啡店》, the title as commonly cited): the salon crowded with revolutionary writers lacked only revolutionary literature — "高喊与咖啡并举，稿子与子弹两缺" ("Shouting goes hand in hand with coffee; manuscripts and bullets are both lacking.") (Our translation.) (a paraphrase of the satirical logic): a specimen precedent of the splitting of accounts between performance and practice**; ③ **remedial work in translation** — **even as he was denounced as "fallen behind", he took charge of introducing the literary theory of Plekhanov and of Falekesevich 法列克塞维奇 (the Weiming Society, 未名社, and the translation series of *Yishulun* 《艺术论》, 'On Art': half the theoretical weapons his opponents used when they reviled him were stock from his own bookshop — the two sides in the debate drew their textbooks from the same source: this is the paradoxical introduction of "Marxist literary criticism" into China**). **A summary in the form of a verdict: the real product of the debate was not who won, but that the four characters of "revolutionary literature" (革命文学) turned from a slogan into a discipline — in 1930 it needed an organisation, and so there was the League of Left-Wing Writers (左联)**.
:::

## Second stop: the institution of 1930 {#def-zhilian}

::: definition An organisational anatomy of the League {#def-jiepou}
**The League of Left-Wing Writers of China (中国左翼作家联盟; founded in Shanghai on 2 March 1930, and dissolved of its own accord in early 1936)** — an anatomical diagram: **the founding assembly = an opening ceremony + the reading of a programme** (**the grammar of a "writers' league": not a learned society, but a "league": the law on forming associations, and the condition of being underground, determined its form — a party-group system, small-group life, an organ periodical (*Qianshao* 《前哨》, 'Outpost', founded in 1930 and banned as soon as it appeared), and a cluster of bodies headed by the character 联, such as the peripheral League of Social Scientists — **for the first time a literary association grew the full set of internal organs of a party organisation: regular meetings, discipline, a budget, a list of the sacrificed**); **the Five Martyrs of the League, February 1931** (Rou Shi 柔石, Hu Yepin 胡也频, Yin Fu 殷夫, Feng Keng 冯铿 and Li Weisen 李伟森 were killed — **the title of Lu Xun's essay 'Chinese Proletarian Revolutionary Literature and the Blood of the Forerunners' (《中国无产阶级革命文学和前驱的血》) is itself the stance — the common account: this is the "blood of the forerunners" (前驱的血) within the "great extermination in blood" (血的大消灭) — what this case means for the study of organisation: the budget for the first time entered a line under the heading of life: from then on, the negotiation over the autonomy of the literary field had a hostage for a bargaining chip (the bill for the "intervention" clause of Chapter 2 fell due)**); **the slogan stratigraphy of "national literature", "national-defence literature" and "traitor literature" (the mid-1930s)** — **each layer is one redrawing of the line of a united front: slogans are the measuring-rod of the strata, and literary history here reads a geological report rather than reading the works**.
:::

::: definition The "third kind of person" and the property in the study {#def-disan}
**The debate of 1932 over the "third kind of person" (第三种人)** (Du Heng 杜衡 and others proposed that, between the "left wing" and "the dominators", there ought still to be a place for a **third kind of person** — **not counter-revolutionary, but wishing to keep "the right not to take a side": an application for real property, to move the study out of the gun-range of the two great camps**); **the mechanics of the refutation**: the core refutation on the left-wing side was structural — **in a polarised field, the "middle position" is not a coordinate that really exists: it is only the projection cast when neither pole occupies it (a paraphrase of their logic)**; **the legacy of the reply**: what was fought over was not the moral qualification of a third party, but **the boundary of the field's autonomy: whether the literary field can have a "domestic law" of its own** — **the value of this case as precedent: for half a century afterwards, the "third kind of person" became, in criticism in Chinese, both a criminal charge and a kind of nostalgia: those who accuse use it to draw a line, those who long for the past use it to measure the space that was lost — the same word is at once a fence and a memorial portrait (the taxonomy of Chapter 1: what is fought over has always been the right to classify)**.
:::

## Third stop: the interface war of 1934 {#def-jekou}

::: definition Three stages in the advance of popularisation {#def-dazhong}
**The discussion, in the 1930s, of the "popularisation of literature and art" (文艺大众化) = an interface war**: **① the level of genre** (**adopting "forms for the masses": songbooks, storytelling, picture-story books, street theatre — the real-world version of the distribution formats of Chapter 8: the first time the new literature studied the product manual of the "sinking market"; Qu Qiubai's 瞿秋白 radical criticism of "the writing of May Fourth" (the gist): "新文学与劳动群众之间横着一道新的户口门槛——白话革命完成了，白话本身又成了新的户口" ("Between the new literature and the labouring masses there stretched a new household-registration threshold — the revolution of the vernacular (*baihua*) was complete, and the vernacular itself had become a new household registration.") (Our translation.)**); **② the level of signs** (**the Latinised New Writing movement (拉丁化新文字): even the Chinese characters themselves were entered on the list of suspects — the common account: a phonetic script was hoped to be a "barrier-free interface" for workers and peasants — a radical recursion of the "open-source the interface" clause of Chapter 1: the vernacular was not enough; Latin letters were required; this link still echoes in the phonetic-script schemes after 1949**); **③ the level of identity** (**the topic of "the writer's origin and the remoulding of standpoint" was set down in writing for the first time: popularisation slid from "for whom one writes" towards "whom one becomes" — the outline of the scheme of 1942 was already fully written here (a preview of the chapter after next)**). **A register of the result of the battle: the market for popular literature was not taken by the left wing (the Mandarin Duck and Butterfly school, 鸳鸯蝴蝶派, went on selling well — the sequel to Chapter 1's "splitting the accounts of the bestseller list and the literary-history list"), but "the masses" from then on became a **permanent shareholder** in Chinese literature: every later reform of literary form has had to show it a scheme of dividends**.
:::

::: definition Dissolution: end-of-life care for the organisation {#def-jiesan}
**The League dissolved of its own accord in early 1936** (**to make way for a united front of the "national-defence literature" and "association of writers and artists" kind — a liquidation, on the common account, that "stopped operating the right of leadership and exchanged it for a seat in the united front"**); **Lu Xun's reservations on the question of dissolution** (**what he called in question was never alliance, but "alliance" becoming another name for annexation: he wanted a united front, but he did not want the independent accounts inside the united front to be "unified" away**). **An inventory for handing on the legacy: the League dissolved, and the grammar remained — "popularisation", "type (the typical)", "exposure and praise", and "world-view and creative method" were all converted into the official grammar of literature after 1949 (rolled on for issue in Chapters 2 and 9: an organisation died, and its database was purchased by the whole industry)**.
:::

::: example Minutes of an editorial meeting in 1933 (simulated) {#ex-huiyi}
Simulated source material: one entry in the minutes of a regular meeting, in 1933, of the editorial board of a certain left-wing periodical — **item one: formula novels of "revolution + love" are selling well on the city's bookstalls; how to "raise" them; item two: contributors often "replace characters with slogans" (the gist of an editor's note); whether to send the manuscripts back; item three: a submission from a "third kind of person" is very high in literariness; whether to publish it**. Using the tools of this chapter, issue a **handling opinion** on each item, and state **which value each opinion sacrifices** (autonomy / combativeness / literariness / the united front).
::: solution
① **Formula novels** (**the classic situation of two fronts: the market wants sugar, the line wants medicine — a resolution often seen: "以健康的恋爱代替感伤的恋爱" ("Replace sentimental love with healthy love.") (Our translation.) (rewrite the recipe rather than ban the sale): what is sacrificed is precisely the freedom of the individual case under "literariness" — using a type to fight a type: the industrialisation of propaganda and the typology of commerce shake hands here (the embryo of the later "three prominences", 三突出, lies in the editorial board's cost accounting)**); ② **slogan characters** (**the documents of self-correction inside the left wing are enough to make the ox sweat: Lu Xun's famous warning against the "**tendency to turn literature into slogans**" (the points, as commonly stated, of the 1930 lecture 'Opinions on the League of Left-Wing Writers', 《对于左翼作家联盟的意见》, including a commonly circulated paraphrase of the sort "‘左翼’作家首先要是‘左’，其次是‘作家’" ("A 'left-wing' writer must first be 'left', and only then a 'writer'.") (Our translation.)) — the line for sending manuscripts back: sacrifice "quantity of combat", and keep the stock of "literariness": the dilemma has no solution, only a choice; this was the daily round of an editorial board in the 1930s**); ③ **a submission from a "third kind of person"** (**the fiercest item: to publish = to admit that a middle coordinate exists (a self-inflicted theoretical wound); not to publish = to prove oneself "not inclusive" (a self-inflicted moral wound) — in the historical record both sides published such work and also did not: the "correct answer" in this case is of the kind history cannot write: the only honest handling is to print the contradiction on the copyright page of the periodical**).
:::

::: intuition Why an organisation needs literature {#int-zuzhi}
The question turned the other way round is the core of this chapter: **why did politics operate a literary group with such seriousness?** (**The answer does not lie in anything as light as "propaganda value": in the Shanghai of the 1930s, the printing trade, the universities and the newspaper houses made up a modern public space — whoever could, inside it, give names to "justice", "suffering" and "the future" held the equity in the soft infrastructure: literature is the **central clearing house of symbolic capital**; if an organisation is to be listed, it must first take a controlling stake in the clearing house — Bourdieu's (布迪厄) "inverted microcosm" from Chapter 2 is here forcibly "re-embedded": the price of a field's autonomy is that it may be bought out at any time**). **This chapter's symmetrical warning: to tell this history only as a "history of acquisition" is equally lazy — the Five Martyrs, the underground print shops, and the special issues that were sealed up prove it: many people truly believed, were truly poor, and truly died — **belief is an endogenous variable of the field, not the consideration paid for an acquisition; to write only the plot and not the martyrdom, and to write only the martyrdom and not the organisation, are two sides of one and the same blindness**.
:::

::: history Six solar terms of the organisational cycle {#sec-jieqi}
**May 1928–1929**, the revolutionary-literature debate (**a reform of the currency: every old account revalued**); **March 1930**, the League is founded, together with Lu Xun's speech 'Opinions on the League of Left-Wing Writers' (**the points as commonly stated: there must be preparation for a front that is "solid and lasting" (坚实而长久), and new people must be trained: a risk notice posted on the day of opening — on the very day of the speech the shareholders were reminded that this was not a social club**); **February 1931**, the Five Martyrs (**the budget enters a line under the heading of life**); **1931–32**, the debate over the "third kind of person" and the "free men" (自由人) (**the property-rights case of the study comes to court**); **the mid-1930s**, the discussions of the popularisation of literature and art, of Latinisation, and of Putonghua (standard Mandarin) (**the interface war**); **1936**, dissolution and the debate over the "two slogans" (**national-defence literature vs the mass literature of the national revolutionary war — the last civil war before dissolution: a struggle over slogans is a struggle to compare the prices of proposals for a merger**); **1937–45**, the organisation moves (**people of the League, and left-wing grammar, entered Yan'an and Chongqing with the flow of people: the two trunk lines of Chapters 8 and 10 fork here — "one dissolution, branch offices in two places"**); **after 1949**, the grammar is regularised (**"popularisation" becomes "the direction", "the typical" becomes a clause in the textbooks, "exposure and praise" becomes a standing committee — the start-up team of 1930 found, in the articles of association of the company of 1949, the original wording of every clause of its own**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of left-wing literature
**Having to choose either a "dark history" or a "glorious history"** (**this chapter's way of reading is double-track bookkeeping in the sociology of organisations and the sociology of literature: to enter martyrdom in the ledger of plots, or to enter the buy-out in a hagiography, is in either case a false account**); **blaming "formulaic writing" on an individual** (**slogan characters are not a matter of some editor's taste; they are the rational output under the dual KPI of "combativeness × literariness": for a structural problem look to the structure, and do not look for a scapegoat (the list of side-effects of the "distribution formats" in Chapter 8)**); **ignoring the parallel data in the right-hand column** (**on the bestseller lists of the same period stood martial-arts novels and romance serials: the anxiety of the discussion of "popularisation" came precisely from "the masses are not reading our literature" — the gap between the movement and the market is the real plot (Chapter 1: the ground-colour, in the study of resources, of the taxonomy)**); **treating 1942 as a sudden change** (**the three-piece set "popularisation — exposure and praise — the remoulding of origin" was already complete in the 1930s (the three-stage advance of this chapter); 1942 is the assembly finished, not an air-drop — the other face of a history of effects: a chronology must also be drawn up for the "causes" (Chapter 9)**).
:::

Arrange "**革命文学—普洛文学—国防文学—民族革命战争的大众文学—人民文学**" (revolutionary literature — proletarian literature — national-defence literature — the mass literature of the national revolutionary war — people's literature) as a columnar stratigraphic diagram; **on each slogan mark three things: the date, the camp that proposed it, and the old slogan it eliminated**; and answer: **what the frequency with which slogans were replaced shows** (hint: slower than a change of regime, faster than a periodical's change of name).
::: solution
Marking: **the order and the dates basically accurate**; **the relations of elimination clear** (each new slogan calls itself a "broader alliance" — **inclusiveness increases, precision decreases: the semantic tax of the united front**); the form of the conclusion ("Slogans are geological strata, not the weather: each layer covers the one before, but when a core is taken from a borehole every layer is still there — from the borehole of 'the people' after 1949 one can still extract the sand grains of 'revolution' from 1928").
:::

Marking points: the five mechanisms (**currency reform = the politicisation of the measure of value; anatomy = an institutional cession of the field's autonomy; the property-rights case = a debate, set down in writing, on the boundary of autonomy; the three stages of the interface = a recursion from genre to sign to identity; regularisation = the death of the organisation and the permanence of the grammar**); the verdict (**the fall account: the "domestic law" of the field was confiscated, and the editorial line for sending manuscripts back is the limit-down of literariness; the victory account: use the institutional account of Chapter 1 as a counter-proof — "literature" has never had an essence beyond history; the left wing only added one new clause to "literature" ("the masses" as a permanent shareholder), and this clause has still not been repealed (textbooks, prizes, grants and trending searches all pay dividends according to it) — **the key to the mark is whether one admits that "the definition of literature" was itself an event in this movement's contest**); if the conclusion can say "**an organisation may dissolve; grammar only retires: what was founded in 1930 was not some school, but a whole editorial office that is still at work today — we all submit to it, and have only forgotten who opened it**", that is an A.

::: summary
The six solar terms, filed: the debate (**revolution becomes the currency of account: the veteran founders are audited**), the institution (**the association grows the internal organs of a party: regular meetings, a budget, a list — the negotiation over autonomy has gained a hostage for a chip**), the property-rights case (**the middle position is a projection: one word is at once a fence and a memorial portrait**), the interface (**a three-stage recursion from genre to sign to identity: the masses become a permanent shareholder**), dissolution (**stop operating the right of leadership, in exchange for a seat: the database is purchased by the whole industry**). The method in one sentence: **look up the organisational table first, then the table of titles, and last the roll of names — only when the three tables line up is this stretch of history there**. The war has already begun. The next chapter: the exile of literature, the move inland, and the base areas.
:::
