---
title: "The new poetry"
---

The previous chapter said the *zawen* (polemical essay) is a reflex arc; this chapter looks at another nerve: **poetry's self-rescue** — the whole plot of forty years of new poetry (modern verse), from 1917 to 1949, can be compressed into one debt dispute: **after prosody was written off, to whom was the bill for rhythm sent?**

## Act One: The overdraft of freedom {#def-touzhi}

::: definition Letting go, and the drift into prose {#def-fangshou}
The programme of new poetry in 1917–1925 was **subtraction** (“诗体的大解放” ('the great liberation of poetic form') — level and oblique tones (level and deflected), rhyme, the number of characters, and parallelism (parallel couplets) sent back item by item: the poetry edition of the blacklist in Chapter 1); **the honest embarrassment of Hu Shi (Hu Shih)'s *Experiments* (*Changshi ji* 尝试集)** (he himself confessed that the old-style ground would not wash clean, and that where it had been washed it looked too much like prose set out in lines — **the merit of opening the mountain and the unsightliness of opening the mountain are one body: the value of the experimental record as historical material is higher than its value in the history of poetry**); the lesion is entered in the register: **“the boundary between poetry and prose has disappeared” = an overdraft on “freedom”** — **when every constraint has left the stage, what remains is only lyric prose in a rhyme-cancelled edition: clear, fluent, and all visible at a glance — and being all visible at a glance is precisely poetry's natural enemy (Chapter 6: suspension is a load-bearing structure — tear the suspension out, and the poem moves into expository prose)**. **Theory pays the fare afterwards**: the early poets of the new poetry discovered that **freedom is not the absence of prosody, but the downward shift of the power to set prosody** — the modern clause that “**the cadence of a new poem has to be generated by the poem itself**” was not formally proposed by anyone until 1926 (the next act); **the true identity of these ten years: not a batch of bad poems, but an idling experiment for which tuition had to be paid — without the extreme test of “no prosody of any kind”, one would not have known which constraints are load-bearing walls** (the blueprint method of Chapter 4, applied at large scale in the history of genres).
:::

## Act Two: The prosody petitioners {#def-qingyuan}

::: definition The three beauties, and cutting the clothes to fit {#def-sanmei}
**The 1926 proposal of the Crescent Moon school, 'The Prosody of Poetry'** (Wen Yiduo) — **“the three beauties of poetry”: the beauty of music (cadence), the beauty of painting (diction), the beauty of architecture (the symmetry of stanzas and the evenness of lines) — the first “positive working drawing” in the history of the new literature** (set against the previous chapter: the eight “nots” are a blacklist; the three beauties are a blueprint: an internal reversal inside the same revolution: in the period of destruction one issues a ban on smoking; in the period of construction one issues a code of building methods); **the received position on “戴着镣铐跳舞” ('dancing in shackles')** — **self-limitation is not oppression but a source of energy: “the more drive one has, the more gladly one limits oneself” (the received view): the exchange rate between shackles and dance-steps = the interest rate of art (the “prohibitions are the marking criteria” of the regulated poem (*lüshi*) in Lesson 3 is rehabilitated here: form is music on deferred delivery — new poetry had been in debt for only two years when it began to miss the interest)**; **the revolutionary character of the proposal lies in “cutting the clothes to fit”** (**prosody is not a restoration of the ancestral house of the Pingshui rhymes; it is measuring each poem and making it a new suit of clothes: the symmetry of the stanza and the neat regularity of the line are decided by the poem itself — “autonomous prosody”: classical prosody is public infrastructure (the Pingshui rhymes = Putonghua (standard Mandarin) + a rhyme dictionary); the new prosody is a bespoke suit: the same tailor does not take two orders of the same cut**); **the grafting works of the sound-measure** (the theory of the “sound-measure”, *yīnchǐ* 音尺, running from Sun Dayu to Wen Yiduo — **translating the English foot into the “sound-measure”, and using the pause in place of the foot (*dùn* 顿, 'pause'): four sound-measures to a line, three “pauses”… — a mechanics report on transplantation across language-systems: Chinese has no stress accent, so the exchange rate is made up out of pauses and character-counts (the modern reissue of “using the pause in place of the foot” from Lesson 1 — from the two-beat rhythm of the *Book of Songs* to the sonnet, the rhythmic unit of Chinese has all along been the “pause” and not the “foot”: where the graft fails, the nature of the rootstock shows most clearly**).
:::

::: definition The mechanics of two samples {#def-yangben}
**'Saying Goodbye to Cambridge Again' (Xu Zhimo)** — “**轻轻的我走了，正如我轻轻的来**” ('Softly I go, just as softly I came.') (Our translation.) (the slow tempo of reduplicated words, the long sounding of the *ang* rhyme — **a substitute brake under the total absence of the entering-tone (checked-tone) brake (retrieved from the lesson on level and oblique tones: with no coda that shuts off short, one slows down by alliteration, rhyming reduplication, and the four-character pause: the brake is changed from a mechanical one to regenerative braking**); “**夏虫也为我沉默，沉默是今晚的康桥**” ('Even the summer insects are silent for me; silence is Cambridge tonight.') (Our translation.) (**anadiplosis (*dingzhen* 顶针): the object of the previous clause becomes the subject of the next — a figure of speech promoted into a structure: between the sentences, as with a lotus root, the break still leaves the threads joined — the path by which “the beauty of music” is realised in Chinese is not stress, but a relayed recurrence of sound**); **'The Rainy Alley' (Dai Wangshu)** — **“悠长、悠长／又寂寥的雨巷”** ('Long, long / and lonely, the rainy alley.') (Our translation.) (reduplication + the prolonged *ang* rhyme + the gasp across the line-break: prosody obeys “the temperature curve of emotion” rather than a metronome (inside the Crescent Moon school of the same period there was already the jibe of “词费” ('a waste of words') — **a famous dispute over routes left a scratch here**; the mechanism: **for the first time the rhythm of new poetry is no longer even, but “now long, now short, following the sigh”: the constant-speed treadmill of the regulated poem is changed into a mountain hike — where each step lands is given by the terrain**); **the verdict of reading the two together: the Crescent Moon school had already forked — one branch towards the tailor of Western suits (architectural beauty), one branch listening inwards to the breath (the dissolving of musical beauty): one route was carrying the next act**.
:::

## Act Three: The Modernist school and Bian Zhilin's magic cube {#def-xiandai}

::: definition The modern equipment of emotion and perception {#def-zhuangbei}
**In 1932 the magazine *Xiandai* ('The Modern') hung out its sign** (edited by Shi Zhecun — **the received programme, “modern life is exposed in the modern poem”: the loneliness of the city, the sediment of memory, the windings of desire — the object of lyric feeling changes from a night scene in the countryside to a cinema of the inner mind (**the traditional lyric stock — moon, willow, a lone sail — finds that inflation no longer works in the modern city: write Shanghai, and one cannot write “孤帆远影” ('a lone sail, a distant shadow'): the image-bank needs a bankruptcy and a restructuring); the legacy of Li Jinfa's early Symbolist school ((the “seeping-in” kind of metaphor along the line of Li Jinfa's 'The Abandoned Woman' — take only the mechanism: the cross-domain grafting of metaphor, and the dislocation of allusion (Chinese and Western vocabularies hard-welded together) — a premature sample, in the China of the 1920s, of a New-Critical kind of “defamiliarisation”: the unpleasant truth first: this school was destined for a small audience — the Modernist school is a retraining centre for demobilised veterans of prosody); Bian Zhilin's 'Fragment' — “你站在桥上看风景，看风景的人在楼上看你。／明月装饰了你的窗子，你装饰了别人的梦。” ('You stand on the bridge looking at the view; the person looking at the view looks at you from the upper floor. / The bright moon decorates your window; you decorate someone else's dream.') (Our translation.) — four lines = an engine for the study of focalisation (Chapter 5 rehires Genette onto the site: the viewpoints of the one who looks and the one who is looked at rotate; the verb-study of “decorate” as a double catalyst; the nesting of dream inside dream — the optimal compression of a short poem: twenty-odd characters run through a whole class of narratology (Bian Zhilin's lifelong keyword, “distance”, is here an automatic gearbox: the aesthetic is the management of distance); the early 'Prophecy' (He Qifang), and Bian Zhilin's doctrine of “distance” — the Modernist school contributed to Chinese poetry one subject, “the technique of perception” (before this, lyric poetry had “feeling” but no “inventory of perceptual equipment”).
:::

## Act Four: Settling the account of breath {#def-huxi}

::: definition Ai Qing: from the beauty of music to the beauty of breath {#def-huxi2}
**1938, 'I Love This Land'** — “**为什么我的眼里常含泪水？因为我对这土地爱得深沉……**” ('Why are there so often tears in my eyes? Because I love this land so deeply…') (Our translation.) — **a patent of new poetry on the hypophora pattern: free verse exchanges “the constant speed of prosody” for “the acceleration of rhetoric” (parallelism, repetition, apostrophe — the “vacant posts” of Chapter 6 are at full wartime strength: mobilising feeling needs a loudhailer, not a bespoke suit)**; **the received view of Ai Qing's *On Poetry* (*Shilun* 诗论): the prose-beauty of poetry, and “poetry is a free language” — the beauty of breath takes the baton from the beauty of music (the long sentence, the line-break, the grit of the colloquial — prosification in form is exchanged for urgency in content: the “lesion” of Act One is remodelled into a “weapon”: the same beauty of prose was a disease in the 1920s and a medicine in 1938 — a literary-historical experiment in dosage and occasion)**; **the revival of recitation = the recolonisation of hearing** (**street poetry of the War of Resistance, and the recitation-poetry movement: poetry returns to sound — but the identity in which it returns is that of the square, not of the banquet: the format of distribution has changed (Chapter 8: genre is a distribution channel — from “literature of the hand” (close reading in print) to “literature of the mouth” (hearing in the square): new poetry completes its secret return to an ancient tradition of Chinese poetics — song and poem as one, and, after a long way round, back to the overflow formula of the Great Preface to the *Book of Songs***).
:::

::: definition The Nine Leaves: the statement of account on settlement day {#def-jiuye}
**The proposal, in the 1940s and around the Southwest Associated University, of the “dramatisation of new poetry”** (the received view of Tang Shi, Yuan Kejia and others: **“不直接说” ('do not say it directly') — scene, aside, the objective correlative: Ai Qing's loudhailer is changed back into a chamber play; Mu Dan's 'Praise'** (**the repetition of “一个民族已经起来”** ('a nation has already risen') (Our translation.): apostrophe is folded into the refrain of the structure — **the dilemmas of lyric feeling and intellection, of the person and the nation, are no longer smoothed flat by a slogan, but hung up on an apparatus of tension**) ) — **“the fusion of the sensuous and the intellectual” (the received view): between Act One and Act Four, new poetry supplies a third choice: neither even, nor shouting, but leaving the contradiction on the stage**. The general ledger of the four acts (this lesson's verdict): **forty years of new poetry = the complete record of a migration of constraints — level and oblique tones abdicate → the blacklist idles → the sound-measure is grafted → breath settles the account → dramatisation reconciles the books: the conclusion is not “did the Crescent Moon school win, or did Ai Qing”, but “freedom has always been more expensive than it thought, and prosody has always been wider than it thought” — the downward shift of the power to legislate is irreversible, but every act of legislating has to publish its budget (what to pledge, what to abandon, and from whom to collect the interest)**.
:::

::: example 'Saying Goodbye to Cambridge Again' and 'I Love This Land': distribution formats compared {#ex-duizhao}
List each of the two poems under five indicators: **① the place of lyric feeling (where the “I” is), ② the rhythmic mechanism (constant speed / breath / the square), ③ the stock of images (a private holding / the public store), ④ apostrophe and the vacant post (who the “you” is), ⑤ the distribution channel (close reading / recitation / the textbook)**; and a final verdict: **'Saying Goodbye' is co-opted by the textbook as a “famous piece of tender feeling”, and 'This Land' as a “famous patriotic piece” — what device of the original did each co-option smooth away?**
::: solution
① **The place of lyric feeling**: the “I” of 'Saying Goodbye' is inside the scene, and **inside the posture of taking leave of the scene** (the leave-taking itself is the protagonist, and Cambridge is the set — “**a sample of the privatisation of the modern lyric subject: even the sorrow is light, because the weight belongs to someone else**”); the “I” of 'This Land' is a bird — **the return of the “hypothetical mask” (the theory of posts in Chapter 6: the lyric “I” is a post that can be lent on, and Ai Qing lends it to “the bird on the land”)**; ② **rhythm**: regenerative braking (reduplication, the *ang* rhyme) against rhetorical acceleration (hypophora, parallelism); ③ **images**: 金柳, 青荇, 康河 ('golden willows', 'green water-weeds', 'the River Cam'; a privately held travel album) against the land, the rivers, the wind, the dawn (**the public store's set of four: requisitioned over and over, and growing heavier for it: the weight of a word is decided by the history of its use — what Ai Qing bets on is the compound interest of public words**); ④ **the you-post**: 'Saying Goodbye' has “作别西天的云彩” ('taking leave of the clouds in the western sky') (Our translation.) (**you = the scenery: the vacant post has been fitted with a camera**); 'This Land' has no “you”, only “这土地” ('this land') (**you = the readers as a body: at the recitation, the whole staff takes up its posts**); ⑤ **channel**: 'Saying Goodbye' was born in the *Crescent Moon* magazine and in classroom chanting; 'This Land' was born in periodicals of the War of Resistance and in the square; ⑥ **the list of what is smoothed away**: when 'Saying Goodbye' is read as “graceful”, its **experiment in architectural beauty** (the sound-measure, the symmetry of stanzas) is cut away — **it becomes a specimen of rhetoric rather than a sample of mechanics**; when 'This Land' is read as “patriotic”, its **prose-beauty and rhythm of breath** (the stagger of the long sentence = the acoustics of a sob) is cut away — **it becomes a slogan rather than a poem: what the two co-options share is this: “how it was written” is deleted, and only “what was written” is kept — a textbook-grade negative instance of the alloy theory of content and form (the sawn section of Chapter 4: once it has been sawn through, only two lengths of timber are left)**.
:::
:::

::: intuition New poetry is an unfinished case {#int-anjian}
The stocktake of 1949: new poetry **has not closed the case**. The architectural experiment of the three beauties has stopped in a few hands; the breath school has won distribution, but owes a debt to close reading; the Modernist school's perceptual equipment has only just been unwrapped — **“the problem of new poetry has never been a problem of poets; it is a problem of infrastructure: the public prosody has been dismantled, and the new one has not yet been laid (Chapter 1: the exercise in classification is forever being done again)”**. **This lesson's counter-intuitive conclusion: what most resembles a success of new poetry is not any one poem, but “the continuous trial and error itself”: three generations of constitution in four years; in every generation someone wrote good poems — the immature period of a genre is precisely the period of the experimental dividend (for comparison: once the regulated poem has settled its form, genius can only do interior decoration within the station: Du Fu's *chényù* 沉郁 ('sombreness and density') = extreme sport at the limit of prosody; Ai Qing and Mu Dan, in the new poetry, = orienteering in the wild: the results are not good, but the map is one they drew)**.
:::

::: history Forty years, four acts {#sec-simu}
**1917–1920**, the period of attempts (*Experiments* — **the form of the experimental record**); **1921**, *The Goddesses* (**the detonation of the Romantic school: free verse offsets “technique” with “energy” — “Whitman's dividend in Chinese”: the form of the poem = the shape of the crater — the technique account is left pending**); **1923–25**, the rise and the ebb of the short-poem form (**the three-line philosophical short poems of *A Maze of Stars* (*Fanxing* 繁星) and *Spring Water* (*Chunshui* 春水): Tagore reincarnated in Chinese — an experiment in the shortest form: one sentence to a line, the typography of the maxim; the ebb of the craze proves, at the same time, that if it is short and has no tension, it turns into a text message**); **1926**, 'The Prosody of Poetry' (**the curtain rises on Act Two**); **1928–32**, the golden age of the Crescent Moon school, plus *Xiandai* hanging out its sign (**the tailor and the psychologist practising in the same building**); **the 1930s**, the Modernist school deepens, plus the dispute in the Chinese Poetry Society over the “mass singing group” (**the debate on “the popularisation of poetry”: the first call for tenders for recitation and the ballad form — the forerunner of the Ai Qing line**); **1937–45**, reorganisation by the war (**recitation poetry, street poetry, “诗到前线去” ('poetry to the front'): a coup in the format of distribution — Chapter 8: the channel determines the genre — the biggest red envelope the War of Resistance issued to new poetry was the square: it let poetry be heard by people again, at the cost that from then on poetry remembers the volume of the square**); **1938**, 'I Love This Land' and 'Snow Falls on China's Land'; **1945–49**, the Nine Leaves (*Jiuye*) and the “dramatisation of new poetry”, and Feng Zhi's *Sonnets* (*Shisihang ji* 十四行集) (**“the coming of age, in Chinese, of the sonnet: Rilke's Chinese pupil, plus a variant of Du Fu — the strictest imported prosody wrote, in Chinese, a poetry of contemplation: proof that the nationality of the shackles does not matter; what matters is who wears them”**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading new poetry
**“New poetry = it does not look like poetry”** (**examining *Experiments* by the acceptance line for Tang poetry = measuring free form with the regulated-verse ruler: fix the genre first, and only then does the charge have grounds (Chapter 1) — the right question to ask: what law is this poem laying down for itself, and how does it enforce it**); **the oversimplification “Crescent Moon = a return to the ancients”** (**the three beauties are made to measure, not the ancestral house: to read the theory of prosody as a royalist party is to miss the radical kernel of “cutting the clothes to fit” — a proposal for the democratisation of prosody**); **the reading “political poetry = slogans”** (**the stagger of the long sentences in 'I Love This Land' is design, not a slip: a way of quoting that cites only the concluding sentence is an accomplice to this chapter's case of “co-option that smooths away”**); **the laziness of the “theory that new poetry failed”** (**“new poetry has not written a famous piece that will pass down the ages”: a famous piece is a unit of the history of effects (Chapter 9: distance in time is a filter) — three changes of constitution in forty years, and still samples of the grade of 'The Rainy Alley', 'Fragment' and 'Praise'; the experimental results should be entered on the record, not in a written judgement**).
:::

::: exercise The sound-measure laboratory {level=1}
Arrange “**我在黄昏的渡口等一条不会来的船**” ('At the ferry, in the dusk, I wait for a boat that will not come.') (Our translation.) once under each of three schemes: ① an even form in four sound-measures (imitating the build of 'Dead Water'); ② a breath form (imitating Ai Qing's long sentences and line-breaks); ③ a short-poem form of focalisation (split into four lines, imitating the change of viewpoint in 'Fragment'); **attach a reading of 60 characters: what each of the three arrangements has turned the waiting into**.
::: solution
Marking: ① evenness (**waiting is uniformed into a guard of honour: even disappointment must queue — the cost of architectural beauty is being made respectable**); ② the line-break (**the three characters “不会来” ('will not come') are flung to the head of the line — the physics of suspense: the break makes the coming-to-nothing arrive early**); ③ the change of position (**“船也在等什么人吗” ('Is the boat, too, waiting for someone?') (Our translation.) — the title to the waiting is transferred to the object — the manual gear of the 'Fragment' machine**); the format of the reading (of the grade “form = different testimonies to the same event”).
:::

::: exercise Detecting what has been smoothed away {level=2}
Choose a poem that a textbook has “thematised” ('Saying Goodbye', 'The Rainy Alley', 'I Believe in the Future', and so on, will all do); **write out two lists for it, a “textbook label” and an “inventory of devices”**, and compare, item by item, what the label has deleted; then, from the inventory, write this poem one **new label** (no more than 12 characters, and adjectives of feeling are forbidden).
::: solution
Marking: **the inventory must be checkable** (the sound-measure, anadiplosis, the line-break, hypophora, the place of the rhyme — **each item must be locatable by recitation**); **a tally of the deleted items** (the label usually keeps “what was written” and deletes “how it was written” — **the alloy theory enforced on the spot**); sample new labels ('The Rainy Alley' = “一条用延音铺成的窄路” ('a narrow road paved with prolonged sound'); 'Saying Goodbye' = “轻的力学” ('the mechanics of lightness') — **labels in verbs and nouns score; labels in adjectives have marks deducted**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the bill for freedom {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing “**forty years of new poetry proved this: cancelling prosody is easy, and cancelling the need for prosody is hard — rhythm is poetry's obligation to breathe; the question has never been whether or not one wants constraints, but who sets the constraints, and to whom the interest is paid**”. You must call on at least these five: **the blacklist idling (the tuition of Act One)**, **cutting the clothes to fit**, **the exchange-rate problem of using the pause in place of the foot**, **the beauty of breath (dosage and occasion)**, **the coup in the format of distribution**; and give a verdict: **in 1949 new poetry had “not closed the case” — if the hearing were continued by you, the first witness you should call is which poem, of which act?**
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**idling = a survey of the load-bearing walls; cutting the clothes = the democratisation of prosody; the exchange rate = the local loss in transplantation across language-systems; breath = the economics of occasion in form; the coup = the channel rewriting the genre**); the verdict on a continued hearing (**an open question; the marks lie in the argument for “standing to testify”: if one chooses 'Dead Water' — “its appearance in court can answer, at the same time, the feasibility and the limit of Act Two: the peak of the even form is the point at which its testimony starts to fail — the summit of one poem is used to demonstrate the ceiling of a route ('a perfectly fulfilled sentence of death')”; if one chooses 'Fragment' — “twenty-odd characters prove that freedom and constraint can be reconciled inside the smallest unit: it is a sample of the implementing rules for the clause on 'the downward shift of the power to legislate'” — choose any one poem from the four acts; so long as one argues that it pulls on the accounts of the other three, give it a high mark**); if the conclusion writes “**prosody is poetry outsourcing its breath to tradition, and freedom is taking it back to run on its own account — running it oneself is not easy: from then on every heartbeat has to answer for itself, and to keep its own books: this is the freedom that new poetry gave to the Chinese language, and that has still not been paid off**”, that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The four acts filed: Act One (**the blacklist idling: without the extreme test of “wanting nothing at all”, one does not know which things are load-bearing walls**), Act Two (**cutting the clothes: prosody is changed from the ancestral house to bespoke — a proposal of democratisation; the exchange-rate loss of the sound-measure**), Act Three (**perceptual equipment: the Modernist school supplies the technical subject of “how one feels”**), Act Four (**breath and the square: prose-beauty turns from a lesion into a weapon; the coup in distribution brings poetry back to the Great Preface**), the general ledger (**“freedom is more expensive than it thought, and prosody is wider than it thought”**). The method in one sentence: **ask what law this poem has laid down for itself, then ask whether it enforces that law fairly**. The chapter on the essay sets out tomorrow: the modern essay — the genre in the new literature that matured earliest, and that is best at disguising itself as “just writing offhand”.
:::
