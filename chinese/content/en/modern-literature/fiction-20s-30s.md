---
title: "Fiction of the 1920s and 1930s"
---

The essay, in the previous chapter, was issued a thin licence; this chapter looks at how fiction **tries on outer garments by the batch**. Across the seventeen years 1920–1937, vernacular fiction sampled, in a single pass, the question of "what it was possible to write it as" — **problem fiction, the autobiographical narrative, native-soil writing, the psychological, the metropolitan, the marketplace, the family, and social analysis: eight patterns cut from one bolt of cloth**.

## A general ledger of the patterns: a sampling experiment by one generation {#def-banxing}

::: definition Two shop signs and one misreading {#def-dianzhao}
**The Literary Research Association of 1921** (literature "for life" — **testimony of blood and tears, taken in an interview on the spot: the Association's "for life" is not a slogan but a form filled in on taking the post: realism, exposure, the reflecting of social phenomena (the gist of the aims of the reform issue of *Short Story Monthly*, 《小说月报》)**) versus **the Creation Society of 1921** (the romantic self "for art" — **companions who had studied abroad, a coterie journal, a start made in polemic: a society = a start-up company, a shop sign = a business plan for raising funds (the Republican roadshow of the exchange in Chapter 2)**). **The key correction: the "opposition" of these two shop signs has been exaggerated by later ages — the party of "for art" shouted for national salvation just the same (the volcano of *The Goddesses*, 《女神》, does not stand idle), and the party of "for life" also spoke of the autonomy of art; the real divergence lies in how the "self" is entered in the books: one treats the "I" as a sampling point for a social problem, the other takes the "I" directly as the work — the quarrel of the shop signs is in fact a quarrel of bookkeeping methods (Chapter 8: the position of style)**.
:::

::: definition Four methods of sampling {#def-quyang}
**① Problem fiction (Bing Xin 冰心)** — "**prescription literature that asks after the illness and does not give the medicine**": it writes the difficulties of women's education, of the family, of war, and of "love" — **each piece has a consultation sheet attached, and the prescription is often left with nothing but "love" (once the "problem" has been posed, the mission is complete: this is the honest version of an enlightenment mode of writing: the doctor first proves that he too is among the sick)**; **② the autobiographical narrative (Yu Dafu 郁达夫, *Sinking*, 《沉沦》, 1921)** — **writing the "not respectable" as evidence**: sexual anguish, melancholy, and the shame of a subject of a weak country are entered on one account inside the same stretch of monologue — "**the humiliation of the person and the humiliation of the nation share a single sentence (the gist of the cry of the protagonist of *Sinking*): the lethality of the I-novel (*sīxiǎoshuō* 私小说) lies not in exposure but in the giving of evidence: so it turns out that 'shame' can have a table of social composition**"; **③ native-soil writing (the successors of Luzhen 鲁镇: Wang Luyan 王鲁彦, Tai Jingnong 台静农, Peng Jiahuang 彭家煌)** — "**nostalgia under a sojourner's pen is the interest on distance**": a desk in Beijing looks down on the country towns of Shaoxing and of Taizhou — "**'sketching from life in quiet indifference', and a sorrow to which no ornament has been added (the gist of Lu Xun's 鲁迅 verdict, in his introduction to the second collection of fiction, 《小说二集》, on one native-soil writer): the structure of the onlooker is replaced by the structure of the account book — the old home is no longer gawked at; it is taken in inventory**"; **④ the new psychological mode (Shi Zhecun 施蛰存, 'An Evening of Plum Rain', 《梅雨之夕》, and 'The General's Head', 《将军底头》, 1932–33)** — **showing Freud in, to Hangzhou and to the Tang: the psychological montage of a chance meeting, and classical reworkings of incest and of patricide — "a Chinese visa for psychological analysis: not a report that interprets dreams, but the instant at which 'the customs house of reason is smuggled through by the unconscious'**".
:::

## The metropolis, the marketplace and the family: three urban grammars {#def-chengshi}

::: definition The New Sensation school: fiction turned into jazz {#def-jushue}
**Liu Na'ou 刘呐鸥, Mu Shiying 穆时英, and Shi Zhecun (in part) — the Shanghai branch (the *Xiandai* line, 《现代》, in the 1930s)**: **Mu Shiying's 'Five People in a Nightclub', 《夜总会里的五个人》 — a bombardment of numbers and colours, montage cutting, film storyboards entering the prose (a retelling in words coined here: five people, each carrying a stretch of despair, walk into the nightclub: the city uses noise to anaesthetise the sleepless — 'fiction turned into jazz': the sentence takes on shutter speed and the colour temperature of neon)**; — **the metropolis, as a new species, becomes the protagonist for the first time: before this, the city in fiction was scenery (the mirror image of Luzhen); here the city is the narrator itself (the politics of the line of sight, of the street and of the department store — "the manner of looking is marked with its price in plain figures: the New Sensation school = a Shanghai branch shop of the Paris arcades in Benjamin's manner)**". **Lao She 老舍: upgrading the speech of Beijing from a dialect to a literary language** — the "parabola of the bankruptcy of individual struggle" in *Rickshaw Boy* (*Camel Xiangzi*, 《骆驼祥子》, 1936): an account-book structure of three rises and three falls (buying the rickshaw — losing it — saving again — losing it again — the novel uses addition and subtraction to work a mathematical proof: when the institutional parameters are unchanged, the convergence value of the effort variable is zero; an opening that stacks attributives in the manner of “体面的，要强的，好梦想的” ('respectable, bent on bettering himself, fond of fine dreams') (Our translation.) — humour in the Beijing flavour (*jīngwèi* 京味) is a cushion, not an anaesthetic: one watches, laughing, as a person is deducted until only the shell of an "individual" is left); the "lyrical study of poverty" in 'Crescent Moon', 《月牙儿》 (the crescent as an image carried all the way through: it does not accuse; it only lights a thing into view — the standing-down of the apostrophe of Chapter 6). Ba Jin 巴金, *Family* (《家》, 1931): the flame of accusation and a call to action — according to the formulation commonly given for the afterword to *Family*: “我写《家》的时候，我仿佛在跟一些人一同受苦，一同在魔爪下面挣扎” ('When I wrote *Family*, I seemed to be suffering together with some people, and struggling together beneath a demon's claws.') (Our translation.): an elegy for youth, and a lashing of "bowing-ism" (Juexin's 觉新 "bowing-ism" and "non-resistance-ism" are the names fixed in the book); a nuclear-grade case in the history of effects: how many readers walked out because of *Family* — empirical proof of a novel as a call to action (Chapter 9: a Manchu–Han banquet of intention ≠ achievement ≠ consequence).
:::

::: definition The flagship of social analysis: Mao Dun's *Midnight* {#def-ziye}
***Midnight* (《子夜》, 1933) — the flagship of the "novel of social analysis"**: the iron grip of the national capitalist Wu Sunfu 吴荪甫 crashes under a three-sided assault of "imperialist capital + comprador finance + rural bankruptcy" (**the duel of long and short on the government-bond market = the climax of the novel: letting share quotations become plot — the maiden voyage, in China, of economics as the dynamics of narrative**); **the method: write the "sensor" of Chapter 2 out as a panoramic model: Wu Sunfu is not a villain and not a hero; he is the graph of the function "the Chinese national bourgeoisie of the 1930s" (a typical character in a typical environment — the most earnest executor of Engels's clause)**; **its cost is equally clear: where the idea comes first, the characters go mute (the opening parable of Old Mr Wu, 吴老太爷, entering the city, the "feudal zombie" — the density of symbols presses down the texture of life: the strength and the weakness of social analysis take the stage together — the later case file of the "theme-first" illness already has its CT film here)**.
:::

::: example Three "entering the city" scenes set side by side {#ex-jincheng}
Three entries into the city by characters in fiction, each standing for one urban grammar: **① Old Mr Wu enters the city (the opening of *Midnight*: "scared to death" by neon lights and motor cars)**; ② **Xiangzi 祥子 enters the city (a young man from the countryside comes to Beiping to buy a rickshaw: the body as a tool of production)**; ③ **Granny Liu 刘姥姥 enters the Grand View Garden (an old case from Chapter 5: an internal "country" enters an internal "city")**. Compare in a list: **in each of the three texts, is the city a stage, a role, or a grammar?**; then answer: **why "the country person entering the city" is the master plot of Chinese fiction in the 1930s** (hint: the historical fact of the 1930s is a two-way flow of rural bankruptcy and the colonisation of the metropolis — **literature does not reflect; it names this stream of people: it gives it a grammar**).
::: solution
① ***Midnight*: the city is at once grammar and murderer** (the death of Old Mr Wu = **a compatibility crash of the old sign-system inside a New Sensation environment: he dies of "seeing" — the city commits the murder by quantity of information: the "sensor" theory of Chapter 1 demonstrating itself at the opening of the novel**); ② ***Rickshaw Boy*: the city is at once an exchange and a slaughterhouse** (**the body is the only capital, and pulling a rickshaw the only option — the city calculates his effort actuarially down to zero, and does not give even the interest: Xiangzi's fall is an actuarial result, not a moral failure**); ③ ***Dream of the Red Chamber*: the city is at once a stage and a specimen cabinet** (**Granny Liu's eyes stick price tags on the Jia household — the classic version of country and city looking at each other; but the Grand View Garden is not a modern metropolis: it has no "quotations", only "rules" — quotations are what modernity is**); **the answer on the master plot**: the three entries into the city cover the whole of the social dynamics of the 1930s — **the metropolis of colonial capital, the bankrupt countryside, and, between the two, that conveyor line of "people": the task of the novel is not to sympathise with the exiles, but to draw the conveyor line itself (the way literature names a flow of people: fitting "entering the city" with three scales — quotations, the body, and the gaze — and so "the country person entering the city" becomes the master question of modern Chinese literature, still unwritten to the end today (migrant-worker literature of the 1980s, the small-town exam-grinder of the 2010s, *xiǎozhèn zuòtíjiā* 小镇做题家: the same big question, with the paper changed)**).
:::

::: intuition A sampling contest among genres {#int-caiyang}
Spread the eight patterns out, and one finds that the 1920s–30s were **a sampling contest held in public**: **each person claims a patch of new ground and sets up a new stele — not one of the inscriptions is written to the very highest standard, but the range the eight steles fence in is the habitat of that new species, "fiction in modern Chinese"** (the collective version of Chapter 3's "a special format": Lu Xun test-drives on his own; a generation tests the road). **A theoretical verdict: the products of an experimental period are to be scored in two columns, "sampling value + degree of completion" — score by degree of completion alone, and you will kill samples in error; score by sampling value alone, and you will take drafts for masterpieces (textbooks of literary history often commit the latter error; the readers used in secondary schools often commit the former)**. **This chapter's own chosen move**: the war of 1937 reorganises the literary world across the board (the next chapter) — **the sampling contest is interrupted: of the eight patterns, six have yet to produce a representative work: the "unfinishedness" of modern literature is not failure; it is time requisitioned by war (a compulsory closing of the books in the history of effects)**.
:::

::: history A seventeen-year inventory of the sampling {#sec-qingdan}
**1921** The Literary Research Association and the Creation Society are founded, and *Sinking* is published (**the shop signs and the autobiographical narrative open for business in the same year: both personalities of the new literature were born in 1921**); **1923–25** a vogue for problem fiction, and the discussion of "for life" (**a contingent of girl-student readers comes in: the first batch of incremental users of the Literary Revolution**); **1925–28** the cluster of native-soil works takes shape (**the periodical network of the Yusi Society, 语丝社, and the Weiming Society, 未名社: literary geography spreads from the capital to the desks of middle-school teachers in the provincial cities**); **1928** Mao Dun's 茅盾 trilogy *Eclipse*, 《蚀》 (**first-hand testimony of the Great Revolution: the three-part form from "Disillusion" to "Pursuit" = a curve recording the mood of the age**); **1931–33** *Family*, *Midnight* and *Rickshaw Boy*, hard on one another's heels (**three great campaigns — the family, finance, and the marketplace: within three years the new literature handed over the "heavy equipment" of three full-length novels**); **1932–34** *Xiandai*, the New Sensation school, and the new psychological mode (**the patent office for urban grammar opens for business**); **1935–36** the "*Jingpai* / *Haipai*" controversy (京派／海派, the Beijing school / the Shanghai school) (**the focus: whether literature should be urbanised and commercialised — the Shanghai school was denounced as "abominably vulgar": a Republican restaging of the hierarchy of genres in Chapter 8: what was fought over this time was not a hierarchy of literary forms but a hierarchy of cities**); **1937.7** the War of Resistance breaks out in full (**the sampling contest sounds the retreat: periodicals cease publication and move inland; recitation and reportage take over the page — an event synchronous with the distribution coup of Chapter 4**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in reading the schools
**Turning "for art versus for life" into a choosing of sides** (**reading a quarrel of bookkeeping methods as a struggle over the line: the two camps' cursing of each other is a prospectus for shares; their borrowing from each other is day-to-day turnover — the real merger is their common turn towards the left wing after the 1930s**); **using "maturity" to scold the early experiments** (**"*Sinking* is too sentimental", "*Midnight* is too conceptual" — both sentences are right, but both are right about the wrong object: the value of experimental literature lies in the coordinates of the sample: first use the two-column table (degree of completion / sampling value), and only then deliver the verdict**); **reading "native soil" as rural policy** (**native-soil fiction is an exercise book for the sojourner's gaze: what it writes is "one cannot go back", not "what is to be done" — to read literature as a programme of government is to complain that the thermometer will not repair the heating**); **ignoring the sampling that was interrupted** (**counting only the completed state after 1949, and judging the pre-war "semi-finished goods" a failure — a closing of the books in the history of effects is not the author's IOU (the Republican carrying-out of Chapter 9's clause on distance in time)**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: how to defend the unfinished {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**whether the 'unfinishedness' of modern literature is an impartial verdict of historiography or an extrajudicial punishment by war: none of the eight patterns grew to fullness, and the verdict ought not to be written by the person who closed the books**". You must call on **the two shop signs (bookkeeping methods)**, **any two of the four methods of sampling**, **any two of the three urban grammars**, **the lesson of the CT film in *Midnight***, and **the two-column scoring of the sampling contest**, at least five items; and adjudicate: **if the war of 1937 had not broken out, would Chinese fiction in 1950 have been greater?**
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**shop signs = a division of labour among positions; sampling = a fence around the habitat; urban grammar = the three scales of quotations; the CT film = a lesion of idea-first that can be warned of in advance; the two columns = the auditing rule for experimental literature**); the adjudication (**both columns must be present: the argument that it would have been greater — the sampling period would naturally have entered a period of accumulation, and the maturing of the full-length novel might have been brought forward by ten years (if the CT film of *Midnight* had not been interrupted by the war, it might have been revised again and again into a more precise social model); the argument that not necessarily — the representative works of the several patterns of the 1930s were in fact mostly produced out of exile and the chaos of war (both *Family* and *Rickshaw Boy* were written in years of upheaval): the interruption thesis underestimates literature's capacity to live as a parasite; and the new order of the 1950s would itself have redrawn the fence: what was not interrupted might also have been another kind of sampling (compare the double line of the areas under Nationalist rule, 国统区, and the Liberated Areas, 解放区)**); extra credit (**pointing out the methodological limit of counterfactual historiography: "if there had been no war" is a proposition that cannot be tested — the use this lesson makes of it is "to treat the war as a natural experiment: to observe which part of literature is the child of the age, and which part is the literary form's own child" — the long-term wager between the sensor of Chapter 2 and the autonomy of Chapter 4**); if the conclusion is written "**closing the books cancels only the balance, and does not cancel the assets: the eight steles did not become eight buildings, but each one is marked with latitude and longitude — those who came after built their buildings by those coordinates**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The fence of eight steles, filed: the shop signs (**a quarrel of bookkeeping methods, not a battle over the line**); the sampling (**problem, autobiographical narrative, native soil, the psychological: four ways of joining the accounts of "I" and of "the world"**); the city (**three grammars: information that commits the murder, the body under actuarial reckoning, the gaze that sticks on price tags**); the flagship (***Midnight*: the graph of a typical function + the CT film of idea-first**); the closing of the books (**1937: unfinishedness is time requisitioned, not an IOU**). The method in one sentence: **first ask what kind of thing it sampled, then ask what kind of thing it grew into**. The next chapter is given over to the world after the books were closed: the left wing, the War of Resistance, and literature forming its ranks again.
:::
