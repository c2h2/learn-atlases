---
title: "Lu Xun’s fiction"
---

The previous chapter said that the starting point of the vernacular (*baihua*) is the peak. This chapter goes into the workshop to look at the **mechanical structure** of that peak: the fiction of Lu Xun 鲁迅 is not a synonym for "profound thought"; it is **a patented combination of narrative devices** — frame, bookkeeping, spectatorship, retelling, double voice, editing, transgression, seven tools, each with a number.

## Device One: The frame and unreliability {#def-kuangjia}

::: definition Self-account under the cover of the case file {#def-kuang}
The **double-layer frame** of 'A Madman’s Diary' (a short preface in Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) declares “此人已愈、候补某地官职” ('This man has recovered, and is expectant of an official post somewhere.') (Our translation.); the vernacular diary of the main text is the original papers of the illness) = the mine buried in Chapter 1 is detonated here: **the frame of recovery pronounces the content mad, while the content prophesies precisely that it is the normality of the frame that counts as the illness** — “**救救孩子**” ('Save the children.') (Our translation.) is shouted out of a case file that has been archived, and legitimacy is confiscated by the cover first; **the reader’s verdict (who is normal) has no guarantee inside the text: the burden of proof is shifted, as a whole, onto the reader — this is the first time in the history of Chinese fiction that "unreliable narration" was used as an epistemological weapon** (Chapter 5’s "the seam is subsidised by the reader" is here the version at the abyss: **the seam is large enough to stuff the whole edifice of the ritual teachings (*lijiao*) into it**). Supplementary clause: the diary form’s **loss of control over duration** (registered as a mechanism: **fear stretches one minute into a page, and ease shrinks a night into a line: the temporal elasticity of the first person = the speedometer of the state of mind**). **Lu Xun’s licence**: he had tried nearly every form (“**表现的深切和格式的特别**” ('the depth of expression and the peculiarity of form') — his own admission in the introduction to *Zhongguo xinwenxue daxi* 中国新文学大系, Second Collection of Fiction (*Compendium of Modern Chinese Literature*)), **and "peculiarity of form" is not a rhetorical modesty: he changes the machine in every piece, which amounts to declaring — the legitimacy of a vernacular literary form does not rest on maturity, but on test-driving without a stop (the curse upon the new literature, that "the starting point is the peak", was turned by him into a laboratory)**.
:::

## Device Two: Name and station, and bookkeeping {#def-mingfen}

::: definition A biography for someone who does not qualify {#def-zhuan}
The "**Preface**" at the opening of *The True Story of Ah Q* is a **farce in the study of literary form**: a review of the qualifications for writing a biography (the name unknown, the native place unknown, even the way of writing “阿 Q” has to be discussed — **the genre of biography has only ever been open to "people worth recording": to write a biography of Ah Q = to climb, treating the genre as a pedigree; the failure to climb is itself the theme**) — **the narrator, even as he writes, apologises for the genre’s failure to fit: the genre is class; the apology is the indictment**. **The method of spiritual victory**, at the level of the device, is not a trait of character; it is **a method of bookkeeping**: every failure is rewritten, inside the narration, as a victory (**“我们先前——比你阔的多啦” ('We used to — be much grander than you!') (Our translation.) = an advance; being beaten = “儿子打老子” ('The son is beating his father.') (Our translation.) = the loan posted in the reverse direction — Ah Q keeps a crooked set of books, and the narrator holds the true set for the reader: the second in which the reader sees both sets at once is what is called irony**). **The execution under the "grand reunion"** (the parade through the streets, the circle drawn in the manner of a dream-message, the onlookers’ satisfaction that “过了十年或者二十年而终于不明不白” ('ten years pass, or twenty, and in the end it was never clear') (Our translation.)) — **up to his death Ah Q never receives one decent plot-line: his "revolution" is only a list of things to grab — the narrative gives him no growth, because the genre gives him none: the type (the typical) is compressed into a function of national character (*guominxing*) (Engels’s "typical environment" from Chapter 2 is used here in reverse: the environment supplies only a slice of Weizhuang 未庄, yet the value of the function holds for the whole country)**.
:::

## Device Three: Onlookers and the chain of retelling {#def-kanke}

::: definition Narration is spectatorship {#def-wei}
'Shown to the Crowd' **has no story**: under a fierce sun the people of one street crowd round to watch a man led by a constable — **the event is absent, and the spectatorship is present: Lu Xun promotes “看” ('looking') itself into plot** (a wiring diagram of eye joined to eye, the ensemble of the hawkers’ cries — **the earliest "pure onlooker-device" in the history of narrative literature**); the execution ground in 'Medicine' (the bird’s-eye description of the watching crowd “伸长脖子” ('craning their necks') — keeping only the mechanism: **the transaction in the human-blood steamed bun is completed under the lighting of "looking" — the tragedy of enlightenment: those who were to be saved look on at the death of the one who would save them, and their taste is excellent**). **The triple device of 'Kong Yiji'**: the young assistant’s **internal focalisation** (“我” ('I') is the only person in the Xianheng wineshop 咸亨酒店 who may listen and need not answer for it — **the apprentice’s point of view = a legitimate overhearer**); **the silence of the accomplices** (everyone laughs, and the narrator records the laughter without stopping it — **inside the structure of spectatorship, even the narrator’s seat is one place in the stands**); **a close on contradictory wording** (“**大约孔乙己的确已经长久没有再出现**” ('Kong Yiji, approximately, has indeed long since failed to appear again.') (Our translation.), contradictory wording of that sort: the double adverb: **an epistemological modal mark — the narrator admits he has no right to know for certain: for one person’s death a society can give only an "approximately, and yet certainly", and that is the sentence passed on the whole text**). **The chain of transmission in 'The New-Year Sacrifice'**: the news of the death of Xianglin Sao 祥林嫂 ('Xianglin’s wife') passes through the mouth of the short-term labourer (the vagueness of “**老了**” ('grown old')); her story is retold through the avoidance and the guilt of "I" — **outside the case of "who killed her" there is another diagram, a topology of the narrative: the "I" who asks the way has knowledge but no authority; Fourth Master Lu 鲁四老爷, who has the authority, has a settled verdict; the short-term labourer who tells the story has a mouth — information attenuates link by link along the chain, and the account of responsibility is diluted link by link along the chain: retelling is a mechanism of exemption**.
:::

## Device Four: Double voice and editing {#def-shuangsheng}

::: definition Two lines and one circle {#def-jianji}
The **structure of a dialogue between two** in 'Upstairs in the Wineshop' and 'The Solitary': the narrator listens while an old friend narrates his own decline (Lü Weifu’s 吕纬甫 “**绕一圈又回到原点**” ('circling once and back to the starting point') — moving the grave of a dead younger brother, taking flowers to an old acquaintance — **people who were once radical run on “应付” ('making do'): what Lu Xun writes is not defection, but the depreciation rate of awakening**; Wei Lianshu 魏连殳 swears to himself “这样的我偏不” ('A self like this, I refuse to be.') (Our translation.) and in the end goes to be adviser to a military governor — **self-narration is nested at a second level: the narrator can only record the friend’s "statement after the post-mortem" — double voice = the confrontation of the voice of the present with the voice of the past, and neither has won**); the **two-line montage** of 'Medicine' (the Hua family buys the medicine — the human-blood steamed bun — Xiaoshuan 小栓 coughs ∥ the Xia family, the execution ground — Xia Yu 夏瑜 dies a martyr — **each of the two lines finishes telling its own "everyday", and the suture is in the graveyard: the two roads meet on the same piece of waste ground, and the wreath is the only commentary — Lu Xun upgrades "juxtaposition" from a rhetorical device of poetry to a structural judgement of fiction: he does not state the causality, and lets the two plot-lines collide in the reader’s mind into causality (the fiction version of Chapter 6’s "juxtaposition is logic")**); the **semiotics of the braid** in 'A Disturbance' (the rumour that the emperor has sat on the dragon throne descends, along the dining table of the family of Seven-Jin 七斤, into panic — “**九斤老太**” (Old Lady Nine-Jin 九斤老太) and her “**一一代不如一代**” ('One after another, each generation worse than the last.') (Our translation.) = **politicising time into a statistical measure: the braid is a sign that can be cut, and the panic is a program that can be reused**); the **lyrical transgression** at the end of 'My Old Home' (the discursive paragraph “其实地上本没有路” ('In fact, to begin with, the earth had no roads.') (Our translation.) — **at the ending the narrative elopes into lyric: the narrator cannot help standing outside the story for three minutes — Chapter 8’s "a breach of genre is also a kind of contractual relation": Lu Xun is the first to break the contract and pay no tax**).
:::

::: example Taking 'Kong Yiji' apart into the seven tools {#ex- kongyi7}
Take 'Kong Yiji' apart again by this chapter’s list: ① the focalising seat (an inventory of the young assistant’s functions as “旁听器” ('overhearer') — what he knows, and what he pretends not to know); ② **two sets of books, for the wine and for the money** (“排出九文大钱” ('lays out nine big cash') and “摸出四文大钱” ('fishes out four big cash') — a plot-line in the difference of one character: the economics of the verb); ③ **the right to confer the nickname** (the origin of the name “孔乙己” — a name given by other people = an identity that has been occupied); ④ **the metronome of the laughter** (“laughter” appears about ten times in this piece — count the rate at which the laughs are bound to the number of Kong Yiji’s entrances); ⑤ the last entrance (the broken legs, coming on his hands — **the narrator stops the mockery for the first time, and the only time: the time-lag of the conscience in the point of view**); ⑥ the epistemology of “大约的确” ('approximately, and yet certainly'); ⑦ **no climax** (the whole event = one person drinking wine — **Lu Xun’s definition of an event: a minute displacement that can change the social coordinate system**. Write a summary of 200 characters: what does this piece do beyond an "anti-feudal" manifesto?).
::: solution
① The overhearer’s function (**"I" is faulted by the proprietor for “没有规矩” ('having no manners'), unfit to wait on customers, fit only to warm the wine — a marginal person’s point of view looking at a marginal person: sympathy is registered in the form of "a mismatch of duties"**); ② 排 → 摸 (**“排” is the performance of showing off wealth, “摸” is the struggle of scraping the bottom: a history of the fall in price, from rich to poor, in the same verb slot — from “排” to “摸” = from social performance to biological instinct; after “打折了腿” ('his legs had been broken') he cannot even manage the performance — narrative economics needs only two verbs**); ③ the right to name (**the name is stolen from the stage: he does not even possess a surname — “孔乙己” is a nickname other people gave him; the subject of the biography is, inside the biography, stripped of the right to a name: the threshold of the biographical genre is reversed here — a person not qualified to be recorded is qualified only to be mocked**); ④ the binding rate of the laughter (**each entrance = one performance of being mocked: his function in existing is to be material for laughter; the count of laughs ≈ his remaining social time — the metronome is a countdown**); ⑤ **only the paragraph that stops the mockery sees a person for the first time** (the objective record “他从破衣袋里摸出四文大钱……他喝完了酒，在旁人的说笑中，坐着用这手慢慢走去了” ('From his torn jacket-pocket he fished out four big cash…… When he had drunk the wine, amid the others’ talk and laughter, he sat, and on these hands slowly went away.') (Our translation.) — **the point of view takes its make-up lesson at the end: sympathy is late for the whole text, and the lateness is itself the indictment: we, like the drinkers, stop laughing only when he walks on his hands**); ⑥ approximately, and yet certainly (**the double adverb = a society’s capacity to certify the death of one of its members: it cannot be measured; not that it is vague, but that there is no instrument**); ⑦ no climax (**the event is re-sentenced from "conflict" to "displacement": fiction proves for the first time that "the disappearance of one person" can be an event on the scale of world history, provided the coordinate system is honest enough — this is the definition, in the study of the devices, of “表现的深切” ('the depth of expression'): not by outcry, but by bookkeeping**).
:::

::: intuition The iron house and the narrator’s authority {#int-tiewu}
The “**铁屋子**” ('iron house') parable in the author’s preface to *Call to Arms* (*Outcry*) (the gist: in a room that can hardly be broken open, a few people wake, yet have nowhere to go — **all of Lu Xun’s narrative devices are wired to this generator**: **the authority of the enlightener (the narrator) is fed by a suspect power supply** — no sooner has the madman finished speaking than he is cured; Xia Yu’s blood is made into breakfast; Kong Yiji’s true words are served as a dish to go with the wine — **the "unreliability" of Lu Xun’s fiction is not a display of technique; it is the narrator’s honest report on his own social efficacy (does waking someone equal doing them harm? He cannot answer, and so every piece leaves a trailing note that cannot be said clearly: “大约的确”, “还是不去为好” ('better, after all, not to go') (Our translation.) — the trailing note is the theme)**). Set against the "problem fiction" of the same time: **Bing Xin 冰心 and her kind supply answers; Lu Xun supplies the difficulty of narrative proof** — **the recipe for "depth": reduce a doctrine by one dimension into a predicament that can be felt, and rewrite the preaching as the record of a failed experiment**.
:::

::: history A property dispute over a peak {#sec-chanquan}
**1918–1926**, three collections (*Call to Arms*, *Wandering*, *Old Tales Retold* — ***Old Tales Retold* writes the ancients anew: an experiment in the "sketch" genre: the time-sense of modernity stuffed into the patterns of bronze vessels, a game instance played across 1922–1935**); **the 1920s–40s**, the "canonisation of Lu Xun" in the present continuous: **in the revolutionary-literature debate of 1928 the Creation Society (创造社) and the Sun Society (太阳社) besiege Lu Xun (the charge of “落伍” ('out of date') — "from being inherited by the revolutionaries and made into a revolutionary teacher, then expelled by revolutionary youth from the place of revolutionary teacher: every time Lu Xun’s position changes hands it has to be measured all over again — a specimen of the human pillar in Chapter 1’s account of institutions"**); **1940**, Mao Zedong’s 毛泽东 *On New Democracy* fixes, as the definitive version, the three hats “伟大的文学家、思想家、革命家” ('a great man of letters, a thinker and a revolutionary'); **the 1950s–70s**, the single-choice pattern "Lu Xun = revolution" in the secondary-school textbooks (**the industry of standard answers to 'Medicine': the function of the wreath is prescribed by the teachers’ reference books — stating only the mechanism: the textbook smooths the trailing note into a full stop: every generation has to discover Lu Xun over again, because he was taught as a full stop**); **the 1980s**, "back to Lu Xun himself" (**Wang Zengqi 汪曾祺, Qian Liqun 钱理群 and others reopen the two-layer discussion of "enlightenment—despair": Lu Xun becomes a method again, rather than an answer**); **now**, the "inflation of Lu Xun discourse" in the age of the internet (**the wholesale industry of spurious quotations: one sentence, “我家门前有两棵树” ('There are two trees in front of my house.') (Our translation.), feeds half the internet — the exchange rate between the Lu Xun who is quoted and the Lu Xun who is read has long stood inverted: the more he is turned into golden sentences, the more the study of the devices is needed — the golden sentence is a cash advance on his credit card; the devices are his assets**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in reading Lu Xun
**The all-purpose sticker "profound thought"** (**praise that does not point to a device is all credit on account: every sentence of "criticising feudal ritual teachings" must be cashed into "how the frame confiscates legitimacy" and "how the verb falls in price" — named, for the Nth time, on this textbook’s blacklist**); **treating characters as a table of class status** (**verdicts of the form "Ah Q = the typical hired farmhand": the value of the function is filled into a cell of the status grid — Lu Xun’s critique of national character is precisely hauling the person out of the status category and weighing them**); **turning the trailing note into a full stop** (**reciting the conclusion and deleting “大约的确”: Lu Xun’s reliability is built on a power supply of self-doubt: to read him as a person of certainty is to replace his power source**); **mixing the accounts of golden-sentence Lu Xun and original-text Lu Xun** (**quoting spurious lines in a response-to-the-reading = paying the tax on the assets with a credit card cashed out for spending: only reading the original text counts as getting in the door — every quotation in this text can be checked; this lesson stands guarantee**).
:::

Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**Lu Xun’s 'depth' is a mechanical structure that can be taken apart and put back together: thought will go out of date, the devices will not — because what the devices handle is the authority, and the cost, of narration itself**". You must call on: **the double-layer frame**, **the two sets of books**, **the onlooker structure (the narrative seat is also one of the stands)**, **retelling as exemption**, **the iron house (the narrator’s doubt about the power supply)**, at least five items; and give a verdict: **if “格式的特别” ('the peculiarity of form') is deleted and only “表现的深切” ('the depth of expression') is kept, is Lu Xun still Lu Xun?** — hint: the "alloy theory" of the previous chapter.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**frame = the procedure that confiscates legitimacy; the two sets of books = the double-track system of irony; the seat = the register of accomplices; retelling = the conveyor of responsibility; doubt about the power supply = the honest report on the enlightener’s authority**); the verdict (**the alloy theory must be cited: to lift "depth" out on its own = the plasticine myth coming back — “救救孩子” ('Save the children.'), once it leaves the cover of the case file, is only a slogan; if “大约的确” is deleted, the death of Kong Yiji is downgraded from an epistemological accident to a missing person — in the ontology of the content, it depends on these devices: what is called "thought" is the noise of the devices running**); extra credit (**a definition of "going out of date": what can go out of date is the verdict (the particular wording of anti-feudalism, and of the diagnosis of national character); what cannot go out of date is the problem-tree (who has the right to narrate, how telling divides the spoils, why sympathy arrives late) — the application to Lu Xun of the "problem-tree" way of reading in Lesson 15**); if the conclusion writes "**the most reliable way to commemorate a writer is to take his machine apart and fit one’s own: a paragraph in imitation does not count as homage; inventing a new unreliability is what counts as inheritance**", that is an A.

::: summary
The workshop inventory, filed: the frame (**the cover confiscates legitimacy, and the verdict is contracted out to the reader**), bookkeeping (**reading the two sets of books against each other = irony; the genre’s pedigree = the politics of *mingfen* 名分 ('name and station') in a biography**), spectatorship (**the seat is complicity; the chain of retelling = a belt that dilutes responsibility**), editing (**juxtaposition collides into causality; lyrical transgression pays no tax**), the power supply (**the iron house: a suspect supply powering the narrator’s doubt about his own efficacy**). One sentence of method: **do not ask "what was criticised"; ask "which machine is doing the criticising"**. The next chapter goes into the darkroom: *Wild Grass* — when the narrator stops telling of other people and begins to operate on himself.
:::
