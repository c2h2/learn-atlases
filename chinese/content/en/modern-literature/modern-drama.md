---
title: "Modern drama"
---

Fiction has the distant kin of the *Records of the Grand Historian*; poetry has the ancestral property of the *Book of Songs*. Spoken drama (*huaju*) is the only account, among the genres of modern Chinese literature, that "has no classical tradition to rebel against" — the previous chapter already said it: zero stock, zero baggage. This chapter watches how this air-dropped genre grew, within twenty years, from the makeshift stage of the performance-outline system into *Thunderstorm*. Its coming-of-age rite is this: to pack "what cannot be said" into a form that "must be said".

## Act One: Drama without a script {#def-mumu}

::: definition The scenario system and "the degeneration of civilised drama" {#def-mingwen}
**Student theatre in Shanghai in 1899, and in the spring of 1907 the Spring Willow Society (Tokyo) performing *The Lady of the Camellias* and *The Black Slave's Cry to Heaven* — the record of spoken drama's delivery; afterwards "civilised drama" (*wenmingxi*) was commercialised: the scenario system** (**only an outline divided by acts, and no script: on the day a "scenario sheet" was posted, and the actors made it up on the stage — "the first tax drama pays to literature: the absence of the script = the absence of the text: it can be performed for ten thousand shows, but it cannot transmit a single one — an orchestra with no score passes the piece on by ear, and when the company breaks up it is lost (Chapter 4: the work ≠ its concretisation — civilised drama has only concretisation, and no work)"**); **"the degeneration of civilised drama" became a fixed criticism of the 1910s–20s** (**the economics of "degeneration": from a lecture platform for "enlightening the people" it sank into a hall of farce — a relapse of the carrier clause in Chapter 1: a genre that had been appointed is dismissed for the first time**); ***aimei* drama takes the baton (around 1921, a transliteration of "amateur" — "drama that is not for sale")** (**"amateur" as a moral asset: not selling tickets = not selling oneself: the second start-up of Chinese spoken drama used "not for the purpose of profit" as its seed capital — Chapter 2: a plain version of the autonomy clause — and the soft spot of *aimei* was equally plain: if you do not sell tickets you cannot keep a professional theatre alive, and once the romantic chain of funding snapped one had to return to commerce (Hong Shen came back from theatrical experiments at Tsinghua and Harvard; after *Zhao the King of Hell* failed in 1923 under the performance-outline system, he turned about, wrote China's first formal screenplay, and for a long period directed professional companies — a specimen of "fail first in the experiment, then succeed in the institution")**).
:::

## Act Two: The physics of two kinds of one-act play {#def-danmu}

::: definition Tian Han's lyricism and Ding Xilin's politeness {#def-dongwu}
**Tian Han (the Nanguo Society, *The Death of a Famous Actor*, 1927/28)** — **"a romanticist isomorphism of drama and life"**: the famous actor Liu Zhensheng dies under the double grinding of "art is worth nothing, and players have no status" — **the stage and life share one plot of destruction: the sorrow of metadrama (the person who acts a tragedy is arranged by life into a tragedy — "the audience watches the play from below the stage; who is living up on it?"); Tian Han's grammar is "poetry enters drama": a speech can be recited on its own, and the characters are vehicles of ardour — the lyric genre counter-attacks the narrative genre (Chapter 6's theory of posts: Tian Han writes the "I" straight onto the face, and declares that this is drama)**. **Ding Xilin (*A Wasp*, 1923; *Oppression*, 1925)** — **the comic mechanics of a physicist**: the equation of *Oppression* — **the landlord's groundless rule “不租给没有家眷的人” ('will not rent to anyone who has no family with them') (Our translation.) = an axiom; the man and the woman, tenants who meet for the first time and do not know each other = the variables; "pretending to be family" = a constructive proof of the theorem; the recognition of the truth at the end =** QED, but the axiom has not been questioned: the humour lies in "the problem is solved, and the absurdity is still there" (**"oppression" has neither name nor form: the house rule is not personified — the anatomical divide between comedy and tragedy: in tragedy there is an enemy, in comedy there is only a system — Lesson 15: functional units of a structuralist kind: Ding Xilin is China's shortest "problem play": three minutes to raise the fog, five minutes to draw the net ("a comedy of wit": humour is the art of giving way — a politeness so courteous that it comes near to violence: letting one "respectability" fluster another, and no one lifts a hand)**)).
:::

## Act Three: *Thunderstorm* — what cannot be said, and what must be said {#def-leiyu}

::: definition Stifling heat, the ledger and shutting the window {#def-yure}
**The structural set of three in *Thunderstorm* (written in 1933, performed in 1934)**: **① the retrospective form (the countdown of a secret)** — **the old accounts of thirty years before (Shiping driven out of the house, her attempt to throw herself into the river unsuccessful) are cleared within a single day: the whole play has nothing new in it; it is all the interest on old business (Chapter 15: the Chinese head office of discovery and reversal: every "recognition" is a reconciliation of the accounts — Lu Gui knows, Shiping knows, the audience comes to know slowly, Zhou Puyuan knows last: information held in distribution = the securitisation of suspense as an asset**); **② a meteorological device of narrative** — **"stifling heat" is a supersaturated physical state: low pressure leaves every person unable to speak, and the thunderstorm is the inevitable discharge: the title is not an atmosphere, it is an equation of dynamics (Chapter 5: the shutter is given to the weather: the whole play gives its slowest shot to "the sound of thunder" — because the dead need drums and music**); **③ the prop-study of "shutting the window"** — **Zhou Puyuan orders “把窗户关起来” ('shut the windows') (Our translation.): Shiping, a servant, answers, and the old habit “这屋子我们从不关窗” ('in this house we never shut the windows') (Our translation.) is leaked — habit is the fingerprint of memory (Chapter 8: style is the leakage of preference: the information in a personality is given up by the quirks he cannot change)**.
:::

::: definition The chief financial officer of patriarchy {#def-puyuan}
**Zhou Puyuan's "commemoration"** — **keeping the old furniture and the old arrangement of thirty years before, remembering Shiping's birthday, insisting on those habits in the house that have to do with her — he sincerely misses a person whose expulsion he himself carried out** — **commemoration is bookkeeping, not repentance: in his choice function "respectability" and "order" are always greater than "the person" (Chapter 8: style is a choice function — personality is a choice function: every choice Puyuan makes is self-consistent to a frightening degree)**; **the flagship case of dramatic irony** — **the "medicine" in the scene where he forces Fanyi to drink it: the audience, present in the theatre, knows how much else is in that bowl (Chapter 15's two-layer contract: no one in the theatre knows an information gap crueller than this — the three characters 喝下去 ('drink it down') are the whole play in miniature: a punishment carried out by means of loving care**); **the distribution of the "Thunderstorm type"** — **the edge of incest (brother and sister), the interlayer of class (a servant's family and a capitalist's family in the same house), the debt between generations (an erotic version of the son paying the father's debts) — three taboos stacked into one pressure cooker: Cao Yu welds the timer of the well-made play onto "the May Fourth family", that statutory site of dispute (Chapter 2: the family = the economic cell of feudalism — *Family* settles the account in fiction, *Thunderstorm* runs the current through in two hours)**.
:::

::: definition The absent villain and the people who are present {#def-quexi}
**The financial structure of *Sunrise* (1935–36)** — **the epigraph quotes the prophetic books of the Old Testament, “你们这些要吞吃穷乏人、使困苦人灭没的” ('you who would devour the poor, and wipe out the afflicted') (Our translation.) (the quotation follows the current translation) — bank interest is written into the cosmology of drama: the afterpiece of the gold bars and the remaindered goods of people are dealt in the same market** — **the great villain Jin Ba never comes on at all: the best dramatic scheme for a structural evil — the one who is not seen is the one who knows all (Chapter 15's dramatic realisation of the array of nothing: if the enemy comes on, he can still be assassinated: only the enemy who does not come on is the system**); **the chain of "those who do the harm" and "those who are harmed"**: Xiaodongxi — Cuixi — Chen Bailu — (Jin Ba, formless, upstream) — **a production line priced by sex and by debt**. ***Beijing Man* (1941)** — **anthropology used as a contrasting mirror: the "health" of Beijing ape-man and of primitive people vs the "frailty" of Zeng Wenqing and his kind — three generations of specimens in a pigeon coop ("primitive man", once he enters, makes "civilisation" show a sickly look — Chapter 2, the sensor installed in reverse: the past becomes an instrument for measuring the present)**.
:::

## Act Four: Wartime history plays, and the return of the square {#def-lishiju}

::: definition Guo Moruo, *Qu Yuan* (1942) {#def-quyuan}
**The monologue "Ode to Thunder and Lightning" = the apostrophic genre of poetry takes over drama** (**on the stage, one person makes a speech to the thunder — the vacant post of Chapter 6 is staffed to the full here: the audience collectively takes up employment as "thunder" and "lightning": drama steps down, the square takes the seat (Chapter 4's format of distribution: the War of Resistance returns poetry to a literature of the mouth, and also returns spoken drama to the ancient oral art of the rhapsody (*cifu*): one of the peaks of Chinese drama turns out to be an "anti-dramatic" monologue — Chapter 7's "drama can only perform the present continuous" is broken: he can perform the present continuous of two thousand years ago**). **The "truth" clause of the history play** (**Guo Moruo's position as generally stated: the history play does not seek the truth of historical fact; it seeks the truth of spirit and of struggle (a current summary of the “失事求似” ('drop the facts, seek the likeness') sort) — an on-the-spot lesson, from Chapter 9, in dividing the report into columns: history fills in the "achievement" column, drama fills in the "effect" column, and Guo Moruo signed both sheets — and the dispute lies precisely here: the person who signs is at the same time an operator of the cultural departments (Chapter 2's exchange: the problem of keeping separate accounts for scholarly judgement and for the interests of a position)**) — **the tide of history plays in the 1940s (the wing of Yang Hansheng and A Ying) = "speaking through the ancients", at the moment when the volume of censorship is turned to its maximum: the instrument of torture that is quotation marks is moved onto the stage (the dramatic reincarnation of move ① among the devices of the *zawen* (polemical essay) in Chapter 3: put “怀王” ('King Huai') where “友邦” ('friendly powers') stood, and the shock is handcuffed just the same**.
:::

::: example Three discovery-machines: *Thunderstorm* × *Oedipus the King* × *The Orphan of Zhao* {#ex-faxian}
All three plays mount the engine on "**discovery**" (Chapter 15's definition: from having no knowledge to having knowledge). Compare them in a list: **① who is concealing / who is leaking; ② the direction of "knowing" (destruction / mobilisation / exculpation?); ③ the design of how information is distributed (who knows how much, and when the audience catches up)**. The final verdict: **why "family secret + countdown" is the all-purpose engine of drama — and which taboo Cao Yu presses on beyond Sophocles**.
::: solution
① **the economics of concealment**: Oedipus — **no one conceals: the oracle is public; it is only that no one will own the account (concealment is commissioned by the collective: the whole company conspires in not knowing)**; *Thunderstorm* — **Shiping kept the ledger for thirty years: a servant's "knowing" is the only asset in the Zhou mansion that cannot be audited (class makes her silence free of charge, and makes her speaking fatal — the moment she opens her mouth, both families go bankrupt at once)**; *The Orphan of Zhao* — **concealment is a righteous act (Cheng Ying's keeping of the secret = the engine of the plot: knowing is a debt, concealment is the repayment)**; ② **direction**: knowing = destruction (Oedipus) / knowing = detonation (*Thunderstorm*: the moment the truth is out, everyone is electrocuted — **the pun in "thunderstorm", at once physical and dramatic: the irreversibility of electric charge**) / knowing = mobilisation (*The Orphan of Zhao*: a restatement of the judgement in Chapter 15 — **in Confucian ethics "knowing" is a document confirming a debt**); ③ **the distribution of information**: Sophocles puts the audience in the **position of omniscience** (the oracle is given early: pity starts its clock from the first minute); Cao Yu puts the audience in the **position of catching up step by step** (each act leaks one tier: the interest on suspense is compounded by the act); Ji Junxiang puts the audience in the **position of complicity** (Cheng Ying keeps the secret = the audience keeps the secret: **to enter is to sign a confidentiality agreement: this, and only this, is the highest contractual form of tragic sympathy**); **the all-purpose engine**: the family = **the site of a dispute over the property in information** (who is the father / who is whose son = the pre-modern balance sheet), the countdown = **the physical constraint of the theatre** (three hours = the deadline) — multiply the two = **"irreversible confirmation" becomes the only thing that cannot be put off until tomorrow**; the further taboo Cao Yu presses = **the edge of incest**: Oedipus is "a fact already accomplished" (fate), *Thunderstorm* is "an edge that is happening now" (desire and class there together) — **the point where the tragedy of fate changes track onto the tragedy of modern society: the secret is no longer placed by a god, it is made by human beings: therefore it can be settled, therefore there must be a thunderstorm**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Why spoken drama matured late {#int-wanshu}
China has drama of a world class (the course file of Chapter 7: role-types, conventional routines, points left openly unfixed). **Why was it "spoken drama", of all things, that matured late?** (**The answer does not lie in "the language was immature": what spoken drama lacked was not a technique of dialogue, but an institutional set of three — a script system (the literary side agrees to hand the text over to collective performance), a theatre system (a fixed picture-frame, and a citizenry that buys tickets), a rehearsal system (the appearance of the director: the "director system" was introduced in the 1920s — Hong Shen, Gu Jianchen and others wrote "the play is brought to completion by one person" into the rules of the trade — Chapter 7's "drama must perform the present continuous" means that it needs an "industrialisation of rehearsal": and industry needs capital**). **The inference: the modernisation of spoken drama = Chinese modernisation in miniature — it is the most "asset-heavy" of the genres: a novel needs one pen, a *zawen* one newspaper, drama a whole building — so every peak it reaches steps on the drumbeat of the city and of capital (Shanghai in the 1930s, Chongqing in the 1940s)**; **and its late maturity also brings a rich gift: drama without a tradition can, with a clear conscience, tell a story of "the human" — the incest and the class of *Thunderstorm* need not consult *The Story of the Western Wing*: the other face of zero stock is zero tariff**.
:::

::: history Twenty years from the performance outline to *Thunderstorm* {#sec-ershinian}
**1907** the Spring Willow Society (**the delivery: patriotic between-class plays by students abroad**); **the 1910s** the commercialisation of civilised drama, and the thesis of its degeneration (**the first bucket of gold, and the first bankruptcy of credit**); **1921** the People's Drama Society, the monthly *Drama*, "*aimei* drama" (**the second start-up: taking "amateur" as a moral asset**); **1923** the failure of *Zhao the King of Hell* under the performance-outline system, and Hong Shen's turn (**experiment fails → the institution pays the fare owed**); **1923–25** Ding Xilin's one-act comedies (**the scholars' school: writing comedy as a proof to be worked**); **1928–30** the Nanguo Society and Tian Han (**poetry enters drama; and: the Nanguo Society's wandering performances = touring strongpoints of drama — where there was no theatre, they built a mobile theatre of their own**); **1930** the Chinese League of Left-Wing Dramatists (**an organisation headed by the character 联, of the same structure as the League of Left-Wing Writers (Chapter 7: the drama branch of the organisational cycle)**); **1934** *Thunderstorm* (**the coming-of-age rite: finished at Tsinghua in 1933, performed by students of Beijing Normal University in 1934, a sensation as a professional production in Shanghai in 1935 — a graduation piece at the level of an exercise became the top graduate of the genre's first examination: Cao Yu was twenty-three at the time — spoken drama took thirty-five years to grow up, and graduated at twenty-three**); **1936–41** *Sunrise*, *The Wilderness*, *Beijing Man* (**from society, written on into the mind, written on into civilisation — Cao Yu changes the machine once a year: the dramatic version of Chapter 3's "special format"**); **1942** *Qu Yuan* and Chongqing's "Fog Season performances" (**the performance season of the provisional capital: the box office in the gaps between the bombing — the most expensive version of Chapter 2's lamplight clause: the audience buys a ticket and, in passing, lives through one more time**); **the 1940s** Chen Baichen's comedies and the tide of history plays (**the relay of satire: the dream-comedy of *The Promotion Chart* — "the official" as a collective cosplay: Chapter 7's "mask" and Chapter 15's "misreading" join forces here**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in reading drama
**Reading a script by the standards of the novel** (**the person who only reads and does not watch loses a third of the text: the script is a score (Chapter 4), and reading is "a silent playing": the position of this lesson — when you read a script, at least once stage a performance in your head: who comes on fast, who drinks water in the pause — stage directions are the director's birth certificate**); **reading *Thunderstorm* as an outline of "anti-feudalism"** (**the theme is not wrong, but on Cao Yu's own account what, in the writing, came before any idea was "the agitation of emotion" (the current way of putting the preface to *Thunderstorm*) — to read him as a propagandist is to delete the whole apparatus of stifling heat, of shutting the window, and of drinking the medicine: Chapter 8 names it on the blacklist: thought is the noise of the apparatus as it runs**); **the single sentence "spoken drama = a Western import"** (**the resources of spoken drama's "oral art" come from the monologue tradition of storytelling and of the rhapsody; the "discovery" machine of *Thunderstorm* is the Chinese branch of a global patent — the imported and the native are two parents, not one (the dramatic version of Chapter 1's being born twice)**); **the superstition that "the premiere is the settled verdict"** (**the premiere of *Thunderstorm* was an amateur performance by students, and the critics were at first half for it and half against: its canonisation took ten years (Chapter 9's rolling issue: do not sign the acceptance at the first performance)**).
:::

::: exercise An experiment in reviving the scenario {level=1}
For a "scenario play" of 3 minutes, write an **outline by acts** (four lines only: characters, aim, obstacle, reversal), and from it rehearse once (or rehearse once in the head); then report in 50 characters: **of the sentences that came out of your improvisation, how many came from "character", and how many from "routine"**.
::: solution
Marking: **the four items of the outline are complete**; an honest self-report of the improvised routines ("the domineering-CEO formula, three misunderstandings in a row, emotive incidental music — **routine is the scenario system's real playwright: it does not write people, it only rebroadcasts (the mechanism of the degeneration of civilised drama: the market's memory takes the place of responsibility to the text)**"); a concluding sentence ("the script does not limit the actor's freedom; it preserves the freedom the actor discovers by chance — **what the scenario loses is not the good line, but the inheritability of the good line**").
:::
:::

::: exercise An audit of the shut window {level=2}
In a film or television work you know, or in a family narrative, find a moment when "**a habit leaks a secret**" (one action leaks a stretch of concealed past), and write a **table of the distribution of information** (who knows / who leaks / when the audience catches up); rewrite it as 60 characters of stage blocking (dialogue forbidden; only action and light allowed).
::: solution
Marking: **the point of the leak can be pointed out** (of the order of "every winter solstice, Mother lays one extra bowl and pair of chopsticks"); **all three columns of the distribution table are there**; the blocking lets "things" carry the lines (**an old coat left hanging, a window pushed open and then shut again — stage directions are the director's birth certificate: your 60 characters must be executable, not merely something that can be felt**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: what cannot be said, and what must be said {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**the fuel of drama has always been 'what cannot be said': fiction can write a secret; drama has to put the secret on a countdown in front of everyone — its form (in public, on the spot, within a time limit) is itself an instrument of torture**". You must call on: **the scenario tax (the inheritability of the text)**, **Ding Xilin's "comedy with no enemy"**, **the three-piece set of *Thunderstorm* (retrospection / weather / shutting the window)**, **the absence of Jin Ba**, **the anti-dramatic monologue of *Qu Yuan***, at least five items; and give a verdict: **whether the thesis that spoken drama "matured late" still applies today — once film and television have taken over the technique of "in public", what is left to the theatre that cannot be replaced**.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**the scenario tax = without a text there is no inheritance; comedy with no enemy = institutional oppression brought into view; the three-piece set = the balance sheet of the secret + dynamics + the fingerprint; absence = structure embodied; the monologue = the square taking the theatre back**); the verdict (**two lines to consult: the view that it applies — in the age of film and television the theatre falls back to hold the ontology of "presence" (the verdict of Chapter 7: dramatic quality = the interest on "in public"): it cannot be shot again, it cannot be played at double speed, and actor and audience share one breath and one risk — "late maturity" becomes "the reverse of early maturity: it has kept the transaction in the living"; the view that it no longer applies — immersive theatre, environmental theatre and script-murder games prove that "in public" can be copied in the manner of film and television, and the theatre's last barrier is "the risk to the person of being present together" (one performance = one collective event that cannot be revoked: Chapter 7, "whatever is in public, there is drama")**); if the conclusion writes "**drama does not produce the secret; it produces the time-limit on a secret: it rehearses the irreversible for everyone to see — and the rehearsal itself is irreversible: this is the reason the theatre is still open for business**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Twenty years, filed: the delivery (**the scenario tax: the genre first makes up the household registration of its text**), two strands of one-act mechanics (**Tian Han's poetry entering drama / Ding Xilin's comedy with no enemy**), the pressure cooker (**retrospection + weather + shutting the window: commemoration is bookkeeping, not repentance**), absence (**Jin Ba does not come on: the system is more omniscient than the villain**), the square (**the Ode to Thunder and Lightning: the anti-dramatic monologue becomes the wartime peak**), the late-maturity thesis (**the most asset-heavy of the genres steps on the drumbeat of the city and of capital; the other face of zero stock is zero tariff**). One sentence of method: **first find the thing that cannot be said, then count how many acts it has left**.
