---
title: "New words and internet language"
---

Vocabulary is the barometer of a language, and the Chinese of the past thirty years has been through one continuous downpour: the reform-and-opening-up word-wave, the market-economy word-wave, the internet word-wave, the mobile-internet word-wave, wave upon wave. This lesson sets out how new words are born, how they die, and how dictionaries recruit them, and whether internet language is, in the end, 'language pollution' or 'language life'.

## The birth certificate of new words {#sec-chusheng}

::: definition Four ways of being born {#def-chusheng}
**The old word-formation machine working overtime**: compounding (下岗 'laid off', 内卷 'involution'), derivation (房奴一族 'the house-slave tribe', ×门 '-gate'), abbreviation (双减 'the double reduction', 空巢 'empty nest') — the great majority of new words use procedures already extant; **old words given new senses**: 潜水 'dive; lurk', 晒 'bask; show off', 卷 'roll; involute', 被×× ('被就业') — the word-form does not move, and a sense is grafted on; **letters and numerals enter the text**: GDP, 996, 520; **proper names generalised**: 山寨 'knock-off', 草根 'grass roots', 黑马 'dark horse' — a common noun condensed out of some one event or some one work. The channels of birth are, for their part, highly concentrated: the media, official documents, technical literature, the marketplace and online communities; **before the internet age the lifespan of a new word was counted in generations, and after it in seasons**.
:::

::: example An 'annual inspection' for words {#ex-nianjian}
Judge the life-stage of each of the following words: 下海, 万元户, 倒爷, 粉丝, 给力, 屌丝, yyds, 内卷, 躺平, 破防.
::: solution
**The ebb layer**: 下海, 万元户, 倒爷 (signature vocabulary of the early market economy in the 1980s–90s of the last century; most have already become words that label an era); **the sediment layer**: 粉丝 (expansion on two tracks, transliteration and original meaning, with conventionalisation complete), 内卷 (an academic word popularised, still in its rising phase after entering general use as a pejorative), 躺平, 破防 (words of social mentality, their popularity high, their lifespan still to be tested); **the fast-born, fast-dying layer**: 给力 (an internet-famous item of the sticker-pack age, already partly conventionalised in the sense of 带劲 'with real drive'), yyds (an abbreviation by initial sounds written in letters, basically indecipherable outside the circle — failure to cross circles is a countdown to death). **The conclusion of the inspection**: whether a word can stay depends on three things — crossing circles, productivity (it can form further words), and whether there is a semantic gap nothing else can fill. On all three, yyds is in doubt.
:::
:::

## The grammar of internet language {#sec-wangluo}

::: definition Internet word-formation's own weapons {#def-bingqi}
① **Letter-abbreviation**: yyds, xswl, nsdd (abbreviation by the initial letters of the Pinyin, with an extremely strong character as an in-group cipher);
:::

::: theorem The decay curve of buzzwords {#rule-shuaibian}
The life cycle of an internet word follows a standard three-stage pattern: **outbreak** (in-group cant, the dividend of marking identity) — **spillover** (taken up by the mass media, and the meaning wears down) — **conventionalisation or death** (it enters the general vocabulary, or it is abandoned). The test is a formula of three questions. **Still understood once the context is gone?** (Can it still be used on its own, away from the meme picture?) **Productive?** (Do derivatives of the sort 卷王 and 卷不动 come in a string?) **Is there serious prose that uses it?** (Citation in a serious register = registering a household.) On this basis, among the internet-famous words of the 2010s, 给力, 逆袭, 剁手 and 佛系 have already sedimented, while 蓝瘦香菇 and 神马 are already out of date — **an elimination rate of nine-tenths or more is the normal case, not an accident**; a 'buzzword ranking' is in essence a live broadcast of language dying.
:::

::: intuition 'Chinese characters are backward' is dead; 'Chinese is in crisis' lives on — the same anxiety in a new skin
Whenever an age of word-waves comes round, a voice is raised that 'Chinese is finished': Russian words mixed in during the 1950s, 'Shanghai-style English' in the 1980s, internet grammar in the 2000s, lettered words in the 2010s, AI translation in the 2020s. The consensus among linguists is that **vocabulary is the layer of a language best able to take a blow, and also the readiest to change** — change is precisely a healthy metabolism. What really ought to be monitored is whether native speakers' **sense of pragmatic appropriateness** is still being generated (being able to choose the occasion for 'yyds', and whether or not one writes a standard composition, are not the same ability) and whether **communication between generations** is blocked. The former is a problem for education; the latter has never occurred in Chinese. The substance of the 'finished' thesis is that **a difference in vocabulary between generations is misread as the decline and death of a civilisation**.
:::

::: history The state reads the words: the once-a-decade 'general survey of new Chinese words' {#sec-pucha}
China's attention to new words was institutionalised long ago. From the 1990s, reprints in *Xinhua Wenzhai* and the like, together with the annual series *Report on the Language Situation in China* compiled by the various publishers (issued each year from 2006 by the Ministry of Education and the State Language Commission), have counted new words, lettered words and buzzwords into official data. Each edition of the *Xiandai Hanyu Cidian* answers reality by the supplements to its word-list (the sixth edition of 2012, which took in 房奴, 团购 and 粉丝, once reached the hot-search list). The State Language Commission also publishes an annual list of 'new words' (for example 房叔, 正能量 and 点赞). Lists of 'internet buzzwords' — *Yaowen jiaozi* 咬文嚼字 ('chewing over the wording'), the State Language Commission, and the platforms, each putting out a list of its own — have become a custom of the New Year. Language planning has turned from 'the norm above all' (the sorting-out of variant characters in the 1950s, and purification) to 'monitor — record — guide' — **the state has become the bookkeeper of language life, not its legislator**.
:::

::: warning Three muddles
(1) **Mistaking register for a fault of language**: internet language used in chat is no violation; used in an official document, a medical record or a written judgement, it is a violation — the problem is a mismatch of register, not the crime of 'internet language'. (2) **Mistaking the generations for decline**: the middle-aged and the old criticising the usage of the young is, like the lament in the age of Confucius, “今人不如古人” ('People of today are not the equal of the ancients.') (Our translation.), a constant between human generations. (3) **Mistaking statistics for norms**: 'chosen for the buzzword list' is not the same as being a standard word, and 'not chosen' is not the same as being wrong — a ranking is a thermometer, not a prescription. Conclusion: towards internet language, **description should be greedy, and the laying down of norms should be restrained**.
:::

::: quiz
Which of the following groups belongs entirely to 'old words given new senses'?
- [ ] 下海、倒爷、粉丝
- [x] 潜水、晒、被就业
- [ ] 给力、内卷、破防
- [ ] 秒、崩、卷 (a group of newly derived verbs)
::: solution
潜水, 晒 and 被就业 are all existing word-forms with a new sense hung on them. 下海 and 倒爷 are new coinages from old morphemes (下海 has existed since antiquity, but the sense was constructed separately, so it only just counts as the assigning of a sense; 倒爷, however, is a new coinage). 粉丝 runs on two tracks, transliteration and original meaning — what this question tests is that 'manner of birth' and 'the old accounts of lexical history' are not the same thing.
:::
:::

::: quiz
In judging whether an internet word can survive, the least reliable ground is ( ).
- [ ] Use across circles
- [ ] Productivity
- [x] The total volume of use in the current period
- [ ] Whether it is cited in a serious register
::: solution
Total volume is a necessary condition, not a sufficient one: 蓝瘦香菇 was, in its year, at screen-flooding level, and still died fast. What survival looks to is penetration of the circles, the capacity to derive, and citation in a formal style.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The phenomenon of 被就业 shows that ( ).
- [ ] The Chinese passive has already died out
- [x] A high-frequency grammatical construction can also be given a new expressive function
- [ ] Internet language has no grammar
- [ ] The verb has undergone nominalisation
::: solution
The illegal pattern “被＋不及物动词/动词” ('被 + intransitive verb / verb') is made legal, and an ironic frame is set over the whole construction — **the making of a grammatical construction into a new word** is the best rebuttal of the thesis that 'internet language has no grammar'.
:::

::: exercise Stratifying word history {level=1}
File the following words under 'sediment / ebb / fast-born and fast-dying', and state one criterion for each: 给力, 小姐, 个体户, 点赞, 神马, 文创.
::: solution
Sediment: 点赞 (crosses circles + productive + cited in a serious register). Ebb: 个体户 (the language of policy has stepped down, and the word has become a label of its era), 小姐 (after pejoration, avoided as a public term of address — an 'ebb of the semantic-degeneration type'), 神马 (the old homophonic meme on 什么 has already withered). Fast-born and fast-dying / half-sediment: 给力 (it once flooded the screen; now it survives in a local patch of the spoken language, and the mainstream media seldom use it — to judge it 'half-sediment', and to explain, is enough), 文创 (in the course of a fresh conventionalisation). Marking looks for an awareness of evidence, and does not require one unique filing.
:::
:::

::: exercise Practice in coining words {level=2}
Using each of the four ways of being born, coin one new word (it must be able to enter a real context), give each one example sentence of use, and predict where it will land on the three-stage decay.
::: solution
An example follows. Word-formation — “自习化” (自习 + 化: improving oneself by study, in the age of self-study rooms for everyone). Assigning a sense — “爬” (gathering data the way a crawler does: “我在爬价格” ('I am crawling prices.') (Our translation.)). Letters — “AI 味” (a quasi-affix use modelled on “人味／水味” 'human flavour / watery flavour': a machine tone that is fake at a glance). Generalisation — “孔乙己” (a literary personal name generalised to 'young people trapped by their educational credentials'). A prediction with its grounds attached (across circles? productive? a gap?) is enough.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is AI a producer of new words, or their terminator? {level=3}
Large models produce headline words and marketing language in bulk, and they also translate every foreign word in real time. Write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters, predicting the birth rate and the death rate of new Chinese words in the age of AI, and whether the 'waves of borrowing' will change course. Requirements: cite this lesson's framework of the 'three questions of decay'; one piece of evidence, from your own observation, of a word generated by AI (for example the further diffusion of 赋能, 闭环 and 颗粒度 in AI contexts); and a judgement that can be tested.
::: solution
Points for the marking. Distinguish 'production' (the model sampling old collocations) from 'coining' (a community assigning a sense) — AI is an **amplifier**, not the Creator. Apply the three questions of decay, one by one, to hot words of AI (赋能 has long since crossed circles, is productive, and has entered serious registers: judge it sediment; a word that is purely a model's verbal tic: judge it fast-born and fast-dying). The prediction must be testable (for example: 'over the next ten years the share of lettered loanwords falls/rises, on the ground that automatic subtitles plus real-time translation have eliminated the gap in understanding'). Concepts are not mixed (do not confuse a 'buzzword list' with a 'norm').
:::
::: summary
Four ways a new word is born (the old word-formation working overtime, an old word given a new sense, letters and numerals, a proper name generalised) + six weapons of the internet (abbreviation, homophony, an old character with a new value, filling a slot in a frame, mixed script, secondment from a dialect); the three-stage decay (outbreak — spillover — conventionalisation/death) and the three questions (intelligible once out of context, productive, cited in a formal register) are the whole of the science of prediction; an elimination rate of nine-tenths is the norm. A mismatch of register ≠ a fault of language, a difference between generations ≠ decline and death, a ranking ≠ a norm. The state's role in a word-wave has already changed from legislator to bookkeeper — the greatest institutional change in language life. The word-wave of the AI age will be faster, more involuted, and more transnational, but the threshold 'it counts as born only when a community has assigned it a sense' is a job the machine cannot yet do in anyone's place.
:::
:::
