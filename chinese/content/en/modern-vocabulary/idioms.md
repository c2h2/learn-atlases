---
title: "Idioms and set phrases"
---

In the four characters of “胸有成竹” a sentence of painting theory lies hidden; in the four characters of “莫名其妙” a dead word of the old language has caved in, “名” ('to utter'). *Chengyu* (four-character idioms) are the **fossil layer** of the Chinese lexicon: the structure is earlier Chinese, the meaning has been turned into allusion, and the use is modern. This lesson sets the *chengyu* inside the great family of set phrases, and looks hard at the temper of each house.

## The genealogy of the set-phrase family {#sec-putong}

::: definition Fixed expressions and the five sibling houses {#def-shuyu}
**A fixed expression (a set phrase)** is linguistic material that is "ready-made, and by habit used as a whole block", marked by **a structure fixed in form, and a meaning taken as a whole**. There are five houses. **The *chengyu*** (mainly four characters; sources in the Classics and in the Histories; a written colouring). **The idiomatic phrase** (mainly the three-character verb–object frame: 碰钉子 'to run into a nail', 穿小鞋 'to be given tight shoes', 敲边鼓 'to tap the side drum'; colloquial; **splittable in use** — “碰了个软钉子” 'ran into a bit of a soft nail'). **The proverb** (sentence-level; folk experience: “朝霞不出门，晚霞行千里” 'Morning glow: do not go out of doors; evening glow: travel a thousand *li*.' (Our translation.)). **The two-part allegorical saying (*xiehouyu*)** (talk in two cuts: a lead-in plus an explanation, “外甥打灯笼——照旧（舅）” 'A nephew carries a lantern — it lights his uncle (the same as before).' (Our translation.); the second half is often a pun on sound). **The maxim** (in the manner of an admonition, and often with a name to own it: “虚心使人进步，骄傲使人落后” 'Modesty makes a person progress; arrogance makes a person fall behind.' (Our translation.)). The criteria for dividing the houses have been quarrelled over for decades. Three questions one can actually work with: **Character count and structure not locked fast? Meaning able to be taken apart? Origin on the page, or in mouth and ear?** — one vote to each question, and most entries can be found a house.
:::

::: theorem Three properties of the *chengyu*: four characters, allusion, double-layered meaning {#rule-sanxing}
**The four-character frame**: the frame of overwhelming predominance among *chengyu* (the five-character “莫须有”, and the longer “有志者事竟成” 'Where there is a will, the thing is in the end accomplished.' (Our translation.), are both a small minority; an exception is left a frame to itself). **Allusiveness**: the source can be pointed to (histories, books of the Masters, verse and prose, Buddhist scriptures) — a popular saying passed on by mouth and ear, even once it has become four characters (“吃一堑长一智” 'Fall in one ditch, grow one share of wisdom.' (Our translation.)), is often classed as a "proverb / common saying" rather than as a *chengyu* in the strict sense, and dictionaries do not treat it alike from one compilation to the next. **Double-layered meaning**: a literal sense plus a sense as a whole. “胸有成竹” has nothing to do with “竹子” ('bamboo') or “胸膛” ('the chest'). **The double layer is precisely the source of the diseases of miswriting a *chengyu* (taking the writing and making a sense) and of misusing one (taking a sense and making a use).** What meets all three properties is a typical member. A marginal member (a newly coined four-character frame of the “蓝天白云” type, 'blue sky and white clouds') may legitimately sit on the fence over "whether it is a *chengyu*".
:::

## Sources of the *chengyu*, and the "reserve of old grammar" {#sec-laiyuan}

::: definition Five routes in {#def-lailu}
① **Fables and myths**: 井底之蛙 ('a frog at the bottom of a well'), 螳臂当车 ('a mantis's arms against a cart'), 邯郸学步 ('learning the Handan walk'), 愚公移山 ('the foolish old man moves the mountains') — the power of the pre-Qin books of the Masters to build a scene is unmatched from ancient times to now. ② **Historical facts and allusions**: 破釜沉舟 ('smash the cauldrons and sink the boats'; at the Battle of Julu, “皆沉船破釜甑” 'They all sank the boats and smashed the cauldrons and steamers.' (Our translation.)), 四面楚歌 ('Chu songs on every side'; the encirclement at Gaixia), 纸上谈兵 ('talking warfare on paper'; the affair of Zhao Kuo 赵括, the wording a later condensation). ③ **Clippings from the text of the Classics and the Histories**: “如履薄冰” (the *Book of Songs*, “战战兢兢，如履薄冰” 'Trembling and cautious, as if treading on thin ice.' (Our translation.)), “他山” (the *Xiaoya*, 'Minor Odes', “他山之石，可以攻玉” 'Stones from another mountain can be used to work jade.' (Our translation.)). ④ **Lines condensed from later verse and prose**: “老当益壮” ('old, and all the more vigorous'; Wang Bo 王勃, *Tengwangge xu* 滕王阁序, 'Preface to the Pavilion of the Prince of Teng'), “青梅竹马” ('green plums and a bamboo horse'; a *yuefu* of Li Bai 李白, a Music Bureau poem). ⑤ **The oral, and modern new coinages**: condensed lines from political argument, of the “百花齐放” kind ('a hundred flowers blooming at once'), enter the *chengyu* — the production line for *chengyu* has never stopped work; only the output is far below what it was.
:::

::: example A gallery of earlier Chinese grammar in the *chengyu* {#ex-yufa}
Point out the phenomena of earlier Chinese grammar preserved in the following *chengyu*: “唯利是图、不耻下问、生死之、汗牛充栋、不名一钱、不远千里、是古非今、不以为然”.
::: solution
“唯利是图” = **fronted object** (the marker pattern “唯…是…”, the same construction as “唯命是从”); “不耻下问” = **putative use** (to take asking of those below as a shame); “生死肉骨” = **paired causative use** (make the dead live, make white bones grow flesh); “汗牛充栋” = **causative use** (make the oxen and horses sweat, make the building fill); “不名一钱” = **a noun used as a verb** (名 = to possess, to own); “不远千里” = **putative use** (to take a thousand *li* as far); “是古非今” = **putative use** (to take antiquity as right, and the present as wrong); “不以为然” = “然” used as a predicate, 'to consider it right' — this is precisely the key to telling apart that “不以为然” is not the same as “不以为意”. Ticket to the gallery: one *chengyu* often preserves one grammatical word already dead in modern Chinese.
:::
:::

## Miswriting · misreading · misuse: three kinds of case {#sec-bingli}

::: example Ten corrections in a row for taking the writing and making a sense {#ex-shilian}
Write the standard form of each of the following *chengyu*, and for each annotate the meaning of one character: ①名列前矛; ②莫明其妙; ③变本加利; ④再接再励; ⑤甘败下风; ⑥走头无路; ⑦黄梁美梦; ⑧迫不急待; ⑨金壁辉煌; ⑩既往不究.
::: solution
By the outpatient-and-emergency clinic of the previous chapter: ① 名列前茅 — 茅 means 包茅 ('bundled cogongrass'; the allusion to a tribute article of the state of Chu, a figure for the van of an army), not a long spear; ② 莫名其妙 — 名 = to utter (a word of the old language that lives only inside this *chengyu*); ③ 变本加厉 — 厉 = the original character of 砺, a whetstone, meaning that the degree is yet more severe; ④ 再接再厉 — 厉 is still 'to grind' (a gamecock grinding its beak), not 'to encourage'; ⑤ 甘拜下风 — 拜 is to cup the hands and bow the head; ⑥ 走投无路 — 投 = to go and seek refuge; ⑦ 黄粱一梦 — 粱 is millet (the story of the eating-house where millet rice was being steamed), not a roof beam; ⑧ 迫不及待 — 及 = to be in time; ⑨ 金碧辉煌 — 碧 = a blue-green pigment (halls of gold and jade and blue-green paint); ⑩ 既往不咎 — 咎 = to reproach. All ten cases lie in **near-homophone characters overstepping their office**: miswriting a *chengyu* is the "retrieval shortcut" disease breaking out again in the fossil layer ([[chinese-characters/standard-characters]]).
:::
:::

::: intuition Why the *chengyu* so favours the disease of "misuse" {#int-yong}
What the three cases share: **the user has met the *chengyu* only as translationese (four characters set against one near-synonym label), and has not looked up its original semantic structure**. The "dictionary sense" of a *chengyu* lies in the etymology. Two items on the prescription: fill the allusion back in, plus analyse the morphemes — whenever, before glossing the “训” of “不足为训” as “教训” ('a lesson'), one first looks that one character up, the case cures itself.
:::

::: history The name “成语”, and set-phrase studies taking their place in the hall {#sec-chengyu-shi}
The word “成语” was already there in the Han. In texts of the Han, the Wei and the Six Dynasties it mostly refers to the writings of the Six Arts and to ready-made lines of the Classics. After the Tang it gradually comes to mean "a finished sentence of old language". Notebooks of the Song made a fashion of verifying that "a saying has a source" (the various notebooks of Hong Mai 洪迈, and Wang Yinglin's 王应麟 *Kunxue jiwen* 困学纪闻, 'Record of What Was Learned through Arduous Study', all contain item-by-item studies of *chengyu* and of stock sayings). In the Qing, Zhai Hao's 翟灏 *Tongsu bian* 通俗编 ('A Compilation of Common Speech') and Qian Daxin's 钱大昕 *Hengyan lu* 恒言录 ('A Record of Constant Sayings') took common sayings and stock sayings into the genealogy together; this was the eve of the study of set phrases. The modern broad fixing of “成语” as a term of pedagogical grammar falls in the textbooks of the national language (*guoyu*) in the early twentieth century. From the 1930s, the "*chengyu* dictionary" became a class of book title on its own. Around the 1980s, *chengyu* dictionaries from such houses as the Commercial Press and the Shanghai Lexicographical Publishing House came out in competition, and the *chengyu* turned from the literati's refined taste for evidential research into a national word-list and a publishing industry. The establishment of the hegemony of the four-character frame, and three cultural projects — the downward shift of education in the Classics, the civil-service examinations in parallel prose (*pianwen*), and rhyme-books of elementary learning (of the *Youxue qionglin* 幼学琼林 type, 'The Jade Grove Primer') — are cause and effect of one another. Form is the institution.
:::

::: warning Three scenes of abuse
(1) **Piling up**: official documents and examination-hall essays fire off a run of “继往开来、砥砺前行、催人奋进” — when *chengyu* are used as decoration their meanings cancel one another, and once the density passes the threshold they become noise; (2) **Mismatched colouring**: “始作俑者” (derogatory) used to pay respect to a first originator, “登堂入室” (a step up in learning) used to praise a refurbishment, “差强人意” (roughly still acceptable) used to mean "cannot be satisfied" — register and affective colouring are the second grammar of a *chengyu*; (3) **Forced parallelism**: “不以为然／不以为意” and “应接不暇／自顾不暇” mixed up — *chengyu* close in form share morphemes, and the source of the misuse is often **similar graphs plus a similar head of meaning**.
:::

::: quiz
The correct use of “下里巴人” is ( ).
- [ ] To criticise someone for uncouth manners
- [x] To refer to a popular work of literature and art
- [ ] To stand for an elder of the village
- [ ] To praise the plainness of local architecture
::: solution
It was originally the name of a folk song of the state of Chu (set over against 《阳春》 and 《白雪》, 'Early Spring' and 'White Snow'). The meaning as a *chengyu* = a popular work of literature and art. It refers to the piece/the work, not to personal character.

::: quiz
In “汗牛充栋”, “汗” is ( ).

- [ ] An adjective: damp
- [ ] A phonetic loan for the character “汉”
::: solution
“汗牛” = to make the ox sweat (the weary labour of the carts and horses that carry the books), a survival of causative use — ranked in the same gallery as “生死肉骨”.

::: quiz
Which of the following is not a typical feature of the idiomatic phrase? ( )
- [ ] Mainly the three-character verb–object frame
- [x] A structure that cannot be split open at all
- [ ] A strong colloquial colouring
- [ ] A holistic meaning, and often an implicit metaphor
::: solution
Precisely the opposite: an idiomatic phrase **can be split** (碰了个钉子, 给他穿小鞋); what cannot be split is the strong point of the *chengyu*; only the proverb is sentence-level.

::: exercise Assigning houses {level=1}
Sort “八九不离十、耳边风、瑞雪兆丰年、打开天窗说亮话、破天荒” into the five houses, and for each give a one-vote reason.
::: solution
八九不离十 ('eight or nine, not far off ten') — a common saying (a marginal household between the idiomatic phrase and the proverb: it cannot be split, and yet it is not a complete sentence of experience); 耳边风 ('wind beside the ear') — an idiomatic phrase (an idiomatic phrase made nominal, which can be set into “把劝告当成～” 'take the advice as ~'); 瑞雪兆丰年 ('timely snow promises a good year') — a proverb (a complete sentence, experience of farming, able to stand alone as an utterance); 打开天窗说亮话 ('open the skylight and speak plain words') — a proverb (straight talk fixed as a complex sentence); 破天荒 ('to break uncultivated ground') — an idiomatic phrase (a Tang legend about *juren*, examination candidates, has an allusion behind it, but the phrase has already been generalised in colloquial use — amphibious between *chengyu* and idiomatic phrase; it is enough to note the marginal status). The point this question tests is precisely the "marginal household": full marks wherever the two votes fall half and half and the reasons hold together.
:::

::: exercise Cracking the case {level=1}
For each of “久假不归、文不加点、不赞一词、差强人意、三人成虎”, write one sentence of correct use plus one easily made wrong use (the wrong sentence need not be explained).
::: solution
久假不归: 他拿书“久假不归” ('He has the book out and it is “久假不归”.' (Our translation.)) (correct: borrowed for a long time and not returned) / wrong: 他请假久了不回来 ('He asked for leave a long while ago and has not come back.' (Our translation.)); 文不加点: 下笔“文不加点” ('The brush goes down and the writing is “文不加点”.' (Our translation.)) (correct: finished in one breath) / wrong: 文章不加标点 ('The writing has no punctuation added.' (Our translation.)); 不赞一词: 对杂议“不赞一词” ('On the mixed debate, “不赞一词”.' (Our translation.)) (correct: not uttering a single word, offering no comment) / wrong: 一句也不夸奖 ('Not one sentence of praise.' (Our translation.)); 差强人意: 表现“差强人意” ('The performance is “差强人意”.' (Our translation.)) (correct: still counts as passable) / wrong: 令人很不满意 ('It leaves one very unsatisfied.' (Our translation.)); 三人成虎: 谣言“三人成虎” ('The rumour is a case of “三人成虎”.' (Our translation.)) (correct: if those who pass it on are many, it seems true) / wrong: 三人合力成事 ('Three people join forces and get the thing done.' (Our translation.)). Whenever a "sentence of misuse" is precisely a high-frequency wrong form of the street, this exercise has met the mark.

::: exercise An investigation {level=2}
Collect five local *xiehouyu* (including one pun on sound and one pun on sense), and mark the type of pun; then choose one example and say whether it can be translated straight into written Putonghua (standard Mandarin), as a test of the claim that "a *xiehouyu* = the rhetoric of a dialect".
::: solution
A specimen: ① “外甥打灯笼——照旧（舅）” ('A nephew carries a lantern — it lights his uncle (the same as before).' (Our translation.)) = a **pun on the character** (照舅 → 照旧); ② “小葱拌豆腐——一青（清）二白” ('Spring onion tossed with bean curd — one green (clear) and two white.' (Our translation.)), a pun on the character; ③ “哑巴吃黄连——有苦说不出” ('A mute eats *huanglian* — there is a bitterness he cannot tell.' (Our translation.)) = the meaning-derived type (no homophony); ④ “竹篮打水——一场空” ('Drawing water with a bamboo basket — the whole of it empty.' (Our translation.)), meaning-derived; ⑤ “狗咬吕洞宾——不识好人心” ('A dog bites Lü Dongbin — it cannot tell a good person's heart.' (Our translation.)), the story type. The straight-translation experiment: once a homophone type leaves the sound system of the dialect (青 and 清 the same sound), the pun collapses — a *xiehouyu* is a **set phrase of the sound-dependent type**, and this is the structural reason it is harder than a *chengyu* to enter a formal register.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is the *chengyu* a liability or an asset? {level=3}
Two claims: "*chengyu* are hard to learn, easy to get wrong, and drag expression slower" and "four characters of a *chengyu* are worth a thousand words". Write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: how should the density of *chengyu* in the written language of modern Chinese be evaluated? Requirements: cite "double-layered meaning" to explain the source of the disease of misuse; give one example from the "reserve of old methods" (causative use / putative use / fronted object) to show the teaching value of the *chengyu*; and give a testable prediction of your own on "the making of new *chengyu* (whether 内卷, 'involution', and 躺平, 'lying flat', can be admitted)".
::: solution
Points for marking: pin the source of the disease of misuse accurately on "the literal sense coming unhooked from the sense as a whole"; use a *chengyu* from the gallery as evidence (to teach “唯利是图” is to teach the fronted object); a prediction about new *chengyu* that has indicators one can check (use across communities, a lifespan of ≥10 years, the structure fixed in form, recognition after the fact by a dictionary — and a clear statement that most present candidates will die, the mechanism of elimination being competition inside the four-character frame); an ability to propose an operationalisation of "density" (the rate at which *chengyu* occur per thousand characters) and a reasonable interval; the stance need only be consistent in itself, and there is no need to take a side.
:::

::: summary
The five houses of set phrases: the *chengyu* (four characters, allusion, a double-layered sense), the idiomatic phrase (three-character verb–object, splittable, colloquial), the proverb (sentence-level experience), the *xiehouyu* (two cuts plus homophony, dependent on sound), the maxim (an admonition, often with a name attached). Five sources of the *chengyu*: fables in the books of the Masters, allusions to historical events, clippings from classical texts, lines condensed from verse and prose, modern new coinages. At the same time it is a **reserve of earlier Chinese grammar** (唯利是图 = fronted object, 汗牛 = causative, 不耻下问 = putative, 名／然／是 = living specimens of dead words). Three names of disease: miswriting (near-homophones overstepping), a mistaken sense (taking the writing and making a sense), misuse (mismatched colouring, piling up). Two items of prescription: fill the allusion back in, and analyse the morphemes. The broadening of the concept “成语” is a modern achievement of national-language textbooks and of the publishing industry — the hegemony of the four-character frame is itself the sediment of cultural projects.
:::
