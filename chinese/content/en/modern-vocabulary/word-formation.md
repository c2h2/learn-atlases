---
title: "How words are formed"
---

The previous lesson established morphemes and words; this lesson looks at **how they are assembled** into words. Chinese has three main processes for assembling words: **compounding** (root + root), **derivation** (root + affix) and **reduplication** (a morpheme paired with itself). Of the three, compounding holds an absolute predominance — Chinese is therefore typed as 'an analytic language in which compounding is highly developed'.

## Five compound types {#sec-wuzhong}

::: definition The internal syntax of compound words {#def-fuhe}
The way two roots combine is isomorphic with the traditional syntactic constituents, and falls into five types:
**Coordinate (parallel)** — 朋友, 道路, 买卖, 开关, 窗户, 国家: the morphemes stand as equals. Note two kinds of phenomenon: in 窗户 and 国家 the meaning leans to one of the two (**compounds of biased meaning**); 买卖, by contrast, has the two morphemes generalised as a whole into the single sense 生意 ('business').
**Modifier–head** — 火车, 雪白, 快速, 微波炉: the first root modifies the second (火车 is a vehicle powered by fire; 雪白 is white as snow is white).
**Verb–object (governing)** — 司机, 管家, 注意, 投资, 洗澡: the second root is the patient. 司机 is 司＋机, the 司 of 司其职 ('to be in charge of that duty'), that is, one who is in charge of a machine — not the mistaken analysis, of the kind that compresses a whole sentence, as 'a person who drives a machine'.
**Verb–complement (supplementing)** — 提高, 推翻, 改变, 学会, 改正: the second root supplements the first by stating its result. This type is **extremely productive** (compounds with a resultative complement are a machine for new words).
**Subject–predicate (stating)** — 地震, 心疼, 海啸, 日食: the first root is the topic, and the second root states something of it.
:::

::: theorem Assigning a structure looks to the double standard of 'historical stratum + present-day transparency' {#rule-liangzhun}
The real trouble is **reanalysis**. 司令, for example, was originally verb–object (司＋令); people today can no longer see the verbal sense of 司, and their linguistic intuition takes the whole as a noun of office — the divergence between the structural sense and the reanalysis is itself a living lesson in the feel for strata. The standard procedure: look up the motivation by which the word was formed (the origin column of a dictionary) → set it against a present-day substitution frame → if both are possible, report both. Structural ambiguity is a permanent resident of word formation, not an accident.
:::

::: example Eight judged one by one {#ex-balian}
Judge the structural type: 地震, 雪花, 提高, 民主, 火热, 注定, 司令, 笔直.
::: solution
民主 — as a modern translated word, a subject–predicate analysis sits more smoothly (人民作主, 'the people are the masters'); if one follows the old sense 民之主 ('master of the people'), it is modifier–head. Both accounts are acceptable.
:::
:::

## Derivation: affixes and 'quasi-affixes' {#sec-paidu}

::: definition The three-piece affix set and the little nominalisers {#def-cui}
**Prefixes**: 老- (老虎, 老大, 老王 — 虎 does not rely on 老 to express its meaning), 第- (第一), 阿- (阿爸, 阿Q). **Suffixes**: -子 (桌子, 罩子 — fully bleached; contrast the root 子 in 莲子 and 种子, which is still to be taken as 子实, 'seed'), -儿 (花儿, 玩儿, 门儿 — a colouring of northern speech, and it also distinguishes both sense and class: 头 (脑袋, 'the head') against 头儿 (领导, 'a leader'; 端头, 'an end')), -头 (石头, 甜头, 苦头). Chinese has no systematic infix; that is passed over. The difference between the 子 of 桌**子** and the 子 of 莲子 is precisely the difference between **affix and root**: the former has had its meaning evaporate, and is productive in its position; the latter still carries the sense 子实 ('seed').
:::

::: theorem Quasi-affixes: the most active intermediate layer of word-making in Chinese {#rule-leicui}
Between the 'pure root' (桌) and the 'pure affix' (老-) there is a broad band: the **quasi-affix** — half empty in meaning, fixed in position, and highly productive. The roster on the books in contemporary Chinese: **～化** (现代化, 绿化), **～性** (可读性, 确定性), **～者** (读者, 作者), **～员** (演员, 售货员 — 员 originally had a concrete meaning, and is now productive as a class of posts), **～手** (歌手, 高手), **～热** (旅游热), **～坛** (体坛, 影坛), **～盲** (文盲, 科盲, 股盲), **～吧** (网吧, 书吧), **～门** (the scandal suffix generalised from 水门事件, the Watergate affair). Three questions for judging: is the meaning half empty? Is the position fixed (always at the end)? Can it take on new items in batches (some every week)? Pass all three, and it is entered in the register — this 'production line on which grammaticalisation is under way' is precisely the sexiest stretch of Chinese word formation.
:::

::: example Issuing birth certificates to new words {#ex-chusheng}
Which process does each of 考研, 内卷, 逆袭, 躺平, 元宇宙 and 晒娃 belong to?
::: solution
考研 (a verb–object compound: 考＋研〔究〕 — 研 is a morpheme got by abbreviation; see the section on abbreviation below); 内卷 (modifier–head: 内＋卷; the sense of squeeze-style competition is a new coinage); 逆袭 (modifier–head — 逆 modifies the manner of 袭); 躺平 (a serial-verb compound: 躺而平之, 'lie down and level it' — Chinese also admits word-making by serial verbs, of the kind of 打架 and 起床); 晒娃 (verb–object plus a semantic borrowing: 晒 has taken on a new function through the 'display' sense of English *share*, while the written form has not moved); 元宇宙 (the modifier–head framework of 元＋宇宙, coined in imitation of the way *metaverse* is translated). One question strings five processes together, which shows that labels of word formation are **coordinates of analysis**, not mutually exclusive drawers.
:::

## Reduplication and the other processes {#sec-chongdie}

::: definition Reduplicated forms {#def-cd}
The repetition of a morpheme or a word upon itself: **AA** forming a word — 妈妈, 常常, 渐渐 (nouns and adverbs of reduplicated syllables; reduplication is here a means of **word formation**); **reduplication that builds a form** — 高高, 干干净净, 研究研究 (reduplication adds no new word; it only adds to the original word **grammatical meanings** such as degree, totality and a brief attempt, and it belongs to **form-building**; the several families — ABB, as in 红彤彤, AABB and ABAB — are the same in this). One operation, two faces: **the boundary between word formation and form-building** shows its face here.
:::

::: history Abbreviation and the 'short-form industry': a fourth process of Chinese word formation {#sec-suolue}
Besides compounding, derivation and reduplication, modern Chinese has a further line that produces words in batches: **abbreviation**. Examples: 北京大学 → 北大, 科学技术 → 科技, 人民代表大会 → 人大, 奥林匹克运动会 → 奥运会, 高速铁路 → 高铁. The mechanism falls into four types: the **extraction type** (one end is taken from each coordinate item: 科技, and 清华北大 → 清北), the **bracketing type** (the outer frame is taken and the core is joined on: 立交桥, 首都机场), the **numerical type** (三好学生, 双减, 三农) and the **collective-name type** (公安＝公＋安, coordinate items fused into one class). Since the 1950s abbreviated words have come in a gusher (扫盲 → 知青 → 两弹一星 → 高铁 → 双减), and the sequence of the times is clear at a glance — abbreviation is fodder that the **tempo of administration and the media** feeds to word formation; half the history of new words in Chinese is a history of abbreviation.
:::

::: warning Three pitfalls in analysing word formation
(3) **Taking a quasi-affix for a pure affix.** ～盲 still carries the lively image of 'being unable to see a certain field' — 科盲 and 股盲 hold, while 色盲 belongs to another family, that of medical terms. High productivity is not the same as evaporation of meaning; **grammaticalisation is a gradient, not a switch**.
:::

::: example An inventory of the grammatical meanings of reduplication {#ex-cdqd}
Write out what meaning each of the three reduplications 高高, 干干净净 and 研究研究 adds to the original word.
::: solution
高高 (the degree is deepened, the sense 'excessively high / very high' — reduplication of an adjective also has the character of describing a state); 干干净净 (the scale is filled: entirely, and thoroughly — 'reduplication marks totality / strengthening of degree'); 研究研究 (the **brief-attempt** aspect: the sense of 一下, 'a bit, have a try', is built into verbal reduplication). All three grammatical meanings (degree, totality, quantity of time) are **supplied by the reduplicated form and not by the meaning of the word** — this is the reason for classing reduplication as a morphological operation. The AA noun 妈妈, by contrast, has no added meaning of this kind; it belongs to word-forming reduplication. The two cases are filed apart.
:::

::: quiz
The compound type of 提高 is ( ).
- [ ] Modifier–head
- [ ] Verb–object
- [x] Verb–complement
- [ ] Coordinate
::: solution
提 is the action and 高 is the resultative complement — verb–complement. The type is productive (推翻, 改正, 打倒, 学会), and is an important production line for new words in Chinese.
:::
:::

::: quiz
Of the following, the 子 which is an affix (and not a root) is ( ).
- [ ] 莲子
- [ ] 种子
- [x] 筷子
- [ ] 子弟
::: solution
The 子 of 筷子 has no concrete meaning, a fixed position, and can attach to X in batches — a typical noun suffix. The 子 of 莲子 and 种子 still expresses 子实 ('seed'); the 子 of 子弟 is even the original meaning, 儿子辈 ('the generation of sons').
:::
:::

::: quiz
What is characteristic of a 'quasi-affix' is ( ).
- [ ] The meaning has evaporated completely
- [x] The meaning is half empty, the position is fixed, and it is highly productive
- [ ] It occurs only in *liánmián* words (连绵词, disyllabic words whose parts are not separate morphemes)
- [ ] It must have the neutral tone
::: solution
The three defining questions of the quasi-affix: the meaning is half empty (～门 has left the proper name 水门), the position is fixed (always placed after), and it is productive (it coins new words in batches). 'Complete evaporation' is the standard for a pure affix (老-); *lianmian* words and the neutral tone have nothing to do with this.
:::

::: exercise Judging the structure {level=1}
Judge: 司机, 雪白, 心疼, 提高, 朋友, 注定.
::: solution
司机 (verb–object); 雪白 (modifier–head, white as snow is white); 心疼 (subject–predicate; a modifier–head double reading may be noted); 提高 (verb–complement); 朋友 (coordinate; the change of meaning between ancient and modern is not examined); 注定 (modifier–head — 注 modifies the manner of 定; the popular judgement that it is verb–object must be corrected).
:::

::: exercise Birth certificates {level=1}
Write a 'word-formation birth certificate' for each of the following new words (process + internal pattern + motivation): 高铁, 点赞, 班味.
::: solution
高铁 = abbreviation + modifier–head (高速铁路 → 高铁); 点赞 = a verb–object assembly that has taken on a new sense (赞 is nominalised from the name of a button, and then abbreviated from 点一个赞); 班味 = modifier–head + the quasi-suffix ～味 (铜臭味 → 人情味 → X味; the new sense, 'the air peculiar to a given group': the weariness of people who go to work). Full marks once all three certificates pass the two columns 'process + motivation'.
:::
:::

::: exercise A survey of biased meaning {level=2}
Collect five compounds of biased meaning (窗户 and 国家 have already taken the slots; please find your own), and design a substitution-frame test that proves the bias.
::: solution
Acceptable samples include 睡觉 (觉 contributes no meaning), 国家 (it refers only to 国, 'the state'), and 恩怨 biased towards 怨 when used as in 记他的怨 ('bearing his grievance in mind'). The standard of the test: the item to which the meaning is biased can be replaced by one synonymous item, and the whole is not damaged (窗户开着 ↔ 窗开着 holds, while 户开着 does not). Whoever cites 东西 (in the sense 'thing') may receive extra marks: it has lost even the original directional meanings.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: are quasi-affixes a flood or living water? {level=3}
房奴, 卡奴 and 孩奴, together with '×门' and '×吧', appear time and again in the papers. Some fear 'lexical inflation'; some welcome the 'dividend of productivity'. Write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters, take a side, and defend it. Requirements: apply in full the three judging questions along 'pure affix — quasi-affix — root'; cite one quasi-affix which you consider already dead or to have died young, and explain the mechanism by which it was eliminated; give your judgement on 'lettered words invading word formation' (Watergate → ～门, E-～).
::: solution
Marking points: the three questions are applied to concrete words (not copied from the definition); the writer can cite one that died young and state why it was eliminated (for example ～吧 ebbed after the 网吧 boom — evidence from a corpus, or from linguistic intuition, must be given); word formation with lettered words is treated in terms of mechanism — whether what is borrowed is the **pattern** or the **material** (what ～门 copies is the path by which the proper name Watergate was generalised); the structure is clear, and there is no hard error of historical fact.
:::

::: summary
The three great processes of Chinese word formation: compounding (five types — coordinate, modifier–head, verb–object, verb–complement and subject–predicate — of which verb–complement is the most productive); derivation (老- / 阿- / 第- together with -子 / -儿 / -头, and above these the intermediate workshop of 'quasi-affixes', half empty in meaning, fixed in position and highly productive: ～化, ～性, ～者, ～盲, ～门); reduplication (two faces: word-forming AA, and form-building AABB / verbal reduplication). In addition, a fourth production line: extractive abbreviation (北大, 科技, '三好'). In assigning a structure, the synchronic and the diachronic are kept in separate ledgers; where both are possible, both are reported as they are. Becoming an affix is a gradient, not a switch, and the list of quasi-affixes is always being updated — that is the electrocardiogram of a healthy lexical ecology.
:::
:::
