---
title: "Layers of the lexicon"
---

Spread open a dictionary of modern Chinese, and words are not one solid plate of iron, but **have strata**: at the very bottom is bedrock that several thousand years cannot scour away (basic vocabulary), and weighing on top of it are visitors from dynasty after dynasty — classical words, dialect words, loanwords, new words. This lesson puts a 'geological hammer' in the student's hand: crack any word open, and hear which layer it belongs to.

## Basic vocabulary and general vocabulary {#sec-jiben}

::: definition The bipartition and the three properties {#def-jwc}
The lexical system falls into two great blocks: **basic vocabulary** — that part which the whole people cannot do without in daily life, which lasts through the centuries and the millennia, and which serves as the basis of word-formation (天, 地, 人, 山, 水, 父, 母, 走, 坐, 大, 小, 一, 二, 我, 你); all the rest is called, collectively, **general vocabulary** — the sum of new words, classical words, dialect words, loanwords, occupational terms, argot and the like. The doctrine of the 'three properties' is a theory imported from Soviet linguistics (after the publication of Stalin's *Marxism and Problems of Linguistics* in 1950, it became the received account in textbooks in China): **extensiveness** (in common use among the whole people, with no division by region or social stratum), **stability** (not changing over a thousand years; "日、月、人" span three thousand years), **productivity** (as germs of word-formation, they derive in batches: “人” gives birth to “人民、人员、工人、人生、人造”).
:::

::: theorem The three properties are an insight and also a soft ruler {#rule-sanxing2}
The insight lies in the **view by strata**: the lexicon has a 'core—periphery', and so graded word lists for teaching (the graded words of the *Outline of Graded Vocabulary for Chinese Proficiency*, 《汉语水平词汇等级大纲》, and the companion word lists of the *Table of General Standard Chinese Characters*, 《通用规范汉字表》) and the fullness or brevity of dictionaries all have a ground to stand on; what is soft lies in the **operation** — extensiveness is measured by word frequency in a corpus, yet genre distribution is mixed in with it; stability rests on survival through time, yet it comes down to 'how many years count as stable'; productivity rests on the rate of derivation, yet it treats quasi-affixes of the “山” type and empty affixes of the “们” type all alike — the three properties define one another, the boundaries are blurred, and the scholarly world has long since reckoned with them. The textbook's stance: **use it as a map, do not use it as a written verdict** — to judge whether a word is a basic word, see whether it meets, all at once, the three tendencies 'high frequency, long endurance, able to have children'; for a borderline case, admit that the doubt remains.
:::

## Classical words and historical words: two archaeological trowels {#sec-guyu}

::: definition Distinguishing the three types {#def-sanxing3}
**Historical words**: the things referred to have already perished, and the words have followed them into the historical archive — 皇帝, 丞相, 妾, 贡院, 鼎甲, 太监. **Classical words**: the things referred to are still here, and only the way of saying them has changed; the words live on, in an 'old waistcoat', in a particular register — one calls one's father “家父”, one asks someone to criticise by saying “雅正”, and the Classical Chinese function words “之乎者也” squat in the written layer. **Old vernacular words**: words that were alive in the early-modern vernacular (Yuan drama, and fiction of the Ming and the Qing) and are dead in modern speech — 衙门, 造化, 不才, 在下, 小二, 客官.
:::

::: example One word, one trowel: judging the layer in practice {#ex-pc}
“朕、丞相、内卷、您、衙门、导致、吧” — which layer does each belong to?
::: solution
“朕” is a historical word (the institutional self-designation has died out); “丞相” is a historical word (the office has died out); “内卷” is a new word (and it carries the colouring of an occupational term: anthropology → a social vogue); “您” is a **commonly used honorific** outside the basic layer of Putonghua (standard Mandarin) (the entry of this northern-dialect form into the common language is already complete); “衙门” is an old vernacular word (now used as a jocular name, or in historical narrative); “导致” is a general word (a word of written colouring with a pejorative tendency: “导致灾难” will do, “导致成功” is awkward — colouring is the ledger of register); “吧” is a modal particle, and belongs to the basic vocabulary (function words all lie in the bedrock — basic words are not divided into content words and function words).
:::
:::

::: intuition Why morphemes of Classical Chinese are 'dead but not stiff' {#int-bzj}
Classical words withdraw in whole batches, but the **morphemes** among them are reassigned, in great numbers, into the word layer: “民” does not make a word on its own (in speech one says “人” or “老百姓”), yet it keeps house inside “人民、民警、民主”; and “语、日、月” are all the same. The mechanism is the Chinese **morpheme recipe**: written colouring does not lie in any one word, but in the combination of morphemes (“吃饭” 'to have a meal', set against “用餐” 'to take a meal', set against “用膳” 'to partake of food' — the three grades of stratum are clear at a glance). So the short cut to learning classical words is not memorising words one by one, but building a **morpheme dossier**: the five characters “之、其、所、以、于” hold up half the skeleton of the written language.
:::

## Dialect words and loanwords: two doors for incoming guests {#sec-fangyan}

::: definition Conditions for entering the common language {#def-jinru}
For a dialect word to enter Putonghua, the textbooks lay down three conditions: **necessity** (the common language has no word of this meaning, or else has a gap — “搞” filled the gap in the division of labour among “做／弄／干”), **generality** (a high degree of currency across dialects), **clarity** (the meaning is stable and does not drift). Star cases: **搞** (a southern dialect word that went north, and has long since entered the dictionaries), **名堂、拆台、拉钩、没门儿、磨叽、埋汰** (contributions of northern Mandarin (*guanhua*) and of Northeastern speech), **尴尬、蹩脚、拎得清** (Wu), **埋单、靓、炒鱿鱼** (Yue (Cantonese), which in the 1980s, with the opening-up, joined a 'northern expedition of southern words'). Whether or not they are imported, and whether or not they are kept once imported, the market is the final court: 北漂 and 忽悠 are alive and kicking, while a batch of southern words has already cooled as the wave of migrant labour subsided.
:::

::: example Rewriting register: three strata of one and the same thing {#ex-gx}
Rewrite “我爸妈挺疼我，不让我干家务” ('My mum and dad are really fond of me, and won't let me do the housework.' Our translation.) into the written register, and into the two styles of the official document and the family letter, and get a feel for the swapping of morpheme ingredients.
::: solution
Written register: “父母钟爱，未尝使操持家务。” ('My parents cherish me dearly, and have never had me take charge of the housework.' Our translation.) Official-document style (a rare occasion, but it can be used for training): “该生家中对其照顾周全，家务参与较少。” ('At home this student is cared for with every attention, and takes relatively little part in the housework.' Our translation.) Table of correspondences: 爸妈 → 父母 → 家中; 疼 → 钟爱 → 照顾; 不让 → 未尝使 → 未参与; 干家务 → 操持家务 → 家务劳动. What every cell changes is a **different stratum of synonymous morphemes**: the colloquial layer, the Classical Chinese layer, the layer of administrative official style. Rewriting is not a competition in 'making it elegant'; it is practice in the appropriateness of register — the teacher of Chinese who marks an essay 'affected' is marking precisely a 'mismatch of strata'.
:::
:::

::: warning Three lapses of decorum in the abuse of classical words
(1) **Honorific and humble forms out of place**: “敬请赐复” ('I respectfully request the favour of a reply') written to a friend; “令爱” ('your daughter') used of one's own daughter (one should say “小女”, 'my daughter'); and, after “抛砖引玉” ('to cast a brick to draw the jade'), holding forth for a full forty minutes. (2) **Classical and vernacular half-cooked**: “既然我们已结束会谈，兹将纪要函告” ('Now that we have finished the talks, I hereby write to inform you of the minutes.' Our translation.) — half classical and half vernacular, resting on neither side. (3) **Historical words used as living words**: in the news, to call a coach “教头”, or a head of a school “山长”, happens now and then for the sake of amusement; a formal document of appointment still says “县长” and not “县令” — reviving a historical word that is an institutional title calls for the utmost caution. What this example makes plain is the baseline “historical word = the label of an institution that has already died”. The principle of appropriateness (the 'register' of systemic functional linguistics) is the final judge of the stratification of the lexicon: **there is no wrong word, only a wrong place**.
:::

::: history The birth of lexical stratigraphy: from *Mashi wentong* to the 'theory of basic vocabulary' {#sec-cengli}
In the same period, surveys of the vocabulary of Chinese dialects, and research on the vocabulary of the early-modern vernacular (exegetical study of the vocabulary of *Water Margin* (《水浒传》) and *The Plum in the Golden Vase* (《金瓶梅》) became a great vogue), gave solid evidence for a 'dialect layer' and a 'vernacular layer';
:::

::: quiz
Which of the following is a 'historical word' ( )?
- [ ] 令尊
- [x] 丞相
- [ ] 搞
- [ ] 内卷
::: solution
丞相 perished with the system of official posts — a historical word; 令尊 is a classical word (the business of referring to “你父亲” 'your father' is still here; only the wording and the occasion have changed); 搞 and 内卷 belong, respectively, to the dialect layer and the new-word layer.
:::
:::

::: quiz
In the doctrine of the 'three properties', the one that can most readily be operationalised (that can be measured) is usually ( ).
- [ ] extensiveness
- [ ] stability
- [x] productivity
- [ ] universality
::: solution
Productivity can be counted from the size of a family of derived words (the character-family of “人” has several dozen entries taken into the dictionaries); the thresholds for extensiveness and for stability, and the specification of what is counted, are still being quarrelled over without settlement — and this is exactly why the doctrine of the three properties has been reckoned down to a 'soft ruler'.
:::
:::

::: quiz
“没门儿” entered Putonghua mainly by which of the three conditions ( )?
- [ ] Necessity (the common language has no single word for the marketplace tone of a flat refusal)
- [ ] Generality (audiences of film and television, north and south, all understand it)
- [ ] Clarity (the meaning is stable)
- [x] The above, acting together
::: solution
An event of a word's entry almost never rests on any single one of them: there is a gap between the sense “拒绝” ('to refuse') and the marketplace tone of flatly turning someone down; the media spread the word; the meaning is single — only the three together, joining forces, obtain a household registration in the dictionary.
:::

::: exercise Judging the layer {level=1}
Label the stratum of each of the following words (basic / general — words of classical and modern Chinese / historical / dialect / loanword / new word): 百姓, 嬷嬷, 买单, 秀 (show), 禾, 桌, 微信, 朕.
::: solution
百姓: the classical word, a general name for “民”, which by way of “老百姓” settled into the basic layer — class it as the 'precipitated layer of a classical word'; 嬷嬷: a dialect term of address, or a jocular name (as to the source, there is one view that it is a Manchu loanword; noting “待考” 'to be investigated' is enough to score); 买单: a Yue dialect word that has entered the common language; 秀: a new word, a borrowing by sound from English *show*; 禾: the Classical Chinese / southern-dialect layer (in the spoken common language the work is divided between “稻谷” and “秧苗”); 微信: a new word; 朕: a historical word; 桌: a basic word (already stable in the Ming). Whoever, for items of the “百姓、桌” kind, answers “基本” ('basic'), and who dares to mark “嬷嬷” as doubtful, is judged to have a feel for the strata.
:::
:::

::: exercise Correcting honorific and humble forms {level=1}
“我对大家的支持抛砖引玉讲三点，还望各位不吝赐教；我的作品拙见如下，请雅正。” ('With everyone's support I shall, casting a brick to draw the jade, make three points, and I hope you will not begrudge your instruction; the humble view of my work is as follows — please graciously correct it.' Our translation.) — Pick out four improprieties and correct them.
::: solution
① After “抛砖引玉”, to hold forth oneself through three points runs counter to the yielding of place that is in “引玉” (either change the wording to “我先抛砖” 'let me cast the brick first', or say less); ② “我的作品拙见如下” piles bed upon bed and roof upon roof — “拙作” ('my clumsy work') and “拙见” ('my humble view') each humble one thing, so take only one; ③ “雅正” is an honorific for asking someone to revise; “请雅正” will do, but to offer “雅正” to everyone one meets becomes a discourtesy of mere piling-up; ④ in a single passage the density of “赐教、雅正、拙见、抛砖引玉” is overloaded. Honorific and humble words are like perfume: two drops are refined, four drops smother. A standard revision: “我先谈几点粗浅看法，算是抛砖引玉，请大家多提意见。拙见如下，请指正。” ('Let me first set out a few rough views, counting it as casting a brick to draw the jade; please give me more of your comments. My humble opinion follows; please point out what should be put right.' Our translation.) — Words of humility and respect are living fossils of the classical stratum; to use them wrong = the scene of a stratigraphic crash.
:::
:::

::: exercise Fieldwork {level=2}
From elders of your home dialect area, collect five dialect words 'for which the common language has no ready-made equivalent', and for each write, one by one: the sound, the meaning, and whether it can be replaced without loss by a synonymous expression in the common language. By the three conditions, judge its 'potential for entering Putonghua'.
::: solution
What is marked is the method, not the number of words. Jin “夜来” (昨天 'yesterday') — “昨天” in the common language can replace it without loss, so judge that there is no necessity; Wu “戆大” (a fool, and one with a dull look) has, beyond “傻瓜” ('a fool'), a further shade of meaning that mocks and yet is light — the near-synonyms are not equivalent, so there is a gap; Northeastern “磕碜” (ugly / shabby / disgraceful) is wider in meaning than any single word of the common language — a gap is enough to apply for entry (in the end one looks at generality and at clarity).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: has the doctrine of the three properties gone out of date? {level=3}
Using the material of this lesson, write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: in the age of corpora and of word frequencies on the internet, is there still any need for the 'three properties of basic vocabulary' to exist? You are to cite one attempt, verified with your own hands, to operationalise the three properties (make a word-frequency count, or a count of a word-family, for such items as “我／的／内卷／尴尬”); to discuss the stratigraphic assumptions of word lists of the kind of the *List of Commonly Used Words in Modern Chinese* (《现代汉语常用词表》); and to give a position on 'whether textbooks should keep this doctrine or delete it'.
::: solution
Points for the mark: the count made with one's own hands is genuine (even if the figures are cut from an existing table of word frequencies); one can point out that the true legacy of the doctrine of the three properties is 'a consciousness of lexical stratification', and not the criteria themselves; of lists of common words, one can state their standard of grading (frequency plus domain of distribution), which is precisely the three properties turned into an engineering practice; the position is clear and can be defended (for example, “保留改名‘核心词与外围词’，删三性判据” 'Keep it, renamed “core words and peripheral words”, and delete the criteria of the three properties.' Our translation.). Those who take the three properties as axioms and prove them, or who take them as false evidence and beat them to death with one stick, both have marks deducted for analysis.
:::

::: summary
The lexicon has two lithologies: basic vocabulary (extensiveness · stability · productivity — the three properties are a theoretical soft ruler imported from the Soviet Union, and a consciousness of stratification is the true legacy) and general vocabulary (the several layers: classical, historical, old vernacular, dialect, loanword, new word). Classical words change their waistcoat while the thing is still there; historical words are buried with the institution; old vernacular words lie in the literary register; morphemes of Classical Chinese are dead but not stiff, and are reassigned as the filler of disyllabic words; dialect words gain entry on the three certificates of necessity, generality and clarity, and words of Yue and words of the Northeast are the two largest contingents of 'new immigrants' of the present day. Rewriting and appropriateness are the ground on which the strata can put their strength to use — there is no wrong word, only a wrong place.
:::
:::
