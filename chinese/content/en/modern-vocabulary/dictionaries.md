Every judgement of the previous eight lessons — this is a morpheme, that is a separable word, this sense is an extended meaning — has in the end to come back to a place where it can be struck on the table and put to the proof: the **dictionary**. This lesson teaches you to use a reference work until it is worn through: to recognise the way in, to read the guide to the reader, to look a thing up accurately, and to compare until a result comes out, and along the way to see how this craft of a thousand years made its way from the bamboo slip to the search box.

## The family of reference works, and the way in {#sec-jiuzu}

::: definition Three types, plus miscellaneous {#def-sanxing}
**Character dictionaries** take the character as the entry, and keep the three accounts of form, sound and meaning: the *Xinhua zidian* 《新华字典》 ('New China Character Dictionary'; first edition 1953, edited in succession by Wei Jiangong and other noted scholars, a national tool on the scale of a world print run), the *Kangxi zidian* 《康熙字典》 ('The Kangxi Dictionary'; 1716, 214 radicals), the *Hanyu da zidian* 《汉语大字典》 ('The Great Dictionary of Chinese Characters'; more than sixty thousand characters, ancient graphs and textual citations both complete). **Word dictionaries (dictionaries of the language)** take the word as the entry, and lay the weight on collocation and usage: the *Xiandai Hanyu cidian* 《现代汉语词典》 ('Dictionary of Modern Chinese'; first edition 1978, the compilation directed in succession by Lü Shuxiang and Ding Shengshu, part-of-speech labelling introduced across the board from the revised edition of 1993), the *Xiandai Hanyu guifan cidian* 《现代汉语规范词典》 ('The Standard Dictionary of Modern Chinese'). **Allusion dictionaries / encyclopaedias** collect allusions and the names of things: the *Ciyuan* 《辞源》 ('The Source of Words'; 1915, the Commercial Press, for the reading of old books alone), the *Cihai* 《辞海》 ('Sea of Words'; 1936, on the encyclopaedic line). Miscellaneous: idiom dictionaries, synonym dictionaries (the distinguishing type is a speciality of Chinese), dialect dictionaries (the *Xiandai Hanyu fangyan da cidian* 《现代汉语方言大词典》 ('The Great Dictionary of Modern Chinese Dialects'), 42 volumes), compendia of ancient graphs, rhyme dictionaries and handbooks of character origins. Choosing a book is like choosing a medicine: **for reading old books, pair the *Ciyuan* and the *Hanyu da zidian*; for writing, pair the *Xianhan* 《现汉》 and a distinguishing dictionary; for looking up character origins, pair the revised tools of the *Shuowen* 《说文》 family (*Shuowen jiezi*, *Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters**)**.
:::

## Using one dictionary until it is worn through {#sec-yongchuan}

::: example A full biography of the character “打” {#ex-da}
Look up the “打” entry in the *Xiandai Hanyu cidian*: note how many readings it has and how many senses, find one sense marked as written and one sense marked as spoken, and how the dictionary handles the filing of “打电话、打酱油、打酒”.
::: solution
“打” has two readings (dǎ／dá; the classifier “一打” comes from a transliteration of dozen — note that the dictionary does not mark a foreign origin after the “dá” sense; that is the job of the *Xiandai Hanyu wailaici cidian* 《现代汉语外来词词典》 ('Dictionary of Loanwords in Modern Chinese')); nearly twenty senses, radiating from the original meaning “击” ('to strike'): an abstract sense (打官司, 'to go to law'), a preposition-like set phrase (打他那儿路过, 'to pass by his place'), and the forming of a phase complement (打听到, 'to find out by asking'). Senses of the “买” ('to buy') type, of the “打酱油／打酒” kind, are often marked 〈方〉 or gathered under the compound entry “打酒” — **the dictionary uses the four small triangles “方、书、口、谚” to stitch the strata of register into the layout** (where the axis of colouring discussed in [[chinese-characters/standard-characters]] signs its name inside the dictionary). To have looked one character through is to have finished a semester of the vocabulary course.
:::
:::

::: example No loss in being able to read the guide to the reader {#ex-fanli}
The guide to the reader of the *Xianhan* states: words with variant readings follow the *Putonghua yidu ci shenyin biao* 《普通话异读词审音表》 ('Table of Adjudicated Pronunciations for Words with Variant Readings in Putonghua'); simplified characters and words with variant graphic forms take the national standard as authority. Answer from this: why, in the same book, is “消息” marked with the neutral tone, while the old sense of “消息” is not? Why, as between “笔画／笔划”, is “笔画” the main entry?
::: solution
(The basis now in force is the 1985 *Putonghua yidu ci shenyin biao*; after a new adjudicated draft of 2016 was published to solicit comment, it has still not been formally promulgated, and the adjustment of certain character readings has been left in suspense along with it — the dictionary therefore 'does not change its word', which is precisely a model of consistency of procedure.)
:::
:::

::: intuition Where a dictionary's 'authority' comes from
It does not come from the editors' titles, but from three pieces of blunt hard work: **the scale of the evidence** (the *Xianhan* built its files early, with several hundred thousand cards made by hand; the *Hanyu da cidian* 《汉语大词典》 ('The Great Dictionary of Chinese') was a campaign of a thousand noted scholars over more than ten years), **the consistency of the procedure** (the guide to the reader is the constitution; everyone operates by the same definitions and the same standards for splitting), **the honesty of revision** (a website that is wrong once a year is railed at by no one; a great canon unrevised for ten years is railed at by everyone). In the age of AI these three are worth more: a model can give a 'definition', but it cannot give 'an editorial procedure that can be held to account' — which is why, when a large model answers on word meaning, one must still **cite a dictionary**.
:::

::: history From the scroll to the search box: three revolutions in the form of reference works {#sec-xingtai}
The first: **the age of classification, of classified compendia and rhyme dictionaries** (the *Erya* 《尔雅》 ('Approaching the Elegant') classifies by meaning; the *Peiwen yunfu* 《佩文韵府》 ('The Peiwen Treasury of Rhymes') is arranged by rhyme), and retrieval depended on reciting the catalogue; the second: **the age of the modern bound volume** (from the *Ciyuan* on: several character-lookup methods — radical, stroke and letter — running side by side; main text + appendices + illustrations; the guide to the reader written out as a text; the copyright page stating clearly 'which edition, which printing'), and the reference work became **an engineered product**; the third: **the age of the database**: the paper editions of the *Hanyu da zidian* and the *Guxun huizuan* 《故训汇纂》 ('A Compilation of Old Glosses') run in parallel with digitisation (the dictionary databases of teams such as Sichuan University's, full-text databases of Chinese books, and databases of character origins and graphs); on the phone, the *Xinhua zidian* app (from 2016, with handwritten and voice lookup built in) and the *Xiandai Hanyu cidian* app, downloaded for a fee; at the national level a national corpus has been built, and the 'National Language Resources Monitoring and Research Centre' (its plaque hung in 2020), to supply word frequency and monitoring. The form changes; the jargon of the trade does not: **collect in full, divide cleanly, exemplify solidly, retrieve quickly** — of the four attainments, the search box has solved only the fourth.
:::

::: warning Four misunderstandings in the use of reference works
(1) **Treating the dictionary as the law**: the senses in a dictionary are a map of forks, not a route sheet; in literary writing, “词典没这个义项” ('the dictionary does not have this sense' (Our translation.)) is not the same as “用错了” ('it has been used wrongly'), for the language community comes before the dictionary; (2) **Skipping on to the headword and not reading the whole entry**: looking up only the first sense, and missing the 〈书〉 and 〈方〉 marks and the “按” note, is the chief source of misuse; (3) **One dictionary eating its way across the world**: looking up “哲学” ('philosophy') in the *Xinhua zidian*, or “内卷” ('involution') in the *Ciyuan*, is in either case to have taken up the wrong microscope; (4) **Treating online translation and question-answering as reference works**: they have no edition, no party that bears responsibility, and no editorial procedure that can be traced back — you may ask the way, but you may not set it down as a record to be kept.
:::

::: exercise Filing an entry {level=1}
Choose any one of the characters “老、上、开”, build a table of all its senses in the *Xianhan* (serial number, grammatical class, a typical example, register mark), and identify the centre of radiation.
::: solution
A complete table, with the classes not wrong (mind the type in which a preposition is hung on after the verb, as in “打他从这路过”, 'pass by from here off him'), and the register marks copied as they stand, is a pass; extra credit to anyone who points out that sense 1 (for example “老”, 'advanced in years') is the centre of radiation for most of the branch-chains.
:::

::: exercise Comparing two dictionaries {level=2}
For the character “下”, look it up once in the *Xianhan* and once in the *Ciyuan*, and write three structural differences in how the two books handle the same headword (the unit at which an entry is set up, the orientation of the senses, and whether textual citations are present).
::: solution
① Under the character head the *Xianhan* hangs phrases and examples of the “～” type, and divides the senses of “下” into verb / adjective / localiser (方位, position); the *Ciyuan* sets up entries by **compounds and allusions** (下问, 'to enquire of someone below', 下走, 'your humble servant', and 陛下, 'Your Majesty', are each an entry), and has no place for the modern colloquial “下棋” ('to play chess'); ② the senses in the *Xianhan* follow present-day frequency, and those in the *Ciyuan* follow filiation in the old books; ③ the *Ciyuan* must note the source of the textual citation in every entry, while examples in the *Xianhan* mostly have no source. Conclusion: one of the present and one of the past; one of words and one of allusions.
:::
:::

::: exercise A live correction {level=2}
A short post reads: “这项政策应予以嘉奖” ('This policy ought to be granted a commendation.' (Our translation.)), “他的成绩不孚众望” ('His results fail to win the public's confidence.' (Our translation.)), “二人一拍即合，狼狈为奸地干了起来” ('The two of them hit it off at once, and set to work in cahoots.' (Our translation.)). For each of the three places, state which part of the dictionary should be consulted (word class? colouring? collocation?), and correct them.
::: solution
① The word class and the pattern of “予以” (予以 + a verbal object) → “应予嘉奖／予以奖励” ('should be commended / be given a reward'); ② “不孚众望” = cannot make the public convinced (derogatory), and is often taken for “不负众望” (to accord with) — look up the “孚” entry together with the usage code on the idiom entry; ③ “狼狈为奸”, which is derogatory, is misused as commendatory cooperation — look up the 〈贬〉 on the entry, or the column for linguistic feel. The corrections: change 嘉奖 to 奖励, change 不孚 to 不负, change 狼狈为奸 to 齐心协力 ('to pull together with one heart'). The three questions correspond, one each, to word class, the meaning of the idiom as a whole, and the colouring column.
:::

::: exercise A short essay: will AI replace the dictionary? {level=3}
Taking this lesson's three pieces of blunt hard work (scale of evidence, consistency of procedure, honesty of revision) as the frame, write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: where do a large model's 'question and answer on word meaning' and the functions of the dictionary overlap, and where can the dictionary not be replaced? Predict the form of the dictionary trade over the next ten years. You are required to cite at least two real experiences of your using two reference works, one example of an “按” note or of the guide to the reader, and to explain the concept of 'responsibility for the edition'.
::: solution
Points for the mark: an analysis of the overlap (looking up a definition, supplying an example sentence) and an analysis of what cannot be replaced (an edition that can be held to account, transparency in which words are included, a procedure of revision); the citations genuine (book title + edition number + one concrete thing found by looking up); the “按” example accurate; the prediction concrete and checkable (for example: mainstream dictionaries will be sold as a three-piece set, 'dictionary + API + an annual monitoring report', and pure paper will withdraw to collecting and to the public notice of norms). Anyone who deifies AI, or who demonises it, is marked on the quality of the argument and not on the stance.
:::

::: summary
Reference works divide into three types — character dictionaries, word dictionaries and allusion dictionaries — plus miscellaneous items, each in charge of one layer: modern sound, meaning and usage belong to the *Xianhan*; allusions in old books belong to the *Ciyuan*; character origins belong to the great character dictionary. The two faces of the dictionary — the eye of description and the hand of prescription — are read by reading the guide to the reader first, then the whole entry, and by attending to 〈方〉, 〈书〉 and the “按” note. Authority comes from blunt hard work: the scale of the evidence, the consistency of the procedure, the honesty of revision. From the scroll to the bound volume to the search box, three revolutions of form have not replaced the jargon 'collect, divide, exemplify, retrieve'; they have only carried 'retrieve' to its limit. The dictionary is not the law, but it is at present the most responsible ledger of human knowledge of word meaning — to use it until it is worn through is the end point of the vocabulary course, and also the starting point of studying on one's own.
:::
