---
title: "Loanwords"
---

A speaker of Chinese unconsciously utters dozens of sentences of "foreign language" every day: 世界 is the written translation of monks who translated the scriptures, 站 is a post station of the Mongol empire, 逻辑 is a phonetic transcription, and 沙发 is one too. Loanwords are the lexicon's **immigration register** — this lesson reads that booklet: how the immigrants come in (mechanisms), in how many waves they come (waves), and how, once in, they change their identity cards (adaptation).

## Four ways in {#sec-sizhong}

::: definition Mechanisms by which loanwords take their meaning {#def-jizhi}
**Pure phonetic transcription**: sound-recording characters throughout — 沙发 (sofa), 麦克风 (microphone), “德律风”, from before the age of Latin letters (telephone), 咖啡 (coffee, passed on through Dutch), 幽默 (humor; the name fixed by a trial translation of Lin Yutang 林语堂 in 1924).
**Phonetic transcription plus a semantically translated category word (half sound, half sense)**: a sound-recording component + a category signific — 啤酒 (啤 = the first sound of beer + 酒), 卡车 (卡 = car + 车), 坦克 (tank + 车), 芭蕾舞 (ballet + 舞), 汉堡包 (Hamburg + 包), 新西兰 (the final sound “兰” of Zeland also takes a meaning).
**Sound-and-sense rendering (meaning fitted by phonetic echo)**: characters are chosen so as to catch a meaning as well — 可口可乐 (Coca-Cola), 哈佛 (Harvard), 俱乐部 (club), 基因 (gene), 维他命 (vitamin, coexisting and competing with 维生素).
**Borrowing of graphic form and calque**: “Japanese-coined Chinese” (和制汉语), Western languages semantically translated by Japanese with Chinese characters and then flowed back (哲学, 经济, 社会, 共和国, 主义); words in Latin letters that take up residence directly (GDP, WTO, KTV, App).
:::

::: theorem Four pressures in the Sinicisation of phonetic loans {#rule-siya}
A phonetic loan, once in, has to pass four rounds of plastic surgery: **① Monosyllabic compression** — a polysyllabic source word is packed into two or three characters (Moskva→莫斯科; steering→方向盘 is an example of abandoning the sound and taking the sense; the “的” of “的士” is carved out as a morpheme, and a whole word family is struck from it: “打的、的哥”); **② Category crowning** — a Chinese semantic-class head is added to the sound-recording signs (啤＋酒, 卡＋车, 巴蕾＋舞); without the hat, no household registration; **③ Tone assignment** — each sound-recording character is forcibly allotted one of the five tones (sofa→shāfā, first tone × 2; hacker→hēikè, which in the same move becomes “黑”客 and takes on a sense as well); **④ Choosing the graph for beauty and for common taste** — the characters chosen bring their own aura: 可口可乐 pleases both refined and popular taste, and the failed translated names of earlier years, of the “蝌蚪啃蜡” type (the nightmare of literal translation that Coca-Cola once had), are precisely the cautionary case — **a sound-recording character does not record the sound; it records the face first**. Together the four pressures explain why loanwords in Chinese look less and less like a foreign language.
:::

## Five waves of immigration {#sec-wubo}

::: history Sanskrit sounds come east: the first high tide of Buddhist-scripture translation {#sec-fanyu}
The sutra-translation movement from the Eastern Han to the Tang brought the first large-scale wave of loanwords in the history of Chinese. **Pure rendering**: 佛陀 (Buddha, abbreviated as “佛”), 菩萨 (bodhisattva, abbreviated in translation), 罗汉 (arhat), 刹那 (kṣaṇa), 劫 (an abbreviation of kalpa, “劫波”), 塔 (an abbreviation of stūpa, “兜婆/塔婆”), 魔 (māra; the original character “麻” written differently), 阎罗. **Coinage by semantic translation**: “世界” — “世”, flux (time), and “界”, orientation (space), were combined in the scriptures and treatises into a disyllabic abstract word; this word later grew into the basic vocabulary, and when people today recite “世界观” there is no longer anyone who knows that it comes from the Ganges. Translation also sustained a whole method of forming words by transcribed sound (the Tang and Song discussion of the principle of five kinds not to be translated), and was China's first systematic study of translation.
:::

::: example Japanese-coined Chinese: “Chinese-character words” that flowed back {#ex-hezhi}
“哲学、经济、社会、主义” are called “Japanese-coined Chinese”, yet every character of them is old material from earlier Chinese — how is this account to be reckoned?
::: solution
A three-layer account: **graphic form and word-material** are China's (“经世济民” abbreviated as “经济”; the alternation of “世” and “代” — the old words are all there); **the grafting operation of “old words, new senses”** was for the most part completed in the hands of Japanese scholars of the Meiji period (concepts of Western learning, on entering East Asia, were first matched with words in Japanese translation circles, as “哲学” was to philosophy); **the further return flow** was brought into the country by Chinese students in Japan and by translated books (a great many political translations between the years yǐsì 乙巳 and bǐngwǔ 丙午). Conclusion: calling them “Japanese loanwords” is right for the mechanism (semantic loans relayed through Japanese); calling them “self-coined from Chinese material” is also right for the formation — the two views are not a quarrel; they are **bookkeeping at different levels**. There are two false impressions that really ought to be corrected. One is “日语发明了汉字词就绑架了汉语” ('Japanese invented Chinese-character words and thereby kidnapped Chinese.' (Our translation.)) (the material is ours, the commission is the West's, the intermediary is Japan). The other is that every disyllabic abstract word is Japanese-coined (因果 and 世界 came early from the Buddhist scriptures, the periods standing more than a thousand years apart).
:::
:::

::: example Entry card for lettered words {#ex-ziimu}
“GDP、WTO、KTV、AA 制、App” — do they count as loanwords? How do the norms deal with them?
::: solution
They count as **graphic loans**: the pronunciation is not transcribed and the sense is not translated; the letters are moved across as a whole block (Chinese grammar uses them as nouns or adverbials in the usual way: “上 GDP”, “查 App”, “用 AA 制”). Normative treatment has two paths: **a paired name** (WTO→世贸组织, the two tracks used together; on first appearance in a medium, the full Chinese name is noted in brackets) and **inclusion in the dictionary** (adding an appendix of lettered words to the *Xiandai Hanyu cidian* 现代汉语词典 (*The Modern Chinese Dictionary*) once provoked a war of the pen). In the 2010s broadcasting issued a restrictive order to “use lettered words with caution”, and public opinion pushed it back; where it came to rest was that comprehensibility comes first: what is transparent to the public (KTV, GDP) is in general use, and what is a niche affectation (self-coined abbreviations) is naturally eliminated. The very liveliness of lettered words proves this: **the newest organ by which Chinese digests the foreign is the straight-tube stomach of “zero translation”**.
:::
:::

::: warning Three pitfalls in judging loanwords
(1) **Folk etymology**: the claim “胡同 = Mongolian” circulates widely, but the evidence is weak (one account, a forced analogy deriving it from “water well”, lacks historical proof); marking it “one account” is the scholarly posture. Cases the other way: 戈壁 (Mongolian, “gravel desert”) and 萨其马 (Manchu) have solid evidence. (2) **Ingratitude**: teaching “电话、世界、经济” as purely native words — the history of the words leaves no trace, but on the level of motivation there is an account; and also **forgetting the other way round**: taking the Arabic ancestry of “咖啡” (qahwa) for its identity in Chinese. (3) **Taking borrowing for replacement**: every tide of lettered words and of Japanese words is sentenced to a “crisis of purity”, yet the Chinese lexical system has always enlarged its capacity by turning no one away — **lexical purity is rhetoric, not linguistics**.
:::

::: intuition How many foreign languages live in your first language
Count them: “世界、哲学、逻辑、沙发、的士、摩登、麦克风、刹那、站、戈壁”, said in a single lesson — more than ten foreign lines of descent. What has never passed the test of linguistic purity is not Chinese, but the concept of “purity” itself.
:::

::: exercise Classifying mechanisms {level=1}
Draw up an “entry card” for each of the following loanwords (source word + mechanism + trace of Sinicisation): 基因, 麦克风, 汉堡包, 俱乐部, WTO.
::: solution
基因 (gene; sound-and-sense rendering: “基” also expresses the sense “foundation”); 麦克风 (microphone; pure phonetic transcription, further abbreviated in speech as “麦”); 汉堡包 (Hamburg + 包; half sound, half sense, plus a category); 俱乐部 (club; sound-and-sense rendering; the two characters “俱乐” bring with them a semantic field of delight); WTO (graphic borrowing of letters; in the norms it is often paired, on two tracks, with “世贸组织”). If both columns of the entry card are complete, it is a pass.
:::

::: exercise Reviewing the merits of translated names {level=2}
Coca-Cola once had the failed old rendering “蝌蚪啃蜡”, and later “可口可乐”; hacker was fixed as “黑客”, while “骇客” was not chosen. According to the “four pressures”, write one appraisal for each, and draft for yourself a scheme you think better for a word not yet translated (you set the source word).
::: solution
Marking points: match them one by one against “choice for beauty / category / tone / monosyllabification” (可口可乐: all four syllables retained + all four characters carrying a positive sense = the ceiling of the pun; 黑客: “黑” has pre-registered the semantic colouring, fitting an underground identity more closely than the neutral “骇客”, but it has already led to confusion between hacker and “白帽” ('white hats') — open to discussion); a scheme of one's own must genuinely pass the four pressures (for example, packing some long word into two characters and putting a category hat on it).
:::

::: exercise Survey {level=2}
Record the loanwords (including lettered words) in your own speech and writing over 24 hours, classify them by the five waves and count the proportions; judge which wave has already been “fully Sinicised” (native speakers no longer aware of the foreign identity).
::: solution
A sample conclusion: the Sanskrit wave and the Altaic wave have almost entirely lost the memory (of 世界, 刹那, 站 and 萨其马, not one makes anyone think of a foreign language); the European–Japanese-coined wave is half-aware (逻辑 and 哲学 can still be pointed out); the English wave is fully aware (KTV and App wear their letter identity cards) — **the “visibility of descent” of a loanword is inversely proportional to the date it was borrowed**, and this is precisely proof that the pressure to Sinicise exists. Genuine statistics earn the mark.
:::
:::

::: summary
Four ways loanwords come in: pure phonetic transcription, phonetic transcription + a category, sound-and-sense rendering, borrowing of graphic form and calque. On entry they undergo four pressures of Sinicisation (syllable compression, category crowning, tone assignment, choosing the graph for beauty). Five waves of immigration: Sanskrit of the sutra translations (世界·刹那·塔), Altaic of the conquest dynasties (站·戈壁·萨其马), European languages of the late Ming and early Qing (几何·轻气养气), Japanese-coined Chinese of the late Qing and the early Republic (哲学·经济·主义), contemporary English (基因·秀·lettered words). Japanese-coined Chinese must be booked in three layers (character-material China / the operation Japan / the return flow the wave of study abroad); recording lexical descent as territorial ownership is a double misreading. Lexical purity is rhetoric, not linguistics — the strength of Chinese has always come from digestion, not from closing the sluice.
:::
:::
