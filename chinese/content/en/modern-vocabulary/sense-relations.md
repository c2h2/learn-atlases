---
title: "Synonyms, antonyms and semantic fields"
---

Lexicographers have an acerbic and exact thing to say about "closeness of meaning": **no two words have completely the same meaning, or the lexicographers would kill one of them off**. This lesson learns three weapons of analysis — sememes (componential analysis), collocational frames, and markedness — so as to take "more or less the same" apart into differences one can see, and then to set the vocabulary on its shelves (semantic fields).

## Synonyms and synonym groups {#sec-tongyi}

::: definition Synonym groups and the words in use {#def-tongyiqu}
**Absolute synonymy** (meanings completely interchangeable) is all but a rare animal in language — the nearest cases are a common name beside an elegant designation, such as 月亮／月球 ('moon' / 'the Moon') and 春天／春季 ('spring' / 'the spring season'); what textbooks call **synonyms** are in fact **near-synonyms**. Near-synonyms gather into a **synonym group**, and inside the group there is one **core word** (highest in frequency of use, productive, and neutral: in the group 父亲／爸爸／爹／令尊／老爷子／家父, 'father' / 'dad' / 'dad' / 'your honoured father' / 'the old gentleman' / 'my father', the core is 父亲／爸爸), while the other members shift along several dimensions. The sources are four: **dialect** (*fangyan*; topolect) (土豆／马铃薯／洋芋, three names for the potato), **survivals from Classical Chinese** (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) (the death family: 卒、殁、崩、殉、牺牲), **euphemistic condensation** (去世、走了、不在了, 'has passed away', 'has gone', 'is no longer here'), and **variation in sound and in morphology** (目／眼睛, 'eye' / 'eyes').
:::

::: theorem Three axes of discrimination: meaning · colouring · use {#rule-sanxiu}
**① The sememe (meaning) axis**: size of range (边疆＞边境＞边陲, 'frontier' > 'border' > 'borderland'), degree, light or heavy (请求／恳求／哀求, 'request' / 'entreat' / 'implore' — the modality rises in steps), collective and individual (森林／树林／树, 'forest' / 'woods' / 'tree'; 衣服／衬衣, 'clothes' / 'shirt'), concrete and general. **② The colouring axis**: affective feeling (成果 — rather commendatory / 结果 — neutral / 后果 — derogatory; 'achievement' / 'result' / 'consequence'), register (the ranking of occasions for 生日 · 诞辰 · 寿辰; 去世／死／崩／驾鹤), image (颤抖／哆嗦／颤巍巍, 'tremble' / 'shiver' / 'all a-tremble'). **③ The use (collocation) axis**: differences of syntactic frame (违背 takes an object, 违心 functions as an adverbial), collocational restrictions (交换物品 · 交换意见, 'exchange things' · 'exchange views'; 交流思想 · 交流经验, 'exchange thoughts' · 'exchange experience'), differences of **distribution** (很 cannot stand as a sentence on its own, 非常 can). The classic examination questions on adverbs of time and aspect fall almost entirely on the axis of use. **The order of the three axes is precisely the order of the columns in which a dictionary for discriminating near-synonyms is compiled.**
:::

::: example The five-word operating table {#ex-wuci}
Compare 改进、改善、改变、改正、改造 ('improve', 'improve', 'change', 'correct', 'transform'): fix the core, mark the differences of sememes, and match each with one typical collocation.
::: solution
Get one cell of the collocation wrong and the stuffing shows: 改正生活 ('correct one's life') and 改善错误 ('improve an error') are both unsayable, while 改变态度 ('change one's attitude') holds. The group of five is a specimen of Chinese "a productive monosyllabic core plus a division of labour among the disyllables" — **morpheme meaning is still at work**, and telling them apart sometimes need only go back to the morpheme (改 a 错, 纠 a 误, 'alter a wrong, rectify a mistake': the direction shows itself).
:::

## Antonymy: four types and markedness {#sec-fanyi}

::: definition A typology of antonymy {#def-fanyi}
**Complementary antonymy** (complementary): it is one or the other, and negating one side necessarily affirms the other — 生／死, 真／假, 正／负 ('alive' / 'dead', 'true' / 'false', 'positive' / 'negative'); the test formula is that "不A且不B" ('not A and not B') should form a contradiction. **Gradable antonymy** (gradable): there is a middle ground, and they can take degree — 大／小, 热／冷, 宽／窄 ('big' / 'small', 'hot' / 'cold', 'wide' / 'narrow'); 不太大 ('not very big') is acceptable, and negation does not entail the opposite term. **Converse relation** (converse): two perspectives on one and the same situation — 买／卖, 教／学, 老师／学生 ('buy' / 'sell', 'teach' / 'learn', 'teacher' / 'student'); Chinese 借 takes in both directions ("借我钱" can be read as lending out or as borrowing in), a famous case of the system tolerating ambiguity. **Directional antonymy**: 来／去, 上行／下行 ('come' / 'go', 'go up' / 'go down'). Well-formedness is also taken hostage by **collocation**: "这茶不热，得加点儿" ('this tea is not hot; a bit more needs adding') — the "cold" of a drink is called 凉 ('cool'): 热／凉 pairs for drinks, 热／冷 for the weather, 深／浅 for how concentrated a colour is, and an antonym pairing **drifts with the word it goes with**.
:::

::: example Three cases of markedness {#ex-biaoji}
① To ask about height one uses 他多高了？ ('How tall is he now?') and almost never 他多矮了？ ('How short is he now?') (the former is a **neutral question**); ② 他不矮 ('he is not short') is more often used than 他不高 ('he is not tall') to say, by way of euphemism, "middling and on the tall side"; ③ 桌子是长的，不是短的 ('the table is a long one, not a short one') and the sentence with it turned round can both be said — which shows the difference in pragmatic information between the two.
::: solution
The **positive term (the unmarked term)** among adjectives of measure takes on questioning over the whole scale, and generic reference: 多高、多长、多宽、多重、多大 ('how tall, how long, how wide, how heavy, how big') — what is asked is the measure, not "tall". 矮、短、窄 ('short in height', 'short in length', 'narrow') are the negative terms: the moment they appear they declare a stance (他矮, 'he is short', is information; 他不高, 'he is not tall', is a euphemism, neutral and slightly derogatory). Both sentences of ③ are sayable, but there is a **pragmatic asymmetry**: only negating "the polarity one is likelier to expect" carries information, so "是长的不是短的" ('it is long, not short') is more like a scene of putting a mistake right. What the method of testing deposits is this: **for every gradable antonymy that can take degree, the positive term swallows the two privileges, of questioning and of generic reference**. This is the only abstraction in the study of antonyms that can be used as a tool — and it is the answer to the riddle of why a child learns first to ask 多大 ('how big') and almost never asks 多小 ('how small').
:::
:::

::: history From mutual glossing to field theory: two thousand years of research on sense relations in Chinese {#sec-hulian}
In the *Erya* 尔雅 ('Approaching the Correct'), section 释诂 ('Explaining Old Words'), "台、朕、赉、畀、卜、阳，予也" ("台, 朕, 赉, 畀, 卜 and 阳 mean 予.") (Our translation.) — piling a string of mutually glossing words under one and the same sense is the primitive form of the synonym group; the *Shuowen jiezi* (*Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*) ties a net by mutual glossing ("坚，刚也" "Hard means firm."; "刚，强断也" "Firm means strongly breaking.") (Our translation.), and relations of word meaning were woven into a net for the first time. Dialect materials were precocious too: Yang Xiong 扬雄, in the *Fangyan* 方言 ('Dialects'), records "猪，关东西或谓彘，或谓豕" ("Pig: east and west of the passes, some call it 彘, some call it 豕.") (Our translation.), the fieldwork forerunner of a regional field of synonyms. The modern turn lies in structuralism's coming on: from the 1930s on, **componential analysis** (of the form man＝[＋成人＋男性], 'man = [+adult +male]') entered Chinese lexicology, and tables of sememes made it a demonstrable science that 哥哥／弟弟 ('elder brother' / 'younger brother') differ only by ［＋年长］ ('[+older]'); semantic-field theory in the 1970s (by analogy with the distinctive features of phonology) explained why colour terms and kinship terms, as whole systems, show gaps and multiple values; contemporary corpora turn the axes of discrimination into retrieval statistics of collocational distribution (the high-frequency objects of 改进 are 方法 and 工作, 'method' and 'work'; those of 改善 are 生活 and 关系, 'life' and 'relation' — **co-occurrence is definition**).
:::

::: warning Three "taken for granteds" in discriminating synonyms
(1) **Morpheme transparency can do everything**: 请求／恳求 can be told apart by 恳 ('earnest'), but the 善 of 改善 has long since not made a meaning on its own — in splitting morphemes one must guard against **morpheme meaning coming unhooked from word meaning** (马虎, 'careless', has neither horse nor tiger); (2) **Antonymy can be extended by analogy**: 大／小 ('big' / 'small') are opposites, but one cannot infer from that that 大米 and 小米 are opposites (they are only names of varieties of rice) — a morpheme inside a word does not contract out a free antonym, and 高兴 ('glad') has no ＊低兴 either; (3) **A difference of sememes is the whole of it**: the sememe table of 咱们／我们 ('we, including you' / 'we') differs only by ［±包括听者］ ('[± includes the hearer]'), but it is their **pragmatics and regional distribution** (the north is fonder of using 咱们 to show closeness) that make up knowledge of use — a discrimination counts as finished only when the two columns "difference of sememes + difference of use" can be written out.
:::

The core axis for discriminating 交换 and 交流 ('exchange' and 'exchange') is (　　).
- [ ] affective colouring
- [x] collocational frame

- [ ] rank of register
::: solution
交换意见／礼物, 交流思想／经验 ('exchange views / gifts; exchange thoughts / experience') — the difference lies in the choice of frame, **which object goes into which verb**, not in commendatory against derogatory, nor in refined against vulgar.

::: quiz
Which of the following belongs to complementary antonymy? (　　)
- [ ] 冷—热 ('cold—hot')
- [x] 生—死 ('alive—dead')
- [ ] 高—矮 ('tall—short')
- [ ] 买—卖 ('buy—sell')
::: solution
不死 ⇔ 生 ('not dead' is equivalent to 'alive') (no middle term) is complementarity; hot and cold are gradable, and one may say 不冷不热 ('neither cold nor hot'); tall and short have a gradient of markedness; buying and selling are a converse relation (two perspectives on one and the same transaction).
:::

::: quiz
That 多高了？ ('how tall now?') can be asked of a short person shows that 高 ('tall'), on the axis of measure, has (　　).
- [ ] a commendatory character
- [x] unmarked (neutral) status
- [ ] collective meaning
- [ ] nominalisation
::: solution
The positive word of measure monopolises the functions of questioning and of generic reference (and likewise 多长/多深/多重, 'how long / how deep / how heavy'): that is, unmarkedness — the most operable finding in the study of antonyms.

::: exercise Filling in the three-axis table {level=1}
For 边疆／边境／边陲 fill in a three-axis table (one sentence each for meaning, colouring and use).
::: solution
Meaning: 边疆 has a large range (the vast territory inside the borders of the country), 边境 refers to the belt along the boundary, and 边陲 = one corner of the frontier (and it is in written style); colouring: 边陲 is the strongest in Classical Chinese / written flavour, 边疆 is in common use in political argument, and 边境 is in general use in the news; use: 边疆 may be followed by 建设～ ('build up the ~'), 边境 often occurs as ～地区／～贸易 ('~ region / ~ trade'), and 边陲 is often joined in attributive–head constructions of the ～小镇 type ('a small town on the ~'). Fill all three columns, with no hard error, and that is full marks.
:::

::: exercise Typing the antonyms {level=1}
Assign a type to each of the following pairs: 男—女, 进—出, 深—浅, 正—负, 老师—学生, 胖—瘦.
::: solution
男/女 = complementary; 进/出 = directional antonymy (when the context locks "在内／在外", 'inside / outside', it approaches complementarity; note this and the mark is given); 深/浅 = gradable; 正/负 = complementary (the domain of signs); 老师/学生 = converse (a relation); 胖/瘦 = gradable, and the negative marking is heavier — to ask the measure of build one habitually uses 他多重？ ('How heavy is he?'), and 多胖 ('how fat') can hardly form a neutral question.
:::

::: exercise Data of linguistic intuition {level=2}
Choose one "synonym pair" of the local dialect and Putonghua (standard Mandarin) (for example 走路／行路, 星期天／礼拜日), explain its **division of labour by register** or its **division of labour by generation**, and then design one minimal substitution sentence to test "what it is that changes after they are swapped".
::: solution
A sample: 俺／我 — synonymous as forms of person, but 俺 brings with it a threefold shift, of the rustic, the intimate and the jocular; the test sentences 俺觉得对 ↔ 我觉得对 ('I think that's right' ↔ 'I think that's right'): the propositional meaning does not change, and what changes is **persona and occasion** (pragmatic meaning, a mark of identity). An answer that passes must bring out that "the difference between synonyms is not in the dictionary; it is on the speaker".
:::

::: exercise Short essay: who needs a dictionary of discriminations {level=3}
Which comes first, a tool of the *Dictionary of Near-synonyms in Modern Chinese* kind, or the "three axes of discrimination"? Write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: what granularity of description of differences in word meaning do three kinds of user — native speakers, second-language learners, and AI language models — each need? Requirements: construct one example each with a sememe table and with a collocational frame, and answer "is a difference the dictionary cannot mark a false difference?"
::: solution
Points for marking: the writer can point out that the three kinds of user do not need the same thing (a native speaker wants prompts on collocation and pragmatics; a learner wants a bank of example sentences and warnings of errors; the training of a model relies on distribution rather than on definitions); the sememe-table example and the frame example are the writer's own and are not mistaken; a layered position is given on "a difference that cannot be marked" (what can be marked but has not been ≠ a false difference; pragmatic knowledge that cannot, in principle, be listed exhaustively ≠ the dictionary failing in its duty — a division of labour between the tool and the discipline); concepts are not mixed (marks are deducted from anyone who takes 标记性, 'markedness', for 标注, 'annotation').
:::

::: summary
Absolute synonymy is rare, and near-synonymy comes in groups: a synonym group takes the core word as its anchor, and differences unfold along three axes — sememes (range / light and heavy / collective and individual), colouring (affective feeling / register / image), and use (collocational frame and distribution); the three axes are the columns under which a dictionary of discriminations is written. Antonymy divides into four types, complementary, gradable, converse and directional; that the **positive term of gradable antonymy is unmarked** (多高, 多长, 'how tall', 'how long') is the most serviceable finding. Semantic fields let each word go to its own shelf: superordinate and subordinate (白菜—蔬菜), coordinates of the same kind, and whole and part; gaps and multiple values inside a field explain variation by dialect and variation through time. Two thousand years, from the mutual glossing of the *Erya* to co-occurrence in the corpus: "the difference is on the speaker" — the end of discrimination is not the definition, but the use.
:::
