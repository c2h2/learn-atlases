---
title: "Rhyme tables"
---

A rhyme dictionary is a telephone directory arranged by rhyme; a *dengyun* rhyme table (*děngyùn tú* 等韵图) rewrites it as a **periodic table of the elements**: initials set out in columns, rhymes and divisions divided into rows, the four tones stacked into layers, one syllable to a cell — where there is a character, fill in the character; where there is none, draw a circle. The people of the Song had no alphabet, and yet they drew the complete matrix of the sound system of Chinese.

## What a rhyme table is {#sec-yuntu}

::: definition The dengyun rhyme table and two representative works {#def-yuntu}
A *dengyun* rhyme table (韵图) turns the system of *fanqie* spelling (*fǎnqiè* 反切) into coordinates: **vertical columns are the initials** (the thirty-six initials, arranged by lips, tongue, molars, teeth and throat, and by voiceless and voiced — *qīngzhuó* 清浊, 'clear and muddy'), **horizontal rows are rhyme and division** (division, also called grade; within one table, filed into the four divisions), **the four tones are the layers** (level, rising, departing and entering tone — the entering tone is also called the checked tone — each occupy one stretch); the cell lists a representative character, and the head character governs the homophones of one cell. Two great systems survive. The *Yunjing* 《韵镜》 ('Mirror of Rhymes'; people of the Song reprinted an older book, five fascicles and forty-three tables, each table headed on the pattern of “东董送屋 通开第一”) preserves the early regular format of the rhyme tables, and is the principal evidence for the structure of the *Qieyun* 《切韵》 ('Rhymes by *fanqie*'). The *Qieyun Zhizhang Tu* 《切韵指掌图》 ('palm charts for the *Qieyun*'; traditionally attributed to Sima Guang 司马光, twenty tables, separating the divisions and open from closed — unrounded and rounded — and looking up a *fanqie* from the table) belongs to the popular line of quick reference, and is occasionally careless. Later works such as the *Yunlüe Yitong* 《韵略易通》 ('Rhymes in brief, readily understood') are a simplified branch. **What the coordinates of a cell mean**: one column is the vertical file of one initial through every alternant of the four tones; one row is the horizontal rank of one rhyme under different initials; one table is the entire household register of one rhyme class (*shè* 摄), one value of open or closed, and one division.
:::

::: example Three steps in consulting a table: from fanqie to coordinates {#ex-chatu}
Locate “东” (德红切) in the *Yunjing*.
::: solution
Step one, **assign the rhyme and set the table**: 东 belongs to the Tong rhyme class, and is open, Division I, corresponding to the first table (the table is named “东董送屋 通开一” — the set of four tones is written straight into the name of the table). Step two, **the upper character looks up the column**: 德 goes to the column of the *duan* initial (端母; a tongue-head initial, *shétóu* 舌头, wholly voiceless, *quánqīng* 全清). Step three, **the lower character fixes the row and the layer**: 红 fixes the final and the level-tone layer. The cell it falls in is read “端母·通开一·平”, and in passing one sees the whole family: the layers above and below this cell are 东—董—送—屋 (the four tones of the same rhyme), and the neighbouring columns of the same row are 冬, 宗 and 聪 (neighbouring rhymes of the same initial). ***Fanqie* is a cipher, and the rhyme table is coordinate paper** — once the table can be read, a *fanqie* formula (*qiēyǔ* 切语) turns of itself into an address.
:::
:::

::: definition Jargon at speed: zhuan, open and closed, table names {#def-zhuan}
***Zhuan*** (*zhuǎn* 转): a group of rhymes that make a set across the four tones (the several rhymes of the Tong rhyme class are one *zhuan*); the forty-three tables are arranged by *zhuan*. **Open and closed**: another axis, the presence or absence of a -u- medial; 东 is open and 冬 is closed, and they are separated into two tables. **Division**: the grading inside a row (Division I is broad, *hóng* 洪; Division II forms a section of its own in the rhyme tables; Division III is the most crowded; Division IV is attached at the end). Jargon of table names, such as “通合一 东董送屋” — memorise the name of the rhyme class together with open or closed and the division, and the table name is a retrieval formula: **the rhyme table is a database, and the table name is the query key**. *Menfa* (*ménfǎ* 门法, the "exception clauses" of the rhyme tables, the rules for assigning a character when the initial and the division of the rhyme do not match) are numerous in their entries and much disputed; a beginner may put them off, and need only know that they exist.
:::

::: intuition One table is worth a set of papers
The deep information in a rhyme table is often underestimated. Cells of the *niang* initial (娘母) are as rare as stars at dawn, and the initial letters of the *fei* group (非组) appear only in closed Division III — these "distributions over the cells" are a first-hand record of phonological restrictions, more honest than any description in words. The **empty circle (○)** marks a seat that 'has the sound but not the character' (“有其音而无其字”): the pronunciation is legal, the vocabulary is absent, and new words of later ages often settle there. The matrix of voiceless and voiced, for its part, has the behaviour of the four tones pre-installed in the system: the split of the level tone into yin and yang is paid out only when voiced initials have undergone devoicing, but the cells of the rhyme tables had long since set up the checkout counter for northern Mandarin (*guanhua* 官话). Modern scholars therefore take the rhyme tables as an independent source of evidence — ***fanqie* gives the classes, the initial letters give the names, and the rhyme tables give the coordinates; only a fix from the three corners is reliable**.
:::

::: history The Indian ancestry of the rhyme tables, and their travels in East Asia {#sec-xilv}
The form of 'setting sounds out in a table' (“以表列音”) is not a native product of Huaxia: it entered in the Tang along with Siddham studies (charts for spelling out Sanskrit). The principle of a sound table, with consonants as the warp and vowels as the weft, prompted Chinese scholars — but the rhyme tables remade that form, taken from a language without tones, into a matrix **layered by the four tones**, and they also invented the row-axis called "division"; all of this is a reinvention in Chinese terms. The table then travelled on. When Korea created the *Hunminjeongeum* 训民正音 ('Correct Sounds for the Instruction of the People', 1443), it took the Chinese study of historical phonology as its reference, and Japanese books of Siddham charts of the four tones can likewise be seen, a surviving offshoot. Both where the tool came from and where it went on to lie on the line along which civilisations meet — joining, head to tail, the story of *fanqie* being born of the Buddhist scriptures.
:::

::: warning Four guards when reading a table
First, guard against **taking an initial letter for a sound value**: the “並” in a cell is not read bìng; an initial letter is a symbol, and the sound value depends on reconstruction. Second, guard against **stringing cells together by today's pronunciation**: the rhyme tables are arrayed on the system of the *Qieyun*; “机” and “诗” are close in the modern reading, yet on the table they sit apart in the two columns of tooth-head and true tooth (*chǐtóu* 齿头 and *zhèngchǐ* 正齿) — palatalisation is something that happened only a thousand years after the tables. Third, guard against **citing editions mixed together**: the several editions of the *Yunjing* and the *Zhizhang Tu* differ in the number of initial letters and in how the tables are divided; a citation must name the edition. Fourth, guard against **over-reading the empty circles**: most empty circles are a sound without a character; a few are a limit of the compiler's system, or an oversight — 'an empty cell is one held in reserve' (“空即预留”) is a good principle, not a key that opens every lock.
:::

::: quiz
In the format of the *Yunjing*, what “横栏分等” ('dividing the horizontal rows by division') really is is ( ).
- [ ] a grading of initials by voiceless and voiced
- [x] a layering, by coordinates and within one table, of finals as broad or fine
- [ ] the order of the four tones
- [ ] the order of strokes for looking a character up
::: solution
Division is a rank on the axis of the final (the difference of broad and fine, *hóngxì* 洪细, and of the medial); voicing is in the vertical columns and the four tones are in the layers, and each of the three axes does its own job.

::: solution
A circle = a legal empty slot for a syllable — the system comes before the vocabulary, and this is precisely the strongest evidence of the rhyme tables' "self-awareness as phonology".

::: exercise Working out the place of a cell {level=1}
Write the coordinates of the following characters in a rhyme table (rhyme class, open or closed, division, tone, column of the initial): ① 公; ② 冬; ③ 送; ④ 屋.
::: solution
公 = 通合一·平·见母 (古红切, the 东 rhyme, closed); 冬 = 通合一·平·端母 (都宗切, the 冬 rhyme); 送 = 通合一·去·心母 (苏控切, the departing tone of the 冬 rhyme); 屋 = 通合一·入·影母 (乌谷切, the 屋 rhyme). The point: 东 and 冬 are the same rhyme class and both closed, yet they are two rhymes (the tables for 东 and 冬 stand next to each other); 送 is the departing tone of 冬, and 屋 is the entering tone of 东 — writing “送属冬韵去声、屋属东韵入声” ('送 belongs to the departing tone of the 冬 rhyme, and 屋 to the entering tone of the 东 rhyme.') (Our translation.) scores full marks.
:::

::: exercise Recruiting for an empty circle {level=2}
The rhyme tables draw a circle for a cell that 'has the sound but not the character'. Give one example of later ages "supplying a character" for an empty circle (a character newly coined, or borrowed, in modern times will do), state its place in the phonology and the age at which it entered, and explain why the early rhyme tables would rather draw a circle than coin a character.
::: solution
A model case: **“她”** — the spoken language had long had a sound for the feminine third person (in the Old Chinese Zhi group, 之部, “彼” was in use; after Middle Chinese, “伊” and “渠” divided the work; when the vernacular, *baihua* 白话, arose, a dedicated character was needed). In the 1920s Liu Bannong 刘半农 and others advocated the character “她”, which was precisely the issuing of a written form to a cell, in the Chinese system of personal pronouns, that had long been drawn as a circle. The reason: the written form is the layer of vocabulary, and the cell is the layer of the system; the compilers of the tables were loyal to the system and did not exceed their office by coining characters — **the circle is the linguist's honesty: rather leave a blank than fabricate** (compare the mechanism by which new words take up residence, [[modern-vocabulary/new-words]]).
:::
:::

::: exercise A triangle of coordinates {level=2}
A certain character has the *fanqie* formula “渠容切” in the *Guangyun* 《广韵》 ('Broad Rhymes'). Infer its coordinates (column of the initial, rhyme class, open or closed, division, tone), and give one character each that shares its cell, its column and its layer.
::: solution
渠 supplies the initial (the *qun* initial, 群母, a molar initial and fully voiced, *quánzhuó* 全浊); 容 supplies the rhyme and the tone (the 钟 rhyme, the Tong rhyme class, Division III, level tone) → the coordinates: 群母·通摄钟韵开口三等·平声, the modern reading qióng. Same cell (the same homophone group): 蛩 (the same *xiaoyun* 小韵 as “渠容切”); same row, different layer: 融 (the *yi* initial · the 钟 rhyme · level tone — within the same table, change the column and not the row); same column, different table: that level-tone cell of the *qun* initial in the table of the 东 rhyme of the Tong rhyme class (identifying the coordinates is enough; whether the cell has a character or not is left to the original table). Marking looks only at whether the three elements of the coordinates (column, row, layer) are free of error.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: phonemics without an alphabet {level=3}
Write from eight hundred to twelve hundred characters, arguing for or against: “韵图与反切系联证明，汉语传统语言学在无字母条件下达到了结构主义音位学的水平。” 'The rhyme tables, and the linking of *fanqie*, prove that traditional Chinese linguistics reached the level of structuralist phonemics without an alphabet.' (Our translation.) Requirements: (a) give three "structuralist features" of the rhyme tables (one each of a table of oppositions, an empty slot, and a distributional restriction); (b) cite the tradition of the "matrix table" in phonemics before Chomsky (a corpus–matrix was the ordinary weapon of descriptive linguistics before Firth) — one sentence of comparison across civilisations is enough; (c) take a position on "it did reach it / it did not", and deal with one strongest counter-example (for instance: no sound values, no synchronic object of description, the fussiness of the *menfa*).
::: solution
Marking points: the three features must be made concrete (opposition: the four positions of voiceless and voiced; empty slot: ○; distribution: the *fei* group is limited to Division III); the comparison must not be forced (a matrix table = a method shared by descriptive linguistics; there is no need to claim a cheap priority of the sort “中国早在一千年前就…” 'China, as early as a thousand years ago, had already…'); the counter-example must be handled honestly (the rhyme tables have no sound values, and they served the chanting of scriptures and the civil-service examinations rather than theoretical science); the conclusion may go either way, and the quality of the argument decides the mark.
:::
:::

::: summary
A *dengyun* rhyme table = the *fanqie* system of the *Qieyun* drawn as a coordinate matrix of initial × rhyme-and-division × the four tones: the forty-three tables of the *Yunjing* preserve the regular format, and the *Zhizhang Tu* serves as a popular lookup for *fanqie*; *zhuan*, rhyme class, open and closed, and division are its retrieval keys, and the empty circle is its testimony across a thousand years that "the system comes before the vocabulary". The distribution of cells in the rhyme tables (the *niang* initial as sparse as stars, the *fei* group limited to Division III) is a true record of phonological restrictions, and together with *fanqie* and the initial letters it forms a triangle of evidence. Read a rhyme dictionary again with the three steps for consulting a table (assign the table, look up the column, fix the layer), and *fanqie* turns from a cipher into an address — in the next chapter we turn the camera about, and use this set of coordinates to look at the still older sounds of Old Chinese.
:::
