title: "Fanqie spellings"
---

In a civilisation without an alphabet, the answer the ancients handed in for indicating the pronunciation of a dead character is astonishingly fine: **take two living characters, borrow the head of one and the tail of the other, and spell a third character** — this is *fǎnqiè* 反切 (*fanqie* spelling). It is the atom of the rhyme dictionary, the first gate of Chinese historical phonology, and the invention in the linguistics of ancient China that most resembles a "technique".

## Principle and how to read it {#sec-yuanli}

::: definition Fanqie spelling {#def-fanqie}
*Fanqie* (called 反 in antiquity, and also called *fǎnyǔ* 反语 and *fǎnyīn* 反音) uses two characters to indicate the pronunciation of one: **the upper character is taken for its initial (the initial), the lower character for its rhyme and tone (the final + the tone)**; read rapidly and run together, they yield the sound. The *Qieyun* 《切韵》 ('Rhymes by *fanqie*'): “东，德红切” ('东, the *fanqie* of 德 and 红.' (Our translation.)) — 德 supplies d-, 红 supplies -ōng (level tone), and joined they make dōng. The ordinary way of writing an example is “×，××切” or “××反”. A direct sound-gloss (*zhíyīn* 直音; “邕音雍”, 'the sound of 邕 is 雍') stalls as soon as it meets a lean year for homophones, and *dúruò* 读若 ('read as') is only a vague analogy — the *Shuowen jiezi* (*Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*) has “瞽读若嗀” ('瞽 is read as 嗀.' (Our translation.)). *Fanqie* was the first device that let **any character at all be spelled out with two characters**, and indicating pronunciation became from then on an engineering that could be systematised.
:::

::: theorem Three corrections when spelling fanqie with modern pronunciation {#rule-sandao}
When old *fanqie* is spelt in Putonghua (standard Mandarin), three places must be corrected. **① Class-separated *fanqie* (*lèigé* 类隔, 'separated by class')** — early initials did not distinguish light labials from heavy labials, nor tongue-head (*shétóu* 舌头) from tongue-up (*shéshàng* 舌上), and by modern classes the upper character is "separated" by one layer. Take “眉，武夷切”: 武 is now read w- (the *wei* initial, 微母), yet what is spelt out is méi — because **in the age when the *fanqie* formula (*qiēyǔ* 切语) was produced, “武” and “眉” were both m-**. Cases of this kind must be respelled according to the initial classes of the rhyme dictionaries. Upper characters of the “非敷奉微” series spelling characters of the *bang* group (帮组) are all like this; they are living fossils of “古无轻唇音” ('in antiquity there were no light labials'). **② The two tracks of the level tone, and voicing (*qīngzhuó* 清浊, 'clear and muddy')**: the lower character fixes level, rising, departing or entering tone (checked tone), while **whether the level tone is yin or yang depends on the voicing of the upper character** — “东，德红切”, 德 voiceless → the first tone (yin-level) dōng; “同，徒红切”, 徒 voiced → the second tone (yang-level) tóng. One and the same character “红”, and the tone is remote-controlled by the upper character. **③ Change in the phonetic value of the *fanqie* characters**: the characters used in the formula have themselves changed, and a reader today must restore their historical identity — “现，胡甸切”, 胡 h- ＋ 甸 -iàn → xiàn ✓; if one takes the modern sound of “许”, x-, and spells “许健切” with it, one misses its original nature as the *xiao* initial (晓母) [h]. The mnemonic: **if it will not spell, do not panic; first check whether it is a sound change, not the ancients writing it wrong**.
:::

::: example Character-by-character construction {#ex-shigong}
⑤匪，非偉切。
::: solution
① h＋áo→háo (the *xia* initial, 匣母, h is of one origin with modern h; spell it straight); ② l＋àn→làn (the lower character “战” supplies the departing tone) ✓; ③ 贯: 古 g- ＋ 患 (the final -uan of huàn, and the departing tone) → guàn ✓; ④ m＋ào→mào ✓; ⑤ 匪: 非 (now f; in the age of the *fanqie* formula it was read as a heavy labial, together with *bang* 帮) ＋ 偉 (-ěi, rising tone) → fěi ✓. That all five examples spell straight through shows that you have got the feel of "take the initial, take the rhyme"; only if you heed the annotation on ⑤ do you count as having grasped the historical layer.
:::

## Where fanqie came from {#sec-lai}

::: history The transcription of Buddhist scriptures and the discovery of the "division of initial and rhyme" {#sec-fojing}
Han commentaries on the classics used only *zhiyin* and *duruo*; initial and rhyme were never set out apart — an analysis of the “东，德红切” type was, in the eyes of people of the Han, quite unnecessary. The situation changed as Buddhism came east: the Siddham letters of Sanskrit have a tradition of analysing phones into “体文＋声点” ('body-letters + sound-dots'); transcribing mantras required a character to be matched to each sound, and monks and literati, standing before the tabular spelling-out of “胡语十四音、四流音” ('fourteen sounds of the Hu language, and four flowing sounds'), saw clearly for the first time the skeleton of Chinese itself. The *Erya yinyi* 《尔雅音义》 ('Sounds and meanings of the *Erya*'), traditionally attributed to Sun Yan 孙炎 of Wei, uses *fanqie* in quantity and is regarded as the beginning of its institutionalisation (the chapter ‘Sounds and words’ (音辞) of the *Yanshi jiaxun* 《颜氏家训》 ('Family instructions of the Yan clan') already looks back on the fashion for *fanyu* among “近为人”, 'people of recent times'); the pure conversation (*xuántán* 玄谈) of Wei and Jin, and the distinguishing of voiceless and voiced, afterwards turned “声类” ('initial classes') and “韻部” ('rhyme groups') into things that could be discussed, and they at last converged in the *Qieyun*. **No single native branch of learning walked up to the door on its own; contact across languages supplied the force that kicked the door in** — this is the verdict that Chinese historical phonology writes into the history of exchange between civilisations.
:::

::: intuition Fanqie is the "invisible alphabet" within the civilisation of Chinese characters
The upper characters of *fanqie*, classified by modern pronunciation, can be arranged into a table of initial classes (linkage in the *Qieyun* yields forty-one classes; see [[phonology/mc-initials]]); the lower characters, filed by rhyme, line up as Divisions I, II, III and IV (grades) and as open and closed (unrounded and rounded) — **the rhyme dictionaries used twenty thousand Chinese characters to make a two-dimensional coordinate system of "a table of initials + a table of finals"**; each character is the label in its cell, and each *fanqie* is a pointer between cells. The rhyme tables of the Song, arranged by division, simply drew the coordinates as a square array ([[phonology/rhyme-tables]]), and *fanqie* was raised from a method of indicating pronunciation to **an operation of phonology**: given a character not entered in the books, one can still, by a chain of *fanqie*, locate its initial, its rhyme, its division, and whether it is open or closed. To say that *fanqie* is the "phonetic-spelling scheme" of the Chinese-character system is not rhetoric; it is a structural fact.
:::

## Scanning the fault between fanqie and ancient and modern sounds {#sec-duan}

::: example Remote control of the tone of "同" and "东" {#ex-dong}
“东，德红切”“同，徒红切”“孔，康董切”“空，苦红切” — all four examples use the broad-sound (*hóngyīn* 洪音) *dong* rhyme (东韵) as their material, yet the modern pronunciations dōng/tóng/kǒng/kōng each differ. List the "variables" of each item, and sum up how the *fanqie* system encodes classes of sound.
::: solution
There are three independent variables. The upper character fixes the initial and, with it, **yin and yang within the level tone** (德, voiceless → yin-level 东; 徒, voiced → yang-level 同; 苦, the *xi* initial 溪, voiceless → yin-level 空). The lower character fixes the final + the tone (红 -ōng, level; 董 -uŋ, rising — note that the lower character of “空” is changed to “红” and the sound does not change, because “康董切” and “苦红切” are an internal division of labour within one rhyme class at different tones: 孔 = rising, 空 = level, and the tone follows the lower character). Division and open or closed are hidden in the lower character (东, closed, Division I). The conclusion for the encoding: ***fanqie* is a two-dimensional code that "splits the tones by voicing, and packs rhyme and division together"** — what it records is not a phonetic spelling, but the raw database of the phonology of the age of the *Qieyun*.
:::
:::

::: warning Three prohibitions in learning fanqie
First, do not **force a spelling with modern pronunciation and then give up**: if what you spell out is a "scrap sound" (“废音”), first check class-separation, check voicing, check sound change; eight times out of ten it is the normal echo of history. Second, do not **take a single *fanqie* and use it to the full as "evidence of the ancient sound"**: one *fanqie* can show only **the sound-class relation between that character and the characters of the formula**; proving “古无轻唇音” takes the **statistical tendency** of several hundred formulas (the method of Qian Daxin 钱大昕; see the phonetic series, *xiéshēng* 谐声, and the tradition of the Qing classicists, in [[chinese-characters/six-categories]]). Third, do not **set the *fanqie* reading against the reading in the tables that adjudicate pronunciation**: normative adjudication of pronunciation fixes the modern reading, while *fanqie* supplies the historical ledger — the dispute over “骑 qí 旧读 jì” ('骑 is qí, formerly read jì') arises precisely from mixing up the two ledgers (the current norm has only qí; jì is the layer of old readings left by the splitting of *fanqie*).
:::

::: quiz
The item that the upper character of a *fanqie* supplies, and the lower character does not, is ( ).
- [x] the initial
- [ ] the final
- [ ] the tone
- [ ] division and mouth-class (*děnghū* 等呼)
::: solution
The upper character is taken for the initial; the lower character is taken for the rhyme and the tone (*denghu* follows the final, and lies in the lower character). "Whether the level tone is yin or yang depends on the voicing of the upper character" is a rule of the second layer.
:::

::: quiz
“眉，武夷切”, spelt with modern sounds, should give méi and not wéi. The reason is ( ).
- [ ] the lower character has lost its reading
- [x] in the age of the *fanqie* formula, “武” was still read as a heavy labial m-; it is a class-separated *fanqie*
- [ ] 眉 is an alternative reading
- [ ] a wrong character in the manuscript copy
::: solution
Light labials (the *wei* initial, w-) come out of the heavy-labial *ming* initial (明母); the *fanqie* formula is earlier than the split, so the initial of the upper character “武” was at that time precisely m-. A class-separated *fanqie* is a growth ring of sound change, not a mistake.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The factor that decides that “同，徒红切” is read as the second tone (yang-level) is ( ).
- [ ] the second tone (yang-level) of the lower character “红”
- [x] the upper character “徒” is a voiced initial; the level tone is assigned to yang according to voicing
- [ ] 徒 is departing tone
- [ ] the final has become a broad sound
::: solution
The lower character gives only the one slot, "level tone"; yin or yang is allotted by the voicing of the upper character. A voiced level tone now falls under the second tone (yang-level); outside the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones, this is one of the most ironclad regularities.
:::

::: exercise Five constructions in a row {level=1}
Spell and read the following *fanqie*, and mark the places that need correction: ①当，都郎切；②遍，方见切；③昏，许昆切；④偶，五久切；⑤空，苦红切。
::: solution
① d＋áng→dāng; the upper character is voiceless and the lower character is level tone → the first tone (yin-level) ✓; ② 方 is now f-, and spelling “遍” gives biàn — class-separation of light and heavy labials, change it to b-; the lower character “见” records the departing tone; spell biàn ✓; ③ 许 is now xǔ; the *xiao* initial was originally h-: h＋ūn→hūn (if one forces it with modern x- and gets xūn, that is wrong; correct for the change in phonetic value); ④ 五 = the *yi* initial (疑母) ŋ-, now become a zero initial; the lower character “久” is rising tone, and a rising tone with a secondary-voiced initial (*cìzhuó* 次浊) is still a rising tone today → ǒu; ⑤ k＋ōng→kōng; set it beside “东德红切”: both are voiceless and yin-level, and only 徒 (voiced) with 红 as the lower character produces the second tone (yang-level) — once the drill in remote control by voicing is passed, that is full marks.
:::

::: exercise Sorting class-separation {level=2}
Judge whether each of the following *fanqie* formulas is “*yīnhé* 音和” ('sounds in agreement') or “*lèigé* 类隔” ('separated by class'), and give the modern spelling (the coordinate system of the judgement takes the age of the *fanqie* formula as its standard; if you adopt the system of the thirty-six initials, declare that separately): ①悲，府文切；②眉，武夷切；③杯，布回切；④展，知堰切。
::: solution
① 悲 is the *bang* initial, p-, and 府 is the *fei* initial, f- (at the level of the thirty-six initials) — judged by the age of the *Qieyun*, this originally belongs to *yinhe* (both are heavy labials); judged by the present system of initial-letters, it wears the look of *leige*. **Declare the coordinate system of the judgement first**; each of the two accounts scores half marks, and stating it clearly scores full marks. ② 武 (the *wei* initial, w-) spells 眉 (the *ming* initial, m-): standard class-separation of light and heavy labials; read the upper character back as a heavy labial and spell méi. ③ 布, the *bang* initial, spells 杯, the *bang* initial: *yinhe*, bēi. ④ the *zhi* initial spells its own class: *yinhe*, zhǎn. The real point of this question: ***yinhe* / *leige* is a judgement of a relation, not a judgement of a character's register** — one and the same *fanqie* is classed differently in different systems of initials, and this is further evidence that "the *fanqie* formula is earlier than the sound change".
:::

::: exercise A little detective {level=2}
Materials, three *fanqie* formulas: ①无，武夫切；②肤，甫无切；③矛，莫浮切。 The principle for linking the upper characters of *fanqie* is “切上字与被切字声母同类” ('the upper character of the *fanqie* and the character being spelled have initials of the same class.' (Our translation.)). The task: from this, reconstruct the relations among the initials of “武/甫/莫” and “无/肤/矛”, explain why the modern readings split into f/w on the one hand and p/m on the other, and say how this set of materials supports Qian Daxin's theory of “古无轻唇音” ('in antiquity there were no light labials').
::: solution
① 武 (now the *wei* initial, w) spells 无 (now zero/w) — 武 and 无 are of the same class, and of the same pattern as ③ 莫—矛 (the *ming* initial, m); ② 甫 (the *bang* initial, p) spells 肤 (now the *fu* initial, f) — light and heavy labials interchange. The three taken together: **for characters now read f/w, the upper characters of their *fanqie* formulas all fall among the heavy labials of the *bang* group (帮武明甫)** — the principle of linking upper characters guarantees the same class, so in the age of the *fanqie* formulas their initials were of the p/m kind. Qian Daxin's “古无轻唇音” was built from the statistics of several hundred examples; this question is a miniature demonstration of it: **first set up the axiom of "the same class", then look at the split in the modern readings; the only explanation is a sound change by differentiation**.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the place of fanqie in the history of ideas {level=3}
Write a short essay of eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: does *fanqie* constitute the "discovery of an alphabet" within the civilisation of Chinese characters? Requirements: cite the assembly-line image of “丢掉/只取” ('discard / take only') and argue that the **separability** of the three parameters, initial, rhyme and tone, had already been made operational; compare the Korean *Hunminjeongeum* 训民正音 ('The correct sounds for the instruction of the people'; 1443, the creation of an alphabet of phones) and explain why "analysis" could stop at the coordinate layer and not descend to the letter layer (consider the prestige of Chinese characters, the view that takes the syllable as its unit, and how deep or shallow the contact with Buddhist scriptures was); respond to one counter-argument (“反切只是查字术，不含理论”, '*Fanqie* is only an art of looking characters up, and contains no theory.' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
Marking points: the core argument holds (*fanqie* = splitting the syllable into three parameters that can be replaced independently of one another, initial / rhyme / tone — a framework of substitution is already an operationalised phonemic analysis); the comparison with the *Hunminjeongeum* is correct in its historical facts (the twenty-eight letters created in 1443 by Sejong 世宗 of Joseon, the ruler and his ministers, used to record Korean and to transcribe the sounds of Chinese); "why analysis could stop at the coordinate layer and not fall to the letter layer" is given reasons of institutions and of ideas (Chinese characters were the orthodoxy of the classics; *fanqie* was already enough; the rhyme tables satisfied the expression of structure; contact with the Buddhist scriptures never reached deeply into the mainstream of the scholar-officials); the counter-argument is taken seriously (the difference between "an art of looking characters up" and "theory" lies precisely in the square array of the *Yunjing* 《韵镜》 ('Mirror of rhymes') and in the Qing scholars' linkage of initial classes — theory lives in the operations and in the transmission).
:::

::: summary
*Fanqie* = a two-character system for spelling sounds, in which the upper character is taken for the initial and the lower character for the rhyme and the tone. It was catalysed into being by the transcription of Buddhist scriptures at the end of the Han, and rhyme dictionaries became possible because of it. Spelling it in modern pronunciation must carry three corrections: class-separated *fanqie* (the growth rings of light and heavy labials, and of tongue-up and tongue-head), yin and yang in the level tone following the voicing of the upper character, and a check on sound change in the characters of the *fanqie* formula themselves. Its weight in the history of ideas: it splits the Chinese syllable into three parameters that can be replaced independently of one another, and it is the "invisible table of letters" inside the civilisation of Chinese characters — without having to invent letters, it still arrived at the coordinate layer of phonology. Once *fanqie* is well practised, the initial classes, the rhyme classes (*shè* 韵摄) and the division ranks of the six chapters that follow are all extended applications of it.
:::
