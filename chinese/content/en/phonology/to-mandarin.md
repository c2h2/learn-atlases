How did the sound system of the *Qieyun* 《切韵》 become the Pinyin input method on a phone today? In the thousand years between, five or six great changes took place, every one of them regular, and every one of them able to be worked backwards. This lesson is the practice ground of the laws of sound change: learn the five laws, and for every character of Putonghua (standard Mandarin) you can state its Middle Chinese household register.

## The *Zhongyuan Yinyun*: a live recording of northern pronunciation {#sec-zhongyuan}

::: history The mixing desk of 1324 {#sec-zhlu}
In the Yuan dynasty, Zhou Deqing 周德清, taking as his evidence the practice of singing arias in the north, compiled the *Zhongyuan Yinyun* 《中原音韵》 (1324) and set the model of “正语” ('rectifying the pronunciation') (Our translation.) for northern *qu*. The sound system is gathered into **nineteen rhyme categories**, and under each rhyme category 586 groups of homophones (*xiaoyun* 小韵) are arranged by the four tone categories yin-level, yang-level, rising and departing (平阴, 平阳, 上, 去; yin-level and yang-level are, in Putonghua, the first, high-level tone and the second, rising tone). Characters that had originally been entering tone are distributed and attached within the four tones — Zhou Deqing himself described his method in the words “入派三声” ('the entering tone is assigned to the three tones') (Our translation.). Set beside the system of the *Qieyun*, the two are sharply different faces. **The entering tone no longer forms a volume of its own** (entering-tone characters are assigned to the three tones, level, rising and departing — “入派三声” is from this point entered in the written record); **the devoicing of fully voiced stops and affricates** is complete (the system of initial letters no longer matches the old tables); **the split of the level tone into yin and yang** is there in black and white. The history of the rhyme dictionaries turns from “照古经” ('following the old canon') (Our translation.) to “录时音” ('recording the pronunciation of the time') (Our translation.) — **a book of the rules of *qu* unexpectedly becomes the birth certificate of the sounds of Early Mandarin (*guanhua* 官话)**, and from this the study of rhyme dictionaries draws two disciplines: the phonology of Middle Chinese (今音学, the *Qieyun* tradition) and the phonology of the recent language (近音学, the *Zhongyuan Yinyun* tradition).
:::

::: theorem Five laws (Middle Chinese → the Beijing pronunciation) {#rule-wutiao}
① **The level tone splits into yin and yang**: the level tone divides according to whether the initial is voiceless or voiced — a voiceless level tone goes to the yin-level tone (阴平, the first, high-level tone) (东, 诗), and a voiced level tone goes to the yang-level tone (阳平, the second, rising tone) (同, 时). ② **Voiced rising becomes departing**: a fully voiced rising tone merges into the departing tone (伴, 淡, 近 — every rising-tone character of the “並定群从崇船” series, the *bing*, *ding*, *qun*, *cong*, *chong* and *chuan* initials, is read today in the departing tone). ③ **Distribution of the entering tone among the other tones** (the Putonghua arrangement): a fully voiced entering tone goes to the yang-level tone (合, 学, 直), a sonorant entering tone (次浊) goes to the departing tone (木, 六, 月), and voiceless entering tones are scattered, with no regularity (一, 八, 出, 北 — the layer learnt by rote). ④ **Fully voiced initials devoice, aspirated in the level tone and unaspirated in the oblique**: 并, 定, 群, 从, 崇, 船 and 禅 (并定群从崇船禅) become voiceless; in the level tone they become aspirated (同 tʰ-, 步 pʰ-), and in an oblique tone they become unaspirated (徒 t-, 部 p-) — hence, for “抱/报” and “洞/动” in Putonghua, a difference of initial is often a difference of tone category. ⑤ **Palatalisation of the velar series before fine finals** (见系细音; comparatively late, completed in the Qing): before -i/-ü, k/kʰ/x become j/q/x (京, 溪, 兴), and merge with the sharp initials of the *jing* group (精组尖音) into the round initials (“酒=九”); parts of Zhongyuan Mandarin (中原官话) and of Jiaoliao Mandarin (胶辽官话) keep the distinction between sharp and round (尖团) — **telling sharp from round is a contour line on the map of the dialects**.
:::

::: example One character at the registration office {#ex-huizheng}
Work backwards through the record of how “同” changed (the *fanqie* spelling 徒红切 in the *Guangyun* 《广韵》), and list, one by one, the laws that are triggered.
::: solution
Household register: the *ding* initial (定母, fully voiced), the *dong* rhyme (东韵), Division I (一等), level tone, the *tong* rhyme class (通摄). ① Devoicing of the fully voiced initial, plus aspiration in the level tone: d-→tʰ- (triggering the “平送” of ② and of ④, 'aspirated in the level tone'); ② the level tone splits into yin and yang: voiced level → yang-level. The modern reading tóng is not a step out. Checked the other way on “洞”: the *fanqie* 徒弄切, the *ding* initial, departing tone — a departing tone does not undergo “平送”, and an oblique tone is unaspirated → d-; the modern reading is dòng ✓. Drill as far as this, and the five laws are already clear.
:::

::: warning Three things to avoid in using the laws
The first is **using the laws to teach the dialects**. The five are the evolution of northern Mandarin (the Beijing sound system). Yue (Cantonese) and Min each have a contract of their own (Yue keeps nine tone categories and six tone values, and three entering-tone codas; in Min the literary and the colloquial stratum stand side by side — when the strata have not been peeled apart, 'exceptions' come in crowds, and that is the overprinting of two strata, not the bankruptcy of the regularity). The second is **forgetting literary and colloquial readings**: “薄” báo (白, colloquial) / bó (文, literary), “滴” dī/dì and the like are the same law signing its name twice, at different strata. The third is **forcing a single character through**: a law operates by **class** (the **one class** of fully voiced entering-tone characters goes to the yang-level tone). To look a character up, first fix the voicing of its *Guangyun* initial and its tone category — check the household register before you move house. The order must not be reversed.
:::

::: quiz
“近” is read today in the departing tone. The law that is triggered is (　).
- [ ] Distribution of the entering tone among the other tones
- [x] Voiced rising becomes departing
- [ ] The level tone splits into yin and yang
- [ ] A fully voiced initial is aspirated
::: solution
“近” is fully voiced (the *qun* initial, 群母) and was a rising tone, and it goes to the departing tone: a standard case of voiced rising becoming departing.
:::
:::

::: quiz
After the entering tone has been distributed among the other tones, the batch whose tone is least regular is (　).
- [ ] Fully voiced entering
- [ ] Sonorant entering
- [x] Voiceless entering
- [ ] Entering with a nasal initial
::: solution
Fully voiced entering tones go to the yang-level tone, and sonorant entering tones go to the departing tone; both are fixed by rule. Only voiceless entering tones are scattered across the four tones, and become the modern 'layer that has to be memorised'.
:::
:::

::: quiz
That “酒” and “九” are not distinguished in the modern pronunciation reflects this historical sound change (　).
- [ ] Devoicing of voiced initials
- [x] Merger of sharp and round (palatalisation of the velar series, merging with the fine finals of the *jing* group)
- [ ] Loss of the *wei* initial (微母)
- [ ] Merger of the open rhyme groups (阴声韵)
::: solution
Fine finals of the k- series are palatalised into the j- series (round initials, 团音), and fine finals of the ts- series (sharp initials, 尖音) follow them into the merger. Dialects that keep the distinction are the counter-evidence to “尖团不分” ('sharp and round not distinguished') (Our translation.).
:::

::: exercise The record filled in backwards {level=1}
For “同”, “步”, “近”, “木” and “京”, fill in, for each, the clause of the change that is triggered (using the numbers of the five laws) and the modern reading.
::: solution
同: voiced level → yang-level, plus aspirated in the level tone, tʰ → tóng; 步: the *bing* initial (並母), departing tone (oblique), unaspirated → bù; 近: voiced rising becomes departing → jìn; 木: a sonorant entering tone goes to departing → mù; 京: the *jian* initial (见母) before a fine final is palatalised, k->j-, plus voiceless level → yin-level → jīng (this character does not involve the entering tone; two laws settle the account). Full marks if each of the five is matched to its number.
:::

::: exercise An audit of the exceptions {level=2}
“白” bái is a fully voiced entering tone (the *bing* initial, 並母, the *mo* rhyme, 陌韵, entering tone). By the law it goes to the yang-level tone — and it really is read in the yang-level tone! But “白” is read bó in “白事”, and in “明白” it is read in the neutral tone, as bai. Please check this, and write an audit of two hundred characters: is this an exception to the law, or the overprinting of strata?
::: solution
A sample of how the audit might conclude: bái conforms to the law (fully voiced entering → yang-level); forms such as “明白” mangbai·bo belong to a lexicalised neutral tone and to the keeping of an alternative reading (“白事” bó is a remnant of the literary-reading stratum). It is a question of **stratum**, not a counter-example to the law. Being able to tell apart the three strata “规律层/词汇层/语体层” ('the stratum of the law / the lexical stratum / the stratum of register') is enough to pass — echoing [[phonology/why-phonology]] “例外即未解条件” ('an exception is a condition not yet solved') (Our translation.).
:::

Write from eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Explain why a person from Beijing who can use a Pinyin input method is able, without knowing the *Qieyun*, to 'recite' most of the results of distributing the entering tone among the other tones — and on which characters one is bound to come unstuck (give three or more characters of voiceless entering tone). One further question: the resurgence of the merger of sharp and round among young speakers and in the dialect areas (survey reports of a 'shift to round initials', “团音化”, of the “小壮” type) shows what property of a law? The essay is required to contain one self-made “我的清入散派清单” ('my list of where the voiceless entering tones have scattered').
::: solution
Points for marking: make clear “个体语言习得重演集体音变结果” ('an individual's acquisition of the language re-enacts the result of the collective sound change') (Our translation.) (the synchronic system is the final snapshot of a thousand years of history, and the key-positions of an input method are the operational layer of the fossil); the list of characters on which one comes unstuck must be genuine and open to checking (笔, 必, 北, 黑, 粥… among voiceless entering tones all four tones have examples); the discussion of sharp and round must come down on the nature of “规律的共时权威 vs 方言变异” ('the synchronic authority of a law vs variation among the dialects') (Our translation.) (surveys may be cited and read; the figures may not be invented).

::: summary
From Middle Chinese to Putonghua is a demolition and relocation carried out under a contract. Two iron laws go first, the split of the level tone into yin and yang, and voiced rising becoming departing; the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones resettles the entering-tone sojourners (voiceless entering tones have no fixed destination); the devoicing of fully voiced initials changes the doorplates according to “平送仄不送” ('aspirated in the level tone, unaspirated in the oblique'); and palatalisation of the velar series before fine finals finishes the work at the end, and merges sharp with round. The *Zhongyuan Yinyun* of 1324 took down the deposition of this demolition. Literary and colloquial readings are the layers of two rounds of building, laid one upon the other, not evidence that the contract was broken. Being able to use an input method is not the same as understanding historical phonology, but every time you type you are casting a vote — for those few laws which, in a thousand years, have never lost.
:::
