title: "The finals and tones of Middle Chinese"
---

The initial is the richest person the syllable has; the final is the household register of the whole clan. How the 206 rhymes of the *Guangyun* 广韵 ('Broad Rhymes') are gathered into sixteen rhyme classes (*she*), what a "division" (grade) really is, and from what the four tones are defined and into what they then split — this lesson clears the two ledgers, of "rhyme" and of "tone", in a single reckoning.

## The hierarchy of finals: homophone group — rhyme — rhyme class {#sec-jiceng}

::: definition Three levels of the household register, and four types {#def-sanji}
The system of finals is administered at three levels: the **homophone group** (*xiǎoyùn* 小韵; a set of homophones, locked by a *fanqie* spelling) → the **rhyme** (206 of them, the unit of classification in the rhyme dictionaries) → the **rhyme class** (*she*; the sixteen *she*, larger classes which people of the Song induced in order to inspect the "同用" ('used together') and "独用" ('used alone') markings of the poetic rhymes — for example the Dang *she* contains Tang and Yang, and the entering tone of the Jiang *she* is assigned to 业鐸). The names of the sixteen *she* are: "通、江、止、遇、蟹、臻、山、果、假、宕、江、曾、流、咸、深、効" (Tong, Jiang, Zhi, Yu, Xie, Zhen, Shan, Guo, Jia, Dang, Jiang, Zeng, Liu, Xian, Shen, Xiao). The mnemonic is recited in three groups: "通江止遇蟹，臻山果假宕，曾流咸深効" ('Tong, Jiang, Zhi, Yu, Xie; Zhen, Shan, Guo, Jia, Dang; Zeng, Liu, Xian, Shen, Xiao.') (Our translation.) (the Xiao *she* 効 governs the household of "盐添咸衔厳"; the Jiang *she* stands alone, and has nothing to do with the character 江 of the *jian* series, 见系). By coda they fall into four types: ***kāishēng*** (vowel coda: 歌戈之微), **yang** (nasal coda -m/-n/-ŋ: 侵覃, 真寒东, 阳江), **entering** (checked tone; stop coda -p/-t/-k: 缉合, 质曷物, 药鐸) — **yang and entering form matched sets**: Dang—Xiao (宕—効; -ŋ/-k), Zhen—Zhi (臻—质; -n/-t), Shen—Qin (深—侵; -m/-p), so neat as to be typologically suspect, which is precisely the ironclad proof of the "composite" character of the *Qieyun* 切韵 ('Cut Rhymes') (the case of the previous chapter). One place, and one only, is vacant: **the Hen rhyme has no entering-tone rhyme heading of its own** (a few entering-tone characters such as 兀 and 哌 are appended under the Hun and Mo rhymes and the rest, and no rhyme heading was established). For a thousand years this has been cited as the specimen of "a crack in the system".
:::

::: theorem Division: a four-rank classification in which rhyme and initial conspire {#rule-deng}
"Division" is the grid of rows and columns into which each rhyme is arranged in the *dengyun* rhyme tables. **Division I**: the main vowel is broad and large, low and back (the *-ɑ type). **Division II**: it carries an -r- medial, or else the vowel is shifted forward and laryngealised (Li Fanggui 李方桂 reconstructs *-r-; the distinction between 江 and 项 in the k-/g- group, as Min and Hakka read them today, is the echo of it). **Division III**: it carries an -i- medial (the most "crowded" large town, by population, in the whole system; labiodentalisation and palatalisation all take place here). **Division IV**: in antiquity it had no -i- medial, and it lay next to Division I (that 先 and 萧 are today read as fine finals is a later palatalisation). Three points that matter: (1) **division is "a property of the whole homophone group", not a property of the initial** — one and the same initial can, by *fanqie*, produce characters of Division III and of Division IV (*jian*, *xi*, *qun* and *yi* are all present in full), so the saying that "*jian*, *xi*, *qun* and *yi* are split into broad and fine" (*hóng* and *xì*) is wrong; (2) **Division II weighs the laryngeals** — restrictions on how finals combine with initials, of the sort "江 has no *jian* or *xi*", are shown directly by the cells of the rhyme tables; (3) **closed Division IV is extremely rare** (only a handful of the 玄 type), so the *Yunjing* 韵镜 ('Mirror of Rhymes') sometimes deals with it by borrowing a cell — the cell left empty is precisely where one sees that the sound system has a boundary.
:::

::: example How a rhyme class is used: looking up the whole family of 江 {#ex-she}
Write out the "three brothers" of a character of the Jiang rhyme in the Middle Chinese system (the correspondence of *kaisheng*, yang and entering) and the *she* it belongs to; then work backwards from Putonghua (standard Mandarin): why are 江 jiāng, 窗 chuāng and 双 shuāng the same rhyme, while 江 is today read with a *jian* initial and a fine final?
::: solution
The Jiang rhyme belongs to the **Jiang *she*** (a *she* in its own right, because the vowel *-ɔŋ differs from the Dang *she*'s *-ɑŋ); the entering tone is the **Jue rhyme** 覚 (coda -k: 捉 and 卓 — Jiang corresponds to Jue); there is no corresponding *kaisheng* (the yang final comes alone). In Putonghua the Jiang *she* was merged into the Dang *she* (-aŋ); the reading of 江 as jiāng is the palatalisation of a Division II *jian* initial (k-→tɕ-, the condition being a fine final; see [[phonology/to-mandarin]]); that 窗 and 双 keep a broad reading shows that characters of Division II went their separate ways — **a *she* is a "province" of historical geography, a rhyme is a "county or city", a homophone group is a "house number"**. Whenever one compares dialects, one rises first to the *she*.
:::

## The four tones: from "sound" to "tone" {#sec-sisheng}

::: definition The three identities of the four tones {#def-sisheng}
**Identity as category**: level, rising, departing and entering are the third layer of features of the syllable (set alongside initial and rhyme); the *Qieyun* relies on them to divide its volumes. **Identity as morphology**: the two paths, rising and departing, are often a means of word-formation — "王 wáng, level tone (noun) → wàng, departing tone (verb: to reign as king)", "衣 yī → yì (to wear)", "治 chí (to order) → zhì (well governed)". This is what is called ***dúpò*** 读破 ('breaking the reading'; distinguishing meanings by the four tones). The departing tone often corresponds to an Old Chinese **-s suffix** (the line of Zhou Fagao 周法高 and Mei Zulin 梅祖麟: the causative, and nominalisation); the rising tone often corresponds to a **-ʔ coda** (the riddle of -ʔ in kinship words and diminutives such as 父, 母, 女 and 宝) — tone is the layer of sediment in which the fossils of word-formation have settled. **Identity as description**: the tone values cannot be known; one can only start from the old rubrics "平言舒、上厉、上去远、入质直" ('level speech is easy; the rising is sharp; rising and departing lie far apart; the entering is plain and straight') (Our translation.) — and, putting together the descriptions of the four tones in the body of texts handed down in the line of the *Diaojing* 调经 ('Classic of Tones'), the setting of "平言" against "上去" ('level speech' against 'rising and departing') in discussions of tonal faults (*shēngbìng*) by people of the Qi and Liang, and the correspondences of tone categories in the dialects of today, reconstruct as follows: level was a long mid-level tone, rising was a rise or a short rise with a glottal stop, departing was a high fall or a long departing tone with a residual -s, entering was short and abrupt with a stop coda — **to write a precise five-level notation for the tone values of Middle Chinese is to exceed one's authority; to write the equations of change for the tone categories is one's own line of work** (the splitting of the level tone into yin and yang, and voiced rising changing to departing: see [[phonology/to-mandarin]]).
:::

::: history The discovery of the "four tones": Yongming prosody and Buddhist *zhuandu* {#sec-yongming}
Chinese has always had people who spoke of "sound", but those who **set the four tones up as a programme for the art of letters** were Zhou Yong 周颙, Shen Yue 沈约 and their circle, in the eighth year of Yongming of the Southern Qi (about 480–490): "欲使五色相宣、八音相让……四声韵遍，八病微避。" ('Wishing to have the five colours set one another off and the eight musical tones yield to one another … the four tones running through every rhyme, the eight faults delicately avoided.') (Our translation.) Shen Yue, in the Discussion attached to the Biography of Xie Lingyun 谢灵运 in the *Song shu* 宋书 ('Book of the Song'), boasts "自骚人以来，此秘未睹" ('Since the poets of the *Sao*, this secret had never been seen.') (Our translation.); Zhong Rong 钟嵘, in the *Pin* 品 ('Gradings'), ridicules this as 襞积细密 ('pleats packed fine and close'). The very period in which the theory of the four tones arose was the time when the *zhuandu* recitation of Buddhist scriptures (expounding the scriptures by means of sound), and Sanskrit treatises on the science of sound (声明论), were being brought in — the "discovery" of the four tones was half a literary self-awareness, and half a by-product of comparison across languages. From then on, the whole path "声病—近体—词律" ('tonal faults — regulated verse (recent-style poetry) — the metrical rules of the song lyric (*ci*)'), all the way to "入派三声" ('the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones') in the *Zhongyuan yinyun* 中原音韵 ('Rhymes of the Central Plain'), is a later chapter of this same lineage.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in learning finals and tones
(1) **Inferring a Middle Chinese rhyme backwards from the rhyme of today**: 街 jiē is, in Middle Chinese, Division II of the Jia rhyme (reconstructions of the Jia rhyme by the various scholars are of the *kˠɛ type, very far from the modern reading) — 佳, 皆 and the various *she* of Xie, read today as -ei/-ai, each have a history of their own; look it up before you speak. (2) **Confusing "division" with "*hu*"**: open and closed are the dimension of lip shape (whether or not there is a -u-); division is the dimension of the vowel and the medial; the coordinates are fixed only when the two axes stand at right angles. (3) **Reading "rising and departing are often suffixes" as "rising and departing are often wrong readings"**: *dupo* is word-formation, not a question of right and wrong; in the teaching of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) one may keep "王天下 wàng", and one should not disparage it as "a bizarre old reading". (4) **Taking the entering tone for a "short sound"**: the essence of the entering tone lies in the stop coda; long and short are derived features. Japanese *kan'on* (the final vowel of setsu is the "shadow" of -t) and Yue (Cantonese for the speech of Guangzhou and Hong Kong; -t kept intact) can both bear witness.
:::

::: quiz
What the classification "division" belongs to is ( ).
- [ ] the initial
- [x] the whole homophone group (a property of the final, which also constrains how initials combine)
- [ ] the tone
- [ ] the rhyme class
::: solution
Division is determined by the rhyme, and the unit is the whole homophone group; initials are, depending on the rhyme, restricted to some of the divisions (for example the *fei* group occurs only in closed Division III). That is a restriction on combination, not the thing the definition is about.

::: exercise Filing the household register {level=1}
For each of the five characters 东, 真, 盐, 歌 and 业, determine the *she*, the type (*kaisheng*, yang or entering), and whether it has an entering-tone partner; then point out which two belong to one and the same "yang–entering pair".
::: solution
东 = the Tong *she*, yang (-ŋ), partner 屋; 真 = the Zhen *she*, yang (-n), partner 质; 盐 = the Xian *she*, yang (-m), partner 帖; 歌 = the Guo *she*, *kaisheng*, no partner; 业 = the Xian *she*, entering (the *fanqie* 魚怯切, -p), and it forms a yang–entering pair precisely with 盐. An answer that "业 belongs to the entering tone of the Shen *she*" scores nothing — the yang–entering pair of the Shen *she* is "侵—缉". Full marks go to an answer that gets all five right and points out the pairing "盐—业".
:::

::: exercise Sorting out the *dupo* readings {level=2}
For each of 衣, 朝, 数, 处 and 食, list the level, rising and departing readings and the meanings that go with them, and conjecture which departing-tone readings may have an origin in a causative or nominalising -s.
::: solution
衣 yī, noun → yì, verb (to wear; a departing tone from noun to verb); 朝 zhāo, morning → cháo, to have an audience at court / the court (noun and verb giving rise to each other); 数 shǔ, verb → shù, noun (a departing tone from verb to noun); 处 chǔ, verb → chù, noun (verb to noun); 食 shí, verb → sì, the verb "给…吃" ('to give … to eat'; causative, a typical candidate for departing-tone -s). Both types, "causative / passive" and "nominalisation", are candidates for -s. This question trains an eye for "tone category = a stratum of the morphology". Taking one's stand on the view of Zhou Fagao and Mei Zulin, or remaining cautious, is in either case reasonable; marks are given for the reasons offered.
:::

::: exercise Comparison at the level of the *she* {level=2}
Dialect material: Xiamen "行 kiâ (to walk) / hiâŋ (conduct)", Beijing "巷 xiàng" set against Yue "巷 hong6". Explain by the upward abstraction of the "*she*": (a) what relation the two Xiamen readings stand in; (b) from which *she*, and by what process, the -iang final of Beijing "巷" comes.
::: solution
(a) Literary and colloquial readings: hiâŋ is the literary-reading layer of the Geng *she* 梗 (行 belongs to Division II of the Geng rhyme 庚, and the -ŋ coda is retained); kiâ is the colloquial-reading layer (the main vowel is raised and fronted, in the same layer as colloquial readings of the 喉 and 鉤 type) — the two readings are not a derivation of child from parent, but inherited layers from two periods and two channels. (b) 巷 is Division II, *xia* initial, of the Jiang *she* (*hrɔŋ); Yue hong6 keeps the main vowel -ɔŋ of the Jiang *she*; Beijing -iang comes from the chain in which, after the Jiang *she* had been merged into the Dang *she* (-aŋ), the main vowel was further raised and fronted — draw one line through the comparison at the two levels, and the apparent "lack of rules" becomes a cross-section of the phonetic values of two periods.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the final is a system, tone is history {level=3}
Write 800 to 1,200 characters. Using the material of this lesson, complete one argument: (a) how the paired phenomena of the "excessive neatness" of yang–entering pairing and of "the Hen rhyme lacks an entering tone" can at the same time support both "the *Qieyun* is a composite system" and "a phonology may be locally asymmetric"; (b) *dupo*, the distinguishing of meanings by the four tones, shows that tone bears a morphological function — how, then, is "a tone category is more stable than a tone value" to be argued back from the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones (the reader assumed: a second-year student in a department of Chinese who has finished this chapter); (c) for one classmate on this course, write a single sentence giving a practical reason for "when you would have a use for a rhyme class".
::: solution
Marking points: (a) neatness = an artificial order, compromised out of many sources; the gap = a natural wound in a living phonology; that the two coexist is exactly what rules out "a single record of a living pronunciation"; (b) tone values can be changed (the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones = a rearrangement of tone values and of their arrangement), while the memory of the tone categories is conserved in the way character dictionaries and dialects assign them; the argument for stability comes down to "a category is the label of a category, a value is the realisation of a value" (the distinction between phoneme and phonetic value, answering from afar to the five-level notation of [[sounds-of-mandarin/tones]]); (c) one true sentence is enough (reading the metrical rules of the song lyric, looking up phonetic loan characters (*tongjia*), comparing dialects).
:::

::: summary
A household register of finals at three levels (homophone group → 206 rhymes → sixteen *she*), and a structure of four types (*kaisheng*, yang and entering, + the one corner where the entering tone of Hen is uniquely missing). Yang–entering pairing is the neatest beauty of the *Qieyun*, and the most suspect. The four ranks of division are coordinates of vowel and medial, not a property of the initial; Division II carrying an -r-, and Division III crowding in an -i-, are the gunpowder of all the great sound changes that follow. The four tones are categories, are fossils of morphology (-s, -ʔ), are an unsolved case of tone value — the working-out of the tone categories may be carried through; the restoration of the tone values is to be shunned. With the telescope of the "*she*" in hand, divergences among dialects turn at once from noise into correspondences; the next lesson looks at how people of the Song set all of this out as a single chart.
:::
