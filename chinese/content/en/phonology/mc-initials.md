---
title: "The initials of Middle Chinese"
---

The rhyme dictionaries supply several thousand *fanqie* spellings, and the division rhyme tables supply some thirty-odd "letters" (*zìmǔ* 字母) — lining the two up is the entire working site of the study of Middle Chinese initials. This lesson first memorises a tabulated "list of initials", and then learns its true identity: **the thirty-six initials are not the table of initials of the *Qieyun* 《切韵》 ('Rhymes Determined by *Fanqie*'); they are an archive of older sounds, several centuries late, given away by an honest spelling system**.

## The thirty-six initials {#sec-zishiliu}

::: definition The five sounds, the six sounds, and clear and muddy {#def-wuyin}
The thirty-six initials are arranged in a matrix by place of articulation (Tang writers called the five sounds "lips, tongue, teeth, molars and throat"; the Song added "half-tongue and half-teeth", making seven sounds) and by manner of articulation (stops and affricates are divided into fully clear, *quánqīng* 全清, secondarily clear, *cìqīng* 次清, and fully muddy, *quánzhuó* 全浊; nasals and laterals are secondarily muddy, *cìzhuó* 次浊; fricatives are mostly classed as clear): **帮滂並明｜非敷奉微** (labials, a heavy set and a light set), **端透定泥｜知彻澄娘** (linguals, a tongue-head set and a tongue-up set), **精清从心邪｜照穿床审禅** (dentals, a tooth-head set and a proper-tooth set), **见溪群疑** (molars), **影晓匣喻** (throat), **来日** (half-tongue and half-teeth). Each character is a representative symbol of one initial class: **帮 is read as "北", and is not read with the pronunciation of the character 帮; it is a label**. The distinction between fully clear and fully muddy is the greatest variable in the history of Chinese tones (the split of the level tone into yin and yang, and the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones, both take it as their independent variable; see [[phonology/to-mandarin]]). The mnemonic of the matrix: "**五音清浊各三位，鼻边归次浊；帮滂並明对非敷奉微，端透定泥对知彻澄娘。**" ('For the five sounds, clear and muddy have three places each, and nasals and laterals are classed as secondarily muddy; 帮滂並明 stand opposite 非敷奉微, and 端透定泥 stand opposite 知彻澄娘.') (Our translation.)
:::

::: history Shouwen's thirty initials → the thirty-six initials {#sec-shouwen}
Among the Dunhuang manuscripts is a fragmentary scroll, *Tangxie Shouwen ruguan wupian* 《唐写守温入关五篇》 ('Five Essays on Entering the Pass, by Shouwen, in a Tang manuscript'), which lists thirty initials: labials, molars, throat sounds and dentals are each grouped by place; there is as yet no independent row for "非敷奉微" (the four letters "不芳並明" carry the work later divided into two rows), nor are "床禅日" and the rest complete as a set. The thirty-six initials current in the Song *Mirror of Rhymes* (*Yunjing* 《韵镜》) were, on that basis, supplemented into a matrix — and the age of the supplementing is precisely the moment when the age in which the light labials and the tongue-up sounds became independent phonemes reached completion. **What lies between thirty and thirty-six is the working site of the sound changes of the late Tang and the Five Dynasties.** From here the study of the letters and the study of the rhyme dictionaries advanced on separate paths and then flowed together: a rhyme dictionary records "字归何切" ('which *fanqie* a character is assigned to') (Our translation.); a chart of the letters records "音有几位" ('how many places the sounds occupy') (Our translation.).
:::

## The letters are not the initial table of the *Qieyun* {#sec-bushi}

::: theorem Between thirty-six and forty-one or forty-two {#rule-siying}
Tested by *fanqie* linking (*xìlián* 系联; see the next section), the initial classes of the *Qieyun* outnumber the thirty-six initials: Chen Lan 陈兰 of the Qing, in his *Qieyun kao* 《切韵考》 ('An Examination of the *Qieyun*'), obtained **forty classes** by linking; modern scholars went on supplementing and correcting this, and the mainstream conclusion is **forty-one classes** (disagreement concentrates on whether the 日 initial and the several classes "俟" and "禅" are to be split or merged — the point lies in the pattern of the differences, not in the final digit): 帮 and 非, 滂 and 敷, 並 and 奉, 明 and 微 are **complementary each to each** — the 帮 set spells only characters of Divisions I and IV, the 非 set spells only closed characters of Division III, and the upper characters of the two heaps of *fanqie* **never meet across rhyme and division**, so that in the *Qieyun* they are in fact one class, while later writers set up two letters apart, to match two ages and two sound values; 端 and 知, and 彻 and 澄, follow the same principle (once the linkings within Division III have been made, the tangle of splitting off "娘" and "日" is still more marked). The conclusion: **the thirty-six initials are a "near relative by projection" of the sound classes of the *Qieyun* at the end of the Tang and the beginning of the Song, not the original itself** — to read the *Qieyun* by the letters is like reading the *Book of Songs* in the rhymed stage speech (*yùnbái* 韵白) of Peking opera: off, yet not so far off as to be simply off; but one must keep the generation gap in mind.
:::

::: example A small case in linking: the family of the 帮 class {#ex-xilian}
The material is three *fanqie*: (1) 杯, spelled 布回; (2) 悲, spelled 府文; (3) 北, spelled 博德. The question: are the upper characters "布" and "博" of the same class as "府"? On what grounds is this decided? If they are judged the same class, how is the difference in the modern pronunciations of the three sets (b/f) to be explained?
::: solution
布 and 博 are both upper characters in *fanqie* of the 帮 initial (杯 and 北 are both characters of the 帮 initial, and the spelling is directly *yīnhé* 音和, 'agreement of sound'); "府" is read f- today (among the thirty-six initials it is assigned to the 非 initial), yet the "悲" which it spells is 帮-initial p- — the modern difference between b and f is precisely the scar of the split between heavy and light labials (the *lèigé* 类隔, 'separation by class', of the previous chapter). By the first layer of linking, "上字用同一字或同组字即同类" ('where the upper characters use the same character, or characters of the same set, they are the same class') (Our translation.), 布 and 府 are linked into one chain by "同被用于帮组字且彼此又音互见" ('both being used for characters of the 帮 set, and each appearing in the other's alternative readings') (Our translation.); but if one goes by **distribution** alone (布 only in Divisions I and IV, 府 only in closed Division III), one reaches the judgement "互补分布、古本一类" ('complementary distribution; originally one class') (Our translation.) — and this is precisely the method of "supplementing by distribution" which Zeng Yungan 曾运干 formulated. The present question is the smallest unit of Chen Lan's work on the forty-one classes: **a class is defined by a chain of evidence, not by the shape of the character or by the modern pronunciation**.
:::
:::

::: definition Four routes of the *fanqie* linking method {#def-xilian}
Four operations, founded in Chen Lan's *Qieyun kao* and set in order by later scholars: **basic linking** (whenever the upper characters are the same, or the characters used serve reciprocally as the upper spelling character of one and the same character, the characters spelled are of the same initial class); **bridging by different *fanqie* of the same character** (of several *fanqie* for one and the same character spelled, the different upper characters bear witness to one another as the same class — materials of alternative readings, *yòuyīn* 又音, supply the bridges in the chain); **the analytic method** (if the two batches of lower characters spelled by one and the same upper character lie in rhyme-and-division ranges that do not intersect, then that upper character in fact combines two classes and must be split — this resolves "一字两读当两职" ('one character, two readings, filling two offices') (Our translation.)); **supplementing by distribution** (two heaps of upper characters are in complementary distribution and correspond in sound, and are judged to be historically the same class). The product of the four is the table of "initial classes": ***Qieyun* initial classes ≠ the thirty-six initials ≠ a reconstruction**. The three layers each keep their own office: a class is a fact of distribution, a letter is a historical name, and a sound value is an inference of reconstruction.
:::

::: warning Three traps in learning the initials
(1) **Memorising the letters as if they were phonetic symbols**: "並" is not read with that sound bíng; it is the symbol for "a fully muddy stop of a determined class". The sound values in the various scholars' reconstructions (*b/ *d/ *g-, with or without voiced airflow) are papers, not dogma. (2) **Forcing the initials of Putonghua into line with the thirty-six initials**: Putonghua f- may come by three paths, 非, 奉 and 微; h- is entangled with 匣 and 晓; and behind the zero initial stand 影, 云, 以, 疑 and 微 — **one-to-many and many-to-one** are the regularity, not the exception, and one must consult the "table of the sources of the Putonghua initials" (a table appended to the next lesson). (3) **Using the *Qieyun*'s lack of a heavy-and-light distinction among "照穿床审" in order to investigate the *Book of Songs***: the study of Old Chinese initials (see [[phonology/old-chinese]]) takes as its evidence the phonetic series (*xiéshēng* 谐声) and phonetic loans (*tongjia* 通假), not the letters of the rhyme dictionaries; the two systems stand a thousand years apart, and chains of evidence must not go visiting in one another's houses.
:::

::: quiz
The fact that "the 帮 set spells Divisions I and IV, the 非 set spells closed Division III, and the upper characters of the two sets are never found in each other" shows that ( ).
- [ ] the manuscripts of the *Qieyun* are corrupt
- [x] the split of the two sets is controlled by conditions of rhyme and division, and at that time they were one initial class
- [ ] the thirty-six initials were fabricated
- [ ] the upper character of a *fanqie* may be chosen at will
::: solution
Complementary distribution plus historical correspondence is iron evidence for Zeng Yungan's "古本同用，后则区分" ('originally they were used as one; afterwards they were distinguished') (Our translation.); the choice of the upper character in a *fanqie* is constrained by sound class, and is not free.

::: exercise Judging *leige* {level=2}
Judge whether each of the following *fanqie* is *leige* at the "level of the modern reading", recover its initial class in the *Qieyun*, and state the conditions of the sound change: (1) 眉, spelled 武夷; (2) 都, spelled 当孤; (3) 悲, spelled 府文.
::: solution
(1) 武 (the 微 initial) spells 眉 (the 明 initial): *leige* between heavy and light labials; in the age of the *fanqie*, "武" was still in the 明 initial — the condition: closed Division III drove heavy labials to be read as light labials, and the 微 initial comes from the 明 initial. (2) 都, spelled 当孤: 端 spells 端; Divisions I and IV do not trigger the shift to tongue-up sounds, so this is *yinhe* — a model of the order "先判类属、再论隔否" ('first judge which class it belongs to, and only then discuss whether it is separated') (Our translation.). (3) 悲, spelled 府文: 府 is f- today (the 非 initial), while 悲 is 帮-initial p-; at the level of the modern reading it presents as separated, whereas in the age of the *fanqie* both were heavy labials and were originally *yinhe* — the same type as (1), but the direction reversed (the one originally the same at that time, the other already split today). Only when the two cases are set side by side does one see the true sense of "*leige* is a judgement about a relation".
:::

::: exercise Tracing the sources {level=2}
Characters with the Putonghua initial f come from which of the thirty-six initials? For each, give two examples, and give the conditions. And what of h, and of the zero initial?
::: solution
f: 非 (飞), 敷 (拂), 奉 (房), 微 (无 — the character "无", of the 微 initial, is a voiced light labial which, after devoicing, merged with the 奉 initial and is read f) — the condition is in every case closed Division III (the "未" and "微" series, being open Division III, are exceptions and go to the zero initial). h: 晓 (火) in Divisions I and II; 匣 (侯), on devoicing of the voiced initial, assigned to h according to the oblique tone. The zero initial: 影 (哀), 喻 (云 and 以), 疑 (broad sounds, *hóngyīn* 洪音, of the 疑 initial, ŋ>∅, as in 我), 微 (open syllables of the 微-未 type) — every strand of the one-to-many relation is a part of "规律无例外" ('a regularity with no exceptions') (Our translation.).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: three lives of one table {level=3}
Taking the thirty-six initials as the subject, write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. How is the table used, respectively, as **a working table for the practice of spelling sounds at the end of the Tang and the beginning of the Song** (the ordering of the upper characters of *fanqie*; the laying out of the cells of the rhyme tables), as **an approximate projection of the initial classes of the *Qieyun*** (the problem of forty, or of forty-one, classes), and as **a comparative coordinate for modern dialects and for transcriptional correspondences** (*duìyīn* 对音: colloquial readings in Min and Hakka, Sino-Vietnamese readings, materials of transcribed names)? Requirements: one example of each use; an explanation of why treating the thirty-six initials as a verbatim record of the *Qieyun* is a methodological accident; and, at the end, one sentence on the practical value of this lesson's three layers, "class, letter and sound value".
::: solution
Points for marking: the three uses are not put in the wrong places (the working table, in looking sounds up in the rhyme tables; the projection, in the statistics of linking; the coordinate, in the comparison of transcriptional correspondences — one example of each, from the lesson or from outside it); the criticism of "worship of the letters" is aimed at confusing a synchronic symbol with a sound across time (isomorphic with "词典两副面孔" ('the two faces of a dictionary') (Our translation.) in [[modern-vocabulary/dictionaries]]); the conclusion on the three layers is to be stated as "证据等级不同的三张地图，不得叠印" ('three maps of differing grades of evidence, which must not be printed one upon another') (Our translation.).
:::

::: summary
The thirty-six initials are a matrix of symbols for initial classes: place (lips, tongue, teeth, molars and throat, plus half-tongue and half-teeth) × manner (clear and muddy). They come from the Tang text of Shouwen's thirty initials, and were supplemented in the age when the separate establishment of the light labials and of the tongue-up sounds was complete. *Fanqie* linking (basic linking, different spellings of the same character, the analytic method, supplementing by distribution) shows that the initial classes of the *Qieyun* are about forty-one, give or take, and that the two groups 帮 with 非, and 端 with 知, are two faces of different ages belonging to one and the same initial class — **a letter is a projection, a class is a distribution, a reconstruction is an inference**. The three layers are the first discipline in using the materials of historical phonology. The next lesson moves the lens from "initial" to "rhyme": rhyme classes (*she*), division (grade) and open and closed (unrounded and rounded), and what the four tones really mean.
:::
