title: "Du Fu"
---

The previous chapter closed by giving notice: among the friends of Li Bai 李白 lay the other half of Tang poetry. Du Fu 杜甫 is **Li Bai's opposite and his intimate**: one lived the empire as an advertisement, the other lived the empire as a medical record. In his lifetime his fame as a poet was ordinary; after his death he was posthumously titled the "poet-sage" (诗圣) — **this chapter has to read two people at once: the Du Fu of the eighth century, and the "Du Fu" manufactured from the Song on**.

## A failure's CV {#def-jianli}

::: definition Beleaguered in Chang'an: a genius's negative course {#def-kunshou}
**The CV**: born into a house of poet-Confucians (his grandfather Du Shenyan 杜审言, one of the Four Friends of Letters — **he is the negative asset of a "literary family": the starting point is already a debt**); twice disappointed in the imperial examinations (the family precept “奉儒守官” ('revere the Confucian teaching and hold to office') collided with Li Linfu's 李林甫 farcical recruitment examination under the slogan “野无遗贤” ('no worthy left unappointed in the wild') — **that mass failure in the sixth year of Tianbao slapped in the face, on the spot, the myth that "the court lets no one slip through"**); ten years beleaguered in Chang'an 长安: “**朝扣富儿门，暮随肥马尘。残杯与冷炙，到处潜悲辛**” ("At dawn I knock at rich men's doors; at dusk I follow the dust of fat horses. Cups with dregs, and meat gone cold — everywhere I hide a bitter grief.") (Our translation.) — **the honest version of the poem that seeks a patron: Li Bai wrote the search for a post as a wager; Du Fu wrote the search for a post as bookkeeping**; only around the outbreak of the An Lushan Rebellion did he obtain a minor post, and at once came capture among the rebels, flight from the troubles, demotion, entry into Shu 蜀, and drifting — **his biography is a fault log of the Tang**.
:::

::: definition The *shishi* method: writing the title as an itinerary {#def-shishi}
**The technical definition of “诗史” (*shīshǐ*, 'poetry as history')**: it is not "writing history inside a poem"; it is **using the archival function of poetry to supply the part the histories refuse to file**. Three tools: **① the title is the metadata** — 《自京赴奉先县咏怀五百字》 ('Going from the Capital to Fengxian County, Expressing My Feelings in Five Hundred Characters') and 《北征》 ('Journey North'): place-names, the length of the route, and the genre all go into the title, **and the annalistic cast of mind of the historian-official is pressed into the poem's title**; **② the verbatim record in dialogue** — “**吏呼一何怒，妇啼一何苦**” ("The officer's shout, how furious; the woman's weeping, how bitter.") (Our translation.) (《石壕吏》, 'The Officer at Shihao'), “**听妇前致词：三男邺城戍。一男附书至，二男新战死**” ("I heard the woman come forward and speak: three sons are in garrison at Yecheng. One son's letter has arrived; two sons have newly died in battle.") (Our translation.) — **the courtyard pleading of the conscripting officer and the old woman is transcribed direct; the poet never appears at any point: the bystander's shot is the strongest testimony**; **③ the official-and-commoner dual soundtrack of “诗可以怨”** ("Poetry can give voice to resentment.") (Our translation.) — the "Three Officers" and the "Three Partings" (三吏三别) **neither indict nor celebrate; they only put "the levy" (征) and "the weeping" (哭) on the same screen**: the state wants soldiers, the family cannot produce soldiers, **and Du Fu copies this equation with no solution exactly as it stands — the depth of *shishi* is that he does not solve the equation**. The famous line is entered on the charge sheet: “**朱门酒肉臭，路有冻死骨**” ("Behind the vermilion gates wine and meat stink; on the road are bones of those frozen to death.") (Our translation.) — **two rooms in the same second: montage, not argument; Du Fu adds no further verdict — parallelism is itself the historical judgement, and the empire's fiscal problem is compressed into a single shot.** **The revolution in household detail**: “**妻孥怪我在，惊定还拭泪**” ("Wife and children find it strange that I am here; the fright settled, they wipe their tears.") (Our translation.) (《羌村》, 'Qiangcun') — **the first response to a reunion in disordered times is not joy but "finding it strange": an ethics of the survivor, a column the histories do not have, and Du Fu opened one**; of his daughters in 'Journey North': “**床前两小女，补绽才过膝**” ("Before the bed, two little girls, their patched clothes barely past the knee.") (Our translation.) — **a thing like trousers too short is written into a long five-character poem: for the first time, poverty becomes a detail of epic**.
:::

::: definition Sage of the regulated poem: in old age he danced the shackles into ballet {#def-lvsheng}
**'Climbing High' (《登高》)**: “**风急天高猿啸哀，渚清沙白鸟飞回。无边落木萧萧下，不尽长江滚滚来。万里悲秋常作客，百年多病独登台。艰难苦恨繁霜鬓，潦倒新停浊酒杯**”

The wind is keen, the sky high, gibbons cry in grief;
The islet is clear, the sand white, birds wheel in flight.
Boundless, the falling leaves whisper down;
Unending, the Long River comes rolling on.
Ten thousand *li* of autumn grief, and always a sojourner;
A hundred years of many illnesses, and alone I climb the terrace.
Hardship and bitter regret thicken the frost at my temples;
Down and out, I have newly stopped the cup of turbid wine.

(Our translation.) — **all eight lines stand in parallel, and a single line holds several layers (Song readers prized the couplet on "ten thousand *li*" — a *li* is the Chinese mile, and its length changed from age to age — for layered folds running to eight or nine meanings) — dense melancholy (*chényù* 沉郁) = several times superimposed inside a single sentence**; Hu Yinglin 胡应麟 ranked it “**古今七律第一**” ('first among seven-character regulated poems, ancient or modern') — **this first place was conferred by later people: the standing of Du Fu's regulated poems arrived in the same shipment as the "poet-sage" (see the section on the "seals", 封条)**; **a demonstration of the method of closure in 'Climbing Yueyang Tower' (《登岳阳楼》)**: “**吴楚东南坼，乾坤日夜浮**” ("Wu and Chu split open to the south-east; heaven and earth float on, day and night.") (Our translation.) — **ten characters swallow the whole of Dongting; “戎马关山北，凭轩涕泗流” ("War-horses to the north of the passes and mountains; leaning on the rail, tears stream unchecked.") (Our translation.) — the scale of the cosmos folds back to the warfare one looks for towards the north: Du Fu's law of closure = deposit personal pain in the account of all under heaven, and only then can the interest be paid**.
:::

::: example 'Spring Prospect': a letter that cannot be sent {#ex-chunwang}
“国破山河在，城春草木深。感时花溅泪，恨别鸟惊心。烽火连三月，家书抵万金。白头搔更短，浑欲不胜簪。”

The state is broken, the mountains and rivers remain;
The city is in spring, the grasses and trees grow deep.
Moved by the times, flowers splash tears;
Hating the parting, birds startle the heart.
Beacon fires have run on for three months;
A letter from home is worth ten thousand gold.
White hair, from the scratching, grows shorter still;
It all but will not bear a hairpin.

(Our translation.) (In 757, while he was trapped in Chang'an.) Please carry out three dissections: ① In the opening couplet, of what does "**remain**" (在), and of what does "**deep**" (深), each announce the death? ② **Who is the subject of "flowers splash tears" (花溅泪) and "birds startle the heart" (鸟惊心)** — is this a question for rhetoric, or a question for grammar? ③ Make an **information-economics** valuation of “家书抵万金” ("a letter from home is worth ten thousand gold") (Our translation.) (beacon fires = communications cut off; ten thousand gold = a coefficient of comparative price), and explain: **why is this poem taken as a specimen of "poetry as history" when it almost does not "record" any event**?
::: solution
① **“山河在” ("the mountains and rivers remain") = everything does not remain** (the city walls and the palaces have burned; only the nature on the map is left — **the character 在, "remain", is the ellipsis of a last testament**); **“草木深” ("the grasses and trees grow deep") = there is no one** (spring keeps shop as usual, but for whom is the shop open: **“deep” is the unit of measure of a city with no people**). ② **Grammatically it may be read either way; rhetorically it must be readable either way**: the flowers make a person weep / the flowers splash tears of their own; the birds startle a person / the one who hates the parting hears the birds and is startled — **the collapse of the boundary between subject and object is precisely the definition of “感时” ("moved by the times"): when the world has broken, you cannot be sure whether the pain belongs to you or belongs to the spring** (the highest grade of what a class in rhetoric calls "the transference of feeling" (移情): not transferring feeling onto things, but **the subject–object system crashing**). ③ **A letter from home is without price ("ten thousand gold" = a polite way of saying that comparison of prices has failed) — the break in communications demotes "one's kin are alive" to a rumour awaiting verification; the poem itself is that letter which cannot be sent: literature here fills the place of the communications system**. ④ A specimen of poetry-as-history, and yet no event — because **what poetry-as-history records is not an inventory of events, but the psychological accounts after the events are over: the histories wrote the military dispatches of the An Lushan Rebellion; what Du Fu deposited is its "emotional balance sheet"** — **both sets of books have to be there; whichever is missing, we cannot read a complete Tang**.
:::

::: intuition The record of two seals being issued {#int-feng}
**④ The modern removal of the makeup**: works such as Feng Zhi's 冯至 *Du Fu zhuan* (《杜甫传》, *A Biography of Du Fu*) restore the "sage" to a "person" — **the Du Fu who is best to read is precisely that old man of the thatched cottage who finds his guests noisy, is tender towards the little children, and says of himself “幽事颇相关” ("quiet matters rather concern me") (Our translation.) — beneath the title of sage lives a neighbour who loves to grumble**. **A method for reading the seals: whenever you read a Du Fu of any "such-and-such-ism", notice which century the hand that stuck the seal on belongs to**.
:::

::: history Li and Du: a lawsuit of a thousand years {#sec-guansi}
**Each admired the other in life; after death they stand as a pair**: Du Fu presented Li Bai with more than ten poems — “**千秋万岁名，寂寞身后事**” ("A name for a thousand autumns and ten thousand years; after death, a lonely affair.") (Our translation.) — early on, he had written a prophecy of the market price the two of them would fetch; Li Bai's poems in reply are cool, and many are false attributions (the poem “饭颗山头” ('Atop Fanke Mountain') is fake news put together by later people); **the mid-Tang**: the tomb inscription by Yuan Zhen 元稹 elevates Du and suppresses Li — the "debate on the relative merits of Li and Du" opens its hearing; **the Song**: the joint style "Li and Du" becomes the default configuration — Su Zhe 苏辙 and Wang Anshi 王安石 each hold to their own account of likeness and difference, and the debate on relative merit becomes a fixed type of question; **the Jin and the Yuan**: Yuan Haowen 元好问, with his quatrains on poetry, rearranges the Tang's order of seating, and Li and Du alike come under his judgement — critics of the Jin take the Tang men's lawsuit as their own chessboard; **the Ming and the Qing**: the *gédiào* school (格调, 'form and tone') and the *xìnglíng* school (性灵, 'native sensibility') each choose the teachers they will follow; those who take Du as their ancestor honour "orthodoxy", those who favour Li take "genius" — a lawsuit in comparative literature, and judge it this way or that, what is judged is each side's own position in poetics; **today**: the textbook's division of labour — Li Bai is given imagination, Du Fu is given reality: two kinds of "handy to use", each in its place (this lesson's reminder: this is a twentieth-century timetable of classes, not an evaluation made by people of the Tang).
:::

::: warning Four traps in reading Du Fu
**Memorising him into "a symbol of worrying for the state and the people"** (**first read the fear, the shame and the joy in 'Qiangcun' and 'Journey North' — morality comes from the reaction on the spot, not from a slogan**: inside his "loyalty to the ruler" there is often tucked a bill of disappointment with one particular emperor); **skipping the titles** (**the title of a Du Fu poem is a third of the text proper: the route, the person addressed, the occasion — the title first, and then the poem**); **taking “沉郁顿挫” (*chényù dùncuò*, 'dense melancholy and broken cadence') for an adjective** (**it is a technique: layered folds (several meanings in one line), broken cadence (a deliberate awkward turn in the regulated line), closure (the person entered in the account of all under heaven) — fit the adjective with a working drawing before you talk of style**); **letting the seals stand in for reading** (**Yuan Zhen's Du Fu, the Jiangxi school's Du Fu, the Song Confucians' Du Fu, the textbook's Du Fu, seals stuck on layer after layer — peeling the seals is not denying the tradition; it is seeing clearly the demand side of each seal**).
:::

::: exercise The emotional balance sheet {level=1}
From a public event of recent years, choose one "official bulletin plus one private personal record" (a Weibo post, a diary entry or a missing-person notice will all do). Imitating the division of labour in 'Spring Prospect', **write two lines of verse, or two sentences**: one line enters the accounts (an inventory of events), one line enters the feeling (the psychological accounts), and in fifty characters explain **why the two lines cannot be merged**.
::: solution
Marking: **the difference of register between the two lines must be visible to the naked eye** (the language of a bulletin against the language of "the grasses and trees grow deep"); the explanation is in place when it says: **to merge them is to have each cancel the other — the accounts swallow the feeling = propaganda; the feeling swallows the accounts = rumour; Du Fu's value lies in insisting that the two sets of books stand together**.
:::

::: exercise The temperature gap between sage and person {level=2}
Two passages are given. 《羌村三首》 ('Three Poems at Qiangcun'): “**苦辞酒味薄，黍地无人耕。兵革既未息，儿童尽东征**” ("They plead hard that the wine tastes thin; the millet fields have no one to plough them. Arms have not yet ceased; the children have all gone east on campaign.") (Our translation.) 《江村》 ('River Village'): “**但有故人供禄米，微躯此外更何求**” ("If only old friends supply the grain of a stipend, what more should this slight body seek?") (Our translation.) Write two hundred characters: to rescue the "person" from inside the "poet-sage", which two scalpels have to be used?
::: solution
Marking: **scalpel one: contextualise** (“但有故人供禄米” ("if only old friends supply the grain of a stipend") = consoling oneself while living under another's roof — **a sage does not beg; Du Fu begs, and he writes it both decently and with a sore heart**); **scalpel two: do not replace the sacred image** (the people's distress in the four lines beginning “苦辞酒味薄” ("they plead hard that the wine tastes thin") is not a persona but the ordinary day — **taking down the sage's title is not for the sake of setting up a little statuette of "an ordinary person": Du Fu's greatness lies precisely in the high-precision burning of ordinary material**); full marks where the lines quoted are accurate and each carries its source.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: why Du Fu is "learnable" {level=3}
Write between six hundred and one thousand characters, assessing "**Li Bai cannot be learned; Du Fu can — but 'learnable' is both the reason he was made a god and the beginning of his being misread**". You must draw on at least four items: **Su Shi's 苏轼 theory of the comprehensive achievement (*jídàchéng* 集大成), in the words “能事毕矣”** ("All that can be done has been brought to completion.") (Our translation.); **the Jiangxi school's pedagogy of line-construction and regulated-verse charts**; **the theory of “体势” (*tǐshì*, 'form and momentum') in Yuan Zhen's tomb inscription**; **“转益多师是汝师”** ("Turn, and draw profit from many teachers: let that be your teacher.") (Our translation.) **from 《戏为六绝句》 ('Six Quatrains Written in Jest')**; and answer: **do "learnable" and "great" contradict each other?** (Hint: take either Kant's theory of genius or the artisan tradition as your comparison; you need not swallow it raw.)
::: solution
Points for marking: the mechanism is stated clearly (**"learnable" = the method can be taught: the comprehensive achievement (he himself turned and drew profit from many teachers) + rules that can be taken apart (the regulated form, method, the craft of the word) — Du Fu is the first writer in Tang poetry to "come with a tutorial"**); the chain of misreading (**learnable → examinable → open to evidential scholarship and to moralising: Song scholars annotated Du, the scholars of the Learning of the Way canonised him as a sage, the textbook fixed the type — the tutorial character of "learnable" was covertly swapped for the sage's character of "assessable"**); the question of contradiction is properly met (**a reference position: they do not contradict — "greatness" sits on the user's side: Li Bai's greatness is a firework that goes off once, Du Fu's greatness is an open factory; the contrary case is also open: to take "learnable" as the standard of value is precisely the beginning of poetics turning from composition to scholarship — the position need only be consistent in itself**); if the conclusion writes "**when we learn Du Fu, what we have never been learning is how to resemble him; it is how he turned into poetry everything that does not look like poetry**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The four on-the-job photographs, the account closed: the ledger of the job search (**the honesty of cups with dregs and meat gone cold, against Li Bai's wager**), the battlefield archive (**the title as an itinerary + the pleading in the courtyard + household detail: the survivor's first reaction is "to find it strange"**), the building site of the regulated poem (**the structure in movements of the sequence 《秋兴》 ('Autumn Stirrings'); "dense melancholy" = several times superimposed inside a single line; closure = the person entered in the account of all under heaven**), the prize podium (**Yuan Zhen sets the tone, Su Shi tops the building out, the Song Confucians stick on the seals, modernity takes off the makeup — the chronology of the seals is a history of demand**). The method in one sentence: **read Du Fu first, and only then read "Du Fu" — reverse the order, and what you read is a stand-in from the history of criticism**. The next chapter is a group portrait: the mid-Tang — the rain of the Ten Talents of Dali, the strangeness of Han Yu, Bai Juyi's strategy of plain speech, and the two voices of Yuanhe.
