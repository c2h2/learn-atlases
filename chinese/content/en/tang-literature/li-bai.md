title: "Li Bai"
---

The chapter must not write the hype, and must not pretend the hype is unexplained. **Its argument: the apparent effortlessness of this poetry is a technique with visible parts; the famous life was assembled by other people for their own purposes; and one political decision in 756 has been read for a thousand years as the poet's failure of judgment, usually by readers who have never had to choose between two claimants to a throne.**

## The voice {#def-sangmen}

::: definition The technical content of genius {#def-technique}
**The standard account**: a poet of the first half of the eighth century, famous in his own lifetime, associated with the older lyric metres and with an audacious handling of imagery; said to have disliked the constrained forms. **What is actually observable in the text, item by item. ① Register jumps inside a single poem** — a colloquial address next to an archaism, the quotidian next to the cosmic — **which is what readers experience as spontaneity, because the transitions are not prepared; later poets prepare theirs, and are called careful.** ② **Metre as argument**: the choice of an older, irregular lyric line lets the syntax run past the couplet, so a sentence can end where the feeling ends rather than where a parallel pair requires. **③ Hyperbole with a mechanism** — the exaggerated number or distance is placed in a clause that is grammatically subordinate, so the reader processes it as description rather than as claim, which is why it does not invite contradiction. **④ The voice of the drink: an audience is conjured in the poem (a friend, a moon, an addressee who cannot answer), which converts a lyric into a scene at almost no cost.** **The lesson's claim: these four are learnable, and were learned; what is not learnable is the proportion. The chapter therefore refuses the word "gift" as an explanation and offers instead "a set of devices deployed at a frequency nobody sustained".**
:::

## The ledger {#def-zhangben}

::: definition How a famous name is paid for {#def-money}
**The standard account**: the poet travelled widely, was summoned to the Hanlin academy and then left the capital, was admired by officials, and was the subject of anecdotes during his lifetime. **Three items the biography under-reports. ① Recommendation cost money and time**: a career without an examination degree had to be made by presentation, patron introduction and hospitality, and the surviving poems of petition and farewell are the receipts (the course's recurring claim that **a literary career is an expense account**). **② The fame was partly other people's work** — a senior poet's praise, a statesman's mention, and then the poetry-talks' accumulation of anecdotes; **a famous name is a subscription list, and the subscribers need the name to be famous for their own story to be worth telling.** ③ **The text is an editorial object**: what survives is what anthologies and commentators chose, in a tradition that kept selecting for the persona it already believed in (**standard account**: the numbers of the collected works and the authenticity of individual pieces are debated). **So the biographical fact and the literary reputation are different kinds of object; the second is the more expensive and the less true.**
:::

::: definition The political bill: the Prince Yong case {#def-zhengzhi}
**The standard account**: in 756, during the crisis of the rebellion, the poet joined the staff of a prince who then moved against an rival centre of power; the prince was defeated and the poet was convicted, imprisoned and exiled, with a pardon in 759. **Three readings the tradition has offered, in order of honesty. ① The innocent-error reading** — an old man flattered into a bad appointment — **which has the merit of explaining the poetry's later bitterness and the demerit of explaining nothing about the choice.** ② **The ambitious reading**: a man without a degree took the only route to office still open (**standard account**: this is the more common view in modern biography). **③ The legibility reading**, this lesson's own: the succession arrangement of 756 was genuinely unclear to contemporaries, several men of judgement served both centres, and we can see the outcome only because we know who won. **Which is not a defence but a method: it asks what the actor could see, rather than what we would have seen.** **The chapter's rule for the whole course: a political mistake in a canonical life should be analysed as a decision under uncertainty before it is analysed as character, because the second analysis is what the tradition wrote and the first is what a historian owes.** And a fact worth keeping beside it: **the poet's politics were mostly the politics of a man who wanted to be useful without paperwork, which is a familiar and unstable combination.**
:::

::: example Manufacturing the Banished Immortal {#ex-zhexian}
A label attaches to the poet — "Banished Immortal" — reported as a senior contemporary's exclamation, then repeated in biographies, then painted, then used as the title of poems about him. **Trace the label through four uses and say what each use needed.**

::: solution
**① In the reported exclamation** the label is a compliment and a classification: it places the man among the transcendent, a live religious register in the eighth century, and it costs the speaker nothing. **② In the biography** it is evidence of character and does structural work — it explains the poet's unsuitability for office in one image. **③ In the paintings and the anecdote collection** it is a product: the drunk, the moon, the water — three properties that any competent workshop could reproduce, which is how a reputation becomes iconography. ④ **In later poems about him** it is an argument: to write to the Banished Immortal is to claim kinship with the tradition's figure for untalent wasted by institutions. **What each needed: classification, explanation, sale, and inheritance.** **Marking: whoever notes that the label has done more work on the poetry than on the poet — that readers hear the persona before they read the line, and that the four devices of the first section are the ones the label predicts — takes an A.** **That is the mechanism this course calls "the anthology controls the reading"; here the epitaph does it, and it is cheaper than an anthology.**
:::
:::

::: intuition Anachronistic honesty {#int-chengshi}
**A temptation the sources invite: to read the poet as a free spirit in an age of careerists, and to date the sentiment to the eighth century. Three correctives. ① The vocabulary of freedom is available in his texts because it was a literary register with its own conventions, not because he felt modern.** ② **The biographical indignities we resent on his behalf — the petitions, the flattery, the waiting — were the ordinary cost of a job, and he paid them knowing the rate.** ③ **The rebellion-era decision is not a fall from innocence but a career decision made under bad information, which is what career decisions usually are.** **What is worth keeping from the romantic reading, because the texts support it: an unusual willingness to be caught mid-feeling, and a refusal to finish the thought neatly. The poems stop where a regulated poet would have summed up, and that habit — not immortality, not wine — is the technical reason the voice sounds candid.** **Exercise: find a contemporary writer whose persona you know better than their technique, and write the technique first.**
:::

::: history A stream of posthumous honours {#sec-zhuifeng}
**During his lifetime**: famous, recommended, briefly at court, and nowhere in the bureaucracy; the reputation is carried by other men's praise (**standard account**). **Late eighth century**: editors assemble the collected works and Bai Juyi and others begin the argument about what kind of poet he was (**standard account**). **Song**: the pairing with the other great name is fixed, and the pairing does the work of a classification system for the whole dynasty (**standard account**); **commentary, imitation and the stele tradition multiply the text.** **Yuan to Ming**: the drama and the fiction make him a character — the drunk, the moon, the river — **and the icon escapes literature entirely** (**standard account**). **Qing**: critics measure him against the regulated tradition and find him deficient by a standard he had not accepted, which is a familiar operation in the history of criticism (**standard account**). **Twentieth century**: translations make him the most familiar Chinese poet abroad, and the persona is read as a universal temperament; **at home he is installed in the school curriculum, where the biography now carries the poetry** (**standard account**). **The chapter's ending, stated plainly: the man's position in the tradition is the accumulation of other people's uses of him, and every use was rational for its user. This does not make the poems a secondary object — the devices of the first section are still there, still unexplained by the label, and still the only reason the label survived being wrong about his life.**
:::

::: warning Four traps in this chapter
**Explaining the poems by the persona** (**list the devices**); **treating the biography as the key** (**the text is an editorial object**); **judging 756 with the map we have** (**what could he see?**); **dating modern candour to the eighth century** (**freedom is a register here, not a temperament**).
:::

::: exercise List the devices {level=1}

::: solution
Marking: **the alternative must be describable as a plausible line**, not as a style ("he could have summed up") (the technical content).

::: exercise Essay: what the label buys {level=3}
In six hundred to a thousand words, discuss the claim that **every large reputation is held up by four columns — a classification, an explanation, a product and an inheritance — and the columns are maintained by people who need them for reasons unrelated to the poems. The historian cannot remove the columns, because the tradition we are reading is built of them; she can only date each one, and record who paid for it.** Deploy at least six of: **the four devices**, **the expense account**, **the subscription list**, **the editorial object**, **the three readings of 756**, **the four uses of the label**, **anachronistic honesty**, **the stream of honours**. Then adjudicate: **should a textbook keep the persona — the Banished Immortal, the Poet-Sage, the Romantic — and if so with what footnote?**

::: solution
Marking: six mechanisms; the adjudication (**a footnote you could actually print**). **Keep it**: the persona is historically effective — it organises eight centuries of reception and is itself a fact students should meet — **but then the chapter must be taught as reception, and the poem read first without the label, which is a sequence most textbooks invert.** **Drop it**: cleaner, and it costs the student the entire tradition of imitation, allusion and painting, which is a larger part of the literature than the poems. **Highest marks for a footnote of this shape: "the label was invented by X, made famous by Y, and tells you more about the century that made it than about the man; read the poem, then read the label as a poem about untalent wasted by institutions."** **That is honest, short, and gives the student both objects** — and it applies unchanged to the Romantic, the Poet-Sage and your own department's founder.
:::
