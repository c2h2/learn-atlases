---
title: "Tang tales"
---

Six Dynasties tales of the strange (*zhìguài* 志怪) did only one thing: **prove that ghosts and spirits exist** (the honesty of the historian's brush). The Tang tale (*chuánqí* 传奇) did one new thing: **making up a story itself became a talent**. That difference of a single word Lu Xun 鲁迅 summed up in four characters — **作意虚构** ('deliberate fabrication'): **where lying had been a problem of moral character, in one new genre of the Tang lying is the quality of the work**. This chapter takes that new machine apart: why it should have started up precisely in the society of the presented scholars (*jìnshì* 进士), and how, by means of the motif of "the woman abandoned", it wrote litigation documents a thousand years early.

## Three engines {#def-fadongji}

::: definition From "street talk and alley gossip" to "skill in one art" {#def-mingming}
**The household-register problem**: the "Treatise on Literature" in the *Book of Han* lists the school of fiction-writers as "**街谈巷语、道听途说者之所造**" ('what the makers of street talk and alley gossip, of tales heard along the road, produce') (Our translation.), and yet the head of the nine streams says plainly "**君子弗为**" ('a gentleman does not practise it') — **to be registered is to be banished: from then on fiction lived with a household register reading "useful, but not of the proper class"**. **The first engine: the brush of historical biography moves downward** — the full kit of the biographical form (name, native place, lineage, fortunes, and the appraisal at the end of the piece) is taken over to open a file on foxes, ants and wandering knights: **write a person who does not exist in the grammar used for writing history, and the reader will believe the non-existence along with the rest** ([[han-to-nanbeichao/tales-criticism]] the "licence of the historian's brush" in *Soushen ji* 搜神记 (*In Search of the Supernatural*) is here endorsed over to a new use: **tales of the strange use the historian's brush to seek the true; Tang tales use the historian's brush to seek the lifelike**). **The second engine: the scroll-presentation (*xíngjuàn* 行卷) of the *jinshi*** — the Song writer Zhao Yanwei 赵彦卫 records that when Tang candidates submitted scrolls they "**先以篇、赋、歌诗，次以杂文，谓之‘温卷’……文、史、议论三体为一体，可见其才**" ('first offered pieces, rhapsodies, songs and poems, and next miscellaneous prose, which was called "warming the scroll" … prose, history and argument, three forms in one body, so that his talent could be seen') (Our translation.), and he asserts that miscellaneous prose of this kind "**即传奇**" ('is the *chuanqi*') (**fiction as a sample of talent: one submission displaying at once the three items of narration, argument and the expression of feeling — the prototype of the modern portfolio**); **scroll-presentation is not necessarily historical fact (a Song writer telling it after the event; Song writers themselves loved doing just this), but it is a true fact for the sociology of literature: the finest group of prose writers in the Tang were precisely *jinshi*, and the Tang tale happened to be the entertainment assignment they wrote for themselves**. **The third engine: the city and its listeners** — Tang documents already record marketplace "talking" (*shuōhuà* 说话, telling stories); among the miscellanies collected in *Taiping guangji* 太平广记 (*Extensive Records of the Taiping Era*) there survive the scenes of competitive telling at wine banquets; temple and monastery stages, and the post roads of merchants and travellers, supplied listeners and a network of transmission. **There is no need to nail every piece of evidence down: of the three engines, the historian's brush is the hardest, scroll-presentation the loudest, and storytelling the hollowest — the proportion of hollow to solid in the evidence is itself the homework this lesson asks you to hand in**.
:::

::: example Two dreams, one written judgement {#ex-liangmeng}
**The World inside a Pillow** (*Zhenzhong ji* 枕中记): in an inn at Handan, Lu Sheng 卢生 sighs that his great ambition is unfulfilled, and Old Man Lü 吕翁 gives him a pillow — the crucial plot: **in the dream he marries into a great house, passes the *jinshi*, is made chief minister, is slandered, has his household confiscated, and on the point of execution takes leave of his wife; when he starts awake, the millet in the innkeeper's pot is still not cooked** — Lu Sheng comes to understand: "**夫宠辱之道，穷达之运，得丧之理，死生之情，尽知之矣！**" ('The way of favour and disgrace, the fortune of straitened and successful times, the principle of gain and loss, the feeling of death and life — I know them all!') (Our translation.) **The Governor of the Southern Branch** (*Nanke taishou zhuan* 南柯太守传): Chunyu Fen 淳于棼, drunk, enters an old locust tree, is received as the princess's husband, and goes out to govern "Nanke Commandery" for twenty years, **his reputation in office extremely good; the princess dies, he loses favour, he is defeated in battle, and is escorted home**; after waking he digs into the hollow to seek it: **the State of Huai'an = the ants' nest under the great locust tree, Nanke Commandery = a little nest on a branch** — thereafter he "**遂绝欢宴，不饮酒**" ('and so he gave up merry feasts, and drank no wine'; the general sense) (Our translation.). The two stories are the same machine. Please answer: ① what anxiety does the "dream" of each piece aim at (one on the point of execution, one losing favour)? ② why must the ending of *The Governor of the Southern Branch* **dig the ants' nest open** — is it not enough to write only as far as "waking from the dream"? ③ why say that these two pieces are "**a psychological test of examination society**", and not a textbook of a passive view of life?
::: solution
① **"The World inside a Pillow" tests "the fear of success and failure"**: every step on the road of rank and fame might be the next step in Lu Sheng's dream — **confiscation of the household and the moment before execution are a horror film that plays automatically inside an examination candidate's head, letting him watch it through in the time of one pot of rice**; **"The Southern Branch" tests "dependence on favour"**: everything Chunyu Fen has comes from the ruler's taking him as a son-in-law and from the father-king's trust — **losing favour is more fatal than defeat in battle; the lesson is not "all is empty", it is "your station was never in your own hands"**. ② **One must dig**: the ants' nest is **the narrative's autopsy report** — "the other world" is demoted to a hole in the courtyard: press the spatial scale, and the scale of a life collapses entirely; **to write only the waking is a lesson; to dig it open is the evidence — the Tang tale for the first time lets the reader see the scaffolding of the story, and this is the confession, in its own hand, of 作意虚构 ('deliberate fabrication')**. ③ **Neither piece urges anyone to give up the examinations**: the story closes on Lu Sheng's great awakening, and Tang readers went on sitting the examinations all the same — their effect is to rehearse "what if I fail" **once in advance: the dream is the stress-test environment of the examination system, and the Tang tale is its user manual**; in passing, the two pieces supplied a perpetual-motion machine for the "Four Dreams of Linchuan" of Tang Xianzu 汤显祖 — **one motif in business for twelve hundred years**.
:::
:::

::: definition The woman abandoned: two kinds of law {#def-nvxing}
**The Story of Huo Xiaoyu** (*Huo Xiaoyu zhuan* 霍小玉传): **the lawsuit kind**. Xiaoyu knows that her family rank is not enough — that phrase of hers, "**不当匹偶**" ('not a fit match'), amounts to **admitting from the start that the contract was never signed**; so she lowers her demand to the minimum: she does not ask to be the principal wife, only to be kept for a few years (**the annexed clause "愿岁稍久" ('I wish the years might last a little longer') is the saddest passage in the whole piece: she negotiates a dispute of love into the renewal of a lease**); the wording of the oaths beforehand is steeped in the flavour of law (pointing to mountains and rivers, swearing by the bright sun — **like the clauses of a contract**), and after she is cast off she issues the most famous prosecution from beyond the grave in the history of Chinese literature: "**我死之后，愿为厉鬼，使君妻妾，终日不安！**" ('After I die, I wish to become a vengeful ghost, and leave your wives and concubines without peace all day long!') (Our translation.) — **turning a household matter into a public case**; then the "stranger in the yellow shirt" drags Li Yi 李益 by force to the meeting — **the knight-errant is a folk patch where the law is absent**; legend has it that Li Yi thereafter sickened with jealousy, "**狂顾乱骂，疑妻有私**" ('glaring about wildly and cursing at random, suspecting his wife of an affair') (Our translation.) — **the way the curse takes effect is exactly what the listeners want: the rest of the faithless man's life is sentenced by legend to life imprisonment**. **Yingying's Story** (*Yingying zhuan* 莺莺传): **the self-defence kind**. Yingying's "**待月西厢下，迎风户半开**" ('Waiting for the moon beneath the western wing, the door that faces the wind stands half open.') (Our translation.) — **a woman who can write poems takes back the initiative with a code and with an action**; when cast off she accepts the account: "**始乱之，终弃之，固其宜矣**" ('He began by leading her astray and ended by abandoning her; that is indeed as it should be.') (Our translation.) — **a moral self-proof carried out beyond reproach is precisely what it looks like when one's standing to sue has been stripped away**; Scholar Zhang's speech for the defence — "**夫天之所命尤物也，不妖其身，必妖于人**" ('Whatever Heaven appoints as a creature of extraordinary beauty: if it does not prove baleful to itself, it must prove baleful to others.') (Our translation.) — **shifts the blame onto the species "尤物" (*yóuwù*, 'a creature of extraordinary beauty'): the most famous cad's oration in literary history**; one sentence at the end of the piece, "**时人多许张为善补过者**" ('People of the time mostly granted that Zhang was good at making amends.') (Our translation.) — **the distance in tone between "people of the time" and the narrator is the distance of the author's conscience**. The case of Yuan Zhen 元稹: the prototype of Zhang has all along been pointed to, from close at hand, by Song writers as the author portraying himself — **a story that lets its protagonist use theory to acquit himself is the most honest written confession**; downstream: *Yingying's Story* → the *zhugongdiao* 诸宫调 of the Western Wing → Wang Shifu's 王实甫 *The Story of the Western Wing* — **a Tang betrothal dispute retried for seven hundred years, every revised judgement standing on Yingying's side**.
:::

::: intuition Why the non-human is better than the human {#def-yilei}
*The Tale of Miss Ren* (*Ren shi zhuan* 任氏传): the fox-spirit Ren is **chaste and righteous**; to keep company with the poor scholar Zheng Liu 郑六 she sets out at the risk of her life, and in the end dies by hunting dogs, her true form revealed — **the non-human lives by human standards, and is verified by a beast's corpse: the earliest "tragedy of a double identity" in Chinese fiction**. *The Tale of Liu Yi* (*Liu Yi zhuan* 柳毅传): a scholar carries a letter a thousand *li* for an abused dragon girl; the Lord of Qiantang 钱塘君, back from rescuing his kin, **forces the marriage at knifepoint** — Liu Yi first refuses to marry, and marries only after he has returned home — **making "repaying a kindness" into a marriage contract of the kidnapping kind; inside a Tang wish-fulfilment set piece there hides a hard flaw in the marriage system that cannot be negotiated**. Taken together: **in the Tang tale the best moral character is assigned to foxes and dragons. It is not that the authors prefer animals; identities in the human world are too full — the great clans, the examination ranks and the marriage brokers each have their statutory place, and only the non-human can draw the established post of "virtue"**. **The non-human is literature's talent-introduction programme** ([[han-to-nanbeichao/tales-criticism]] "a coat of ghosts and monsters, a core of legal principle" is here upgraded into a criticism of institutions).
:::

::: history The drift of the genre {#sec-piaoyi}
**The mid-Tang explosion**: Shen Jiji's 沈既济 "Inside the Pillow" and "Miss Ren" (between the Dali and Zhenyuan eras), Xu Yaozuo's 许尧佐 *The Tale of Miss Liu* (*Liu shi zhuan* 柳氏传), Bai Xingjian's 白行简 *The Tale of Li Wa* (*Li Wa zhuan* 李娃传), Jiang Fang's 蒋防 *The Story of Huo Xiaoyu*, Yuan Zhen's *Yingying's Story*, Chen Xuanyou's 陈玄祐 *Record of the Soul's Departure* (*Lihun ji* 离魂记) ("**离魂**" (*líhún*, 'the soul's departure') = the body asking leave on love's behalf — **the ancestor of the later *Qiannü lihun* 倩女离魂 (*Qiannü's Soul Leaves Her Body*) and *The Peony Pavilion***), Li Chaowei's 李朝威 *The Tale of Liu Yi*, Niu Sengru's 牛僧孺 *Records of the Mysterious and Strange* (*Xuanguai lu* 玄怪录), Li Fuyan's 李复言 *Continued Records of the Mysterious and Strange* (*Xu Xuanguai lu* 续玄怪录) — **single pieces and collections side by side: the Tang tale begins to receive the treatment of a "literary collection"**; **the late Tang**: Pei Xing's 裴铏 *Chuanqi* 传奇 (**the book's title became the name of the genre: "*chuanqi*" = the marketing victory of this book of Pei Xing's**), Xue Yongruo's 薛用弱 *Records of Collected Anomalies* (*Jiyi ji* 集异记), Kang Pian's 康骈 *Records of Unrestrained Talk* (*Jutan lu* 剧谈录) — **the number of pieces surges, and the quality turns towards the strange: the banquet is made literary (competitive telling among the seats); the "shortness" of the late-Tang tale is precisely the gene of its "telling"**; **the Song**: the five hundred *juan* of *Taiping guangji* take them in on a grand scale (**the Tang tale is "filed" by a classified encyclopaedia: to enter the library is to enter the canon**), and the storyteller's script (*huàběn* 话本) makes it colloquial; **Jin and Yuan through Ming**: **Dong Jieyuan's 董解元 Western Wing, the variety play (*zaju*) *The Story of the Western Wing*, the "Four Dreams of Linchuan" — the name "*chuanqi*" is taken over entire by Chinese opera (*xìqǔ* 戏曲): fiction in Classical Chinese (*wényán* 文言) yields its place to the stage, and the story changes its currency and keeps circulating**; **the Qing**: Pu Songling's 蒲松龄 *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio* records the strange by the method of the Tang tale — **the Tang "deliberate fabrication" returns in the Qing as "**用传奇法，而以志怪**" ('using the method of the *chuanqi*, and yet recording the strange') (Lu Xun's words) (Our translation.)**; **the present**: **the "underdog reversal, then collapse" template of *The World inside a Pillow* is standard kit in the structure of web fiction — the *chuanqi* is the longest-lived genre: it changed its name and its generation long ago, and lives on in every trade that "tells stories"**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading the Tang tale
**Reading political plot as historical source** (**the theory that projects the Niu–Li factional struggle over-interprets "Miss Ren" and "Huo Xiaoyu": fiction is not a codebook — "having a factual basis" (*běnshì* 本事) and "having a veiled allusion" are two different things: the former is a capacity of the genre, the latter is an economy of gossip**); **trying the characters as a moral case** (**Zhang is a cad, Li Yi is faithless, Liu Yi calculates: the value of the Tang tale lies precisely in making morality into a device (self-defence / the curse / repaying a kindness) rather than an answer**); **underestimating the difficulty of "deliberate fabrication"** (**that human beings "make up stories and are proud of it" is a late invention: Greece and Rome took epic as true, and the Six Dynasties took tales of the strange as fact — Tang *jinshi* were the first to stamp fiction as talent**); **taking "Huo Xiaoyu" for a love tragedy** (**it is the case file of a marriage by family rank: Xiaoyu herself says it clearly — "**妾本倡家，不当匹偶**" ('I am originally of a courtesan house, and not a fit match.') (Our translation.): the tragedy is not a change of heart; it is that the contract was never signed from the start**).
:::

::: exercise Opening a file on a non-human {level=1}
In the form of a historical biography, write a "biography" of 150 characters for a fictional being (the last train on the metro, a takeaway box, or an urban fox): **it must have a native place, a lineage, and a "the appraisal says" at the end of the piece**, and the words of the appraisal must be **wrong with a straight face** (registered, and thereby banished, as with "a gentleman does not practise it"). In mutual review: which sentence made you "believe the non-existence along with the rest"?
::: solution
Marking: **the kit of historical biography complete** (native place + genealogy + fortunes + appraisal; deduct one for each item missing); **"wrong with a straight face" = fitting an irrelevant statute onto the non-human** (the takeaway box "**以准时为节操，以差评为大故**" ('it takes punctuality as its moral integrity, and a bad review as a grave event') (Our translation.) — **the more formal the misapplied law, the funnier, and the funnier, the more like history**); the test of the telling sentence: hide one piece of real social criticism inside the "appraisal says".
:::

::: exercise Rewriting the written judgement {level=2}
*The Story of Huo Xiaoyu* and *Yingying's Story* are each missing one document: **Xiaoyu's indictment** (what crime is charged? a forecast of the defendant's plea) and **Scholar Zhang's answer** (which line of his own will nail him?). Choose either and write 150 characters in the style of a legal document; **you must quote the original text in full at two places**.
::: solution
Marking: **the cause of action accurate** (Xiaoyu's case: fraud + breaking a vow + discrimination by family rank — note that what she claims is never "freedom of love"; it is "the oath made good": **use her language, not our -isms**); **the point at which Zhang gives himself away**: set "**非礼之遇，没身不忘**" ('an encounter contrary to ritual, which to the end of my life I shall not forget') (Our translation.) against "**尤物必妖**" ('a creature of extraordinary beauty must prove baleful') — **the same mouth citing ritual and fear one after the other is his falsity**; full marks where the documentary form (three sections: facts / grounds / request) holds.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the licence to fabricate {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**the Tang tale is the first time Chinese literature applied for a licence to 'lie': the licence is issued by the examination system, and stamped by the urban audience**". You must call on at least four of: **the "a gentleman does not practise it" of the *Han zhi* (*Book of Han*, "Treatise on Literature")**, **the theory of warming the scroll (*wēnjuàn* 温卷), and the strength of its evidence**, **the dream-machines of "Inside the Pillow" and "The Southern Branch"**, **the non-human establishment in "Miss Ren"**; and deal with one objection: **if fiction obtains its legitimacy by being "useful" (displaying talent / entertaining the townspeople), then when does "fabrication for fabrication's sake" appear?** (Hint: you may choose either end, the "**孤愤**" (*gūfèn*, 'solitary indignation') of *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*, or a modern view of literature).
::: solution
Marking points: the licence mechanism (**household-register banishment + a *jinshi* sample + an urban market = a "legitimacy of use" for fabrication: Tang fiction was never approved in the name of "art"; it cheated a pass by being "useful"**); the strength of the evidence for the warming-the-scroll theory must be stated honestly (**a Song writer telling it afterwards, and modern scholars have doubts — to expound a doubted historical fact as a mechanism is itself a model of academic writing**); the dream-machine (**a confession in the form of an "autopsy report": fiction for the first time makes its own construction public**); the non-human establishment (**places in the human world are saturated; virtue can be drawn only by the non-human**); handling the objection (**"fabrication for fabrication's sake" is a later, modern licence — but the Tang had already laid the ground: when "use" is exhausted (the examinations abolished, storytelling upgraded), the legitimacy of fabrication turns towards "sincerity" — the solitary indignation of *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio* is precisely the intermediate state of exchanging the old licence for a new certificate**); a conclusion that writes "**every genre first pretends to be useful, and only then is permitted to become important**" is an A.
:::

::: summary
Closing the books on the new machine: engines (**the historian's brush seeking the lifelike / a sample for warming the scroll / an urban audience**), dreams (**a stress test of examination society + the confession, in its own hand, of digging the ants' nest open**), women (**a vengeful ghost's lawsuit and a cad's self-defence: morality made into a device**), drift (**a book title becomes a genre, the genre becomes Chinese opera, Chinese opera becomes web fiction — the champion at changing its name and its life**). Method, in one sentence: **to read the history of fiction, first ask the reason each age allowed for lying**. The next chapter closes this volume: how the song lyric (*cí* 词) went from a popular song at the wine party to literature.
