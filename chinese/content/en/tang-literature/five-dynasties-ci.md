---
title: "The rise of the *ci*"
---

For the first half of its life the song lyric (*ci*) did not take its surname from "literature"; it took it from "music": it was **song words for the singing world**, an assignment the author wrote for the singer. This chapter tells how it climbed, step by step, from a popular song at a drinking party up into the main hall of literature — **four steps in all: the wild specimens from Dunhuang, the deluxe debut of *Among the Flowers* (*Huajian ji* 《花间集》), privatisation at the Southern Tang court, and a coronation, of burial-offering grade, for a ruler who had lost his state**.

## Step one: song words {#def-geci}

::: definition *Yanyue* · tunes · filling in the words {#def-yanle}
**The matrix of the song lyric is *yànyuè* 燕乐**: the new musical system of the Sui and Tang, formed by combining Hu music, popular music and *qīngyuè* 清乐 — **the tune comes first, and the words are fitted afterwards: "依调填词" ('filling in the words to the tune'; setting characters to the metre of the tune title), and for the first time the poet delivers the goods to the beat of "Party A"** (the opposite direction from the *yuefu* practice of "采诗入乐", 'collecting poems and taking them into the music': **with the Han *yuefu*, the authorities went and supplied the music; with the tune-lyrics of the Tang and Song, the literatus came of his own accord to apply for the post**). **Wild specimens**: the Dunhuang tune-lyrics (manuscripts that slept for a thousand years, mixed in among the scripture scrolls) — *Pusa man* 《菩萨蛮》, "**枕前发尽千般愿：要休且待青山烂。水面上秤锤浮，直待黄河彻底枯**" ('Before the pillow I have poured out every one of a thousand vows: if we are to part, then wait until the green hills rot. Until a steelyard weight floats on the face of the water; straight on until the Yellow River is dry to the very bottom.' (Our translation.)) — **a lovers' oath written as an inventory of the universe breaking the rules: no literati allusions, only hills, a river, a steelyard weight — before the song lyric entered the city, it was a folk song that had put on a new tune**. Three birth certificates: **① the tune title = a format contract** (*Huanxi sha* 《浣溪沙》 and *Pusa man* 《菩萨蛮》 are each a fixed tune-form; the lyric's own title is often missing, and **the tune title (*cipai*) precedes the topic: the song lyric is born a "composition on an assigned theme"**); **② the singer = the first reader** (whether it is well or badly written is inspected and accepted by the ears around the mats — **this explains the softness, the shortness and the gorgeousness of the song lyric in its early years: a banquet needs background music, and does not need a memorial to the throne**); **③ wineshops, temples and abbeys = the distribution network** (the two characters "**花间**" ('among the flowers') were in origin the scenery of a banquet).
:::

## Step two: the deluxe debut {#def-huajian}

::: definition *Among the Flowers*: the first anthology of song lyrics {#def-hj}
**In 940**, Zhao Chongzuo 赵崇祚 of the Later Shu compiled *Among the Flowers* in ten fascicles by eighteen writers — **the first anthology of song lyrics in Chinese literature, announcing, earlier than any criticism, that the song lyric was a genre that could be made into a "collection"**. Ouyang Jiong 欧阳炯 wrote the preface, and at the opening states the editorial policy in his own words: **poetry is for "述德立功" ('recounting virtue and establishing merit'); the song lyric is for "用助妖娆之态" ('serving to aid an alluring manner') and for "命家僮歌之" ('ordering the household servants to sing it') — the preface itself presses the song lyric down onto the birth-bed of the "艳科" ('gorgeous category')** (**the song lyric's "declaration of origins" is written without embarrassment: this collection of ours simply supplies goods to banquets**); the preface itself gives the programme of the craft — **"镂玉雕琼" ('carving jade and engraving jasper'), "裁花剪叶" ('cutting flowers and clipping leaves'): language is worked as jewellery, and the song lyric is registered as a project under craft**. **The "香而软" ('fragrant and soft') shots of Wen Tingyun 温庭筠**: "**小山重叠金明灭，鬓云欲度香腮雪。懒起画蛾眉，弄妆梳洗迟。照花前后镜，花面交相映。新帖绣罗襦，双双金鹧鸪。**" (*Pusa man* 《菩萨蛮》) — 'Hill upon little hill, the gold flashing and dying away; the cloud of hair at the temples is about to cross the snow of a fragrant cheek. Too languid to rise, she paints her moth-eyebrows, and lingers over her toilet, slow to comb and wash. She looks at the flowers in the mirror before her and the mirror behind; flower and face reflect one another. Newly pasted on the embroidered silk jacket, pair upon pair of golden partridges.' (Our translation.) — **the whole piece is a montage of close-ups of objects and of actions: there is no "I", there is no event, only the optics of the dressing table — Wang Guowei 王国维 called it "深美闳约" ('profoundly beautiful, ample and spare')** — **the revolution in Wen's song lyrics is in the "shot": for the first time lyric poetry does not express feeling, and only sets things out — later ages call this "密丽" ('dense and ornate'), and one may also call it this**: **the earliest MV aesthetic in Chinese literature**. Set beside his "**照花前后镜，花面交相映**" — 'She looks at the flowers in the mirror before her and the mirror behind; flower and face reflect one another.' (Our translation.) — **the two mirrors are infinity for one another: deep inside a shot of this kind, the song lyric for the first time shook off poetry's "speaking" and began to "screen"**. **Wei Zhuang's 韦庄 remedy**: "**未老莫还乡，还乡须断肠**" (*Pusa man* 《菩萨蛮》) — 'Do not go home before you are old; if you go home, your heart must break.' (Our translation.) — **the same tune, and the subject has come back: Wen is an objective shot (the song lyric is as its name "花间" says, 'among the flowers': nobody there); Wei has the singer walk up to the microphone — the lyrical subject of the song lyric registered a household in the Five Dynasties**.
:::

## Step three: privatisation at the Southern Tang court {#def-nantang}

::: definition What is that to you?: the song lyric's exemption clause {#def-fys}
Feng Yansi 冯延巳 (a chancellor, and a writer of song lyrics: carried on a dual establishment): "**风乍起，吹皱一池春水**" (*Ye jinmen* 《谒金门》) — 'The wind springs up all at once, and wrinkles a whole pool of spring water.' (Our translation.) — the Middle Ruler, Li Jing 李璟 (中主), asked in jest: "**吹皱一池春水，干卿何事？**" ('A pool of spring water wrinkled — what is that to you?' (Our translation.)) (Ma Ling 马令, *Nan Tang shu* 《南唐书》) — **one question and one answer between ruler and minister signed and issued, on behalf of the song lyric, a historic disclaimer: what the song lyric writes is privacy on the scale of a pool of water, and it has nothing to do with governance and moral instruction**. This jest is a fossil in the history of genres: **poetry's obligation of "美刺" ('praise and satire') is here formally untied — the song lyric's exemption is the song lyric's liberation** (set beside that shackle of Qu Yuan 屈原 and Du Fu 杜甫, "每饭不忘君", 'not forgetting one's lord at every meal' — **one and the same Southern Tang court could not, in its dealings outward, hand over troops, and did, inwardly, hand over the song lyric's autonomy**). The good of Feng's song lyrics lies in "闲情" ('idle feeling'): "**谁道闲情抛掷久？每到春来，惆怅还依旧**" (*Die lianhua* 《蝶恋花》) — 'Who says this idle feeling was cast aside long ago? Every time spring comes, the melancholy is still as it was.' (Our translation.) — **sorrow with no event, wistfulness with no object: for the first time the song lyric makes a special business of "emotion that has no reason" — the low-pixel line of work that the language of *shi* poetry cannot do**. Li Jing: "**细雨梦回鸡塞远，小楼吹彻玉笙寒**" (*Tanpo Huanxi sha* 《摊破浣溪沙》) — 'Fine rain; the dream breaks, and Jisai is far. In the little tower a jade *sheng* (a mouth organ), played through to the end, is cold.' (Our translation.) — **the waiting of the woman who longs, and the fighting on the frontier, answer one another across a single fall of rain: the court writer of song lyrics tuned "the gorgeous" and "the state of the world" to one and the same low temperature**.
:::

## Step four: a coronation among the burial goods {#def-liyu}

::: definition Li Yu: a lost state in exchange for the song lyric's authority {#def-liyu2}
**The early period is consumer goods of the highest grade**: "**刬袜步香阶，手提金缕鞋**" (*Pusa man* 《菩萨蛮》 — 'In stocking feet she steps along the scented stairs, a gold-threaded shoe carried in her hand.' (Our translation.) — **a live broadcast of a secret meeting with the younger Empress Zhou (小周后): a lyric of a stolen love, written without the least guilty conscience, because he did not know that he himself was the last of the line**); "**花明月暗笼轻雾**", the opening line of the same piece — 'Flowers bright, the moon dim, wrapped in a light mist.' (Our translation.) — **the shot-aesthetic of the Southern Tang court at full level: and then the state was gone**. After the surrender to the Song, four works make up the song lyric's "coronation rite": ① *Xiangjian huan* 《相见欢》, "**林花谢了春红，太匆匆。无奈朝来寒雨晚来风。胭脂泪，相留醉，几时重。自是人生长恨水长东**" — 'The flowers of the wood have shed their spring red, all too soon. Nothing to be done: cold rain comes with the morning, wind with the evening. Tears of rouge; we linger, drunk; when will it be again? It is simply that the long regret of a human life, like the water, runs long to the east.' (Our translation.) — **a jump straight from fallen flowers to the master ledger of a life: no explaining, and no lodging it in any event; "long regret" is promoted to a constant of the universe**; ② *Langtao sha ling* 《浪淘沙令》, "**流水落花春去也，天上人间**" — 'Flowing water, falling flowers, and spring is gone; heaven, and the human world.' (Our translation.) — **eight characters see the funeral of the former state through to the end**; ③ *Yu meiren* 《虞美人》, "**问君能有几多愁？恰似一江春水向东流**" — 'I ask you, how much sorrow can there be? It is just like a whole river of spring water flowing to the east.' (Our translation.) — hypophora, plus a vehicle of comparison sized to infinity: **for the first time sorrow has hydrological data** ("**词至李后主而眼界始大，感慨遂深，遂变士大夫之词**" — 'When the song lyric reaches the Last Ruler Li, its field of vision for the first time grows large, and its stirred feeling grows deep; thereupon it changes into the scholar-official's song lyric.' (Our translation.) — Wang Guowei, *Remarks on Song Lyrics in the Human World* (*Renjian cihua* 《人间词话》): **the key to the song lyric's main hall is cut to the pattern of the key of a lost state**); ④ *Pozhen zi* 《破阵子》, "**四十年来家国，三千里地山河**" — 'Forty years of a ruling house and its realm; three thousand *li* of mountains and rivers.' (Our translation. The *li* is a measure of distance; its length changed from age to age.) — plus "**最是仓皇辞庙日，教坊犹奏别离歌，垂泪对宫娥**" — 'Worst is the day I left the temple in panic and haste: the *jiaofang* 教坊 (the court entertainment bureau) was still playing the song of parting, and with tears falling I faced the palace women.' (Our translation.) — **entering the song lyric with the syntax of *shi* and the scale of history: the song lyric's load doubled overnight**. Another sentence of Wang Guowei's, to close the coffin: "**后主则俨有释迦、基督担荷人类罪恶之意**" — 'The Last Ruler, for his part, seems truly to bear the sense of Shakyamuni and of Christ taking the sins of humankind upon themselves.' (Our translation.) — **hyperbole, and pitched exactly right: Li Yu's 李煜 personal loss is converted, by this genre the song lyric, into a bill that humankind holds in common**.
:::

::: example A quality inspection of two sorrows {#ex-zhichou}
The same subject, sorrow: Wen Tingyun, *Genglou zi* 《更漏子》, "**梧桐树，三更雨，不道离情正苦。一叶叶，一声声，空阶滴到明**" ('The *wutong* tree; rain at the third watch; it pays no heed that the pain of parting is at its bitterest. Leaf by leaf, sound by sound, it drips on the empty steps until dawn.' (Our translation.)) vs Li Yu, *Yu meiren* 《虞美人》, "**问君能有几多愁？恰似一江春水向东流**" ('I ask you, how much sorrow can there be? It is just like a whole river of spring water flowing to the east.' (Our translation.)). Please carry out three checks of quality: ① where each of the two puts the sorrow (in an object / in the subject); ② the difference between the two forms of time, "滴到明" ('dripping until dawn') and "向东流" ('flowing towards the east'); ③ if an *Among the Flowers* lyric is "the sorrow of a product", and the Last Ruler's lyric is "the sorrow of a person", **why the song lyric's "literary standing" is established only after the latter has appeared** — answer on this lesson's "steps" model, and point out the bias of that model.
::: solution
① **Wen: the sorrow is left in the keeping of the timer that is the sound of the rain** (the person does not appear; the pain of parting is broadcast, in the person's stead, by the eaves — **the objective shot at its limit**); **Li: the sorrow is carried on his own subject** (the 君 of "问君" = I — **a bankruptcy report in the first person**). ② **"滴到明" is time taking a beating, passive: one night is cut into "one leaf, one sound", and pain is metered by the unit**; **"向东流" is time in flood, of its own accord: one night is stretched into forever, and sorrow acquires a river-course and a volume — from timer to river-water is the song lyric changing gear, from craft to climate**. ③ The steps model: **literary standing = a history of the weight of "who is speaking"** — song words (a product with no person in it) → the shot (the craftsman) → the scholar-official's song lyric (one particular person, and that person a ruler who had lost his state): **the song lyric's "rise in grade" depends on the subject growing steadily heavier**. A check for bias (the item on which this question gives full marks): **the model takes "sincere personal expression" as its end, and it is the order of values of Wang Guowei and of people today; the *Among the Flowers* system is entirely entitled to countersue: dropping the subject, and prizing craft, is precisely another summit of the song lyric — Wen Tingyun's "nobody there" is a liberation too (only from the point where it no longer has to "have a person" does the song lyric begin to look like the song lyric)**. **Conclusion: the one seated in the song lyric's main hall is Li Yu, but the house was built by *Among the Flowers* — both cases must be entered on the record**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The name is an anxiety about identity {#int-mingzi}
The song lyric has been through a string of names: people of the Tang called it "曲子" ('tunes') and "曲子词" ('tune-lyrics'); people of the Song called it "长短句" ('lines long and short'), and also gave it a fatal other name — "诗余" (**the literal wording is a job title: a product of poetry's spare time — there is no need to investigate the origin of the word; the history of the use of this name alone is enough to read by: a genre obtained the qualification to enter the catalogue by belittling itself**); later still it was called "*yuefu*" (*Dongpo yuefu* 《东坡乐府》 — **claiming kinship: having the song lyric adopted into that respectable family, the Han *yuefu***). **Every change of name is one lobbying for status: tunes → long and short lines → *yuefu* → poetry's spare — four calling cards line up, exactly, as a history of rank among genres**. Set this beside the opening of the lesson: ***Among the Flowers* is headed "诗客曲子词" ('tune-lyrics by guests of poetry') — "诗客" ('guests of poetry') is a wish to keep face, and "曲子词" ('tune-lyric') is the trade name of the goods: the whole life of the song lyric is a pulling between face and goods**.
:::

::: history The song lyric changes its household registration {#sec-hukou}
**High Tang and Mid-Tang**: *yanyue* of the borderlands and popular tunes flowed side by side (the Dunhuang manuscripts give no author's name — **the song lyric's age without names: the song words belong to the tune, and do not belong to a person**); **Mid-Tang and Late Tang**: literati filled in words to the tune (Bai Juyi 白居易 and Liu Yuxi 刘禹锡 each have a piece to *Yi Jiangnan* 《忆江南》 — **the three characters "忆江南" were in origin a tune title: poets began to take work under the tunes**); **the Dazhong years**: Wen Tingyun filled in song lyrics in quantity — **the first professional specimen of a writer who made his name by the song lyric (the ready talent of "温八叉" spent on the short lyric (*xiaoling*): genius used a grade below itself, which was precisely the fuel for upgrading the genre)**; **940**: *Among the Flowers* is completed — the song lyric acquired the household registration of an "anthology"; **the Southern Tang**: Feng and Li — the song lyric's turn to the court, and its turn to the private; **978**: Li Yu went over to the Song; on the Double Seventh he was commanded to a banquet, wrote *Yu meiren* 《虞美人》, and it circulated in singing; it is said that he was granted the *qianji* drug 牵机药 (recorded in the notebooks of Song writers — **the song lyric killed its author: the only emperor in the history of genres to be executed by a work**); **the Song**: Liu Yong's 柳永 long lyrics (*manci*), and Su Shi's 苏轼 "以诗为词" ('using *shi* as *ci*') — **two "confirmations in the regular post" for the song lyric: one a turning-up of the volume (the long tune), one a seizure of power (speaking one's intent)**; in Song prefaces to song lyrics one also often meets the self-deprecation of "薄技" ('a paltry skill') and "游戏" ('a game') — **self-abasement and self-satisfaction in the same sentence-pattern**; **the Qing**: Zhang Huiyan 张惠言 of the Changzhou school of song lyrics read Wen and Wei by way of *bi* and *xing* (comparison and evocation) (**retroactively issuing the gorgeous category a bloodline from *Encountering Sorrow* (*Li Sao* 《离骚》): the writers of *Among the Flowers* woke to find that they had become heirs of the *Songs of Chu* (*Chuci* 《楚辞》) — the most absurd posthumous award in the making of a canon**), and Wang Guowei's *Remarks on Song Lyrics in the Human World* sets Li Yu as the capstone — **the criticism in this lesson is that book's grand-disciple**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in reading the song lyric
**Scolding the song lyric, by the standard of *shi*, for a "small scope"** (**the KPI of banquet song-words was never praise and satire: first take delivery against the contract of the genre, and only then talk of breaking the contract**); **taking the "delegated-voice form" for a dossier of character** (**lyrics in the *Among the Flowers* manner are mostly men writing a boudoir voice: the eyes behind the shot are chancellors, monks, and officials awaiting appointment — to read a "woman's tone of voice" as the author's history of love affairs is the oldest gossip in the study of the song lyric**); **deifying "the Last Ruler's naturalness" and slighting the craft of *Among the Flowers*** (**"naturalness" is a rhetoric too: the Last Ruler's not using allusion is another technical choice — the countersuit in this lesson's quality-inspection question**); **taking "诗余" ('poetry's spare') and "小道" ('the minor way') for a settled verdict** (**that is public-relations talk in which the parties belittled themselves — to cite the language of self-abasement as the written judgement is the same as accepting the defendant's terms of settlement**).
:::

::: exercise A speech for the defence of the gorgeous category {level=2}
Suppose you are the proprietor of a bookshop in the Southern Song, and you are to write a preface of a hundred characters for a new collection of song lyrics in the manner of *Among the Flowers*: you must both say that it is "harmless" (the exemption clause) and say that it is "worth printing" (market patter), **and you must adapt two original phrases from this lesson** (remodelling, as you choose, "干卿何事" and "用助妖娆之态").
::: solution
Marking: **both tracks of the patter are complete** (the exemption track: it does not touch governance and moral instruction / there is precedent among the ancients; the value track: indispensable at a banquet / to be sung and passed on is sales); **the adaptation is natural** (it is enough to come up to the grade of parallelism of a model such as "**风若不语，池水何皱；词若不艳，宴席何设**" — 'If the wind does not speak, how should the pool's water wrinkle? If the song lyric is not gorgeous, for what is the banquet laid?' (Our translation.); those who paste the original phrases on stiffly lose marks); anyone who can plant in advance a hint of "a reversal of the verdict another day" (a modest phrase of the "聊备一格" kind, 'merely to fill one category') gains marks — **a modest phrase was the disclaimer of that age**.
:::

Points for marking: the birth-bed mechanism (**the music business supported the early authors: room for exemption = room for experiment — the song lyric's "lightness" let it dare to try the low-pixel inner life that *shi* poetry did not dare to try**); the logic of five steps (**wild → deluxe binding → privatisation → a heavier subject → recognition after the fact — status is always the last to arrive**); handling of the opposing case (**a candidate example: bullet comments / memes — their "steps" can be predicted: first an anonymous chorus (as with the Dunhuang tunes), then anthologising (a year-end roundup of hot memes), then making it a person's own (signed works by the authors at the head) — the prediction is allowed to be falsified, but the reasoning must follow the model**); if the conclusion writes "**what is called the refined is the vulgar of that earlier time, once it has saved up enough seniority**", that is an A.

::: summary
Closing the account of the four steps: song words (**the tune-title contract, the singer as reader, the vow of hills and a steelyard weight**), the deluxe binding (**the household registration of an anthology, plus the declaration of origins as the "gorgeous category"; Wen's shots and Wei's subject**), privatisation (**"干卿何事" = exemption is liberation; sorrow without a reason is the song lyric's new line of business**), the coronation (**with the loss of his own one state, the Last Ruler prepaid the whole of the song lyric's fee; "bearing the sins of humankind" is hyperbole, and it is also the fact**). One sentence on its names: **every calling card the song lyric has carried is a piece of canvassing**. This volume ends its course here: from the examination hall of the Early Tang to the river at Bianjing, Tang poetry is complete, and there is already a queue at the door of the Song song lyric — the next volume, *Song Literature* (《宋代文学》), begins from that seizure of power, "**以诗为词**" ('using *shi* as *ci*').
:::
