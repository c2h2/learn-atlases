---
title: "Translating into modern Chinese"
---

Translation is the final examination of a Classical Chinese course, and it is also the everyday assignment of two thousand years: when the ancients annotated the Classics, the *zhuàn* 传 ('commentary') was the modern translation of its own day. This lesson gives a **checkable process of decompression**: five moves, three grades of standard, and one marking sheet, restoring the compressed package as modern Chinese with no data lost.

## Five moves {#sec-wu}

::: definition Keep, replace, supply, delete, reorder {#def-wu}
**Keep**: proper names and institutions are carried straight across — names of persons, places and offices, and reign titles (“贞观” and “丞相”, 'Zhenguan', 'chief minister', are copied as they stand, or given a footnote, and are not knocked into the reseller's format of “唐太宗年间宰相”, 'a chief minister in the years of Tang Taizong'); and words that have been the same from past to present (“山”, “水”, “笑”, 'mountain', 'water', 'to laugh'). **Replace**: a monosyllable is replaced by a disyllable, an old word by a modern word — before “戈” is replaced by “兵器” ('a weapon'), first ask whether the context needs the edge of “戈” kept (to replace a word is to pay a semantic tax: see [[classical-chinese-1/old-and-new-meanings]]). **Supply**: supply what is omitted (subject, object, preposition — the brackets owed by the previous lesson are settled here); supply logical glue (the relations implied by “之乎者也” are translated “因为/虽然/那么”, 'because / although / then'); supply a cultural footnote (“有司” = the official in charge; if the main text cannot hold the addition, annotate). **Delete**: delete function words with no meaning (sentence-initial “夫”, the “者” that raises the topic and pauses, and “之” used only to fill out the count of syllables — the “夫” of “夫战，勇气也”, 'War is a matter of courage.' (Our translation.), is deleted; leave it in, and the result is the translationese of “那战争啊…”, 'ah, that war…'); delete the stock phrases of official papers (“谨按”, “伏惟” and “即冒昧” go into the bin for formula words). **Reorder**: reorder the word order — fronted objects, postposed objects of prepositions, and postposed attributives are all put back into the modern formation (“何陋之有→有何陋”, 'what crudeness is there?'; “青于蓝→比蓝更青”, 'bluer than blue'). **Mnemonic: keep proper names, replace ordinary words, supply what is omitted, delete empty padding, reorder inversions — the five characters each govern one class; before you take up the knife, go back to the lesson on sentence patterns and call up the four X-rays.**
:::

## Three grades of standard {#sec-san}

::: definition Faithfulness, clarity and elegance, in the version for Classical Chinese {#def-xin}
**Faithfulness**: reconciling the information — not one content word in a wrong sense, and the tone of the function words not pulled out of shape (“其…乎”, a surmise, translated as an assertion = loss of blood; “不亦…乎”, a rhetorical question, translated as a statement = amputation). The self-check is **back-translation**: turn your modern Chinese back into Classical Chinese, compare the syntax and the number of words with the original, and the place of the difference is the scene of the crime. **Clarity**: the translation must itself be fluent modern Chinese; “文言腔混进白话户” ('a classical tone moving in with a vernacular household') is not allowed (“进行学习” and “做出观看”, 'carry out study' and 'perform watching', are double-strength translationese, [[rhetoric/choosing-sentences]], the Europeanisation account). **Elegance**: a sense of proportion — if the original is plain, the translation does not embroider (the *Analects* turned into a prose poem = over-processing); if the original is profound, the translation does not dress itself up as shallow (the bizarre wit of the *Zhuangzi* must have a counterpart in bizarre wit). **Yan Fu 严复, in the 1897 preface to *Tianyan lun* 天演论 ('On Evolution')**, proposed “译事三难：信、达、雅” ('In translating there are three difficulties: faithfulness, clarity and elegance.' (Our translation.)). It was designed for the eastward spread of Western learning; today, turned the other way about to govern an “古文东渐” ('eastward spread of ancient prose'), it works just as well. The three grades have a priority: **faithfulness and clarity first, elegance according to ability; anyone who harms faithfulness for the sake of elegance does not pass.**
:::

::: example Marking three translations {#ex-ping}
The original: “吾妻之美我者，私我也。” ('That my wife finds me beautiful is her being partial to me.' (Our translation.)) (《邹忌讽齐王纳谏》, 'Zou Ji Remonstrates with the King of Qi'). A: “我的妻子认为我美，是偏爱我。” ('My wife considers me beautiful; that is her favouring me.' (Our translation.)) B: “妻以我为美，缘私我也。” ('My wife takes me as beautiful, by reason of being partial to me.' (Our translation.)) C: “我太太觉得我好看，因为她偏心我呀。” ('The wife thinks I look nice, because she's soft on me, you know.' (Our translation.)) — mark the papers by the five moves and by faithfulness, clarity and elegance.
::: solution
A: a baseline paper that passes — the putative “美我” is reordered as “认为我美” ('considers me beautiful') (reorder ✓); the nominal-sentence frame “者…也” is replaced by the character “是” ('is') (replace ✓); nothing missing, nothing added. The deduction is for blandness: the original's tone of reasoning, layer on layer, has lost its current. B: not translated, only the form changed (vernacular read off as Classical Chinese = zero decompression; **the reverse case of "harming faithfulness for the sake of elegance": refusing clarity for the sake of elegance**). C: the run of “太太”, “好看” and “呀” turns a strategist's domestic drama into a situation comedy — the register leaks current (“私” as “偏心”, 'biased', is a strain; adding “她” to supply the subject ✓); the interjection at the end is made out of nothing. **Marks deducted under faithfulness**: clarity and elegance have both gone too far. A model for awarding marks: **first reconcile the information account (faithfulness), then test for modern speech (clarity), and last weigh the register (elegance)** — the order of marking must not be mixed up.
:::
:::

::: definition A list of the hard bones: where literal translation fails {#def-yinggu}
Five classes of word, five different keys. ① **Names of things and institutions** (“仗下”, “趋”, “再拜”, “牺牲”) — there is no equivalent, so only a **double track, inside the text and outside it**: “趋而进” is translated “小步快走上前（表恭敬）” ('comes forward at a quick short step (showing respect)'); the brackets are the plain person's edition of a footnote. ② **Cultural semantic fields** (“社稷” = the state, but carrying the scent of incense to the gods of soil and grain; “桑梓” = one's native place, but carrying the shadow of the trees) — keep the image and annotate its source, or else simply replace the dead metaphor (“国家”, 'the state'); choose one of the two, and do not cook up a half-fried dish on the pattern of “种桑树的地方” ('the place where mulberries are planted'). ③ **The system of humble and honorific language** (“寡人”, “不谷”, “足下”, “执事”, “窃”, “敢”) — the present has no equivalent ranks of humility and respect; render them generally as “我/您” ('I / you'), plus a compensation of register (when speaking to a king, change “我” to “在下/臣”, 'this humble person / your subject', as the case requires). ④ **Rhetoric of sound** (linked binomes, reduplication, allusion, puns) — once the sound is lost, both cannot be kept; **keeping the sense and losing the sound is the usual indemnity, and losing the allusion along with the sense is an accident** (the “夭夭” of “桃之夭夭”, 'The peach, so young and strong.' (Our translation.), is glossed “少壮貌”, 'the look of being young and strong'; the rhyme is lost in the open, and honourably). ⑤ **The last leap of the false friends between ancient and modern** (“牺牲” translated “纯色祭品”, 'a sacrificial offering of pure colour'; “感激” translated “心被感动奋发”, 'the heart is moved and stirred to action') — the list of false friends in [[classical-chinese-1/old-and-new-meanings]] is paid at face value on the translation side.
:::

::: history From "*gǔ*" to the marking sheet {#sec-jinyi}
China's "study of translation into modern Chinese" has two thousand years of service. The 'Royal Regulations' of the *Book of Rites* designs an officer of “移用语” ('transferring speech'), in charge of “达言语、通志意” ('getting language through, and communicating what is intended'): the starting point, in the system of offices, for translation across dialects. The ***Erya***, nineteen chapters — “释诂、释言、释训” ('Explaining glosses', 'Explaining words', 'Explaining set phrases') turn ancient language and dialect into the common language. To explain ancient words by the words of one's own day is called “*gǔ*” 诂, and exegesis is the modern-translation line of high antiquity. Teachers of the New Text Classics in the Han, hearing them from mouth to ear and writing them down in fixed form, were in themselves a project of translating from what was heard. **In the Tang, Kong Yingda 孔颖达 led the compilation of the *Correct Meaning of the Five Classics***. Fearing that the old commentaries of the Han and the Wei, “辞意幽深” ('wording and sense deep and obscure'), would lose scholars on the way, he composed the subcommentaries along with them — a national committee for approving modern translations of classical texts. **Beginning in 1958, the collated and punctuated "Twenty-Four Histories" were published, collated and punctuated.** The age of the annotated translation opened, and the translation was promoted from “注的赠品” ('a free gift with the commentary') to an independent commodity. **Questions of Classical Chinese translation on the *gaokao* give marks by two to four scoring points** — "faithfulness" for the first time had a form of marking that could be operated. This learning of "translation" has been examined out of the learning of the royal officers and into the final paper.
:::

::: warning Four faults of translation into modern Chinese
One, **letting translation replace explanation**: the whole sentence is translated literally, yet the senses of the words are entirely wrong (“再拜” translated “拜两次”, 'bows twice', counts as half right; a typical case: “肉食者谋之” translated “吃肉的人谋划它”, 'the people who eat meat plan it' (Our translation.), is right on the face of the characters, while the cultural layer is lost clean — “肉食者” means those who hold power; **a literal translation may be the scaffolding; it cannot be the thing delivered**). Two, **adding drama**: in translating “醉翁之意”, to add “他多么渴望啊” ('oh, how he longed' (Our translation.)) is to act the reserve of the original out as a one-act play — **words may be added only to supply grammar; they may not supply emotion**. Three, **cutting characters**: “贤哉回也” translated “颜回很贤” ('Yan Hui is very worthy.' (Our translation.)) — the exclamatory frame “哉…也” is folded into a statement; tone is information, and to delete it is loss of blood. Four, **printing from a set plate**: every “之乎者也” is translated “的了吗呢” — whoever translates the “是” of “唯利是图” as “的” is gaoled before the court then sitting (the case of the standard-bearer of the fronted object; a function word is not a modal particle's employee on the permanent staff).
:::

::: exercise Self-marking of scoring points {level=1}
Translate the three sentences, and mark the scoring points yourself (two or three places in each sentence, noting the type of point tested): ① “夫晋，何厌之有？” ② “我孰与城北徐公美？” ③ “所以遣将守关者，备他盗之出入与非常也。”
::: solution
Reference: ① “晋国有什么满足的时候？” ('Is there any time when the state of Jin is satisfied?' (Our translation.)) — scoring points: 何…之有, a fronted object (sentence pattern); 厌 = 满足, 'satisfied' (content word); 夫 deleted (function word). ② “我跟城北徐公谁更美？” ('Who is the more beautiful, I or Xu Gong north of the city?' (Our translation.)) — 孰与 = the fixed pattern “与…相比谁更”, 'who is the more, compared with …' (sentence pattern). ③ “派将领守函谷关的原因，是防备其他盗贼进出和意外变故” ('The reason for sending generals to guard Hangu Pass is to guard against other bandits going in and out, and against unexpected turns of events.' (Our translation.)) — 所以 = the reason for … (sentence pattern); 出入 = a compound biased in meaning, the meaning biased to “入” (guarding against coming in; translating it “进出”, 'in and out', is also acceptable, and pointing out the bias counts as a bonus scoring point); 非常 = 意外变故, 'an unexpected turn of events' (change of meaning). Marking two scoring points on each of the three sentences is full marks — **marking them accurately comes closer to this lesson's hard measure than translating them prettily.**
:::

::: exercise Balancing the register {level=2}
Translate the same “公等遇雨，皆已失期，失期当斩” ('You gentlemen have met with rain and have all missed the appointed time already; to miss the appointed time is to be beheaded.' (Our translation.)) (《陈涉世家》, 'The Hereditary House of Chen She') into three modern registers: a news retelling, a line for a history course, and copy for a short video — one sentence each, and explain the move that balances the register.
::: solution
Reference: news register, “据报道，戍卒九百人途中遇雨误期，依秦法误期当斩” ('It is reported that nine hundred garrison soldiers met with rain on the way and missed the time; under Qin law, missing the time was to be beheaded.' (Our translation.)) (an objective frame, plus the character “据” as a disclaimer; the urgency of the original gives way to accuracy); courseware register, “遇雨→失期→当斩：一条法律如何逼出陈胜吴广” ('met with rain → missed the time → to be beheaded: how one law forced out Chen Sheng and Wu Guang' (Our translation.)) (the causal chain turned into arrows; the clickbait title is the elegance of a teaching aid); short-video register, “迟到就处死？这班我是不上了!” ('Put to death for being late? I'm not taking this shift!' (Our translation.)) (second-person identification, plus a rhetorical question for suspense — drama may be added at the **level of the frame**, and must not be forged at the level of the original: the words of the judgement, and the actions of the persons, must have a source). Naming the balancing move of each of the three forms is full marks.
:::

::: exercise A complete delivery {level=2}
Choose a passage of Classical Chinese from the course, of 100 to 150 characters, and produce a set of three: ① a sentence-by-sentence parallel table (three columns: original / modern translation / scoring points); ② one fluent translation of the whole (for reading aloud); ③ a process report of 150 characters (which of "keep, replace, supply, delete, reorder" were used, the hardest place, and the alternatives).
::: solution
Points for awarding marks: the parallel table has no wrong sense, and the scoring points are named with terms from the course (fronted object / putative use / false friend …); the translation of the whole has clarity, and the register is balanced; the process report is honest (it owns to one "loss", and reckons clearly why the chariot was given up to save the general — of rhyme, image and tone, choosing two out of the three is the normal way of the trade) — **only when you can reckon the account of losses do you count as having entered the door of translation.**
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is every translation an interpretation? {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Taking “民可使由之”, or one difficult sentence you have translated yourself, as the example, argue that "a translation is one interpretation of the original, and not a shadow of the original". You must use: the terms of the five moves; the priority of faithfulness, clarity and elegance; and one example of "two translations standing together and bearing each other out". The conclusion answers: if every translation is an interpretation, why do examinations still judge translations right or wrong?
::: solution
Points for awarding marks: the claim lands on **interpretation under constraint** (there is room for ambiguity, but grammar, the age and the edition are the boundaries — “可，使由之” is judged illegitimate precisely because the stock of syntax of the same age does not support it); use "two translations standing together and bearing each other out" (for example the old dispute over 罔 in “学而不思则罔”, 罔 = 迷惘, 'bewildered', or 欺罔, 'deceived'; the two accounts of the one case set out side by side); the conclusion does not slide into relativism (what the examination judges is right and wrong inside the boundary, not the single best) — cite the claim in the course that "*judou* 句读 (traditional pause-marks) = interpretation", and compare the two lessons' "freedom and boundary".
:::

::: summary
The five moves of translation into modern Chinese: keep proper names, replace ordinary words, supply what is omitted, delete empty padding, reorder inversions. The priority of the three standards: faithfulness (reconciling the account by back-translation) > clarity (a self-check in modern speech) > elegance (balancing the register); one who harms faithfulness for the sake of elegance does not pass. Each of the five classes of hard bone has its key (a double track for institutions, annotating the source of what is cultural, compensation for humble and honorific language, an open indemnity for loss of sound, false friends paid at face value). From the "*gǔ*" of the *Erya* to the collated and punctuated Twenty-Four Histories, and on to scoring points, this Chinese learning of "translation into modern Chinese" has been answering for two thousand years — the next lesson puts all five moves on the machine: close reading of whole pieces, from the *Zuo Tradition* to 'On the Love of the Lotus'. The moment to inspect the goods has come.
:::
