---
title: "Function words"
---

The grammar of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) wears no morphological clothes, and depends entirely on function words to light the lamp. **之其而于为乃则者所以** — ten characters govern the greater part of the realm of syntax, logic and mood. This lesson gives one card to each king: the throne (word class), the territory (uses), the criterion (how to recognise one), and the mnemonic (how to translate).

## The pronoun group and the particle group {#sec-dai}

::: definition 之: the busy character of three generations under one roof {#def-zhi}
① **Pronoun**: 主之 and 客之 (a host-之 and a guest-之). “使之闻之” ('cause him to hear it') uses two 之 in succession (the pivot and the object each have a master of their own). ② **Verb**: 'to go' — “吾欲之南海” ('I wish to go to the South Sea.' (Our translation.)); a place name joined on after it means it is a verb, and this criterion is the firmest. ③ **Particle**, in three offices: **the structural particle 的** (“古人之道”, 'the way of the ancients'); **the standard-bearer of object-fronting** (“何陋之有”, 'What meanness is there?' (Our translation.); “句读之不知”, 'not knowing the *judou*, the traditional pause-marks' (Our translation.) — 之 lifts the object up before the verb, and is the dedicated signal-flag for fronting the object); **cancelling the independence of a sentence** (“师道之不传也久矣”, 'That the way of the teacher is not transmitted has long been so.' (Our translation.) — insert one 之 between subject and predicate, and the sentence is demoted to a phrase, fit only to be a subject, an object, or a temporal adverbial clause). Translation mnemonic: **an independence-之 is not translated as 的; an object-fronting 之 is translated by moving the object forward**.
:::

::: definition 其: a pronoun by proper name, mood as a side post {#def-qi}
① **Genitive of the third-person pronoun**: 其子, 其妻 — 'his / hers / its'. By word-class shift it becomes a **shifted reference to oneself**: “余亦悔其随之而不得极夫游之乐也” ('I too regret that I followed them and could not take the pleasure of that excursion to the full.' (Our translation.)) (Wang Anshi 王安石, 《游褒禅山记》, 'A Record of an Excursion to Baochan Mountain'; 其 = oneself) — the reflexive use is something the ancients did only now and then, and examination papers often set it. ② **Demonstrative pronoun**: “其巫，老妪也” ('That shaman is an old woman.' (Our translation.)) (the in-course text 《西门豹》, 'Ximen Bao' — 其 = 'that', pointing at an old woman). ③ **Modal adverb**: conjecture, as in “其皆出于此乎” ('They probably all come from this.' (Our translation.)) ('probably'); imperative, as in “尔其无忘乃父之志” ('You are to be sure not to forget your father's purpose.' (Our translation.)) ('do be sure to') — a modal 其 sits at the head of the sentence or before the predicate, and **is translated 'probably / could it be / do be sure to', entirely by the look on the context's face**. Criterion: what can be filled out with 'his' is a pronoun; what can take 'probably' added is modal; what touches neither, check the demonstrative again.
:::

::: definition 者 and 所: two nominalising machines {#def-zhesuo}
**者**: ① verb or adjective + 者 = the doer of the action, or the holder of the feature (“学者” in its old sense: a person who pursues learning; “来者”, “往者”, “大者”, “远者”, 'one who comes', 'one who goes', 'a great one', 'a distant one'); ② **a modal pause**: it marks a pause and a judgement (“陈胜者，阳城人也”, 'Chen Sheng was a man of Yangcheng.' (Our translation.) — a cushion after the subject of a nominal sentence); ③ 者 + a numeral = listing under a class (“此五者，邦之蠹也”, 'These five are vermin of the state.' (Our translation.)). **所**: 所 + verb = the patient, or a related item (“所爱”, “所见” = the person who is loved / the thing that is seen); **为…所…** = the passive (“为人所欺”, 'be deceived by others'); **所以** = the reason for … / the means used for … (“此先汉所以兴隆也”, 'This is why the Former Han flourished.' (Our translation.)), and it is a false friend of the modern conjunction that marks a result ([[classical-chinese-1/old-and-new-meanings]] is on the register). The criterion when the two strike together: **the character 者 is active, the character 所 is passive** — the reader and the reading matter, the one who studies and what is studied.
:::

## The preposition and conjunction group {#sec-jie}

::: definition 而: one connecting nerve {#def-er}
而 connects only **predicative** constituents (verbs, adjectives, clauses), and has four faces: **coordination** (“敏于事而慎于言”, 'quick in deeds and careful in words' (Our translation.) — it can be swapped for 'and / also'); **succession** (“学而时习之”, 'study and in due time practise it' (Our translation.); “退而甘食其土之有”, 'withdraw, and then gladly eat what their land possesses' (Our translation.) — first 'withdraw', then 'gladly eat', a chain in time); **adversative** (“青，取之于蓝，而青于蓝”, 'Blue is taken from indigo, yet is bluer than indigo.' (Our translation.) — it can be swapped for 'yet'); **modification** (“吾尝跂而望矣”, 'I once stood on tiptoe and gazed.' (Our translation.) — it links an adverbial to the predicate, and is translated with 地, or not translated). The three stock blows of the criterion: **what can be swapped for 'also / and' is coordination; what can be swapped for 'yet' is adversative; where what comes before and what comes after stand in sequence, it is succession; what remains, clinging before the verb, is modification**. 而 does not connect nouns — if you see 而 connecting nouns, this 而 is a phonetic loan for 尔 ('you').
:::

::: definition 以, 于 and 为: three prepositions plus {#def-yiwuwei}
**以**: ① the preposition 'using, taking, by means of, because' (“以子之矛”, 'with your spear'; “以其境过清”, 'because its scene was too bleak' (Our translation.) — instrument, basis and cause, one umbrella and three rains); ② a conjunction, close in use to 而 (“作《师说》以贻之”, 'He wrote "On Teachers" (*Shishuo*) in order to present it to him.' (Our translation.) — purpose, the way of translating that uses 来); criterion: **after 以 a slot for a nominal object must be left** (the object of 以 is often fronted or omitted: “一言以蔽之”, 'to cover it with a single saying' (Our translation.) = 以一言; “何以知”, 'by what does one know?'). **于**: the all-purpose introducer — place (“战于长勺”, 'fought at Changshao'), target (“告之于帝”, 'reported it to the Sovereign'), comparison (“青于蓝”, 'bluer than indigo'), the agent of a passive (“受制于人”, 'be controlled by others'); the name one translates it by changes its trick with the predicate, and **the iron rule of position is: the preposition and its object always stand after the verb** (the modern footprint of the postposed adverbial, [[modern-grammar/special-constructions]]). **为**: ① the preposition 'for, to, by, because' (the passive in “为人所欺”; “不足为外人道”, 'it is not worth telling to outsiders' (Our translation.)); ② the verb *wéi*, 'do, be, become' (“如今人方为刀俎，我为鱼肉”, 'Now others are just the knife and the block, and I am the fish and the meat.' (Our translation.) — two 为 of a nominal sentence); ③ the sentence-final modal particle *wèi* (“何以伐为”, 'What is the attacking for?' (Our translation.) — 'what does one want … for', paired with the frame 何…为). When the three kings are heard in the same case, first separate the pronunciations (*wéi* / *wèi*), then fix the position, then fit the translation.
:::

::: definition 乃 and 则: road-signs between two clauses {#def-naize}
**乃**: ① the adverb 'only then, unexpectedly' (“乃入见”, 'only then went in to the audience' — the times follow hard on each other; “今其智乃反不能及”, 'Now their wisdom unexpectedly cannot, on the contrary, reach it.' (Our translation.) — 'unexpectedly', the mood turning a corner); ② the judgement-word 'is' (“此乃臣效命之秋也”, 'This is the hour in which your servant gives his life in service.' (Our translation.) — a judgement by the character 乃, of the same family as 者也); ③ the pronoun 'your' (the office of the pronoun 'your' is seen now and then in the commentaries and subcommentaries; when revising for the examination, do not linger in the fight). **则**: ① succession, 'then, and so' (“学而不思则罔”, 'Study but do not think, and one is bewildered.' (Our translation.) — the signpost of a conditioned reflex); ② adversative, 'yet, rather' (“于其身也，则耻师焉”, 'But in his own case, he is ashamed to take a teacher.' (Our translation.)); ③ coordinate items, 'then … (and at the same time) …' (“居庙堂之高则忧其民，处江湖之远则忧其君”, 'Seated high in the court, he worries for his people; dwelling far off among the rivers and lakes, he worries for his prince.' (Our translation.) — two scenes set against each other); ④ judgement, 'is, is precisely' (“此则岳阳楼之大观也”, 'This, then, is the grand sight of Yueyang Tower.' (Our translation.)). **For 乃, look at the drop in time or in mood; for 则, look at whether the logic runs straight or turns a corner.**
:::

::: example All ten characters come to court {#ex-ting}
“臣闻吏议逐客，窃以为过矣……此四君者，皆以客之功。” ('I, your servant, have heard that the officials discuss expelling the guest officers, and I privately consider this a fault … These four lords all relied on the achievements of the guest officers.' (Our translation.)) (《谏逐客书》, *Jian zhuke shu*, 'Memorial against Expelling the Guest Officers') — state what office each of 以, 为, 者 and 之 holds.
::: solution
“窃以为” = the fixed pattern of “私意谓” ('privately consider that') (“以为” used as a succession, 'to consider' — **a fossil of two words welded into one**); the 者 of “此四君者” = a modal pause (a cushion under the subject of a nominal sentence); the 以 of “皆以客之功” = 'relying on, depending on' (along the path of verbal meaning, or glossed as 因, 'because'); the 之 of “客之功” = the structural particle 的. The court precept: **the same character in different offices depends on position; one word with many translations depends on the sense of the matter**. This sentence of 《谏逐客书》 is precisely testimony to “逐客之非” ('the wrong of expelling the guest officers'); only when the function words are judged aright can the political view be read so as to leave the mouth.
:::

::: history Function words: from a feel for the language to a science {#sec-xuci}
The ancients, reading Classical Chinese, did not teach function words first. In the Tang and the Song, elementary learning of “语例” ('examples of usage') relied on recitation, until **Chen Kui 陈骙 of the Southern Song, in *Wenze* 《文则》 ('Canons of Prose')**, systematically compared the breath of phrasing, and the study of chapters and sentences first took on the build of literary theory. **Lu Yiwei 卢以纬 of the Yuan, *Zhuyu ci* 《助语辞》 ('Glosses on the Particles')** — the first specialised Chinese book on function words, published in the early 14th century expressly to explain to elementary pupils the tone of “之乎者也”. **Liu Qi 刘淇 of the Qing, *Zhuzi bianlüe* 《助字辨略》 ('Brief Notes Distinguishing the Particles')** (1711), and **Wang Yinzhi 王引之, *Jingzhuan shici* 《经传释词》 ('Explaining Words in the Classics and Their Commentaries')** (1798), by the strength of scholars of the Classics, gathered in the whole dossier of pre-Qin function words — evidential research of the sort that gives the character 之 dozens of senses, and says that the character 其 should be read as 几, the house craft of the Qianjia school. ***Mashi wentong* 《马氏文通》** (1898) at last issued function words with a Western household registration as 'preposition / conjunction / particle' (the Latin screwdriver applied to the three grades of “虚字” is disputed, but the terms have been current ever since). The irony of studying function words today: **in those days it was a mother-tongue feel for the language; now it is a foreign-language skill** — the ten cards are a prosthesis fitted to a lost feel for the language; once it is fitted, one must walk a great deal (read a great deal) before it seems the real thing.
:::

::: warning Four misdiagnoses of function words
First, **taking one word as one sense**. Once one has memorised '之 = 的', one translates “何陋之有” as “什么的” — every time you meet a function word, first ask the sentence pattern (nominal sentence? passive? object-fronting?); the pattern fixed, the function word is fixed. Second, **position looked at and not seen**. Translating “青于蓝” as “青在蓝” drops the comparison — a preposition and its object after the verb is an iron rule of Classical Chinese; in translating into modern Chinese one must move them back to before the verb (if they cannot be moved, it is a special sentence pattern; see the next lesson). Third, **false friends between ancient and modern**. “所以” marks a reason or a means; “无论” is 'let alone' (“更别说”); “虽然” = 虽 + 'this being so' — when syllables combine into a disyllabic word, split them first. Fourth, **over-precision**. Coordination and succession in the conjunction 而 are often both open in verse and prose (“敏而好学”, 'quick and fond of learning' — is it coordination or succession? the old commentaries long ago opened the quarrel); the ancients used function words by a feel for the music, and exegesis that forcibly divides the five sense-organs on the contrary loses the blend — **to judge 'either may be followed' is more like scholarship than to force a choice of one side**.
:::

::: quiz
The 之 of “师道之不传也久矣” is ( ).
- [ ] the structural particle 的
- [x] cancelling the independence of the sentence
- [ ] the marker of a fronted object

::: solution
A 之 is inserted between subject and predicate, and the sentence is demoted to a temporal clause — do not translate it as 的.

::: quiz
The 而 of “吾尝跂而望矣” marks ( ).
- [ ] adversative
- [ ] coordination
- [x] modification (linking the adverbial and the predicate)
- [ ] cause and effect
::: solution
“跂” is the posture of “望” — adverbial + 而 + verb; translate with 地, or do not translate.
:::

::: quiz
The structure of “为人所欺” is ( ).
- [x] the passive 为…所…
- [ ] a nominal sentence
- [ ] a fronted object
- [ ] a pivotal construction
::: solution
为 introduces the agent, and the character 所 cushions the object — one of the three-piece set of the Classical Chinese passive.

::: exercise Ten cards for judging the words {level=1}
Assign an office to the marked function word in each sentence: ① 何陋**之**有 ('What meanness is there?' (Our translation.)) ② 其皆出于此乎 ('Do they perhaps all come from this?' (Our translation.)) ③ 退**而**甘食 ('withdraw, and then gladly eat') ④ 作《师说》**以**贻之 ('write "On Teachers" in order to present it to him' (Our translation.)) ⑤ 青，取之于蓝，**而**青于蓝 ('Blue is taken from indigo, yet is bluer than indigo.' (Our translation.)) ⑥ 先臣**乃**忘之? ('Have the former ministers, then, forgotten it?') Replace: ⑥ 度我至军中，公**乃**入 ('Reckon on my reaching the midst of the army, and you, sir, only then go in.' (Our translation.)) ⑦ 此则岳阳楼**之**大观也 ('This, then, is the grand sight of Yueyang Tower.' (Our translation.)) ⑧ 学**而**时习之 ('Study, and in due time practise it.' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
① the standard-bearer of object-fronting (restored as “有何陋”, 'what meanness is there'); ② a modal adverb of conjecture ('probably'); ③ succession (first withdraw, then eat); ④ a conjunction of purpose (来, 'in order to'); ⑤ adversative ('yet'); ⑥ the temporal adverb 'only then' ('by the time that'); ⑦ the structural particle 的 (a marker of the attributive, not cancellation — the skeleton of the nominal sentence in the character “此” is there); ⑧ succession or coordination, either may be followed — judge it open both ways and state the grounds, and that is full marks. All eight cards right, and one has finished the apprenticeship.
:::

::: exercise A consultation over the case notes {level=2}
At the translation clinic: “其巫，老妪，不去，为河伯娶妇，巫之患也” ('That shaman, an old woman, does not leave; she takes a wife for the River Earl; it is the shaman's affliction.' (Our translation.)) (a faulty translation made up here: “那巫婆不离开，替河伯娶老婆，成了巫婆的病患”, 'That witch does not leave, takes a wife for the River Earl, and it has become the witch's illness.' (Our translation.)). Point out three lesions in the function words or the sentence pattern, and translate afresh.
::: solution
Lesions: ① “不去” here = 不离开 ('not leave') (走) — the ancient and modern senses of 去 run in opposite directions, yet the translation, following “不离开”, ties the tongue (if 去 in this sentence is taken as “不离开”, ancient sense and modern sense are in a fight; it should be changed to “不肯离开”, 'unwilling to leave', or one should check whether the original sentence is “不去” or “不敢去” — **a translation that contradicts itself is the alarm**); ② 为, *wèi*, the preposition 'for', ✓, but a possible passive has been left untranslated (if the original sense is 'encumbered by the proposal that the River Earl take a wife', one should use 为……所); ③ the 之 of “巫之患” is not 的 carried smoothly on, and it should be translated as the judgement 'the witch's anxiety about this matter / it is the witch's calamity'. A model of the new translation: 那巫婆是本地老妪，年年不去，全赖河伯娶妇一事敛财——这正是巫婆生财也生畏的关窍。 ('That witch is a local old woman; year after year she does not go; she relies entirely on the business of the River Earl's taking a wife to gather wealth — this is precisely the crux at which the witch both produces wealth and produces fear.' (Our translation.)) Marking points: one can show for oneself that the illness lies where the function word and the word's meaning join forces, and the new translation forms a sentence.
:::

::: exercise A practical test on the bare text {level=2}
A practical check against the recitation: using this lesson's cards, within the opening 60 characters of 《谏逐客书》, point out one place each of 之 / 以 / 于 / 者 / 乃 and mark the name of the office (looking up the original is allowed — what an open-book examination tests is the judging of offices, not the merit of having memorised).
::: solution
The standard check: the opening of the original, “臣闻吏议逐客，窃以为过矣” ('I, your servant, have heard that the officials discuss expelling the guest officers, and I privately consider this a fault.' (Our translation.)): 以为 = a fixed pattern; “昔穆公求士，西取由余于戎，东得百里奚于宛” ('Of old Duke Mu sought officers: to the west he took You Yu from the Rong, to the east he obtained Baili Xi from Wan.' (Our translation.)): 于 for place and for target, two occurrences; “并国二十，遂霸西戎。孝公用商鞅之法” ('He annexed twenty states, and so became hegemon of the Western Rong. Duke Xiao employed the laws of Shang Yang.' (Our translation.)): 之 as 的; “此四君者，皆以客之功” ('These four lords all relied on the achievements of the guest officers.' (Our translation.)): 以 as 'relying on', 者 as a pause. Marking: each of the five places has coordinates in the original, and the name of the function word's office is accurate — looking up the original does not lose marks; this is the true sense of an examination taken with the scroll in hand.

::: exercise Short essay: function words and the character of a language {level=3}
Write from eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. The function words of Classical Chinese are the master switch of 'grammar joined by sense' (意合) (no morphology; function words plus word order hold up the whole of the syntax). On this basis discuss: what features belong to the thinking cultivated by a language of 'little morphology and many function words'? (You may call on the vague linking of 而 as against the explicitness of English which/that; the conclusion must be open to rebuttal — give one argument in support of a language with morphology.)
::: solution
Marking points: there is an in-lesson definition, and an example sentence, for 意合 / 形合, 'joining by sense / joining by form' (the 'joining by sense and joining by form' of [[modern-grammar/complex-sentences]] is taken up again here); the comparative argument is concrete (the function word 而, one link and four senses = a blank left open; a pronoun in a relative clause must name its antecedent = locking it down); the conclusion admits a double-edged sword (the blank left open produces a blended whole and a poetic quality, and also produces traps on the examination paper and disputes of exegesis); only if one manages to give an argument for the other side are the marks full — one-sided lyricism is sent back.
:::

::: summary
The ten cards each go back to their place: 之 (three offices, pronoun / verb / particle; object-fronting and the character that cancels independence are the examination points), 其 (the genitive as the main use, and a conjectural mood at the head of the sentence), 者 and 所 (a nominalising lathe, one active and one passive), 而 (four faces, three stock blows), 以, 于 and 为 (prepositions plus: instrument—cause, the all-purpose introducer, the split of pronunciations *wéi* / *wèi*), 乃 and 则 (a drop in time, and a turn in the logic). The history of function words = a history of compensation, as a mother-tongue feel for the language degenerates into a foreign-language skill; the cards are a prosthesis, and only much reading is the rehabilitation. The next lesson is word order: the nominal sentence, the passive, the fronted object, the postposed attributive — four X-ray plates of the syntax of Classical Chinese.
:::
