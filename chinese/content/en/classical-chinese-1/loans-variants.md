“学而时习之，不亦**说**乎？” 'To learn and, at the due time, to practise it — is that not a **delight**?' (Our translation.) — in your mind you know that the character is 悦 ('delight'), but in your hand you suspect a misprint: **it is not a misprint**. The writing system of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) was short of hands, and the standard characters were not yet fixed, so three regimes of "borrowed household registration" grew up: the phonetic loan (*tongjia*), ancient and modern forms of a word, and the variant form. Fail to tell them apart, and you cannot read the "wrong characters" of two thousand years ago.

## Phonetic loans: a character borrowed by clutching the Buddha's feet {#sec-tongjia}

::: definition The proper character and the borrowed character {#def-benjie}
**Phonetic loan** (*tongjia*): in writing, one temporarily borrows a character **of the same or a similar sound** to stand in for the proper character — “不可不**蚤**自来谢项王” (蚤 = 早) 'You must not fail to come **early** in person and apologise to King Xiang.' (Our translation.); “愿伯具言臣之不敢**倍**德也” (倍 = 背) 'I wish you, sir, to tell him in full that I would not dare to **turn my back** on his kindness.' (Our translation.); “阳货**归**孔子豚” (归 = 馈, 'to present') 'Yang Huo **presented** Confucius with a pig.' (Our translation.); “知者乐水，仁者乐山” (知 = 智, read in the departing tone) 'The wise delight in water; the humane delight in mountains.' (Our translation.). Three points. ① **A phonetic loan is a synchronic borrowing** — 蚤 and 早 existed at the same time. The writer was not making a character; it was a habit of use in which the "wrong character had been made legitimate" (in high antiquity characters were few, authority over the written form was unsettled, and what passed from master to pupil was spoken, weighting the sound and not the shape). ② **The criterion = sound + examples**: the sound must share an Old Chinese rhyme group (蚤 and 早 share the 精 initial and the 幽 rhyme group; 倍 and 背 share the 並 initial and the 之 rhyme group — put them to the test against a table of Old Chinese sounds; see [[phonology/old-chinese]]), and the examples must be endorsed by old glosses (Zheng Xuan 郑玄, commenting on the *Rites of Zhou*, had already opened the granary: “故书假借字多矣” 'The old text is full of borrowed characters.' (Our translation.) — the Han already granted that the old files are covered, page after page, in borrowed characters). ③ **The reading = acknowledging the thief as one's father**: the borrowed character is read with the meaning of the proper character, and the pronunciation too is given a diverted reading (破读) so as to follow the proper character (说 *yuè*, 知 *zhì*, 女 *rǔ*, 亡 *wú*).
:::

## Ancient and modern forms, and variant forms: two kinds of "one household, two addresses" {#sec-gujin}

::: definition Ancient and modern forms: one word, several changes of identity card {#def-gujin}
**Ancient and modern forms**: the same word, written with a different graph in a different age — the old character held too many posts, and a component was added to "divide the household": **说→悦** (the 说 of “学而时习之” also covered “喜悦” 'delight'; later the heart component was added, and that sense had a post to itself), **知→智**, **然→燃** (the original meaning of 然 is 'to burn' — there is fire underneath; after it was borrowed to write “然而” 'and yet', its old trade was on the contrary taken over by the new character, the most blackly comic change of places among ancient and modern forms), **益→溢** (the oracle-bone form of 益 is water brimming out of a vessel, and the original meaning is 'to overflow'; after it had been borrowed away to serve as “利益” 'benefit', the water component came back to fight the fire), **县→悬** (the ancient graph of 县 is a head hung upside down on a tree, and the original meaning is 'to hang'; after the sense 'prefecture and county' had rush-registered the character, the character 悬 was made), **取→娶、田→畋、要→腰、食→饲**. The test on which they part from phonetic loans: **ancient and modern forms have a hereditary tie in the business of making characters** (the modern character is made to the measure of one sense of the old character, and the graphs are kin — usually by adding a component), whereas a phonetic loan is only **a temporary tenancy of the same sound** (蚤 and 早 are not kin in their graphs; it is a bargain in sound alone).
:::

::: definition Variant forms: conjoined twins of the same name {#def-yiti}
**Variant forms**: same sound, same meaning, **interchangeable on any occasion**, and different only in graph — 峰/峯, 群/羣 (the parts shifted between top and bottom), 村/邨, 咏/詠, 遍/徧, 线/綫. Variant forms do not change the language; they only change the copyist's hours of work. Historically the great households of variant forms were brought in by **character dictionaries and edicts on the standard character** (the *Ganlu zishu* 干禄字书, 'Character book for seeking office', distinguishes ancient, modern and popular forms; the *Kangxi Dictionary* 康熙字典 of the Qianlong reign fixed the standard forms; in 1955 the *Diyipi yitizi zhengli biao* 第一批异体字整理表, 'First list for the arrangement of variant characters', eliminated 1,053 variant entries at one stroke). Their value for a reader lies in two places: when you look a character up, **knowing who is whose twin** (so that you do not make two trips for “巖/岩”), and, in a dictionary of calligraphy, enjoying the seventy-two transformations of one and the same character ([[chinese-characters/simplification]] many of the knife-strokes of simplified characters — “虫” swallowing “蟲”, “网” taking in “網” — are at bottom the official continuation of the logic of variant forms).
:::

::: example A joint hearing of the three passes {#ex-hui}
Judge the household registration of each of three sentences (phonetic loan / ancient and modern forms / variant form / polysemy of the proper character): ① “属予作文以记之” (《岳阳楼记》, 'Record of Yueyang Tower') 'He charged me to compose a piece to record it.' (Our translation.) ② “属引凄异” (《三峡》, 'The Three Gorges') 'The note ran on, mournful and strange.' (Our translation.) ③ “愿伯具言臣之不敢倍德也” (《鸿门宴》, 'The Feast at Hongmen') 'I wish you, sir, to tell him in full that I would not dare to turn my back on his kindness.' (Our translation.).
::: solution
① 属 = 嘱: 嘱 is a differentiated character of later origin, and on a strict judgement this is **ancient and modern forms**; a quick judgement in class often files it under "phonetic loan" — and the force of this case as a precedent lies exactly here: **a phonetic loan and a pair of ancient and modern forms are different historical cross-sections of one and the same phenomenon** (what is borrowed, and no new character made, is a phonetic loan; what has been borrowed long enough to be regularised, with a new character dividing the household, becomes ancient and modern forms). Judging it a "phonetic loan" earns a pass; judging it "ancient and modern forms" earns full marks; judging that it is "on the way from a phonetic loan to ancient and modern forms" earns extra marks on the short essay. ② 属 = 'continuous': **polysemy** of the proper character (属 *zhǔ* already carries the sense 'to connect', so no loan is needed — do not treat every difficult character as a phonetic loan; that is a presumption of guilt applied to the script). ③ 倍 = 背: a **phonetic loan** (a tenancy of the same sound, a tenant of a thousand years, and no new character has been seen to divide the household). Once each of the three cases has been judged on evidence, it may leave the court.
:::
:::

::: history Seeking the meaning from the sound: two thousand years of decoding {#sec-jiedu}
Confucian scholars of the Han, explaining the Classics, already understood “就其声以求其义” 'Seek the meaning by way of its sound.' (Our translation.) — Zheng Xuan 郑玄, annotating the Three Rites, was forever writing “某读为某” ('X is to be read as Y') and “故书某为某” ('the old text writes X as Y'), and he processed *jiajie* (phonetic loan, the rebus) as ordinary case files. In the Qing, **Duan Yucai** 段玉裁 said the thing outright (the *Shuowen jiezi zhu* 说文解字注, *Annotation of Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*, on *jiajie*: a borrowing in the use of characters, the whole principle of which lies in the sound). **Wang Niansun** 王念孙 **and his son Wang Yinzhi** 王引之, in *Jingyi shuwen* 经义述闻 ('Record of what has been heard on the meanings of the Classics'), used sound to get through to the exegesis, and overthrew countless old Han accounts of "reading the sense off the face of the graph" — in the *Book of Songs*, “终风且暴” 'Wind all through, and violent.' (Our translation.): Wang Niansun read 终 as 既, and “既风且暴” 'both wind and violence' (Our translation.) settled the matter at one hammer-blow. It is the signature case of *Jingyi shuwen*. Scholars of the twentieth century opened a modern general ledger for *jiajie*: **"*jiajie* for which no proper character ever existed" (the original sense within the six categories (*liushu*): recording a word by a borrowed sound, as with 而 and 汝, for which nothing could be drawn) and "*tongjia* for which a proper character already existed" (a borrowing in the use of characters, as of 蚤 for 早) were formally entered in separate accounts**, and from then on textbooks have not stewed every same-sound substitution of characters in one pot. Decoding asks after the initial and asks after the rhyme, and it checks the books and checks the commentaries and subcommentaries — the chapter in the history of Chinese characters that most resembles criminal investigation.
:::

::: warning Four defences against borrowed characters
One. **Whatever is difficult must be a loan**: the moment a character will not read smoothly, to cry "phonetic loan" is a master key opening every lock — first exhaust the extended meanings of the proper character (the 属 of “属引凄异” 'The note ran on, mournful and strange.' (Our translation.) already carries, of itself, the sense “连续” 'continuous', and there is no need to borrow); three proofs for judging a phonetic loan: closeness of sound (the Old Chinese standard), sameness of example (parallel examples from the same period), and old glossing (an earlier master in the commentaries and subcommentaries has already said “某读为某” 'X is to be read as Y'); two. **Judging an ancient case by the modern pronunciation**: that 蚤/早 are the same in modern pronunciation is not the hard part; the hard part is “女/汝”, whose modern pronunciations have already split but which were the same in Old Chinese, and 归/馈, whose modern initials already differ but which in antiquity were neighbours among the velars (牙音) — the measure of the sound must take a table of Old Chinese as its ruler (of unjust cases over borrowed characters, half are ruined by judging an ancient case on the modern pronunciation); four. **Ignoring the descent of traditional and simplified forms**: a simplified graph may cover several sources (“干” covers 乾/幹, and “后” covers 後); in reading a borrowed character, first return to the traditional-character household register, and only then open the court. Three. **Mixing phonetic loans with ancient and modern forms in one judgement**: the criterion looks at one point only — whether there is the household-dividing fact of "making a new character for the borrowed sense" (嘱/燃/悦 have it; 蚤/倍 do not); four. **Ignoring traditional and simplified forms**: in reading the traditional characters of old books, the character “说” holds several posts at once (*shuō*/*yuè*, two readings and two meanings) — when traditional and simplified are read against each other, first restore the head character, and only then look the loan up ([[chinese-characters/simplification]] the clause on one character with several sources).
:::

::: exercise Registering the households {level=1}
Classify the eight sentences (phonetic loan / ancient and modern forms / variant form / polysemy of the proper character): ① “学而时习之，不亦说乎” 'To learn and, at the due time, to practise it — is that not a delight?' (Our translation.) ② “寡助之至，亲戚畔之” 'When want of help reaches its extreme, his own kin rebel.' (Our translation.) ③ “日知其所亡” 'Each day know that which one lacks.' (Our translation.) ④ “师者，所以传道受业解惑也” 'A teacher is one by whom the Way is transmitted, a calling passed on and doubts resolved.' (Our translation.) ⑤ “玉不琢，不成器” 'If jade is not cut and polished, it does not become a vessel.' (Our translation.) ⑥ “将军身被坚执锐” 'The general himself put on stout armour and took up sharp weapons.' (Our translation.) ⑦ “属予作文以记之” 'He charged me to compose a piece to record it.' (Our translation.) ⑧ “蟹六跪而二螯” 'A crab has six legs and two claws.' (Our translation.).
::: solution
① 说/悦: ancient and modern forms (悦 is a differentiated character; an answer of "phonetic loan" receives partial marks, on the same grounds as ⑦). ② 畔/叛: a phonetic loan (no new character was made). ③ 亡/无: a phonetic loan (the original meaning of 亡, “逃跑” 'to flee', is a separate case — “日知其所亡” borrows it to stand for “无” 'to lack'). ④ 受/授: ancient and modern forms (受 originally combined the two senses “授予” 'to bestow' and “接受” 'to receive'; 授 is a differentiated character of later origin). ⑤ “玉不琢” has no loan (a distractor: do not catch the complaint "whatever is difficult must be a loan"). ⑥ 被/披: ancient and modern forms (披 is of later origin; the original meaning of 被 is 寝衣 'a garment for sleeping' — the precedent of lesson 2 is called back in). ⑦ 属/嘱: ancient and modern forms (嘱 is of later origin). ⑧ 跪: the proper character — the “跪” of a crab is an extension of meaning to the leg (the same character holding a second post), not a borrowed character; it is enough to judge it "polysemy / the proper character". Six of the eight cases judged rightly is full marks; two tenths of the ground is left to scholarly dispute (the dispute over the cross-section between phonetic loans and ancient and modern forms).
:::

::: exercise Cross-examination on the phonology {level=2}
Using ordinary knowledge of Old Chinese sounds, explain: why may 女 stand in a loan for 汝, while 妈 may not stand for 马? Then look up the relation of initial and rhyme group for one pair, 归/馈.
::: solution
女/汝: in Old Chinese they share the **鱼 rhyme group, with the 日 and 泥 initials neighbouring** (and 女 already has a reading *rǔ* — the diverted reading is a fossil); the sounds are close enough to borrow, and the fossil of the diverted reading *rǔ* is there to testify. 妈/马: the sounds are close, but the character 妈 appeared late, and the writings of one age after another never borrow “妈” for “马” (a borrowed character must have examples); to open a case on closeness of sound alone is a machine for manufacturing miscarriages of justice. “归/馈”: both have velar initials (见 and 群 are neighbouring initials), and the rhyme groups 微 and 物 stand in *duizhuan* — the closeness of sound is well grounded; 馈 is also a differentiated character of later origin, so the one case sits on the border of the two systems. Marking points: state clearly that "closeness of sound is only the ticket of admission; examples of use plus old glosses are the written judgement"; it is enough that the direction of the phonological reasoning does not overturn.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: how a wrong character becomes legitimate {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. The essence of a phonetic loan is "the wrong character of its own day, the orthography of later ages". Set 蚤/早 against today's tug of war between 登陆/登录 and between 账户/帐号, and discuss: once enough people have used a character wrongly, it is regularised — where is the boundary between a norm for the script and a democracy of the language? Requirements: apply the three proofs of a phonetic loan to modern cases, one proof to each; you may cite old accounts, from within the course, of rulings on Putonghua pronunciation ([[sounds-of-mandarin/putonghua-test]]); give three articles of your own "procedure for regularisation".
::: solution
Marking points: the three proofs transfer reasonably (a modern case of a borrowed character likewise needs closeness of sound, examples of use, and an authority's endorsement — entry in a dictionary = the new edition of an "old gloss"); the anatomy of the “帐号” case must keep apart **economy of the stock of characters** (a borrowed character saves strokes) and **entropy of the system** (several meanings on one character increase the ambiguity); the procedure for regularisation must be one that can be carried out (for example: a threshold of rate of use + breadth of distribution + an audit of the cost of ambiguity, three checkpoints); cite “郑玄承认老文件满纸借字” 'Zheng Xuan granted that the old files are covered in borrowed characters.' (Our translation.) as a historical footnote.

::: summary
Get the three household offices by heart before you set out. A phonetic loan = a tenancy of the same sound (蚤 has moved into the household of 早; no kinship; the case is judged on Old Chinese sound + old glosses). Ancient and modern forms = a household divided by sense (说 is portioned out to 悦, 然 yields its place to 燃, 益 invites 溢 back — there is kinship, and the modern character in most cases adds a component). A variant form = a double-yolked egg (峰 and 峯 are one household and one fate; a single sheet of an edict on standard characters merges the households). In reading a borrowed character, recognise the proper character, and break the sound so that it follows the sense. Before judging a phonetic loan, first pass the check for "polysemy of the proper character"; do not run a presumption of guilt upon the script. The next lesson drills a consummate skill: *judou* (traditional pause marks) on the bare text (*baiwen*) — two thousand years without punctuation; what did the ancients rely on to divide a book in the right places?
:::
