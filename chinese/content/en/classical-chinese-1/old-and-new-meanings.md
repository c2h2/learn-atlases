---
title: "Content words and changes of meaning"
---

Content words are households that move house. For some, the doorplate has not moved, but the people inside have changed; for some, people and door together have moved across the street. **The greatest danger in reading Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) is not the new word, but the "familiar" word** — you think you know 去; the person it knows is the you of two thousand years ago.

## Four great routes of migration {#sec-sidaolu}

::: definition Widening, narrowing, transfer, rise and fall {#def-kuoda}
**Widening**: 江 and 河 were originally proper names (the Yangtze, the Yellow River); after the Tang they became common nouns, and the 江 of 江南 came to have ten thousand of them. 脸 originally referred to the two cheeks, and now takes in the whole face. 菜 originally referred to the vegetable kind, and now meat and vegetables are gathered into one pot. **Narrowing**: 臭 (xiù→chòu) originally meant any smell (其臭如兰, 'its smell is like that of the orchid' (Our translation.)); now it is reserved for a foul smell. 禽 was originally the general name for what the hunt takes (飞禽走兽皆禽: birds in flight and beasts on the run are all 禽); now it is reserved for birds. 宫 originally referred broadly to houses and rooms, and after the Qin and Han was reserved for the imperial house — imperial power's semantic enclosure. **Transfer**: moving house, and changing the focus. 兵 originally meant weapons (弃甲曳兵而走, 'they cast aside their armour and trailed their weapons as they ran' (Our translation.)); later it means soldiers, and warfare. 獄 originally meant a lawsuit (小大之狱，虽不能察，必以情, 'In cases great and small, though I cannot examine them all, I must decide by the facts' (Our translation.)); later it means the place where people are confined. 書 originally meant writing and the written marks (the *Shuowen jiezi*, *Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*: 书，著也, 'to write means to set down' (Our translation.)); later it shifted to the pages of canonical books. 去 originally meant **to leave** (去国怀乡, 'to leave the capital and yearn for home' = 离开国都, 'to leave the capital'); afterwards the direction turned completely round into 'to go towards' — the chief assassin among ancient and modern equivalents that point the opposite way. **Rise and fall**: 爪牙 was originally commendatory, referring to the fierce generals who guarded the state on every side (praise on the pattern of Han memorials, 将军者，国之爪牙, 'A general is the claws and teeth of the state' (Our translation.); 股肱之臣, 'ministers who are thigh and forearm', still enjoy the benefit today). 乖 originally meant being perverse and going against, and after the Tang and Song turned to 乖巧 ('clever and pleasing'), a pejorative washed clean — a word on the way down, rising. Word meaning has, in itself, no praise and no blame; on the ledger there are rises and falls.
:::

::: definition A list of false friends: the trap in disyllabic words {#def-jieipengyou}
A disyllabic combination in Classical Chinese is often **two monosyllabic words standing shoulder to shoulder for the occasion**, same in form as a modern disyllabic word and not of one mind. The most dangerous list: **卑鄙** (Zhuge Liang 诸葛亮, 先帝不以臣卑鄙, 'The late emperor did not think me low in station or shallow in knowledge' (Our translation.) = of low status and short in experience, said in self-deprecation; now = base in character); **感激** (in the same piece, 由是感激 = the heart was moved and roused to act; now = to give thanks); **牺牲** (the *Zuo Tradition*, *Zuozhuan*: 牺牲玉帛，弗敢加也, 'The sacrificial beasts, the jade and the silk — I have not dared to add to them' (Our translation.) = sacrificial offerings of animals pure in colour and whole in body, a noun; now it carries of itself the nobility of 'giving up one's life'); **地方** (地方千里 = the land's extent in the square, two words: 地 + 方; now = administered area); **婚姻** (the *Zuo Tradition*: 婚姻之好 = the households joined by a marriage alliance; now = the institution of marriage). The mnemonic for spotting them: **with a disyllable, first split it in two, and check each half's household register** — if it still holds together after the split, it is a true disyllabic word; if it falls to bits, it is a false friend (the method continues at [[modern-vocabulary/morphemes-words]]).
:::

::: example A file card for one word: “汤” {#ex-tang}
Build a four-column file for 汤 (the route from old meaning to new, textual evidence, surviving fossils, the risk of a modern misreading).
::: solution
The file. (1) Old meaning = **boiling water / hot water** (及其日中如探汤, 'and when it comes to noon, it is like putting a hand into boiling water' (Our translation.); 赴汤蹈火, 'to go into boiling water and tread on fire' — this 汤 is of the same family as the 汤 in 热汤). (2) The route of transfer: hot water → the liquid drawn off by boiling (茶汤, 羹汤) → today's 菜汤 — the same kind of thing, the focus moving from 'scalding' to 'what is in it'. (3) Surviving fossils: the four-character idioms (*chengyu*) 赴汤蹈火 and 固若金汤 (金城汤池 = a moat of boiling water, the fierce version of a fortification), and hot-spring place names in 汤 (汤山, 汤池) — inside idioms and place names the old meaning plays dead, and may sit up like a corpse at any moment. (4) The risk of misreading: see 汤 and translate it as 菜汤, and 及其日中如探汤 becomes, at noon, putting a hand into vegetable soup — a scene of punishment turns into a canteen. When all four columns are filled, that is the model.
:::
:::

::: definition Grades in the manner of dying: the euphemism system {#def-weishi}
死 ('to die') is a rich mine of Chinese euphemism. The ritual books give a **graded word-list**: for the Son of Heaven, 崩; for a feudal lord, 薨; for a grand officer, 卒; for a *shi*, 不禄; for a commoner, 死 (*Liji* 礼记, *Book of Rites*, 'Quli' 曲礼) — a euphemism for death is status's last broadcast. There are also battlefield euphemisms (填沟壑, 'to fill the ditches', in self-deprecation; 山陵崩坏, 'the hills of the imperial tombs collapse', in taboo avoidance), sickbed euphemisms (不在, 'not here'; 大行, 'the great departure'; 宾天, 'a guest in Heaven'), and, in modern and contemporary times, successors who have reported for duty (走了, 'has gone'; 去世, 'has left the world'; 长眠, 'the long sleep'; 圆寂, 'perfect stillness'; 见马克思, 'gone to see Marx'). One rule runs through them all: **a euphemism is contaminated by the thing it covers, and must constantly put something new on the shelf** ([[rhetoric/registers]] is the master ledger of the lexical history of the euphemism arms race) — 去世 was originally of the highest solemnity, and once used to excess it is neutral; when hospitals change their wording to 走了, that is a new round entered on the ledger.
:::

::: history Old words in new clothes: the last great wave of migration {#sec-yiceng}
In the late Qing and the Republican period, in order to translate concepts from foreign lands, a batch of old words was called back and dressed in new clothes. **经济** (old = ordering the age and relieving the people, great talent put to a great use; new = economy). **社会** (old = the customary rural gathering of the soil-altar day and the village head; 社会 was originally an offshoot of the sacrifice at the soil altar; new = society). **革命** (old = to change its Mandate of Heaven; the *Yijing* 易经, *Book of Changes*, has 汤武革命, 'Tang and Wu changed the Mandate' (Our translation.), the edge of the blade pointed at Heaven; new = revolution, aimed at the institutions). **文化** (old = transforming people by civil virtue, with the temperament of a verb; new = culture, kept in the noun cabinet). This batch of 'graphic-translation words that came back' (回归形译词) was, for the most part, sold back along the channel of Japanese loanwords ([[modern-vocabulary/loanwords]] keeps the account). Their mark is **a written form with no seam, and a meaning whose heart has been changed** — they know better how to deceive than a new word does. Read 社会 in the *Hou Hanshu* 后汉书 (*Book of the Later Han*), read 经济 in the *Songshi* 宋史 (*History of the Song*), and the window that pops up of itself in the mind is precisely the wrong reading. In the institutional making of the discipline of modern Chinese, presided over by Wang Li 王力, the study of word meaning was always delivered with a hand on the shoulder of 'history' — this lesson takes the gist of one sentence as its teaching: **the history of the lexicon is the history that least heeds 'thus it has been for all ages'.**
:::

::: warning Three high-frequency traps
First, **judging the past by the present**: 父母妻子 in the old language = four people, father, mother, wife and son (妻子 is a same-form disyllabic false friend); read it in the modern sense, and the household register is short by half. Read 牺牲 in the *Zuo Tradition* in the modern sense, and a sacrifice turns into a memorial for martyrs. Second, **covering the present with the past**: holding that 江, ancient and modern, differs only in 'size' — a proper name and a common noun differ in the **nature of the meaning**, not in scale. Third, **ignoring the slice of time**: the sense 'to go towards' of 去 already shows its first shoots in the Tang and the Five Dynasties, and prevails widely after the Song — dating a meaning to a period is an interval, not a switch. To judge 'ancient or modern', first ask **the date when the text was written** ([[classical-chinese-1/what-is-wenyan]]: the stratigraphic account is honoured here).
:::

::: exercise A census of household registers {level=1}
For each of the six words, judge one route and give one piece of textual evidence or one fossil: 江, 臭, 獄, 乖, 币, 汤.
::: solution
江 = widening (proper name → common noun; 江南 may refer to the south of many waters). 臭 = narrowing (a general name for smell → a bad smell, surviving in the xiù reading of 乳臭未干, 'the smell of milk is not yet dry'). 獄 = transfer (a case → a place; 小大之狱 is a case). 乖 = elevation (going against → clever and pleasing; the direction is turned over). 币 = transfer (the proper name of a gift of silk → the general name for money — the object of social exchange has changed its occupant). 汤 = transfer (boiling water → a liquid; 赴汤蹈火 is a fossil). The route and the evidence count for half each; that is full marks.
:::

::: exercise Arresting false friends {level=2}
In each of the following sentences, point out the most dangerous disyllabic combination and explain the trap: 妻子, 地方, 感激, 学者.
::: solution
妻子 = wife and son (a set of two people; Du Fu 杜甫, 却看妻子愁何在, 'I turn to look at my wife and children: where is their sorrow?' (Our translation.), can still be read that way today, but in documents of the pre-Qin and the Two Han, 妻子 often really brings sons and daughters with it — the trap is in how 子 is counted). 地方 = land + extent in the square (地方千里 = an area of a thousand *li*, not a region). 感激 = moved and roused to act (由是感激 contains no character 谢, 'thanks'). 学者 = a person who pursues study (学者有为, 'one who studies has something to accomplish'), or the later-arising 'person of learning' after the split from 道术之士 ('a person of the Way and its arts') — judge which period it is, and only then place your stone. Full marks when each of the four cases points out the trap and assigns it to a period.
:::

::: exercise A chronology of euphemism {level=2}
Draw up a ten-word euphemism table for 死: 崩, 薨, 卒, 不禄, 死, 填沟壑, 山陵崩, 仙逝, 去世, 走了 — file them in three columns, by rank of status, register and period, and predict the next word to succeed to the post.
::: solution
The rank column (the five grades of the *Book of Rites*): 崩 — emperors and kings; 薨 — feudal lords (still used today at the level of a head of state); 卒 — grand officers (in writing today, 病卒, 'died of illness'); 不禄 — the *shi*; 死 — commoners. The register column: 山陵崩 (taboo avoidance for the reigning house); 填沟壑 (self-deprecation); 仙逝 / 宾天 (a religious colouring); 去世 (neutral, in writing); 走了 (on active service in speech). A reasonable prediction of the next incumbent is enough (去了 and 不在了 are on active service; 睡去 is a candidate) — the mark rests on filing them correctly, plus an explanation of the mechanism that uses the rule of 'contamination, then something new'.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the biography of one word {level=3}
Write 800 to 1,200 characters. Write a word-biography (biography) of 经济, or of 社会, or of 网络, from the original meaning, to the return in new clothes, to a further migration in the digital age (for example the new combinations 经济下行, 'the economy heading downward', and 被××). It must contain: at least three pieces of textual evidence for the original meaning and the development; one instance of misreading a same-form false friend; and one sentence stating a position on 'whether a word meaning has a correct reading'.
::: solution
Marking points: the three citations can be checked, and their dates are not wrong; the false-friend instance is genuine (the essay cites a document in which 经济 means ordering the age and relieving the people, and the author is the one who cites it); the sentence of position is clear (for example: the correct reading = the reading agreed for that age; a dictionary is the archive of the written judgements, not the constitution) — quoting this lesson's metaphor of the 'lease', and rewriting it, gains extra marks.
:::

::: summary
Word meanings move house by four routes: widening (江 and 河 knock rooms together), narrowing (臭, 禽 and 宫 give up the lease), transfer (兵 and 獄 change address; 去 even changes the direction it faces), rise and fall (爪牙 falls; 乖 is washed clean). Disyllabic false friends must have their household registers taken apart (妻子 really brings the child along; 感激 does not thank anyone). Euphemism is the long-distance race, for euphemistic words, of contamination and putting something new on the shelf. In the late Qing and the Republic, 'old words in new clothes' completed the greatest migration in which the heart was changed (经济, 社会, 革命). The history of the lexicon is the history that least submits to 'thus it has been for all ages' — this lesson hands over a pair of lenses: **at a familiar word, grow suspicious; meeting a disyllable, split it first; when you settle a meaning, you must mark the date.** The next lesson brings on the ten great function words, 之其而于为乃则者所 — the pacemaker of the grammar of Classical Chinese.
:::
