The works of the previous chapters lie on the paper; the works of this chapter **live once in front of a group of people, and are then destroyed** — narrative and lyric flow together here, and one further witness is invited: **the audience**. The theoretical questions change shape with that: **why do we cry when we know it is false? Why is “one person pretending to be another person” civilisation, and not fraud?**

## Definition: the two-layer contract {#def-hetong}

::: definition Pretending to be another person, in front of another person {#def-zuixiao}
The minimal definition of drama: **one person, in front of another person, pretends to be another person** — four elements, and not one may be missing (actor, audience, pretence, on the spot). From this comes the **two-layer contract**: **the audience holds two tickets at once — “this is false” (handed back when the house empties) and “this is true” (counted once there is applause)**; what Aristotle’s “catharsis” (the **purification** of pity and fear) means at the level of the contract: **the theatre is a safe pressure cabin for the emotions: tears are paid by the hour here, and the overtime is voluntary**. **The division of labour with narrative and lyric**: a novel may write ninety years (Proust), a poem may be only a single sound (“啊”, ‘ah’), and drama **can perform only the present continuous** — **everything past must be rewritten as a conflict in the present (“prior history” is contraband stock in drama, and can be smuggled only by instalments, in the manner of “disclosure”: a messenger’s report, a ghost’s telling of his own story, a wet-nurse’s recollection — the ghost in *Hamlet* is an illegal flashback machine)**. **Presence is a tax regime**: drama pays every structural tax on behalf of “this moment” — the three unities are not a tyranny; they are an extreme edition of the tax code (see the next section).
:::

## The dynamics of plot {#def-qingjie}

::: definition The grammar of reversal, recognition and action {#def-tuzhuan}
The factory blueprint of the *Poetics*: **plot is the soul, and action is the object of imitation (only when people “act” are there sorrow and joy; character is merely a taste)** — **the first lesson of Western narratology treads “what person was written” underfoot: “what happened, and how it is joined into necessity” is the engine**. Two trump-card tools: **reversal** (peripeteia: **the action turns to its opposite**) and **recognition** (anagnorisis: **from no knowledge to knowledge**) — “**in the best plots the two happen at the same time**”, and the specimen is still *Oedipus the King*: **the messenger from Corinth comes on in order to comfort (“你怕的杀父娶母命案，父母都不是你双亲”, ‘the case you fear, of killing your father and marrying your mother — neither of those parents is your own parent’) (Our translation.), and one sentence completes the turn from the greatest fear to the greatest confirmation — the good news is the ceremony of unsealing a death sentence; the shepherd forced to speak = the “recognition” closes the last gap — two thousand three hundred years later, the entire skeleton of the detective novel is issued its birth certificate in this play**. The corresponding item in the shop-talk of Chinese playwrights: **“文似看山不喜平”** (‘writing is like looking at mountains: it does not like the flat’) (Our translation.) (**曲坡, 倒插, 横云断岭 — winding slopes, retrospective insertion, clouds cutting across the ridge — the technical names of suspense are earlier than the theory, and the theoretical names are later than the technique: the eyebrow notes of Jin Shengtan 金圣叹 are notes toward a *Poetics* with no Aristotelian frame of reference**); Li Yu 李渔’s clause of the “**great closing**” (*da shousha*) (**“必使此段之情，始终合一”**, ‘the feeling of this stretch must be one from beginning to end’ (Our translation.): **the end of a full-length southern drama (*chuanqi*) is not the resolving of the conflict, but the settling of relations — the grand reunion = the accounts cleared**).
:::

::: definition The three unities: the constitutionalisation of a hermeneutic accident {#def-sanyilv}
Aristotle speaks of “unity of action”; commentators of the Italian Renaissance read out of it “**one place, and twenty-four hours**”, and this was then institutionalised by classical tragedy in French — **the most famous “over-extension laying eggs” in the history of dramatic theory**: **in the controversy of the 1630s around Corneille’s *Le Cid*, the newly founded French Academy came forward to give a ruling (the first time a literary institution, in the capacity of judge, publicly penalised a play — from then on “criticism” had an authorised post on an office’s books)**. The speech for the defence of the three unities is **possibility**: “**two thousand years played on the stage does not look true; two hours played does**” (the received account of the position in Corneille’s *On the Three Unities*) — **“the believable” and “the true” part company: this is the common ancestor of the sensors in Chapter 2 and the focalisation in Chapter 5: the object of a rule has never been reality; it is the audience**. The counter-evidence of the Chinese stage is far more elegant: **one night-watch drum is three years, and one circuit of the stage is a thousand *li* — space signs no realist contract, and time is priced by the aria (see the next section)**.
:::

## Role-types, convention and a native scheme for the contract {#def-chengshi}

::: definition The painted face is a prefabricated reading {#def-juese}
**The role-type system** (*jiaose*: 生 *sheng*, 旦 *dan*, 净 *jing*, 丑 *chou*) = **a human-flesh index of morals and character**: the moment one comes on, the painting of the brows, the stage beard and the stage walk have already declared, of themselves, good and evil, the thick and the thin — **this is zero focalisation made into a thing (the “camera position” of the previous chapter walks on in costume); critics call it “type” (type) rather than “individual” (character) — but type is efficiency: the audience buys its stance ready in three seconds, and saves its attention for the surprises of the plot**. **Supposition and convention** (one table and two chairs; a whip stands for a horse; an oar stands for a boat) = **making Ingarden’s “spots of indeterminacy” into a public institution**: **space, time and objects are all filled, and funded, by the audience according to convention — Chinese opera (*xiqu*) never pretends to be reality, and therefore never worries about the artifice showing** (**drama with realist scenery is the opposite: it buys out the spots of indeterminacy, the audience’s construction crew is laid off, and the tax on imagination is remitted with it — two genres, two tax burdens, two pleasures**). **“*Ke*” and “*kehun*”** (the clown’s line, whose business is inserting gags) = **the relief valve of the emotional pressure cabin: when grief reaches a full ten, the clown inserts one line — quantity control on the engineering of catharsis: Chinese theatre troupes fitted a fuse to tears earlier than Lessing**.
:::

::: example *The Orphan of Zhao* and *Oedipus the King* taken apart against each other {#ex-zhaoshi}
Ji Junxiang 纪君祥’s *The Orphan of Zhao* (a Yuan variety play, *zaju*): Tu'an Gu 屠岸贾 exterminates the house of Zhao; Cheng Ying 程婴 **substitutes his own son for the orphan of Zhao** (**passes his own child off as the enemy’s child and sends him to death**), serves the enemy for twenty years, swallowing the humiliation, and the orphan, learning the truth, takes revenge. Please: ① point out where the **reversal and the recognition** each lie (which layer? completed by whom?); ② collate them with Oedipus: **in each of the two plays, a weapon pointed in what direction is “knowing”**; ③ Wang Guowei 王国维 takes as his ground “**列之于世界大悲剧中，亦无愧色也**” (‘placed among the great tragedies of the world, it would have no cause to blush’) (Our translation.) (*A History of Song and Yuan Drama*) — **which version of “tragedy” is the standard of his judgement**; ④ the problem of the prince’s version: **the “grand reunion” of Yuan *zaju* (the revenge succeeds) and Voltaire’s adaptation *The Orphan of China* (1755, closing on moral conversion) — one story, two settlings of account: what clause of each genre do they show**?
::: solution
① **Recognition** advances through several layers: the orphan’s “recognition of his birth” is spread into a whole scene of telling it himself (painted likenesses, Han Jue 韩厥, Cheng Ying disclosing in sections — **“recognition” is stretched into a long take of suspense two thousand years long: the closing of the gap which the West completes in one act, China, across five acts, makes into a slow punishment**); the **reversal** is at Cheng Ying’s “giving up his son”: **the action turns to its opposite = the first move in protecting the orphan is the murder of his own child — the moral yield of the reversal exceeds that of Oedipus (the latter does not know; the former knows it all: Aristotle says that the best tragedy is “not knowing”, so as to avoid moral disgust; this play knows, and does it all the same — in modern words: what it challenges is the boundary condition of “pity”**). ② **The direction of “knowing”**: Oedipus — **knowing is destruction (the search for truth leads to exile of the self: the theme of the price of knowledge)**; *The Orphan of Zhao* — **knowing is mobilisation (the moment the birth is clear, there is revenge — knowledge is an obligation: in Confucian ethics “knowing” is not an epistemological state; it is a document confirming a debt)**. ③ What Wang Guowei uses is the double standard of **a Schopenhauer–Hegel version of “tragedy” plus the Chinese tradition of “怨愤” (‘resentment and indignation’)** — **the very syntax of “无愧色” (‘no cause to blush’) is an act of comparing prices: the scene of the birth of comparative literature (using European categories to claim a nationality for Chinese works — a note for this chapter: the import duty on categories has always been paid by the weaker party**). ④ **Revenge = the “closing” settlement of Yuan *zaju* (the ledgers of 冤—申, grievance—redress, cleared on both sides: the accounting nature of the grand reunion); Voltaire changes revenge into forgiveness and moral conversion — the Enlightenment needed China to demonstrate, on his behalf, that “reason conquers the blood-feud”: the same material is moved to another factory between two demands of edification (a transnational branch of the exchange office in Chapter 2)**.
:::

::: intuition The third person: the audience is a required field {#int-guanzhong}
The reader of a novel may pretend not to exist, and a lyric poem may have no one to pick up, but **the definition of drama must contain a third person**: **“we are watching” is a hidden line of the script** — which is why political censorship has always watched the theatre first (**a performance is a gathering; the lines are the spark**), and why the “fourth wall” (the ideal of nineteenth-century naturalism: the audience is a voyeur) is in fact **the shortest-lived contract in history**: from Shakespeare’s soliloquies laying the truth straight before the audience, to Brecht’s “**estrangement**” (**sing it out, stop, explain — stuff “this is a play” back into the audience’s hands: inside the two-layer contract, deliberately tear open the upper layer, so that the audience can settle accounts with the other half of its mind**), **the “wall” was only a brief amnesia in the age of the bourgeois drawing room** — **the law of this chapter: attention in the theatre is always a three-party contract: two people on the stage, one person below, and no one is permitted to pretend not to see the third person**.
:::

::: history The theatre’s ledger {#sec-zhangben}
**The 5th century BC** the competitions at the Athenian festival of Dionysus (**tragedy is a public rite of the city-state: a whole city watches the plays, and lawsuits and the citizens’ oath share the ground — the site of the “institution-first principle” in the study of drama**) + the anatomy in the *Poetics* (**plot-centred theory opens for business**); **Han–Tang** *juedi* (horn-butting contests), the adjutant play (*canjun xi*), *Tayao niang* (the lady who steps and sways) (**farce, and short pieces of song and dance: the many sources of Chinese “drama” flowing into one, for a long time never entered in the official register of “literature” — the specimen of the institutional account in Chapter 1: the Collections division does not admit plays**); **the 13th–14th centuries** the golden age of Yuan *zaju* (**men of letters go into the trade and write for the *goulan* playhouses: Guan Hanqing 关汉卿’s 〔不伏老〕 (‘Refusing to Bow to Age’), with its “蒸不烂煮不熟” (‘steamed, it will not fall apart; boiled, it will not cook soft’) (Our translation.) — the author’s persona and the role’s persona brought to market in one mixed pack**); **the 16th–17th centuries** Ming–Qing *chuanqi* and *The Peony Pavilion* (**“情不知所起，一往而深，生者可以死，死可以生”** — ‘I know not where feeling begins; once it goes, it deepens; the living may die, and the dead may live.’ (Our translation.) — **Tang Xianzu 汤显祖 promotes the lyric subject directly to a principle of the cosmos: drama as the court in which “feeling” (*qing*) is spoken**) + Italian improvised comedy + the codification of the three unities in France; **the 1670s–1760s** Voltaire’s adaptation *The Orphan of China*, and Lessing’s *Hamburg Dramaturgy* (**bourgeois tragedy + the journal of criticism: the period in which a national drama was planned**); **the 18th–19th centuries** the well-made play (**“the play of a structure ingeniously arranged”**: situation — secret — escalation — the explanation that has to be there: ISO certification for the plot industry: Ibsen graduates from it and then rebels**); the 1870s–1900s** naturalism and the “fourth wall” (**Ibsen, Chekhov: fitting the drawing room with a keyhole**); **the 1920s** expressionism, Brecht’s epic theatre, Artaud’s “theatre of cruelty” (**language yields its place: the body, sound and light, “a metaphysical physicality”**); **the 1950s–60s** Beckett (**waiting when there is no plot to wait for: once the plot has been withdrawn, what remains on the stage is waiting itself — the theatre of the absurd is the genre’s self-test routine: drama sits in on its own trial**); **the 1960s** environmental theatre and the “Living Theatre” (**Artaud’s posthumous papers come up in the draw: the mirror is taken down, and the audience is invited onto the stage**); **now** the immersive, and the impact of film and television (**presence has become a scarce good, and the ticket price has settled back onto the four characters “你也在场” (‘you too are present’) — the hundred-year lawsuit between drama and cinema, the judgement written in the theatres whose lights came on again after the pandemic**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in analysing drama
**Scolding drama by the standards of the novel** (**“too much dialogue, and no description of the inner life” — in drama the inner life can become only a soliloquy, an action, or a slip of the tongue: the medium is a list of possibilities (the construction code of Chapter 4)**); **taking the “law of conflict” as a commandment of Heaven** (**Hegel’s conflict is the grammar of the modern nation-state: the ending of *The Peach Blossom Fan* is not a resolution but a withdrawal — “渔樵话兴亡” (‘the fisherman and the woodcutter talk of rise and fall’) (Our translation.) is a zone where the law of conflict does not apply, not a zone of failure: first ask what contract of reconciliation this genre has signed with the audience**); **the thesis that “role-type = character”** (**forcing character-criticism onto convention: to press for “the psychological development of Dou E 窦娥” is to go mountaineering with a fishing rod — what the type performs is the universal, and convention is the uniform of the universal**); **“tragedy is what China does not have”** (**the old wound of comparison in the Hegelian manner: Wang Guowei’s “no cause to blush” is a defence; what is to be done today is to change the ruler: from “fate / conflict” to “冤/申” (‘grievance / redress’) — the ledger-logic of Chinese tragedy (the judicial version of the lamplight clause in Chapter 2)**).
:::

::: exercise Smuggling prior history {level=1}
Write a one-act dialogue of 400 characters: **two people eat a meal, and get across a thing that “happened twelve years ago” — the past may not be narrated straight**, and only the means of “disclosure” are allowed (one sentence that slips out, one evasion, one old object, one stretch of song that goes off-key); then mark each other’s work: **from which word you inferred the whole case**.
::: solution
Marking: **a pass only if direct narration is at zero** (the appearance of “当年……” — ‘back in that year…’ — is a breach); **the design of physical evidence and of slips of the tongue must be such that one can reason from it** (how far the reader can reconstruct the case is the mark sheet); **the pressure of the “present continuous”** (the thing from twelve years ago decides every way of sitting, like a bomb under the table — **the standard of a prior history is seen in the manner of its leak**).
:::

::: exercise Manual for the painted face {level=2}
For a contemporary public figure, or for a “role-type” close at hand (the colleague deep in involution, the old man out walking his bird, the livestreamer selling goods), write a **role-type entrance poem plus three lines of self-announcement** (borrowing the logic of the role-type system: good and evil, the thick and the thin, declared first), and then **design a surprise in the plot that makes the type break**; state: **in which second the audience’s purchase of a stance is refunded**.
::: solution
Marking: the entrance poem’s **quality as an index** (the stance is bought ready in three seconds — the information is all on the price list of brows and eyes); the point where it breaks must **grow from inside the type** (not an accident fitted on from outside: the break for the colleague deep in involution must come from the logic of involution pushed to its extreme — the weld between type and surprise); locate the moment of the refund (**“他原来是怕被抛下”** — ‘so he was, it turns out, afraid of being left behind’ (Our translation.) — the line on which the stance is repriced, from a moral judgement to an understanding of the situation: this is the everyday edition of “recognition”).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the theatre is the court of genre {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**a novel may lie, a poem may lose the power of speech, drama must be on the spot: it is the only genre that accepts the audience as a jury**”. You must call on: **the two-layer contract**, **the tax regime of presence (the smuggling of prior history)**, **reversal / recognition**, **convention = public spots of indeterminacy**, **Brecht tearing the contract**, at least five items; and give a ruling: **have film and television (recorded, editable, with no audience) put an end to “the dramatic” — or, the other way round, sold the two-layer contract to the whole world**?
::: solution
Points for marking: the five mechanisms (**contract = the suspension is pageantry, not a belief; tax regime = the medium is a list of possibilities; trump cards = the weaponisation of knowledge; convention = the tax burden of imagination, outsourced; estrangement = the “false” ticket held up, and the account settled on it**); the ruling (**two reference answers: the end-theory — film and television cancel “on the spot”; the actor no longer knows that an audience is present; a bad take cannot be shot again: irreversibility is the very being of the theatre — watching a play on a streaming service at double speed is pressing the rewind key on the “present continuous”: the dramatic withers; the migration theory — the cinema is likewise a collective presence in the dark, and the “audience” fulfils its jury duty late, by way of the box office, bullet comments and ratings: the two-layer contract is rebooked as an asynchronous contract — “this is false” is written into the membership agreement, and “this is true” is paid for by tears, by the second**); if the conclusion writes “**司法也可以直播，但陪审团必须在场：戏剧性不是文类专利，是“当众”二字的利息——凡是当众，皆有戏剧**” (‘justice too may be broadcast live, but the jury must be present: the dramatic is not a patent of genre; it is the interest on the two characters “当众” (‘in the presence of others’) — wherever there is a “当众”, there is drama’) (Our translation.), it is an A.

::: summary
The theatre’s ledger, filed: the contract (**two tickets: when the house empties, the false ticket is returned; applause counts the true ticket**), plot (**action is the soul, and reversal and recognition are the trump cards — “knowing” may be a weapon and may be a poison**), the tax regime (**it can perform only the present continuous: prior history depends on smuggling**), convention (**spots of indeterminacy made public: it does not dress up as reality, and so the artifice does not show**), the third person (**two on the stage and one below: the fourth wall is the shortest-lived contract**). The method in one sentence: **first check how many layers are on the audience’s ticket, then check who on the stage is pretending not to know about the third person**. The next chapter draws up one table for them together: genre and style — that hand, genre, manages a wider ground than you think.
:::
