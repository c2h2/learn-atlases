---
title: "The levels of a literary work"
---

The author takes a curtain call (just seen off in the previous chapter), society leaves the stage (the mirror has gone into the museum), and at the centre of the stage only **the work itself** is left — this chapter takes that "all that is left" apart and looks: **is the work one level, or many? Is it the ink on the paper, the meaning in the head, or a full score that has to be performed afresh every time?**

## First battlefield: content and form {#def-neiwai}

::: definition The plasticine myth and how it dies {#def-xingshi}
Plain common sense pictures the work as a **plasticine set meal**: content (what is told) is a lump pinched into shape beforehand, form (how it is told) is the wrapping paper — **this piece of common sense was precision-blasted by formalism in 1915–1917**: Shklovsky's "**content has never been anything else; it is precisely the burden of form**" (the famous judgement in 'Art as Device') — **the same lump of "content" in a different box is another work: *The Sorrows of Young Werther* turned into a long news dispatch is nothing at all; '武松打虎' ('Wu Song fights the tiger') and '鲁提辖拳打镇关西' ('Major Lu punches the Lord of Guanxi') are the same kind of material, and wholly different pieces of writing**; a Borev-style extra thrust cuts deeper still: **"in art there is no content that other means could not replace" — content = the form you have not yet found**. On the Chinese line the acceptance sentence is ready to hand: **Frost's self-assessment of the craft, "poetry is that which gets lost in translation" — if content really were an independent freight container, the loss in transport ought to be zero;** **where there is loss, the packaging is the goods themselves**. **But the blasting crew should not have a total victory either**: fiction's "what to write" (the stock of subject matter) does indeed feel the pull of a pre-text (the sensor of the previous chapter). **This lesson's judgement: content and form are an alloy cast in a single pour; only in dissection do we saw it open and look at the cross-section — and the sawn cross-section is no longer the work (advance notice of the next chapter: narratology is the person who looks at the cross-section through a magnifying glass)**.
:::

## Second battlefield: the floors of the work {#def-cengci}

::: definition Ingarden's cutaway of the flat {#def-yinjiadeng}
Roman Ingarden (phenomenology) splits "a work" into **strata**: **① the sound stratum** (speech sounds, rhythm, euphony — **the level-and-oblique tones and the rhyme positions from the lessons on regulated verse and on the song lyric all set to work on this stratum**); **② the stratum of units of meaning** (the meaning of the sentence, and the "objects" it projects); **③ the stratum of schematised aspects** (the work supplies only an account "as seen from certain angles" — **'枯藤老树昏鸦' ('withered vines, old trees, dusk crows' (Our translation.)) gives the vines and the trees and the crows, and does not give the density of colour or the distance of the shot**); **④ the stratum of presented objects** (the world that is projected, whose "represented objects" may be stratified further). **The soul clause: schematisation necessarily leaves "spots of indeterminacy"** — "**the work is a drawing; the construction is completed by reading: concretisation = the reader fills in the spots of indeterminacy, and each job of construction is not entirely the same**". **Theorem: the work ≠ the concretisation** (*Hamlet* is not any one performance; **the relation of score to performance: one and the same work permits countless concretisations that are not of equal value, and is still recognised — "it is a type that can be identified again, not a thing"** — nor does it equal any one person's mental impression: a work can die, can be discovered, and can be misread, precisely because it is not an impression). **Entered in the side-effect register: the clause on spots of indeterminacy is the birth certificate of reception aesthetics (reception theory) (the protagonist of Chapter 9 hangs a plate here) — the text leaves the blanks, the reader clocks in**.
:::

::: definition Chinese pre-assembly: word–image–meaning {#def-yanyxi}
Fifteen hundred years before Ingarden, Chinese criticism had already fitted out a three-storey flat, and the **tools for taking it apart**: the 'Xici' of the *Book of Changes*, "**书不尽言，言不尽意**" ('Writing does not exhaust speech; speech does not exhaust meaning.' (Our translation.)) — **the pessimistic clause of expressionism: there is loss at every level (Frost's loss had an Eastern version before the battle)**; Zhuangzi's "**得鱼忘筌**" ('Having got the fish, forget the trap.' (Our translation.)) is taken up by Wang Bi 王弼's "**得意而忘象，得象而忘言**" ('Having got the meaning, forget the image; having got the image, forget the words.' (Our translation.)) — **the three-level structure comes with its own one-way lift: once meaning is in hand, the scaffolding may be dismantled ("forgetting" = not clinging to the level; it is not the same as the level not existing)**; Mencius's "**以意逆志，是为得之**" ('Use one's understanding to meet the intent: that is to have got it.' (Our translation.)) — **the reader takes their own "understanding" (意) to meet the author's "intent" (志): a docking protocol across ages**; add the working rule of Han interpreters of the *Book of Songs*, "**诗无达诂**" ('The *Songs* admit of no definitive exegesis.' (Our translation.)) — **a plurality of interpretations is not an accident; it is how the institution was designed**. Remember them against each other: **Ingarden's "spots of indeterminacy" and "speech does not exhaust meaning" are one and the same coin: one says there are holes between level and level, the other says that holes are the normal state — but the Chinese scheme is in no hurry to fill the holes ('不着一字，尽得风流', 'Not a word set down, and the whole charm is caught.' (Our translation.)), while phenomenology is in a hurry to map where the holes are: two scholarly temperaments, one room**.
:::

::: example An acceptance check of the four levels of 'Quiet Night Thoughts', plus an inventory of the spots of indeterminacy {#ex-jingye4}
“床前明月光，疑是地上霜。举头望明月，低头思故乡。” ('Before my bed the moonlight is bright; I take it for frost on the ground. I raise my head to gaze at the bright moon, and lower it, thinking of home.' (Our translation.)) ① File a report level by level, following levels ①–④ (for the sound stratum, cite the conclusion of the lesson on level and oblique tones: no entering-tone ending, an open-mouth rhyme; on the stratum of meaning, note the grammatical suspension of 疑是); ② **list at least five spots of indeterminacy** (whose bed? which dynasty? why raise the head? what is there in the 故乡?); ③ adjudicate: **are the spots of indeterminacy defects, or load-bearing walls** — try filling each of the five in solidly once (written out as a piece of exposition of 50 characters), and report the list of losses; ④ a thousand people can recite this poem, and no one recites it wrong, yet its "concretisations" are plainly different from person to person — **use the theorem "the work ≠ the concretisation" to explain what has not changed, and what is changing**.
::: solution
① The sound stratum (isometric lines of 27 phones, plus the long-vowel rhymes ou/ang: a long sounding at low volume — **there is no entering-tone brake: the whole night is a drawn-out insomnia**); the stratum of meaning (疑是 suspends the judgement — **for the first time the subject looks uncertain in the poem, and this is precisely the source of its universality**); the stratum of aspects (no near or far, no temperature of a season, no furniture); the stratum of objects (a "person" without a biography, in a space without a date). ② An inventory of the spots of indeterminacy: **the bed (a seat? a couch to lie on? the rail of a well? — a thousand years of evidential research is precisely the perfect specimen of a "construction dispute": research = trying to adjudicate art by engineering), whether the frost is real or unreal, the ages of the moon and of the person, the people and the things in the old home, why there is no sleep**. ③ Filling them solid is **reconstructing a murder**: every settled answer murders the other readings — **the spots of indeterminacy bear the load: the structural strength of this poem is in direct proportion to its degree of blur (the heat of the research on 床 is precisely proof that the public will not let the wall be monopolised by an official drawing)**. ④ What has not changed: **the schematic skeleton (the four-level structure and the distribution of the spots of indeterminacy — the score)**; what is changing: **the filler of each job of construction (each reader's bed, age, and reason for not sleeping — the performance)**; **"no one recites it wrong" = the score is stable, "everyone reads it differently" = the performances are not of equal value: the way a work endures is precisely "one and the same drawing, infinite works of construction"**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The experiment of cutting off both ends {#int-jiezhi}
New Criticism's prescription is the most radical: the **intentional fallacy** (taking the author's own account as the verdict = appealing to evidence from outside the court) + the **affective fallacy** (taking the reader's being moved as the verdict = measuring an electrocardiogram and calling it a literary prize) — **cut off both ends, and leave only the "public text": an autonomous machine born for close reading (a paradox factory: ambiguity, irony, tension, paradox — Brooks's judgement is that the only grammatical sentence-form of a poem is "mimetic affirmation" (拟态肯定)**). **The spoils of the experiment**: every operating term of the modern university classroom in poetry ("look at how this poem runs **itself**") is a dividend it pays; **the after-effect**: the work is enshrined as an **object of worship** — history, politics, gender and the market are all shown the door, and "what this poem is about" degenerates into a single-choice question (the remote ancestor of the "central idea" industry is registered here). **This lesson's position: ontological criticism is an excellent workshop and an extremely poor constitution — studying the text as an object (object) does not prevent using it at the same time as a tool (tool): write the workshop and the constitution separately, and the disciplines do not fight**.
:::

::: history A history of the transfer of title to the floors {#sec-guohu}
**5th century BC–3rd century** materials got ready for the debate on words and meaning (the *Yizhuan*, the *Zhuangzi*, the *Mencius* — **an ontology dashed off in passing in the age of the study of the Classics**); **the 220s** Wang Bi's commentary on the *Changes* — **forgetting words and forgetting images takes its settled form: the three-storey flat is issued its title deed**; **11th–12th centuries** Shao Yong 邵雍's "observing things by way of things", and the Jiangxi school's making "**using one's understanding to meet the poet's intent**" an everyday matter — **clauses on the reader's side begin to accumulate case law**; **18th–19th centuries** German idealism promotes the work to an "organism" (Goethe–Schlegel–Hegel: **the Romantic version of "content and form cannot be separated": a work of art is a plant, not a machine — this lesson's reminder: the organism metaphor is the ontological placenta of later New Criticism**); **1926–1931** Ingarden's *The Literary Work of Literature*, and the prehistory of Gadamer — **the theory of levels comes onto the market**; **1946–1948** Wimsatt and Beardsley publish their articles on the two fallacies; **1948** Wellek and Warren, *Theory of Literature* — **the "external/internal" zoning map becomes the map projection of comparative literature and of departments ("internal" = levels, style, imagery, myth — from then on textbooks had a table of contents)**; **1960s–70s** the counter-attack of reception aesthetics (Jauss's "history of effects", Iser's "implied reader" — **the clause on spots of indeterminacy founds a state of its own: the question changes from "what the work has" to "what the work does"**); **1980s–** context strikes back ("text" is merged back into "practice": the history of the book, institutional criticism); **today**: **the classroom still lives in New Criticism's house ("back to the text" is the default address), while articles mostly live upstairs in sociology — this chapter's diagram of the floors is the floor plan of the discipline: whichever floor you live on, that is the floor where you pay the rent**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the theory of levels
**The two-part formula "main content + artistic features"** (**the tax system currently in force in the central-idea industry: sawing the alloy open and selling it as two commodities — this lesson's verdict: any list of "features" that cannot answer "what would happen to the content if it were removed" is void**); **reading the spots of indeterminacy as a blank cheque** (**"all readings are equal" is the inflation of the spots of indeterminacy: the distribution of the holes is specified by the drawing — one may quarrel over the position of the bed, and nobody changes the number of characters in the poem** — **freedom to build ≠ freedom to demolish**); **using the levels as a constitution** (**take New Criticism's workshop rules out into society and make law with them, and there will be trouble: it cannot tell "how this poem runs" from "how this poem ought to be managed"**); **forgetting words turns into scrapping words** (**the popular misuse of "得意忘言" ('having got the meaning, forget the words') = not reading the text, and distilling chicken soup straight from it: Wang Bi's lift is one-way, but you have to board the lift first — forgetting is the second jump, not a free ticket**).
:::

::: exercise An audit of the load-bearing walls {level=1}
Choose the opening two lines of a short poem, list every spot of indeterminacy, and classify them: **load-bearing wall (fill it solid and it collapses) / soft furnishing (fill it solid and nothing is harmed) / unauthorised structure (extra drama you have imagined in)**; from the distribution, judge this poem's "drawing style" (detailed drawing / sketch / abstract picture).
::: solution
Marking: **at least one of each of the three kinds, and with a reason** ("who is speaking" is often load-bearing, "the season" may be soft furnishing, "the author's mood that night" is mostly an unauthorised structure); **the verdict in the language of drawings** (the sketch school = the type, in Tang frontier poetry, of where the moon shines; the detailed-drawing school = the type, in Song poetry, that brings a short preface of its own in the notes); extra: **give one "spot of indeterminacy that evidential research wants to adjudicate"** (the dispute over the surveyed coordinates of Yumen Pass in "春风不度玉门关" ('The spring wind does not cross Yumen Pass.' (Our translation.)) — **evidential research is the engineering way of settling an artistic dispute, and it is bound to lose the case**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Comparing two jobs of construction {level=2}
Choose two concretisations of one work (a Yueju (Yue opera) adaptation of *Dream of the Red Chamber* vs the 1987 television series; or two English translations of 'Quiet Night Thoughts'), **point out which holes the other side filled that your side left empty**, and locate the way the "original" exists within the comparison.
::: solution
Marking: **take the spots of indeterminacy as the unit of account** (more precise than "fidelity": Yueju fills Baoyu and Daiyu in as the role-types of Chinese opera, the 1987 version fills them in as evidential research on Qing costume — **two jobs of construction, the same drawing, different materials**); **the "original" = the drawing that forces both jobs of construction to count: it is not inside either job, but it is what signs them off**; full marks to whoever can recognise that "**the construction crew brings its own code**" (the set conventions of an operatic genre, the running length of a television series — **the medium is the construction code, the ontological foundation of the study of adaptation**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is the centre one whole piece of meat, or an empty chair? {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim that "**the 'centre' of a work is not a treasure hidden in the building, but the invisible column that joins all the rooms into a building: it takes up no space, and yet it determines the space**". You must draw on at least five items: **the death of plasticine**, **spots of indeterminacy / concretisation**, **word, image and meaning, and the forgetting of words**, **the two fallacies and the autonomy of the text**, **the score and the performance**; and adjudicate: **are "the death of the author" (the protagonist of the next chapter) and "the death of the work" (the alloy theory of content and form) the same murder?** — if not, who is each killer, and to whom does the inheritance go?
::: solution
Marking points: the definition of the column (**the centre = the principle that makes the distribution of the spots of indeterminacy intelligible: it is not itself among the floors, just as the foundation occupies no room — "不着一字尽得风流" ('Not a word set down, and the whole charm is caught.' (Our translation.)) is the column made visible in poetics**); the five items in place (**the alloy theory = dismantling the treasure; concretisation = a different person sits in the empty chair each time; forgetting the words = the column cannot be put into words ≠ the column does not exist; autonomy = sealing the column in as the only resident is legislating too far; the score = the column is repeatable identifiability**); the adjudication of the two deaths (**for reference: not the same murder — the death of the author is inherited by the reader (a transfer of the right to interpret), the death of the work (its death as an independent box of content) is inherited by the text (the general ledger of form); the one that really is the same murder is the 1960s: when the work was again declared to be an event rather than an object, the two inheritances were merged back into the reader's hands —** **the order of the deaths is the chronological table of the history of the discipline**); if the conclusion is written "**an empty chair does not mean there is no king; it means that the court can still run when the king is not present: the progress of literary theory is to read 'His Majesty' as the name of an institution**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The floor plan, filed: the alloy (**content and form cast in a single pour: sawing it open to look at the cross-section is dissection, not life**), the flat (**four storeys + spots of indeterminacy: the dialectic of drawing and construction — the text leaves the blanks, the reader clocks in**), the pre-assembly (**word, image and meaning, plus a one-way lift: not clinging to a level ≠ the level not existing**), the excision (**the two fallacies build an autonomous machine: an excellent workshop, an extremely poor constitution**). The method in one sentence: **ask on how many levels it runs, then ask whom it calls in to work**. The next chapter dissects one of the three operating protocols: narrative — who is speaking, and to whom.
:::
