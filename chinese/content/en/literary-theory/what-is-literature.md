---
title: "What literature is"
---

The first lesson begins by trying the word it itself uses: **what is “文学”?** The question looks as if it were nursery-school work. It is in fact the first case still pending after two thousand five hundred years of literary theory — **because every time the court sits, even the defendant, “文学”, has been replaced**.

## The defendant's identity card {#def-shenfenzheng}

::: definition One word, born twice {#def-liangci}
**The Chinese household register**: the four subjects of Confucius's disciples, “德行” (virtuous conduct), “言语” (speech), “政事” (government), and **“文学”** (*Analects*, “先进”) — **the “文学” of that time = learning in the documents, a certificate of ability and not a genre**. For the thousand years after, the boundary of “文” was measured out again and again: the Wei–Jin **distinction of *wén* and *bǐ*** (文笔之辨: what has rhyme, and a brilliance of sound-pattern, 声律镳铄, counts as “文”; unrhymed notes and letters count as “笔” — **the first time the Chinese marked out a production zone for “pure literature”**), the *Wenxuan* (《文选》, *Selections of Refined Literature*) does not take the *Laozi*, the *Zhuangzi*, the Classics or the Histories (Xiao Tong 萧统's **anthology is the definition**), and the tug of war between the Collections division and writing as a vehicle of the Way (Zhou Dunyi 周敦颐, “**文所以载道也**” — “Writing is the means by which the Way is carried.” (Our translation.) — **the metaphor of the cart and the cargo: literature's identity is that of the carrier**). **The Western household register**: literature, in its early years, also meant **the learning of reading and writing, and the written record**, and only in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries narrowed to “imaginative, creative writing” — **“literature”, as a modern institution, is no more than two hundred years old; yet we use it to register two thousand years of households (the reason this lexicon gives: to sift the *Siku*, 《四库》, the *Complete Library of the Four Treasuries*, with the modern word “literature” is to take today's directory of occupations and claim every person of antiquity)**. The two births together reveal: **“文学” is not a kind of thing; it is society's work of classification — the classification changes, and the work is always done over again**.
:::

::: definition Candidate definitions, and how they die {#def-dingyi}
**① The fiction account** (literature = imaginative fiction) — dies of counter-examples: does the *Records of the Grand Historian* (《史记》) count? And the parables of the *Zhuangzi* (《庄子》)? Autobiographical writing and the prose poem are, year in year out, breaking out of prison. **② The aesthetic account** (literature = beautiful language) — dies of ugly masterpieces (Baudelaire, and the coldness of Lu Xun 鲁迅) and of fine writing that runs smoothly off the tongue. **③ The form account** (Russian Formalism: **literariness = language responsible to itself** — Jakobson: the poetic function “throws the language **towards the message itself**”; Shklovsky: art = **making it strange** (使之陌生), “**making the stone be a stone**” — **this one is the hardest, and it is hard in being operable: test a sentence for “replaceability” (the method of substitution from the previous chapter is here given its permanent post as a definition)**). **④ The institutional account** (a reckoning of Eagleton's kind: **“literature” is the value-label we stick on certain writings — there is no eternal literature, only texts “read as literature”**). **Where the court stands**: ①②④ each govern one stretch — **the fiction account governs the novel, the aesthetic account governs advertising copy, the form account governs the classroom, the institutional account governs the history of scholarship** — **this lesson pronounces no verdict; it only teaches the working principle of each of the four thermometers**.
:::

::: example Four fates of the same line {#ex-tongyi}
Take “**床前明月光，疑是地上霜**” (“Before my bed the moonlight is bright; I take it for frost on the ground.” (Our translation.)) and stick it in four places: ① an anthology of Tang poetry; ② the dossier of a theft case (the suspect's statement: “我当时看见地上有霜状物质” — “At the time I saw a frost-like substance on the ground.” (Our translation.)); ③ someone's exercise in the new poetry, 《霜》 (“Frost”), appropriated as it stands; ④ a university examination paper in “古代诗词默写” (“writing ancient poetry from memory”; the cloze has already supplied the preceding line). Ask: **in which of these situations is it literature?** For each of ①–④, choose one account and answer by it; finally answer: **does its “literariness” lie on the page, or in the reading agreement?** (This question allows any answer to be overturned, on condition that evidence from section 1 is cited.)
::: solution
Reference verdict: **the anthology = a perfect specimen of the institutional account (it has always been literature, because it has always been taught so); the dossier = literariness put to a use at reduced rank** (the same string of characters is required to be read by reference — **literature is not a property of the characters; it is a clause of the reading contract: does this contract require one to “attend to the language itself”?**); **appropriation = a case of intertextuality: the exercise gains a borrowed literary halo from a sentence it has stolen — the institutional account readily explains “why we do not call it plagiarism but call it homage”**; **the examination paper = literariness under torture, to force a confession: being able to write it from memory does not prove that it has been understood**. To close: the form account shows its boundary here — **the replaceability test on “床前明月光” (“Before my bed the moonlight is bright.” (Our translation.)) does not in fact score high (experiments in changing the characters have, age after age, all failed: is that because it was shallow to begin with? or does it count as good only when it is shallow to the point of transparency? — this is the old case of Yan Yu 严羽 sneering “太白率而能古”, “Taibai 太白 (Li Bai) is offhand, and yet can be ancient.” (Our translation.))**; **conclusion: the four thermometers measure four numbers, and all are true; a reading from any single one is dangerous — the whole honesty of this course begins here**.
:::

::: intuition Family resemblance: a definition that does not define {#int-jiazu}
Wittgenstein's prescription: **“literature” may have no common essence at all, only a net of similarities** — the novel and the drama share fictionality, the drama and poetry share verse, poetry and the diary share the first person, the diary and the epic share “the real”… **A line runs between any two items, but no line passes through the entire hall.** **This is not conceding defeat; it is changing the question: from “what is literature?” to “when is it treated as literature?”** Soften the question, and every wrongful case (reportage, *záwén* 杂文 (the polemical essay), online genres, AI poetry) is rerouted on the spot: they need not prove “I am literature”; they need only state **“who is reading me, on what occasion, and under what contract”**. The chapters that follow in this course all do their building on this contract: the narrative contract, the lyric contract, the dramatic contract, the contract of interpretation.
:::

::: history Six waymarks in the history of the definition {#sec-lubiao}
**5th century BC**: literature = scholarship (the four subjects of the Confucian school). **217**: Cao Pi 曹丕, “**经国之大业，不朽之盛事**” (“a great enterprise in the ordering of the state, a magnificent affair that does not decay.” (Our translation.)) — **raised from an ability to an enterprise, with an option on immortality thrown in**. **501**: Liu Xie 刘勰, *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (《文心雕龙》) — **the first family portrait painted for “文” (the three chapters 原道, “Origin in the Way”, 征圣, “Testimony of the sages”, and 宗经, “The Classics as the root”, plug literature into the power supply of the cosmos)**. **The 1090s**: Zhou Dunyi, “文以载道” (writing as a vehicle of the Way) — **the carrier's clause comes into force for a thousand years**. **Around 1800**: the birth of European “imaginative literature” (**what happened at the same time: the print market, public reading, copyright, courses in the national language — four modern institutions stand on literature's birth certificate**). **1917**: defamiliarisation (陌生化) — **literature is moved into the laboratory**. **1921**: Zhou Zuoren 周作人, “**人的文学**” (“literature of the human”) — **the modern Chinese idea of literature touches down: the right to define literature passes from the anthologist to the definition of “人”, the human** — **one sentence exchanges the cargo of “carrying the Way” for “the human”**. **The 2020s**: **AI writing strikes the gate: the right to submit manuscripts, for whoever passes the Turing test, becomes the new facts of the case — the custom of the history of definition is that every victory of the goalkeeper ends in a change of trade**.
:::

::: warning Four pits of definition
**Pouring the modern “文学” backwards into antiquity** (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* is not a textbook of “literary theory”; it is a handbook for the governing of “文” — **tailor the ancients with today's words, and everything tailored away is what one cannot oneself understand**); **putting a definition in the place of analysis** (**to have got by heart “literature = the art of language” is not to be able to judge any case at all — a definition is a receipt; it is analysis that does the shopping**); **jumping in with the answer “literature ought to be useful”** (**the three camps, carrying the Way / the aesthetic / engagement, have been brawling with one another for two thousand years; first ask “who is demanding that it be useful, and useful for what?” — a question of use, not a question of essence**); **packing a value judgement into a relation of inclusion** (**what the quarrel “does online literature count as literature?” is quarrelling about is eligibility; what is really being quarrelled over is reading figures and funding — nine-tenths of disputes in taxonomy are disputes in the study of resources**).
:::

::: exercise The identity-vetting committee {level=1}
The committee receives six applications: ① 《滕王阁序》 (“Preface to the Pavilion of the Prince of Teng”); ② the first ten chapters of a certain hit web novel; ③ one obituary; ④ a product manual (rhymed version); ⑤ Lu Xun's *Wild Grass* (《野草》); ⑥ a screenshot of a chat log (passed round online as the “most heartrending dialogue”). **Take a reading on each with one thermometer** (fiction / aesthetic / form / institution), and write a verdict of one sentence (each sentence ≤ 20 characters).
::: solution
Marking: **the thermometers are not cross-wired** (read the manual on the aesthetic thermometer and the form thermometer and the needle goes off the scale, while the institutional reading falls to zero — **the split in the readings is itself the diagnosis**); the obituary = **a case of literariness parasitic on practical writing** (the moment it is screenshotted and forwarded, it enters the track of “being read as literature” — the verdict must point out **the switching of the reading contract**); the chat screenshot = a new case of the present: **“the real” drops out of the chain of evidence, and an emotional structure completes the narrative — whether you judge that it gets in or that it cannot, what is being tested is precisely how you rewrite the fourth candidate definition**.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: can AI write literature? {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim that “**the real win and loss in the dispute over AI writing poetry lie not with the machine but with the human: it forces every age to hand over the original of that ‘literature’ contract it holds**”. You must draw on at least four items: **the two births**, **the four thermometers**, **family resemblance (the question of the contract)**, **“人的文学”** (“literature of the human”). And rule on one mock question: **“if a work by an AI is read as literature by ten million people, is the institutional account forced to admit that it is literature?” — how admitting it, and how not admitting it, can each be made self-consistent**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the historical argument (**the institution of literature has never been an iron plate: the four-piece set of copyright, textbooks, prizes and anthologies can all be enlarged — to admit AI is not a logical obstacle; it is a regrouping of interests**); the thermometers (**the form account is the most embarrassed: AI perfectly reproduces the low-scoring sentences of the “replaceability test”; the aesthetic account is the most tolerant; the fiction account saves the most trouble; the institutional account is the most honest, and also the most without a position**); literature of the human (**the “人” of 1921 is a double meaning, humane content plus the standing of a subject — the AI case forces it into a single-choice question: does the qualification for literature attach to the author's “人”, or to the reader's “人”?**); consistency on either horn (**the side that admits it: a theory of the reader's contract, and ten million signatures bring it into force — the price: from then on, definition is wholly marketised; the side that does not admit it: a theory of the author's intention, or a theory of the “certificate of suffering” — the price: a boatload of works to which a collective order bore witness, the Homeric epics among them, are once again asked to leave the boat**); if the conclusion can be written “**every round of a new medium asks the same sentence: what is literature's minimum wage? — the one who pays the wages has always been the person who reads**”, that is an A.
:::

::: summary
A memo as the first case leaves the court: the household register (**literature is the work of classification, not a kind of thing**); the four thermometers (**fiction governs the novel, the aesthetic governs advertisements, form governs the classroom, the institution governs scholarship — a reading from any single one is dangerous**); the contract (**ask “when is it read as literature?”, and every wrongful case is rerouted**); the waymarks (**from ability to enterprise to carrier to “the human” — the cargo has been changed four times, and the station is still there**). The method in one sentence: **when you meet “does ×× count as literature?”, first find out who is paying the wages**. Next chapter, onto the scales: literature and society — the stretch of history in which the mirror account was at its most splendid, and at its most aggrieved.
:::
