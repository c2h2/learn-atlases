title: "Schools of modern theory"
---

This concluding chapter turns the court into a **school factory**: twentieth-century theory is not ten ideas, but **ten machines**, and each one bites a corner of the four-element map in Chapter 1 (world—work—author—reader). The debts run up by the nine chapters before this one are paid off today in a single **four-school consultation**.

## The map: ten machines bite the four corners {#def-ditu}

::: definition A school = a problem tree {#def-wentishu}
The right posture for reading a school is not to recite its -ism, but to **look up its problem tree**: **core belief (the part that cannot be given up) + protective belt (tactics that may be revised) + the problem sacrificed (what it cannot explain, and does not intend to explain)**. An example: **the core of psychoanalysis**, "**creation = the substitutive satisfaction of a daydream: the work is a symptom formed by compromise, and to read a work = an analysis that does not put questions to the person concerned**" (a paraphrase of the received account of Freud's 'Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming', 1908) — **its protective belt was revised all the way from "sexual symbols" to "the mother–infant relation" and "the Imaginary—the Symbolic" (the Lacanian school); what is sacrificed is form and institution (a symptom needs neither rhyme nor a writing fee)**; **the core of Marxism**, "**literature and art are the production of ideology — a work does not reflect ideas; it processes ideas into a perceptible sound: the task of aesthetic form is to develop the image of ideology (and to make it visible for the first time — just as what the inventor of the microscope saw first was not the cell, but that he himself had all along been living in a drop of water)**" (the received account of the Benjamin–Althusser–Macherey line) — **what is usually sacrificed is the side of "reception": a work leaves the factory bearing a class mark, but what it becomes in which class's hands is the business of the reader in the previous chapter**. **Consulting the table is better than taking a creed: first ask which corner of the map this machine bites, and what it sacrifices, and only then ask whether it works — otherwise a history of schools will be read as a history of sects**.
:::

::: definition The author's side: from biography to gender {#def-zuozhe}
**Biographical evidential research** (a regular in the dock for the intentional fallacy in Chapter 4) split in the twentieth century into two branches. **Psychoanalysis** (entered in the file in the section above) resentences the "author" from citizen to **subject**: a breach in the text (a slip of the tongue, a repetition, a place that will not come clear) is the way in — **"do not ask what this poem means; ask what its illness is" (a formula of the received account)**. The two campaigns of **feminism**: **the first wave is discovery — "revalue the tradition: from women writers who were buried to Xiao Hong 萧红; the deleted book list is the discipline's base draft (the gendered version of a 'genealogy of the repressed' — of 'the canon is a rolling revised release' (the Tang poetry course): who controls the print run"**); **the second wave is diagnosis — *The Madwoman in the Attic* (1979): Bertha in *Jane Eyre* is read as Jane's** split mirror-image: the madwoman is a surrogate for the woman writer's anger, and the prison room is the floor plan of marriage **("if the anger cannot be written, send a madwoman to write in its place" —** the mechanism of repression turned into a genre**); the ultimate question of "gendering the author" (Xiuming's question, "How does a woman write?" — the answer: either she does not answer, or she writes the question itself as the work**).
:::

::: definition The world's side: from class to the colonial {#def-shijie}
**Marxist literary criticism** (Benjamin, "literature as social practice"; the stance of the received account: **"every text is a symptom; class opens its mouth where the text is unconscious"**) — **kinship with the reflection theory of Chapter 2, and the splitting of the household: reflection theory asks "what society has been written"; symptomatic reading asks "in what form society keeps silent inside the text" (the place of the silence is a topographic map too)**; **the postcolonial three-piece set**: **Said, *Orientalism* (1978) — "the Orient" as Europe's post for speaking: it is not that I speak, but that I am authorised to speak (**"travelling theory" (Said): the luggage list for when a theory moves across countries — which sharp parts are detained at customs**)**; **Spivak, 'Can the Subaltern Speak?' (1988) — the verdict "cannot" is not a gagging order, but a microphone check: while the heroine of *Jane Eyre* goes mad in the attic, the Bertha of 安提俄比亚 has never possessed a sentence (pushing "who is narrating" as far as nationality and tongue**); Homi Bhabha's "hybridity / the third space" — the authority of colonial discourse leaks a breach when it is mimicked ("the mimic Briton" — the spots of indeterminacy in Chapter 4 are upgraded to cultural spots of indeterminacy: meaning drops off in the translation zone, and grows again there) — the after-effect of the two streams joining: the list of the canon becomes an exhibit (university courses in comparative literature share an origin with imperial language policy — the shareholding chart of the exchange in Chapter 2).
:::

::: definition The text's side and the reader's side: grammar and revolt {#def-yuyan}
The two calling cards of **structuralism**: **Propp, *Morphology of the Folktale* (1928) — Russian folktales taken apart into 31 functions: "a periodic table of the elements: the characters change faces, the functions do not change" (Lévi-Strauss came to blows with him, and yet each is the outside and the inside of the other — "the deep-grammar school vs the combinatory school")**; Todorov and Genette wrote narratology as **a textbook of tense and voice** (the vocabulary of shots in Chapter 5). **Semiotics** (Barthes, from *Writing Degree Zero* to 'The Death of the Author' to '**the pleasure of the text**' — **"a triple jump: from critic to grammarian to theorist of the erotic — the history of schools in a personal edition: a person can move house; a street cannot"**); **deconstruction** (Derrida's "**différance**: meaning is postponed without end along the chain of differences — **the demolition crew of the metaphysics of "presence": it does not produce interpretations; it pulls up the floor of interpretation: the nuclear weapon of the civil war inside the literature course (the 1981 *The School of Negation* and the "against theory" debate — the explosion, in the American classroom, of "why read theory now" — China's "method year" in the 1980s and the wave of translation and introduction: the Asian track of the same star**); the reader's side and new historicism (Greenblatt: "the textuality of history and the historicity of texts" (the two halves of the Montrose formula) — the problem of "anonymity and canonisation": whose writing is the writing that is kept? "the luck of the archive" as a unit of theory: Foucault's "author function" — "the author is not a person, but a classificatory function of discourse: pushing the 'institutional account' of Chapter 1 up to the level of mechanism ('once a text is marked with an author's name, it begins to earn interest at the author's rate"); cultural studies turns the whole building into a shopping mall ("literature is not an essence, but a practice: textbooks, prizes, reviews, book lists, AI rankings — Chapter 2 and Chapter 9 share an office here").
:::

::: example A four-school consultation on *The Peacock Flies Southeast* {#ex-zhenfei}
The long narrative *yuefu* (Music Bureau poem) of the Han, *The Peacock Flies Southeast* (孔雀东南飞): Liu Lanzhi 刘兰芝 and Jiao Zhongqing 焦仲卿 are torn apart by Jiao's mother, and both take their own lives; the preface says "**时人伤之，为诗云尔**" ('People of that time grieved for them, and made a poem, which follows.' (Our translation.)), and the poem ends "**多谢后世人，戒之慎勿忘**" ('We earnestly charge those of later ages: take warning, and be sure not to forget.' (Our translation.)). The four schools each issue a **written diagnosis** (each ≤80 characters): ① psychoanalysis (**made out to Jiao's mother**); ② Marxism / symptomatic reading; ③ feminism; ④ postcolonialism–new historicism (**made out to the history of the text's transmission**). Last, a self-assessment: **which school's prescription contains the dregs of the other three**.
::: solution
① **The case of Jiao's mother**: "**此妇无礼节，举动自专由**" ('This woman has no sense of the rites; her every move follows her own will alone.' (Our translation.)) — in the words of the accusation, 专由 ('entirely of her own will') is the key term: **the son's obedience to his mother is experienced by her as possession, and driving out the daughter-in-law is the clearing away of a "sharer" (the mother's version of the Oedipal structure: symbiosis in a widowed household — at once beneficiary and prisoner of the institution of "worthy grandsons and filial sons": the text leaves her motive speechless the whole way through, and speechlessness is precisely the object of the analysis)**. ② **Symptomatic reading**: **Lanzhi's inventory of labour ("鸡鸣入机织，夜夜不得息" — 'At cockcrow she goes in to the loom and weaves, and night after night she cannot rest.' (Our translation.)) and the bidding, between county magistrate and governor, to take her in marriage — two ways of pricing the same female body: the womanly work of a weaving household, and the market in marriage alliances with officials; her own request to be 遣归 ('sent back to her parents') falls where the accounts of labour-value and marriage-value conflict (the feudal teaching of the rites is the judgement; the economic structure is the collegiate bench)**. ③ **Feminism**: **before "自挂东南枝" ('he hangs himself from the southeast branch' (Our translation.)) she first "举身赴清池" ('she lifts her body and goes into the clear pool' (Our translation.)): the order is sovereignty — she is not dying for love; she is the first contracting party to withdraw; the charge at the end of the poem is issued from her side ("多谢后世人，戒之慎勿忘" — 'We earnestly charge those of later ages: take warning, and be sure not to forget.' (Our translation.) — the addressee is "later ages"): in the narrative poem the victim obtains the right to write the history, and in Han texts that is not the default setting**. ④ **History of transmission**: **"时人伤之" ('People of that time grieved for them.' (Our translation.)) = a collective signature (a news poem by the nameless); it is taken into *Yutai xinyong* 玉台新咏 (*New Songs from a Jade Terrace*) (a palace-style (*gongti*) anthology takes in an accusation against marriage — the use of an anthology has always outweighed its stance — the institutional account of Chapter 1); across a thousand years it is adapted into Cantonese opera, Huangmei opera and textbooks — every age casts itself into the story in the proportion of Jiao's mother (a home specimen of the chapter on "effective history": distance in time is a filter, and also a furnace)**. Self-assessment: **② is the richest — class analysis automatically draws out ① (the emotional economy of the family as a unit of production) and ④ (the way of pricing changes as the text is transmitted), while ① is the poorest (individual psychology cannot explain why this mother and this woman become a riddle in precisely this poem)**.
:::

::: intuition Every theory is a pair of glasses {#def-yanjing}
The final gift that a history of schools gives the learner is **a pair of glasses that can be changed** (the full cashing-in of Chapter 1's "methodology is glasses, not a filter"): **psychoanalysis sees the breach, Marxism sees the silence, feminism sees the deletion, postcolonialism sees the map, structuralism sees repetition, deconstruction sees the floor, reception aesthetics sees the blanks to be filled, cultural studies sees the shelves** — **each pair makes one direction clear and another direction blind: that is the definition of "short-sightedness", and it is the honest version of it** (**there is no panorama: there is only a rota for wearing the glasses in turn — to conclude the course is not to graduate; it is to obtain a licence in optometry**). **After theory** (the "post-theory" condition of the 2000s: **the cycle on which the league table of schools is replaced has shrunk from 30 years to 5: cognitive poetics, ecocriticism, the digital humanities, AI criticism — the moiré effect (a third kind of pattern, produced where two net-patterns are laid one over the other: schools, when they meet, no longer produce truth; they produce a grain — and a grain can also be studied; that is how "metatheory" makes its living)**).
:::

::: history A chronology of the machines' importation {#sec-yinnian}
**1890s–1900s** Psychoanalysis enters culture (*The Interpretation of Dreams*, 1900; 'Creative Writers and Day-Dreaming', 1908 — **"Freud is the first professor of literature in the twentieth century to have no text: he does not read new works; he rewrites the case histories of all the old works"**); **1920s–30s** Formalism → the Prague School → Bakhtin (**the childhood of structuralism, in a crack in the Soviet Union**) + Benjamin's Arcades Project (**the prehistory of the "culture industry": a reckoning with aura in the age of mechanical reproduction**); **1950s–60s** structuralism at its height (Lévi-Strauss, Barthes, Lacan, Althusser — **"the great unity of the social sciences: every object can be taken apart into a system of differences"**); **1966–1968** deconstruction comes on (Derrida's lecture at Johns Hopkins — **structuralism is still at its meal, and its stepson has already changed his name**); **1970s** feminism and narratology (Gérard Genette, *Narrative Discourse*, the French edition of 1972 — **"the last great cathedral of poetics, and the first notice of its demolition, issued in the same season"**); **1978–1988** the postcolonial decade (*Orientalism* — *The Location of Culture* — 'Can the Subaltern Speak?' — **"from the text towards the world: theory's geographical turn"**); **1981** Yale's *The School of Negation* + "theory is in a civil war" in America; **1980s** China's "method year" and the wave of translating and introducing the schools (**"scholars of three and four generations share one library card: theory fever = a collective crash course in optometry"**); **1990s** new historicism / cultural studies are institutionalised; **2000s–** "post-theory" and the various new empiricisms (the cognitive, the ecological, the digital humanities — **"theory passes from its revolutionary years into its civil-service years: each takes one problem-desk, and approves the homework of the chapters"**); **now** **large models walk into the consultation room**: **the defendant of Chapter 1 and the protagonist of Chapter 9 sign at the same table (Will AI misread? Will it "misread strongly"? — is Bloom's licence to be issued to a probability machine — the next chapter (the far distance of each course) is already queueing at the door)**.
:::

::: warning Four pits in studying schools
**Reciting doctrine and not consulting the problem tree** (**"Derrida holds that everything is text" = introducing the demolition crew as the landlord: miss either the core belief or the list of what is sacrificed, and it is a fail**); **one key for every door** (**reading Lu Xun 鲁迅 by psychoanalysis, Du Fu 杜甫 by feminism, and 'Quiet Night Thoughts' by postcolonialism — theory is not a vote cast for a stance: what a mismatched machine puts out is called a "tautology" (assume first, prove afterwards)**); **the laziness of the "outdated" line** (**"formalism is dead": it is not dead; it has retired into the contents list of your textbook — the way a school is laid off is by becoming common sense, and that is its success, not its death**); **taking the civil war for gossip** (**"whom did Said curse; who won, Chomsky or Barthes" — the score of the debate does not matter; the "point where the problem trees collide", behind the score, is the whole of the examination's scope and the whole of academic history**).
:::

::: exercise Test-driving the machines {level=1}
Take one contemporary text you know well (a series, a novel, or a hit short drama — any of these will do), **put on each pair of glasses and write a diagnosis of 60 characters** (psychoanalysis, Marxism, feminism, postcolonialism — choose any four), and for each pair add one sentence: "**here I have gone blind**".
::: solution
Marking: **the diagnoses do not repeat adjectives** ("it embodies patriarchy" scores zero; "the mother-character's lines are entirely about price — emotion comes on in the form of a price tag" scores); **the blindness sentence is honest** (if the Marxist version cannot see gender, it must report the fact itself — **the root-hairs of the problem tree are in the self-assessment column**); **the four pieces can be fitted into the text's four-corner map** (a complete jigsaw is a pass: schools are a division of labour, not a competition).
:::
:::

::: exercise The debate back in court {level=2}
Choose any one pair (Said vs his critics, Bloom vs de Man, the infighting among the four of *The School of Negation*), and **write a hearing minute of 200 characters**: the point in dispute (one), the core belief of each side, **the presupposition shared by both sides (that is, the ceiling of this debate)**.
::: solution
Marking: **the point in dispute is made single** (packing three questions into one fight = not having understood); **the shared presupposition is located accurately** (if, for example, both sides take it as given that "literary education carries great ethical weight" — **what they are in fact quarrelling over is "who teaches what", and the ceiling is therefore the discipline's self-preservation**); the form of minutes (no verdict, only a record — **this lesson's closing position: the restraint of criticism is also the rhetoric of criticism**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: can AI contract "the anxiety of influence"? {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**if influence is misreading, then in a world where ten thousand machines imitate one another, 'the anxiety of influence' has a statistical shape for the first time: there is no father, only a prior distribution**". You must call on at least these five: **the problem tree**, **Bloom's strong misreading**, **Foucault's author function**, **intertextuality / genre as protocol (Chapter 8)**, **"the intention of the text" (Eco)**; and give a ruling: **does a large model's "collage" count as a misreading — and if it does not, which machine in this chapter fails on it**.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**problem tree = first ask which corner it bites; strong misreading = premised on "killing the father"; a probability machine has no father, only a corpus (the genealogy is there; the patricide is absent); author function = the signature is still in force, only the signatory does not repay the debt; protocol = it pays the genre tax-bill as charged, but it never breaches the contract (a breach requires a remainder of intention); intention of the text = a check for coherence can still be run on it — this machine does not fail, and that is worth recording**); the ruling (**two reference answers: the side that says it does not count — "misreading needs interest and fear: the clinamen is a decision that costs, and resampling is not — anxiety is endocrine, not a product of training; the list of failures: the whole psychoanalytic machine, and every way of reading that takes the 'symptom' as its way in (it has no unconscious, only an unobserved sampling bias — what is interesting is this: the word 偏差 is current on both sides, while the mechanisms are entirely different — a missing-person case of terms that share a name)**; the side that says it does count — "it exposes, precisely, that human misreading has all along been statistical: what is called 'the anxiety of influence', looked at again, also resembles a distribution dressed by survivorship bias — **the last fortress of anthropocentrism is taken by comparative statistics: this is not necessarily a bad thing**"); if the conclusion is written "**a machine can continue every style, and cannot continue one concrete fate: the hardest lesson literary theory teaches the age of AI is to distinguish "generation" from "giving an account" — and "giving an account" (accountability) needs a person who can die**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Ten machines and one map, filed: the author's side (**daydreams, the madwoman, the deleted book list — "a symptom needs no rhyme"**), the world's side (**symptomatic reading asks about the silence, Orientalism asks about the microphone, the subaltern and the third space — the list of the canon becomes an exhibit**), the text's side (**31 functions, narrative grammar, différance pulling up the floor**), the reader's side (**blanks to be filled, shelves, the author function, the historicity of texts**), conclusion (**every theory is a pair of short-sighted glasses: there is no panorama; there is a rota; the way a school is laid off is by becoming common sense**). Method, in one sentence: **put the glasses on, take the reading, take them off — then change to the next pair**. Ten lessons, and the work stops here: from the household register of "what literature is", to the factory of "what a school is" — back to that sentence in Chapter 1: **theory is glasses, not a filter; and a poem is the face you still recognise in the second you take the glasses off**.
:::
