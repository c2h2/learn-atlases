---
title: "Genre and style"
---

The last two chapters took apart three operating protocols, narrative, lyric and dramatic. This chapter stands higher and looks at **where the protocol itself comes from, and why it is changed** — and presses the most elusive word of all: **style. How can a person 'resemble himself'? And on what grounds do we recognise that 'resemblance'?**

## Definition one: genre is a distribution format {#def-wenlei}

::: definition First the format, then the file {#def-geshi}
The naive view takes genre for a **shelf** (poetry in one section, fiction in another, and each new book, once shelved, goes to its place). The theoretical view takes genre for a **protocol**: **a legal code of expectations (the contact lenses a reader puts on when coming in: reading a *chuánqí* 传奇, a tale of marvels, believe its marvels; reading satire, believe its harshness; reading the pastoral, believe its 'truth') plus an assignment sheet for writing (to set the question is already to set the constraints: choose 'elegy', and you may not let the dead come back to life)**. Three hard arguments: **① innovation can be recognised only by a genre** ('**every poem strikes a pose towards the genres that already exist: inheritance, tampering, or a forgetting worn as a disguise — the very claim to be "unprecedented" has to be entered on the roll of some genre: no precedent = citation of the highest grade**'); **② a genre exists before the single piece** (first there is the place called *yǒngshǐ* 咏史, 'poetry on history', and only then does 'Red Cliff' speak from that place — **the duty of a poem on history is to borrow the past; Du Mu 杜牧 insists on reversing the verdict, and the reversal proves that the duty is collecting a tax**); **③ the death of a genre is more thorough than the death of a work** (the Nine Songs are no longer produced; the parallel rhapsody, *piánfù* 骈赋, has its supply cut off — **a work may be lost; a genre may be unemployed; and an unemployed genre is reincarnated as rhetoric: the rhapsody (*fu*) becomes 'rhapsody style' (*fùtǐ* 赋体), and *yuèfǔ* 乐府 (*yuefu*, Music Bureau poems) becomes a title for the song lyric — the macro version of Chapter 4's 'migration of constraints'**). **Bakhtin's supplementary clause: 'Genre is the grammar of collective memory' —** the epic makes heroes, the novel makes philistines: a genre does not record persons; a genre manufactures persons who can be understood ('the "superfluous man" is not a Russian phenomenon; it is the product of one combination of genres, the long novel plus the idle talk of aristocrats').
:::

::: definition The hierarchy of genres is a class system {#def-dengji}
Classical Europe had a clear **food chain of genres**: epic and tragedy at the top (grave persons, a grave style), comedy, satirical writing and the bucolic below (low persons, a mixed style) — Aristotle's 'objects of imitation better than we are / worse than we are' had already written in the ticket prices. Around the **1750s this chain is cut through the middle by the 'weeping play' (bourgeois tragedy)**: **the bourgeois ascend the stage of tragedy, on condition that the moral vocabulary changes generation: one is no longer fitted for tragedy by birth, but by a 'sensitive heart' (melancholy, kind, able to weep) — weeping became the new aristocratic bloodline**. **Verdict: abolishing the hierarchy of styles is not aesthetic progress; it is the exchange issuing further shares (Bourdieu's periodisation in Chapter 2) — every change in 'who may weep, who is to die, who is qualified to be pitied' is a class audit**. The Chinese line of comparison is equally hard: **the orthodox order poetry — prose — song lyric (*ci*) — *qu* (song or aria) — fiction (the Collections division, and the appellations 'leftover from poetry', *shīyú* 诗余, and 'leftover from the song lyric', *cíyú* 词余, are precisely the tax bill)**, and the route of fiction's comeback is isomorphic with the 'weeping play': **commentators of the Ming and Qing first prove that fiction is 'of use to the world' ('petty officials', *bàiguān* 稗官; 'wings to the orthodox histories' — **first file an application for tax exemption, then receive the crown**), and *Dream of the Red Chamber* goes straight from 'petty official' to sit at 开天辟地，从未有之书 'a book that opens heaven and splits the earth, such as there has never been' (Our translation.)** (a remark in the Rouge Inkstone commentary).
:::

## Definition two: style is a systematic deviation {#def-fengge}

::: definition The fingerprint of a choice function {#def-zhiwen}
The operational definition of style: **within the room for freedom that a given genre allows, one author's systematic preference in choosing — style = the fingerprint of a choice function** (not a certificate for 'writing well', but **a deviation that can be counted**: an inventory of images (Li Bai's 李白 moon, Du Fu's 杜甫 autumn, Li Shangyin's 李商隐 screens and lamps), syntactic preferences ('the tide of Han and the sea of Su' = two hydrological reports: one pours in a torrent, one holds a vast store in calm — **the metaphor first naturalises style, then has landscape claim it — and this is precisely the rhetoric this chapter is to take apart**), function words and terms of address (Du Fu is fond of steering the tone with 且, 更, 偏 and 祇 — **function words are the micro-expressions of style**)). **The threefold trouble of 'the style is the man' (Buffon, 1753: the style is the man himself — a sticky note for an inaugural address at the French Academy, and from then on the perennial signboard of comments on compositions)**: **① Yuan Haowen 元好问 had already opened fire: 心画心声总失真，文章宁复见为人 'The picture of the mind and the voice of the heart always miss the truth; how can writing let one see the person again?' (Our translation.) —** what he denies is not that the fingerprint exists, but the necessary link between fingerprint and soul: the forgery industry proves that style can be imitated and personality performed**; ② the reverse selection of science (a paper is required to have no style — **'the more it resembles someone, the more it is worth; the more it resembles no one, the more it is correct': on the style tax, literature and science are two tax zones**); ③ the test for counterfeits (**黄河之水天上来 'The waters of the Yellow River come down from heaven' (Our translation.): forge one line and it gives itself away. The giveaway rate is the very measure of style — style is not 'written well'; it is 'irreplaceably this'**). **The other half of style belongs to the system.** The correct use of large words such as 'period style' and 'national style' is not a pile of adjectives ('the air of the High Tang'), but **the statistical result of shared constraints: the same training for the civil-service examinations + the same one rhyme dictionary + the same kind of market in presentation scrolls (*xíngjuàn* 行卷) = Tang poetry; this is not national character, it is institutional (Chapter 2: the way the mirror is silvered)**.
:::

::: definition Chinese stylistics: from a physiology of constitution to a field guide of grades {#def-tili}
**Cao Pi 曹丕, in *Dianlun*, 'Discourse on Literature', writes 文以气为主，气之清浊有体，不可力强而能 'In writing, *qì* 气 ("vital energy") is the master; whether *qi* is clear or turbid has its constitution, and it cannot be forced into ability.' (Our translation.)** — **the opening of style theory as a physiology of constitution: talent and inborn endowment determine the manner of the writing, and father and son, elder brother and younger, differ ('Xu Gan at times has the *qi* of Qi', 徐干时有齐气; 'Ying Yang is harmonious but not robust', 应瑒和而不壮 (Our translation.) — the fountainhead of stylistics done as commentary: a pause every four characters, and after death each person collects a courier's slip of character)**; Liu Xie 刘勰, in 'Style and Nature' (*Tixing*), upgrades this to a **typology** ('**若总其归途，则数穷八体 "If one sums up the ways they tend, the number is exhausted at eight styles." (Our translation.)**: the elegant and proper (典雅), the remote and abstruse (远奥), the precise and spare (精约), the clear and closely joined (显附), the lush and ornate (繁缛), the grand and splendid (壮丽), the novel and strange (新奇), the light and extravagant (轻靡) — **the "eight styles" do not judge better and worse, only schools: the key difference from the European hierarchy of genres is that China, in dividing "styles" (*tǐ* 体), does not speak of class, and speaks only of "practice" (*xí* 习) ("才有天资，学慎始习 'Talent has its natural endowment; in study, be careful of the habits one first forms.' (Our translation.) — style can be trained)**); Sikong Tu 司空图, in *The Twenty-Four Modes of Poetry* (*Ershisi shipin*), upgrades it again to a **field guide of taste** (twenty-four personified realms: of the kind of **采采流水，蓬蓬远春 'Bright and full, the flowing water; lush and thriving, the far spring.' (Our translation.)** (纤秾, the delicately lush), **figuring a manner by a scene** — Chinese stylistics refuses definition and will only demonstrate: **it does not tell you 'what the magnificent is'; it simply takes you to see twenty-four landscapes**). **The trio *shényùn* 神韵 ('spirit and resonance'), *xìnglíng* 性灵 ('native sensibility') and *gédiào* 格调 ('manner and tone') (Ming and Qing)**: **Wang Shizhen's 王士禛 *shenyun* is the model construction of 'a style of having no style' — not a word set down, the grace of the antelope — turning an absent style into the highest style (a preview of Chapter 10, 'theory against theory')**; Yuan Mei's 袁枚 counter-attack in the name of *xingling* (**诗写性情，惟吾所适 'Poetry writes nature and feeling; I suit only what suits me.' (Our translation.) — changing style from a deposit held by a school into personal cash**); while the Tongcheng principles and methods (*yìfǎ* 义法) make style into a **syllabus** ('**因文见道 "to see the Way by way of the writing"** together with the two grades of the firm-masculine and the yielding-feminine — **the last time stylistics was put on an imperial establishment**').
:::

::: example A giveaway test for imitations {#ex-fangzuo}
A: '**举头望明月，低头思故乡。**' 'I raise my head and look at the bright moon; I lower my head and think of my old home.' (Our translation.) B (an imitation in the cento manner, *jíjù* 集句): '**举头望月久，低头思乡频。**' 'I raise my head and look long at the moon; I lower my head and think of home again and again.' (Our translation.) — ① From three directions, issue a giveaway report: **the type of parallelism** (flowing parallelism against strict parallelism), **the distribution of the weight of words** (the weight of the whole words 明月/故乡 against the breaking-apart in 月久/乡频), and **the unfolding of tone** (the sense of closure in 明月 and the sense of fragmentation in 乡频); ② add one piece of **institutional evidence** (the status of 望月 and 思乡 as ready-made lines before the Tang — **the flaw in a forgery often lies not in what it wrote, but in what it copied without knowing that it copied**); ③ generalise this into **a procedure for telling false from true**: given one online poem 'suspected to be by Xi Murong 席慕蓉 or Cangyang Jiacuo 仓央嘉措', list your four-step test (image inventory, syntactic preferences, the period of the vocabulary, habits of punctuation).
::: solution
① **Parallelism**: A is **pseudo-parallelism plus flowing syntax** (the actions 举头/低头 cohere, like one complete event of raising the head and then lowering it: **the parallelism is the coat, the loose sentence is the innards — 'looking parallel, but in fact flowing' is the patented close of a quatrain's final couplet**); B welds the two verb phrases into a **symmetrical ledger** (久/频 = a statistical pair of duration and frequency: **once the parallelism is strict, it is also dead — an event has been set parallel into a row of a report**); ② **the weight of words**: 明月 and 故乡 are **whole words of two syllables**, and they land with an echo; 月久 and 乡频 **split words** in order to scrape a parallel together (in Tang poetry 明月 is an established word, and one rarely sets 月久 parallel to 乡频 — **splitting words is the imitator's most common rut: he wants the nouns, and so he will not have the compounds**); ③ **tone**: both characters of 乡频 are light (level tone, a thin sound), and the close is like a whisper; 故乡 enters the rhyme by a departing tone (乡 is level to begin with, but the lead-in of 故 adds weight), **and closes like the shutting of a door** (**a quatrain wants a door, and wants it shut; it does not want a breath blown out**); ④ **institutional evidence**: B's lines are '**ready-made lines made of ready-made lines**' — 举头/低头 and 思乡/望月 are all a second assembly of ready-made modules: **when genuine Tang poetry uses a module, there must be one place that cannot be attributed (the 疑是 of 疑是地上霜 'I suspect it is frost upon the ground' (Our translation.)): an imitation can be attributed the whole way through — the fingerprint of a forgery is that it is too tidy (the sharpest sentence of this chapter: unexpectedness is the signature of an original; being proof against every objection is the name of the counterfeit's disease)**.
:::
:::

::: intuition How far statistics can go {#int-tongji}
The real record of stylistics by statistics (stylometry): **cases of attributing authorship (the ruling, by the frequency of function words, between Madison and Hamilton in *The Federalist Papers*), the detection of forgeries, the analysis that divides Shakespeare's texts among hands — it is good at 'who', and understands nothing of 'whether it is good'** (**to hand 'whether it is good' over to statistics is to hand the quality of a canteen's food over to the speed of serving it — the measurement is not wrong; the trespass is**). **What the study of style studies is preference, not level: frequency tells you what Li Bai 'uses'; silence tells you what he 'does not use' — the 'not-used list' is often closer to character. Du Fu does not write the sensuality of the inner chambers; Li Bai does not write the fineness of ploughing and weaving — a forbidden zone is character** (**statistics are the mirror of stylistics; and Chapter 2, as this chapter has it, wrote long ago: the problem with a mirror has never been that it cannot reflect, but that it cannot reflect what it itself does not reflect**).
:::

::: history A double history of shelves and fingerprints {#sec-shuangshi}
**4th century BC** Aristotle's three-way division (medium / object / manner — **the first blueprint of shelves for genre theory, the ticket-price system written in at the same moment**); **3rd–6th centuries** China opens for business on its own (Cao Pi's '*qi*' → the catalogue, in Zhi Yu 挚虞, of 'types of composition' → Liu Xie's eight styles, and *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Determining the Momentum': '**文因情变，势随体成 "Writing changes as feeling changes; momentum is completed by following the type." (Our translation.)**' — ***shì* 势 ('momentum') = the physics of genre: water finding its way back to the marsh — in China, stylistics and genre theory are on the same production line**); **12th century** the tradition of ranking by grades, with commentaries running all through the *Shipin* (*Grades of Poetry*) (**advance notice of the protagonist of Chapter 10**); **17th–18th centuries** the European hierarchy of genres loosens (**the weeping play, bourgeois tragedy, the novel steps up to set the price — 'the sentimental' as a new currency**); **1798–1830s** the Romantics recast the idea of genre (**Schlegel's 'poetry that grows': Romantic poetry refuses to be exhausted by any theory — the Romantics changed genre from a legal code into an embryo: a genre became an experiment under way, rather than a partition already fixed**); **1857** Taine's race–environment–age (**the social determinism of style written down as statute**); **1919** Shklovsky's '**Art as Device**' — **literary history = the dynamics by which devices and genres succeed one another (old devices become automatic, new devices take the place above — for the first time 'innovation' has a mechanism, rather than a revelation from a god**); **1950s–60s** reception aesthetics revises genre (Jauss: the horizon of expectations; Todorov and Genette: structuralist poetics — **the high point of the school that takes genre as grammar**); **1970s** Foucault's discourse, and feminist 'writing the body' (**'who is permitted to possess a style' is brought back into court: literary history's names for the styles of women writers are often gendered — is 'femininity' a style, or a ceiling?**); **1980s–90s** historical poetics and the cognitive turn (**genre is changed from a legal code into a 'form of life' (Wittgenstein's entrance)**); **now** **the great cycle of style: AI can imitate 'the Li Bai manner'; the market value of style shifts from 'whom it resembles' to 'why, rather, this was chosen' — a fingerprint can be copied; the motive of the choice cannot be outsourced (see the next chapter: it is the reader's turn to name a price)**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of genre and style
**Using 'style' in place of analysis** (**'the language is fresh and natural' is a compliment carrying no information — rewrite it as items one can check: are kitchen smoke, and chickens and dogs, in the image inventory? Does the loose sentence stand first in the syntax? — style is the compressed package of the evidence, not a stand-in for analysis**); **prosecuting genre as if it were law** (**'this poem does not rhyme; does it still count as a poem?' — genre is a descriptive library, not a court: description that steps over the boundary is a false charge; modern poetry breaches the contract, and breach is also one kind of contractual relation (a silent negotiation)**); **the all-purpose sticker 'spirit of the age'** (**the explanatory power of 'the self-awareness of the Wei and the Jin' fluctuates from about 0 to 100 — to call out the spirit of the age without inspecting the institutional carriers (hereditary family rank, the selection of officials, the sites of pure conversation, *qīngtán* 清谈) is to blame heaven without checking the meter**); **in telling true from false, a superstition about a single indicator** (**'if it uses no allusion, it is not Song poetry' — a fingerprint can be shared by many people: statistics give only a probability; a single piece of evidence does not decide the case — the first lesson of the study of forgeries: a real writer will also, now and then, resemble a fake writer**).
:::

::: exercise Mapping the forbidden zones {level=1}
Choose an author you know well (a writer, a singer or a video uploader will all do), and list the top five of that author's **image inventory** and a **'not-used list' (one subject, one item of vocabulary and one sentence pattern that he never touches)**; from the two lists write one sentence of 'style verdict' (no more than 25 characters; adjectives forbidden).
::: solution
Marking: **the inventory must be nameable** (down to the word); **the not-used list must be real** (it can be refuted: produce one that he really did use, and the item is void — **a forbidden zone is the costliest evidence of personality, and also the easiest place to boast**); the form of the verdict (the sentence pattern 'uses X and avoids Y' — **style = the projection of a choice into the two columns**).
:::
:::

::: exercise An experiment in distributing the same topic {level=2}
Of one and the same event ('a food-delivery rider falls in a rainstorm; the meal is not spilt') **write three genre versions**: a seven-character quatrain, a short news item, and the opening of a very short story (each no more than 80 characters); attach an explanation of 100 characters: **what each version was compelled by its genre to 'omit'**.
::: solution
Marking: **the genre tax bill is clear** (the seven-character quatrain omits cause and effect, and names, and keeps only rain and light; the news item omits every adjective and every mood; the very short story must give an inner life and a prior history — **the same weather, three sets of accounts**); **omission is a stance** (the grammatical count of 'who falls' and 'who does not fall': the implied author hides inside transitivity — **the rider 'was laid flat by the rain' (made intransitive = liability waived) and 'the vehicle hit the railing' (no subject = made into an accident)**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the author's fingerprint in the age of AI {level=3}
Write between six hundred and a thousand characters, assessing '**when a machine can forge every fingerprint, style for the first time shows its original form: it is not a handwriting, it is a signature — a signature works because the person who signs is willing to answer for it**'. You must call on at least five items: **the choice function**, **the giveaway rate**, **the not-used list**, **genre = protocol**, **'period style = institutional statistics'**; and give a ruling: **after generators of 'the Li Bai manner' circulate in quantity, will the value of the real Li Bai rise or fall** — hint: the price mechanism of antiques and of authentic works.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**choice function = the susceptibility of preference to being counted; giveaway rate = the measure of the degree of irreplaceability; not-used list = a forbidden zone is personality; protocol = the genre's tax bill; institutional statistics = the disenchantment of 'the High Tang'**); the ruling (**two references: the rise account — a flood of imitations drives up the price of the genuine and of provenance: Li Bai is promoted from 'writes well' to 'has lived, has endured, has chosen' (**the scarcity of style changes from an aesthetic entry to a biographical entry: the more the fingerprint can be copied, the more the hand is worth**); the fall account — when 'resembling Li Bai' costs nothing, ordinary readers' capacity to feel the Li Bai manner is diluted by inflation: the aura of a famous work depends on scarce distribution (the Benjamin line) — the real risk is not that Li Bai loses value, but that the reader's taste is priced by cheap samples**); if the conclusion is written as '**the value of a signature comes from a chain of responsibility: the person who writes can answer for it, and only then does the person who reads dare to take it as real — AI owes nobody a style, and that is exactly why, however closely it writes, it still does not count as putting a name to the work**', it is an A.
:::

::: summary
The double history, filed: the shelf (**genre = protocol: innovation can be recognised only by a genre; 'no precedent = citation of the highest grade'**), class (**the food chain of genres and its revolution: the new aristocratic bloodline of weeping; fiction first files an application for tax exemption, then receives the crown**), the fingerprint (**style = a choice function: the giveaway rate is the measure, the forbidden zone is personality; Buffon's sticky note is to be shared by half with Yuan Haowen**), the schools of style (**from Cao Pi's physiology of constitution to the landscape teaching of the twenty-four modes: China divides styles and does not speak of class**). The method in one sentence: **first inspect the tax bill (genre), then inspect the accounts of preference (style), and last inspect what he does not dare to touch**. The next chapter comes round to the person opposite: reading and interpretation — time for the reader to take up the post.
:::
