The previous chapter took the work apart into storeys; this chapter returns to the storey that is in operation: **someone is telling something to someone else** — this seemingly transparent act is a jargon-production workshop that narratology spent a whole century taking apart: **"who is telling" and "who is living through it" are not the same thing, "seeing" and "saying" are not the same thing either, and "how many times it was told" is itself a taking of a stand**.

## The first layer: who is telling {#def-shuijiang}

::: definition Three "persons", and only one is compiling the dictionary {#def-renge}
**The author** (a biographical entity, a citizen who draws a wage) ≠ **the implied author** (Booth, *The Rhetoric of Fiction*: **the gatekeeper-personality of the text — "a version of myself": the sum of choices, of ordering, and of the setting of values**) ≠ **the narrator** (the voice that tells the story, an employee of the text). **The separation of the three seats is the first theorem of narratology**: a first-person narrator is not the author (**otherwise every autobiographical novel is a confession**); the implied author has no name and no photograph, only **a table of contents and footnotes** (**the evidence for it lies entirely in what the text keeps and what it cuts: what is written and what is not written is its signature**). **The unreliable narrator** = the case law of Booth–Meyerson: **the crack that opens between "what is said" and "what happened" is subsidised by the reader** — when the narrator's judgement departs from the implied author's system of settings (**Huck says “下地狱我乐意” ('I would gladly go to hell.' (Our translation.)) while the text judges him saved — the narrator's moral blind spot is precisely the moral scale of the work**), narration becomes **double-entry bookkeeping**. The Chinese classical tradition comes with a notary of its own: **the storyteller's “看官听说”** (**a narratorial persona openly acknowledged: I do not pretend to be transparent; I claim my stance — the omniscient authority of the nineteenth-century European novel already had a work-badge of the same model in the Song–Yuan storyteller's scripts (*huaben*)**); and **"*Chunqiu* phrasing"** (**“赵盾弑其君” ('Zhao Dun assassinated his ruler.' (Our translation.)): the historian convicts by his wording — zero focalisation gives way to praise and blame: who is named, who is omitted, whether one uses 弑 ('to assassinate a superior') or 杀 ('to kill'), and the verb is the written judgement — the ethics of narratology had already opened for business in the China of the fifth century BC**).
:::

## The second layer: who is seeing {#def-shuikan}

::: definition Focalisation: the camera is a moral organ {#def-jujiao}
Genette's three-way division (it is enough to note it down as camera positions): **zero focalisation** (the narrator knows more than any character — **omniscience: the camera position of God, standard equipment in the nineteenth century**); **internal focalisation** (following some one character's perception — **what he does not know, we do not know either; "what is in the room" depends on whether he raised his eyes — Granny Liu 刘姥姥 enters the Grand View Garden, in *Dream of the Red Chamber*: the classic plug-in of internal focalisation: borrow a pair of eyes that "do not recognise", and every furnishing prices itself (the extravagance of the Jia household needs no voice-over from then on — even Ban'er 板儿 crowing becomes the going rate)**); **external focalisation** (it only shoots, and does not see through — **hard-boiled photography in Hemingway's manner: an inventory of actions, motives left as unsolved cases — "why he does not leave the table" becomes the signature question of the modern novel**). **The separation of "who speaks" and "who sees" is the most profitable asset of this zone**: the first-person "I" may be the teller, while the gaze belongs to another "I" (**an adult recalling childhood is two cameras set side by side: "the narrating-I > the experiencing-I — the Gestalt of growing up is the parallax of the two I's"**). The mechanism of **point of view = moral stance** is frighteningly plain: **whose verb one writes with** — “他偷了面包／他顺手拿走／他饿到拿了” ('he stole the bread / he took it away in passing / he was hungry enough that he took it.' (Our translation.)) — **the same action, three sets of gavels: narratology's first lesson in ethics is not on the level of theme, but on the level of word class**.
:::

## The third layer: how it is arranged {#def-shijian}

::: definition Duration: the shutter speed of time {#def-shiju}
The ratio of story time (h) to text length (T) = **speed**: **summary** (thirty years in one line — **before “林黛玉进贾府” ('Lin Daiyu 林黛玉 enters the Jia household'), first a fast-forward on the scale of “如去一年” ('as if a year had gone')**), **scene** (dialogue and action at approximately the same speed — **“一两句话一顿饭” ('a sentence or two, one meal'): a scene is time in slow motion**), **pause** (description suspends the story — **an inserted commercial of landscape and costume: here the narrative breathes**), **ellipsis** (not a word said of the story — **ellipsis is the honest record of the author admitting that he has decided not to look**), **summing-up and stretch** (**the four characters of “他想了很久” ('he thought for a long time') cover an hour: length does not stand for speed — speed is a trick of grammar and vocabulary, not a count of characters**). **The core use: the allocation of speed = the allocation of value** — **whoever a novel gives its slowest shot to, it is saying that this is who is most worth living through** (Tolstoy gave Anna's last night to scene, and Napoleon's campaigns to summary — **a stance does not depend on adjectives; it depends on the shutter**).
:::

::: definition Frequency: thematising "again and again" {#def-pinlü}
“他每周日去教堂” (**iterative narration**) and “他去了教堂” (**a single time**) are not of equal value — **frequency is narrative's compression protocol, and also its device for taking a stand**: the character “**初**” in the *Zuo Tradition* (**the opening gong of analepsis: one “初” registers one look backward, and buries cause and effect in the ledger of foreshadowing; at the end of the piece, “遂为母子如初” ('And so they became mother and son as at the beginning.' (Our translation.)) folds frequency back to the origin — the “初” of reciting poems in the tunnel and the “如初” answer each other from afar — one adverb completes the loop — here the ethics of narrative and the grammar of narrative are the same character**); **the modern use of iterative narration = turning "how other people talk" into an event** (**the narratological weight of the three characters of “都说他疯了” ('everyone says he has gone mad'): there is no fact, only transmission — rumour enters in the identity of frequency**).
:::

::: example An external-focalisation rewrite of the tiger-fight {#ex-wusong}
Rewrite “武松打虎” ('Wu Song 武松 fights the tiger') as **external focalisation from the innkeeper's point of view**: you may not enter Wu Song's mind, and the tiger's point of view may not appear; you may use only “听见、看见、官府告示、伙计的话、次日路过的血迹” ('hearing, seeing, the official notice, the waiter's words, the bloodstains one passes the next day'). After rewriting, answer: ① how is the heroic air **retold**? ② what function does the signboard “三碗不过冈” ('Three bowls, and you do not cross the ridge.' (Our translation.)) take on in the new version? ③ which charms of the original **depend exactly** on internal focalisation (whose is the “啊呀” at the instant the staff snaps)? ④ if it is changed again into the style of an "official document", how does the moral evaluation flip a third time?
::: solution
① **Retelling demotes the hero to gossip, and promotes him again to legend**: external focalisation cancels the display of confidence — **reader and villagers share a disadvantage in information: fear becomes the only pass (the subjective courage, in the original, of “明知山有虎” ('knowing well that the mountain has a tiger') has nowhere to be reimbursed: a zero-degree Wu Song becomes the marketplace tale of “那人不要命” ('that man holds his life cheap.' (Our translation.)) — point of view is the system of evaluation)**; ② the signboard = **the narrator's prophetic camera position** (the innkeeper's words = an embedded narration of warning — **“三碗不过冈” is the folk's zero-focalisation statistics on the tiger: the most economical generator of suspense in narrative**); ③ the staff section: the charm of the original lies in **the inner scene of a misjudgement** — **the collapse in the instant of striking a withered tree by mistake, and that “啊呀” which never left the mouth, are assets owned by internal focalisation alone; external focalisation is left only with the physical evidence of the three characters “棒折了” ('the staff has snapped'): every thrill is a privileged perception**); ④ the flip in official-document style: **“阳谷县景阳冈都头武松射虎有功，赏” ('Wu Song, constable of Jingyang Ridge in Yanggu County, has merit for shooting a tiger; reward him.' (Our translation.)) — the same event is raised to an achievement of public order: fighting the tiger changes from legend into administrative merit: a fourth camera (the institution) enters — every change of focus in narrative is a reshuffle of the court**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The narrator is the tax that fiction pays {#int-shui}
A symmetry often overlooked: **the more "real" a novel is, the more complex the narrative apparatus it needs** (the diary form, the epistolary form, the testimony form, multi-voice oral history — **every kind of "I was there" is a new rhetoric**); the naivety of "**first person = real**" was harvested again and again in the eighteenth century (Defoe's "I" is a manuscript an editor discovered — **the preface pays the novel's "reality tax" for it: fiction is licensed to exist by perjury, of the same family as the previous chapter's *chuanqi*, "licensed to matter only by first pretending to be useful"**). Evidence the other way: **the "I" of a memoir is the least reliable, and yet is protected by contract (the label "non-fiction" is a subsidy of credibility granted by publishing law)** — **narratology's sobering dose: person is only a camera, reliability is an effect, reality is a contract: the three are often sold together, and you learn the going rate only when you take them apart and buy them singly**.
:::

::: history The camera changes generation {#sec-jingtou}
**The fifth century BC** — *Chunqiu* phrasing: **wording is the licence to judge**; **Han** — the *Records of the Grand Historian* and the **method of mutual illumination** (**one person's truth is split up and shared out among many: each biography holds one face of the mirror: narrative "omniscience" is held up by the invisible seams of the jigsaw — where the basic annals and the biographies contradict is the hiding-place of historical insight**); **Tang tales** (*chuanqi*) — the narrator shows himself (a notarising formula of the “**贞元中某公**” type ('in the Zhenyuan era, a certain gentleman')); **Song and Yuan** — the storyteller's “看官听说”: **the narratorial persona hangs its sign in public**; **Ming and Qing** — the chapter-novel and commentary (**Zhiyanzhai 脂砚斋, “草蛇灰线，伏脉千里” ('A grass-snake, a line of ash; the hidden vein runs a thousand li.' (Our translation.)) — a narratology native to Chinese: a handbook for managing the points where narrative information is buried: foreshadowing = deferred payment, the answering of the plant = the day of settlement: the critic named "the logic of plot" before the theorist did**); **the nineteenth century** — omniscient authority at its height (a period of balance: Dostoevsky's polyphony was already detonating inside it — **Bakhtin: “众多独立声音的小说” ('a novel of many independent voices.' (Our translation.)) gives advance notice of the democratisation of the camera**); **1900s—50s** — the dogma of point of view (**Lubbock, *The Craft of Fiction*: "where one looks from" becomes the religious question of the modern novel: Henry James's "centre of consciousness"**); **1960s—70s** — narratology established as an institution (Todorov gives it its name, the grammar tables of Genette's *Narrative Discourse*, Rimmon-Kenan's textbook — **the "grammar faction" converts the camera into rules; afterwards the dispute over "natural/artificial" narrative, classical vs postclassical (crossing into the fields of the cognitive, of feminism, and of the study of ethics)**); **now** — film, television and games take over the experiment (**POV shots, multi-playthrough narrative, the deconstruction of voice-over — narratology moves from the novel's dissecting room to the municipal by-laws of the whole screen**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of narrative analysis
**Taking the narrator for the author's mouthpiece** (**"Lu Xun 鲁迅 says 'I' think" — the first person is not a confession: the separation of the three seats is this chapter's passport**); **taking focalisation for an "opinion"** (**internal focalisation is not sympathy: seeing through Hitler's eyes ≠ defending him — the position of perception and the system of evaluation are two different banks**); **the progress thesis that "omniscience is already dead"** (**it is not that the nineteenth-century novel was unable to restrict point of view; it was another contract: a fast-forward in the manner of La Fontaine has an aesthetic of its own; telling literary history as a history of camera upgrades = telling style as technology**); **using terms in place of observation** (**a page full of "narrator / focalisation / secondary narration" without pointing out the effect of any one concrete verb — Genette is a ladder, not the landscape; this chapter's line for acceptance: every term must be exchanged once for a description of a shot**).
:::

::: exercise The verb court {level=1}
Take “他迟到了” ('he arrived late') as the event, and write three versions: the teacher's focalisation / the mother's focalisation / the person's own internal focalisation; **each version exactly one sentence, adjectives of psychology forbidden, only verbs and adverbs allowed**; assess between you which version "convicts most heavily", and how the verbs bring it about.
::: solution
Marking: **the three focalisations do not mix (the teacher's version sees only the door, the mother's version sees only the meal, the person's own version hears only the heartbeat)**; **the verbs give evidence** (“闯进来／还知道回来／卡在门口” ('bursts in / still knew to come back / stuck at the door') — **the evaluation is hidden entirely in the transitivity**); the ruling on conviction can use the terms (the weight of external focalisation vs the lightness of internal focalisation — **sympathy comes from being able to get in; a heavy sentence comes from being able to see that one cannot get in**).
:::
:::

::: exercise The shutter graph {level=2}
Choose one chapter of a novel, and **make an "electrocardiogram of temporal density"**: register, section by section, the span of story time and the number of characters in the text, and draw a broken line; find the **slowest** and the **fastest** place in the whole chapter, and explain each in one sentence: "**why the camera stops here / skips past**".
::: solution
Marking: **the registered data are real** (key stretches may not be estimated); **the slowest place is often the site of value** (a meal, a raising of the head; **description inserted at a pause is often a display case of class and gender**); **the fastest place is often the narrator's place of shame, or his abacus** (a stance hides inside the summary: **the five characters of “她嫁了人” ('she got married') dispose of a life — speed is indifference, and indifference is the verdict**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: point of view is morality {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**the novel does not teach a person what is right; it only forces a person to practise living once through someone's senses: this is a way of educating slower, and also deeper, than a textbook of ethics**". You must call on at least five items: **the separation of the three seats**, **unreliable narration**, **focalisation ≠ sympathy**, **the shutter allocates value**, **草蛇灰线 (the grass-snake and the line of ash; deferred payment)**; and rule: **is second-person narration (“你走进酒吧” ('you walk into a bar')) a breakthrough in point of view, or a rhetorical trick** — hint: check by whom “你” is assigned, and whether it can be refused.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**the three seats = a system of separate accounts for moral responsibility; unreliability = the reader is forced to serve as judge: the prototype scene of narrative ethics; focalisation ≠ sympathy = the bank of perception and the bank of evaluation keep separate accounts; the shutter = the constitution of the distribution of attention; foreshadowing = a contract of trust: deferred payment tests the credit of both parties**); the ruling on the second person (**two reference lines: the breakthrough thesis — “你” pulls the reader onto the witness stand: person is a summons, and the default spectatorship of reading is revoked (Calvino's *If on a winter's night a traveller* writes “你” as the reader in person: the question of point of view is promoted to a contract of existence); the trick thesis — “你” can at any time be reduced to a usurper of “我” or “他” (**“你” has no organ of perception of its own: it borrows the reader's body: this is forced migration, not a new continent**); the mark turns on whether one points out that “你” can be refused — **the reader can say “我不是你” ('I am not you.' (Our translation.)): a successful second-person novel = a “你” one cannot manage to refuse**); if the conclusion writes "**ethics tells you which side to stand on; the novel has you change shoes once: a person who has changed shoes is not necessarily kinder, but can never again pretend that there is only one way of walking**", that is an A.
:::
