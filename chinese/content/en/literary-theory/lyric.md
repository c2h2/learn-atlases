title: "Lyric"
---

The narrative of the previous chapter was like a river (a source, a channel, a "what comes next"); the lyric of this chapter is like **one stroke of a bell**: no plot, only **a sound swinging in time** — the theorist's task is to explain **why "not having said clearly what it is" is, on the contrary, "having said it all"**.

## Definition 1: Non-narrative logic {#def-feixushi}

::: definition Juxtaposition, leaps and suspension {#def-luoluo}
The grammatical dossier of lyric poetry: **juxtaposition** ("鸡声茅店月，人迹板桥霜" — "Cockcrow, thatched inn, moon; footprints, plank bridge, frost." (Our translation.) — **six nouns and zero verbs: the causal chain of narrative is contraband here; only images are permitted to light one another**); **leaps** ("何当共剪西窗烛，却话巴山夜雨时" — "When shall we together trim the candle at the west window, and speak then of this time, the night rain on Mount Ba?" (Our translation.) — **three tenses, the present, the future and a future recollection, crammed into one couplet: the time of a poem is a folding accordion**); **grammatical suspension** ("沧海月明珠有泪，蓝田日暖玉生烟" — "Vast sea, bright moon, pearls have tears; Lantian, warm sun, jade gives off mist." (Our translation.) — **"Is it a metaphor? A memory? A wish?" Syntax refuses to answer: the ambiguity is not a failure to write it well; it is this poem's only load-bearing structure**). Its name in theory (a popular version of Frye's *The Language of Poetry*): **the logic of poetry is an "emotion–energy" logic, not a "reference–proposition" logic — its coherence rests on tension, not on temporal order**. The exemption-from-inspection order of Chinese criticism is issued at home: Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修, in *Liuyi shihua* 六一诗话 ("Liuyi's Remarks on Poetry"), tells in his own words how he came to understand poetry from famous lines whose wording "seems not to make sense" (语似不通) — "**作者得于心，览者会以意，殆难指陈以言也**" — "What the writer gets in the heart, the reader meets with the mind; it can scarcely be pointed out and set forth in words." (Our translation.) — **some sentences have "wording that seems not to connect" (语若不通) and yet "a meaning wonderful of itself" (意自妙): the logic of juxtaposition is something Chinese criticism discovered, not imported goods**; set beside this Wang Fuzhi 王夫之's formula from the positive side: "**情景名为二，而实不可离**" — "Scene and feeling are named as two, but in fact they cannot be parted." (Our translation.) **The acceptance sentence: rewrite any line of a lyric poem as "因为……所以……" ("because … therefore …"); if the rewritten version loses no information, the original line was not a poem to begin with (this chapter's variant of the substitution method)**.
:::

## Definition 2: Who is speaking {#def-shuiwo}

::: definition The first person is a post that can be transferred {#def-zhiwei}
The narrative lesson taught that "author ≠ narrator"; the corresponding theorem of the lyric lesson is more counter-intuitive: **the "I" in a poem is a mask, not a medical record** — the mourner of the dead, the self-elegist and the bewildered speaker of "**锦瑟无端五十弦**" ("The patterned zither, for no reason, has fifty strings." (Our translation.)) have quarrelled for a thousand years **without arguing the matter down to "Li Shangyin 李商隐 himself": the "I" of the poem lets every reader take up the post**; in "**商女不知亡国恨**" ("The singing-girl does not know the grief of a fallen state." (Our translation.)) even the "I" is absent (**隔江犹唱 ("still singing across the river") is a commentator's seat with no speaker**), and only **a tone of comment** is left. **The lyric subject = a post of tone**: it is assembled from metre, allusion and forms of address (**the parallelism (parallel couplet) of the regulated poem (*lüshi*) forces out a "two-channel subject": the first line of the couplet is the official person, the answering line is the commoner's plain cloth — Du Fu 杜甫, between the two couplets, holds the post and has resigned at the same time**). **From this, the lyric's "paradox of sincerity"**: we know perfectly well that "感时花溅泪" ("Grieving for the times, flowers splash tears." (Our translation.)) is rhetoric; why are we still struck? — **because the poem does not offer evidence that "he is truly sad"; it offers a specimen of "what sadness looks like inside language": a lyric poem is not an electrocardiogram; it is a dance — a dance does not "express" exhaustion, yet no one denies that it understands exhaustion better than a yawn does**.
:::

::: definition Apostrophe: the vacant workstation {#def-hugao}
The signature move of lyric poetry is, in grammar, **a pragmatic transgression**: speaking to someone who is not there — "**子不我思，岂无他人**" ("If you do not think of me, have I no others?" (Our translation.)) (a face-to-face charge against the faithless person, who has never appeared — **the whole poem is a trial with no defendant**); to the moon ("明月几时有，把酒问青天" — "When did the bright moon come to be? Cup in hand, I ask the blue sky." (Our translation.)), to the willow, to a dead wife ("十年生死两茫茫" — "Ten years: the living and the dead, both a vast blur." (Our translation.) — **the moment one opens one's mouth one breaks the first rule of conversational logic: whom are you speaking to?**). Rendered as theory: **apostrophe gives the genre away — the "you" of a lyric poem is a vacant workstation; the moment a reader sits down in it, the commission is paid** (**the slot of "person" (人) in "但愿人长久，千里共婵娟" ("I wish only that people may long endure, and a thousand miles apart share the lovely moon." (Our translation.)): for a thousand years the contract has been signed in turn, and everyone thinks it is their own name being called**). A by-product: **"entrusting to an object" (托物) and "lodging one's meaning" (寄托)** (speaking to the fragrant plant and the beautiful person = speaking to the ruler without writing an addressee — **the politics of *bǐ* 比 ("comparison") and *xìng* 兴 ("evocation"): the pragmatic loophole of apostrophe is precisely bulletproof glass against the literary net** — the account in [[pre-qin-literature/songs-of-chu]] is wired in here).
:::

## Definition 3: Sound is meaning at a slow speed {#def-shengyin}

::: definition The rhyme position = the reinforcing bar of the content {#def-yunwei}
The previous chapter said that "content and form are an alloy"; here, at lyric poetry, the material is tested: the scene where the substitution method fails — in "**一弦一柱思华年**" ("One string, one bridge, thinking of the splendid years." (Our translation.)), replace 弦柱 ("string and bridge") with 丝弦 ("silk strings") and the floor gives way; replace 搔首沉吟 ("scratching the head and murmuring") with 搔头低语 ("scratching the head and whispering") and it goes off pitch — **once sound has taken on the meaning, the poem enters a zone that cannot be written across (Frost's definition is paid out at the lyric: the rate of loss in translation = the reading of how much poetry is there)**. A register of the mechanisms (the whole of it recovered from the lesson on metre): **rhyme** ("**the rhyme word is the fuse of memory**: the moment a rhyme is opened, the reader restocks of itself — to rhyme is to promise; to pay the promise out is pleasure"), **level and oblique tones (also called "level and deflected") and the entering tone (checked tone)** ("the entering tone is an emergency brake: feeling may have a rhyme-ending that stops dead"), **open-mouth and even-teeth finals** (the openness of 十灰 and the bleak clarity of 萧 are **colour temperatures preset at the factory**), **parallelism** ("**two lines of meaning assembled in parallel: the reader reads in the error of the alignment — parallelism is the standardised workshop of the logic of juxtaposition**"). **Conclusion: the "musicality" of the lyric is not a grace note; it is its only legitimate way of transporting meaning — this is also why the song lyric (*ci*) "is a house of its own" (别是一家) (Chapter 7), and why poetry, once it reaches the lyric, must be handed back to sound** (the overflow formula of the "Great Preface", *Shi daxu* 诗大序, to the *Book of Songs* is given away early: "**情动于中故形于言，言之不足故嗟叹之，嗟叹之不足故永歌之，永歌之不足，不知手之、足之、蹈之**" — "Feeling stirs within and therefore takes form in words; words not being enough, one therefore sighs it; sighs not being enough, one therefore draws it out in song; song drawn out not being enough, one does not know one's hands doing it, one's feet doing it, stamping it." (Our translation.) — **a slide all the way from "words" to "stamping": the far end of the lyric is the body; musicality is not an invention; it is an overflow**).
:::

::: example Dissecting "静女其姝" with three tools {#ex-jingnv}
"静女其姝，俟我于城隅。爱而不见，搔首沉吟。/ 静女其娈，贻我彤管。彤管有炜，说怿女美。/ 自牧归荑，洵美且异。匪女之为美，美人之贻。" "The quiet girl is lovely; she waits for me at the corner of the city wall. Loving, and not seeing her, I scratch my head and murmur. / The quiet girl is beautiful; she gives me a red tube. The red tube is radiant; I delight in the beauty of 女. / From the pasture, back with a shoot of thatch-grass, truly beautiful and rare. It is not you that are beautiful; it is the gift of the beautiful one." (Our translation.) ① Identify the **nature, as a post**, of the "I" (the biographical poet? a lover being played?); ② whether the **"女"** in "说怿女美" ("delighting in the beauty of 女") refers to the red tube or to the quiet girl — does this ambiguity belong to the load-bearing structure or to a fault in the construction (cite the clause on "grammatical suspension"); ③ the **logic of entrusting to an object** in the third stanza ("匪女之为美，美人之贻" — "It is not you that are beautiful; it is the gift of the beautiful one." (Our translation.) — **use the theory of apostrophe and of lodging one's meaning the other way round: not speaking of something else by way of an object, but the whole value of the object credited to the giver — the commodity pricing of feeling**); ④ a trial: **treat this poem with the three tools of narratology (the separation of the three seats, focalisation, the shutter)**, and list the exact names of the three failures.
::: solution
① **The post theory**: the Mao preface (毛诗序) enters it under the official business of "satirising the times", and so has the character who is present speak by proxy — **the commentators need a note on the background in which to settle this "I" that sings at full voice: the institutional embarrassment confirms the post theory exactly: the "I" is a vacancy; anyone can fill it; how to read it: the shift-lead may take up the post; there is no need to check an identity card**. ② **Load-bearing**: "女（汝）" drifts between the tube and the person; syntax supplies no verdict — **to remove the ambiguity is to dismantle the poem: the joy of the second stanza is precisely the grammar of the state "everything I look at looks like you" (suspension is a special effect, not an accident)**. ③ **The pricing of feeling**: the thatch-grass is "洵美且异" ("truly beautiful and rare") — **the value does not come from the thing; it comes from the relation: the economic law of lyric poetry is the opposite of the law of commodities (a thing is prized for the person: the Chinese version of gift theory — "匪女之为美" ("it is not you that are beautiful") is the earliest proposition in the whole book that "use value is conferred by someone"**). ④ The list of three failures: **the separation of the three seats fails (there is no distance between narrative voice and character: tone is everything)**; **focalisation does not apply (there is no window onto a scene: the corner of the wall is only a stage direction — the space of the poem is not got by "seeing"; it is got by "waiting")**; **the shutter fails ("搔首沉吟" is neither summary nor scene: it is the imitated sound of time stopping — the time of the lyric is the heartbeat's, not the lens's)**; **verdict: open a poem's lock with a novel's key, and what twists off is the key — each tool has the range of its licence**.
:::
:::

::: intuition A crib for the "world without self" {#int-wuwo}
Wang Guowei 王国维 sets the levels of *jìngjiè* 境界 ("world; realm"): "**有我之境，以我观物，故物皆著我色彩；无我之境，以物观物，故不知何者为我，何者为物**" — "In the world with self, one views things through the self, and so every thing takes on the colour of the self; in the world without self, one views things through things, and so one does not know which is oneself and which is a thing." (Our translation.) — **two volume-settings of the lyric subject**: with self = apostrophe, the transfer of feeling, splashing tears; without self = the lens giving up its place, juxtaposition governing itself (**in "明月松间照，清泉石上流" ("The bright moon shines among the pines; the clear spring flows over the stones." (Our translation.)) no person can be found, yet the "not finding" is itself a carefully assembled way of being present: the subject kept out of sight is the greatest display**). **Theorem: a lyric poem has no "having no point of view", only "the point of view diluted across the whole picture" — what is called objective description of a scene is the highest-privilege mode of the subjective**.
:::

::: history A constitution and its commentary {#sec-xianfa}
**Pre-Qin** "**诗言志**" ("poetry expresses intent") (the "Canon of Yao", *Yaodian* 尧典, in the *Book of Documents*, *Shangshu* 尚书 — **the first constitution of Chinese poetics; the three thousand years after it are all amendments**) + the overflow formula of the "Great Preface" (**a genesis: *qì* 气 ("vital energy") — words — sigh — song — dance**) + the start-up clause of *bi* and *xing* (**"*xing*" is institutionalised as the hermeneutics of the study of the Classics: the Mao commentary (毛传) marks "*xing*" — first admit that this place does not go by reason, then fit it with an official manual that does go by reason — the birth of the commentary trade**); **3rd–6th centuries** Lu Ji 陆机, *Wen fu* 文赋 ("Rhapsody on Literature"), "**诗缘情而绮靡**" — "Poetry follows from feeling, and is ornate and delicate." (Our translation.) — **a constitutional amendment: the account is split off from "intent" (the account of political and moral instruction) to "feeling" (the personal account): the two words "follows from feeling" (缘情) authorise the lyric to open an account of its own**; **11th century** Ouyang Xiu records "可以妙不可以理求" — "One may have the wonder; one may not seek it by reason." (Our translation.) — **the anti-reason clause is set down in writing**; **12th century** Yan Yu 严羽, "**不涉理路，不落言筌**" — "It does not tread the path of reason, nor fall into the fish-trap of words." (Our translation.) — **the anti-reason clause is raised to the programme of a school (the doctrine of wondrous awakening, *miàowù* 妙悟: the epistemology of lyric poetry = the immediate perception of Chan)**; **the 1630s–17th century** Wang Fuzhi, "情景二而不可离" — "Scene and feeling are two, and yet cannot be parted." (Our translation.) — **the ontological volume bound as one**; **1908** the theory of *jingjie* (**the two volume-settings are written into the textbooks: from then on "describing a world" and "creating a world" (写境 and 造境) are middle-school grammar**); **1920s–40s** the lyric hegemony of new poetry (modern verse) (**the Symbolist school to the Modernist school: from Dai Wangshu 戴望舒 to Bian Zhilin 卞之琳: the debate on "pure poetry" = a redrawing of the boundary between lyric and narrative/drama; Feng Zhi 冯至, *Shisihang ji* 十四行集 ("Collection of Sonnets"), makes a trial of "the meditative lyric" — sound begins to turn inward**); **1950s–70s** the main current of "**political lyric poetry**" (**apostrophe pushed to the extreme: the poet's "I" competes for the post of the age's "we": a Whitmanesque "I" put on the collective establishment — the post theory of the lyric subject is put through a large-scale stress test here**); **1980s** the counter-hiring of "**Misty Poetry** (Obscure Poetry)" (**Bei Dao 北岛's "我不相信" — "I do not believe." (Our translation.): the addressee of the apostrophe is changed for the whole official "you": mistiness = leaving the "you" slot blank again**); **now** **the industry of song lyrics takes over verse: the promise of rhyme is paid out by popular music — the prosodic heritage of old poetry goes on working in the chorus**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the lyric
**Taking the "I" for a medical record** (**the evidential-research habit of proving events from the poem: every poem is asked "is it true?" — a poem's verdad is grammatical, not a matter of the household register: ask "how the sadness is", not "whether there is sadness"**); **the prejudice that "lyric = weakness"** (**treating a genre as a grading of character: narrative = epic = the masculine, lyric = the small self = the feminine — this nineteenth-century table of military ranks was abolished long ago; the lyric force of "凭谁问，廉颇老矣" ("Who is there to ask? Lian Po is old." (Our translation.)) is no less than any report from a battle**); **taking "without self" for "without a point of view"** (**hiding the subject is the deepest operation of a stance: the "of itself, naturally" of pastoral poetry is precisely rehearsed with care**); **the all-purpose label *yìjìng* 意境 ("the realm of meaning; artistic conception")** (**the moment a poem is seen, stick on "the *yijing* is far and deep" — the official still in office from the previous chapter's blacklist: *yijing* is a description of a relation (scene and feeling, thing and self), not an exclamation mark of praise**).
:::

::: exercise Designing a suspension {level=1}
Write a short poem of four lines. The requirements: **at least one grammatical suspension** (the syntax refuses to give a verdict), **one apostrophe** (to someone who is not present), and **the words 因为, 所以 and 然后 must not appear** (the entry visa for causal connectives is suspended); in a note of your own: **point out which place is the load-bearing ambiguity, and why it cannot be rewritten into clarity**.
::: solution
Marking: **the suspension is real** (what would be lost if it were made clear must be sayable — **if it cannot be said, the ambiguity has been stuck on**); the "you" of the apostrophe **is a vacancy one can sit in** (it does not announce its own name); the absence of causal connectives meets the mark — **the whole piece bridges by juxtaposition and the rhyme position: the piers are not there, and yet the bridge is; that is a pass**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Two volume-settings {level=2}
Write "coming home from overtime late at night and seeing the moon" in two versions: **a with-self version** (viewing things through the self, so that things take on the self's colour) and **a without-self version** (each thing as it is of itself, the person withdrawn outside the picture); assess one another: **which version is more "true", and why this question is itself invalid** (cite the post theory).
::: solution
Marking: the with-self version may transfer feeling (**the moon "has got fatter", the street light "has clocked off" — a verb that crosses its boundary is the volume**); the without-self version forbids adjectives (**only nouns and words of place are allowed: juxtaposition is responsible for everything**); the account of why the question is invalid (**"truth" belongs to the household-registration system; poetry issues only a licence to "be eligible to take up the post": both versions are assemblies of tone; the difference is the volume knob, not true and false — to jump in and say which version is "more like the person himself" has already broken this chapter's first theorem**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the truth and falsity of the lyric subject {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**the 'lyric self' is the most brilliant fiction in the history of literary theory: it has no birth certificate, and yet it has drawn three thousand years of royalties**". You must draw on at least these five: **the post theory (a mask, not a medical record)**, **the vacant workstation of apostrophe**, **the overflow formula**, **the "follows from feeling" amendment**, **the competition for the post between "I" and "we" in political lyric poetry**; and give a ruling: **whether modern poetry in the manner of the confessional school, "confessing what is private", overturns the post theory ("this time it really is I myself")**.
::: solution
Marking points: the five mechanisms (**the post = a vacancy the reader can take up; apostrophe = a trial with no defendant; overflow = a genetic defence of the nature of sound; "follows from feeling" = the papers that open the personal account; "we" = the extreme case of a lyric subject put on the establishment — the post of the subject can be sublet by the collective**); the ruling on the confessional school (**two points to go by: ① it is not overturned — "confession" is a new craft of the mask: a Proustian "writing shame into syntax"; the sound is still assembled from rhyme and pause; "truth" is a sense of trust produced by rhetorical effect (that a confessional poem reads as "candid" shows precisely that it has been written successfully — not that it was not written); ② a partial revision — the confessional school pastes the "recruitment notice for the post" onto the front page of the newspaper (details of private life become part of the metre: the contract has changed, but the parties who sign are still the reader and the tone, not the reader and the biographical person) — the mark depends on whether "the truth of an effect" is distinguished from "the truth of a fact"**); if the conclusion can be written "**the poem does not guarantee you that someone has lived like this; it guarantees you this: the language of having lived like this is, at this moment, in your mouth — the letter of authorisation of a lyric poem has always been issued by the reader**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The bell-stroke report, filed: logic (**juxtaposition, leaps, suspension: coherence by tension; if a rewriting loses no information, it is not a poem**), the subject (**the "I" is a post that can be transferred: the poem does not provide an electrocardiogram; it provides a specimen**), the "you" slot (**apostrophe = a vacant workstation; the reader pays the commission; lodging one's meaning is its bulletproof glass**), sound (**the rhyme position is the reinforcing bar of the content: musicality is an overflow, not decoration**), the constitution (**expressing intent — following from feeling — not treading the path of reason — *jingjie*: three thousand years of amendments**). The method in one sentence: **ask who is on duty inside this "I", and ask which "you" has an empty seat**. The next chapter enters the theatre: narrative and lyric speak at once — drama.
:::
