---
title: "Reading and interpretation"
---

The previous eight chapters have, all the way along, asked the author to step down from the court (the intentional fallacy), taken the work apart into storeys (spots of indeterminacy), and sent society into the museum (the mirror retired) — **who is to sit in the seat that is left? This chapter opens court to try the "reader": is he the work's construction crew (engaged in advance in Chapter 4), a consumer of meaning, or a new tyrant?**

## The first court: is the author's intention the standard {#def-yitu}

::: definition Coordinates of the three schools {#def-sanpai}
**Anti-intentionalism** (Wimsatt and Beardsley, already on file in Chapter 4: **intention is "searching for a private scene inside the author's brain" — no evidence is found, and it is irrelevant to the court**); **hypothetical intentionalism** (Hirsch's counter-attack in *The Aims of Interpretation*, 1967 — **"If one denies that the meaning of a work is distinct from the meaning of its interpreter, the word 'validity' loses all its meaning" (in substance): without a separate accounting of "meaning" (meaning, what the author intended) and "significance" (significance, every effect the work has on later people), every way of reading has equal rights, and the discipline dissolves**); **the intention of the text** (Eco's court of arbitration — **"A text is not an object to be dressed up as one pleases" (*The Limits of Interpretation*, 1992, in substance): the one who rules is neither the author (he has already left the court) nor the reader (his suits are unlimited), but the *intentio operis*, the "intention of the text": the text's coherence, its internal consistency, and the degree to which it can accommodate the other parts appear in court to testify — "an open work is not a work that accepts every interpretation"**). **This lesson's reading of the coordinates**: what the three schools dispute is not "whether there is an author's intention" (there is, in the archive), but **"whose appeal has the right of final judgement" — an ontological question (where meaning lives) has quietly been exchanged for a question of procedure (who gives evidence, and who may veto)**.
:::

## The second court: the reader's work permit {#def-duzhe}

::: definition The implied reader and interpretive communities {#def-yinhang}
**Iser**: the text has built into it an **implied reader** — **not some particular person, but a "list of construction posts" reserved by the work (the job description, from Chapter 4, of the spots of indeterminacy: who is filling the blanks, when one is led astray, when one is required to reread — "the implied reader of a detective novel is 'a suspect the author has trained': it teaches you to grow excited in the wrong places"**); **Fish** goes a step further: **an "interpretive community" decides what the text is** (**"Form is not read out; it is decided by strategies of interpretation: file a stretch of gibberish under the heading 'Misty Poetry', and its mistiness then has its reasons" — misreading is not an accident; it is the normal output of different regimes of interpretation**). **An acceptance test (this chapter's method of substitution)**: take one and the same "misty poem" (file Li Shangyin's 'The Ornamented Zither', 《锦瑟》, separately in three catalogues, "a *daowang* collection" (悼亡集, poems mourning a dead spouse), "set by the poet at the head of his own collection" (自题诗集卷首), and "an anthology of love poems" (爱情诗选), and collect three readers' first reactions: **taxonomy (Chapter 1) and hermeneutics converge here: the shelf is the first interpretation**).
:::

::: definition Fusion of horizons and effective history {#def-shiyu}
Gadamer's philosophical refit: **"Fore-judgement (prejudice) is not an obstacle to understanding; it is a condition of understanding" — "We do not exist in history; history exists in us" (*Truth and Method*, 1960, in substance)**; and so **the "hermeneutic circle" is promoted from a logical flaw to an ontological structure** (**the part gains its meaning from a whole anticipated in advance, and the whole is confirmed by the parts: this is not a vicious circle; it is a business's start-up capital — you take the stock you hold at 19 to read *Dream of the Red Chamber*, and at 49 you come to check the accounts, and the books renew themselves of their own accord**); **temporal distance is not a gulf that must be crossed, but a filter** ("**Only the heat of the moment cannot be handed on: what circulates relies on misreading to sift out the classics — the philosophical headquarters of Chapter 4's 'a classic is a share issued on a rolling basis': meaning is not a mine that gets dug out, but compound interest accrued year by year — Hirsch calls it significance, Gadamer calls it effective history, and the two ledgers record one and the same sum of interest**"). Jauss's "**horizon of expectations**" (the mirror image, on the reading side, of Chapter 8's tax bill for genre: **literary history = a running daybook of the horizon of expectations being established, satisfied, broken and rebuilt — "the value of a work is often measured by 'its being an outrage against its contemporaries'" (Baudelaire, as cited by Valéry)**) — **novelty has a price only inside effective history: something that nobody on the spot thought new may perfectly well, two hundred years later, be posthumously enfeoffed as a revolution**.
:::

## The third court: a licence for misreading {#def-wudu}

::: definition Strong misreading and the canon's tax {#def-qiang}
Bloom, *The Anxiety of Influence*: **"Influence is misreading — a strong poet must perform a 'revisionary misreading' (clinamen, a deliberate swerve) upon his precursor: in the manner of Oedipus: kill the father, and file a false genealogy" (the system, in substance)** — **resentencing "misreading" from an error to the proper name of creation: tradition is not inherited; it is robbed**; and, going with it, "**the canon = the works that make us misread more: it asks much more than we do**" (the common account, as reported, of what Bloom says of *The Canonisation of Shakespeare*). The native Chinese case file: **"六经注我" ("the Six Classics annotate me"; Lu Jiuyuan)** — **the most overbearing manifesto a reader has issued: the classics are demoted to material by which one proves oneself**; opposite it is the exegetical line of **"我注六经" ("I annotate the Six Classics")** — **the Qing classicists' programme of objectivity ("由字以通其词，由词以通其道" (Dai Zhen, 'A Letter to Shi Zhongming on Learning', 《与是仲明论学书》): "From the characters one gets through to the words; from the words one gets through to the Way." (Our translation.) A dispute over the ownership of meaning, with the verdict overturned by a chain of linguistic evidence — the two schools are not a dispute about attitudes; they are a dispute in the law of evidence**); and **Jin Shengtan's coup by *pidian*** (commentary and critical punctuation) (**an "imperial authorisation" of the "Six Works of Genius" (六才子书), plus cutting *Water Margin* off at the waist: *pidian* is not criticism; it confiscates, all at once, the right to interpret, the right to cut and alter, and the right to rank — "deleting everything after the amnesty (招安) = drawing up a will for a novel: later people can only choose a side from among his legitimate heirs"**). **The lesson of the collected judgements: the "dispute between Zhu and Lu" in Chinese hermeneutics (道问学, following the path of inquiry and study, vs 尊德性, honouring the moral nature) is isomorphic with the European dispute between Hirsch and Gadamer — what both dispute is "to whom objectivity belongs": the evidential school stakes its bet on evidence, the *xingling* school (native sensibility) stakes its bet on experience; neither case has been judged, and each stays alive by the other (insight with no evidence is folk science; evidence with no insight is an archive)**.
:::

::: example A three-party hearing of "春眠不觉晓" {#ex-chunmian}
Meng Haoran, 'Spring Dawn' (《春晓》): "春眠不觉晓，处处闻啼鸟。夜来风雨声，花落知多少。" Spring sleep, and I do not notice the dawn; everywhere I hear the birds cry. In the night, the sound of wind and rain — how many flowers have fallen, who knows? (Our translation.) A hearing is opened on "what this poem is writing about": **① the intentionalist prosecution** (please summon the author: Meng Haoran's life, the dating of this poem in Meng's collected works, the Tang examinees' convention of "the meaning of the set topic" — **can one bring evidence that "the author intended cherishing the spring / lack of recognition / pure sensation"**); **② the anti-intentionalist defence** (**a search of the author's brain finds no evidence: the poem does not contain a single character "惜", and the "lack of recognition" reading rests entirely on association from the biography**); **③ the arbitral court of the intention of the text** (**a test of how far this poem coheres with texts of the "boudoir complaint" and "examination piece" kinds: in which set of conventions it is most consistent with itself**). The final ruling: **which answer may close the case, and which can only be archived**.
::: solution
① **The ceiling of intentionalism**: the life can prove that Meng Haoran "did have" a grievance at lack of recognition (**but it cannot prove that this poem "is" that grievance: relevance is not a signature**); if the dating does fall in his period of reclusion, that only **puts down a deposit on the reading as "leisure and ease"; it does not buy the reading out**. ② **The floor of anti-intentionalism**: it wins on "**no evidence found under search**", and loses on "**no counter-evidence found either**" — a pure ban on intention would void, entire, "the intertextual evidence of the author's other poems" (**too strict: a court that excludes even good indirect evidence cannot try any case**). ③ **The court of the intention of the text**: this poem **has a grievance belonging to no one (no boudoir, no ruler, no friend) — only a hierarchy of hearing (birds crying → wind and rain → the imagined arising of falling flowers)** — conventions of the boudoir-complaint kind need the furniture of "toilet table, mirror, curtain, moon" (calling back the spots of indeterminacy from Chapter 4: **this poem's list of furniture is empty: it is not consistent with itself inside the convention of "the meaning of an examination topic" (no praise and no satire), and it is most sparing of paper inside the convention of "landscape and fields-and-gardens"**) — **"a purely sensory spring morning, plus one thread of 惜 ('regret', 'cherishing') that cannot be priced, is the lowest bid of the intention of the text"**; the ruling: **archive the "lack of recognition" reading (interesting, without evidence, not able to close the case); the "cherishing the spring" reading may be judged (but the character "惜" never appears in the whole poem — the written judgement must state: this is the reader's supplementary writing, not the original wording of the text — Eco's limit and Fish's community shake hands here: supplementary writing is lawful; passing it off as the original is not)**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Intention equals neither achievement nor consequence {#def-berlin}
A stabilising valve from the history of ideas (Berlin's common sense): **"what the author wanted to do ≠ what he succeeded in doing ≠ what consequences he brought about" — three accounts, three statements, and nine-tenths of the quarrels in the history of criticism of literature and the arts come from using the wrong statement** (**"The officers who collected the songs for the *Book of Songs* wanted to observe the condition of the people (intention); the poets were making love songs (the other half of the achievement); the scholars of the Classics read out the virtue of the consorts (consequence) — all three are right, and none of the three comes under the others' charge"**). **The everyday use, in a class on reading**: once you have read, fill in the form first — **is this sentence about the author (biography), about the work (structure), or about me (effect)? A paper that fills the wrong column is called "关公战秦琼" ('Lord Guan fights Qin Qiong'); a scrappy paper that fills the right column still beats a brilliant essay that fills the wrong one**.
:::

::: history A ledger of the reader standing up {#sec-fanshen}
**Pre-Qin to Han** the clauses of interpretation are already complete ("以意逆志", using one's understanding to meet the poet's intent; "诗无达诂", "the *Songs* have no settled exegesis" — **plurality is not a modern invention; it is the daily round of classical scholarship**); **the 12th–13th centuries** Lu Jiuyuan's "六经注我" ("the Six Classics annotate me") (**a Chapter 8 preview of the reader's sovereignty**); **the 17th–18th centuries** Schleiermacher, "to understand the author better than the author himself" (**hermeneutics is promoted to a universal method: the doctrine of the circle is set down in writing — note the ambition: he wants to restore the original meaning of the text, and is still the last giant of author-centredness**); **the 1920s–50s** Ingarden's school of concretisation gets under way (**a three-in-a-row of the reader's revolution, the 1960s–70s: Jauss, 'Literary History as a Challenge to Literary Theory' (1967); Iser, *The Act of Reading* (1976); Fish, 'How to Be an Anti-intentionalist — Actually, You Don't Need To' (1971) — within ten years the "reader" rises from supporting part to leading part, and then to principal speaker (literary history is played with the reader in the lead)**); **1967** Barthes, "The Death of the Author" (**"The unity of a text lies not in its origin but in its destination" — the coronation speech of the reader as "destination"**); **1971–75** the debate between Hirsch and Gadamer (*The Aims of Interpretation* vs the third German edition of *Truth and Method* — **North America vs continental Europe: the school of method vs the school of ontology — both are wounded, and both come out whole: thereafter everyone calls himself a centrist**); **1990–92** Eco, *Interpretation and Overinterpretation* (**a hearing on "the limits of interpretation": the text will resist, but what it resists is marked out by the community**); **the 2000s** cognitive reading, neuroaesthetics, the digital humanities (**the "implied reader" begins to be sampled by eye-trackers: the reader returns to the laboratory — the black-box interface of Chapter 3 is copied on the reader's side**); **today** **algorithmic recommendation takes over the "horizon of expectations" ("a user profile = an institutionalised fore-judgement" — the recommendation system renews, in perpetuity and on your behalf, the bias you had at 19: the interest of effective history begins to be settled per click — the next hearing in the theory of reading is already at the door: when the reader has been modelled, who is responsible for misreading)**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of interpretation
**"I think" running naked** (**the reader's revolution is not a firearms licence for everyone: "I think" must be cashed into "which place in the text authorises me to think so" — Eco's limit is the safety on this chapter's chamber**); **the folk-science version of the intentional fallacy** (**reading "the death of the author" as "no need to look anything up": archival evidence remains valid for good in the sense that "intention ≠ achievement" — what dies is the right of final judgement, not the right to testify**); **effective history as good for everything** (**"how later ages read it" cannot be poured back upstream into "what it is": the virtue of the consorts is the history of 'Guanju' (《关雎》), not the semantics of 'Guanju' — unless what you are arguing is precisely this stretch of history itself**); **treating the circle as sophistry** (**the attack "you presupposed the conclusion" holds only when "the presupposition cannot be revised": go in carrying the hypothesis, and let the details change it — that is called method; refusing to change, come what may, is what is called a settled prejudice (the reader's version of Chapter 3's "the censor's volume")**).
:::

::: exercise An experiment with three catalogues {level=1}
Take a short poem that contains neither persons nor a title (you may use 'Spring Dawn', 《春晓》, or 'Climbing Stork Tower', 《登鹳雀楼》), and **file it in three catalogues** (for example "mourning the dead", "fields and gardens", "Chan principle" — you need only forge the catalogue and three neighbouring poems). Give it to three readers, and from each take one sentence of "what it is writing about". Tally: **which way the neighbouring texts pull the interpretation, and whether anyone holds out against the catalogue**.
::: solution
Marking: **the catalogue and the neighbouring items are an experimental apparatus of "intertextual context"** (making empirical Chapter 4's claim that the shelf is the interpretation); the evidence used by anyone who holds out must be logged (**most people will cite details that are not there — "supplementary writing from memory" is a direct recording of the construction crew clocking on**); the conclusion is forbidden to say "this shows that the readers are at different levels", and must state a **mechanism** ("the neighbouring items supplied the missing key to the convention").
:::
:::

::: exercise Sorting the statements into columns {level=2}
On the "problem of the last forty chapters" of *Dream of the Red Chamber*, write a short comment of 200 characters, **forcibly divided into three columns**: a column for the author's intention (Cheng and Gao, and the present state of the evidence that "the last forty chapters are a continuation / are a remnant"); a column for the structure of the work (the structural effect of "Lin Daiyu dies" as an ending, without asking who the original author was); a column for effective history (how Gao E's edition shaped "Honglou meng" as this text of the whole nation). **At the end of each column, assess yourself: what question is this column answering.**
::: solution
Marking: **the three columns must not cross into one another** (the commonest breach: arguing, in the structure column, that "Cao Xueqin would not have written it this way" — **biographical evidence breaking out of jail**); **the column for effective history must grant that "what we love is precisely this one text" and "the study of collation has another text" stand side by side without conflict ("work and edition: one aesthetic object, two documentary objects")**; a self-assessment column that writes out traces of applying "intention ≠ achievement ≠ consequence" gets full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is the reader a new tyrant? {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**what the movement to liberate the reader in the twentieth century signed at the end was a blank cheque: the one who issues it and the one who honours it are both the reader, and so 'over-interpretation' became the only ground for refusing payment — and the certificate that identifies 'excess' is still issued by the reader**". You must call on at least five items: **the coordinates of the three schools**, **the implied reader / interpretive communities**, **the fusion of horizons and temporal distance**, **strong misreading**, **the case file of "六经注我"**; and give a ruling: **whether "recommendation algorithms have institutionalised the horizon of expectations" shifts "responsibility for misreading" from the reader to the platform**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the five mechanisms (**coordinates = the dispute over the right of final judgement; construction posts and communities = the reader's double staffing; temporal distance = misreading of the sifting sort; strong misreading = another name for creation; "the Six Classics annotate me" = the native version of a declaration of sovereignty**); the reply to the blank cheque (**for reference: it is not that there is no voucher for refusing payment — the text's resistance (Eco), the consensus of the community (a brake Fish fitted himself), and the hardest of them: whether what a misreading produces supplies more testable connections than the original text does (the operational definition of "excess" = the extra cost in hypotheses added in order to save the conclusion; this is a theory of limits in the manner of economics — imperfect, but open to audit)**); the ruling on the algorithms (**the transfer partly holds: the profile replaces "the sifting done by temporal distance" with "reinforcement by instant feedback", and the reader loses surprise in Gadamer's sense — a model judgement, for reference: "the platform does not manufacture misreading; it only cancels the right to be corrected" — the remainder of the responsibility still lies with the reader: one can be fed by recommendations, and one can also be woken hungry by a single line of verse**); if the conclusion writes "**the reader's crown did not come by coronation; it was delivered at a birth: the pair of hands the text lacks on the production line — but so long as it still lacks them, the reader is forever both sovereign and hired hand: this is the best news hermeneutics has**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The three courts close and the files are put away: the author's seat (**meaning and significance kept in separate accounts — what dies is the right of final judgement, not the right to testify**), the reader's seat (**the list of construction posts, plus the regime of interpretation: misreading is the normal output of different regulations**), misreading's seat (**the canon is a heritage that was robbed; "the Six Classics annotate me" and the chain of the old glosses keep each other alive**), and the three statements (**intention ≠ achievement ≠ consequence: filling the right column is more basic than answering the question right**). The method in one sentence: **first ask which statement, then ask right or wrong; first check who authorises it, then shout what I think**. The next chapter is the last act: the modern schools — opening this chapter's court into a chain of courts across a hundred years.
:::
