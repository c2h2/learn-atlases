title: "Style"
---

Register is the uniform of the occasion; style is **a personal scent one refuses to take off**. This lesson pans the camera from 'society' back to 'the person': what style is, whether it can be classified, whether it can be learnt, and how much interrogation the old saying “文如其人” ('The writing is the person.') (Our translation.) can withstand.

## The definition of style and two pairs of scales {#sec-dingyi}

::: definition Style: the fingerprint of a system of choices {#def-fengge}
**Style = the system of choices one person has settled into a stable habit within the range a register permits** — writing likewise of parting, Wang Wei's “西出阳关无故人” ('West out of Yangguan, there are no old friends.') (Our translation.) is a level gaze, one line worth ten thousand; Wang Bo's “无为在歧路，儿女共沾巾” ('Do not, at the fork in the road, wet your kerchiefs together like children.') (Our translation.) writes the consolation as a head held high; Li Shangyin, though, with “相见时难别亦难” ('To meet is hard, and to part is hard as well.') (Our translation.), circles the pillar three times. The measurable points where it settles all lie in the old tools of the lessons: **preferences of word frequency** (Eileen Chang's love of the colour-word stock “铜锈绿” ('copper-rust green') and “月白” ('moon-white')); **syntactic fingerprints** (Lu Xun's short sentences with a residue of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*), used together with winding complex sentences; the punctuation character of Wang Zengqi's “短。极短。” ('Short. Extremely short.') (Our translation.)); **the stock of figures** (Qian Zhongshu's arsenal of metaphor vs Zhao Shuli, who almost never steps beyond the bounds of plain description (*baimiao* 白描)); **rhythmic tics**. Period style is a fingerprint enlarged: the vigorous grandeur of Tang prose and the familiar intimacy of the late-Ming *xiaopin* (小品, 'short informal essay') are not the difference between two authors; they are **a change of generation in the stock of registers and in the expectations of a theme**.
:::

::: definition Two classic pairs of scales {#def-kedu}
Of the hand-cranked dial of stylistics, the two pairs that wear best: **plain–ornate** (“清水出芙蓉，天然去雕饰” ('The lotus comes out of clear water; by nature, carved ornament is gone.') (Our translation.) vs the laying-out of colour and the plucking of pattern in the Han rhapsody (*fu*), and the place-name dictionary of 《子虚》 (*Zixu*) and 《上林》 (*Shanglin*); note that plain ≠ crude — **when splendour is at its height, it returns to the bland** is the second peak, words from Su Shi's later years); **concise–abundant** (every character of the *Spring and Autumn Annals* a judgement of praise or blame, vs García Márquez, one paragraph of which walks through three generations; the Chinese critical tradition leans towards the concise — “句有可削，足见其疏；字不得减，乃知其密” ('If a sentence can be cut, that is enough to show it is loose; if a character cannot be taken away, then one knows it is tight.') (Our translation.), Liu Xie's 'Melting and Cutting', a ten-character needle). The scales themselves carry no praise or blame; **only position has weight**: abundance out of measure is called 冗蔓 ('redundant sprawl'); concision out of measure is called 枯窘 ('barren dryness') — the scoring of style always carries the counterweight of the theme's aim (a memorial address values concision; a rhapsody values elaboration).
:::

::: example Blind test: who owns the three sentences {#ex-mangce}
Judge the owner of each of the three, and give evidence of the manner (an imitation, too, must be identified as 'the manner of such-and-such a writer'): ① “南京的鸭巷我已不大记得，只记得盐水鸭确实比别处淡些。” ('The duck alleys of Nanjing I no longer much remember; I only remember that the brined duck really was a little blander than elsewhere.') (Our translation.) ② “于浩歌狂热之际中寒；于天上看见深渊。” ('Amid the frenzy of vast song, cold strikes; in the sky one sees an abyss.') (Our translation.) ③ “月亮升起来，院子里凉爽得很，干净得很。” ('The moon rises, the courtyard is very cool, and very clean.') (Our translation.)
::: solution
① **the Wang Zengqi manner** — lyricism so pale that it has lost its adjectives; a memory of taste in the spoken tongue; the roundaboutness of “我已不大记得” ('I no longer much remember'); ② Lu Xun's *Wild Grass* — a Classical Chinese skeleton that opens on a prepositional phrase, plus paradoxical antithesis; the cold, hard metaphysics of “于天上看见深渊” ('in the sky one sees an abyss'); ③ **the Sun Li manner** (in the line of 《荷花淀》 ('The Lotus Marsh')) — plain description, plus the dialectal tone of stacking “得很” ('very'), plus short sentences as clean as a courtyard that has been washed. **A verdict may cite only countable evidence** (lexicon / syntax / punctuation); “我感觉像” ('I feel as if') is forbidden — feeling is the masquerade of a conclusion.
:::
:::

::: theorem The writing is the person: half holds, half is a debt {#rule-zhuren}
Yang Xiong said “言，心声也；书，心画也” ('Speech is the voice of the heart; writing is the picture of the heart.') (Our translation.); traditional criticism, on that ground, takes style for a receipt of moral character. The modern interrogation returns a compromise verdict: **style is like the person in 'the way they exact things from language', not in their moral accounts** — Haizi's paradoxical short sentences are of one structure with the martyrdom he truly believed in (this holds); the humble long sentences of a certain major writer are not in contradiction with the sharpness of that writer in private (a long sentence is a stylistic personality, not a passport personality). The distinction one can operate: **linguistic personality** (a system of preferences that can be proved inside the text) and **experiential personality** (the truth of the biography) — stylistics promises only the former; to convict a person by style, or to whitewash one by it, is in either case to take correlation for cause. But the 'half that is a debt' has to be paid as well: a person who writes for a long time is indeed shaped by the manner of the writing — **the probability is not low that the way you speak is the way you think about things**, which is why rhetoric has never dared to file the character 诚 ('sincerity') away.
:::

::: history From 'Style and Nature' in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* to the statistics of style {#sec-lishi}
The first system of Chinese stylistics is Liu Xie's 'Style and Nature': the **eight styles** (典雅 'orthodox elegance', 远奥 'remote depth', 精约 'tight economy', 显附 'clear aptness', 繁缛 'rich ornament', 壮丽 'stately splendour', 新奇 'striking novelty', 轻靡 'light frivolity') form axes two by two, and he throws out a proposition that has lasted a thousand years, “**才性异区，文辞繁诡**” ('Talent and nature lie in different regions, and the wording of writing is profuse and varied.') (Our translation.) — style is rooted in talent and nature, yet “学慎始习” ('In study, be careful of what is first practised.') (Our translation.), and it can also be moulded (“摹体以定习，因性以练才” ('Copy a style in order to fix the habit; follow nature in order to train talent.') (Our translation.)). Zhong Rong's *Shipin* (《诗品》, 'Gradings of Poetry') divides into grades, and Yan Yu's *Canglang shihua* (《沧浪诗话》, 'Canglang's Remarks on Poetry') takes 入神 ('entering the spirit') as the highest rank; both are descendants of this measuring-rod. On the side of Western learning, Buffon's inaugural address in 1753, on taking his seat as a member of the French Academy, threw out the famous remark “**风格就是人本人**” ('Style is the man himself.') (Our translation.) (le style n'est que l'homme même) — and claps palms with Yang Xiong across one thousand eight hundred years. At the end of the twentieth century a new weapon comes on: **computational stylistics** scores the attribution of authorship by word frequency, the distribution of function words, and n-grams (the statistical divergence between the first eighty chapters and the last forty of *Dream of the Red Chamber* has become a case in open courses; chapters suspected of joint authorship are judged to show 'a third brush', “第三支笔”) — **the fingerprint metaphor completes its digital reincarnation: from “体性” ('style and nature') to vector space, stylistics has always been the study of recognising “谁在说话” ('who is speaking').**
:::

::: warning Three delusions of style
First, **the fetish of style**: taking a verbal tic for genius (“他句子短，所以他也该短句” ('His sentences are short, so he too ought to write short sentences.') (Our translation.) — learning the style and not its theme and aim, and learning only the pores). Second, **the slippery slope of stylistic egalitarianism**: “各有各的好，无法比较” ('Each has its own merits; they cannot be compared.') (Our translation.) — scales cannot be compared, positions can: barren dryness and honed concision (简练) are two different things; the right to score lies in the evidence, not in the mood. Third, **the omnipotence of statistics**: judging an author by a fingerprint of word frequency has as its precondition that **the manner of writing and the period are controlled** (training a model on the modern vernacular (*baihua*) in order to judge Song prose is like measuring a living person's foot with the mould of a fossilised shoe-sole) — the method is a microscope, not a hammer of judgement.
:::

::: exercise Imitation and the giveaway {level=1}
With one sentence each, imitate the 'Lu Xun manner' and the 'Wang Zengqi manner', and yourself expose the two points of an imitation most apt to give it away.
::: solution
A model: the Lu Xun manner — “我家门外可以作证：有两棵树，此外便没有别的什么了。” ('What is outside my door can bear witness: there are two trees, and apart from that there is nothing else.') (Our translation.) (a Classical Chinese skeleton + cold irony + the straight face of “此外” ('apart from that')); the Wang manner — “豆腐很嫩。酱油是后加的，倒也不妨。” ('The tofu is very tender. The soy sauce was added afterwards, and that does no harm either.') (Our translation.) (short sentences + a small sure pleasure of taste + the easy kindness of “倒也” ('and yet, all the same')). A demonstration of giveaway points: the Lu Xun manner most dreads slickness (turning irony into a comic bit); the Wang manner most dreads forcing it (the moment the expression of feeling shows its head, one is out of the scene). Two imitations and two self-exposed giveaways, and that is full marks.
:::

::: exercise Testing the word-stock {level=2}
Take two short pieces by the same author (300 characters each), count the top fifteen of the high-frequency content words and function words, and test whether a 'lexical fingerprint' exists; then compare the side-by-side table with another author, and state your degree of confidence.
::: solution
Points for scoring: the counting can be reproduced (the lists are attached afterwards); the evidence for a fingerprint must land on **function words and preferences of collocation** (the class of “其实” ('in fact') / “大抵” ('for the most part') / “自然” ('naturally')), and words of subject matter must not be used to make up the numbers; the statement of confidence includes the limit of the sample size — statistical honesty is the first piece of modern training this lesson gives students of the humanities.
:::

::: exercise A consultation on style {level=2}
A brand asks two copywriters to write on the theme “老味道” ('the old flavour'). A: “时光煨旧了烟火，舌尖上的乡愁从未走远。” ('Time has simmered the hearth-smoke until it is old; the homesickness on the tip of the tongue has never gone far.') (Our translation.) B: “还是那口锅。汤比二十年前，浓。” ('Still that same pot. The soup, set against twenty years ago, is thick.') (Our translation.) Judge the style coordinates of the two pieces (a position on the two pairs of scales + fit with the theme's aim), and state the channel in which to place them.
::: solution
A = the ornate × abundant end (the authorisation for metaphor is fully open; “舌尖上的” ('on the tip of the tongue') is already public stock — a sense of staleness collides with the nostalgic aim): suited to the voice-over of a holiday film of feeling. B = the plain × concise end (“还是那口锅” ('still that same pot') starts from zero rhetoric; the single character “浓” ('thick') closes the foot of the sentence; the evidence of taste supplies itself): suited to in-store posters and packaging. Points for scoring: the coordinates, plus one 'why it suits' for each, landing on the physical properties of the medium (how long one watches and listens / the distance of a glancing read).
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: can style be taught? {level=3}
Write eight hundred to one thousand two hundred characters. Liu Xie says “摹体以定习，因性以练才” ('Copy a style in order to fix the habit; follow nature in order to train talent.') (Our translation.) (first copy a form of style so as to settle the habit, then follow temperament so as to train ability); Buffon says that style is the man himself — what, in the end, can a writing class teach? Using as evidence your own corpus from one semester (two early drafts + the current draft), identify one **teachable** micro-habit (punctuation pairs, sentence length, withdrawal from stock phrases, and so on) and one thing you judge **unteachable**, and give the boundary of your theory of teaching.
::: solution
Points for scoring: the evidence comparing the three drafts is genuine (the places changed are marked); the 'teachable' item must be an action one can carry out and check (not the empty spinning of “多读多写” ('read more and write more')); the 'unteachable' item shows an awareness of counter-examples (one case of having been taught it and not having learnt it, and one of having learnt it and been hurt instead); the ending answers the dispute between Liu Xie and Buffon, and **with reservations** — a conclusion that “可教的是戒律，不可教的是胃口” ('What can be taught is the precept; what cannot be taught is the appetite.') (Our translation.) is allowed, but it must be proved from your own case.
:::

::: summary
Style = a system of choices settled into habit within what the register permits; lexicon, syntax, figures and punctuation are its four sets of fingerprints; the two pairs of scales, plain/ornate and concise/abundant, carry no praise or blame, and position has weight; 'the writing is the person' awards the linguistic personality a half-share of the property; statistical method makes the fingerprint countable, but a microscope is not a hammer of judgement. Liu Xie's “摹体定习、因性练才” ('model a style and so fix habit; follow nature and so train talent') (Our translation.) is a syllabus from a thousand years ago, and it is still not out of date — what can be taught is precept and the eye; what cannot be taught is appetite and fate. The next lesson is the closing live practice of the whole course: to perform the surgery of rhetorical analysis on real texts.
:::
