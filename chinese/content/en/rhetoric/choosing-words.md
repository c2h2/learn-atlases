---
title: "Choosing words"
---

A dictionary is an inventory that sits still; writing is a requisition from it dozens of times a second. Choosing words has four axes: **precision, colour, sound, freshness** — the first two govern "right or not", the third "easy on the ear or not", the fourth "willing to look once more or not".

## Axis one: precision {#sec-zhunque}

::: definition Three gradations of precision {#def-zhunque}
① **Breadth of the semantic range.** “结果/成果/后果” are three stretches of one and the same axis (neutral — commendatory — derogatory); “处罚/惩处” are one light and one heavy — choosing a word begins by fixing **scale and polarity**. ② **The household register of collocations.** “发扬” lodges only with “传统/精神/民主”; “改进” takes in “工作/方法/服务” — the collocation files of [[modern-vocabulary/sense-relations]] are, at the writing end, the files for choosing words, and the case of “肩负传统” in the chapter on faulty sentences is an unregistered household. ③ **A fitting superordinate and subordinate.** “他买了水果” ('He bought fruit.') (Our translation.) is empty talk to his mother, and precise on a customs declaration — **precision is always relative to the aim of the piece**; there is no dogma that "the more specific, the better" (a poem wants “橘”, a customs form wants “柑橘属水果”).
:::

## Axis two: colour {#sec-secai}

::: definition The three coats of colour {#def-secai}
**Emotive colouring**: 聪明/机灵/狡猾/精明 are four testimonies to one and the same quality; to choose one is to cast a vote. **Register colouring**: 老公/丈夫/爱人/先生/我家那口子 — five words point to the same person, and the occasion of each is different (the whole lesson [[rhetoric/registers]]); to mix the words of Classical Chinese, “之/其/所”, into a recipe is to stir-fry in evening dress. **Image colouring**: “明晃晃、湿漉漉、米白、藕荷” come with their own strip of sensory film — where abstract words leave their seats, image-bearing words take up the post. But all three coats grow old: “可爱” was once purely commendatory, “奇葩” has already changed its face, and colour is a living ledger ([[modern-vocabulary/word-meaning]], semantic shift).
:::

::: example Three layers of dress for one circular {#ex-tongbao}
The same accident in a factory, with one principal sentence rewritten for each of three audiences: ① an internal circular ② a submission to the regulator ③ a post on a public account. The raw sentence: “车间老张违规操作，机器轧伤了手，大家注意。” ('Old Zhang in the workshop operated in breach of the rules; the machine crushed his hand; everyone, take note.') (Our translation.)
::: solution
① Internal: “成型车间张某未按规程作业，致手部受伤——各部门本周复训。” ('Zhang, of the forming workshop, did not work to the procedure, and his hand was injured — every department is to retrain this week.') (Our translation.) (precision, plus the wording in which responsibility is pursued; “大家注意” is raised to an instruction). ② For submission: “2024年3月X日，我司成型工序发生机械伤害一般事故，伤者张某，无生命危险。” ('On day X of March 2024, a general accident of mechanical injury occurred in the forming process of our company; the injured person is Zhang; there is no danger to life.') (Our translation.) (the term “机械伤害”, the grade “一般事故” — the household register of the regulatory register is complete). ③ The post: “3月7日下午，一台机器咬伤了一位老师傅的手。我们想讲讲规程背后的人。” ('On the afternoon of 7 March, a machine bit the hand of an old master-worker. We want to speak of the person behind the procedure.') (Our translation.) (personification shifts the cutting edge; a soft narrative — the aim turns from accountability to a culture of safety). The same facts, three coats; not one of them may be worn in at the wrong door.
:::
:::

## Axis three: sound {#sec-shengyin}

::: definition Syllables, level and oblique tones, and taboo sounds {#def-shengyin}
**Balancing the syllables**: Chinese favours a symmetrical "foot" — “开展调查” runs on, “开展查” will not go through; the disyllabification of verbs in official documents (“收钱” becomes “收受”, “买房” becomes “购置”) is half register and half the balance of the syllable ([[modern-vocabulary/word-formation]], disyllabification). **The natural rule of level and oblique tones**: even without studying regulated verse, one still lines a sentence up by ear — “因地制宜”, two level and two oblique, and the steps land steady; the close of a long sentence is judged by ear: **key information stands more firmly when it falls on a resonant syllable (an open final, a close on an oblique tone)**. **Taboo and homophony**: a “4” floor is changed to an “F” floor, “伞” is not given as a gift, “破财” is changed to “碎碎平安” — the rhetoric of speech sound is tied to the psychology of folk custom (for the homophonic figure, see [[rhetoric/figures-of-form]]).
:::

## Axis four: freshness {#sec-xinxian}

::: definition Defamiliarisation and its budget {#def-mosheng}
In 1917 the Russian formalist Shklovsky proposed **defamiliarisation** (остранение): automatisation brings it about that “石头不成其为石头” ('a stone fails to be a stone') (Our translation.); the duty of art is “使石头成其为石头” ('to make a stone be a stone') (Our translation.) — to lengthen the working hours of perception. At the level of choosing words, that is: avoid the vocabulary of the vending machine (“岁月静好”, “感恩遇见”), and put an unaccustomed face on an old matter (do not say “天亮了” but “东天泛起蟹壳青” — 'Day has broken' set aside for 'A crab-shell blue wells up in the eastern sky.' (Our translation.) — and one example of it done properly: “东风夜放花千树” ('The east wind by night sets a thousand trees flowering.') (Our translation.), which speaks of a city full of lanterns as flowers opening through one night). But freshness has a **budget system**: one essay's quota of unfamiliar faces is limited, and past that load the reader goes on strike; **freshness is a by-product of precision** — the case of the character “绿” in Wang Anshi 王安石's “春风又**绿**江南岸” ('Again the spring wind greens the southern bank.') (Our translation.): changed to “到、过、入、满”, none of them harmonised; at the last an adjective was called in to serve as a verb — that was not a pursuit of novelty; it was that “到/过” could not hold “看得见的春风” ('a spring wind one can see').
:::

::: history From "refining a word" to the "collocation dictionary" {#sec-lianzi}
Classical rhetoric already had its chapter on choosing words: Chen Kui 陈骙 of the Southern Song, in *Wenze* 文则 ('Rules of Prose'), by comparing things and linking them by kind, opened the way for the study of figures of speech; Liu Xie 刘勰 has a chapter to itself, "Lianzi" 练字, in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*; add the Tang writer's “吟安一个字，捻断数茎须” ('Chant one character into place, and twist off several stalks of beard.') (Our translation.) (Lu Yanrang 卢延让) — **the choice of a single character can weigh more than the whole piece**: “吟安一个字，捻断数茎须” ('Chant one character into place, and twist off several stalks of beard.') (Our translation.) (Lu Yanrang 卢延让) is the testimony of a Tang writer trading his life for a character. In the twentieth century, choosing words moved from remarks on poetry into the schoolroom: composition textbooks fit near-synonyms with a "table of distinctions"; the *Xiandai Hanyu Cidian* 现代汉语词典 ('Modern Chinese Dictionary') marks style; and the age of computing has further produced **collocation dictionaries and corpus frequency** — the dispute over “的/地”, the choice between “作出” and “做出”. Today's answer lies half in a feel for the language, and half in a database.
:::

::: warning Three diseases of choosing words
One, **near-synonym blindness**: “发挥作用” and “提高水平” are out the moment the mouth opens, and one never asks why “发挥” cannot be paired with “水平” — leave the household register of collocations unchecked, and one queues at the clinic for faulty sentences. Two, **the cancer of the stock phrase**: “在……的大背景下，以……为抓手，赋能……闭环” — a stock phrase is a vote to abstain from choosing; through a whole paragraph there is not one place of personal observation. Three, **an overload of freshness**: coinages from one end to the other (“雨很伤心地下” 'The rain came down very sadly.' (Our translation.)), Classical Chinese barging in (“吾辈打工人” will do as a joke; the moment an official document uses it, it is a fault) — **defamiliarisation is insurance on the points that matter, not a coat of paint over the whole text**.
:::

::: exercise Discerning the subtle {level=1}
Choose any three pairs, distinguish them, and make one sentence for each to prove that you can keep them apart: ① 断定/肯定 ② 违反/违犯 ③ 启动/起动 ④ 搜集/收集.
::: solution
① 断定 draws a conclusion (the evidence is sufficient); 肯定 expresses confirmation, or not denying (a matter of tone). ② 违反 goes with discipline and regulations (the object is ordinary); 违犯 goes with the law or the fundamental order (the nature is grave, and it carries a colouring of crime). ③ 启动 = to set a procedure or a project in motion; 起动 = the engine of a machine begins to run (a technical term). ④ 收集 lays the stress on gathering in what is already scattered; 搜集 lays the stress on searching about in every direction (搜 = active scanning). If the sense of the sentence you make proves itself, that is full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise New papers for an old word {level=2}
Choose one word that use has blunted (any one of “正能量”, “打卡” and “绝了”), and write 150 characters: first list three examples of its automatised use, then design for it a use that "defamiliarises it again", and explain by what mechanism the new use takes effect.
::: solution
Reference mechanisms: lowering and raising of level (use “打卡” of the heartbeat: “给心动打卡” — a big word put to a small use); revival by taking it literally (“正能量” returns to the context, in physics, of work being done — the etymology taken apart); a mismatch that grows a sense (“绝了” restores the original meaning “绝 = 断” and is used to write martial-arts fiction). Name the mechanism, and do not force the feel of the language: that is full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Editing in the field {level=2}
Revise the following copy (as few changes as possible, and state the reason for each): “在这个春暖花开的季节里，我们怀着无比激动的心情，隆重迎来了本届读书节盛大开幕。” ('In this season when the spring is warm and the flowers open, we, with feelings of incomparable excitement, solemnly welcome the grand opening of this reading festival.') (Our translation.)
::: solution
Reference: delete “在这个春暖花开的季节里” (a stock phrase, and nothing necessary tying it to the reading festival); delete “无比激动” (a stock phrase, its degree inflated and empty); “隆重迎来了……盛大开幕” is a blended construction, and the adverbials are piled up — change it to “本届读书节开幕”. A model for the marking: every cut can name the type of the lesion (the terms of [[modern-grammar/faulty-sentences]]).
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: my ledger of words {level=3}
Write from eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Collect twenty "automatic words" that you really said or wrote in the last week (high-frequency words from chat); audit each one on the four axes (precision / colour / sound / freshness); discard five, and give a replacement word and a reason for each. Finally answer: when a person keeps an account of his or her own language, what is at stake for thought? (Hint: Orwell says in 'Politics and the English Language' that the degradation of language and the degradation of thought cause each other — with how many tenths of this do you agree?)
::: solution
Points for the marking: the twenty words must really be there to check (it is enough to record for oneself a week of chat as a corpus); the audit uses the terms of the four axes, and the replacement words must be equal to "why they are better"; the ending takes **a position with reservations** on Orwell's proposition (agree or disagree, one must be able to produce a counter-example: new thought often has new words first).
:::
:::

::: summary
The four-axis mnemonic: precision checks the household register (semantic range, collocation, superordinate and subordinate); colour looks at the occasion (emotive, register, image); sound weighs the light and the heavy (syllables, level and oblique, taboo); freshness is used sparingly (defamiliarisation has a budget, and is always a by-product of precision). Classical "refining a word" and the corpus's "collocation frequency" are the upstream and the downstream of one river — where choosing words ends is not the word, but your power to tell the world apart. The next lesson draws the camera back from the word: the choice of sentence form — long and short, tight and loose, active and passive, the regular pattern and the variation.
:::
