title: "What rhetoric is"
---

The two characters 修辞 stand on the timetable of a Chinese department, and yet year after year they are mistaken for “a craft class that teaches you to write pretty words”. This lesson begins by rectifying the name: **the study of rhetoric is a science of “choice” — language gives one something to choose; whether the choice is right, and on what grounds one calls it right.**

## Rectifying the name: beginning from “修辞立其诚” {#sec-zhengming}

::: definition Rhetoric and the study of rhetoric {#def-xiuci}
The Chinese “修辞” has existed since antiquity. In the “Wenyan” 文言 (‘commentary on the words’) on the Qian hexagram of the *Zhouyi* 周易 (*Book of Changes*), “修辞立其诚” (‘Cultivating one’s words establishes one’s sincerity.’) (Our translation.) originally means the cultivation of speech and the steadfast keeping of sincerity. The Western word rhetoric comes from Aristotle, and its original sense is “在任何给定情境中发现可用的说服手段” (‘to discover the available means of persuasion in any given situation’) (Our translation.). The two lineages join into the definition of the modern discipline — **the study of rhetoric investigates the regularities of researching and adjusting language use so as to suit the purport and the situation** (题旨情景): the charter clause of Chen Wangdao 陈望道’s *Xiucixue fafan* 修辞学发凡 (‘An introduction to rhetoric’), “修辞以适应题旨情景为第一义” (‘The first principle of rhetoric is to suit the purport and the situation.’) (Our translation.). Three key words: **choice** (the language system provides a stock; rhetoric is choosing within that stock), **fitness** (there is no absolutely good sentence, only a sentence that is right for the occasion), **effect** (rhetoric that does not achieve its effect is a piling-up of ornaments).
:::

::: theorem Three lines of defence: what rhetoric is not {#rule-fangxian}
① **Rhetoric ≠ ornate embellishment.** “他走了” (‘He has gone.’) (Our translation.) in an obituary is rhetoric of the highest grade (it suits the purport of “respect and grief”); piling up “溘然长逝化作夜空中最亮的星” (‘He departed in a moment and became the brightest star in the night sky.’) (Our translation.) is, on the contrary, a breach of propriety — **adjustment** (passive rhetoric, 消极修辞: accurate, concise, smooth) and **ornament** (active rhetoric, 积极修辞: figures of speech, imagery) are the two wings of rhetoric, and a teaching tradition that makes much of ornament and little of adjustment is to favour one side of the subject. ② **Rhetoric ≠ a complete inventory of figures of speech.** A figure of speech is the historical crystallisation of choice, not choice itself; the language of the essay ‘Beiying’ 背影 (‘The sight of a back’), which uses no figure of speech at all, is rhetoric of the first rank. ③ **Rhetoric ≠ the opposite of grammar.** “语法问通不通，修辞问妥不妥” (‘Grammar asks whether it gets through; rhetoric asks whether it is apt.’) (Our translation.) — “她死了。死得很轻。” (‘She died. Died very lightly.’) (Our translation.) is grammatically acceptable and rhetorically brilliant; “美丽的悲伤” (‘beautiful sorrow’) is grammatically acceptable, and rhetorically it depends on the occasion. **Three levels of judgement: fitting the grammar → fitting the logic → fitting the situation. The study of rhetoric sits on the third level.**
:::

::: example One sitting, three questions {#ex-sanzuo}
For one and the same event (the teacher announces that the monthly examination is cancelled), write one sentence each: for a class meeting of the students, for a parents’ group, and for a work report to the headteacher — and mark, for each sentence, the judgement of purport and situation.
::: solution
Class meeting: “月考取消——大家先别急着欢呼，我们把复习计划升级一下。” (‘The monthly exam is cancelled — don’t rush to cheer; we’ll upgrade the revision plan.’) (Our translation.) (purport: reassurance + guidance; situation: peers present, spoken language, a high allowance for humour); parents’ group: “月考已取消，后续安排将另行通知，请各位家长放心。” (‘The monthly exam has been cancelled. Further arrangements will be notified separately. Parents, please rest assured.’) (Our translation.) (purport: information + calming; situation: formal and written, the other party is expected to be anxious, and the two characters 放心 are rhetoric aimed at the symptom); report: “本学期起优化过程性评价，原月考安排相应调整。” (‘From this term, process assessment will be optimised, and the original monthly-exam arrangement will be adjusted accordingly.’) (Our translation.) (purport: the wording of policy; situation: answerable to those above; softening “取消” (‘cancel’) into “调整” (‘adjust’) is both euphemism (讳饰) and a requirement of institutional register). **Not one of the three sentences is “pretty”, and all three are good rhetoric.**
:::
:::

::: definition Two schools and one tradition {#def-liangpai}
Within the study of rhetoric in modern China there is a division that has both a head and a proper name: the **figure school** (格义派: taking figures of speech as the framework, a learning of inventories that compiles the “ninety-seven patterns”, carrying on the “catalogue” tradition of studying figures since the Southern Song *Wenze* 文则, ‘Rules of writing’) and the **school of principles and methods / the adaptation school** (义法派/适应派: taking purport and situation as the framework; *Xiucixue fafan* brings it to completion: a division into passive rhetoric and active rhetoric, with figures of speech demoted to “special cases of active rhetoric”). The three stock axes used in textbooks today, “a compilation of words and phrases + sentence patterns + figures of speech”, are the common denominator after a compromise between the two schools. A small test for telling the schools apart: if a book’s table of contents is “比喻/比拟/借代……” (metaphor and simile / personification (and its reverse) / metonymy …), it belongs to the figure school; if it declares at the outset “适合题旨情景” (‘suit the purport and the situation’), it belongs to the *Fafan* school — **the two schools are not a matter of right and wrong; they are a telescope and a landscape painting**.
:::

::: history The four lives of rhetoric {#sec-siming}
**Classical sprouting:** Confucius’s “辞达而已” (‘In wording, it is enough to convey the meaning.’) (Our translation.), the “修辞立其诚” of the *Zhouyi*, and the cutting away of floating verbiage in the “Rongcai” 镕裁 chapter (‘Casting and pruning’) of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* — there was no discipline, only scraps of gold and shards of jade in poetry talks and pointed comments. **The modern turn:** school textbooks turned “methods of composition” into exercises (the 1921 *Zuowen yigui* 作文轶规, ‘Informal rules of composition’); in 1932 *Xiucixue fafan* set up the charter of “purport and situation”, and the name “the study of rhetoric” was fixed. **The age of standardisation:** in 1951 *Yufa xiuci jianghua* 语法修辞讲话 (‘Talks on grammar and rhetoric’) brought rhetoric into the national project of “writing Chinese that gets through”; from then on rhetoric shared a desk with faulty sentences and shared an examination paper with punctuation. **The age of crossing boundaries:** after 1980, pragmatics, stylistics, cognitive linguistics and communication studies all came to take a share — advertisements, the arena of public opinion and diplomatic press conferences became laboratories of the new rhetoric. The overarching theme of the four lives does not change: **how speech can be worthy of the occasion and of what one means.**
:::

::: intuition “修” is not “饰” {#int-xiu}
At the “修” in “修辞”, a reader today thinks at once of “beautifying”. But in earlier Chinese “修” is first of all **putting in order and adjusting** — repairing a roof is not painting the roof; it is mending it until it does not leak. The passive rhetoric of *Xiucixue fafan* (clear, smooth, even) is precisely this craft of “not leaking”: delete one superfluous word, adjust one word order, change one register, and the text goes from “able to be read” to “repaying the reading”. **Great rhetoric leaves no trace:** all florid wording is a sticking plaster, and real rhetoric is often a matter of returning the sentence to the shape it ought to have — the sentence “我买几个橘子去” (‘I’ll go and buy a few oranges.’) (Our translation.) in ‘Beiying’ has not one character of rhetorical artifice, and it is all rhetoric.
:::

::: warning Three disorders of naivety
First, **the ornament disorder**: turning “rhetoric” into “a contest in copying out fine words and fine sentences”, and stuffing compositions with “岁月静好” (‘years quiet and fine’) and “星河滚烫” (‘a river of stars, scalding hot’) — stock words that do not answer the purport are a vending machine of language. Second, **the figure disorder**: on reading “他放下行李，也放下了心” (‘He put down his luggage, and put down his heart as well.’) (Our translation.), one cannot tell the double simulation (双拟) apart and does not analyse the effect, and only shouts “比喻！” (‘Metaphor!’) — recognising the figure is not the study of rhetoric; arguing the effect is. Third, **the inspiration disorder**: “写作靠感觉，规则误英雄” (‘Writing depends on feeling; rules lead the hero astray.’) (Our translation.) — feeling itself is the deposit of long training in choice. The whole ambition of this lesson is to spread that “feeling” on the table and inspect it.
:::

::: exercise Three versions of an advertisement {level=1}
Write three lines of copy for the same vacuum flask: the market of older people, outdoor circles, and gifts for the office building — one sentence each — and state the judgement of purport and of register.
::: solution
For reference: older people, “一口热的，从早暖到戏散” (‘One hot mouthful, warm from morning until the opera lets out.’) (Our translation.) (situation: morning exercise and Chinese opera (*xiqu*), spoken language, benefit = lasting); outdoors, “零下二十度，第一口水是烫的” (‘Twenty degrees below zero, and the first mouthful of water is scalding.’) (Our translation.) (purport: reliability at the limit, numbers as testimony, detail of bodily feeling); a gift, “他加班到十点，杯子替他记得喝水” (‘He works overtime until ten, and the cup remembers for him to drink water.’) (Our translation.) (a ledger of personal feeling in which the recipient is not present — the character 替 is the eye of the rhetoric). Full marks if the verdicts on the three sentences are on target.
:::
:::

::: exercise Finding “rhetoric one cannot see” {level=2}
Read the selected passage of ‘Beiying’ (the passage in which the father buys oranges), and list at least three rhetorical means in it that **take effect without the help of any figure of speech**.
::: solution
Points for reference: delay and slow motion in the narration (“他用两手攀着……” a chain of verbs step by step — ‘clambering with both hands …’; the rhythm of the plain sketch (白描) is the feeling); insertion of colloquial direct speech (“我买几个橘子去” — ‘I’ll go and buy a few oranges.’ (Our translation.) — the original colour of a character’s language enters the text); restraint in number and colour (“黑布马褂” ‘black-cloth magua’, “深青布棉袍” ‘dark-blue cloth padded robe’ — dark colours laid as a ground let “橘” (‘orange’) shine; rhetoric of colour that does not use a single metaphor). Three such places, plus an argument of the effect, earn full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: who is a person of rhetoric {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Choose a stretch of real speech from around you (mediating a quarrel in a family group chat / barrage comments / a sales livestream — any one), and argue that “人人都是修辞人” (‘everyone is a person of rhetoric’) (Our translation.): point out at least five identifiable points of choice (why this word, this sentence, this emoji, this modal particle), and assess how well each fits the purport and the situation. The language data must be marked with their time and occasion.
::: solution
Marking points: the data are real and can be checked; points of choice ≥ 5, each supplied with a comparative argument of “why the alternative was not chosen” (the method of the study of rhetoric is precisely **to prove that a choice once existed**); the assessment of fitness takes a restatement of the purport as its baseline; do not hand down unanalytical verdicts of the kind “他口才真好” (‘His eloquence really is good.’) (Our translation.).
:::
:::

::: summary
Rhetoric = making a choice, in the stock of the language, for a particular purport and situation, and answering for the effect. “修辞立其诚” gives the ethics, “适应题旨情景” (‘suiting the purport and the situation’) gives the method, and “修是整治不是化妆” (‘修 is putting in order, not putting on make-up.’) (Our translation.) gives the temperament. The figure school and the *Fafan* school are a telescope and a landscape painting. Once the three lines of defence (the three naiveties of ornament, figures and inspiration) have cleared the ground, the lessons that follow can settle to the work: choosing words, choosing sentences, figures of speech, sound-patterning, register and style, all the way to performing analytical surgery on a real text.
:::
