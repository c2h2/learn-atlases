title: "Sound and rhythm"
---

Chinese carries a piece of musical luggage with it: syllables cut to a regular measure, tones, and finals that can be looked up in the standard books. This lesson takes stock of three resources of sound patterning — **rhyme, tone and metre** — and then looks at how they practise their trade on different ground, in the two cities of poetry and the slogan.

## Rhyme: the anchor of the echo {#sec-yun}

::: definition The mechanism of rhyme, and its ledger {#def-yatie}
**Rhyme** = the use, at the ends of a number of lines (or at a fixed place within the line), of characters with the same final, so that a periodic echo produces the pleasure of "foreseen before it arrives — paid out when the hit lands": **rhyme is a memory prosthesis come by through hearing** (oral culture relies on it to carry the *Book of Songs* and the epics; the clarity of the syllable in [[sounds-of-mandarin/speech-sounds]] earns compound interest here). The ledger of poetic rhyme differs from dynasty to dynasty: the 206 rhymes of the *Qieyun* 切韵 ('Cut Rhymes') system were the statutory rhyme stock for regulated verse (recent-style poetry) (the Pingshui rhymes merge them into 106; see [[phonology/rhyme-books]]); the *qu* rhymes of *Zhongyuan yinyun* 中原音韵 ('Rhymes of the Central Plain') split the level tone into yin and yang and distributed the entering tone among the three tones ([[phonology/to-mandarin]]); singer-songwriters today use the thirteen *zhe* 辙 of Putonghua (standard Mandarin) and the Pinyin finals directly — **a rhyme dictionary is a compressed history of pronunciation; to choose a rhyme is to choose an age**. How dense or how sparse the rhymes of a lyric are is reckoned on an abacus of its own: dense rhyme in the chorus becomes a hook, sparse rhyme in the verse does the narrating; a change of rhyme is the feel of a section that changes its breath. **Alliteration and same-final pairing** (*shuangsheng* 双声 and *dieyun* 叠韵) are the micro-rhymes inside the word: alliteration = the same initial (“参差” cēn cī); same-final pairing = the same final (“窈窕” yǎo tiǎo; the “玄黄” of “我马玄夫” in the *Book of Songs* is 'sick and exhausted', a same-final pairing that has become a word) — *lianmian* words (联绵词) are for the most part fossils of alliteration and same-final pairing, welding the aesthetics of the syllable fast into the shape of the word ([[modern-vocabulary/morphemes-words]], the entry on *lianmian* characters).
:::

::: example The scene of the sound evidence {#ex-zhengjv}
From *Pipa xing* 琵琶行 ('Song of the Pipa'): “大弦嘈嘈如急雨，小弦切切如私语。嘈嘈切切错杂弹，大珠小珠落玉盘。” The thick strings hammer like urgent rain; the thin strings murmur like speech in private. Hammering and murmuring, they are plucked all in a tangle; big pearls and little pearls fall onto a jade plate. (Our translation.) List its crew of sound-builders.
::: solution
① Reduplication: 嘈嘈 (the thick string, heavy hammer-blows), 切切 (soft speech, fragmentary plucks) — the AA pattern of depiction comes with a volume parameter of its own; ② the engineering of the change of rhyme: in the first four lines “雨” and “语” link on, round-mouth finals and thin sounds, and at “盘” there is a sudden shift to an open final — **the mouth's aperture on the final is the mixing desk of volume**, and pearls falling on a jade plate must be taken up by "an"; ③ “嘈嘈切切” comes out again, and the three-character oblique-tone ending of “错杂弹” strikes a pause — onomatopoeia, recurrence and rhythmic cadence, three crews starting together, are what one gets in exchange for the synaesthesia of "pearls falling on the plate". This is Bai Juyi 白居易's construction log; every line of it can be checked.
:::
:::

## Tone and metre: level and oblique, and the foot {#sec-tiao}

::: definition Level and oblique: fitting a rise and fall to the writing {#def-pingze}
The binarisation of the four tones is a simplifying stroke of genius in the study of rhythm in Chinese: **level (the level tone, long and steady) against oblique (rising, departing and entering, short and abrupt, high and low)** — level and oblique tones (also called level and deflected). During the Yongming era of the Southern Qi, Shen Yue 沈约 (441–513) discovered the four tones and advocated the "theory of sound-faults" (the ban on the eight faults); by the Early and High Tang this had been codified as two iron rules, "opposition" (对: within one couplet the level and oblique tones are reversed) and "adhesion" (黏: from couplet to couplet the level and oblique tones carry on) — the tonal links between lines — and from then on regulated verse had a scale (the detail is in [[phonology/mc-finals-tones]]; the practical working of the rules is in [[poetry-metrics]]). **Prose does not speak of opposition and adhesion, but it still pays the bill for level and oblique**: four-character idioms (*chengyu*) favour the four-syllable template that sets “平平仄仄” (level-level-oblique-oblique) against “仄仄平平” (oblique-oblique-level-level) (“高山流水”, “地久天长”); a level tone at the foot of the sentence leaves a lingering resonance (suited to closing), an oblique tone cuts short (suited to imposing authority); the "sinking at the end of the sentence" in an announcer's delivery is in fact the departing tone taking charge. **The foot** (a group of syllables) is the second metronome: the "pause" of modern verse (the three-three-five breathing of “轻轻的我走了，正如我轻轻的来” Gently I have gone, just as gently I came. (Our translation.)), the "two-two-three" seven-character post of slogans, and the five-character, three-pause form of the “发展是硬道理” type (Development is the hard principle. (Our translation.)) — **the essence of rhythm is regular repetition plus a foreseeable breach**; level and oblique, the foot, and the rhyme word are three metronomes set side by side.
:::

::: definition The twin cities of musicality: poetry and the slogan {#def-shuangcheng}
The same set of resources of sound patterning serves the aesthetic in **poetry** (rhyme may be sparse, may change, and may deliberately leave the rhyme so as to produce a stumble — the discord of leaving the rhyme is itself rhetoric) and serves memory in **the slogan** (policy slogans use rhyme as a memory card: “车到山前必有路，有路必有丰田车” When the cart reaches the mountain, there is sure to be a road; where there is a road, there is sure to be a Toyota. (Our translation.) Anadiplosis (顶真) plus rhyme: a double insurance. Chinese advertising is fond of the "four-four" and "seven-seven" templates and of end-rhyme, because **words that neighbour one another by a rhyme are misremembered by the brain as closely related** (the psychological "rhyme-is-truth" effect: a rhyming maxim is judged more credible — the aesthetics of sound intervene directly in belief, and the experimental literature has already seen replications). "A rhyming lie is quickly said; a truth that does not rhyme is a labour to say" — this is the ethical dark side of sound patterning, and the next lesson, the one on register, will make the outcome plain.
:::

::: history From "the note follows the prolongation" to the "rhyme tables of the new poetry" {#sec-lishi}
The 'Canon of Yao' (尧典) in the *Book of Documents* (*Shangshu* 尚书) (the book completed in the Warring States, a retrospective record fathered on antiquity) already has a one-sentence general programme: “**诗言志，歌永言，声依永，律和声**” — "Poetry expresses intent; song prolongs the words; the note follows the prolongation; the pitch-standards harmonise the notes." (Our translation.) — draw language out and it becomes song, match the sounds and they become the pitch-standards: a construction drawing unchanged for three thousand years. Shen Yue's theory of the four tones (around the 480s) was the first time Chinese historical phonology fed the project of poetry in return; after the *Qieyun* was entered in the record in 601, poets handed in their homework against the rhyme dictionaries for one thousand three hundred years (in 1941 *Zhonghua xinyun* 中华新韵 ('New Chinese Rhymes') grouped the rhymes by modern pronunciation: the official consolidation onto a single track, that of "changing over to rhyme by Putonghua"). Over its hundred years the new poetry (modern verse) has followed two lines: the lingering musical resonance of the **Dai Wangshu 戴望舒 'Rainy Alley' (*Yuxiang* 雨巷)** kind (“她彷徨在这寂寥的雨巷” She wanders to and fro in this lonely, still rainy alley. (Our translation.) — a recent symphony of same-final pairing and recurrence) and the industrial sound-patterning of **recitation poetry and song lyrics** (how many "pauses" there are to a line became the core measure in the metrics of modern verse — He Qifang 何其芳's "theory of pause-counts", the 1950s). Rap lyricists today have revived this old craft with dense rhyme and internal rhyme: **the rhyme word has never gone out of date; it has only changed the stage it plays on.**
:::

::: warning Three prohibitions in sound patterning
First, do not **let rhyme harm the sense** (a "rhyme-word accident"): forcing a line into being in order to rhyme on "ang", “伟大的辉煌照亮了前方的方向” The great splendour has illuminated the direction ahead. (Our translation.) — rhyme is a servant; let the flavours cross, and host and guest change places. The illness of doggerel has never been a failure to rhyme; it is **rhyming until the rhyme has become an automatic association-machine**. Second, do not fall for **the rhythmic illusion of press-release prose**: a whole text of "four-four opposition plus end-rhyme"; solemnity has not been stored up, while the ridiculous has, on the contrary, filled the warehouse (the modern version of the debate between parallel prose (*pianwen*) and free prose, [[rhetoric/figures-of-form]]). Third, do not **let onomatopoeia loose without restraint**: “噼里啪啦轰隆隆” (a clatter and a boom) spread without distinction over every genre but children's literature — use a resource of sound in the wrong register, and it is the same as bringing your own loudspeakers to a funeral.
:::

::: exercise A check-up on level and oblique {level=1}
Mark the level and oblique tones of “无边落木萧萧下，不尽长江滚滚来” (Boundless, the falling leaves come soughing down; endless, the Yangtze comes rolling on. (Our translation.)) — modern pronunciation is acceptable — and say which rule it satisfies, and what effect the tones at the line-end have.
::: solution
The modern-pronunciation version: 平平仄仄平平仄／仄仄平平仄仄平 (“不” was an entering tone and is now departing; “滚” is rising — the rising tone counts as oblique) ✓ it makes a complete “对”; at the line-end, “下” (oblique) drives a pause and “来” (level) sends the sound far — the upper line cannot draw the close in, the lower line can let it open out; the urgency of the falling leaves and the remoteness of the Yangtze are read off from the sound first. A correct marking plus the two explanations is full marks (note: in the old pronunciation, entering-tone characters of the “陌” and “质” rhymes and the like count as oblique; to go by the Pingshui rhymes is stricter; all of these pass).
:::

::: exercise Mixing engineer for the slogan {level=2}
Rewrite “请给老年人让座，注意扶梯安全” (Please give up your seat to elderly people, and mind the safety of the escalator. (Our translation.)) as two metro announcements, each of no more than 14 characters, rhyming and appropriate in register, and explain your choice of where the rhyme falls.
::: solution
For reference: ① “您让一个座，暖一程车” You give up one seat, and warm one stage of the ride. (Our translation.) (uo/e, neighbouring rhymes in a cross-rhyme; the warm register of colloquial speech); ② a scheme for the young-age register, “握紧扶手，稳稳走走” Hold the handrail tight, and walk on nice and steady. (Our translation.) (the ou rhyme used in a stack; usable on a parent-and-child special train; a little soft for the main line); a fair copy for reference in the solemn register, “先下后上，文明礼让” Off first, then on; be civil, and yield. (Our translation.) (the ang rhyme is solemn; many closes on the departing tone; the crisp, instruction-giving type). An explanation of where the rhyme falls that touches any one of the three — aperture, tone, register — passes. A failed attempt is allowed: worse than being slick is having no scheme.
:::

Choose one Chinese lyric from your playlist (eight lines or more), count the density of the rhyme words and the places where the rhyme changes, judge how the rhyme scheme fits the function of the sections, and pick out one place of "harming the sense for the sake of the rhyme" or praise one place where "a difficult rhyme succeeds".

::: summary
The four metronomes of sound patterning — rhyme (a periodic echo, plus the mixing desk of how wide the mouth opens), tone (the opposition and adhesion of level and oblique; the line-end decides the drawing-in and the letting-out), metre (the pause-count of the foot; regular repetition plus a foreseeable breach), grain (the micro-rhyme inside the word, alliteration and same-final pairing). Poetry tunes them to an aesthetic use; the slogan tunes them into a memory card; the "rhyme-is-truth" effect reminds us that what is pleasant to hear and what is truly so are two independent tracks. The previous lesson was form, this lesson is sound, and the next lesson leaves the sentence and enters society: register — who is speaking, on what occasion, and in what capacity.
:::
