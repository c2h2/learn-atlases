title: "Analysing rhetoric"
---

The tools of the eight lessons before this one are all on the table. This lesson teaches only one thing: **how to perform, on a stretch of real discourse, an operation that can be checked**. Analysis is not "I feel that it is good"; it is "here it made this choice; the mechanism is thus; the effect is thus; the evidence follows".

## Methodology: three steps, one case sheet {#sec-fangfa}

::: definition Description—explanation—evaluation {#def-sanbu}
**Step one, description**: identify, one by one, the points of choice in the text (word, sentence, figure of speech, sound and rhythm, register, signals of style) — record the facts only; passing a judgement is forbidden. **Step two, explanation**: each point of choice answers three questions — *why this was chosen* (the question pressed upon it by the aim and the situation (题旨情景)), *why the alternatives were not chosen* (**prove that a choice once existed**; the point on which marks are most often lost in this lesson), *how the mechanism runs* (which line of which lesson: is the focus at the end of the sentence? are the level and oblique tones at the foot of the sentence? does the metonymy depend on the stock?). **Step three, evaluation**: score the effect and write down **the scoring criteria** (does it convey the meaning? is it appropriate (得体)? does memory retain it? can the audience's response be observed?). One case sheet, five columns: extract of the material | point of choice | mechanism | alternative | effect and criteria — when all five columns are filled, the operation counts as complete; an "appreciation" made only of adjectives is the same as having made the incision and written no case notes.
:::

::: definition A decision tree for figures of speech {#def-panding}
Faced with a suspected figure, ask in order: ① Has the meaning **moved house**? If the move relies on **similarity** → the metaphor family (a word of comparison present = simile; a judgement frame = metaphor; only the vehicle left = implicit metaphor); if, by "**treating as**", it goes straight into the action → personification (and its reverse); if it relies on **relatedness** → metonymy; ② is there **speech beyond the measure**, and is the intention open → hyperbole; ③ is it **travelling in reverse**, and is there a reading → irony; ④ does one rope **hang two hooks** → pun; ⑤ is only the **pattern** moved (symmetry, recurrence, steps) → the figures of the hall of form. A model precedent: “他是我们村的气象台” ('He is the weather station of our village.') (Our translation.) — ① moved house ✓ similarity ✓ → the metaphor family → the “是” judgement frame → **metaphor** (forecasting the weather = a metonymy for the authority of speech ✓, two layers nested; pass no judgement on the second layer — **being able to judge one figure, and not judging two, is the restraining virtue of a precedent**).
:::

::: example A demonstration of the operation: one policy slogan {#ex-shufa}
“绿水青山就是金山银山。” ('Green waters and green mountains are gold mountains and silver mountains.') (Our translation.) — apply the table above, and cut.
::: solution
Points of choice: ① the frame of the judgement sentence “A就是B” (metaphor + a tone of categorical assertion; the character “就” welds the equivalence shut, and leaves no concessive seam for “像” ('like')); ② the parallel pairing “绿水青山/金山银山” (modifier–head structures in mirror image, colour against colour, the treasures of nature against the treasures of the human world — an antithetical account of **exchanging space for time**); ③ the reappearance of “山” set against “山水” (a chain-like feel in the manner of anadiplosis (顶真); the same character recurring = the slogan's incised mark); ④ zero connectives (causality and condition are staked entirely on the juxtaposition — the highest grade of parataxis (意合); the judgement is the argument); ⑤ the syllables 4+2+4, the level-tone ending “山” set far off (a sustainable lingering resonance is contracted to the open sound an of the rhyme). Proof by alternative: if it were changed to “保护生态能促进经济繁荣” ('Protecting the ecology can promote economic prosperity.') (Our translation.), the terms would be correct, but there would be **no picture, no symmetry, no incised mark** — calculate the communication ledger once, and it loses. Effect: parallelism + metaphor + categorical assertion, the three as one, compressing a complex policy into a **spatialised truth** that can be recited, written out and painted on a wall. The dispute comes along with the mechanism as well: an equative judgement sentence states the relation “可以转化” ('can be transformed') as the identity relation of “就是” ('is, precisely') — **rhetorical intensity and logical precision are an explicit exchange-rate pair**. This is exactly the remark the evaluation column should write: do not judge whether it is right or wrong; judge **what it bought and what it paid**.
:::
:::

::: definition Four case-sites: speech, advertisement, news, song lyric {#def-sianchang}
**Speech**: check the disposition of the three appeals (ethos, the construction of identity — the density at which self-naming and a record of career are put in; pathos — story, apostrophe, metrical feet driving the swell; logos — data and frames), and check **the affordances of the live scene** (the rate at which repetition and answering echo are heard again in the ear determines the rhetorical mix; the place where a written script read aloud is bound to crash is the evidence); **Advertisement**: check the axis of appeal (informational / conversion), the grammatical packaging of the promise (the disclaimer slope of “有助于” ('is of help towards'), the comparative trap in which “更” ('more') has no baseline), intertextual current-theft (a **parasitic rewriting** of four-character idioms (*chengyu*), song lyrics and famous sayings — the rhyme of a familiar line is a ready-made loan of attention); **News**: check the system of transitivity (“警方使用武力” ('The police used force.') (Our translation.) as against “冲突中有人受伤” ('Someone was injured in the conflict.') (Our translation.) — agent shown or hidden = the assignment of responsibility), the grading of quotation (the politics of distance in direct / indirect / free indirect speech), nominalisation (“降价” ('a price cut') becomes “优化价格策略” ('optimising the pricing strategy')) — the inventory of systemic functional linguistics is equally sharp in Chinese ([[modern-grammar/sentence-patterns]] the camera position of the *bei* passive); **Song lyric**: the meshing of rhyme-density with the syllables of the melody, and function words making way for vowels — when lyric and tune fight for the beat, meaning yields its place; the degree of opening of the first character of the chorus = the high note's partner.
:::

::: history From the Great Preface to critical discourse analysis {#sec-piping}
Systematic interpretation of texts in China begins with the study of the *Book of Songs*: the Great Preface (*Shi daxu*) has “诗言志” ('poetry expresses intent') fix the aim, and the *Mao Commentary*'s (*Mao zhuan*) method of reading off “赋比兴” (*fu*, *bi*, *xing*: exposition, comparison, evocation) is the earliest **decision tree for figures** (a thousand years later, Zhu Xi 朱熹, in *Shi jizhuan* 诗集传 ('Collected commentaries on the *Songs*'), still annotates line by line on this basis); the **interlinear comments and eyebrow comments** of Jin Shengtan 金圣叹 and Zhiyan Zhai 脂砚斋 are a formal invention of rhetorical analysis in Chinese — the analysis exists in the form of a parasitic text, on the same screen as the object of the operation. On the Western side, Aristotle's three appeals are the all-purpose starting point; the New Rhetoric of the twentieth century (Wayne Booth redefining rhetoric by “认同” ('identification')) widened the battlefield from persuasion to **identification** (identification: rhetoric is the grammar of saying “我们是一伙” ('we are one crew') (Our translation.)); after the 1970s, critical discourse analysis (Fairclough) institutionalised the audit of power as a routine procedure — the wording of news, the scripts of institutions and the syntax of agendas can all be questioned in court. The stance of this lesson: **the four case-sites are a hybrid of the whole spectrum** — the decision tree is the *Mao Commentary*'s, the three appeals are Aristotle's, the audit of power is Fairclough's, and the dissection of slogans is Chinese's own.
:::

::: warning Four prohibitions in analysis
The first is **label-sticking analysis**: “运用了比喻的修辞手法，生动形象” ('It uses the rhetorical device of metaphor, vivid and graphic.') (Our translation.) — zero mechanism and zero evidence, which is the same as not making the incision and only reporting the knife-count; the second is **over-interpretation**: pinning a charge on every function word (reading “了” as “作者对时代的告别” ('the author's farewell to the age') — a tense particle will not take that blame) — **every judgement must be falsifiable**: only when you can say “若此处换X则此判不成立” ('If X were substituted here, this judgement would not hold.') (Our translation.) does a case stand as filed; the third is **leaving the genre**: holding a product launch to the standard of a eulogy — the occasion is wrong, and every exquisite analysis is a handsome wrong paper; the fourth is **no ethics column**: analysing persuasive technique without marking its dose of manipulation (the "anxiety–antidote" formula in the court of advertising, the "*bei*-passive disclaimer" of a circulated release) is to write the report of the operation as a post that plants a craving — **the professional ethic of the school of rhetoric: the scalpel must write clearly which way it points**.
:::

::: exercise A complete operation {level=2}
For one slogan, line of a song or advertising line that you often use, do a full case on the five-column case sheet (no fewer than 5 points of choice). In the "proof by alternatives" column, each point must give a wording that could replace it and state why that wording loses.
::: solution
Points for marking: points of choice ≥ 5 and spanning ≥ 3 layers of tools (word / sentence / figure / sound / register); the alternatives really exist (the replacement sentence can stand, but loses on the communication ledger); the mechanism is named down to the lesson; the evaluation column contains a checkable criterion you have set yourself — if any column is missing, send it back.
:::
:::

::: exercise The debating court: manipulation or rhetoric {level=3}
Choose one disputed persuasive text (sales patter that moves goods / a public-interest advertisement that frightens / a golden line from a campaign). First analyse the mechanism, then open an "ethics court": the prosecution points to its points of manipulation, the defence pleads that "the audience can refuse", and you write the judgement — the criteria must be explicit (of the three, transparency of information, reversibility and vulnerable groups, use at least two).
::: solution
Points for marking: the mechanism column and the ethics column stand apart and are not mixed (write the knife-work clearly before you speak of the wound); the judgement uses explicit criteria and can be reused on a new case; give an empirical rebuttal, or empirical support, of "the audience can refuse" (the cost of refusal in the media situation); the conclusion may allow a grey zone, but must draw the position of the red line as you judge it.
:::
:::

::: exercise A revision paper for the whole course {level=2}
Without looking through your notes, and from memory, list for each of the nine lessons one proposition to "carry with you" (a single sentence), and with three of those propositions analyse one and the same micro-text of no more than 30 characters.
::: solution
A reference stream of propositions: rhetoric = choice, and a fit with the aim and the situation (1) / the four axes of choosing words, accuracy first (2) / sentence pattern is a stance, and end-weight is a follow-spot (3) / metaphor is a load-bearing wall, not fireworks (4) / form = order prepaid, and the business of the decoding rate (5) / four metronomes, and pleasant to hear is not the same as true and exact (6) / a breach of appropriateness (7) / style is a fingerprint, a personality in language (8) / the three questions of analysis + proof by alternatives (9). Three propositions and one text = a suturing operation that passes; being able to point out the tension between propositions (for example freshness vs appropriateness) gains extra marks.
:::

::: summary
The three steps of analysis: description only records, explanation must prove that "a choice once existed", evaluation displays the criteria first; the decision tree can judge figures of meaning and figures of form in one net, genre is the first line of the judgement document, and the ethics column is the rhetorician's licence to practise — **the scalpel must write clearly which way it points**. With this, all nine lessons of rhetoric are complete: from the charter of "what rhetoric is", through the six procedures of choosing words, choosing sentences, figures, sound and rhythm, register and style, and at last standing on the operating table. The stock of the language (the vocabulary course), the skeleton (the grammar course), the acoustics (the two courses on phonetics and phonology), the script (the course on Chinese characters) — you have seen them all. Rhetoric is the hand that brings them onto the table together. The next stop goes forward by the map: argumentative writing — when rhetoric walks from "saying it well" into "telling the reason so that it wins".
:::
