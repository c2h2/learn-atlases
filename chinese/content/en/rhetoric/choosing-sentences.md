---
title: "Choosing sentence forms"
---

Words are bricks; sentence form is the bays of the house and the light that comes into them. The same lot of words, set in another sentence form, meets another fate: **the long sentence is precise, the short sentence is sharp; the tight sentence draws up in ranks, the loose sentence carries the breath; the active holds the knife, the passive receives the blade; the regular form is the road, variation is lightning.**

## Long and short {#sec-changduan}

::: definition How long and short sentences divide the field {#def-changduan}
A **long sentence** relies on layers of attributives and adverbials to say a thing 'with every seam closed' — legal, academic and scientific writing depend on it to delimit a concept (“因不可抗力不能履行合同的，根据不可抗力的影响，部分或者全部免除责任” — 'Where a contract cannot be performed owing to force majeure, liability is exempted in part or in whole according to the effect of the force majeure.' (Our translation.) — not a single semicolon may be moved); a **short sentence** relies on the break to make speed and emphasis — the home ground of the emergency order, of poetry, of colloquial speech and of the eulogy (“失火。速来。” 'Fire. Come at once.' (Our translation.)). Writing falls ill in two directions: **long-sentence cancer** (three layers nested in a single sentence, and the reader loses contact inside the subordinate clauses — the harm of Europeanisation; see below) and **a mania for short sentences** (the whole piece in telegram style, every connective deleted, the thought forced to assemble itself). The masters' technique is precisely **alternating long and short**: “曲曲折折的荷塘上面，弥望的是田田的叶子。” ('Above the winding lotus pond, what fills the view in every direction is leaves spread thick upon the water.' (Our translation.) (*Hetang yuese* 荷塘月色 ('Moonlight over the Lotus Pond') lays the scene out in a long sentence, then suddenly “叶子出水很高，像亭亭的舞女的裙” — 'The leaves stand high out of the water, like the skirts of dancing girls, tall and poised.' (Our translation.) — is sent forward pause by pause) — an old saying in the study of rhythm: **if a long sentence is short of breath, break it; if a short sentence is too hurried, carry the breath through.**
:::

## Tight and loose, active and passive {#sec-zhengsan}

::: definition Three pairs of variables in sentence form {#def-sandui}
A **tight sentence** (整句: structure the same or closely similar, syllables even — antithesis and parallelism drawn up in ranks) excels at momentum and at sequence — “横眉冷对千夫指，俯首甘为孺子牛” ('With fierce brows I coldly face a thousand pointing fingers; head bowed, I am glad to be an ox for the children.' (Our translation.)); a **loose sentence** (散句: uneven in length, free in structure) excels at naturalness and at flow. Good writing **uses the loose to carry the tight**: a tight sentence is a parade, a loose sentence is a march — all parade, and it goes stiff; all strolling, and it scatters. **Active and passive** are a choice of viewpoint ([[modern-grammar/special-constructions]] the 'camera and stance' of the *bei* passive is reused here): “失误造成了三百万元损失” ('The error caused a loss of three million yuan.' (Our translation.)) makes the calling to account unambiguous; “三百万元被损失了” ('Three million yuan was lost.' (Our translation.)) has no grammatical standing at all — the passive has conditions of admission of its own. **In choosing a sentence form, pass the gate of grammar first, and only then talk of rhetoric.** **Affirmation and negation, and regular order and inversion** (“怎么了，你？” — 'What's wrong, you?' (Our translation.) — inversion is feeling jumping the chain of command) each form a set of their own, and there is only one principle: **the place of the focus** — the end of a Chinese sentence is a natural spotlight; whoever ought to be seen sits in the last row.
:::

::: example Four camera positions for the same news {#ex-sijiwei}
Raw material: “昨日某化工厂排放超标，附近河水出现死鱼。” ('Yesterday a certain chemical plant discharged over the limit, and dead fish appeared in the river nearby.' (Our translation.)) Rewrite it as: ① a self-inspection report from the plant ② a letter of accusation from an environmental organisation ③ a spoken television bulletin ④ a poem (the viewpoint is free to choose).
::: solution
① “昨日系统检修期间，出水口数值出现短时波动，公司已启动核查。” ('During yesterday's overhaul of the system, the readings at the outlet showed a brief fluctuation, and the company has started a check.' (Our translation.)) (the agent is hidden away as “数值波动” ('a fluctuation in the readings') — nominalisation plus passivisation that takes the guilt out, a classic use of the *bei* passive in official documents); ② “超标排放！昨天，这条河被投了毒——死鱼是证词。” ('Discharge over the limit! Yesterday this river was poisoned — the dead fish are the testimony.' (Our translation.)) (the patient placed first, plus the moral frame of “被投毒” ('was poisoned'), opening on a cry); ③ “昨天，一家化工厂排放超标，河水里出现死鱼，居民反映强烈。” ('Yesterday a chemical plant discharged over the limit, dead fish appeared in the river, and residents responded strongly.' (Our translation.)) (opening on a time word, active narration, the short pauses of speech); ④ “鱼翻白的时候/厂区的灯还亮着” ('When the fish turned belly-up / the lights of the plant were still on.' (Our translation.)) (juxtaposition with no connective — a montage of images, the relation handed to the reader). **Not one sentence in the four versions is a 'falsehood', and the four versions are already four worlds** — grammatical proof that sentence form is stance.
:::
:::

::: history A century of Europeanisation: the long sentence invades, and a reconciliation {#sec-ouhua}
Around 1919, Fu Sinian 傅斯年 cried out for the making of “欧化的国语” ('a Europeanised national language') — argumentative prose needed the close-woven machine of European grammar; half a century later Yu Guangzhong 余光中 went through the ledger, and *Zhongwen de fanyiti* 中文的翻译体 ('Chinese in Translationese') rebuked the 'pronoun disease, the noun disease, the adjective disease': “对……进行了调查” ('carried out an investigation of …') and “作出重要的贡献” ('made an important contribution') bury the advantage of the Chinese verb under the cushion of an all-purpose verb plus a noun. Today the account is half settled: **restrictive long attributives have landed as legitimate** (academic language cannot be written without them), and **the abuse of “进行/加以/给予” ('carry out / apply / give') has been recognised as padding**. Yu Guangzhong's *Tingting na lengyu* 听听那冷雨 ('Listen to That Cold Rain') showed a way to the reconciliation: Western layer set on layer, and the rhythm of the Chinese short sentence, smelted in the same furnace — Europeanisation is not the crime; Europeanisation that does not understand the mother tongue's own voice is.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of sentence form
One, **addicted to forcing a frame**: “通过……使……” ('by means of … make …') and “随着……的……” ('along with the … of …') are long sentences spinning in neutral (the case of the drowned subject, in the chapter on faulty sentences, is a repeat offender here) — a prepositional frame is scaffolding, not the house; two, **a compulsion for tight sentences**: whatever the matter, it must be “一方面……另一方面……” ('on the one hand … on the other hand …'), and two paragraphs cannot even be begun without 'on the one hand' — the thinking of an outline passing itself off as structure; three, **abuse of the *bei* passive**: “他被看见了这只鸟” ('He was seen this bird.' (Our translation.)) is syntactically illegal — the agent of “看见” ('see') is in the nature of the case a person, and cramming it onto “他” ('he') leaves the two of them with no bed to lie on; check the relation of agent and patient for yourself first, and only then talk of rhetoric; four, **the ending loses its focus**: information that ought to be seen is buried in a long attributive (“由市级部门在三年前启动的、以社区为单位的、覆盖十万家庭的试点已经评估完毕” — 'The pilot launched by a municipal department three years ago, taking the community as its unit and covering a hundred thousand households, has already been evaluated.' (Our translation.) — the point “评估完毕” ('the evaluation is complete') is moved forward: **end-weight is rhetoric; end-lightness is an accident**).
:::

::: exercise Tuning long and short {level=1}
Rewrite the long sentence as a group of short sentences, and then say in which situation the long form is used and in which the short: 该校对因家庭困难而未能参加上周校外研学活动的学生进行了名单复核并安排补活动。 ('The school rechecked the list of students who, because of difficulties at home, had been unable to take part in last week's off-campus study activity, and arranged a make-up activity.' (Our translation.))
::: solution
The short-sentence version: “名单复核完了。没参加研学的孩子，学校另安排一次。” ('The list has been checked. For the children who did not go on the study activity, the school will arrange another.' (Our translation.)) (a notice in the parents' group: short sentences + rhotacisation (*erhua*) + no technical terms at all). The long sentence is for an official document to be filed (responsibility, scope and conditions locked shut in a single sentence); the short version is for reaching people — the marking points: the rewrite keeps the three elements of fact (hardship / absence / making it up), and a reason of register is given for each.
:::
:::

::: exercise Practice with camera positions {level=2}
For “电梯里有人抽烟” ('Someone is smoking in the lift.' (Our translation.)), write two sentences in each of three forms: a notice from the property management, a satirical sketch, and a passive style of accusation.
::: solution
Notice: “电梯为封闭公共空间。吸烟者将被纳入监控取证。” ('The lift is an enclosed public space. Smokers will be brought under surveillance and the taking of evidence.' (Our translation.)) (short sentences + the agent made plain + the consequences); sketch: “总有人把两平米的铁箱子当成自家的烟囱。” ('There is always someone who takes a two-square-metre iron box for the chimney of his own home.' (Our translation.)) (a metaphor + “自家的” ('one's own') as an irony upon the limit of ownership); the accusatory form: “早高峰的电梯被一支烟占领了。我们被允许屏住呼吸。” ('The lift at the morning peak was occupied by a single cigarette. We were permitted to hold our breath.' (Our translation.)) (the ground colour of 'suffering' in the *bei* passive + the irony of “被允许” ('were permitted') — a legitimised use of the online pattern of the *bei* passive). Full marks when the three forms bear one another out in stance and in sentence form.
:::
:::

::: exercise The bomb-disposal expert {level=2}
Take the long sentence apart and cure it (keeping every piece of the information): 针对目前存在的部分员工在未经审批的情况下擅自使用个人账户为公司收付款项的现象财务部门决定从本月起开展专项清理。 ('With regard to the phenomenon, now current, of some employees who without approval and on their own authority use personal accounts to receive and pay money for the company, the finance department has decided to carry out a special clean-up from this month.' (Our translation.))
::: solution
A reference version — split into three sentences: “近期，部分员工未经审批，使用个人账户收付公司款项。该行为违反财务制度。财务部决定自本月起开展专项清理。” ('Recently some employees, without approval, used personal accounts to receive and pay the company's money. This conduct violates the financial rules. The finance department has decided to carry out a special clean-up from this month.' (Our translation.)) (three faults in the original: the subject is held off at a long distance by the prepositional frame “针对……现象” ('with regard to the phenomenon of …'); for want of a dividing pause, “现象财务部” ('phenomenon' and 'the finance department') stick together; the chain of verbs is overloaded — break the sentence and set the parts back in place, and it is cured). Full marks for naming the lesions accurately and losing nothing of the meaning.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: sentence form and truth {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Collect one passage each from three texts of the same event (celebrity gossip or a public event will both do) — a press release, a commentary and a joke — and analyse how their arrangement of sentence forms (long and short, active and passive, tight and loose) has in each case 'manufactured' one side of the facts. Answer this: if sentence form is stance, does objective reporting exist? You must use this lesson's case of the 'four versions of the news' as a comparison.
::: solution
Marking points: statistics of sentence form for the three texts that can be reproduced (any two of mean sentence length, the proportion of *bei* passives, and the density of tight sentences); the mechanism of stance is laid bare, and not with cynicism (objective ≠ the absence of a sentence form; objective = an honest proportion between sentence form and the aim of the piece); the case of the 'four camera-position versions' is cited, and is carried further.
:::

::: summary
Long and short are rhythm (precision and sharpness each have a battlefield; the masters earn their living by the alternation); tight and loose are the formation (use the loose to carry the tight; the parade cannot take up the whole of the route); active and passive are camera positions (the spotlight falls always on the end of the sentence — end-weight is rhetoric, end-lightness is an accident); the regular form and variation are expression (inversion is feeling's report that jumps the chain of command). A century of Europeanisation tells us this: the stock of sentence forms will gain in capacity, but every degree of that close weave may charge interest against the voice. The next lesson enters the main hall of the gallery of figures: metaphor and simile — the oldest subject in the study of rhetoric, and the one that has been studied the most wildly.
:::
