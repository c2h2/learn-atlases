---
title: "*Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*"
---

The fox immortals of the previous chapter spoke for people on the stage; in this chapter they move into the study — **what *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio* (*Liaozhai zhiyi* 《聊斋志异》; hereafter *Liaozhai*) invents is not the writing of ghosts. It is the cultivation, for an age not permitted to speak, of a whole set of speakers who cannot be suppressed: the fox, the ghost, the dream, and the Historian of the Strange (异史氏). A flourishing of literature under constraint is often a species explosion of rhetoric.**

## Definition: three forms compounded into one book {#def-santi}

::: definition The Tang writers' method, a Six Dynasties subject, the situation of the early Qing {#def-fangfa}
**The common account cites Lu Xun's 鲁迅 verdict in *Zhongguo xiaoshuo shilüe* 《中国小说史略》 ('A brief history of Chinese fiction')** (“用传奇法，而以志怪” — eight characters: 'using the method of the Tang tale, and yet recording the strange' (Our translation.) — **which set out this book's formula clearly:** the subject matter of tales of the strange, *zhiguai* (gods, ghosts, foxes and demons), plus the technique of the Tang tale, *chuanqi* (writing the person, writing the detail, writing the dialogue, writing the mind); **the result is that the Six Dynasties kind of thing, “丛残小语” ('fragmentary scraps of talk'), is packed into the psychological depth of Tang fiction** (Lesson 12, “方法是借的，题目是新的” — 'the method is borrowed; the subject is new' (Our translation.); and it is also a reproduction, at the level of literary form, of Chapter 3's “以故为新” ('making the new out of the old')); **why this formula appears in the early Qing** (written by those formed through the examinations, written by resident tutors, written by those who did not obtain their will in the examinations — **a capacity for the long sentence, trained to the limit and with nowhere to be used, found a subject that did not need to pass the censor** (the “受理名单” ('list of cases accepted for hearing') at the end of Chapter 7 is updated here: **the netherworld, and the foxes and ghosts, are the only route of advancement in the Kangxi reign that sets no limit on the number of places**)).
:::

## Conditions of production: writing a book in someone else's house {#def-shengchan}

::: definition Resident tutoring, the examination yard, and solitary indignation {#def-zuoguan}
**The record** (**the common account: Pu Songling 蒲松龄 (1640–1715), courtesy name Liuxian 留仙, literary name Liuquan 柳泉, already in childhood made his name by taking first place on the list, *anshou* 案首, in the county examination, and yet was repeatedly defeated in the provincial examination; he tutored in residence, *zuoguan* 坐馆 ('keeping a tutor's post in a patron's house'), in the household of Bi Jiren 毕际人 for about thirty years, and only at the age of seventy and more did he finally go out as a tribute student, *gongsheng* 贡生 (the thread of the common account) — **this book was written in the patron's study and in lodgings on the road:** short pieces, in Classical Chinese (*wenyan*), which could have the brush set down at any moment: **the conditions of production determine the form** (the advanced version of the economy of paying calls on patrons in Chapter 4: **he did not sell poems in exchange for banquets; he sold his writing in exchange for thirty years of board and lodging, and then wrote these nights into a book**); ***Liaozhai zizhi* 《聊斋自志》 ('The author's own account')** (**a quotation in the common account:** “集腋为裘，妄续幽冥之录；浮白载笔，仅成孤愤之书：寄托如此，亦足悲矣” — 'Gathering underarm fur to make a robe, I rashly continue the records of the netherworld; I drain the cup and charge the brush, and have completed only a book of solitary indignation: to lodge one's meaning thus is itself enough for grief.' (Our translation.) — **note that the preface's introduction of itself uses the vocabulary of Sima Qian 司马迁 and Han Yu 韩愈** (“孤愤” 'solitary indignation', “发愤” 'venting pent-up purpose'): **the lowliness of the genre (tales of the strange) and the grandeur of the ambition (continuing the *Zhuangzi* and the *Sao*, that is *Encountering Sorrow*) collide in the same sentence** (the specification of this book's entire tension: **on the one hand it calls itself useless; on the other it demands to be taken as a Classic**)); **the age of manuscript copies** (**the common account: in his lifetime it circulated in manuscript within a circle of literati friends; Wang Shizhen 王士禛 (Yuyang 渔洋) was known for having written verses on it; old accounts of whether the friendship of the two was deep or shallow are mostly forced elaborations; this lesson takes only the thread of the common account; the Qingke Pavilion imprint of the Qianlong reign (1766) was the first to spread widely (the common account) — **a book completed, and not printed:** publication delayed by sixty years, which instead won it the aura of a “秘本” ('secret copy') (the format of distribution in Chapter 1: **first draw a circle round the readers, then become a classic: the manuscript copy is social life, the printed edition is the market**)).
:::

## A taxonomy of foxes and ghosts {#def-fenlei}

::: definition Three kinds of speaker {#def-sanfa}
**① The fox = the other whose identity can be taken off** (**she may take the initiative, she may be wealthy, she may live without marrying** (the common account: fox women have much wealth, much knowledge, and a gift for talk — **to love a person who has no place on the household register is to go round the whole system of household registration** (**the sociological meaning of the fox: she is that share of freedom outside the registered households** (the reverse of Chapter 6's “第三方栏” ('third-party column'): **the fox is not entered on the roll, and therefore does not have to pay the bill**))); **② The ghost = the appellant who has not been filed** (**those who bear a wrong, those who failed the examinations, substitutes who were drowned — **the existence of a ghost just is an unclosed case** (Chapter 1, “团圆＝司法宣告” ('reunion = a judicial pronouncement'): the reason *Liaozhai* has so many ghosts is that verdicts are too few**); **③ The Historian of the Strange = the author's double who disclaims liability** (**the common account: the “异史氏曰” ('The Historian of the Strange says') at the end of each piece imitates “太史公曰” ('The Grand Historian says') — **the craft of the historian is put to use, at a lower rank, as the fiction writer's lifebuoy:** when the story does not dare to deliver the verdict itself, it has a fictional witness called “异史氏” sign (Chapter 6: the double who signs a name; Chapter 9, “说教是叙事焦虑的止痛药” ('moralising is the painkiller for narrative anxiety'): **the painkiller really is a medicine: it let readers in the Qing dare to leave this book on the desk**).
:::

::: definition The fox's gender ledger {#def-xingbie}
**The merit** (** *Yingning* 《婴宁》 writes a person by means of laughter** (the plot in the common account: she laughs all the year long, up to “虽故逗之亦终不笑” — 'even if one teases her on purpose, in the end she never laughs' (Our translation.) — **a woman is written as a kind of sound, and not as looks:** and the fiction lets this sound be 'cured' by marriage: **this is the most honest, and the most unsettling, sentence-form in *Liaozhai*** (the Qing version of the “沉默的婴儿” ('the silent infant') clause in Chapter 5: **we have remembered the laughter, and we have also remembered the ending in which there is no laughter**)); **The compensation** (**the appeals in *Cuzhi* 《促织》 ('The cricket') and *Xi Fangping* 《席方平》 are lodged by men against the institution; the debts of kindness in *Xiaocui* 《小翠》 and *Jiaona* 《娇娜》 are repaid by fox women — **Pu Songling enters “德性” ('virtue') in large amounts on the women's side of the ledger, and leaves “申诉” ('appeal') to the men:** this is not modern equality; it is the largest sum a short piece in Classical Chinese can give (Chapter 2, “丽娘把处境说成语言” ('Liniang speaks her situation into language'): the women of *Liaozhai* can often speak, and yet are not free**).
:::

## Anatomy of three pieces {#def-sanpian}

::: definition 'The Cricket': one insect's balance sheet {#def-cuzhi}
“宣德间，宫中尚促织之戏，岁征民间。” ('In the Xuande years, the palace favoured the sport of crickets, and levied them yearly from among the people.' (Our translation.)) (a quotation in the common account) — **the opening sentence fixes the demand side in the palace and the cost side among the people:** the cause and effect of the whole piece have already been declared at the ninth character (the Classical Chinese version of Chapter 3's algorithm, but this time the cause is the institution and not a person**); **The accounts** (**the *lizheng* 里正 ('village head'), the *youxia* 游侠 ('knight-errant'), the crab-shell insect, the transformation of the soul of Cheng Ming's 成名 son into a cricket; the plot in the common account: the son's soul becomes an insect and fights well, is presented to the throne, and is rewarded:** this is the most appalling substitution of assets in Chinese literature: **a person's value can be realised only by turning into an insect that can win** (Chapter 6, “以物的价值反推人的价格” ('inferring the price of a person back from the value of a thing'): for the first time, on the books, a life is entered as a pet**); **the sentence “独是成氏子以蠹贫，以促织富” closes the account** ('It is only that the son of the Cheng family was impoverished by the bookworm and enriched by the cricket.' (Our translation.)) (the gist of a line cited in the common account) — **the absurdity is not the plot; it is a misplacement in the chart of accounts** (**the poverty and the wealth of one and the same family exist only because the heading was changed from “读书” ('study') to “虫” ('the insect')**).
:::

::: definition *Xi Fangping*: the higher the level of hearing, the more corrupt {#def-xifangping}
**The plot in the common account** (**the father is extorted to death; Xi Fangping enters the netherworld to bring the suit: he is passed along through the city god, the prefectural office and King Yama, and undergoes every cruel punishment without changing; exactly the opposite of *The Peony Pavilion* and its “冥府比县衙讲理” ('the nether court talks reason better than the county yamen') (**two political theologies of the same subject, about a hundred years apart:** in 1598 one could let the judge talk reason; around 1700 one could only let the plaintiff hold out under the torture (a measurable pessimisation of literary history: this is the contraction curve of Chapter 7's 'list of cases accepted'**); **Technique** (**a persistence of the kind “席不肯归，只愿一讼” — 'Xi will not go back; he wishes only to pursue the one suit' (Our translation.) (the gist of the common account) — **the protagonist's aim is not to win the suit; it is to carry the procedure through to the end:** both the level of hearing and the torture become evidence he is to record (Chapter 8, “程序是弱者唯一付得起的武器” ('procedure is the only weapon the weak can afford'), and here the bill is the body (** *Xi Fangping* goes further than *Wu Song* 《武松》: Wu Song builds his own court; Xi Fangping maims himself to obtain evidence**); **A resolution, at the end, of the “天恩” ('Heaven's grace') type** (**the plot in the common account: the Lord on High (上帝) tries the officials, and father and son return to life — **criticised as feeble; this lesson reads it as a realistic writing of the institution:** in Pu Songling's universe, justice can arrive only in the form of a pardon: it still has to come from above (the last shot fired for Chapter 1's “团圆＝宣告” ('reunion = a pronouncement'), and the most powerless one**).
:::

::: definition *Siwenlang* and the examination ghost {#def-siwenlang}
**The plot in the common account** (**a blind monk smells essays with his nose in place of his eyes, and can tell high from low; after the results are posted, the inferior essay passes and the fine essay is rejected — **the examination system is handed to a blind man to judge, and then even the blind man is overruled** (the ultimate answer to Lesson 1's “谁给文章定价” ('who sets the price of a piece of writing'): **when the judging cannot be trusted, criticism can appeal only to a sense that is impossible** (**a nose smelling an essay is the best “外行” ('layman') declaration in the history of literary criticism:** it admits that every aesthetic judgement is only a physiological response, and thereby refuses to be reconciled with the examiners**)); ***Ye Sheng* 《叶生》** (**the common account: the scholar's talent is high and he meets with no recognition; his soul follows the one who knows him; he passes, and only then learns that he is already dead — **how strong an obsession does a failed candidate need, before his soul will go on revising** (**“考运” ('luck in the examinations') is written, in *Liaozhai*, as a mishap in the netherworld:** when an institution loses its legitimacy, the first place it is questioned by ghosts is in fiction (the examination version of Chapter 4's Xin Qiji 辛弃疾, “把不能写成梦见” ('writing what one cannot do as something dreamed'): **Pu Songling writes not-passing as a not-passing that continues after death:** this, and only this, is the deepest solitary indignation in the whole book: even death does not solve the problem**).
:::

## 'Painted Skin' and visibility {#def-huapi}

::: definition Dissecting the irony {#def-fengci}
**The plot in the common account** (**the common account: the beautiful woman is in fact a yaksha; she puts on a human skin and paints it; Scholar Wang is pleased and harbours her; a Daoist detects her “妖气” ('demonic *qi*'); he understands and returns; the door is shut and the heart breaks; a mad beggar, with spittle, transforms a substance into a heart and sets it in the breast (the gist) — **the technical verdict** (**① the title comes first:** the reader is told from the first line that the 'skin' exists; the suspense of the narrative is not 'who is she' but 'when does he find out' (Chapter 3's spoiler clause: **after the spoiler, all that remains is a comedy of cognition**); ② **the capacity to see is allotted to the least presentable** (the Daoist, the mad beggar), while the victim is a man of letters who owns property (**a parallel operation to Chapter 7's “孔尚任把最高唱段给身份最低的人” ('Kong Shangren 孔尚任 gives the highest aria to the person of the lowest status'):** Pu Songling issues epistemological privilege to people on the margin, and leaves stupidity to the gentry — this is the highest grade of the literati's satire upon themselves**); ③ **the 'heart' made into a thing** (**spittle is spat and becomes a heart; it is set in the breast and he revives:** abstract virtue is written as an organ that can be transplanted: **this is the blackest stroke in the whole piece:** it hints that the 'heart' was, from the start, something that came from someone else (**read to this point, the nose of *Siwenlang* 《司文郎》 ('The officer in charge of letters') and the heart of 'Painted Skin' (*Huapi* 《画皮》) make up a complete science of satire, in *Liaozhai*, directed at the faculties of the body**).
:::

::: example The extremely spare and the extremely full reckon the same account {#ex-liangzhang}
'The Cricket' writes “征虫—赔虫—魂化—进贡—赏赐” ('levying the insect — making good the insect — the soul's transformation — presenting it as tribute — the reward') in several hundred characters in all; *The Plum in the Golden Vase* may use two chapters to write a single funeral. From each, choose one place of “花费” ('expenditure'), and fill in: **① the unit of account, ② who bears it, ③ what the reader is allowed to see**; and answer: **why, for one and the same account, Classical Chinese closes on shock, and the vernacular (*baihua*) closes on elaboration**.
::: solution
**① The unit** — **'The Cricket':** an insect's victory or defeat = taels of silver = the price a life is converted into; ***The Plum in the Golden Vase*:** silver, cloth, the feast as laid, monks and Daoists, scriptures and rites of repentance: **the former chain of conversion is short to the point of cruelty; the latter is long to the point of the trivial** (**length itself is ideology: trivial matters do not make way for death**); **② Who bears it** — **'The Cricket':** the household of Cheng Ming, and it is paid by the son; ***The Plum in the Golden Vase*:** shared out across the whole family, each person pays a little, and therefore no one is held to account (**the dispersal of an institutional cost: two ways of filling in Chapter 4's 'third-party column'**); **③ Visibility** — **'The Cricket':** the reader sees a ghost (the soul transformed), because the short piece in Classical Chinese needs one metaphysical verdict; ***The Plum in the Golden Vase*:** the reader sees only the accounts (**the discipline of disenchantment in the long vernacular novel: it refuses to supply a supernatural explanation** (Chapter 6: its fall, too, does not depend on the will of Heaven**); **The general judgement** — **the Classical short piece is fast, and within a few hundred characters can drive a person to the point of “非人不可” ('no way through but to cease to be a person'):** shock is the rightful ending of the short piece; the long vernacular novel is slow, and can dilute one bad act into two chapters of the everyday: **the commonplace is the rightful ending of the long form** (each of the two forms chose one shape of evil: **extortionate levies, and the domestic ordinary: these are the two ends, on the moral spectrum, of *Liaozhai* and *The Plum in the Golden Vase* — **taken together they make up the two account books of Qing fiction, one recording death, one recording depreciation**).
:::
:::

::: intuition A species explosion of rhetoric {#int-zhonglei}
**How constraint manufactures diversity** (**one cannot discuss the affairs of the court → one discusses the city god; one cannot revile the examiners → one lets a blind monk smell the essays; one cannot love freely → a fox woman knocks at the door by night; one cannot sign one's name to a criticism → one sets up a “异史氏” (**one kind of prohibition brings forth one kind of fiction: the table of species in *Liaozhai* is the photographic negative of the Qing environment of speech** (Lesson 16's genealogy of the oblique brush, *qubi* 曲笔 ('the brush that will not go straight'), and Chapter 7's “梦是管辖区” ('the dream is a jurisdiction') meet here: **high pressure does not extinguish expression; it changes the biology of expression**); **Why *Liaozhai* was later cut** (**anthologies of the late Qing and the early Republic often take the charm of feeling and discard the resentful gloom; modern textbooks for the most part include 'The Cricket' and 'Painted Skin' (the thread of the common account) — **the *Liaozhai* we get to read is an abridged edition of 'tales of the strange + satire',** and the part that takes up the most space (the bitterness of the examinations) is exactly the part later ages need least** (Chapter 7, “精选即删改” ('to select with care is to cut and alter'): **each generation carries off the ones that do not hurt it**).
:::

::: history A secret copy made public {#sec-miben}
**1640–1715** Pu Songling (**born at the end of Shunzhi, and through the Kangxi years trapped in the examination ground:** the spirit of a dynasty, written on one man's examination admission slip**); **Before the 1700s** the circle of manuscript copies (**copying back and forth, verses inscribed in response, additions and supplements:** publication is postponed, and reputation is brought forward (the perfect tense of Chapter 1's “抄本是社交” ('a manuscript copy is social life')**); **1766** the Qingke Pavilion imprint (**Zhao Yin 赵音 cut the blocks at Licheng, and only then was there a widely current edition (the common account) — **the bookshops finally believed that this book had a market:** and it was already a matter of sixty years before (becoming a classic has a kind of lag, and that lag is the price of the ticket into the age of manuscript copies**); **The 1800s** Dan Minglun's 但明伦 commented edition (the common account — **the fifth commentator enters:** *Liaozhai* is read as prose composition, with notes on its “笔力” ('force of the brush') and its “文法” ('methods of composition') (the turning, in Chapter 3, of fiction criticism into teaching material: **a book of ghosts is taught as a class in writing**)); **The 1900s to the present** from 'The Cricket' entering the textbooks to translation into other languages (**comparisons of the Kafka kind are put forward again and again (the thread of the common account) — **the hat, 'the classical writer in China who most resembles modernism', is a curriculum vitae that the twentieth century compiled for Pu Songling** (Lesson 15, recognition after the fact: **we love him first because we need him to represent us**)).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of *Liaozhai*
**Closing the matter with the one sentence “借鬼写人” ('borrowing ghosts in order to write of people')** (**it is a stock phrase, not an analysis:** it must be said clearly which concrete problem of narrative, or of censorship, the ghost of each piece solves (the taxonomy of this lesson is that list**)); **taking the fox women for the liberation of women** (**they take the initiative, they are full of feeling, they are capable, and yet they also come and go of themselves and do not seek a recognised status:** 'not occupying the institution' is the reason they are permitted to exist (Chapter 2, “愿望满足的代价” ('the price of a wish fulfilled'): **the fiction makes women perfect, so as not to let them have power**)); **taking *Liaozhai* for historical source material on the Qing** (**what it proves is this: what kind of wrong can be told inside literati circles, and to what degree** (Chapter 3, a change of heading: **it is an archive of the climate of opinion, not an archive of the household registers**)); **ignoring its craft in Classical Chinese** (**to talk only of the stories is to miss the whole difficulty of this book:** inside a thousand years of the Classical Chinese tradition, it does description of the mind with an extremely small number of characters (Lesson 12: what is still left after the adjectives have been deleted — that is the secret of *Liaozhai***).
:::

::: exercise A table of species {level=1}
List three “不会被追责的发言者” ('speakers who will not be held to account') in the environment where you are (they may be jokes, sticker-memes, anonymous accounts, or some kind of literary form). For each, write one line on whom it speaks for, and what it said that could not be said outright.
::: solution
Marking: **the three must come from different channels** (**three variants of the same kind do not count as three species**); **the content column must be specific down to the type of sentence** (**is it an accusation, a mockery of oneself, or a call for help:** the taxonomy of *Liaozhai* was made to exactly this standard (Lesson 1, “做别人省掉的账” ('keeping the accounts other people leave out')**).
:::

::: exercise Judgement by a faculty {level=2}
In imitation of *Siwenlang*, write a passage of 100 characters. Use a faculty other than sight (smell, hearing, or touch) to judge a 'work' (any one of a notice of admission, a design proposal, or an essay written to a call), and make the result of the judgement the opposite of the actual result.
::: solution
Marking: **the faculty must carry the argument** (**not an ornament of rhetoric: the nose must be able to give a reason (**this is the *Liaozhai* form of the “外行声明” ('a layman's declaration'): admitting that a judgement is a physiological response**); **the reversed ending must land on the institution** (**and must not land on fate:** to land on fate is a lament; to land on the institution is satire (the reverse of Chapter 6's “假深刻” ('false depth')**).

::: exercise Short essay: the biology of the negative {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing “一个时代不许说什么，就会在虚构里长出恰好能说出它的器官：蒲松龄的狐、鬼、梦、异史氏，不是想象力的放纵，是一套严格的适应性演化；研究这类文学若只谈‘浪漫主义’，就错过了它的科学价值——它是言论环境的标本，越精致，环境越窄” — 'What an age does not permit to be said will grow, inside fiction, exactly the organ that can say it. The foxes, the ghosts, the dreams and the Historian of the Strange of Pu Songling are not an indulgence of the imagination; they are a strict adaptive evolution. If the study of literature of this kind speaks only of "romanticism", it misses the scientific value of that literature — it is a specimen of the environment of speech: the more refined it is, the narrower the environment.' (Our translation.) You must call on at least six items: **the compounding of the method of the Tang tale with the subject of tales of the strange**, **the conditions of production in resident tutoring**, **the three kinds of speaker**, **the misplaced heading of the account in 'The Cricket'**, **the layman's declaration in *Siwenlang***, **the sixty years from manuscript copy to printed edition**; and you must rule on **whether 'constraint produces form' is enough to explain one flourishing of literature — you must give a counter-example of 'the constraint very loose, and the literature very mediocre', and say what is still lacking**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**the formula, production, species, misplacement, the layman, the time lag**); the ruling (**for reference: not enough — **constraint explains the direction of the form, and does not explain the quality:** quality further requires a writer able to sit down over a long time, a set of old techniques that can be borrowed, and a circle of readers willing to copy one another (counter-examples are easy to find: any period in which information is loosely controlled, and a tradition of training together with a community of readers is lacking, has a high output and a flat quality (the marking looks at whether the three things have been written clearly, any one of them missing: constraint is the mould, technique is the material, the circle of readers is the kiln; with only the mould one cannot fire porcelain**); if the conclusion can be written “所以我们读《聊斋》，不该只夸它想象奇诡：它是一份用一生时间做出的实验记录，记录一个人如何在所有出口被封死后，继续练习说话——器官是长出来的，不是想出来的” — 'So, when we read *Liaozhai*, we should not only praise the strangeness and cunning of its imagination: it is an experimental record made with the time of a whole life, a record of how one person, after every exit had been sealed shut, went on practising speech — organs are grown, not thought up.' (Our translation.), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed in the study: the formula (**the subject old, the method new**), production (**writing in someone else's house: resident tutoring determines the form**), the speakers (**the fox is exempt from the household register; the ghost is an unclosed case; the Historian of the Strange is a signature that can be denied**), the account books (**'The Cricket' enters a person as a pet; Xi Fangping walks the procedure until it becomes the taking of evidence**), the afterlife (**sixty years of manuscript copies traded for a halo; the textbooks delete the part that hurts most**). The method in one sentence: **read this book as the photographic negative of a lie detector: what it records is not imagination, but the inventory of what cannot be said**. Next chapter: the ghosts of the court of rank and fame — *The Scholars*.
:::
