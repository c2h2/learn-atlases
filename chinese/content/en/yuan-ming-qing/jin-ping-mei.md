title: "*The Plum in the Golden Vase* and the vernacular story"
---

The previous chapter wrote zero out into a hundred and eight thousand characters; this chapter writes the hero as an accountant — **the charge against *The Plum in the Golden Vase* is that it replaces the hero with the counting-house, the battlefield with the inner quarters, and fate with a bill; its honour is the same thing: from then on, the Chinese long novel is able to write "how a person ruins himself when nothing is happening"**.

## Definition: derivation and retirement {#def-yanhua}

::: definition Borrowing a subplot from someone else's ten chapters {#def-jie}
**The right to derive** (**the usual account: *The Plum in the Golden Vase* takes the story of Wu Song 武松 in *Water Margin* as its lead-in, and expands it into a hundred-chapter novel of the household —** this is the first self-conscious "fan writing" in the history of the Chinese novel: it leads away, out of someone else's story, the minor figure who was sacrificed, and gives him a point of view, a ledger and a death certificate (the making-up of the accounts in Chapter 4's "third-party column": **the man who sells steamed cakes is a prop over there, and the head of the household over here**)); **Default at the opening** (**Wu Song's killing of Ximen Qing 西门庆 is rewritten in the early part of the novel (the usual plot: a mistaken killing, penal exile, a pardon),** the hero's revenge is postponed, knocked out of shape, and eaten by the system: **this is the genre's declaration of independence — it announces that it will no longer pay the reader's sense of justice on schedule (Chapter 9, "the rate of honouring": ***The Plum in the Golden Vase* deliberately pays out at a low rate; what it means to sell is not the thrill, but evidence**)); **The author's disappearance** (**"Lanling Xiaoxiaosheng" 兰陵笑笑生 is a pen-name; the real name remains, to this day, an unsolved case (the usual account); the book was completed in the Wanli reign and circulated in manuscript, and there was a block-printed edition in the Tianqi reign (the usual outline) —** a long novel unwilling (not daring) to sign its name: **anonymity here is not freedom but a risk exposure (the counter-example to Chapter 9's "an unnamed column is hot money": **namelessness here is the preservation of assets (**and at the same time it produces the most expensive consequence in the history of Chinese literature: the editions, the dating and the commentaries of the whole book have to be argued over for four hundred years under a name that has no owner**).
:::

## The invention of everyday time {#def-richang}

::: definition The greatest quiet in the history of the novel {#def-anjing}
**The structure of the title** (**the characters "金", "瓶" and "梅" are taken from the names of three women (the usual account: Pan Jinlian 潘金莲, Li Ping'er 李瓶儿 and Pang Chunmei 庞春梅) —** the title is not a theme; it is a personnel list: **the protagonists of this novel are three personified positions in a set of relations (the reverse of Lesson 5's "title = prospectus": **a prospectus sells a vision; this title sells only a list of names**)); **The material of time** (**weather, the turns of the seasons, birthdays, entertaining guests, cutting out clothes, the rate at which silver is exchanged for cash, illness, menstruation, jealousy —** density of plot is replaced by density of life: **"nothing is happening" becomes a subject for the first time (the formal cause of Chapter 4's "banding together looks bad" is overcome here: **the family is a collective that does not need events; it comes with the difficulty of having no point of view — the solution is to make time itself the opponent**)); **The place of sex** (**the usual account: its explicit writing has always had two readings — the admonitory reading and the indulgent reading; the preface claims to give warning, the main text supplies pleasure (Chapter 2, "what the author says on the back cover does not count", and Chapter 8, "the disclaimer clause": **the book's preface is its lawyer; only the main text is its confession**); **This lesson's technical judgement** (**what sex undertakes in the book is the function of an account book: **it is one part of Ximen Qing's composite income (marriage = merger and acquisition, favour = a quota, childbearing = a transfer of property rights) (**delete it, and this novel is only a dispute over family property; keep it, and the reader can see that every intimate relation is being converted into the second column (Chapter 4: the two columns of righteousness and profit; here, the two columns of feeling and price**).
:::

::: definition The family as an enterprise {#def-qiye}
**Composite income** (**the usual plot: a shop selling raw medicines, taking on lawsuits, salt certificates, dealings with officials, and the dowries and private savings of wives and concubines —** Ximen Qing is the first complete "commercial personality" in Ming fiction: **he does not cheat the system; he rents the system (the worldly version of Chapter 5's "relations decide the ending": **the yamen-isation of the heavenly court comes down to earth here as the yamen-isation of the county, and the rent is paid by the month**)); **Marriage as merger and acquisition** (**the marriages to Meng Yulian 孟玉莲 and to Li Ping'er both bring capital in through the gate (the usual plot) —** for the first time someone writes a marriage as a balance sheet: **the ranking of wives and concubines correlates closely with the ranking of the family property; even "being in favour" can be explained as a flow of capital (**this is not the author's scorn for the world; it is the narrative point of view being placed at the level of the firm: **when a novel looks at people with an accountant's eye, people are remembered with an accountant's eye**)); **The mechanism of the decline** (**the usual ending: the household scatters, the people die, the property is confiscated: **there is no external enemy, only exhaustion from within and books that do not match the assets (**the cause of tragedy is changed from "treacherous ministers harm the loyal and good" to "outgo exceeds income, and there is no heir": this is the most worldly, and the hardest to refute, collapse in the history of Chinese narrative (Chapter 3's algorithm: **the cause at last falls on a heading that can be audited**).
:::

::: definition Pan Jinlian: giving the villain the right to speak {#def-pan}
**A return to the "silent ones" clause of Chapter 5** — **in *Water Margin* she is one item on a charge sheet; in *The Plum in the Golden Vase* she is an occupational situation: no property, no son, no way out, and her only assets are looks and a mouth (**to give the villain a point of view is not to pardon her; it is to make the accounts look worse (Chapter 4's third-party column is filled in by her own hand**); **The technical consequence** (**the reader is for the first time forced to quarrel with his own moral intuition: **she is clever, quick-witted, pitiable and cruel; the four words are equally true, and none gives way to another (the counter-example to Lesson 2's "a contradictory sentence cannot serve as a claim": **a character may be contradictory; an essay may not — the novel's patent lies exactly here**)); **Zhang Zhupo's 张竹坡 theory of "cold and heat"** (**the usual outline: in the Kangxi reign of the Qing, Zhang Zhupo commented on the edition called the "First Extraordinary Book", accounting for its structure by "cold and heat" and by "dissatisfaction", and composed a "How to Read" —** a banned book is read as a book that has a structure: **the redemptive function of criticism: fitting a grammar onto a scandal (the third part of the trilogy of Jin Shengtan 金圣叹 in Chapter 2 and Mao Zonggang 毛宗岗 in Chapter 3: **the seventeenth century is the century of Chinese fiction criticism**).
:::

## Simulated storyteller's scripts (*nǐhuàběn* 拟话本): the market for the short story {#def-nihua}

::: definition The "Three Words" (*Sānyán*): literature's public-safety function {#def-sanyan}
**The naming is the programme** (**the usual account: the "Three Words" compiled by Feng Menglong 冯梦龙 are named with 喻, 警 and 醒 — enlightening, warning and awakening —** the Way is rewritten as lessons of experience: this is the marketplace version of "writing as a vehicle of the Way", and the budget is transferred from the court to parents (the third allocation in Chapter 1's dispute over the budget**); **'The Oil-Seller Wins the Queen of Flowers for Himself'** (**the usual plot: the oil-seller saves money for three years, and asks only for one meeting; with sincerity and patience he obtains a whole life —** the simulated storyteller's script invented "a minor figure's whole life" as a unit that can be narrated: **three years of scrap silver take the place of family standing and martial prowess: time is the only capital a poor person can accumulate (Chapter 3, "evidence that can be checked back": **the novel puts up three years of small change as security for a love; this is the most solid payment in the history of literature**)); **'Jiang Xingge 蒋兴哥 Meets the Pearl Shirt Again'** (**the usual plot: a man in trade is long away from home; his wife is deceived by someone; the pearl shirt passes from hand to hand and serves as proof; after they part they meet again, each marries anew, and yet feeling remains between them —** divorce is written as a negotiation in which the accounts can be reckoned and feeling can be kept (**the merchant's moral elasticity: he does not demand purity; he demands clarity; that pearl shirt is a title deed that can move, and also a letter that will not be destroyed (**this is the first time the Chinese novel makes "forgiveness" look reasonable, because it takes place between two people who both have a credit record**).
:::

::: definition The "Two Slaps" (*Èrpāi*): swindles, the sea and writing as play {#def-erpei}
**The mode of production** (**the usual account: Ling Mengchu 凌濛初, invited by a bookshop, wrote the "Two Slaps", taking what had been heard and seen of past and present and spinning it out into stories —** the author claims the status of "writing as play" in order to protect himself: **"play" is a fig leaf, and also a disclaimer (Chapter 8, the weighting of voice-parts and of bearing responsibility: **he says that he is not present, while the bookshop knows that he is**)); **'The Man Whose Luck Turns Chances on Dongting Red'** (**the usual plot: oranges of the Dongting Red kind cross the sea and earn a profit at a multiple, and on a desert island he picks up a strange object —** firsthand collateral evidence of global trade in the late Ming: Huizhou merchants, seagoing ships, foreign silver, exchange rates (Chapter 3, "fiction is not a historical source; change the heading": **it cannot tell you which ship in which year, but it can tell you how an ordinary person imagines the world market: **risk, luck, and the moral tension of gain without labour, are all written into a single voyage**)); **Narratives of swindles** (**the *Erpai* write a great deal about cheating: swapped notes, false silver, assumed names, brokers in collusion (the usual outline) —** an age in which credit expands violently is bound to be an age that writes a great deal about broken faith (the Ming branch of Chapter 8's assessment of information: **the simulated storyteller's script is a *Handbook against Fraud* compiled among the people; its optimism is a by-product of commercial society: so long as there is still a transaction tomorrow, a person is worth forgiving**).
:::

::: example Making up Wu Dalang's accounts {#ex-buzhang}
Read "the death of Wu Dalang 武大郎" in the ten Wu chapters of *Water Margin* against the corresponding passage in *The Plum in the Golden Vase*, and fill in three columns: **(1) who is narrating; (2) under which heading the cause of death is entered (morality / the body / the institution); (3) who is required to pay**. Then answer: **why the same death is a piece of evidence of a crime in *Water Margin*, and an accident of the business in *The Plum in the Golden Vase***.
::: solution
**(1) Narration** — ***Water Margin*: an external narration, of the law and of the rivers and lakes (*jiānghú* 江湖) (death = a grievance waiting to be lodged, which issues a licence for Wu Song's action); *The Plum in the Golden Vase*: a narration internal to the household, in the manner of the counting-house (death = the breaking-off of an item of expenditure, the taking-over of a shop, the specification of a coffin and the silver for it**); **(2) Headings** — **the former is entered under "lust and violence" (a moral heading, and the case can be closed); the latter is entered under "the body, medicine, persons and inheritance" (headings of physiology and of institutions, and the case cannot be closed, because what it pulls on is a net: **the items on the county's form for examining a corpse, the silver for the corpse-examiner, the neighbours' depositions (the negative use of Chapter 8's "testimony is a load-bearing wall": the testimony has been bought**); **(3) Who pays** — **the former is paid by the villain (a fast account); the latter is shared among everyone, including the dead man's own shop and his wife (a slow account); **Overall judgement** — **evidence of a crime asks only who did wrong; an accident of the business asks at which link the system allowed the thing to happen —** the former satisfies the sense of justice, the latter produces knowledge (Lesson 1, "doing the part of the accounts that other people leave out": ***The Plum in the Golden Vase*'s courage is not that it dares to write evil; it is that it refuses to tell evil as a person who can be dismissed**).
:::

::: intuition The difficulty of writing "nothing happening" {#def-wushi}
**Why *Dream of the Red Chamber* is still needed after *The Plum in the Golden Vase*** (** *The Plum in the Golden Vase* proved that the everyday can carry a long novel, but its everyday is a flow driven by desire: **it has accounts and no poetry (the reverse of Yan Shu 晏殊 in Chapter 1, "not giving out incidents": **it gives out every incident, and it does not give you the weather**); what *Dream of the Red Chamber* solves is exactly this step — **writing the same set of household accounts into an expectation of "returning in the end to emptiness", so that the everyday has weight and not only quantity (Chapter 10**); **The formal legacy of the simulated storyteller's script** (**the structure of introductory tale (*rùhuà* 入话), main tale (*zhènghuà* 正话) and closing the case settles into a fixed form in the short story (the clause in Chapter 9) —** it trained the sense of rhythm of several generations of readers: a story must have a head and a tail, and the judgement must be read out: **this contract remains in force all the way to the chapter novel of the twentieth century and to today's "three-minute explanation" (the proof of the longevity of Chapter 9's "the judicial function of reunion"**).
:::

::: history Two books, two fates {#sec-mingyun}
**1500s—1600s** *The Plum in the Golden Vase* circulates in manuscript, and is block-printed and published in the Tianqi reign (**the usual outline:** a book is copied from hand to hand in the studies of scholar-officials, printed in the bookshops, and sold as a bestseller inside the prohibition (the strongest case of Chapter 2's "a prohibition is a label, not a floodgate"**)); **1620s—1630s** the "Three Words" (the Tianqi reign) and the "Two Slaps" (the Chongzhen reign) are published (**the usual account:** the short-story collection is the bookshop's targeted placement with urban readers: **the editor replaces the author as the genre's prime mover (the industrial end-state of Chapter 1's theory of anthologies**)); **1680s** Zhang Zhupo's commented edition (**the designation "First Extraordinary Book" and the "How to Read":** a critic turns a banned book into a school's admissions prospectus**); **1700s—1900s** banned and cut again and again (**the prevailing system of "clean editions":** excision becomes a new system of editions: the *Plum in the Golden Vase* that a modern reader meets is a product of twentieth-century editing (the Nth firing of the starting gun for Lesson 1's clause on editions**)); **1900s—the present** scholarly rehabilitation (**from "pornographic book" to "novel of manners", "*cihua*" 词话 ('prosimetric narrative') and "historical source for Ming society" (the usual outline):** the dispute over a work's character is in the end decided by the composition of its readers: **when the university begins to teach it, it acquires constitutional status (the institutionalising of the after-the-fact recognition in Lesson 15**).
:::

::: warning Four traps in this chapter
**Settling it at one blow as "the victory of realism"** (**read *The Plum in the Golden Vase* as an encyclopaedia of Ming society, and there is no longer any need to talk about its sex, its misogyny and its coldness: **the "encyclopaedia" reading is an exemption from inspection (Lesson 1, "substituting theme for technique"**)); **Judging it on a moral account of the "persuading a hundred and satirising one" kind** (**to say that it "expounds the teaching by means of lust", or that warning is its basis, is in either case to take the preface for the main text: **an author's self-defence cannot serve as the final judgement of a work's nature (Chapter 2, "the back cover does not count"**)); **Citing the simulated storyteller's script directly as a historical source** (**"the Three Words and the Two Slaps contain merchants, seagoing ships and brokerages, and therefore late-Ming commerce was such and such" —** they can prove the distribution of imagination; they cannot prove the volume of trade (Chapter 3's citation by changing the heading: **the correct use: how people of the time imagined the merchant**)); **Ignoring the division of labour between the two lives** (**the long form and the short form are not a difference of size; they are two kinds of contract: **the long form sells immersion in time, the short form sells the density of cases (stretch 'The Oil-Seller' out and it becomes a soap; compress *The Plum in the Golden Vase* and it becomes a summary of a case**).

::: exercise The second column {level=1}
Choose a narrative of an intimate relation that you know (film or television, fiction, or a story from the elders of your own family), list three moments in it that are converted into the "second column" (money, housing, household registration, care), and write an explanation of 120 characters: **whether the narrative itself acknowledges this layer of conversion**.
::: solution
Marking: **the three moments must be identifiable** (**a conversation, an object, a silence**); **the crux is "whether it acknowledges"** (** *The Plum in the Golden Vase* acknowledges all the way to coarseness, *Dream of the Red Chamber* acknowledges all the way to the ominous, and most popular narrative denies all the way to sweetness (Lesson 1, "the part of the accounts that is left out": **denial is also a kind of bookkeeping, entered off the books**).

::: exercise Writing a "closing of the case" for a simulated storyteller's script {level=2}
Take one modern dispute (sharing a rental, an online loan, or betrothal gifts — any one of them) and write it as a simulated storyteller's script of 180 characters: one sentence of introductory tale, one paragraph of main tale, one sentence closing the case. The tone of the closing must sound like "看官听说" ('Now hear this, good audience') and not like a written judgement.
::: solution
Marking: **the closing of the case must not declare who has broken the law** (**it must declare "the regularity of this sort of thing":** the closing in a simulated storyteller's script is teaching by cases, not an order of enforcement (the clause in Chapter 9**); **the introductory tale must share the structure of the main tale** (**a small matter rehearses a large one (Chapter 5, "introductory tale = a moral preview"**); **Extra marks** (**if the closing sentence is made to go against the person who was harmed:** that fits the Ming arrangement exactly: preaching often stands on the side of order, and the reader's work is to make up the accounts on the victim's behalf**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the accountant's eye {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters assessing this claim: "**What *The Plum in the Golden Vase* invented was not pornography, but the accountant's eye: it believes that every relation will in the end meet on some slip — sex, family feeling, friendship, allegiance; looked at with this pair of eyes, a family is a miniature state, and the fall of that state will not be written in the history books, but only in the inventory of a division of the household. The most profound tradition of the Chinese novel thereafter is, in every case, a branch of it: writing how a system converts its members into quantities that can be exchanged**". You must call on at least six items: **the making-up of accounts in a derivation**, **everyday time as the opponent**, **marriage as merger and acquisition**, **Pan Jinlian's occupational situation**, **the simulated storyteller's script's system of lessons of experience**, **Zhang Zhupo's structural theory of "cold and heat"**; and you must rule on this: **whether "converting relations into quantities that can be exchanged" is the novel's epistemological contribution or its moral compromise — the answer must take the form "if the novel refuses the conversion, what it would lose, and what it would keep" (you are required to give, as an example, a novel that refuses the conversion)**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**making up the accounts, time, merger and acquisition, situation, lessons, cold and heat**); the ruling (**for reference: the contribution side — conversion produces knowledge (who paid how much money for this marriage), and knowledge is the precondition of critique; the compromise side —** once conversion becomes the only eye, a person is left with nothing but rows, channels and a price list: the novel begins to assess an infant and a curtain on the same account book (in marking, see whether a specimen that refuses conversion is offered, such as *Dream of the Red Chamber* writing the death of Qingwen 晴雯 — **on the account book that is one dismissal, but the novel refuses to let the narrative stop there: it adds an elegy, a dream, and a tone that will not disperse — this is what is "kept"**); if the conclusion can be written "** *The Plum in the Golden Vase* gave us the accountant's eye, and the cost is that we nearly came to see only numbers; the greatness of the Chinese novel is that, even as it keeps the accounts, it leaves at the end of the account book a few lines that will not balance**", that is an A.

::: summary
Filed under the novel of manners: derivation (**making up the third party's accounts; anonymity is the preservation of assets**), the everyday (**density of life replaces density of plot: let time be the opponent**), the family (**marriage is a merger and acquisition; the decline is books that do not match the assets**), Pan Jinlian (**four words equally true: a character may be contradictory; an essay may not**), the simulated storyteller's script (**a minor figure's whole life becomes a unit that can be narrated: three years of scrap silver put up as security**), fate (**a prohibition is a label, excision becomes an edition, the classroom is a constitution**). Method, in one sentence: **first find how many conversions this book acknowledges, then see on which line it refuses to go on recording**.
