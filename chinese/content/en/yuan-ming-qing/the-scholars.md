---
title: "*The Scholars*"
---

In the previous chapter, foxes and ghosts spoke for people; this chapter lets people speak for themselves, and then shut their mouths — **the cruelty of *The Scholars* is that it punishes no one; it only lets them go on living: the highest form of satirical literature is not to make the reader laugh, but to make the reader recognise that he himself is the one he is laughing at**.

## Definition: a long novel with no protagonist {#def-meizhujiao}

::: definition Two ways of judging 'rather the same as short pieces' {#def-duanzhi}
**The received account cites Lu Xun's 鲁迅 comment in *A Brief History of Chinese Fiction* (《中国小说史略》)** (“**如集诸碎锦，合为篇幅，虽云长篇，颇同短制**” — 'Like gathering scraps of brocade and joining them into one length: though it is called a long work, it is rather the same as short pieces.' (Our translation.) — **this is the book's verdict on its own structure, and it is also its key; both ways of judging hold:** ① criticising it as loose (taking the unified plot of the Western long novel as the ruler); ② granting that it is another kind of genre (**a pathological atlas**); **this lesson takes the second, and gives the proof** (**the whole book does not cohere by events; it coheres by one category: everyone is valued on the same table — 'examination rank' (*gongming* 功名)** (**let the institution be the protagonist, and no point of view is needed: this is how 'the collective has no point of view' in Chapter 4 and 'the difficulty of banding together in a collective biography' in Chapter 1 are at last solved** (**the novel invented 'the environment as protagonist'**)); **the evidence of the preface** (**the received account: in the eighth year of Jiaqing, in the Qing, the Old Man of Xianzhai (闲斋老人) wrote a preface to *The Scholars*, taking “功名之念” ('the thought of examination rank') as the key line of the whole book (the gist follows the received account)** — **a critic of the same age had already written the programme on the contents page:** **it proves that it is not that the author never thought of structure, but that the structure is not on the level of plot**.
:::

::: definition The prologue: stick the standard of judgement on the door {#def-xiezi}
**Wang Mian 王冕 in the first chapter (the received plot)** (**herding cattle, teaching himself to paint lotuses, refusing to see the powerful, fleeing to Mount Kuaiji (会稽山), and uttering one judgement on the civil-service examinations (the gist of the received account: once the method of the eight-legged essay (八股) was fixed, remarks of the sort “一代文人有厄” ('a generation of men of letters will meet with disaster'))** — **the prologue = the abstract of an essay:** **first set up a man who does not enter the examination yard, and only then do all the characters in the book have a scale** (the long-novel form of Chapter 9's 'the introductory tale = a moral preview': **this novel's preview is an entire person**); **why the benchmark must be 'without succession' (无后)** (**Wang Mian produces no disciples in the book, and does not enter the network (the received structure)** — **he does not win, does not lose, and is not ratified after the fact:** **Wu Jingzi 吴敬梓 places the only complete person outside the story, which is to admit that this kind of character cannot be continued inside the world of the novel** (**this is the most despairing structural arrangement in the Chinese novel: the protagonist must be a guest appearance**.
:::

## The accountant's eye {#def-kuaiji}

::: definition Passing the provincial examination changes present value {#def-fanjin}
**Fan Jin 范进 (the received plot)** (**he sat the examinations at twenty, went on until he passed the provincial examination at fifty-four, and on seeing the announcement of his success was so overjoyed that he went mad; Butcher Hu (胡屠户) was first arrogant (“现世宝穷鬼”, 'a living disgrace, a pauper-devil') and later obsequious (“贤婿老爷”, 'my worthy son-in-law, my lord'); the local gentleman Zhang (张乡绅) at once sent silver and sent a house)** — **Wu Jingzi does not comment by a single word; he only renders the account:** **when the same person's 'pass' (*zhong* 中) changes, every distance between people is recalculated** (Chapter 6, 'the accountant's eye', + Chapter 8, 'the same 50%, two lives': **the mother-in-law's mouth is the measuring instrument, and the local gentry's congratulatory gift is the table of interest rates**); **the technical meaning of the madness** (**there is no transition between the report of good news and the going mad:** **the institution presses the weight of one event onto one person's nerves, and the body collapses before morality does** (**satirical fiction, written to this point, already comes near tragedy: Wu Jingzi makes the reader laugh, and also makes the reader not dare to finish the laugh** (Lesson 1, 'the opponent is the quality inspector': **his opposing side always walks in of itself, and brings a pot of wine**)); **Zhou Jin 周进 strikes the cell-boards** (**the received plot: an old *tongsheng* (童生) enters the examination compound, sees the cell-boards, and throws himself to the ground weeping aloud** — **the two middle-aged men's loss of composure forms one paired contrast:** **one collapses from joy, one collapses from grief, and the cause of the collapse is the same: they have paid a whole life to a counter that does not take returns** (the ultimate version of Chapter 4's 'list of ways out': there is only one item on the list.
:::

::: definition Two stalks of lamp-rush: the accountant's character {#def-yanjiansheng}
**The received plot** (**on his deathbed Yan Jiansheng 严监生 would not breathe his last; he held up two fingers; no one understood; Madam Zhao (赵氏) picked away one stalk of lamp-rush; he nodded, and then died** — **the most famous shot of a miser in Chinese literature;** **correcting a misreading** (**it is often read as 'loving money as one's life'; this lesson reads it as** loyalty to the number: **what pains him is not the money, but the unwritten-off expenditure of 'one stalk too many'** (**if a man has lived himself into one line of a balance sheet, then death can only be the settling of the account** (Chapter 6: its decline depends neither on Heaven's will nor on passion, only on a refusal to balance the books**); a comparison of the same kind** (**Yan Gongsheng's 严贡生 forcible seizure and extortion (the received plot)** — **of the two brothers, one is greed that restrains itself, the other is evil that exempts itself:** **Wu Jingzi writes the same value-orientation at two concentrations; this is the method of grading in the pathological atlas** (a clause on Lesson 1's list: **classification is not sticking on a label; it is letting those who are similar reveal one another**.
:::

## The fall needs no devil {#def-duoluo}

::: definition Kuang Chaoren's compound interest {#def-kuang}
**The chain of the received plot** (**a filial son from the countryside, getting by slaughtering pigs for others, going to the capital to study, receiving pointers from men of Old Zheng's (郑老爹) sort, being selected, sitting the examinations in other men's places, boasting and lying his way through company, putting his wife aside and marrying again, and not acknowledging old kindness** — **turning bad is written as a series of tiny choices, each of which can be forgiven on its own** (**no devil, no villain to egg him on, only compound interest:** **each step takes a little more advantage than the step before, and each step still lies inside the tolerance of 'ordinary human feeling'** (the moral version of Lesson 2's 'overdrawing on intensity': **an overdrawn conscience can be paid at the minimum repayment**); **the technical verdict** (**Wu Jingzi passes no verdict on Kuang Chaoren 匡超人; he only records that the tone in which the man speaks changes, and changes again:** **a character's moral level can be measured by his list of boasts** (**early on, what he boasts of is his foster-father's illness; later, what he boasts of is his own writing; this is the earliest method in the Chinese novel of 'language = evidence of character'** (the ancestral text of Lesson 12's 'a change of vocabulary is a change of character'.
:::

::: definition Famous scholars, anthologists and the workshop of poetry {#def-mingshi}
**The West Lake poetry society and the young gentlemen of the Lou family** (**the received plot: selecting poems, having drafts cut for print, swaggering about, paying respect to the wrong person, and taking a mediocre work for a masterpiece** — **the famous scholar (*mingshi* 名士) is written as a title one can print for oneself** (**the 'anthologist' (*xuanjia* 选家) is one of this book's sharpest inventions: the person who judges compositions cannot himself write a composition:** **the business of criticism itself enters the pathological atlas** (the eighteenth-century version of Chapter 9's 'inflation of words of recommendation': **when anyone can bring out a collection of poems, praise becomes air, and so the power to 'select' becomes a trade** — **Wu Jingzi discovered, more than two hundred years before our algorithms:** **a surplus of content makes judging the only scarce good**); **the group portrait of famous scholars (the received account)** — **they write 'elegance' (*fengya* 风雅) as a verb of social intercourse, not as aesthetic taste:** **once an aesthetic activity becomes a currency in circulation, genuine and fake are no longer an aesthetic question, but a question of recognition** (Chapter 8, the assessment of information: the Qing 'four questions for a source' are rehearsed in advance inside the novel.
:::

::: definition Women's quota {#def-nvxing}
**Shen Qiongzhi 沈琼枝 (the received plot)** (**tricked into becoming a concubine, running off, supporting herself by embroidery and by selling her writing, and answering in open court without deference** — **she is not a hero; she is someone who lives by her own labour:** **the verdict-word Wu Jingzi gives her is 妙 ('marvellous'); the occupation he gives her is an income** (**set against the fox-woman's quota in Chapter 8: Pu Songling 蒲松龄 makes women perfect and does not give them a recognised status; Wu Jingzi does not praise her, and only lets her have money coming in** (**the latter is a colder and also more solid respect** (the Qing version of Chapter 2's 'speaking a situation as language': Shen Qiongzhi is the one who appears in court herself (the tradition of seeing to the procedure oneself, as with Wu Song 武松 and Xi Fangping 席方平, is carried through by a woman.
:::

## The two ends of the structure {#def-jiegou}

::: definition The four extraordinary men and the frame of the book {#def-siqiren}
**The ending (the received plot)** (**Ji Tianxin 季恬欣 wrote 《添四客述往思来弹一曲高山流水》 ('Adding four guests, recounting the past and thinking of what is to come, playing one tune of High Mountains and Flowing Water'. (Our translation.)):** **in the metropolis there are four extraordinary men of the marketplace — one good at calligraphy, one who can paint, one who works the fires and sells paper, one who keeps a butcher's shop; the four are both literary and artistic, and they do not pursue examination rank** — **join the beginning to the end:** **a painter who herds cattle in the first chapter, four craftsmen who do not sit the examinations in the last chapter:** **frame structure = an essay's opening and close answering each other, and this answering is a negation of every protagonist in the book** (the best demonstration of Lesson 5's 'structure is argument': **it does not rely on a character saying 'the examinations are hateful'; it relies on placing two kinds of people at the two ends of the book, and letting the reader walk the distance himself** (Lesson 6, clearing the ground: **the strongest conclusion is to leave the choice until the last step**).
:::

::: definition A defence of looseness, and the cost {#def-songsan}
**A defence of looseness** (**there is no event running through, yet there is a category running through: each group of characters is one disease (the examination disease, the purchased-rank disease, the hermit disease, the anthologist disease, the filial-and-fraternal disease** (**the 'filial-and-fraternal disease' is the cruellest:** **Kuang Chaoren ends in unfilial conduct, while the most filial people in the book are usually those with the least way out** (the reverse use of Lesson 1's 'a list is a pair of spectacles; do not take the world for a collection of wrong answers': **this novel is precisely a deliberately lucid collection of wrong answers** (**its pain is that the author knows that even the correct answer cannot graduate**); **the cost (the honesty column)** (**it does not offer a single line of fate:** **the reader cannot 'follow one person through to the end', and therefore it does not wring tears, does not make gods, and is not adapted** (Chapter 9, the constraint of the box office: ***The Scholars* is one of the least turned into film and television among China's famous books, because a chain structure refuses to be cut into a single line** (**its greatness and its failure in popular circulation come from the same source**.
:::

::: example The pathological atlas {#ex-bingli}
Open a medical record for each of the following people: **① Fan Jin ② Yan Jiansheng ③ Kuang Chaoren ④ Zhuang Shaoguang 庄绍光 (the sort of 'upright man' who is summoned to office and declines)** — for each, fill in: **the name of the disease, the symptoms (one sentence citing the plot), the prescription (the prescription the people in the book give themselves, and the prescription the author implies)**; finally write: **if this book is to treat one disease, what medicine does it prescribe (you may not use 'criticising the feudal system')**.
::: solution
**① Fan Jin** — **name of the disease: dependence on present value; symptoms: he goes mad on seeing the announcement of success; prescription (the people in the book): hold a few altar-services of offerings, and let those with money come to congratulate; prescription (implied by the author): first give him an identity that does not depend on examination rank** (**no one in the book can prescribe this medicine, because no one has this medicine**.
**② Yan Jiansheng** — **name of the disease: loyalty to the number; symptoms: two stalks of lamp-rush; prescription (the people in the book): pick away one stalk; prescription (implied by the author): give him one matter more urgent than 'balancing the books'** (**his death is therefore not greed, but emptiness**).
**③ Kuang Chaoren** — **name of the disease: a minimum-repayment conscience; symptoms: the list of boasts changes from his father to himself; prescription (the people in the book): change to a better city and worse companions of the same trade; prescription (implied by the author):** **give him a skill that cannot be resold (Shen Qiongzhi has one; the four extraordinary men have one**).
**④ Zhuang Shaoguang** — **name of the disease: a lawful exit; symptoms: summoned to office, he declines, withdraws and writes a book; prescription (implied by the author): the author respects him, but does not place him in the first chapter; prescription (structural):** **an exit still has to be negotiated with the system, so he is still a patient** (**the only healthy man is Wang Mian in his guest appearance: he is not inside the arena, so he is not ill** (**the prescription for the whole book** — **it does not prescribe; it registers the patients:** **making examination rank into something that can be named is this book's only treatment** (the reason for 'you may not use "criticising the feudal system"': **that is the name of a department on the diagnostic form, not a medicine: Wu Jingzi's medicine is to let each disease have a name of its own, so that the patients begin to recognise one another** (Lesson 1, 'classification is sovereignty': **the tenderness of this novel is that it issues a medical-record number to everyone who is laughable**.
:::

::: intuition The ethics of satire {#int-lunli}
**Why the author must not come on** (**the verdict is given by the characters themselves in the dialogue (the self-contradictions of Butcher Hu and the local gentleman Zhang are accomplished entirely by the mouth)** — **the author's silence is the legal condition of satire:** **once the author opens his mouth, the reader is demoted from judge to someone sitting in the gallery** (Chapter 6, 'give no conclusion; give a cost'; the reverse of Lesson 10, 'value it five times, because zero times = a propaganda cart with no engine': **the highest persuasion is to refuse to persuade**); **the boundary of the satire** (**it satirises the institution and the human heart, but it does not ask where the institution came from:** **after Wang Mian, no one can organise an alternative life** (the four extraordinary men are an exit from the stage, not a proposal (**so the moral intensity of this book stops at 'lucidity', and not at 'liberation'** (Chapter 3, the boundary of the algorithm: **the cause has been found, but it cannot be rewritten: this is the ceiling of the eighteenth-century Chinese novel, not Wu Jingzi's**.
:::

::: history Two kinds of slowness in one book {#sec-liangzhong}
1701–1754 Wu Jingzi (the received account: born into a family long successful in the examinations, contributed funds, moved to Nanjing, failed the examinations again and again, and was in straits in his later years — every character he wrote had a deposit paid by himself (the biographical version of Lesson 2's 'the collateral is the procedure'); the 1750s the book was completed in his lifetime (the Woxian Caotang edition (卧闲草堂本) is the earliest printed edition now known; the preface is signed in the eighth year of Jiaqing (1803) by the Old Man of Xianzhai (the thread of the received account) — a novel's 'delay in publication' runs parallel to *Liaozhai* (*Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*) (Chapter 8: a manuscript copy is exchanged for a halo, a block-printed edition is exchanged for a market, and what satire is exchanged for is the reader's courage); **the 1900s** retrospective recognition by the New Fiction movement (**at the moment when Liang Qichao 梁启超 and his generation advocated 'fiction and the government of the group', he was cited as the ancestor of satire and of writing the real** (the thread of the received account: **modern literary history needed an ancestor of 'premature realism', and Wu Jingzi was invited out** (Lesson 15, recognition after the fact: **his lucidity is precisely our own need**)); **the 1900s to the present** anthologies and textbooks (**'Fan Jin Passes the Provincial Examination' (《范进中举》) stands as a piece on its own:** **the excerpt turns it from an atlas into a display of a single item, and so the criticism 'loose' becomes the standard remark in the textbooks** (Chapter 7, 'a careful selection is already a cutting and a rewriting': **the defect we have been scolding for fifty years is a product of the cutting**.

::: warning Four pitfalls in the grove of scholars
**The reading 'satire = scolding'** (**this book almost never scolds:** its technique is to let the boaster expose himself (Lesson 12, 'irony = a dislocation of word and judgement': **the author even omits the tone of satire**) — misread it as scolding, and one will take the index of a satirical novel to be the decibels of anger).
**Taking the number of characters for the structure** (**'the characters are many; it keeps a running day-book'** — **it goes by category, not by time** (**to measure it with the ruler of a unified plot is to measure it with some other ruler** (Lesson 1, where the ruler was made**); praising Wang Mian as an 'ideal character'** (**he is a standard who is absent:** read him as a model, and one misses the layer of design in which 'he is outside the book' (the advanced form of Lesson 5's 'showing the stake': **the stake is not in the book**); **explaining the whole book by the author's life** (**'he scolds the examinations because he could not pass':** biography is the backdrop, not the argument (Lesson 2, the mediation paper: **the author was alive (the production end), and the work is also effective (the interpretation end), but the two cannot be evidence for each other**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the price of lucidity {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters assessing '**satire is a costly kind of eyesight: it sees everyone's motives clearly, yet usually cannot see the lever that would change those motives; *The Scholars* takes this eyesight to its limit, and so it is at once the most lucid novel in China and the novel most without a solution — it issues a medical-record number to every laughable person, yet not one of them can be admitted for treatment**'. You must draw on at least six items: **a structure with no protagonist**, **the prologue as abstract**, **present value recalculated (Fan Jin)**, **the accountant's character (Yan Jiansheng)**, **a minimum-repayment conscience (Kuang Chaoren)**, **the extraordinary men at the two ends of the frame**; and decide: **is 'only diagnosing, and not prescribing' a dereliction on the part of satirical literature, or its honesty** — you must take a satirical work that 'did prescribe' (Chinese or Western, either one) as a comparative experiment, and point out the two kinds of efficacy of that dose, inside the book and outside it.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**structure, abstract, valuation, the account, compound interest, the frame**); the comparative experiment (**candidates: *Gulliver's Travels*, *The True Story of Ah Q*, *Dead Souls*, and the like; the mark does not lie in which is chosen, but in the 'two kinds of efficacy'** — **inside the book: the medicine cures the disease (the emperor is persuaded, the landlords are driven off); outside the book: the medicine becomes the sugar-coating of an allegory, and the reader shuts himself out of the company of the patients** (in marking, see whether the paper has written: **satire that prescribes is easy to forgive, because it supplies a conclusion; satire that does not prescribe is hard to forgive, because it shifts the duty of diagnosis onto the reader** (Lesson 6, 'the end of refutation is mutual examination': ***The Scholars* refuses to close the case, so it demands a hundred years of mutual examination**); if the conclusion can be written '**so this is how the price of lucidity is paid: the author gives up being championed, the reader gives up being comforted, and we are still paying the bill for this book's defects — because it does not give us a villain we can laugh at all the way to the end**', that is an A.
