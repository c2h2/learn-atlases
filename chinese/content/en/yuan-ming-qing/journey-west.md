title: "*Journey to the West*"
---

The ledger of the previous chapter stops at the amnesty; this chapter changes to a blank ledger — **the greatness of *Journey to the West* lies not in what it says, but in its allowing three readings to stand at the same time, and each of them can find its own proof inside it: this is the formal feature of allegory — its meaning lies not in how much the author deposited, but in how much the reader can deposit**.

## Definition: three readings and a lawsuit over authorship {#def-sandu}

::: definition Three prescriptions {#def-chufang}
**① The Way-attesting reading** (**the eighty-one ordeals explained in the terminology of Quanzhen internal alchemy: lead and mercury, the infant and the maiden, heart and kidneys (the usual line of the account: Ming imprints mostly carry a Daoist colouring, and Qing Confucians had a theory that it was a “丹经” ('elixir scripture')) — **its evidence is very hard: the book does indeed use a vocabulary of cultivation; its weakness is equally hard: the power to explain is too strong, and anything can be explained (Lesson 1: 'an irrefutable explanation is not an explanation; it is a stance'**); **② the world-mocking reading** (**satire of late-Ming officialdom under a shell of gods and demons: imperial edicts, memorials, mediation, backgrounds (the usual line of the reading) — **the evidence is in the administrative procedure of the details: the heavenly realm has a whole yamen manner of doing things (**it is the most popular, because it is the most practical: read as satire, the reader redeems laughter from superstition**); **③ the mind-cultivating reading** (“心猿” ('mind-monkey') and “意马” ('will-horse') are vocabulary the book uses of itself (the usual account) — **the fetching of the scriptures is read as a history of a mind's training:** its strength is to turn the whole book into one continuous action, rather than a string of encounters**). **The lawsuit over authorship** (**the earliest full recension now extant is the Shidetang edition of the twentieth year of Wanli (1592); the earliest full recension now extant is the Jinling Shidetang printed edition of the twentieth year of Wanli (1592); the author of the preface, and the course by which the book came to be completed, are discussed separately (the usual account); in the Qing a theory “邱处机作” ('written by Qiu Chuji') prevailed (the mix-up came because the *Great Tang Record of the Western Regions* and *Changchun's Journey to the West* share a name); Lu Xun and Hu Shi (Hu Shih), on the strength of the record in the *Tianqi Huai'an Prefectural Gazetteer*, attributed it to Wu Cheng'en (the usual line of the account) — **a book's title can have its author stolen by another book for three hundred years (the most exaggerated version of the authenticity case in Chapter 9:** a signed name is not the truth; it is a contract that has to be kept**).
:::

## The economics of allegory {#def-jingji}

::: definition Writing a mental process as an adventure {#def-xinyuan}
**The mind-monkey and the will-horse** (**“心猿” was originally a ready-made metaphor in the Buddhist scriptures and in the discourse of the Song and the Ming (the usual account); the invention of *Journey to the West* is to treat the metaphor as a cast list:** once a metaphor has been assigned a wage, it has to go to work every day (**the economics of allegory: one inner event must sell one scene:** gluttony has to grow into a pig, and anger has to grow a staff (the cost-reduction project of Chapter 3, transplanted to the psychological level: **“多因” ('many causes') is compressed into “有人” ('there is a person'), and the 'person' here is one's own organ**)); **a taxonomy of the monsters** (**the opponents across the eighty-one ordeals are, roughly, several kinds of disorder: mouth and belly (gluttony of the Bajie sort), empty fame (contests of magic, and wagers), sexual desire (the Kingdom of Women, the scorpion spirit), fear of death (the whole line of seeking long life) — **and so 'fighting monsters' acquires a meaning that can be retold:** but the novel never spells it out; spell it out and it becomes a book of sayings (**the sense of measure in allegory is to keep the scene independent: this is also why it can be rented out, over and over, among the three readings (the psychological version of the 'super IP' in Chapter 9**).
:::

## The yamenisation of the Heavenly Palace and Spirit Mountain {#def-yamenu}

::: definition The logical boundary of rebellion {#def-zaofan}
“**皇帝轮流做，明年到我家。**” ‘Emperors take turns; next year it comes to my house.’ (Our translation.) (quoted in the usual account) — **note the grammar of this sentence: it does not deny imperial power; it denies the incumbent (Chapter 3, 'the map that saves words':** even rebellion does not question the system:** what Sun Wukong wants to strike down is only the one sitting on the dragon throne; the documents of the Heavenly Palace, its system of offices, its queues and its promotions are all kept (**this is also the precondition of his amnesty: what he wants is an establishment post, not a reform (**the imagination of rebellion touches its ceiling here: this novel later makes this monkey a Buddha, but it does not have any Heavenly Palace hold a new election**).
:::

::: definition The fillet: a technology of governance {#def-gu}
**The function of the tightening fillet** (**the smallest device of external coercion:** it does not ask about motive; it only penalises the act (**the mark of its success is that in the end it disappears: a closing, at the end of the fetching of the scriptures, of the sort “箍儿自不在了”, 'the fillet itself was no longer there' (Our translation.) (plot in the usual account) — **the definition of discipline completed is that the constraint becomes invisible (the heroic version of Lesson 7's 'a limit = a bumper':** the fillet is a 'policy qualifier' struck onto a person: it turns the overdraft of the intensity of “齐天” ('Equal to Heaven') into a route that can be carried out (Lesson 2, three grades of intensity: if the fillet is not put on him, he does not live past the third chapter**)); **it is at the same time an ethical problem** (**Guanyin bestows the fillet, Tang Seng recites the spell, Wukong takes the force:** of the three parties, not one can appeal (Lesson 6: the four virtues of the person being refuted have no place here, because this is a disciplining with no opponent:** the violence of love is often exempted from review in just this way (**the honesty of the novel is that it does not pretend Wukong does not hate: time after time he wants to leave, and he stays because of the fillet, because of righteousness, and because he cannot win the fight**).
:::

## The sociology of the monsters {#def-shehui}

::: definition Relations decide the ending {#def-guanxi}
**The backstage rule** (**the usual reading: a monster who has a master is led back; one who has no master is beaten to death (the golden-haired hou, the green ox, Yellow Brows vs the White Bone Spirit, the spider spirits, the six-eared macaque) — **this is not the author's satire; it is the novel's model of cause and effect:** conduct decides only the process; relation decides the ending (the *Water Margin* mirror of the algorithm in Chapter 3: there it is “哪一栏” ('which column'); here it is “谁家的” ('whose household') (**what is fiercer is that it does not even grant an exception: the six-eared macaque has no backer and is beaten to death — it is not even a monster; it is the embodiment of “二心” ('a second mind') (**the causal model sutures itself here: even the counter-example is recruited into the doctrine (Lesson 1: irrefutable**)); **Sun Wukong's art of seeking help** (**his plan of battle changes from 'fighting alone' to 'checking the household register':** first investigate the genealogy of where they come from, and the relation of employment, and only then decide whether to fight or to invite (**this is the most modern layer of the whole book, and the most bureaucratic: the efficiency of force depends on whether it can be absorbed by a superior's procedure (the monster-subduing version of Chapter 8's 'four questions on sources':** first ask “你是谁的人”, 'Whose person are you?' (Our translation.)**).
:::

## The count of ordeals and the wordless sutra {#def-nanshu}

::: definition The self-completion of a KPI {#def-kpi}
**The eighty-one ordeals** (**plot in the usual account: at the end the register of ordeals is checked, the count falls short of eighty-one, and so one more ordeal is supplied (the old turtle of the Tongtian River flips the ice) — **the goal changes from 'fetching the scriptures' to 'making up the number':** the institution discovers, in its own degree of completion, a new item that can be measured (the absurd version of Lesson 10's 'an inspection is a second audit':** when the acceptance standard itself can have a scene added to it, the work is never finished (and the novel writes this as a joke: this is its coldest page**).
:::

::: definition The wordless true sutra {#def-wuzi}
**Plot in the usual account** (**Ananda and Kasyapa ask for a “人事” ('a personal gift') and do not get it, and hand on the wordless sutras; the Eastern Land finds this strange, they go back and ask again, and only then are the true sutras with words obtained — **three layers of irony stacked together: ① **the Buddha, too, wants money (**profanation down at the level of a monastery's accounts**); ② **only the wordless is the true (**the content is declared to be zero**); ③ **but the human world needs paper (“**只可向那边求之”, 'it can only be sought over there' (Our translation.): the reader is required to fill it in (the antithesis of Lesson 1's 'the format of distribution decides life and death':** if format decides everything, then the content can be zero; and this novel insists on writing that zero as a bestseller of a hundred chapters:** it is demonstrating the cost of 'emptiness': in order to get people to accept the wordless, one must first produce the characters of a hundred and eight thousand *li***).
:::

::: example Three readings of the White Bone Spirit {#ex-baigujing}
‘Thrice beating the White Bone Spirit’ (plot in the usual account): using each of the three readings, ① Way-attesting, ② world-mocking and ③ mind-cultivating, write an interpretation of 60 characters; then write a fourth, 'non-allegorical' reading: **treat it as a principal–agent story of asymmetric information**, and say **which of the four readings is the hardest to refute, and why**.
::: solution
**① Way-attesting** — **demons arise from one's own water and fire: what is beaten is the deluded stirring inside one's own body; the three beatings are three evening-outs of the firing-time (**the merit: compatible with the terminology of the text; the risk:** it cancels the external world entirely**.
**② World-mocking** — **the monster can change, and so can official seals and documents; the hard part is not the beating, it is getting a superior to believe that you have already done it (**it reads the whole book as a mishap in official paperwork: Sun Wukong's grievance is the classic narrative of the grass-roots cadre (**the evidence: every time, Tang Seng demands 'testimony'**)).
**③ Mind-cultivating** — **what Wukong sees is the fact; what Tang Seng sees is the motive; the three beatings write 'trust has a cost, and the cost is paid by the one who is trusted' (**the easiest for a modern reader to quote, because it is material for a management class**.
**④ Principal–agent** — **the principal (Tang Seng) cannot observe the result of the action, and can observe only the agent's apparent behaviour ('beating a girl to death'); the agent (Wukong) holds all the information but is not believed; the optimum in a repeated game is **to show goodwill first and only then strike**, but Wukong's temperament does not support it (**information economics translates 'righteousness' into a problem of contract**.
**Verdict** — **the hardest to refute is ④:** because it does not demand authorisation from the text (it does not need the two characters 信息 ('information') to appear in the book), and needs only an isomorphism of structure (this is exactly the common strategy of every 'scientific reading', and it is also the mechanism of an allegory's long life: **its skeleton is abstract enough to be adopted by any set of contemporary terms (Lesson 15: 'a classic = a precision that can be misread again and again'**); **the easiest to refute is ①** (**it claims that this is the original intent of the author and of the religious tradition — **original intent can be refuted by the text and by the historical sources; structural isomorphism cannot (Lesson 15, the intentional fallacy: what the reader can deposit is always more than what the author deposited — but to treat the deposit as the principal is the beginning of the lawsuit**).
:::
:::

::: intuition Allegory is open source {#def-kaiyuan}
**Sequels to, and rewritings of, *Journey to the West* have never stopped** (**sequels from the end of the Ming, Chinese opera (*xiqu*) of the Qing, picture-story books (*lianhuanhua*) of the Republic, animation and film (the usual line of the account) — **how long an allegory lives depends on how many times it can be wrongly understood:** every misreading supplies a new market (the strongest case of Chapter 3's 'universe that can be licensed':** Sun Wukong is the most successful open-source character in the Chinese-language world: on his licence only one sentence is written, “不许停下来”, 'You are not to stop' (Our translation.)**); **'rendering it for children' is a great reduction of dimension** (**cultivation and satire are cut out, and the monster-fighting is kept: the twentieth century read *Journey to the West* as a fairy tale (the usual line of the account) — **this is not a degeneration; it is a genre changing its battery:** an allegory has to let even the smallest reader in, or it deserves only to live among the commentaries and subcommentaries (the positive case of Chapter 7's 'to select is to delete and alter':** sometimes to delete and alter is to save a life**.
:::

::: history The successive changes of hands of one book {#sec-zhuanshou}
**645** Xuanzang returns to his country, and the *Great Tang Record of the Western Regions* (**the Histories division:** a journey becomes a geographical report**); **1100s—1200s** the story of fetching the scriptures passes into popular telling (**“猴行者” ('the monkey pilgrim') enters the *huaben*, the storyteller's scripts (the usual account):** a supplement, from the monk to the monkey: the story begins to hire casual labour**); **1220s** *Changchun's Journey to the West* (**a Daoist travel record:** a book of the same name, with nothing to do with fetching the scriptures, which later steals the author (the three-hundred-year unsolved case of Lesson 1's 'the same name is a false claim'**)); **1592** the Shidetang edition (**the bookshop wraps a hundred chapters of 'new' as 'old':** the preface says “或曰……”, 'some say…', and the signed name is left hanging:** a bestseller dresses as an old book from the first day (the parallel case of Chapter 2's 'a forged ancient edition'**)); **1600s** the line of commentary editions of Yang Minzhai and Li Zhuowu (**commentary comes on again:** the critic applies for literary standing on the novel's behalf (the clause of Chapter 4**)); **Qing** the theory 'written by Qiu Chuji' circulates, and *The Original Purport of the Journey to the West* is turned to the Way of the elixir (**handed on to religion: a novel can become a scripture**); **1920s** Lu Xun and Hu Shi fix the author as Wu Cheng'en (**the first confirmation of title in modern scholarship:** the author is found out, not remembered**); **1900s—the present** from picture-story books to film (**handed on to children and to audiences:** the most active readers of this book no longer read it (Lesson 15: the form of reception is the form of the work**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the Journey
**The obsession with 'the only correct reading'** (**the Way of the elixir, political satire, the learning of the mind, and scientific allegory each claim to be the exclusive one — **the definition of allegory is that it can be rented out: to declare an exclusive is the greed of criticism, not a property of the text (this lesson sets the three readings side by side, and gives an ordering by refutability**)); **taking 'the spirit of resistance' as inborn** (**Havoc in the Heavenly Palace is read only as far as 'rebellion', and not as far as his wanting an establishment post:** the real content of this stretch of narrative is the amnesty (the monkey's version of the banner clause in Chapter 4:** to read the ending as a betrayal is to keep the wrong books for the character**)); **ignoring the violence of the fillet** (**praising the growing up and not the coercion:** this is a novel about domestication, and domestication has a victim (an extension of the 'silent infant' clause in Chapter 9:** the person who is recited into a headache has no veto**)); **settling rank by a present-day adaptation** (**using the values of some film or television version to judge the original — **what you are judging is your adaptation (Lesson 15: the form of reception is the form of the work, but do not treat it as the principal**).
:::

::: exercise Recruiting the metaphor {level=1}
Choose one emotion that everyone has but is embarrassed to name (any one of procrastination, jealousy, or the impulse to curry favour), and run one 'recruitment' for it: give an image (appearance plus a weapon), one good thing it has done, and one record of its harming someone, 120 characters in all.
::: solution
Marking: **an ability and a post must be given** (**a grotesque look alone is a cartoon:** allegory requires that it really discharge a duty in the team (the cost-reduction of Chapter 3's 'there is a person': here, one column of oneself is written as a character**); **the 'good deed' column may not be omitted** (**Bajie's gluttony is at the same time the team's appetite: a character who is wholly harmful is propaganda, not psychology**.

::: summary
The blank ledger, filed: the three readings (**set out side by side, with refutability set beside them**), the mind-monkey (**once a metaphor has been assigned a wage, it has to go to work every day**), the Heavenly Palace (**what rebellion wants is an establishment post**), the fillet (**the mark of a constraint's success is that it cannot be seen: and the novel does not apologise for him**), relations (**the six-eared macaque sutures causation: even the counter-example is recruited**), the wordless (**writing zero as the characters of a hundred and eight thousand *li***). The method in one sentence: **first ask how many readings this book allows, then ask which lump it has left you that you cannot swallow**.
