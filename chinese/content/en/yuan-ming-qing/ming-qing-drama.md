---
title: "Ming and Qing drama"
---

The novel of the previous chapter swapped the hero for an accountant; this chapter lifts the camera back onto the stage — **Tang Xianzu's (汤显祖) contribution is not the celebration of love. It is to submit *qing* (*qíng* 情, 'feeling'; passion) as an actionable reason: thereafter, in Chinese narrative, 'I am willing' (我情愿) becomes for the first time an independent item of evidence; and the cost of that is written in *The Peach Blossom Fan*: when *qing* and the state lose their cases together, only one Daoist priest can declare the court adjourned**.

## Definition: the formal system of *chuanqi* {#def-tizhi}

::: definition The democracy of shared singing, and the length of a celebration {#def-chuanqi}
**Origins and format** (**from the southern drama of the Song and Yuan (*nánxì* 南戏) to Ming *chuanqi* (传奇: southern drama, not the Tang tale): division into scenes (readily thirty or forty *chū* 出), the *jiamen* (*jiāmén* 家门, the prologue that states the play) and the opening by the *fumo* (*fùmò* 副末, the supporting male role), a double line of *sheng* (*shēng* 生, male lead) and *dan* (*dàn* 旦, female lead), and singing shared among many (the received account of the form) —** the opening speech is the company's specification and its disclaimer: **“某县某人，今演某记”** ('So-and-so of such-and-such a county now performs such-and-such a play.') (Our translation.) First it settles what kind of story this is (exhorting filial piety, exhorting loyalty, punishing licentiousness), and only then does the lying begin (the stage version of Chapter 6's 'a preface is a lawyer'**); **the democratisation of the right to lyric expression** (**the acoustic problem at the end of Chapter 1 is solved outright here:** *sheng*, *dan*, *jing* (*jìng* 净, painted face) and *chou* (*chǒu* 丑, clown) may all sing — and so 'many people having an inner life at the same time' becomes possible. *Chuanqi* can therefore write the household, the official world and war as going on together (the cost is an inflation of length: **the number of inner lives × the number of arias each person is supposed to sing = fifty scenes**)); **who sets the length** (**banquet performances (*tánghuì* 堂会), birthday plays, celebrations: one performance may run for days on end (the received line of account) —** looseness of structure is not the author being idle; it is the shape of the mode of patronage (the banquet-performance version of Chapter 1's 'length is a physiological quantity': **when the box office is swapped for a feast, the plot must be able, at any moment, to give up its seat to people coming in and going out —** this is the formal reason *chuanqi* is brilliant in the single scene and loose across the whole (it is also why later extracted-scene plays, *zhézi xì* 折子戏, have on the contrary preserved it: 'Strolling in the Garden' (《游园》) and 'Seeking the Dream' (《寻梦》), which we still perform, are the victory of a history of cutting (Chapter 6, 'cutting and altering is rescue'**))).
:::

::: definition How one musical reform occupied the stage {#def-kunqu}
**The polishing of Kunshan musical style (*kūnshān qiāng* 昆山腔) by specialists in musical rules of Wei Liangfu's (魏良辅) kind, and the staging of Liang Chenyu's (梁辰鱼) *Huansha ji* 浣纱记 ('Washing Silk')** (**the received line of account: around the Wanli reign) —** the water-polished tune (*shuǐmó diào* 水磨调) slows the speed of the singing and splits open the head, the belly and the tail of each character's sound: **this amounts to reassigning the whole genre's bandwidth to 'slow' and 'fine'** (the Kunqu version of Lesson 14's prosody and the speed of thought: **once the audience is used to one character sung for as long as a stick of incense burns, any narrative dashed off in haste is judged coarse**); **institutional consequences** (**pure singing (*qīngqǔ* 清曲), troupes, societies of scholar-officials who perform plays as amateurs, household troupes that keep actors (*jiābān* 家班) (the received line of account) —** Kunqu became the common interface of elite culture: **to be able to set words to a southern *qu* (*nánqǔ* 南曲) was a qualification for society** (the advanced version of Chapter 5's economy of household tutorships and Chapter 4's economy of paying calls: **at this point the brick for knocking at the door is exchanged for a whole study of the modes, *gōngdiào* 宫调**)); **the price of one genre's seizure of the field** (**for two hundred years there was almost only one grammar of the stage (the received line of account) —** once the technical infrastructure has set, innovation can happen only in the wording: **this is the background against which *The Peony Pavilion* is a miracle: it made no revolution in form; inside the form that was permitted, it said the furthest thing there was to say** (the old question of Lesson 10, 'format and freedom': Kunqu gave Tang Xianzu a cell, and he painted the walls into a garden**)).
:::

## *The Peony Pavilion*: the lawsuit of *qing* {#def-mudanting}

::: definition The prefatory inscription is a prospectus {#def-tici}
“**情不知所起，一往而深。生者可以死，死可以生。**” ('Feeling does not know where it rises; once it sets out, it runs deep. The living may die; the dead may live.') (Our translation.) (cited, on the received account, from the prefatory inscription to *The Peony Pavilion*) — **Tang Xianzu wrote the essay before he wrote the play:** this short text is the product specification for the whole book. What it declares is not the plot, but the nature (** *qing* is defined as an energy that can violate the conservation of life and death** (the reverse of Lesson 2's 'policy proposal': **he does not ask any institution to change a single clause; he only advocates an experience that cannot be signed off**)); **the climate of thought** (**the received line of account: the line of the Taizhou school (泰州学派) and the various doctrines of the 'childlike mind' were current in the late Ming, and '*qing*' and 'the genuine' (真) became topics one could debate —** Tang Xianzu did not put forward a philosophy. What he did was turn a topic in philosophy into a performance of two hours: **actionability moved from the study to the stage** (the best case of Lesson 15's 'an idea needs a format for distribution'**)).
:::

::: definition Strolling in the garden: discovering waste {#def-youyuan}
“**原来姹紫嫣红开遍，似这般都付与断井颓垣。**” ('So this is how it is: the brilliant purples and the lovely reds have bloomed everywhere, and all of it, like this, is given over to a broken well and a crumbling wall.') (Our translation.) (a quotation on the received account) — **note that her first emotion is not longing, but regret:** what she sees first is that it lies waste (**Du Liniang's (杜丽娘) tragedy is not that she is forbidden to love. It is that, for the first time, she realises she has been produced as an asset nobody uses** (an upgrading of Yingying's (莺莺) 'information situation' in Chapter 2: **Yingying can only lie; Liniang can say what is there in front of her: from 'unable to say it' to 'at last said aloud', what lies between is three hundred years and a garden**)); **“良辰美景奈何天，赏心乐事谁家院”** ('A fine hour and a fair scene — what is one to do with heaven? Heart's delight and joyful things — whose family's court?') (Our translation.) — **it sets the weather beside rights of property:** a fair scene is an asset, and it has no owner; no owner means waste (**the persuasive force of this aria is that it is an economic argument: Liniang books her own desire to the account of 'waste', and 'waste' is the only charge her father and her teacher also admit** (Chapter 2, 'using the other side's words': **the second person, after Hongniang 红娘, to win a case with the other side's dictionary**)).
:::

::: definition The dream: a parallel session that is actionable {#def-meng}
**The technical legitimacy of 'The Startled Dream' (《惊梦》)** (**reality gives her no seat from which to speak, so the narrative opens a parallel session: the dream** (a contrast with Chapter 5's 'writing "cannot" as "I dreamed"' — **Xin Qiji's (辛弃疾) dream is a permitted exit; Tang Xianzu's dream is a jurisdiction:** **in the dream Liniang is not being permitted; she is exercising jurisdiction** (she is the active party, and afterwards she refuses to treat it as a dream**)); **what is more ruthless in 'Seeking the Dream'** (**she goes back to the same place to collect evidence** (**the plot on the received account) —** the dream is required to produce material evidence: this is the most important step in the whole book: **'I did truly live, once' needs a place that can be checked back** (Chapter 3's 'evidence that can be checked back', applied inside love; and the reverse of Chapter 5's 'a recommendation is a prophecy': **she is not standing guarantor to other people; she is giving evidence to herself**)); **'Judgement in the Underworld' (《冥判》)** (**Judge Hu (胡判官) checks the register and lets her pass;** the plot on the received account — **even the underworld has to consult the files:** the way Tang Xianzu lets *qing* win its case is not by abolishing the institution, but by inventing an institution whose procedure is more correct (Chapter 1, 'reunion = a judicial declaration': this play splits the declaration into a first hearing, a review and an imperial grant (**its satire is hidden in the efficiency: the underworld court is more reasonable than the county yamen:** this is no longer a love play; it is criticism of administration**))).
:::

::: definition The portrait and the audience to come {#def-xiao}
**'Painting the Portrait' (《写真》)** (**Liniang paints her own spring likeness and leaves it for those who come after** (the plot on the received account) — **she makes herself into a recommendation handed on to the future** (**the limit of Chapter 9's clause on words of recommendation:** **a triple signature, of author, character and portrait, is completed by one person about to die**)); **after 'The Soul's Departure' (《离魂》)** (**burial beneath the plum, the coffin opened, the return to life —** death in the play becomes a procedural appeal: **the living world will not allow her *qing*, so by dying she moves the case into a higher court** (another version of Chapter 6's 'a court one makes oneself': **Wu Song 武松 builds his own procedure; Du Liniang builds her own level of court**)); **a return visit to 'the three juries of Chapter 2'** (**The Story of the Western Wing* was on trial for eight hundred years; *The Peony Pavilion* was on trial the moment it came onto the market:** Tang Xianzu was read, all at once, as a 'teaching of *qing*', as the religion of those mad for the play, and as a defendant charged with 'inciting to lust' (the received line of account: from the late Ming into the Qing, bans and their lifting ran side by side with ardent devotion) — **a book needs three kinds of reader before it has the volume of greatness**).
:::

## Two works of the early Qing: settling accounts with *qing* {#def-qingchu}

::: definition *The Palace of Eternal Youth*: on trial with the person one loves most {#def-changsheng}
**Hong Sheng's (洪升) structure** (**the gist on the received account: the passion of Li and Yang is threaded through the An–Shi Rebellion; sweet in the first part, regret in the later; rise and fall, and the scenes 'Hearing the Music' (《闻乐》) and 'Reviling the Rebel' (《骂贼》), are woven in —** this preface is an honest lawyer: **it admits that it must punish the character it loves most** (the control group for Chapter 6's 'a preface is a lawyer': **most prefaces get themselves acquitted; this one convicts the character, and then goes on loving him**)); **technique** (**'love' and 'ruining the state' are written onto the same pair of protagonists, and so the audience is split:** romance and political argument are carried by the same character (a merging of Chapter 4's 'two theories of legitimacy, one person each' — **when two theories are squeezed into one and the same body, the play becomes repentance**)); **dismissal from office set off by a performance** (**the received line of account: *The Palace of Eternal Youth* was in vogue during the run of performances by the Neiju troupe (内聚班); Hong Sheng was struck off on account of the matter; in the same year Kong Shangren 孔尚任 also left office —** 'a staging by the Office of Music' became a political event: **the economy of troupes and political risk close their loop in this chapter** (Chapter 1's 'a cough in the playhouse' is upgraded to 'risk before the throne': **change the patron, and the boundary of the script changes**)).
:::

::: definition *The Peach Blossom Fan*: refusing to issue the written judgement {#def-taocha}
“**借离合之情，写兴亡之感。**” ('Borrowing the feelings of parting and reunion, I write the sense of rise and fall.') (Our translation.) (cited, on the received account, from the general sense of the beginning and end of *The Peach Blossom Fan*) — **one sentence states this play's method of bookkeeping:** the state is written onto the lovers' account (**the fall of the Southern Ming is not argued; it is 'paid through the account' of these two people's separation and reunion**); **'Refusing the Trousseau' (《却奁》)** (**Li Xiangjun 李香君 returns the dressing-case** (the plot on the received account) — **a political declaration made with one ornament** (the contrast with the pearl shirt in Chapter 6: **the pearl shirt is a property right that moves from person to person; the trousseau is a position that refuses to move:** **the same 'thing', in the two works, bears union and rupture respectively.** Blood stains the poem-fan, and the dots and washes become peach blossoms — **she makes the wound into a logo** (**this is the most modern stroke in the whole play:** **the victim herself takes over the brand design of the narrative**)); **'Denouncing the Feast' (《骂筵》)** (**she rebukes Ruan Dacheng 阮大铖 to his face** (the plot on the received account) — **the distribution of the right to speak is pushed to its limit here:** **a prostitute becomes the only person in the whole play who does not lie** (Chapter 1, 'whoever has the sung words = whoever has an inner life': Kong Shangren gives the highest passage of song to the person of the lowest status (while the scholar-officials queue up to surrender**)); **the ending** (**the received account: the Daoist priest Zhang (张道士) smashes the fan on a stone, shouts the two of them apart, and they enter the Dao —** reunion = a judicial declaration (Chapter 1), and here the play refuses to issue the written judgement (**no ruling of reunion, no ruling that the loyal and the treacherous are both cleared, no ruling of a life to come:** adjournment is its judgement (the antithesis of Chapter 5's 'a dream is also the future tense': **'To My Son' (《示儿》) pushes the sign-off onto the son; *The Peach Blossom Fan* pushes the sign-off onto the audience: you see to it yourselves**))).
:::

::: example Two permissions: climbing the wall and entering the dream {#ex-liangxu}
The 'climbing the wall' of *The Story of the Western Wing* and the 'entering the dream' of *The Peony Pavilion* solve the same difficulty: how to let the audience see a man and a woman come together under the rites and the law. Compare them in the three columns **① who bears the responsibility, ② who is lying, ③ whether it can be denied afterwards**, and explain: **why the 'dream' is the higher technique, and 'climbing the wall' the more comic**.
::: solution
**① Responsibility** — **climbing the wall: Student Zhang's (张生) rashness and Hongniang's arrangements share the burden** (**the event is put down to the man's impetuousness and the servant's quick wit, and Yingying's will is outsourced** (Chapter 4, 'a character cannot sing their own shame'**); entering the dream: **Liniang herself is the initiator** (**the responsibility is entirely hers, and so the cost is entirely hers: she died** (**the difference in this one step is the weight of the two plays: *The Western Wing* makes the breach cost nothing; *The Peony Pavilion* writes out a bill for the full amount**); ② **who is lying** — **the scene in *The Western Wing* in which the letter is disowned (赖简): Yingying lies** (**her lie is a defence**); *The Peony Pavilion*: nobody lies, **only reality is declared insufficient** (**this is the real function of the 'dream' as a device of genre:** **it need not charge anyone; it need only add a jurisdiction**); ③ **whether it can be denied** — **climbing the wall can be taken for a misunderstanding and a joke (the protective colouring of comedy); the dream** cannot be denied, because it has 'Seeking the Dream' as a procedure for going back — **a dream that can be re-checked becomes experience, and is no longer desire** (**the comic comes from an external action (a person climbing a wall); the higher comes from an internal confirmation of right (a person insisting that she did live**).
:::

::: intuition Making *qing* actionable {#def-kesu}
**Tang Xianzu's politics is procedural** (**he never advocates '*qing* higher than the rites'; he advocates '*qing* ought to be accepted for hearing':** the judge, the envoy of Jin, Du Bao 杜宝, the emperor — the characters of the whole play are all hearing cases, and the only difference is whether they are willing to read the evidence (Chapter 2, 'beating the other side with the other side's words': the trilogy of Hongniang, Liniang and Li Xiangjun, **all three refuse to invent a new value, and all three victories are procedural**)); **why this claim became expensive in the Qing** (**the adjournment in *The Peach Blossom Fan* and the dismissal from office over *The Palace of Eternal Youth* (the received line of account) —** once the system for accepting cases is tightened, a play can only be rewritten as repentance or as leaving home for religion: **the later 'degeneration of the grand reunion' in Chinese drama (from a judicial declaration it degenerates into a placebo) is connected with this** (the long-term consequence of the clause in Chapter 9: **the audience wants writs of execution, and the yamen no longer issues them, so the theatre begins to print its own counterfeit notes**).
:::

::: history Three hundred years on the stage {#sec-sangaibai}
**1400s—1500s** The transition from southern drama to *chuanqi*, and the various texts of *Jing*, *Liu*, *Bai* and *Sha* (荆、刘、拜、杀) (the received line of account) — **early *chuanqi* is mostly on themes of moral exhortation and chastisement:** **the list of subjects was written out in advance by the Learning of Principle (*lǐxué* 理学); music and elegance of diction were squeezed in only afterwards**; **1500s** the reform of the Kun musical style and *Huansha ji*; **1598** *The Peony Pavilion* is completed (**the received account**); **1600s** the dispute between the two schools of Linchuan and Wujiang (**the received line of account: the debate between Tang and the line of Shen Jing 沈璟 over 'fitting the musical rules' and 'idea and interest') —** a formal controversy rare in the history of Chinese literature, 'the prosody school vs the school of expressed feeling' (the Ming echo of the two acts of legislation by Zhou and Li in Chapter 3: **the old account of constitution and copyright is reckoned again inside Kunqu** (**in the end both sides are right, and the theatre has only one throat**)); **1670s—1680s** the method of writing plays in Li Yu's (李渔) *Xianqing ouji* 闲情偶寄 ('Casual Notes of Idle Feelings') (**the received line of account: his discussions of 'structure' and of 'lyrics and arias' treat *chuanqi* as a product and teach people how to write it:** **the earliest Chinese manual for writing fiction and Chinese opera (*xìqǔ* 戏曲) comes from the hand of a man who sold manuscripts and went on tour with a household troupe** (the Qing textbook version of Lesson 10's 'a procedure can be taught'**)); **1688／1699** *The Palace of Eternal Youth* and *The Peach Blossom Fan*; **1700s—1800s** the dispute between the *huabu* and the *yabu* (*huābù* 花部, the 'flower' or popular division, and *yǎbù* 雅部, the 'elegant' division) (**the received line of account:** **the stage, occupied by the scholar-officials for two hundred years, is seized back by regional vocal styles with 'bustle':** **the replacement of an aesthetic hegemony depends on a new box office, not on a new theory** (the reverse of Chapter 5's 'emptying-out is the loser's dividend': **the dividend is over, and the audience has moved its stake**)).
:::

::: warning Four pits in this chapter
**Reading *The Peony Pavilion* as the liberation of love** (**its claim is '*qing* ought to be accepted for hearing', not 'marriage ought to be free':** **to raise a plea up into a programme is the habit of later anthologies** (Chapter 2, 'taking lyric expression for a programme'**)); **letting 'feudal ritual teaching eats people' govern the whole play** (**Du Bao is written as a symbolic villain:** **he is the most dutiful official in the play, and the most heartbroken father:** **Tang Xianzu's difficulty is precisely that his opposite is also right** (Lesson 6, the steel man: in a good play every line the opponent speaks has its reasons, and the arrangement is still wrong**)); **looking only at the text and not at the stage** (** *chuanqi* is a composite of singing, acting, companies and celebration:** **the tradition of extracted scenes is the form in which it actually survives** (Chapter 6, 'cutting and altering is rescue': **half of *The Peony Pavilion* that is alive today is the result of editing by actors**)); **taking *The Peach Blossom Fan* for history** (**its bookkeeping is a borrowed account: rise and fall are written onto the lovers;** **to cite it as proof of the historical facts of the Southern Ming, one has to change the subject** (Chapter 3: it can prove 'how people of the Qing imagined the fall of a state'**)).
:::

::: exercise Using the other side's dictionary {level=1}
Write a plea in the manner of 'strolling in the garden', of 120 characters: for a claim of the present (for example 'I want to change jobs' or 'I want to drop an unreasonable class'), argue using only the highest values in the other side's system (duty to the family, the company's performance, the school's reputation), and do not let 'I like' or 'I am willing' appear.
::: solution
Marking: **every value-word comes from the other side** (**to break the ban is to breach the terms:** **the trilogy of Hongniang, Liniang and Li Xiangjun is this one rule at three grades of difficulty**); **there must be one argument from 'waste'** (**Liniang's killing stroke is an economist's:** **a fair scene with no owner is waste**; write how your asset is even now lying waste (more persuasive than a complaint of hardship**).
:::

::: exercise A defence of Du Bao {level=2}
In Du Bao's voice write a plea of 150 characters (after the daughter's 'return to life'). The requirement: **he must not admit that he was wrong, but he must admit one thing he had not foreseen**.
::: solution
Marking: **'not admitting fault' is a hard constraint** (**his position holds inside the institution:** **the steel man's standard is to let the other side speak better than the original text**); **'the thing he had not foreseen' must be concrete** (**for reference:** **he foresaw his daughter's illness, ghosts, and a corpse rising false; the one thing he did not foresee was that someone would open a case on behalf of a dream —** **to admit an expansion of jurisdiction is more honest than to admit an error, and more dignified** (the advanced version of Chapter 5's 'a concession must be recoverable'**).

::: exercise Short essay: actionable *qing* {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**To turn one kind of experience into a reason that can be accepted for hearing is literature's most effective attack on order: it does not demand that the rules be abolished; it demands that the rules acknowledge that this experience, too, belongs inside their jurisdiction. *The Peony Pavilion* won this battle, and won it so thoroughly that the two authors of the early Qing began to doubt whether this procedure for accepting cases was worth saving — so one of them has the emperor issue an edict of reunion, and one of them has a Daoist priest kick the pair apart with a single foot: Chinese drama went from opening a case to adjourning the court, and it used only a hundred years**". You must draw on at least six items: **the specification-function of the prefatory inscription**, **the argument from 'waste'**, **the dream as a parallel jurisdiction**, **the portrait and the reader to come**, **the self-punishing structure of *The Palace of Eternal Youth***, **the refusal of judgement in *The Peach Blossom Fan***; and give a ruling: **whether 'making a certain experience actionable' can serve as a standard for measuring progress in literature — you must cite one contemporary experience that is 'not yet actionable' (of your own choosing) and say whether, for literature to make it actionable, what is most lacking is courage or format**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**specification, waste, jurisdiction, portrait, self-punishment, adjournment**); the ruling (**for reference: the standard can be used, but on a condition —** **it must at the same time check 'accepted for hearing by whom': if actionability is achieved by adding an underworld that is more reasonable, literature has only moved hope into a fictional higher office** (marking looks at whether this layer of reservation is written out: **the greatness of *The Peony Pavilion* is that it makes *qing* actionable; its naivety is that in the play there is always a judge willing to listen** (a reference answer to the question 'courage vs format': **most of the time what is lacking is format: words that can be quoted, evidence that can be re-checked, a form that can be submitted — literature's contribution is often not to shout louder, but to invent the format into which the experience can be entered** (the positive version of Chapter 8's 'procedure is the only weapon the weak can afford'**); if the conclusion can be written "**and so the real legislative work of literature is not to announce what is right, but to design, for a certain kind of pain, a form on which a case will be accepted; once that form at last appears, people will suppose that society has suddenly become civilised**", that is an A.

Filed with the court: the form (**the democracy of shared singing, and the length of a celebration: extracted-scene plays are the victory of a history of cutting**), Kunqu (**the bandwidth was given over to the slow and the fine: the cell and the garden**), Liniang (**she sees the waste first; the dream is a jurisdiction; seeking the dream is collecting evidence**), and what comes after (** *The Palace of Eternal Youth*, on trial together with the person one loves most; *The Peach Blossom Fan* refuses judgement**). The method in one sentence: **look, in a play, at who may apply and who must prove their own case — the list of what will be accepted for hearing is that age's inventory of inner lives**.
