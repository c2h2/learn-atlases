---
title: "*Dream of the Red Chamber*"
---

The novel of the previous chapter issued a medical-record number to every ridiculous person; the novel of this chapter issues each person an advance payment on fate — ***Dream of the Red Chamber* settles the old problems of the Chinese novel in one clearing of the account: it spoils the plot, it keeps the books, it refuses valuation, it has no ending; at the last it writes "unfinished" into the work as well, which is why everyone who has read it to the end wants to finish the writing of it on its behalf, and the moment it has been written to the end, it dies**.

## Definition: two endings, one open interface {#def-banben}

::: definition Manuscript copies and printed editions {#def-ben}
**Two systems** (**the usual account: a manuscript system (the various "Zhi editions" (*zhiben*) of the Jiaxu 甲戌, Jimao 己卯 and Gengchen 庚辰 years, eighty chapters preserved, the commentators closely connected with the author) and a Cheng–Gao system of printed editions (the 120-chapter books composed and printed in movable type in 1791 and 1792, the last forty chapters supplied by others, the usual account) —** the most authoritative text of the Chinese novel has come down as two copies standing side by side, "incomplete + completed": **this is not a failure of the work of putting the text in order; it is the open interface it leaves to later ages (the extreme of the "manner of death" clause in Chapter 2: **every prophecy in the first eighty chapters is a cheque not yet honoured**)**); **conditions of production** (**the usual account: Cao Xueqin 曹雪芹 (c. 1724–1763); the family had served as Textile Commissioner at Jiangning, and had received the emperor more than once; in the Yongzheng reign the household was confiscated; straitened in his last years, he "增删五次" ('added and deleted five times') —** the cycle in which one book is produced is longer than its author's life (the sixty years of manuscript copies of *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio*, in Chapter 8, become here a lifelong project: **"unfinished" is not a regret; it is a physical property of this book**).
:::

::: definition The opening publishes the encryption protocol {#def-jiemi}
Self-statements of the kind "**真事隐去，假语存焉**" ('True events are hidden; false words remain.' (Our translation.)) (the usual account cites the gist) — **in the first chapter the author already makes public his rules for keeping the books:** one and the same thing is entered at once on the ledger of family affairs and on the ledger of allegory (**this is not concealment; it is the designing of dual accounts: only so can the book both be read and be pardoned (Chapter 7, "the actionability of feeling": **Tang Xianzu 汤显祖 added a jurisdiction of the netherworld; Cao Xueqin added a jurisdiction of "假语村" ('Village of False Words')**)**); **two generations of misreading** (**the line of the usual account: a Qing school of *suoyin* 索隐 ('searching out what is hidden') guessed that the book was a veiled allusion to some particular person and some particular event; Hu Shi 胡适 (Hu Shih), in *Hongloumeng kaozheng* 红楼梦考证 ('An evidential study of *Dream of the Red Chamber*') (1921), took the history of the Cao family as proof and set up the theory of the "autobiography" (自叙传) (the usual account) —** the two schools make one and the same mistake: they take the production end for the interpretation end (Lesson 2's mediation agreement, signed again: **evidential research is an excellent foundation, and also an excellent ceiling —** it disproved "a veiled allusion to the political world", and it has often, in the reader's stead, read the book thin**).
:::

## The spoiler as a business model {#def-jutou}

::: definition The advance payment: registers and songs {#def-yufukuan}
**The structural place of the fifth chapter** (**the usual account: Jia roams in spirit through the Illusory Realm of the Great Void (太虚幻境), reads the judgement verses (*panci*) in the registers of the "twelve hairpins of Jinling" (金陵十二钗), and hears the twelve songs of *Dream of the Red Chamber* —** the ending of the whole book is broadcast here in a single transmission (the spoiler clause of Chapter 3, made into an institution: ***Red Chamber* makes the spoiler into the skeleton of the work**); **the revenue** (**once "what will happen" has been withdrawn, the whole income of the narrative comes from "how it is honoured, a little at a time":** the reader's pleasure shifts from suspense to recognition (the psychological version of the super-IP in Chapter 7, "the reader already knows the ending; the tension moves across to how it is told") —** this is the most advanced business model the Chinese novel has produced: what it sells is not the result, but the process of carrying the result out (and the carrying-out is slow enough to break the heart: a family uses eighty chapters to learn that it will die**); **the cost** (**it leaves the person who writes the continuation no road to take:** however the plot of the last forty chapters is written, it is set against the judgement verses already broadcast (the extreme of the "concession passage" in Lesson 1: **Cao Xueqin first writes the worst result as verse, and then lets the characters pass their days in high spirits:** from then on the reader lives between two tenses**).
:::

::: definition An external audit: Granny Liu {#def-liu}
**Three entries into the Rongguo Mansion** (**the plot in the usual account: the first entry is to seek a handout (*da choufeng* 打抽丰), the second to trade amusement for things, the third saves Qiaojie 巧姐 —** a farmer walks into the centre of the balance sheet; **jests of the sort "老刘，老刘，食量大如牛"** ('Old Liu, Old Liu, an appetite as big as an ox.' (Our translation.)) (the usual account cites the gist of the line) —** this is an open quotation of the price of dignity exchanged for cash, and the novel writes the quotation down on paper and does not pretend that it did not happen (the accountant's eye in Chapter 6: ***The Scholars* writes of everyone being valued; *Red Chamber* writes of how the person who is valued carries this transaction on**); **the function of the audit** (**she does not understand the rules, so the rules are repeated once for the reader to hear; she will not eat the aubergine, so the aubergine is explained as a working procedure (the "茄鲞" section in the usual account) —** a stranger is the best voice-over: **a novel about a great family needs a guest from outside, to stop it turning into an internal commemorative album (the solution in the theory of point of view in Chapter 4: **give the zero-focalisation school a guide**).
:::

## Youth deducted by the month {#def-yuan}

::: definition The garden has a budget {#def-yusuan}
**The building of the villa for the homecoming visit** (**the usual account: a garden made for Yuanchun's 元春 return home to see her family, young girls bought in, costumes provided —** the garden of youth is put up out of the budget of one grant of political favour (its foundation is a deficit**); **the accounts in step** (**the plot in the usual account: the rent schedule handed in by the steward Wu Jinxiao 乌进孝, Wang Xifeng 王熙凤 on "出的多进的少" ('more goes out than comes in'), Tanchun 探春 managing the household by promoting what pays and clearing long-standing abuses, the pawnshop and the confiscation of the house —** the Grand View Garden (大观园) is not an escape from reality; it is deducted, month by month, by reality (the household version of "the accounts do not match the facts" in Chapter 6; it is also the algorithm of Chapter 3: **the cause again falls under an account heading that can be audited, only this time no one is dismissed, because no one is solely responsible**); **Tanchun's economics** (**the usual account cites her words "**百足之虫，死而不僵**" ('The hundred-legged worm dies, but it does not stiffen.' (Our translation.)), together with her remark "我但凡是个男人" ('If only I were a man'), and, while she manages the household, "开源节流" ('open the sources and restrict the outflow') —** she sees two places out of repair: who is admitted, by gender, and inheritance; her reform is vetoed by bloodline (the question of who is to carry out the policy proposals of 6: **Tanchun is the earliest lecture in institutional economics, and its most failed field experiment (**her shrewdness can only slow the depreciation; this is the novel's highest respect for the "capable minister", and its most ruthless verdict**).
:::

::: definition The person who refuses valuation {#def-baoyu}
**Baoyu's *chi* 痴 ('foolish attachment')** (**the essential point of the usual account: he is weary of the examination career, he likes to keep company with the sisters, he calls girls "the pure" (清净), and, when the talk is of an official career and of practical statesmanship, he offers remarks of the sort "好好的英雄贤圣，无端加以‘禄蠹’"** ('Perfectly good heroes and sages, and for no reason one sticks on them the name "禄蠹" ('salary-grubs').' (Our translation.)) (the usual account cites the gist of the line) —** the definition for this lesson: **his *chi* is a capacity to refuse valuation (**a refusal to convert persons, time and marriage into the present value of the examinations and of family rank (the antithesis of Chapter 6: there, everyone has a price list (***Red Chamber* is the mirror of *The Scholars*: in *The Scholars* no one resists valuation; in *Red Chamber* one person refuses valuation, and the price is an exit by leaving home to take vows —** note the division of labour between the two satires: Wu Jingzi 吴敬梓 makes you laugh at them; Cao Xueqin makes you ache for yourself**)**); **Daiyu burying the flowers** (**the plot in the usual account, and 'The Lyric on Burying Flowers' (《葬花词》) —** she writes her own depreciation ahead of time, as a poem: **set against Yan Jiansheng 严监生 (Chapter 9): the same sensitivity to "number", one of them the greed that will not allow one extra stem of lamp-wick, the other the purity that counts every petal of a fallen flower (**so *Red Chamber* is not opposed to keeping accounts; what it opposes is there being only one way of keeping them: its *qing* 情 ('feeling') is a heading that is never entered in the books**).
:::

## *Hongxue* ('Red Chamber studies'): one learning, three births {#def-hongxue}

::: definition Commentary, evidential research, philosophy {#def-sanci}
**① Commentary (*pingdian* 评点) (Zhiyan Zhai 脂砚斋, 'Rouge Inkstone', the eighteenth century)** (**the comments give away the author's family affairs and the process of cutting and rewriting (the usual account) —** the most intimate reader is at the same time the most troublesome witness: **he stands surety for a public text with a private memory, so that every comment of his is both material and interference (Chapter 8, "independent sources": the Zhi comments (脂批) and the main text cannot be one another's only evidence**); **② Evidential research (Hu Shi and Yu Pingbo 俞平伯, the 1920s)** (**the line of the usual account:** **author and editions are set up as the foundation of the learning:** **this is modern scholarly method making its entrance; for the first time the Chinese novel is treated as a document, and not as a book for idle hours**); **③ Philosophical criticism (Wang Guowei 王国维, *Hongloumeng pinglun* 红楼梦评论 ('A critique of *Dream of the Red Chamber*'), 1904)** (**the usual account: it discusses literature by way of Western tragedy and the doctrine of release (解脱), and stands at the head of modern criticism; afterwards it was also criticised as "forcing a philosophy onto the work" —** the first time a tool from outside was used to read the Chinese novel: **the tool can be replaced; the method cannot be reversed (Lesson 15, "a scientific way of reading needs only a sameness of structure": the reading hardest to refute is often not the most fitting reading**); **how the learning came to be** (**one novel can keep a whole field of study, because it offers, at the same time, three things that can be fought over:** an unfinished manuscript (the study of editions), family affairs that have been hidden (evidential study), and an open interpretation of the tragedy (the study of theory) (***hongxue* is not the glory of *Dream of the Red Chamber*; it is a consequence of the book's structure (Chapter 3, "a universe that can be licensed": it leases the right of interpretation to three tenants who will not yield to one another**).
:::

::: example Auditing one advance payment {#ex-yufu}
Choose one prophecy from the first eighty chapters (any one of a judgement verse, a song text, a prophetic line in a play, a dream, or a lantern riddle) and the corresponding episode in the last forty chapters, and fill in: **① the literal sense of the prophecy, ② how the continuation honours it, ③ the difference**; then judge: **is the difference a failure of the continuation, or the irreconcilability of two moral views (if the latter, write what each of the two moral views is)**.
::: solution
**A sample (setting a general prophecy of the sort "树倒猢狲散／落了片白茫茫大地真干净"** ('When the tree falls, the monkeys scatter / what is left is a vast white land, truly clean.' (Our translation.)) **against the close of the last forty chapters, "家道复初、兰桂齐芳"** ('the family's fortunes begin again, and orchid and cassia flourish together')) — **① the literal sense** (**a thorough scattering: it keeps back none of the lingering warmth of ancestral offerings, and it gives the examinations no recompense**); **② the honouring** (**after the confiscation there is still an act of imperial grace, a pass in the provincial examination, and the family name set in order again (the plot in the usual account)**); **③ the difference** (**the continuation rewrites "emptiness" as "the wrong is cleared + a moral reimbursement":** the good suffer a little, and are then taken back into service by the system**); **the judgement** — **it is chiefly irreconcilable, and not a technical failure:** the continuation holds to a moral bookkeeping of the kind "good and evil are repaid in the end, and order is worth repairing" (the orthodox version of "reunion = a judicial pronouncement" in Chapter 1); Cao Xueqin's advance payment is of another kind: **it maintains that order itself is a bad debt, so the mechanism of retribution cannot be used (the difference between the two moral views is the different weight that "reunion" has in the two books:** the former needs a universe that can close a case; the latter needs only a reader who can remember (this is exactly the technical reason for two hundred years of readers' discontent: **we need not agree with "emptiness", but the judgement verses have already persuaded us — and the judgement verses are in the author's own hand**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The ethics of the unfinished {#int-weiwancheng}
**Why a continuation always fails** (**in a work that takes prophecy for its skeleton, the ending is the one component that cannot be replaced (the highest version of "three things an ending must avoid" in Lesson 5: **here the ending is not a winding-up; it is the subject matter of the contract**); **and incompleteness has one advantage:** it keeps the reader on the production line for good (**every generation can continue it, wants to continue it, and continues it badly — and so the book goes on being used, and not merely being cited (the upgrade of "a classic = continually cited" in Lesson 15: **a classic = continually mis-continued**); **the modern reputation of "unfinished"** (**the line of the usual account: from the twentieth century on, the incompleteness of *Dream of the Red Chamber* has often been read as modernity (it is like a contemporary novel with no final draft) —** this is another adoption by sameness of structure (Chapter 8: the hardest to refute, and the most like ourselves**).
:::

::: history The public career of one book {#sec-shengya}
**1750s–1763** the draft is added to and cut, and manuscript copies circulate among friends (**the usual account**); **1791/1792** the Chengjia 程甲 and Chengyi 程乙 editions are composed and printed (**movable-type printing turns a private book into a commodity (Chapter 1, "the format of distribution decides life and death": **without a composing room, *Dream of the Red Chamber* has no standing in the nation**)**); **the 1800s** commentary and continuations flourish (**ways of reading and of rewriting run side by side (the industrial-scale version of "the commented copy is the established text" in Chapter 2**)); **1904** Wang Guowei's essay; **1921** Hu Shi's evidential study (**the two pillars of the scholarship are set up:** one asks how it was written, one asks who wrote it, and no one any longer asks "whether it is about Zhang San's 张三 family"**); **the 1900s to the present** from the "New *Hongxue*" to research institutes, records of the scholarship, and adaptations for film and television (**one book becomes a course, a profession, and a national rhetoric:** the existence of the title "*hongxue* scholar" is the proof that it has at last become a classic (it is also the beginning of its being used to excess (the academic version of the "annual invitation to tender" in Chapter 9: **once every ten years, the commissioning party for the interpretation is changed**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in *Red Chamber*
**Taking evidential research as the end point** (**"the Cao family's affairs" explain where the material came from; they do not explain the effect of the work:** a foundation is not a ceiling (Lesson 2's mediation agreement**)); **treating the characters as defendants** (**"why the love of Bao and Dai could not come to be" is treated as an examination question, and fitted with a standard answer:** the novel's verdict is a stacking of responsibilities of more than one kind (institutions, the economy, temperament, timing), while what the reader wants is an object that can be sued (the algorithm of Chapter 3: we are still using it to talk about *Red Chamber*, just as Cao Xueqin is criticising it**)); **taking "anti-feudal" as the highest praise** (**this is a certificate of merit issued by the twentieth century:** it reads a book about the fleeting and about memory as a book about the legitimacy of political power (the retrospective ratification in Lesson 15: **a certificate is also another name for a misreading**)); **pressing the charge against the continuation all the way to the persons** (**Gao and Cheng, the two of them, have been reviled for two hundred years:** the more useful question is why readers in the Qing could accept that ending, and why we cannot (the answer is not in the continuation; it is in our appetite for "retribution" (the long-span version of the box-office constraint in Chapter 9**).

::: exercise An exercise in recognition {level=1}
Choose one merry scene from the first eighty chapters (any one of a round of drinking commands, linked verses, or a birthday feast), mark three places where there is "a double sounding, produced because the reader already knows the ending" (an object, a joke, or a passage from a play will all do), and write an explanation of 80 characters: **how one and the same sentence has a meaning in each of the two tenses**.
::: solution
Marking: **the three places must be identifiable** (**a page number or a chapter heading: the precondition of recognition is that they can be located (the clause in Chapter 3 on being able to look a thing up again**); **"the two tenses" must be written in concrete terms** (**the people of that moment are laughing; the person who reads is mourning:** if that layer of laughter is not in the text, the mourning does not hold: **the volume of the tragedy is supplied by the comedy (the auditory version, in Chapter 5, of "no human voice, and a horse's neigh"**).
:::

::: exercise Name a way of keeping the accounts {level=2}
In *Red Chamber* there are at least four kinds of "bookkeeping": the examination account (Jia Zheng 贾政), the household account (Xifeng, Tanchun), the account of feeling (Baoyu, Daiyu), and the lifespan account (Qin Keqing 秦可卿, Jinchuan 金钏). Choose any two, write one sentence for each on **their exchange rate** (how many taels of silver equal one betrothal? how many poems equal one forgiveness?), and point to one chapter in which the exchange rate fails.
::: solution
Marking: **the sentence that states the exchange rate must be open to rebuttal** (**do not write a lyric with no exchange rate, of the sort "情比钱贵" ('feeling is dearer than money'):** give figures, or a ratio of exchange (Chapter 3: figures must have a stated scope**); **the place where it fails is what matters** (**for reference:** when Xifeng cannot get a thing done with silver, and Baoyu cannot get the same thing done with feeling either, the two sets of accounts are void at once — **that is the night of the search (Chapter 6's clearing of the scene: here no one can be judged, so no one can close the case**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the accounting of memory {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**Dream of the Red Chamber* is the first book in Chinese literature to take 'remembering' as a moral obligation: it puts no one on trial; it only guarantees that no one is left out — judgement verses, songs, registers, threnodies, dreams, all of them are devices against forgetting; and its own unfinished state leaves this list forever a few lines short, so that every reader has to go on remembering on its behalf: a novel becomes a debt that has not yet been settled**". You must call on at least six items: **the open interface of the two endings**, **the encryption protocol and the dual accounts**, **the income model of the advance payment**, **Granny Liu's external audit**, **the garden has a budget (Tanchun)**, **Baoyu, who refuses valuation**; and give a ruling: **can "remembering" take the place of "judgement" as a literary justice — you must set beside it one work that "ends in a judgement" (Chinese or Western, either one), and say, for each of the two justices, who survives and who slips the net**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**the interface, the dual accounts, the advance payment, the audit, the budget, the refusal of valuation**); the comparison (**candidates: the case closed by revenge in *The Orphan of Zhao*; the upright official clearing the wrong in *The Injustice to Dou E*; the verdict and the idlers in *The True Story of Ah Q*; the confession and the serving of the sentence in *Crime and Punishment*; the marks fall in the two columns "survivors" and "those who slip the net" —** under a justice of trial, the survivors are the names that have been cleared, and those who slip the net are all the dead who come to an end without bringing a charge; under a justice of memory, the survivors are the list read out one by one, and those who slip the net are those who have not left even a name (in marking the paper, see whether it is pointed out that: **the memory in *Red Chamber* in fact plays favourites too: it remembers the young ladies and the maids, and it hardly remembers the stewards and the tenant farmers — on the page where Wu Jinxiao hands in the rent, the number of people is a figure (Chapter 4's third-party column: Cao Xueqin, too, left a blank page in his own accounts**); if the conclusion can be written "**so both books are lying, only in different directions: judgement says 'the crime deserves its punishment', memory says 'no one is forgotten'; and the honesty of *Dream of the Red Chamber* is that it writes out even those who slip its own net — those stewards who come to pay the rent are not worth even a name**", that is an A.
:::
