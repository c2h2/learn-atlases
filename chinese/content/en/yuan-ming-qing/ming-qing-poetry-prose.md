title: "Ming and Qing poetry, prose and criticism"
---

The first ten chapters are works; this chapter is the way those works were argued over — **the poetry and prose of the Yuan, the Ming and the Qing do not stand, as the song lyric (*ci*) of the Song and the *qu* of the Yuan stand, in the manner of "the literature of an age" (一代之文学); they stand by way of programmes: the Former and Latter Seven Masters (前后七子), *xìnglíng* 性灵 ('native sensibility'), *shényùn* 神韵 ('spirit and resonance'), *jīlǐ* 肌理 ('muscle and grain') and *yìfǎ* 义法 ('substance and method') — every one of these words is a call for tenders. Finish reading this course and you ought to see it: the main battlefield of the literature of the late empire is not on the paper; it is in the four characters "what counts as good" (什么算好)**.

## Definition: the programme is the work {#def-gangling}

::: definition Why so many "schools" grew {#def-weihe}
**Four conditions arrived at the same time** (**① the market in printing and in collected works (**carving a collection was faster than writing a collection: a collection of poems became a cost of social life (the scholar-official's enlarged version of the economy of paying calls in Chapter 4**)); ② the examinations trained a vast number of people who could write poetry and prose but whose choice of occupation was limited (**a surplus population of writers, so differentiation had to rely on slogans**); ③ there were already too many classics (**Tang poetry and Song prose had each become a monument:** when the exemplars cannot be surpassed, the only thing left to compete over is "whose list of exemplars is the correct one" (the retrospective recognition in Lesson 15: an allegiance to the Tang and an allegiance to the Song are two competing schemes for distributing an inheritance**)); ④ societies, and the trade in *píngdiǎn* 评点 ('commentary written into the text') (**a society needs a charter, and a charter needs an exclusion clause:** "不读李、杜以下书" — 'Read no book from after Li and Du.' (Our translation.) — is an exclusion clause (**so half the catalogue of a history of Ming and Qing literature is a history of societies (the scholar-official's version of the "peer-review system" in Chapter 1**))).
:::

## The Ming: imitating the ancients, and the rebound {#def-mingdai}

::: definition The algorithm of the Former and Latter Seven Masters {#def-nigu}
**The Former Seven Masters (led by Li Mengyang 李梦阳 and He Jingming 何景明, in the Zhengde and Jiajing reigns, the common account)** (**the common account of the programme cites its main drift: claims of the sort "**文必秦汉，诗必盛唐**" — 'Prose must be Qin and Han; poetry must be High Tang.' (Our translation.) — **this is not a theory of copying; it is a theory of *qǔjìng* 取径 ('the route by which one learns'):** it holds that the "pool of best models" for learning poetry ought to be compressed into a very small window (Lesson 15, "the exemplar is a quota": **shrinking the set of choices is a crude way to raise average quality (and a precise way to suppress a personal style**))); **there was a rebound on the inside from the start** (**He Jingming and Li Mengyang later argued it out, and he was mocked for the distinction in his "拟议变化" ('deliberated imitation and transformation') (the thread of the common account) — **a movement, while its founders were still alive, split into two methods of learning:** is the object of imitation syntax, or method? (this is the Ming scene of Lesson 2's "learn the procedure, not the mood", and it is also the old case of the Jiangxi school brought back to court**); **the Latter Seven Masters (in the Jiajing and Longqing reigns, led by Li Panlong 李攀龙 and Wang Shizhen 王世贞, the common account)** (**made the programme into an institution: model yourself on the measurements, keep to *gédiào* 格调 ('manner and tone'), and do not step outside the circle — **returning to the ancient was promoted from a claim to a marking standard (the third time Lesson 10's "marking standards shape the genre" is cashed:** the consequence is that it can be copied:** a poetry society in one city could, within a fortnight, turn out three hundred acceptable seven-character regulated poems, not one of which was worth remembering (**the positive correlation of output and pass rate is exactly the shape of the sieve of literary history**)).
:::

::: definition The Tang–Song school and the middle road of "Gui Youguang" {#def-tangseng}
**The question shifts** (**Should prose imitate the vocabulary of the Qin and the Han, or imitate the "method" of the Qin and the Han? The Tang–Song school (Wang Shenzhong 王慎中, Tang Shunzhi 唐顺之, Mao Kun 茅坤 and Gui Youguang 归有光, the common account) advocated a return to the line of Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修 and Zeng Gong 曾巩 — **they changed the definition of "ancient" from a period into a lineage (Lesson 2, the right to define:** whoever controls the character "ancient" controls the whole call for tenders (Mao Kun's selecting the *Tang Song ba dajia wenchao* 《唐宋八大家文钞》, 'Transcriptions of Prose by the Eight Masters of the Tang and Song', is precisely compiling a household register for "ancient" (the closed loop of the Song-prose clause in Chapter 8:** the Eight Masters are a "Song dynasty" picked out by men of the Ming**))); **Gui Youguang** (**the thread of the common account: he brought the small matters of ordinary household life into ancient-style prose (*gǔwén* 古文) (pieces of the kind *Xiangji xuan zhi* 《项脊轩志》, 'Record of the Xiangji Studio') — **he cut a small slice from the budget of "carrying the Way" and gave it to "remembering":** one room, two women, one tree (a close of the kind "今已亭亭如盖矣" — 'Now it already stands tall, like a canopy.' (Our translation.) (the general sense of a sentence cited in the common account) — **this is the most important technical event in this chapter: the art of memory in the late-Ming brief essay (*xiǎopǐn* 小品) and in *Dream of the Red Chamber* both draw their current from here (the first sign of the "accounting of memory" in Chapter 10**).
:::

::: definition *Xingling*: inviting the author back {#def-xingling}
**The three Yuans and the Gong'an school (in the Wanli reign, the common account)** (**the common account of the programme cites its main drift: "**独抒性灵，不拘格套**" — 'Express native sensibility alone, and do not be bound by set patterns.' (Our translation.); Yuan Zhonglang 袁中郎, in *Xu Xiaoxiu shi* 《叙小修诗》 ('Recounting Xiaoxiu's Poems'), says of his younger brother's poetry something of the sort "**不当从绳墨尺寸间求之**" — 'It should not be sought between the ink-line and the measurements.' (Our translation.) (the general sense of sentences cited in the common account) — **note the structure of the argument:** it does not claim "I write better"; it claims that the unit of evaluation ought to be the person, and not the pool of models (Lesson 15, taste is legislation: **this was a movement that seized the power to legislate back from the classics and gave it to the author**); **before *xingling* there was a defendant** (**the thread of the common account: Li Zhi's 李贽 doctrine of the "childlike mind" (*tóngxīn* 童心) circulated among lower scholars and merchants, and those in power regarded it as throwing the world into disorder — **the fate of a concept is decided by the social composition of its readers (**when your readers include merchants, block-carvers and the women of the household, your "orthodoxy" becomes a problem (Chapter 8, the sociology of sources: **who is reading decides how you are classified**))); **rebound, and the cost** (**the thread of the common account: the Jingling school applied itself to what is secluded and deep; Qing writers from Qian Qianyi 钱谦益 downward pursued them for their "狭与鬼" ('narrowness and ghostliness'), and tied the decline of Ming poetry to partisan societies and factional gates — **once the slogan of *xingling* had become a school, it turned into a new set of patterns:** the institutionalising of "不拘格套" ('not bound by set patterns') is the most predictable self-destruction in the history of literature (Lesson 6: **a position cannot be both a position and an institution**.
:::

## The Qing: legislating for success {#def-qingdai}

::: definition Three programmes, three bureaucratic aesthetics {#def-sange}
***Shenyun* (Wang Shizhen 王士禛, in the Kangxi reign, the common account)** (**he held up something of the sort "不着一字，尽得风流" — 'Not a single word is set down, and yet the fluent grace is got entire.' (Our translation.) (the general sense of a sentence cited in the common account), and took the line of Wang Wei 王维 and Meng Haoran 孟浩然 — **this is the poetry policy best suited to a new dynasty:** it does not write about politics, it is extremely well written, and it can be appreciated by the emperor (the official upgraded version of Lesson 1's "do not produce an incident": **the "incident" is deleted in its entirety, and what remains is a lake with no wind on it**); ***gediao* / Shen Deqian 沈德潜 (in the Qianlong reign, the common account)** (**he upheld "温柔敦厚" ('gentle and sincere'), and selected *Gushi yuan* 《古诗源》 ('Sources of Ancient Poetry') and the *Biecai* collections 《别裁集》 ('collections of discriminating selection') in order to set up models — **the teaching of poetry merged with the examinations and the court academies:** an old poet, by means of anthologies, served for half a century as the marking standard (the confirming case of Chapter 1's "a catalogue is a censorship": **he does not delete you; he only puts you in a "separate record"**)); ***jili* (Weng Fanggang 翁方纲, in the Qianlong–Jiaqing period, an evidential scholar discussing poetry, the thread of the common account)** (**he moved the scholar's method of work into poetry:** use annotation in place of inspiration, and take the density of materials as the index of quality (the cautionary example under the entry "abundance" in Chapter 6: **an abundance of materials can be an expenses claim for a shortage of imagination**)); **what the three doctrines have in common** (**all of them answer the same question:** on condition that there is no risk of heterodox thought, how to keep poetry in production (**the literary policy of the late empire is not censorship; it is the turning of censorship into an aesthetic preference (this is the most important sentence of this chapter:** when self-censorship has matured to the point that it can be written up as a theory of poetry, it obtains an exemption in the name of aesthetics**.
:::

::: definition The second entrance of *xingling* {#def-xingling2}
**Yuan Mei 袁枚 (in the Qianlong reign, the common account)** (**he upheld *xingling*, took disciples on a wide scale, women disciples included, made his living by selling his writing and by paying calls, and lived at Suiyuan 随园 — **a man who lived "the author is the standard" out as an occupation (**he charged fees, he selected poems, he ran a poetry arena: the trade of criticism became, in the eighteenth century, a line of work that could support itself (the economy of private lodges in Chapter 5, and the "words of recommendation" in Chapter 9, here become a business licence**)); **Zhao Yi's 赵翼 supplement** (**something of the sort "**诗文随世运，无日不趋新**" — 'Poetry and prose follow the turn of the age; there is no day on which they do not tend towards the new.' (Our translation.) (the general sense of a sentence cited in the common account) — together with "江山代有才人出" — 'Age upon age, the rivers and mountains bring forth men of talent.' (Our translation.) (the common account) — **writing innovation down as a law of history rather than as a personal preference:** this is a rare evolutionary form of sentence in Chinese literary theory (the evolution clause of Lesson 15: **it may be true, and it may only be the victors' way of keeping the books — Yuan Mei's party did in fact win, and so "the new" entered our default settings**)); **the other side of Zhao Yi** (**his *Nian'er shi zhaji* 《廿二史札记》 ('Notes on the Twenty-Two Histories') is a famous work of evidential research (the common account) — **the same set of people wrote, at the same time, a theory of poetry that "tends towards the new" and the most conservative evidential study of historical sources:** *xingling* was in charge of the expression of feeling, evidential research was in charge of facts, and the two sets of accounts were kept very clearly apart (the final form, in the eighteenth century, of the system of separate accounts in Chapter 1**).
:::

::: definition Tongcheng *yifa*: a grammar of prose {#def-tongcheng}
**Fang Bao's 方苞 doctrine of *yifa* (the early Qing, the common account)** (**"言有物" ('words have something in them'), "言有序" ('words have an order'); he also said, in his own person, that ancient-style prose must not be mixed with the language of commentaries and subcommentaries, the language of recorded sayings, the language of letters, or the language of stele inscriptions and accounts of a life (the main drift as cited in the common account) — **this is the cleanest "separation of registers" in the history of Chinese literary criticism:** it marked out, for each genre, a zone of vocabulary that one was permitted to borrow (the genre version of Lesson 2's "a limiting word is a bumper": **a restriction is not oppression; it is what lets a genre be reused for a long time without rotting**)); **Yao Nai's 姚鼐 three elements (principle, evidential research and literary composition — 义理、考据、词章, the common account)** (**he set Song learning, Han learning and belles-lettres side by side as one marking sheet — **a harmonising programme, its function like that of a constitutional amendment:** it let the three schools coexist inside the same society (Chapter 1, peer review: **a person who has learned how to classify need never be a minority**)); **downstream of that, Yanghu and Xiangxiang** (**the thread of the common account: the line of Yun Jing 恽敬 and Zhang Huiyan 张惠言, and the "Xiangxiang school" in the private secretariat of Zeng Guofan 曾国藩, all went in and out by way of Tongcheng — **a prose school lived for two hundred years, and what it relied on was not genius but a triple use: it could be examined in the civil-service examinations, taught in the academies, and employed in the private secretariat (*mùfǔ* 幕府) (**the essence of *yifa* is a set of skills that can be trained (Lesson 10: a procedure can be taught — **the Tongcheng school is the most successful reform of a writing course in China**.
:::

## Criticism as a genre {#def-piping}

::: definition Five forms of criticism {#def-xingshi}
**① Anthologies** (of the kind *Tangshi biecai* 《唐诗别裁》 ('A Discriminating Selection of Tang Poetry') and *Guwenci leizuan* 《古文辞类纂》 ('A Classified Anthology of Ancient-Style Prose') — **a catalogue is a verdict (the Nth time the clause of Chapter 1 is cashed**); **② *Pingdian*** (**Jin, Mao, Zhang and Dan (four entrances, in Chapters 2, 3, 6 and 8) — **criticism written into the gaps of the main text:** this is a rare "parasitic literary theory" in the history of world literature; it assumes that the reader will read two layers at once (Chapter 9, "a way of reading": a critical note is six sentences at the head of a section**); **③ Remarks on poetry (*shīhuà* 诗话)** (**from *Liuyi shihua* 《六一诗话》 ('Liuyi's Remarks on Poetry') to *Suiyuan shihua* 《随园诗话》 ('Remarks on Poetry from Suiyuan'):** one item to an entry, no system, no citing of evidence, and idle talk is allowed (downstream of the entry-form of Song prose in Chapter 8: **the notebook form (*bǐjì* 笔记) is the place of shelter for literary theory: it accepts judgements that have not yet become an established doctrine**); **④ Ranking by grades** (**grading by "rank", "manner" and "realm" (品, 格, 境) — **a system of marks is older than argument:** it can make a person accept the conclusion without having to accept the reasons (Chapter 9, "a star rating must be reducible to a sentence of judgement": the ranking of the Ming and the Qing often refuses to be reduced (**this is what is most fascinating about it, and also the point at which it is least submitted to examination**))); **⑤ History and evidential study** (**claims of the sort made by Quan Zuwang 全祖望, Dai Zhen 戴震 and Zhang Xuecheng 章学诚, "六经皆史" — 'The Six Classics are all histories.' (Our translation.) (the thread of the common account) — **putting the text back on the foundation of institutions and of philology:** this line in the end took over the study of literature in the twentieth century (the academic precondition of the evidential study of the *Red Chamber* 《红楼》 in Chapter 8**).
:::

::: definition A failure that has to be recorded {#def-shibai}
**The rearguard from Zhang Taiyan 章太炎 and Liu Shipei 刘师培 downward** (**the thread of the common account: the literary view of plain learning (*pǔxué* 朴学) and the prose of men of letters coexisted at the end of the Qing**); **the real turn** (**the thread of the common account: the tabloids of the *Youxi bao* 《游戏报》 ('News for Amusement') school, the newspaper prose of Liang Qichao's 梁启超 "new style" (*xīn wéntǐ* 新文体), and, later, *Mashi wentong* 《马氏文通》 ('Ma's Grammar', 1898), which cut the word classes of Chinese with Latin grammar — **a new course was invented inside the classroom (Lesson 17, the study of grammar):** **"composition" changed from a craft that needed exemplars into a technique that needed rules;** **every programme in this chapter (*gediao*, *shenyun*, *jili*, *yifa*) was, within half a century, replaced by one and the same thing:** they were all answers to "whom does one learn from?", whereas the new question is "how does one write a sentence so that it gets through?" (**this is the true graduation ceremony of the literature of the Yuan, the Ming and the Qing: it is not that poetry died; it is that the institution of evaluation was changed (academy → newspaper office → school**).
:::

::: example Writing a call for tenders for one kind of poetry {#ex-zhaobiao}
Suppose you are a provincial director of education in the Qianlong reign, and you are to write, for "the poetry topic of the provincial examination", a marking programme of 120 characters (a call for tenders). It is required that: **① you state plainly which manner you will not take; ② you state plainly which manner you will take; ③ it contain one loophole that a candidate can exploit**; then, in the person of a candidate, write two lines of thinking for a sample poem that is "optimised but not honest", and point out **how the loophole is exploited**.
::: solution
**A model call for tenders** ("**凡试帖诗，取声律匀稳、用事有出处、结归颂扬者；其险韵僻典、狂吠叫呶、以及词采胜而意旨漫者，皆不取。愿学李、杜者宜先检《文苑英华》，愿学王、孟者宜先检《河岳英灵》；凡用事与题面双收者上，单收者次，事与题两失者黜。**" — 'For all examination poems (*shìtiě* 试帖), take those whose prosody is even and steady, whose allusions have a source, and whose close returns to praise. Hazardous rhymes and recondite allusions, mad barking and bawling, and pieces in which the colour of the diction wins while the purport wanders: take none of them. Whoever wishes to learn from Li and Du should first consult *Wenyuan yinghua* 《文苑英华》 ('Finest Flowers of the Garden of Letters'); whoever wishes to learn from Wang and Meng should first consult *Heyue yingling* 《河岳英灵》 ('Eminent Spirits of the Rivers and Peaks'). Wherever the allusion and the face of the topic are both secured, rank it high; where only one is secured, rank it next; where the matter and the topic are both lost, reject it.' (Our translation.)); **The loophole** (**"the close returns to praise" is the item of lowest cost:** any one contentless line in praise of merit can pass, and it holds down no requirement of quality whatever (Lesson 6, "a slogan is a compressed biscuit": **when the marking sheet has one column that can be filled at a cost of zero marks, the whole sheet collapses towards that column**)); **The candidate's optimisation** (**first fix the rhyme, then pick from a classified compendium (*lèishū* 类书) two allusions that share a character with the face of the topic, and last piece together a line in praise of the sage — **none of the three things involves observation (the reverse side of "abundance" in Chapter 6: **allusion and material can be an expenses claim for a shortage of imagination**)); **Marking** (**what must be written out is "the candidate's rationality", and not "the candidate's shamelessness":** the standard for judging the design of an institution is how bad its worst use is (the examination-hall version of the three questions about a countermeasure in Chapter 5: who carries it out, who pays the cost, and how the side-effects are watched — **the provincial director of education did not ask the third question**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The use and the harm of a programme {#def-yongchu}
**A programme is the best of teaching tools** (**"诗必盛唐" ('poetry must be High Tang') can let a teacher in one county teach poetry that passes:** teachability lets a genre continue (the imperial version of Lesson 10's "minimum runnable package"**)); **a programme is also the most effective of sieves** (**in the same movement by which it weeds out bad poems, it weeds out works that cannot be classified:** the cost of the taxonomy is paid by a few, and the return is collected by the whole market (the aesthetic version of the "map that saves words" in Chapter 4**)); **hence the method of this chapter** (**do not take any school as "the truth", and do not take any as "an error":** treat them as four different "systems of quality assurance" (an ISO version of Li Mengyang and of Wang Shizhen 王士禛), and ask one and the same question: what does this standard make possible, and what does it make unreadable (**this is the hands-on exercise, in Lesson 15 of this course, for "the history of literary ideas": ideas are not a list of right and wrong; they are a list of equipment**).
:::

::: history A catalogue of four hundred years {#sec-mulu}
**1400s** the cabinet style (*táigé tǐ* 台阁体) and the lingering echo, at the beginning of the Ming, of the court academies (**the common account:** the style of composition to imperial command, in years of peace: **its "good" is ritual, not observation (another bill for the interest on an age of peace, in Chapter 1**)); **1500s–1570s** the Former and Latter Seven Masters, and the Tang–Song school; **1570s–1620s** the circulation of Li Zhi's doctrine, the three Yuans of Gong'an, and Jingling (**the thread of the common account:** in the Wanli reign, "人自为谱，家自为集" — 'Each man made his own register; each house made its own collection.' (Our translation.): **the flourishing of the book-carving trade let a programme spread through the whole of the Yangtze basin within three months (technical infrastructure decides the tempo of a debate (the mirror image of the Kunqu clause in Chapter 7: **what changed this time was not the voice, but the printed page**))); **after 1644** Gu Yanwu's 顾炎武 argument that "文须有益当世" — 'Prose must be of benefit to the present age.' (Our translation.) — and the prose of remnant subjects (*yímín* 遗民) in the early Qing (**the thread of the common account:** the pain of a lost state beat *gediao* back into its original shape: **when the person writing dies on the battlefield, a slogan shows its cost (the contrast with "the dividend of the elegant" in Chapter 5: **this time no one could translate failure into elegance, because they refused to translate**))); **1660s–1710s** Wang Shizhen 王士禛 presides over the league; **1730s–1790s** Shen Deqian, Weng Fanggang, Yuan Mei, Zhao Yi and the three founders of Tongcheng are alive in the same age (**a rare scene in the history of Chinese literature:** four programmes that would not yield to one another did business at the same time in the same examination market (the upgraded version of Chapter 5's "three sources of income decide three styles of writing"**)); **1840s–1890s** the statecraft prose school and the Xiangxiang school, and Liang Qichao's new style; **1898** *Mashi wentong* 《马氏文通》; **1917–1919** the Literary Revolution (**the full stop of this chapter:** the dispute over programmes ended in "setting up a further new course" (the most thorough version of Lesson 1's "a coup in literary form": **what was overthrown was not the previous school, but the institution of schools itself**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in this chapter
**Reading a "school" as a club** (**the Former and Latter Seven Masters had no clubhouse and no charter:** most of them are camps that later people retrospectively recognised from a few letters and a certain number of prefaces and colophons (the clause in Chapter 1, "four masters named as one set": **the efficiency with which a historian of literature classifies is often higher than the historical facts (**but the business of "taking the Tang as one's ancestral model" did change the feel, in the hand, with which several million people wrote poetry: the camp is a fiction; the discipline is a fact**))); **assigning seats by "progressive / reactionary"** (**imitating the ancients is not the same as being conservative, and *xingling* is not the same as being progressive:** Yuan Mei's poetry is not necessarily better than Shen Deqian's, and Zhao Yi's judgements are not necessarily more correct than Weng Fanggang's (the evolution clause of Lesson 15: **the criterion: what equipment did this school provide for the next fifty years?**)); **ignoring the institutional layer** (**remembering only the slogans, and not the examinations, the academies, the cost of carving and the private secretariats:** the programme is a literary thing, and the market is an administrative thing (the combined accounts of the economy of paying calls in Chapter 4 and of the economy of private lodges in Chapter 5**)); **taking the end of the Qing for "decline"** (**the new style and the textbooks of grammar are not a degeneration of poetry and prose; they are a moving of the institution of evaluation:** from "who writes so as to resemble the ancients" to "who writes so that people can understand" — the latter is an achievement of modernity, and it also plants a new problem of what cannot be classified (Lesson 17 and Lesson 19 each take one share**).

::: exercise Writing a programme {level=1}
For the field you are in, write a "programme" of 80 characters (you may imitate the sentence-form of "诗必盛唐", 'poetry must be High Tang'), required to: **① exclude one item explicitly; ② be teachable; ③ explain in one sentence its worst manner of use**.
::: solution
Marking: **the item excluded must be specific** (**"do not take what is vague and floating" cannot be carried out:** "do not take anyone who cites data but does not give the scope" can be carried out (the dead-method test in Lesson 2**)); **the column for the worst use is the pass line** (**a programme from which the worst use cannot be written out shows that the author does not intend to carry it out (the three questions about a countermeasure in Chapter 5**).
:::

::: exercise The reducibility of ranking {level=2}
Take a scheme of marking that you know well (the grading of essays, a restaurant list, the stars in an app store) and test: **whether its grades can be reduced to a string of sentences of judgement**; if they cannot, point out what it relies on in order to be accepted.
::: solution
Marking: **you may not say only that "it is not objective"** (**you must make clear the source of its legitimacy: expert consensus, traffic, seniority, or history? ::: exercise Short essay: the literature of calls for tenders {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**the literature of the late empire is a system that keeps issuing calls for tenders: every programme claims that it is protecting quality, while in fact each of them is defining who is qualified to submit a work; they attacked one another for four hundred years, and in the end were replaced by three new institutions — the newspaper office, the school and the publishing house; from then on the question was no longer 'by whose model?', but 'for whom is it to be seen?'. The irony is that the new system issues programmes in the same way; it has only exchanged 'like the ancients' for 'like what the reader wants'**". You must call on at least six items: **the four conditions under which a programme grows**, **route theory**, **the Tang–Song school's shift from period to lineage**, **the professionalisation of *xingling***, **the bureaucratic aesthetics of *shenyun* / *gediao* / *jili***, and **the trainability of Tongcheng**; and give a verdict: **of "literature that has a programme" and "literature that has no programme", which more easily produces masterpieces — you must give evidence from one concrete historical stretch of time, and you must admit the sampling bias of the evidence you give**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**conditions, route, lineage, professionalisation, bureaucratic aesthetics, trainability**); the verdict (**for reference: the side that has programmes —** the High Tang, the Jiangxi school and the archaist period of the mid-Ming all have an extremely high density of technique; a programme presses scattered talents into a trade; the side that has no programme —** the late-Ming brief essay, and May Fourth: a period in which standards are a vacuum often has an explosion of forms (the key to the marking sits in the column "sampling bias": **the reason we think that certain periods have many masterpieces is that those periods happened to leave good anthologies and good criticism behind them — **an anthology is itself a kind of programme (the self-reference of Chapter 1's "a catalogue is a censorship": **this question cannot be answered without relying on a programme, and to admit that is the graduation mark of this lesson**)); if the conclusion can be written "**a programme is not the cause of masterpieces; it is the bookkeeping of masterpieces: it decides what gets written down, what gets counted as a poem, and what is left for the next age to regret**", that is an A.

::: summary
The calls for tenders, filed: conditions (**the market, a surplus population, too many classics, societies**); the Ming (**the route, and the rebound; the Tang–Song school changed "ancient" into a lineage; Gui Youguang cut out a budget for "remembering"**); the Qing (***shenyun* deletes the incident, *gediao* sets up the models, *jili* submits the materials, *xingling* becomes an occupation**); genres (**the anthology, *pingdian*, remarks on poetry, ranking by grades, evidential research**); the end (**the institution of evaluation moves: academy → newspaper office → school**). The method, in one sentence: **to read the literature of the late period, first find its call for tenders, then look at what was written on the sly outside the tender document**. Eleven chapters have now been read to the end: from the voice of the Yuan variety play (*zájù* 杂剧) to the programmes of the Qing, the real general title of this course is — **how literature, in the hands of one generation after another, kept hold both of form and of discontent**.
