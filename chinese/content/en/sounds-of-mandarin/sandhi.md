---
title: "Tone sandhi, the neutral tone and erhua"
---

The dictionary says that 你好 is two third (dipping) tones (nǐ hǎo), yet your mouth has never read it that way — what comes out of its own accord is ní hǎo. Tone sandhi, the neutral tone and rhotacisation (*erhua*) are three side doors that connected speech opens onto the tone system: the sound of a character on its own is the system's file, and only continuous connected speech is the site where the system actually runs. This chapter says the rules of the three doors right through to the end: when third tones stand next to each other, which of them rises; what tone 一 and 不 change to; how sentence-final 啊 changes its face with the sound before it; which syllables grow light enough to lose their tone; and how 儿 shrinks from an affix into a single retroflex gesture on the final. Every one of them is grammar that is **systematic, obligatory, and of the kind teaching does not confer — present even with no teaching at all**: you can do it, but you cannot say it; this chapter says it for you to hear.

## Tone sandhi: tones giving way to one another in connected speech

::: definition Tone sandhi {#def-biantiao}
A change of tone that a syllable undergoes in continuous connected speech under the influence of a neighbouring sound is called **tone sandhi**. Unlike the free variation brought on by speed of speech or by emphasis, tone sandhi is systematic: the condition is fixed (what stands before what), the direction is fixed (which tone it changes to), and native speakers are at one. The table of tone values (pitch contours) for Putonghua (standard Mandarin) ([[sounds-of-mandarin/tones]]) records the **isolation tone** (citation tone, the 'basic tone' of the dictionary); in connected reading it is rewritten by the rules of this chapter.
:::

::: theorem Third-tone sandhi: successive thirds take each other's place, and the low never shows its bottom {#rule-shangsheng-biantiao}
(1) **When two third tones are adjacent, the first changes to a second (rising) tone** (35): 你好 [ni³⁵ xau²¹⁴], 水果, 洗脸, 管理. (2) **A third tone before a non-third is read only as a 'half-third'** (21, low and not rising): 北京, 语言, 火车. (3) When three or more third tones are adjacent, **apply the rule in two layers, by tone group**: the first syllable of each two-syllable group changes to 35, and the last syllable keeps the full tone 214; the grouping follows the grammatical structure (the worked example in the next section).
:::

::: proof Why the low rise is always the one that gives way
The perceptual capital of the third tone is 'low' (the remark in [[sounds-of-mandarin/tones]]). When two low dips stand next to each other, the rising tail of the first has not yet got under way before it is swallowed by the falling head of the second, and the low stretches blur into one mass — so the system switches the first syllable onto the second-tone contour, which has the higher contrast, and lets it give way (the older account calls this 'dissimilation': adjacent tones that are the same push each other apart). The 'half-third' is the same logic: when what follows is not a low dip, the rising tail was unnecessary to begin with, and the low level stretch is enough to show what the tone is. This mutual giving way has a variant of its own in each of the Chinese dialects (the sandhi of connected reading in Wu becomes a 'systematic overhaul': the tone value of a syllable is rebuilt for the whole group, far beyond the scale of the patching Putonghua does), from which it can be seen that 'isolation tones belong to the dictionary, and the tones of connected reading belong to the grammar' is the general rule for tone in Chinese.
:::

::: example Grouping when three third tones meet {#ex-san-shang}
小老虎 and 管理好 are both three third tones. What is the actual tone value of each, and how does the grammatical grouping leave a footprint in the pronunciation?
::: solution
管理好 = ［管理］＋［好］ (verb–complement; a Chinese complement, not the complement of English grammar): 管 (before the low dip of 理) → 35, 理 (before the low dip of 好) → 35, 好 → 214, read as [uan³⁵ li³⁵ xau²¹⁴]. 小老虎 = ［小］＋［老虎］: inside the group, 老 before 虎 changes to 35; what 小 sits against is already a risen 35 (no longer a low dip), so by the rule it is read only as a half-third, 21: [ɕiau²¹ lau³⁵ xu²¹⁴]. When a slow reading keeps the grouping distinct, the two patterns stand opposed; in the flux of a fast reading most people level 小老虎 out to 35 35 214 as well — sandhi is applied in a cycle, foot by foot, and both structure and frequency are on the books. To hear that the first two syllables of 管理好 both rise, while the first syllable of 小老虎 is low and level, is precisely the ear's skill in hearing grammatical structure.
:::
:::

::: theorem Tone sandhi of 一 and 不 {#rule-yibu-biantiao}
一 (basic tone yī 55): **before a fourth (falling) tone it is read as a second tone, 35** (一定 [i³⁵ tiŋ⁵¹]); **before a first (high level) tone, a second tone or a third tone it is read as a fourth tone, 51** (一天, 一年, 一起); **used alone, in counting off numbers, in ordinals, and at the end of a word, it returns to 55** (第一, 唯一, '一、二、三'). 不 (basic tone bù 51): **before a fourth tone it is read as a second tone, 35** (不必, 不去); otherwise it does not change. Two notes attached. In the inserted forms 看一看 and 试一试, 一 is already weakened to the neutral tone, and sandhi is not spoken of on top of that. In the Beijing vernacular, 七 and 八 also have a changed reading before a fourth tone (the old-school reading of 七块, close to qí kuài); the Putonghua norm does not adopt it, and the test follows 55.
:::

::: example “坐一天船，看一看，一定不迟到” ('Spend a day on a boat, take a look, and be sure not to be late.' (Our translation.)) {#ex-yi-bu-real}
Mark the actual tone value of every 一 and every 不 in the sentence, and state the condition for each.
::: solution
The 一 of 一天 stands before the first tone 天, and is read 51: [i⁵¹ tʰiɛn⁵⁵]. The 一 of 看一看 is caught between the two halves of a reduplicated verb; the whole syllable is weakened to the neutral tone, and the sandhi table has no entry for it. The 一 of 一定 stands before the fourth tone 定, and is read 35: [i³⁵ tiŋ⁵¹]. The 不 of 不迟到 stands before the fourth tone 到, and is read 35: [pu³⁵]; 迟 remains a second tone, 35, as before. The whole sentence in connected reading (neutral tones inside words not marked in detail): [tsuɔ⁵¹ i⁵¹ tʰiɛn⁵⁵ ʈʂʰuan³⁵，kʰan⁵¹ i· kʰan⁵⁵，i³⁵ tiŋ⁵¹ pu³⁵ ʈʂʰɿ³⁵ tau⁵¹]. The trap in this problem is precisely 看一看: that is the channel of **neutral-tone weakening**, not the channel of tone sandhi — the two pipelines are entered in separate ledgers (the next section, [[#def-qingsheng]]).
:::
:::

::: quiz
In the two words 水果 and 水桶, the actual tone values of the third-tone character 水 are, respectively, (　　).
- [ ] Both are 214
- [x] Both are 35
- [ ] The former is 35, the latter 21
- [ ] The former is 21, the latter 35
::: solution
果 and 桶 are both third tones, and both words belong to 'two third tones adjacent', so the first character changes to 35 in every case. The temptation in this question is to suppose that 桶 and 果 have different tones — looking up the basic tone first, and only then applying the rule, is the only way to solve a sandhi question. If one wants 水 to be read 21, the following character has to be changed for a non-third (水缸).
:::
:::

## The sound change of 啊: the sentence-final modal particle changes costume with the sound before it

The modal particle 啊 (neutral tone, basic sound [a]), stuck on after any syllable, fills in at the boundary a glide of the same colour as the **closing gesture of the preceding sound** — there is no vacuum in connected speech, and a vowel at the end of a sentence is always coloured by the habitual gesture of the segment before it.

::: theorem The sound change of 啊 {#rule-a-bianyin}
Look at the closing gesture of the preceding syllable. Ending in a, e, i, o or ü, it becomes **呀** [ia] (来啊 lái ya, 对啊 duì ya, 去啊 qù ya); ending in u, ao or ou, it becomes **哇** [ua] (走啊 zǒu wa, 好啊 hǎo wa, 漏啊 lòu wa); ending in n, it becomes **呐** [na] (看啊 kàn na, 天啊 tiān na); ending in ng, it becomes a nasalised [ŋa] (唱啊 chàng nga, 冷啊 lěng nga); ending in an apical vowel -i[ɿ] or -i[ʅ], it becomes a continuation of that apical position plus [a] (字啊 [tsɿa], 是啊 [ʂʅa]); with rhotacisation before it, it becomes [ra] (花儿啊 huār ra).
:::

::: proof The consistent logic of assimilation
The i of [ia] is the lingering resonance of the high-vowel tongue position of the preceding sound, the u of [ua] is the lingering resonance of lip-rounding, the n of [na] is the tail of an apical nasal not yet released, and the nasalisation of [ŋa] is the soft palate not having had time to rise — assimilation lets neighbouring gestures save effort, and the written 呀, 哇 and 呐 do no more than enter, on the register, the colouring the ear has heard (editions differ in how strict or how loose the orthography is: the *Xianhan*, *The Contemporary Chinese Dictionary*, mostly gathers them all under 啊 and notes the sound change, while Lü Shuxiang 吕叔湘, in *Eight Hundred Words of Modern Chinese*, *Xiandai Hanyu babai ci* 《现代汉语八百词》, discusses each 啊 separately). Remember that this is an **inevitable** sound change: in natural connected speech, reading 看啊 as kàn a, with a perfectly clean gap in the middle, actually breaks the illusion.
:::

## The neutral tone: the syllable that has had its hat taken off

::: definition Neutral tone {#def-qingsheng}
The phenomenon in which certain syllables in connected speech lose their basic tone and are read **both light and short**. The neutral tone is not a fifth tone category ([[sounds-of-mandarin/tones]]); it is the **neutralisation and weakening** of tone: the duration is shortened, the intensity is reduced, and the vowel is centralised (the vowel of the second syllable of 妈妈 is close to [ə]). Its pitch is not its own to decide, and **is fixed by the tone value of the preceding syllable**: after a first tone ≈ degree 3, after a second tone ≈ degree 2, after a third tone ≈ degree 4, after a fourth tone ≈ degree 2 (the notation in general use; from one author to another the decimal places differ).
:::

::: theorem Where the neutral tone lives {#rule-qingsheng-jia}
(1) **Grammatical elements**: particles (的, 了, 着, 过, 吗, 呢), directional complements (拿起来), the second syllable of a reduplicated verb (看看), the classifier (measure word) 个, and some pronominal suffixes (们). (2) **Word endings**: 子, 头 and 巴 (桌子, 石头, 尾巴). (3) **Markers of a word's senses**: 东西 (dōng·xi, an object / dōng xī, a direction), 兄弟 (xiōng·di, a younger brother / xiōng dì, an elder and a younger brother), 大爷 (dà·ye / dà yé). (4) **Habitual disyllabic words in common use**: 妈妈, 星星, 客气, 商量 — this batch of neutral-tone words, 'with the dictionary's pronunciation-marking as the authority', makes up the word-list of the test (the word-list of neutral-tone syllables in the *Implementation Outline of the Putonghua Proficiency Test*), and it is also the auditory watershed of 'whether or not one can be said to speak Putonghua'.
:::

The four horizontal lines stand for 'the relative height for as long as the neutral-tone syllable lasts' — it is short, which is why the lines need not be drawn long.
:::

::: example The acoustic file on 妈妈 {#ex-mama}
Compare the two syllables of 妈妈 màma, item by item: initial, final, tone value, duration, and how clear the vowel is, and set the differences out one by one.
::: solution
Same source and same sound, and yet different at every point (the basic tone of both syllables comes from 妈 mā): the first syllable is a full 55, its duration ample, its vowel [a] full; the second is neutral tone, its duration about half the first, its pitch about degree 3, its vowel centralised in the direction of [ə], and its initial m short as well. If the second syllable is restored to 55 (mā mā), the impression on the ear at once becomes 'emphasis of the roll-call kind'. The conclusion: the neutral tone is a weakening of the whole set of four, 'tone + length + strength + clarity', leaving in pitch alone a tail that is pushed about by the preceding syllable — and it is exactly for this reason that, when a machine reading aloud pronounces a neutral tone as the original tone, the human ear knows at a single hearing that it is a 'foreign accent'.
:::
:::

::: warning The neutral tone is not light just because you want it light
The **range of neutral-tone words in Putonghua is smaller than in the Beijing vernacular**: in Beijing dialect 告诉 is read close to gàosong, 事情 close to shìqing, and 地方 close to dìfang, whereas a Putonghua dictionary either notes an alternative reading or takes the basic tone as primary — a learner need only take the *Xianhan* and the word-list of the test as authority. Do not, on the ear of a 'Beijing accent turned all the way up' in film and television, expand the output on your own and read 曾经, 必须 and 友谊 light as well; that is passing a dialect habit off as the norm — if the dictionary does not make it light, you do not make it light. When the line taken in the examination and the line taken on the street do not agree, use the former to answer the examination; understanding the difference is the whole of the good faith this chapter has.
:::

## Rhotacisation: an affix shrunk into the final

::: definition Rhotacisation and the rhotacised final {#def-erhua}
When the suffix 儿 (ér) loses its independent syllable in connected speech and merges the retroflex gesture into the final of the preceding syllable, that is called **rhotacisation**; the final so treated is called a **rhotacised final**. In Pinyin it is written by adding r after the original final (花儿 huār, 小孩儿 xiǎor), and 儿 is no longer spelled as a syllable on its own. The 39 finals of Putonghua are merged by rhotacisation into twenty and more rhotacised finals (26 in the account generally current). Merger is its nature: 根儿 and 歌儿 are homophonous after rhotacisation, [kɚ⁵⁵] — the distinction between en and e collapses, in front of the retroflex gesture, into one and the same cell.
:::

The main sound changes of rhotacisation (simplified for teaching; in the detail, authors differ in how they transcribe it): finals ending in -i or -n **drop the coda** (小孩儿 xiǎor [ɕiauɚ²¹⁴], 门儿 mér, 圈儿 qiūr); finals ending in -ng **drop ng and nasalise** (瓶儿 píngr [pʰiɚ̃³⁵], 棒儿 bàngr); the open rhotacised form whose main vowel is i or ü **inserts a mid vowel** as a cushion and then retroflexes (鸡儿 jīr [tɕjiɚ⁵⁵], 小鱼儿 yúr [yɚ³⁵]); the apical vowels -i[ɿ] and -i[ʅ] are replaced wholesale by [ɚ] (字儿 zìr, 事儿 shìr); the remaining finals retroflex directly (花儿 huār, 味儿 wèir [uɚ⁵¹]).

::: example Restoring three rhotacised forms {#ex-erhua-3}
For each of 门儿, 瓶儿 and 事儿, mark the actual phonetic value, say which layer of the final each has moved, and point out one piece of evidence of 'merger'.
::: solution
门儿 [mɚ̃⁵⁵]: the -n coda is dropped, and the vowel is nasalised and retroflexed. 瓶儿 [pʰiɚ̃³⁵]: the -ng coda is dropped, and it is likewise nasalised and retroflexed. 事儿 [ʂɚ⁵¹]: the retroflex apical vowel gives up its place entirely to [ɚ]. The basic-tone finals of the three forms are en, ing and [ʅ] respectively; after rhotacisation they are squeezed into two neighbouring cells (the first two both carry a nasalised [ɚ̃], and 门儿 in its turn stands opposite 歌儿 a cell apart — the distinction between en and e has already collapsed in front of the retroflex gesture). The ledger of the merger is clear at a glance: the rhotacised final is a 'simplified copy' of the final system of Putonghua, and the distinctiveness of the open, even-teeth, closed and round-mouth classes (*kaiqi hecuo*), and of -n and -ng, gives way across the board before the retroflex gesture.
:::
:::

::: theorem The three functions of rhotacisation {#rule-erhua-gongneng}
(1) **Distinguishing meaning**: 头 (the head) / 头儿 (one end; the person who leads), 面 (flour) / 面儿 (something in the form of a powder: 药面儿), 眼 (the organ) / 眼儿 (a small hole), 亮 (an adjective) / 亮儿 (a noun: a place that is bright). (2) **Distinguishing class**: adding 儿 to an adjectival or verbal morpheme and turning it into a noun is a common method of word-formation (尖 → 尖儿, 盖 → 盖儿; whether it can stand as an entry on its own is decided by the headword in the *Xianhan*). (3) **Colouring**: smallness, warmth, casualness (小孩儿, 门缝儿, 玩儿, 在哪儿). Note that rhotacisation in Putonghua **is limited by the headword** (the marking in the *Xianhan* is the authority, and the test outline lists separately a table of rhotacised words in common use). It is not free formation of word-forms on the principle of 'retroflex whenever you feel like it': 水儿 and 狗儿 are not entered in the dictionary, and to write them into a composition is a dialect pronunciation, or the old vernacular, coming on stage.
:::

::: proof How 儿 shrank from a noun into a small gesture
儿, in Middle Chinese the 日 initial and the 支 rhyme, meant 'infant, tiny'. From the Tang on it was bleached into a nominal ending that marks smallness; in the northern speech of the Song and the Yuan the ending 儿 clung closer and closer to the preceding syllable, and in the end it fell from an independent syllable (ér) to a lightly read suffix, and fell again to a retroflex feature on the final — a textbook path of grammaticalisation and of phonetic weakening (of the same kind: 们 in Putonghua, and the nasalisation of English -ing). The evidence is right there on the face of the ledger: an independent 儿 is still alive in 儿子, 儿童 and 儿戏, while the retroflex r is on duty only at the end of a word; the two lifespans of a single character coexist in one and the same dictionary (see [[history-of-chinese/lexical-history]]).
:::

::: quiz
Between 花 huā and 花儿 huār, what has the phonology lost, and what has it gained?
- [ ] One phoneme fewer, and one tone more
- [x] The two characters merge into one sentence, and the final takes on a retroflex feature
- [ ] One rhotacised final fewer, and one phone r more
- [ ] Nothing has changed; only the spelling is different
::: solution
花儿 is a single syllable ([[#def-erhua]]): the spelling has one more r, and in the phonology it is 'a suffix merged into its host' — two syllables shrink to one syllable, and the final gains the retroflex feature of [ɚ]. The formulation 'one phone fewer' is wrong in that r is not an independent phoneme. Listen to 花儿: the basic tone of 儿 cannot be drawn out of it, and that is the proof.
:::
:::

::: quiz
Of the following words, in which does the neutral tone **not distinguish meaning**?
- [ ] 东西 dōng·xi
- [ ] 兄弟 xiōng·di
- [x] 妈妈 mā·ma
- [ ] 大爷 dà·ye
::: solution

:::

::: history
The written evidence for rhotacisation is later than the evidence from speech: in the padding words of Yuan *qu*, and in the vernacular fiction of the Ming and the Qing, the suffix 儿 is already there wherever one looks (the density with which *The Plum in the Golden Vase* (*Jin Ping Mei*) and *Xingshi yinyuan zhuan* 《醒世姻缘传》 ('Marriage Destinies to Awaken the World') use the character 儿 is precisely the density of retroflexion when the text is read aloud), whereas scholars of the rhyme tables were not willing, until the Qing, to found a doctrine for it — from Pan Lei 潘耒 on, 'rhotacisation' gradually entered the footnotes of the rhyme dictionaries. After Putonghua had been established, the neutral tone and rhotacisation, because of their origin in 'Beijing pronunciation', went through a tug-of-war over their status: linguists of the 1950s and 1960s drew up table after table of 'which words should be light, and which should take 儿' (the 1963 *Table for Settling the Pronunciations of Words with Variant Readings in Putonghua (draft)*, and the later pilot textbooks of Putonghua, each set aside a table of its own); the adjudication table of 1985, and the editions of the test outline of 2003 and of 2021, set that line down on the examination paper (a revised draft of the adjudication table was put out for public comment in 2016, and as of 2026 it had not been formally promulgated to replace the edition of 1985 — when you cite a norm of pronunciation, mind which document it is) — the neutral-tone word-list in your hands is the finished product of a seventy-year project of setting the norm.
:::

::: remark The 'domain of jurisdiction' of tone sandhi, the neutral tone and rhotacisation
The three phenomena each take a different linguistic unit as the range within which they operate. Third-tone sandhi takes the **foot/word** as its domain (the disagreement over how to group 小老虎 is living evidence of 'how large the domain is'). The sandhi of 一 and 不 falls between the word and the phrase: a function word is weakened when its meaning is full, and changes when its meaning is empty. The neutral tone and rhotacisation, for their part, are phenomena of **word-morphology** — which words are light, and which words take 儿, is registered word by word in the lexicon (which is why there is a 'word-list' to go by). Carry this clue of the 'domain of jurisdiction' into the dispute, in [[modern-grammar]], over inflection as against word-formation, and into the problem, in [[linguistics/morphology]], of 'what a word is', and one finds that the three side doors of this chapter in fact lead to the main gate of morphology.
:::

## What comes next

The 'execution on site' of tone sandhi, the neutral tone and rhotacisation is put to a combined test in the connected speech of reading aloud, and [[sounds-of-mandarin/putonghua-test]] gives the question-types of the test. For the typology of tone sandhi in connected reading (the 'systematic rebuilding' route of the connected-tone sandhi of Wu), see [[dialects/wu]]. The origin and the dating of that batch of lightly read suffixes, 儿, 们 and 了, belong to [[history-of-chinese/lexical-history]] and to the study of grammaticalisation. The check-up sheet for the points this chapter asks you to know is hidden in the speech you produced yesterday: record one minute of free chat, and count how many neutral tones you actually used and how many times third-tone sandhi occurred — you will find that not a single rule was missed; they have simply never asked you.

::: summary
- Tone sandhi is an obligatory rewriting, worked by connected speech: when two third tones are adjacent the first changes to 35; a third tone before a non-third is read as a half-third, 21; three third tones read in a row are handled by grouping according to the grammar ([[#rule-shangsheng-biantiao]]).
- 一: 35 before a fourth tone, 51 otherwise, and 55 when alone, in an ordinal, or in final position; 不: 35 before a fourth tone ([[#rule-yibu-biantiao]]).
- Sentence-final 啊 changes, with the closing gesture of the preceding sound, into 呀哇呐 or nga — assimilation, not child's play ([[#rule-a-bianyin]]).
- The neutral tone is a weakening of the whole set of four; its pitch follows the preceding syllable (3/2/4/2), and it lives in grammatical elements, word endings, sense-distinguishing entries, and the list of habitual words.
- Rhotacisation shrinks 儿 into the final: dropping the coda, nasalising, merging finals. Its functions are distinguishing meaning, distinguishing class, and colouring; its range takes the word-list as its authority, and does not rely on inspiration.
- The three phenomena have different domains of jurisdiction: sandhi belongs to the foot, and the neutral tone and rhotacisation belong to word-morphology — this clue leads straight to morphology.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise The pitch of the neutral tone {level=1 check="4"}
By the figures in general use, in a combination of the 桌子上 type, 'third tone + neutral tone', at about which degree is the pitch of the neutral-tone syllable?
::: solution
4, the fourth degree (the type of 姐·姐 and 奶·奶): a rebound from the trough of the preceding syllable, and the neutral-tone syllable lands at mid-high. The four-value mnemonic is 阴三阳二上四去二 ('first three, second two, third four, fourth two') ([[#def-qingsheng]]).
:::

::: exercise The account of the rhotacised finals {level=1 check="26"}
Into how many rhotacised finals do the textbooks in general use (the Huang–Liao textbook) merge the finals of Putonghua once they have been rhotacised?
::: solution
26 (the counts differ slightly from one author to another; the examination follows the textbook). Merger is the nature of rhotacisation: finals one can tell apart are, at a single 儿, squeezed into the same cell — 根儿 and 歌儿 being homophonous is exactly that (see [[#ex-erhua-3]]).

::: exercise Mark the actual tone values {level=1}
For each of the three groups 保险箱, 老读者 and 李小姐, mark the **actual tone value** of every syllable (not the basic tone), and write the grouping.
::: solution
保险箱 = ［保险］＋［箱］: 保, coming before two third tones, changes to 35; what follows 险 is a first tone, so it is read as a half-third, 21; 箱 is 55. The whole form is [pau³⁵ ɕian²¹ ɕiaŋ⁵⁵]. 老读者 = ［老］＋［读者］: 老, before the second tone 读, is read 21; 读 is 35; 者, at the end of the word, is read in the full tone 214. The whole form is [lau²¹ tu³⁵ ʈʂɤ²¹⁴]. 李小姐 = ［李］＋［小姐］, and inside the group 小姐 the syllable 小 before 姐 changes to 35; 李, in its turn, before the underlying third tone 小, changes to 35; 姐 is 214. The whole form is [li³⁵ ɕiau³⁵ tɕiɛ²¹⁴]. The three groups demonstrate three depths of grouping. The weight of this problem lies in 'group first, then apply' — whoever does not ask about the structure, and simply memorises 'three third tones = 35 35 214', is counted as not having completed it.

::: exercise The five faces of 啊 {level=2}
For each of 快来看啊, 真好啊, 加油啊, 唱啊 and 是啊, write the actual pronunciation of 啊 (using characters such as 呀, 哇 and 呐, together with the phonetic value), and note the preceding sound it follows.
::: solution
来看啊: 看 ends in [n] → [na] (呐). 好啊: 好 ends in [u] → [ua] (哇). 加油啊: 油 ends in [u] (the coda of iou) → [ua] (哇). 唱啊: 唱 ends in [ŋ] → [ŋa], or nasalised. 是啊: 是 ends in -i[ʅ] → [ʅa] (the auditory impression lies near 扎, between unaspirated and voiced). When the characters are written, following custom with 呀, 哇 and 呐 is acceptable, and writing 啊 throughout is also acceptable; the phonetic notation must be true to the fact.
:::

::: exercise The neutral tone and the meaning of a word {level=2}
“他说她是我的**兄弟**。” ('He says that she is my 兄弟.' (Our translation.)) “这**东西**不错。” ('This 东西 is not bad.' (Our translation.)) Is the neutral tone there in each sentence, or not? What does each of the two readings mean? Then make up for yourself a pair of sentences that distinguish meaning by the neutral tone.
::: solution
兄弟: read xiōng·di (with 弟 light) it means the younger brother, one person; read xiōng dì (both in the basic tone) it means the elder and the younger brother, two people (or a polite form they use of each other). 东西: dōng·xi means an object; dōng xī means a direction. A sample of sentences made up for the purpose: “他［盖ɡài］被子” and “他盖了个［盖儿ɡàr］” — a verbal reading and a reading as a thing, one of them light and one of them rhotacised, each doing its own job. Any opposition that falls inside the word-list (of the kind 头／头儿 and 眼／眼儿) counts. What the marking looks at is whether one accepts that **the meaning of the full-tone reading is also a legitimate sense** (and not 'a misreading'), which answers to the third kind in [[#rule-qingsheng-jia]].
:::

::: exercise Judging whether a rhotacisation is legitimate {level=2}
小孩儿, 花儿, 猫儿, 水儿 — decide, word by word, whether each is a standard headword of Putonghua, and state your three-step method of judging.
::: solution
Three steps. First, look it up in the *Xianhan*: where the headword carries 儿, it stands as standard (小孩儿 and 花儿 are both entered; 水儿 and 猫儿 are not). Second, test the feel for the language: taken out of context, can it come straight out of the mouth? Third, match it to a function: which of the three in [[#rule-erhua-gongneng]] does it fit? Conclusion: the first two are legitimate (belonging, respectively, to a sense of colouring and to a headword in common use); the latter two have no entry on the dictionary's books, and belong to a free dialect form or to the old vernacular. In composition, and in the test of reading aloud, they carry no r, in any case. 猫儿腻, in an affected literary tone, is an idiom as a whole, and it cannot save 猫儿 as an entry on its own. What this problem trains is not the guessing of words, but **the accomplishment of looking a norm up**: every character has a source, and every item can be checked against the table.
:::

::: exercise A sandhi check-up on a passage {level=3}
Read aloud, and record: “晚上好，老李！我找小李，他家里人说他在午睡。等一会儿再打搅他，好不好？” ('Good evening, Old Li! I am looking for Little Li; the people at his house say he is taking his midday nap. Wait a moment and then disturb him — all right?' (Our translation.)) Mark in it the third-tone sandhi, the half-thirds, the sandhi of 一, the neutral tones and the rhotacisation; count the places of each kind; and check them against the recording.
::: solution
A reference list (the line taken by a slow reading; pauses and personal habit will produce variants, and what you can defend is what counts): 晚上 — 上 is neutral tone, and 晚 is accordingly read as a half-third; 老李 and 小李 — 老 and 小, before 李 (a third tone), change to 35; 我找 — 我, before 找 (a third tone), changes to 35; 午睡 — 午, before a fourth tone, is read 21; 一会儿 — 一, before the fourth tone 会儿, is read 35, and 会 is rhotacised as huìr; 打搅 — 打, before 搅 (a third tone), changes to 35, and 搅, followed by the neutral-tone 他, is read as a half-third, 21; 好不好 — the earlier 好, before the lightly read 不, is for the most part read as a half-third, and the 好 at the end of the sentence keeps the full tone 214. The count, roughly: a change to 35 where third tones meet, about four places; half-thirds, four or five places; one change of 一; neutral tones, two or three places; rhotacisation, one place. The marking looks at the procedure: the list, the recording and the check against each other, all three present, and any place that clashes with the headword pronunciation in the *Xianhan* already noted.
:::

::: exercise The give in the norm {level=3}
The 们 of 我们 and of 他们, and the neutral tone of 事情, 告诉 and 地方, are not marked alike in the dictionaries and in the test outline (an alternative reading; light or full, either will do). Some teachers hold that 'if it can be light, make it light'; some textbooks for broadcast announcing hold that one should 'follow the dictionary'. Write three hundred characters on how you would deal with it: how the three coordinates — the norm, the feel for the language, and the examination room — are to be weighed.
::: hint
Materials you may use: what the guide to the use of the *Xianhan* says about the marking of the neutral tone; the neutral-tone word-list in the *Outline of the Putonghua Proficiency Test* (take the edition you have to hand as the authority); three minutes of news read aloud, and three minutes of *xiangsheng* (cross-talk), that you have listened to.
:::
::: solution
A position offered for reference. The dictionary's 'may be light or may be full' records synchronic variation as it is; it is not a piece of evasion. The test takes the word-list as its authority, because marking needs a boundary. One's own speech follows the feel for the language (close to natural spoken Beijing), and there is no need to 'recite the list and read light'; otherwise a 'bookish lightness' becomes a new kind of unnaturalness. When the three conflict, the order switches with the occasion: in the examination room the word-list comes first, when pronunciation is being marked in writing the dictionary comes first, and in free conversation what is natural comes first — an answer that states the coordinates clearly is a good answer.
:::
