Word classes are the first periodic table in the study of grammar — hydrogen, helium, lithium and boron line up readily, while carbon, nitrogen and oxygen come to blows. This lesson inventories the content words of Chinese against the delivery note of the “twelve classes” of pedagogical grammar, then surveys in particular three boundary lines: noun and verb, verb and adjective, and that “distinguishing word” which nobody is willing to take in.

## The master table of the twelve classes and the gates of the six content-word houses {#sec-shier}

::: definition The twelve classes and the three criteria {#def-shier}
Pedagogical grammar (the Huang–Liao framework) divides words into twelve classes: **six content-word classes** — nouns, verbs, adjectives, numerals, classifiers (measure words) and pronouns; **four function-word classes** — adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions and particles (a lesson to themselves in the next chapter); **two independent classes** — interjections and onomatopoeic words. Classification weights three criteria: **distribution** (which frames a word can enter) is primary, **meaning** (lexical-semantic class) is secondary, and **function** (whether it can be used alone, and as which constituent) is the check. A shorthand for the house rules of the six content-word houses: nouns enter “数量×、×的”; verbs enter “×不×、×着/了/过、(+宾语)”; adjectives enter “很×、×不×” and refuse an object; numerals and classifiers, “数+量+名”, run in one continuous chain; what a pronoun eats is the “指代框架” (the frame of pointing and standing for).
:::

::: theorem Three stock moves for telling the big three apart {#rule-sanbanfu}
**The noun–verb dispute**: what can take an object is a verb (“学习”); what can be modified by a numeral and a classifier is a noun (“一种学习”); where both are possible, one decides between “动名兼类” (verb–noun dual membership) and “动词名用” (a verb used as a noun) by looking for a **gap in meaning** — in “进行调查研究” the meaning of “研究” has not changed, and the mainstream judgement is nominalised use rather than dual membership. **The adjective–verb dispute**: if “很×” works (很干净) and the word **cannot take an object** (*很他头发), it is an adjective; if it can take an object, and can take “了” to mark a change (变了颜色), it is a verb — the middle ground is called **state verbs / psychological verbs** (“怪、愧、急人”). The ultimate move is the **“×不×” question test**: predicates in Chinese all take it (好不好 / 去不去 / 是不是); only what the question cannot stump is not a predicate.
:::

::: example Four questions in a row on 给 {#ex-gei}
Judge the word class of 给 in these four sentences: ①他给我一本书。②你给我老实点。③衣服给洗了。④给大家讲讲。
::: solution
① A verb of giving (at home in the double-object construction); ② in an imperative, “给” has already bleached to the edge of a modal or urging marker (the field may class it as a preposition or as an auxiliary verb; an answer that notes the disagreement counts as correct); ③ a passive marker (the sense of “被”; “给” marking the passive is mostly used for undesirable events, and has a strong colloquial colour); ④ a preposition (it introduces the target, in the sense of “向大家讲讲”). One chain of bleaching runs through them: verb of giving > beneficiary/target preposition > passive and urging marker — **the same graph carries four stations on a spectrum of word classes**, a specimen still in service on the grammaticalisation ladder (the grammaticalisation entry under [[modern-vocabulary/lexicon-layers]]).
:::
:::

## Three households on the boundary {#sec-bianjie}

::: definition Distinguishing words, state words and time words {#def-sanhu}
**Distinguishing words** (non-predicative adjectives): 金、男、野生、主要、初级、大型 — they can only modify a noun directly, and they **do not enter “很×”, do not serve as a predicate, and with “的” added in front become a “的”-phrase** (*很男、×这很金; “正的” and “副的” ✓). This is where a case left open for a hundred years, outside the “介词” ('prepositions') of *Mashi Wentong* 《马氏文通》 ('Ma's Comprehensive Grammar'), finally settled: the subclasses of the noun cannot take them in, and adjectives cannot take them in (they cannot be made into predicates), so a household was set up for them alone. **State words**: 绿油油、干干净净、血红、傻乎乎 — “很” will not eat them (*很绿油油), yet they carry degree meaning of their own, and as a predicate they must take “的” (脸色煞白的); the relation of the adjective to the state word is roughly that of a property to a snapshot. **Time words**: 明天、刚才、从来 — serving as subject is disputed (the schools differ in their criteria for “明天是周六”), but they **serve directly as an adverbial and need no preposition** (明天见), and they can also be modified by “的” (下周二**的**会) — a noun's household register and an adverb's timetable; pedagogical grammar lodges them in custody with the noun, while each school of linguistics hands down its own verdict.
:::

::: history A hundred years of change in views of word class {#sec-cilei}
In 1898 *Mashi Wentong* 《马氏文通》 set up nine classes — 名字 (nouns), 代字 (pronouns), 动字 (verbs), 静字 (stative words), 状字 (adverbs), 介字 (prepositions), 连字 (conjunctions), 助字 (particles) and 叹字 (interjections) — and the study of Chinese word classes opened for business; Li Jinxi 黎锦熙, in *Xinzhu guoyu wenfa* 《新著国语文法》 (1924), took “依句辨品” ('telling the class by the sentence') to an extreme — words have no fixed class, and the slot determines the office; after the war the pedagogical mainstream compromised on the “多功能说” ('the multifunction theory': a word has a proper class, and may be borrowed); the great discussion of word classes in the 1950s (the dispute over the criteria for word classes in Chinese) established the distributional criterion as primary; Lü Shuxiang 吕叔湘, in *Hanyu yufa fenxi wenti* 《汉语语法分析问题》 (1979), made the “词类两难” ('the dilemma of word class') into common sense that anyone can see, and settled the matter in one sentence: distribution is the hard truth, and the boundary is living history. The twelve classes of the Huang–Liao textbook are the agreement now in force from this hundred-year negotiation.
:::

::: warning Three things to guard against in word-class exercises
First, guard against **cutting by meaning in one stroke**: “勇敢” is an adjective — “很勇敢” ✓, and “勇敢” as subject ✓ (“勇敢是一种品质”) — an adjective need not change its form to serve as subject or object, and anyone who judges by meaning that it is an “抽象名词” ('abstract noun') comes to grief; second, guard against **checking the household register of every word**: a word class is a statistical tendency of a **class**, and individual cases always drift (words such as “代表”); what must be memorised is the frame, not the label; third, guard against **neglecting how systematic classifiers are**: the semantic choice between noun classifiers and nouns (条 for long things, 张 for flat things, 粒 for small round things) is the most poetic part of Chinese grammar, and also a volcano of errors in Chinese as a foreign language (*一匹牛) — the function words of the next lesson, and the classifiers of this lesson, are two vital points of Chinese grammar.
:::

::: quiz
Which of the following can **best** prove that “金” is a distinguishing word rather than an adjective? (　)
- [ ] 金 can modify a noun
- [x] “金” cannot be said as “很金”, nor can it serve as a predicate
- [ ] 金 is a nominal morpheme
- [ ] 金 can form “金的”
::: solution
The three refusals of the distinguishing word: it does not enter “很”, it does not serve as a full predicate, and it does not take “了”; it serves only as a direct attributive (or becomes a “的”-phrase) — negative evidence from distribution is the most solid.
:::
:::

::: quiz
What the opposition “很干净 / *很雪白” shows is (　).
- [ ] 雪白 is a noun
- [x] A state word carries degree meaning of its own and refuses degree adverbs
- [ ] 干净 is a state word
- [ ] Chinese has no system of degree
::: solution
Qualitative adjectives can eat “很”; state words (雪白、绿油油) are already saturated for degree — a specimen of the division of labour between form and meaning.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The main grammatical function of the classifier system is (　).
- [ ] To mark the plural
- [x] Together with numerals, to measure nouns, and to carry information of shape and of pragmatics
- [ ] To emphasise the time-span of a verb
- [ ] To stand in for a preposition
::: solution
Inside the frame “数+量+名” the classifier is at once obligatory (*三书) and meaningful (the choice of 条 / 张 / 粒 exposes the shape class of the noun) — a poetry of grammar that Chinese alone possesses.
:::

::: exercise Sorting by frames {level=1}
Using the four frames “很×、×不×、+宾语、数量+×”, test each of “容易、讨论、丰富、骄傲” once, judge its class, and point out where a word has more than one function.
::: solution
容易: 很 ✓, ×不 ✓, object ✗ → adjective; 讨论: 很 ✗, object ✓ (讨论问题), and it can reduplicate (讨论讨论) → verb; 丰富: 很 ✓ plus object ✓ (丰富文化生活) → adjective–verb dual membership (the causative use has already become fixed); 骄傲: 很 ✓, object ✗, serving as subject ✓ (“骄傲使人落后”) → adjective (occupying subject or object position ≠ compulsory nominalisation). Judging the classes and pointing out that “丰富” is amphibious earns full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise A clinic for learner errors {level=2}
Write a rule-based explanation of three erroneous sentences by foreign learners: ① “*我明天见了你”; ② “*他很高了个子”; ③ “*三条马”.
::: solution
① “明天” locates the event in the future, which conflicts with the “了” of completion — a time word is a locating frame, not an ordinary noun; ② an adjective in Chinese cannot take a patient object, and the sentence has to be changed to “他个子很高” (a sentence with a subject–predicate predicate; see [[modern-grammar/special-constructions]]) or to “他高”, which is limited to the frame of a comparative sentence; ③ classifiers are chosen by semantic class: horses take “匹” — errors with 条 / 张 / 粒 expose transfer from a native language that has no classifier system. If each of the three explanations hits the vital point, that is full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: are word classes discovered or invented? {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Are word classes like chemical elements (waiting objectively to be inventoried) or like administrative divisions (useful, but open to choice)? Build the argument on the three “boundary households” of this lesson (distinguishing words, state words and time words): for each household, compare the scheme of setting it up alone with the scheme of lodging it in custody with another class; in one sentence of comparison, cite the English point that “nominalisation needs no change of form” and the conjugation of Japanese adjectives; finally, state your choice of framework (priority to pedagogical concision versus priority to theoretical consistency).
::: solution
Marking points: for each of the three households, give two schemes of placement and the cost of each (for example, merging distinguishing words into adjectives → the “很” test fails; merging them into nouns → the attributive function is in surplus); the comparison must not overstep (in a language rich in morphology, word class is a morphological class; in Chinese it is a distributional class — the two are commensurable, but one must not be forced onto the other); the statement of a position must show an awareness of criteria (the optimal cut of the same material differs with the purpose — invention is in the service of discovery).
:::

::: summary
The six houses of content words issue identity cards by distributional frames; the big three are sorted with three stock moves (taking an object, 很×, ×不×); the three boundary households — distinguishing words, state words and time words — remind us that word classes in Chinese are **prototype categories with a statistical tendency**, not a household register of black and white. The classifier is at once the grammatical calling card of Chinese and a powder magazine for teaching the language to non-native speakers; the four questions in a row on “给” rehearse in advance the life story of the protagonists of the function-word chapter — content words bleach, function words take on substance, and the riverbed of word classes is forever changing course. Memorising the table is no match for practising the frames: the next lesson is function words, looking specifically at the “语法泥金” ('grammatical gold in the silt') that these changes of course have laid down.
:::
:::
