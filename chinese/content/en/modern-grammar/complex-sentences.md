A simple sentence is a detached house; a complex sentence is a housing estate: the property-management contract (the connectives) is sometimes written in as fixed terms, and sometimes left entirely to the neighbours' tacit understanding. This lesson does three things: draw clear the boundary between simple and complex sentences (a famous gradient), recognise the eleven kinds of relation, and learn to "cut the storeys" of a two-layer complex sentence.

## The boundary between simple and complex sentences {#sec-jie}

::: definition The boundary and three tests {#def-jie}
The principle: what **contains two or more subject–predicate structures, none of which is a syntactic constituent of another** is a complex sentence within one sentence; otherwise it remains a simple sentence. Three tests. ① The **constituent test**: in 我知道他会来 ('I know he will come'), 他会来 as a whole is the object of 知道, so the sentence is simple. ② The **sharing test**: 他拿了票进场坐下 ('he took his ticket, went in and sat down') shares a subject, and the actions come in a run, so it is a serial-verb simple sentence (one "set" of actions, not several sentences). ③ **Connective assignment**: if a connective can be inserted and the clauses can each stand independently as a sense of their own, it is for the most part a complex sentence (他不但来了，还迟到了, 'not only did he come, he was late'). An honest statement: **the boundary between simple and complex sentences in Chinese is a gradient**. Serial-verb constructions, pivotal constructions, subject–predicate structures used as objects, and flowing clauses arrange that middle ground into a spectrum. Pedagogical grammar draws the line by the double condition of "structure + meaning": a compromise that can be used, which is not the same thing as a perfect one.
:::

::: definition Two families for the eleven relations {#def-shiyi}
**Coordinate complex sentences** (the clauses are of equal rank): parallel (既…又…/一边…一边…), successive (先…然后…/……接着…), progressive (不但…而且…), elucidative (也就是说). **Subordinate complex sentences** (one main clause, one dependent): causal (因为…所以/既然…就), adversative (虽然…但是/然而), hypothetical (如果…就/要是…的话), conditional (只要…就/只有…才/无论…都), concessive (即使…也), purposive (以便/免得). Conjunctions may be **entirely absent**: "下雨了，不去了。" ('It's raining, so I'm not going.') — a paratactic complex sentence (意合), and the default mode of Chinese. Connectives are parts that make the relation explicit; whether to fit them, and which pair to fit, is in every case a decision of meaning and register.
:::

::: example Cutting the storeys of a two-layer complex sentence {#ex-erchong}
"虽然天气很冷，但是大家干得热火朝天，因为任务明天必须完成。" ('Although the weather was very cold, everyone worked at a fiery pitch, because the task had to be finished tomorrow.') — cut out two layers of relation.
::: solution
The first cut falls at the **adversative**: "天气冷" and the whole of what follows stand in an adversative relation (虽然｜但是+因为). The second cut, inside the "但是", falls at the **causal** (干得热火朝天‖因为任务明天必须完成). How to draw it: first find the **connective pair with the widest scope** ("虽然…但是…" spans the whole sentence, and so is the first layer), then look inside what remains for the second layer. **The test for storeys** is a demolition test. Remove the "因为…" clause, and the adversative relation of the whole sentence is still intact; remove "虽然…", and the causal stretch stands on its own, but its tone is left hanging. Which layer is higher and which is lower can then be judged at once.
:::
:::

::: intuition The flowing sentence: Chinese's clause motorway
"武松把门只一推，天天地下望时，静嘻嘻的没有一个人坐。" (*Water Margin*, old vernacular) — Wu Song gave the door only a single push; when he looked down at the ground under the sky, it was giggling-still, and not a single person was sitting. (Our translation.) Clause follows clause, relying not on conjunctions but on temporal order and a topic that runs on unbroken. This is the **flowing sentence** (*liushuiju*): there is no marking of main and subordinate, and the relations assemble themselves from word order and context. It is the norm of speech and of the classical vernacular, and a hard problem for the theory of complex sentences (analysts from Zhu Dexi 朱德熙 to the contemporary generative school have all been fitting the flowing sentence with "invisible structure"). The pedagogical position: **the flowing sentence is the zero-marked form of the complex sentence**. Reading it depends on a feel for Chinese; writing it depends on a sense of measure in punctuation. The comma is the waterline of the flow; only the full stop is the dyke.
:::

::: history Parataxis and hypotaxis: a naming in typology {#sec-yihe}
The claim that "Eastern languages are paratactic (意合, parataxis) and Western languages hypotactic (形合, hypotaxis)" is a stock pair in the typological linguistics of the twentieth century. From Lü Shuxiang 吕叔湘, *Hanyu yufa fenxi wenti* 《汉语语法分析问题》 ('Problems in the Grammatical Analysis of Chinese'), onwards, the field of modern Chinese has said again and again that Chinese clauses are joined by meaning and word order, and that connectives are "optional explicit marking", whereas English is the reverse, and subordination is syntactically obligatory. This pair of concepts tells the question "why Chinese people, in writing compositions, are fond of carrying one comma all the way to the end, and why a British teacher marks a run-on sentence" as **a typological fate rather than personal carelessness**. Discourse training in writing (cohesion and coherence) takes the baton on from grammar at that point (see [[rhetoric]] and the group of writing courses).
:::

::: warning Four ailments of connectives
First, **mismatched pairing**. "由于…因此…" mixes two sets (由于…所以／因为…因此, each of which keeps to its own number, and the registers differ as well). Second, **positional violation**. There is a rule for a connective travelling with the subject. **When the two clauses have the same subject, the connective comes after the subject** (他不但学习好，体育也好). **When the subjects differ, the connective comes before the subject** (a correct example: 不但他不肯来，他父亲也不肯来 — "不但" leads in the first subject). Third, **redundancy**. "之所以…的原因是因为…" is a three-headed causal monster. Fourth, **breach of logic**. "因为…所以…" is forced onto two matters with no causal link between them (因为今天星期三，所以天气好 — a connection in form, and bankruptcy in meaning; the chapter on faulty sentences will hear it as the same case).
:::

::: quiz
"我知道他会来。" is (　).
- [ ] a causal complex sentence
- [x] a simple sentence (a subject–predicate phrase as object)
- [ ] a pivotal sentence
- [ ] a serial-verb sentence
::: solution
他会来 as a whole is the object of 知道. Once it is a constituent of the larger sentence, the whole is a simple sentence. The constituent test is the first gate between simple and complex sentences.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The relation in "路远，我不去了。" is (　).
- [x] paratactic causation (zero marking)
- [ ] parallel
- [ ] adversative
- [ ] conditional
::: solution
Meaning assembles the causal relation by itself, and connectives are entirely absent. Parataxis is the Chinese default, not an ellipsis.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The first-layer relation in "虽然他说得对，但是你不该顶嘴。" is (　).
- [ ] causal
- [x] adversative
- [ ] progressive
- [ ] explanatory
::: solution
The span of "虽然…但是…" covers the whole sentence, and so is the first layer. There is no second layer inside: a single-layer complex sentence of two clauses.
:::

::: exercise Sorting simple from complex {level=1}
Judge simple or complex, and give the grounds. ①他低着头走过去。②他低着头，走了过去。③组织讨论并通过名单。④群众讨论了，也通过了这个方案。
::: solution
① A serial-verb simple sentence (a shared subject, the actions forming one set). ② The comma divides it in two. On most accounts it is judged a **successive complex sentence** (the clauses are not constituents of one another). It is also permissible to judge a "comma inside a same-subject sentence" as a simple sentence — noting the disagreement earns the mark. ③ A simple sentence with a coordinate phrase as its predicate (no pause, and a shared object). ④ A complex sentence (parallel: the shared object "方案" is placed later, that is, parallel clauses with ellipsis depending on what follows, 蒙下省略). Full marks if all four judgements are grounded.
:::
:::

::: exercise Surveying the storeys {level=2}
Cut the layers of this three-layer complex sentence and label the relations: "如果下雨，运动会就改期或者取消，因为会期已定，不能轻易改动。"
::: solution
The first layer is **causal** (the first two clauses ‖ 因为…不能轻易改动 — "因为" governs through to the end of the sentence). The second layer is inside the "如果" stretch: **hypothetical** (如果下雨 ‖ 就改期或取消). The third layer, "改期或者取消", is **alternative** (inside a coordinate phrase, and already down at phrase level — whether it counts as a third layer depends on the account; it is enough to keep phrase level and clause level distinct). Full marks if the cuts and the relations are all correct.
:::
:::

::: exercise Doctor to faulty writing {level=2}
Revise, and classify the cause of the fault. ①他不但成绩好，而且也很努力。②尽管天气不好，可是仍然出发。③我所以喜欢《水浒》，是因为人物写得鲜明的原因。
::: solution
① The "也" after "而且" is redundant beside the progressive frame (delete "也"), and the two axes ought to be of the same rank: "成绩好" and "很努力" are one a result and one a cause, so the progressive relation is strained. Revise to "他不但成绩好，而且很用功" (the subject is the same, so "不但" comes after the subject). ② The second clause lacks a subject ("尽管天气不好，可是我们仍然出发"). The subjects differ, so ellipsis depending on what precedes (蒙上省略) is not legitimate. ③ "之所以…是因为…的原因" is a three-headed causal monster; delete "的原因". The three faults, one apiece: connective redundancy, omission of the subject, and a mixed construction. Full marks if all are correct.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the national ailment of one comma all the way to the end {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Take as a sample a real piece of online writing or a student composition (no fewer than 60 characters, with the source noted, or marked as written by yourself), count its commas and its problems of sentence division, use the theory of this lesson to explain the causes of "one comma all the way to the end" (the trio of the flowing-sentence tradition × the absence of teaching on punctuation × Europeanised long sentences), and set out a "proposal of norms for dividing sentences" (three items each, for public accounts or for official documents).
::: solution
Marking points: a genuine sample, plus a count of comma density; each of the three voices of the cause is accounted for (the paratactic tradition supplies the soil, there is no training in the semicolon or the full stop, and there are chain sentences in translationese); the suggestions can be carried out (for example: consider a sentence break as soon as the subject changes; a long chain after "的" or "了" that runs past 40 characters is to be checked without exception; the semicolon is used for large parallel clauses). Do not commit the relativist error that "Chinese does not attend to syntax, and so one may divide sentences at random".
:::

::: summary
Between the simple sentence and the complex sentence there is a spectrum (the constituent test, the sharing test and connective assignment are three vernier callipers). The eleven relations fall under two great families (coordinate and subordinate), and connectives are explicit-marking parts that can be fitted or taken off. The Chinese default is parataxis, and the flowing sentence is the zero-marked motorway. The mnemonic for cutting storeys: the greatest span goes first, and what remains is handled by recursion. The four ailments of connectives (mismatch, misplacement, redundancy, breach of logic) and "one comma all the way to the end" are regulars of this lesson's clinic. Beneath the complex sentence are the constituents; above the complex sentence is the text. The next lesson is also the close of this course: the faulty-sentence clinic, which puts every tool you have learned through one round of diagnosis.
:::
