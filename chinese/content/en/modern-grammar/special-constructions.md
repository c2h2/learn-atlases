“把, 被, 存现, 连动, 兼语” — the *ba* construction, the *bei* passive, the existential sentence, the serial-verb construction and the pivotal construction — the five great thief-catchers of pedagogical grammar: each of them an everyday matter, each of them high-risk for foreigners. This lesson opens the museum's display cases room by room: on each construction hang three plaques — **formal conditions, semantic ground colour, and historical origin**.

## The *ba* construction: the modern signboard of the disposal construction {#sec-ba}

::: definition The three-piece set of the *ba* construction {#def-ba}
Pattern: **subject + 把 + object + verb + following element**. Three checks at the gate: ① the predicate cannot be a bare verb (*我把书看 → 我把书看了／看完了／看了一遍 — 'I *ba* read the book' gives way to 'I have read the book / finished reading the book / read the book once through'; only when a following element is added does it stand firm); ② the object of 把 is **definite** (我把那本书看完了, 'I finished that book', / *我把一本书看完了, 'I finished a book'); ③ the verb must carry the force of disposal (*他把孩子很听话×, 'he *ba* the child very obedient'; psychological verbs of the unwilling kind and of the recognising kind, 不情愿 and 认得, are out). Negation and modal verbs stand before 把: 我没把作业做完, 'I did not finish the homework', ／他应该把真相说出来, 'he ought to speak the truth out'. Semantic ground colour = **disposal** (the action is applied to an object and causes that object to be affected), so the *ba* construction is by nature the golden partner of the resultative complement (把窗子打开, 'open the window', ／把敌人打得落花流水, 'beat the enemy into fallen flowers and flowing water'). Origin: the original meaning of 把 is 'to hold, to take' (in Du Fu 杜甫, “醉把茱萸仔细看”, 'Drunk, I hold the dogwood and look at it with care.' (Our translation.), it is still a holding in the hand); in the Tang it was bleached into a preposition of instrument or of object, and the disposal construction flourished in the Song–Yuan vernacular (*baihua*) — **the *ba* construction is a history of grammaticalisation held in the hand**.
:::

::: example Clinic for the *ba* construction {#ex-ba2}
Judge whether each is legitimate, and say at which gate it is caught: ① 我把他认出来了 ('I recognised him'). ② 我把眼泪流了下来 ('I flowed the tears down'). ③ 他把球踢进了球门 ('he kicked the ball into the goal'). ④ 他把戏演得很精彩 ('he performed the play brilliantly').
::: solution
① Marginally legitimate: the disposal sense of 认出 ('recognise') is weak, and the sentence stands only because 出来 ('out') supplies the result — the *ba* construction's demand that the verb have a 'sense of acting-upon' is strictest here; ② not legitimate: shedding tears is an event that happens of itself, and the tears have not been disposed of — **the object of 把 must be what is affected**; ③ meets the standard: 踢进 ('kick in') = action + result, and the object is definite; ④ legitimate (“演得很精彩”, 'performed it brilliantly', completes the predicate) — but a continuous reading of 把戏 collides with the noun 把戏 ('a trick, a stunt'), and it is ambiguous to the ear. Full marks when each of the four cases hits the mark.
:::
:::

## The *bei* passive, existential sentences, serial verbs and pivots {#sec-bei}

::: definition The *bei* passive and its kin {#def-bei}
Pattern: **patient + 被 (+ agent) + verb + following element** (他被我批评了一顿, 'he was given a telling-off by me'; *他被我打, 'he was hit by me'). Three gates: the predicate likewise refuses a bare verb; the agent may be omitted (信被撕了, 'the letter was torn up'); the classical ground colour of 被 is **the undesirable** (a passive calamity of the type “韩信被诛” in the *Records of the Grand Historian* (*Shiji*), 'Han Xin was put to death.' (Our translation.)), and in the modern language it already covers the neutral and the commendatory wholesale, yet the colloquial 让, 叫 and 给 still lean towards the negative (钱包让小偷摸走了, 'a thief made off with the wallet'). Kin in other forms: **见 and 于 are the passives of Classical Chinese** (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) (见笑于人, 'laughed at by others'; 吾常见笑于大方之家, 'I have often been laughed at by those of great attainment.' (Our translation.)); “被 + verb” freezes into a word (被迫, 'compelled', 被动, 'passive', 被告, 'the accused' — a fossil stratum of lexicalisation); the internet age has rebounded with a **coercive “被××”** (被就业, 'bei-employed', 被增长, 'bei-increased': X is often intransitive, 被 comes again to hold the old meaning 'involuntary', and one construction feeds half a history of public opinion).
:::

::: definition Existential sentences, serial-verb constructions, pivotal constructions {#def-cunxian}
**Existential sentence**: place + verb + the thing that exists or appears — “门口有棵树｜台上坐着主席团｜昨天村里死了一头牛” ('at the doorway there is a tree | on the platform sits the presidium | yesterday a cow died in the village'). The three patterns (有/V 着/V 了) share the information flow 'set the scene → the subject appears' (a textbook specimen of the information structure 'old scene, new thing'). **Serial-verb construction**: one subject, several verbs, no connective, and temporal order or a purpose (他拿钥匙开门走了, 'he took the key, opened the door and left'; 我去商店买瓶水, 'I went to the shop to buy a bottle of water'; 他骑自行车上班, 'he cycles to work') — between the verbs one may add 就, 再 or 着 to test the layers. **Pivotal construction**: the object of V1 doubles as the subject of V2 (我请他来｜老师批评他迟到｜妈妈让我穿秋裤, 'I asked him to come | the teacher criticised him for being late | Mum had me put on long underwear' — V1 is limited to the family of **causative command, appellation and selection**: 请, 叫, 让, 派, 逼, 命, 选, 称, 'ask, tell, let, dispatch, force, command, choose, name'). **Distinguishing a pivot from a subject–predicate phrase used as object**: “我知道他来” ('I know that he is coming') — 他 is not the patient of 知道 ('know'), and there is no pivot (a simple test: **if V1 has no causative-command sense, it is not a pivot** — what follows verbs of the 知道, 觉得, 看见 kind, 'know, feel, see', is a subject–predicate phrase used as the object); a pivot may be turned into a *bei* passive (他被我请来了 ✓, 'he was asked by me to come'), but the knowledge kind may not (*他是被我知道的来 — it does not work).
:::

::: example A run-through of the four constructions {#ex-chuanyan}
With the same cast (manager / client / contract), make one sentence each of 把, 被, the serial verb and the pivot; then, in one sentence, say where the information focus of each of the four falls.
::: solution
经理把合同签了 ('the manager signed the contract'; focus: the result of handling the contract); 合同被客户退回来了 ('the contract was sent back by the client'; focus: what the contract undergoes); 经理拿着合同去找客户 ('the manager took the contract and went to find the client'; focus: the course of the string of actions); 经理请客户确认合同 ('the manager asked the client to confirm the contract'; focus: the causative command and its purpose). The four constructions = four ways of lighting the same world: **把 lights the patient, 被 lights what is undergone, the serial verb lays the actions out on a time axis, and the pivot bridges two actions** — a construction is not a synonymous rewriting; it is the language of the camera.
:::
:::

::: history Growth rings of the constructions {#sec-nianlun}
The 被 of the *bei* passive originally meant 'to cover, to lay upon' (the 被 of “被之衣服”, 'to clothe him'), was extended to 'to suffer', and the pattern “诛—被诛” ('put to death — be put to death') is already found in the Qin and the Han; the ancestor, in high antiquity, of the existential sentence stands in the *Book of Songs* (*Classic of Poetry*, *Book of Odes*; *Shijing*), “山有榛，隰有苓”, 'On the hills there are hazels; in the wet lowland there is liquorice.' (Our translation.); the serial verb and the pivot are the two wings of Chinese's **tradition of doing without conjunctions** (English must hitch them on with *then* or *to*, while Chinese ranges the clauses side by side — syntactic evidence of a paratactic character, 意合, taken further in the chapter on complex sentences); the *ba* construction appeared late, and yet with force, and in the Song–Yuan vernacular it was already a main force of narrative (*ba* constructions come in clusters in *Zhuzi yulei* 《朱子语类》, *Classified Conversations of Master Zhu*). **Every special construction is the terminus of a grammaticalisation railway** — special only because we have forgotten that it was once a move of house.
:::

::: warning Four guards on the use of constructions
First, guard against **swapping 把 and 被 at random**: “我把他没办法” ('there is nothing I can do about him') is legitimate, “我把办法没办法”? — the verb of a *ba* construction must be capable of disposal, and the idiomatic phrase 没办法 ('no way out'), being a whole, cannot be split; “被他看见了” ('seen by him') is legitimate, but “*这被大家知道” ('this was known by everyone'; the knowing kind has no disposal) is awkward. Second, guard against **an obsession with a subject in the existential sentence**: to insist on finding an agent subject for “台上坐着主席团” ('on the platform sits the presidium') is to wrench a topic language by force into a subject language. Third, guard against **a mixed diagnosis of pivot and serial verb**: “让他进去” ('let him go in'; a pivot) and “拿了票进去” ('having taken a ticket, went in'; a serial verb) differ by one **bridge of causative command**. Fourth, guard against **a fastidiousness about commendatory *bei***: “他被选为代表” ('he was elected a representative') is legitimate and in general use, while the internet “被××” is a productive new construction — the rules of language are more numerous than school rules, and newer than school rules too.
:::

::: exercise A laboratory for rewriting constructions {level=1}
Rewrite “风把屋顶掀了” ('the wind tore the roof off') as the three forms of an active sentence, a *bei* passive and an existential sentence; state the focus of each form and the shift of register.
::: solution
Active: 风掀了屋顶 ('the wind tore the roof off'; neutral narration). *Bei*: 屋顶被风掀了 ('the roof was torn off by the wind'; the patient as topic, plus the perspective of what is undergone — the register of reporting). Existential: 昨夜村东头掀了一片屋顶／屋顶上如今又盖了新瓦 ('last night, at the east end of the village, a stretch of roof was torn up / on the roof, new tiles have now been laid again'; the existential form lays the stress on presenting the event, and the agent may be omitted — commonly used in narratives of disaster). Full marks when the three forms, the foci and the registers are each in place.
:::
:::

::: exercise A double probe of the pivot and the serial verb {level=2}
Distinguish: “他推着车卖菜｜他推着车进了院｜老师让他重复一遍｜我有个朋友在外地” ('he pushes a cart along, selling vegetables | he pushed the cart into the courtyard | the teacher had him repeat it once | I have a friend who is away'). For each, give one sentence of evidence from a test.
::: solution
推 + 卖: a serial verb of actions going on together (manner + purpose; 着 or 就 may be inserted); pushing the cart + entering the courtyard: a serial verb of temporal order (which came first can be checked); 让: a pivot (the bridge of causative command ✓; it can be turned into a *bei* passive, “他被老师叫去重复一遍”, 'he was called by the teacher to go and repeat it once'); “有个朋友”: an existential in which a serial verb encases a pivot (“有” takes the pivot “朋友” plus a clause — an existential variant of the pivotal construction; split it as “有一个朋友，他在外地”, 'there is a friend, and he is away', and its shape appears). Full marks when all four judgements are backed by evidence.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the social life of constructions {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: collect five instances of “被××” from the media or from Moments (朋友圈) over the last three years, analyse the features of their arguments (transitivity? commendation or censure? is the institutional party responsible concealed?), and compare the political-rhetorical energy of 把 in “把权力关进制度的笼子”, 'Lock power inside the cage of institutions.' (Our translation.); the conclusion is to answer: why are the 'special constructions' of Chinese in fact the regular weapons of public discourse? The instances must be checkable, and date and source must be marked.
::: solution
Points for the mark: five genuine instances, plus an analysis of the arguments (the three features — intransitive, the agent concealed, and an involuntary sense — each have a distribution of their own); the political rhetoric of 把 (the agency in the sense of disposal: the institution as subject, power as patient — a living use, in public texts, of the 'disposal' ground colour of the *ba* construction); the conclusion lands at the level of construction = a resource of stance; the dates of the data can be checked.
:::

::: summary
The five great thief-catchers each carry a ground colour: the *ba* construction speaks of disposal (the object must be definite, and the predicate must 'have something to do'), the *bei* passive speaks of undergoing (an old grievance put to present use, and a return of the tide on the internet), the existential sentence sets the scene and only then sends the actors on, the serial-verb construction ranges time side by side, and the pivotal construction gives one object two offices. All are termini of a grammaticalisation railway; 'special' only because a move of house has been forgotten. A construction is not a machine for synonymous rewriting; it is camera and stance — 把 lights the patient, 被 lights what is undergone, the existential gives the scene, the serial verb gives the order, and the pivot gives the relation. The next lesson is the close: the complex sentence — when the sentence begins to organise its own traffic.
:::
