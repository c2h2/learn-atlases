How do words assemble into a wall of building blocks? This lesson gives two tools: **five structural relations** (the five-element table of the phrase) and **immediate-constituent analysis** (peeling, layer by layer, the three-dimensional skeleton out of a linear sentence). With the tools in hand, ambiguity turns from metaphysics into geometry.

## Five structural relations {#sec-wuzhong}

::: definition The five-feature system of the phrase {#def-wuxing}
Divided by the relation between constituents, there are five types: **subject–predicate** (他来了｜心思｜细腻), **verb–object** (看书｜打篮球), **modifier–head** (adverbial–head: 仔细看｜attributive–head: 新书), **verb–complement / predicate–complement** (看完、跑得快、洗干净), and **coordinate** (调查研究、京津、伟大而平凡). The first three types "have a head" — the function equals that core item (endocentric constructions: attributive–head = nominal, adverbial–head = verbal, verb–object = verbal); the coordinate type is the most democratic (the function of the whole = the function of the members); the complement is a speciality of Chinese — a five-complement system of **resultative / degree / directional / potential / quantitative** (打完·好得很·站起来·打得完·看一眼). Meanings that English can express only with a clause, Chinese brings in under a single complement.
:::

::: example A demonstration of immediate-constituent analysis: 咬死了猎人的狗 {#ex-gou}
Cut it, and explain the two readings.
::: solution
Reading A (the dog did it): [咬死了猎人的]狗 — an attributive–head construction; inside the attributive is a verb–object, [咬死了[猎人]]. Reading B (the scene of the killing): [咬死了[猎人的狗]] — a verb–object construction, the object = 猎人的狗. **The same string of words, two hierarchies** — immediate-constituent analysis draws "whose fault" on the tree: whichever layer the modifying relation is hung on, the meaning belongs to that one. The examination mnemonic: **find the head first; the moment the head changes, the tree splits apart**.
:::
:::

::: definition Three iron rules of immediate-constituent analysis {#def-tier}
① **Binary division** is the main rule (each cut makes two halves; a coordinate construction may be divided into more); ② **every segment is meaningful and the structure holds** (the two pieces cut out must themselves be legitimate grammatical units); ③ **cutting does not change class** (after the cut, each piece is still combined according to its original class, and function words attach to the head word and go with it). The procedure: **from large to small, the backbone before the branches** — subject–predicate and verb–object are split apart first, then adverbials and attributives are peeled off, and finally one reaches the word. It is the version of American structuralism's "immediate-constituent analysis" (IC) made for use on Chinese, the source of thirty years of nationwide cursing in pedagogical grammar, and also the first time humanity let primary-school pupils **see** the bones of a sentence.
:::

::: intuition Endocentric and exocentric: the locomotive principle
Modifier–head, verb–object and subject–predicate are all "endocentric" — the whole train has one head (the head), and the function follows the head (红苹果 is still nominal). But Chinese has two "exocentric trains" that have given structuralism a lifelong headache: **“我的书” as a whole is a noun, and the 的-phrase “卖的” is also a noun, yet neither “书” nor “的” is a head**; **“看书” is verbal, yet “书” is a noun**. In his day Bloomfield was stumped by "the ten o'clock train"; Chinese versions are everywhere. Two schools of solution: either admit that 的 is a declensional affix (the formal school), or give up the equation "function = the sum of the heads" (the functional school). The classroom stance: **the five-feature system is a teaching map, not an ontological verdict** — the map is useful; do not take it for the territory itself.
:::

::: history From peeling an onion to a tree: the introduction and domestication of the tools of analysis {#sec-gongju}
Immediate-constituent analysis (IC) comes from American structuralism (Harris and Bloomfield, the 1930s–50s); the core idea is only one sentence: **linguistic units are built in layers, and meaning is distributed in the hierarchy**. In the 1950s it entered Chinese grammar circles along with the "method fever", and together with Soviet grammar shaped China's pedagogical syntax; in 1979 the essays of Lü Shuxiang's 'Some Questions concerning Chinese Morphology' laid the Chinese difficulties of the "morphology–syntax interface" out on the table; the Huang–Liao textbook fixed immediate-constituent analysis as a basic skill for departments of Chinese, and from then on "binary division down to the word" became the first night of grammar for several generations. After the 1980s the **tree diagram** (labelled diagram) of generative grammar came ashore, binary division was upgraded to "tiered projection", and the grandchildren of immediate-constituent analysis can already draw movement and empty categories — but this lesson still starts from peeling the onion: **every tree grows in a hand that can peel an onion**.
:::

::: warning Three guards against bad cuts
First, guard against **cutting too much or too little**: is “看了三天” cut as “看了/三天” or as “看/了三天”? Decide by the law that function words attach (“了” clings tight to the verb and goes with it, so the cut falls after “看了”); letting the knife fall before the cut has been tested is a common fault. Second, guard against **taking coordinate for modifier–head**: can “和” be inserted into “新材料”? “新和材料” cannot be said, so it is modifier–head — the **insertion test** (和/的/地) is the reagent. Third, guard against **cutting only as far as meaning**: immediate-constituent analysis judges structure and does not judge truth or falsity; the tree of “无色的绿色思想” ('colourless green thoughts') is perfectly legitimate, and the absurdity lies in the dictionary, not in the syntax (the Chinese double of Chomsky's famous sentence).
:::

::: quiz
The structural type of “洗干净” is ( ).
- [ ] verb–object
- [x] verb–complement
- [ ] modifier–head
- [ ] coordinate
::: solution
“干净” supplements the result of “洗” — a resultative complement; that it can be expanded into the potential forms “洗得干净/洗不干净” is the identity card of the complement family.
:::
:::

::: quiz
The function of “卖菜的” as a whole is nominal, yet no nominal head can be found — what this exposes is ( ).
- [ ] immediate-constituent analysis is invalid
- [x] the endocentric/exocentric dispute: function need not equal the sum of the heads
- [ ] “的” is a verb
- [ ] the construction is ungrammatical
::: solution
The 的-phrase is a classic exocentric construction — the gap between the teaching map and ontology lies precisely here.
:::
:::

::: quiz
When immediate-constituent analysis cuts “看了三天”, the correct first choice is ( ).
- [ ] 看了／三天 (verb–object)
- [x] 看了／三天 (verb–complement) — “三天” supplements the duration
- [ ] 看／了三天 (attributive–head)
- [ ] cannot be cut
::: solution
The time-measure “三天” supplements the length of the action, and belongs to the duration complement — a verb–complement frame; a verb–object analysis cannot explain the difference in nesting between “看三天” and “看三天书”.
:::

::: exercise Hands-on with the onion {level=1}
Make an immediate-constituent analysis of “他认真地看完了那本旧书” by binary division all the way down, and mark the relations of the five types.
::: solution
他‖认真地看完了那本旧书 (subject–predicate) → 认真地/看完了那本旧书 (adverbial–head) → 看完了/那本旧书 (verb–object) → 看/完 (verb–complement) ＋ 那本/旧书 (attributive–head) → 那本… classifier–noun, 旧/书 adjective–noun. All five types present, and binary division with no violation, is full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Two trees for an ambiguity {level=2}
For “开刀的医生” and “学习文件很重要”, draw two trees each, and state the semantic reading of each tree.
::: solution
“学习文件很重要”: tree A, [[学习文件]很重要] — a subject–predicate construction; inside the subject “学习文件” is a verb–object (“研习文件这件事很重要” 'The business of studying documents is very important.' (Our translation.)); tree B, [学习[文件很重要]] — verb–object, the verb “学习” taking a clausal object (“课文告诉我们：文件很重要” 'The text tells us: the document is very important.' (Our translation.)). “开刀的医生”: A=[开刀的]医生 (attributive–head: 给病人开刀的医生 'the doctor who operates on the patient'); B=开[刀的医生]? It does not hold — the only legitimate tree for this string is attributive–head; switch to an exocentric construction of the “缝裤子的” type ('the one who sews trousers'), and one can see that the 的-phrase forms a case in its own right. Extra marks for anyone who can invent a genuine two-tree example and draw it correctly.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: does syntax have a colour {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: “无色的绿色思想在愤怒地睡觉” ('Colourless green thoughts are sleeping angrily.' (Our translation.)) is syntactically perfect and semantically absurd — does this show that syntax is an **autonomous module**? Drawing on this lesson (the tree does not consult a dictionary) and on lexical semantics (the clash of semantic features between “绿色” and “思想”), write a moot court: the prosecution (the autonomy of syntax) and the defence (a syntax–semantics continuum) each make a statement of three hundred characters; you sit as the jury and give the reasons for your verdict. You are required to cite the "endocentric/exocentric dispute" as a precedent for "rules, too, have an opening for exceptions".
::: solution
Marking points: the prosecution's argument (hierarchical structure can be detached from a test of truth value; a faulty sentence ≠ ungrammatical); the defence's argument (selectional restrictions, frame semantics, the selection of word classes by constructions — syntax is not wholly autonomous); the verdict is not required to fudge a compromise, but must give **layered criteria** (what the test of structural well-formedness is, and what the test of semantic well-formedness is); the exocentric construction is appropriately cited as a precedent for an "opening in the rules".
:::
:::

::: summary
The five-feature system of the phrase (subject–predicate, verb–object, modifier–head, verb–complement, coordinate) characterises a combination, and immediate-constituent analysis gives a shape to a linear string: under the three rules of binary division, verification, and not changing class, “咬死了猎人的狗” turns from one sentence into two trees. The endocentric/exocentric dispute reminds us of the difference between the map and the territory; Chomsky's absurd poem then draws the boundary: the tree does not consult a dictionary. The next lesson presses this skeleton onto the sentence — seven kinds of sentence constituent come on stage, and a long sentence can from then on be read by being taken apart.
:::
