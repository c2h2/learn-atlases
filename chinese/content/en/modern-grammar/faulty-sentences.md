title: "Finding and correcting faulty sentences"
---

All the tools issued in the previous eight lessons are queueing to register in this one consulting room. The Gaokao's standard line for the teaching of faulty sentences is **six major types** (wrong word order, mismatched collocation, missing or redundant constituents, tangled structure, unclear meaning, and illogicality); this lesson gives three things: **a genealogy, a diagnostic procedure, and an ethics of correction**.

## A genealogy of the six major types {#sec-zupu}

::: definition Six families and fifteen subtypes at a glance {#def-liu}
**One. Wrong word order:** disordered multiple attributives (the echelon: possessor → quantity → cause → property → head — “村里刚出土的两件宋代瓷器”); the position of connectives (whether the subjects are the same or different decides what comes before and what after, [[modern-grammar/complex-sentences]]); disordered ranks of adverbials (adverbials of scope and adverbials of degree each have a seat: does “都” come before “也许”? — open the case on a feel for the language, and settle the verdict with contrastive sentences). **Two. Mismatched collocation:** verb and object (“扩大规模” and “提高效率” each in its proper place; \*“提高范围” ×); subject and predicate (\*他的去世使我悲哀很久); positive and negative in clash (“能否…是关键”, two sides yoked to one side). **Three. Missing parts and redundancy:** a preposition drowning the subject (通过…使…); a missing head (“据…显示” and “…的结果” drawn together from the two ends); stacked redundancy (“大约…左右”, “忍俊不禁地笑了”, “个人私见”). **Four. Tangled structure:** blended sentence patterns (“原因是因为”, “本着…为原则”); changing tracks midway (the subject of the earlier clause, only halfway through its life, changes car). **Five. Unclear meaning:** reference left hanging (“这” refers to nothing); ambiguity of where to cut (三个中学老师的爸爸); two readings, as agent and as patient (“他的笑话” — a joke he tells, or other people making a fool of him: the possessive reading, and the subject–object reading). **Six. Illogicality:** self-contradiction (约…左右); faulty negation (“避免不” and “未尝不会不”, nested like dolls); improper classification (newspapers, television and the like treated as literary works); a causality imposed from outside.
:::

::: example Twelve linked cases in the consulting room {#ex-shier}
Assign a type: ①学习成效的高低，取决于努力。②他马上立刻集合了队伍。③通过这次活动，使大家受到了教育。④我们肩负着并继承了老一辈的优良传统。⑤三个县里的干部来开会。⑥古典芭蕾舞剧《天鹅湖》曾来过中国。⑦他连小学没毕业。⑧为了避免不再生事，他搬了家。⑨报纸、广播、电视和各类文艺形式。⑩他的家乡是青岛人。⑪这个问题我解决不了他。⑫我基本上完全同意。
::: solution
① Two sides against one side (the positive–negative clash within mismatched collocation — change “努力” to “是否努力”, or delete “高低”); ② adverbials stacked into redundancy; ③ a preposition drowning the subject (a missing constituent); ④ a covert mismatch of collocation: “肩负” can take only “使命” or “任务”, and inside a coordinate predicate it silently fails to match the shared object “传统” — split them and balance the pairing: “继承了老一辈的优良传统，肩负着新的使命”; ⑤ ambiguity of where to cut (三个/县里); ⑥ subject and predicate fail to match (a drama cannot “来”; change it to “曾来华演出”); ⑦ the “连” frame is incomplete (it should be “连…都/也”); ⑧ faulty negation (避免 plus 不 makes a double negative that does the bad thing — delete “不”); ⑨ a coordination that contains both a genus and a species (a newspaper is itself a form of literature and art, or of communication; the set contains itself — improper classification, under illogicality); ⑩ subject and object are not of a kind (a hometown is a person — blended sentence patterns: 他家乡是青岛 / 他是青岛人). ⑪ two sentences stuck together (blended sentence patterns: “这个问题我解决不了” and “他解决不了这个问题” are run onto one track and fail to match) — choose one; ⑫ self-contradiction (“基本上” and “完全” exclude each other). Get all twelve cases right, and the apprenticeship is over.
:::
:::

::: theorem Three steps of diagnosis and three virtues of correction {#rule-liucheng}
**Three steps of diagnosis:** ① **the contraction method** — extract the backbone (who or what, plus does, plus what); subject, predicate and object are held up at one stroke to the demon-revealing mirror (mismatched collocation, missing parts and blends all show their true shape); ② **the leaf-reduction method** — check the adjuncts layer by layer (the order of attributives, adverbials and complements; redundancy; two sides matched to one side); ③ **logical review** — is the sense of the matter consistent with itself (contradiction, negation, classification, cause and effect)? **Three virtues of correction:** **preserving the true sense** (the original meaning is Party A, the alteration is Party B, and Party B must not turn from guest into host); **minimality** (if deleting will do, do not rewrite; if changing one word will do, do not change the whole sentence — “correcting too much” is itself a new disease); **leaving a trace** (in teaching and in editing, mark the grounds of a change, so that it can be checked again and appealed).
:::

::: history A thousand years, and seventy, of awareness of faulty sentences {#sec-bingju}
When the ancients collated books they spoke of “鲁鱼亥豕” ('鲁 taken for 鱼, and 亥 for 豕'); that is a fault of the script. **Faults of syntax**, as a subject for the whole people, begin with the newspaper column of 1951, *Yufa xiuci jianghua* 《语法修辞讲话》 (*Talks on Grammar and Rhetoric*) — it turned “写通了没有” ('Has it been written so that it comes through?') from the self-cultivation of the scholar-official into a citizen's skill, and the faulty-sentence questions on Gaokao papers are its great-grandchildren. Across those seventy years two public cases are worth remembering: whether or not to delete “凯旋而归” (a repeated sense that has become a word; the dictionaries have already regularised it); whether or not to take “十分浪费” in hand (the collocational range of degree adverb plus verb keeps drifting) — **a faulty sentence is a moving boundary line**, but “moving” is not “at will”: word of what is current comes from corpora and from the community, which is not the same as “大家都错那就对了” ('If everyone is wrong, then that makes it right.' (Our translation.)).
:::

::: warning Three medical accidents in the consulting room
First, **taking style for a disease**: “她死了。死得很轻。” ('She died. Died very lightly.' (Our translation.)) (a fragment running across sentences is rhetoric) — a verdict that something is a faulty sentence must have a **comparison in the same frame** (a conventional expression does indeed exist, and comes closer to the standard). Second, **taking change for a disease**: “十分+名词” (十分现代) and “被就业” are both a construction expanding the ground it holds; an editor may keep to what is established, while the study of grammar must enter it in the books. Third, **taking correction for writing**: after the change the sentence is correct but lifeless — the highest grade in the teaching of faulty sentences is **to write a sentence that needs no correction**; graduating from diagnostics is matriculating in immunology.
:::

::: quiz
The lesion in “通过这次学习，使我提高了认识。” is (　　).
- [ ] mismatched collocation
- [x] prepositional frames piled one on another, drowning the subject (a missing constituent)
- [ ] wrong word order
- [ ] unclear meaning
::: solution
Two frames fight over one room for a subject — delete “通过”, or delete “使”: a model case of the smallest change.
:::

::: quiz
The cause of the fault in “能否刻苦钻研，是提高成绩的关键。” is (　　).
- [x] two sides mismatched with one side
- [ ] faulty negation
- [ ] blending
- [ ] redundancy
::: solution
“能否” is two sides and “是关键” is one side — supply “是否”, or delete “能否”: the standard case of the positive–negative clash.
:::

::: quiz
In judging a faulty sentence, the “紧缩法” ('contraction method') means (　　).
- [x] extracting the backbone and testing whether subject, predicate and object fit
- [ ] deleting every attributive
- [ ] turning a long sentence into short sentences
- [ ] abridging the whole text
::: solution
The backbone first, and the twigs and leaves after: under a bare inspection of the backbone, missing parts, failed matches and blends have nowhere to hide.

::: exercise The attending physician {level=1}
A close diagnosis of two chosen from six (assign a type, plus the smallest correction, plus the grounds): ①中国是世界上人口最多的国家之一。②他的革新老实方法值得学习。③我们既要学会做事，也要学会做人。④这部电影，我在去年就看过它了。⑤谁也不能否认家庭教育不是影响一生的。⑥两个工厂的工会领导开会。
::: solution
① **No fault** (“最…之一” is a measure consistent with itself; a trap question; to correct it is an accident); ② “革新” is better changed to “改进” (“改进方法” and “革新技术”, each returning to its own sense); ③ no fault (the progressive relation is legitimate) — a trap again; ④ the resumptive “它” is redundant (join the words into a sentence: 电影我看过了 — delete “它”); ⑤ a triple negation overturns the cart (“不能否认不是” amounts to denying that it is so; delete “不是” and write “是”, or delete “不”); ⑥ ambiguity of where to cut (两个/工厂的 — “工厂的两个工会领导”). A close diagnosis of any two (type, plus correction, plus grounds) scores full marks.
:::

::: exercise The advertising court {level=2}
Three celebrated advertising cases come before the court: “默默无蚊（闻）”, “骑乐无穷（其）”, “酒负盛名（久）”. The prosecution (the prescriptivist side) and the defence (the creative-use side) each state their case; you sit on the jury: by setting (directions for a medicine / a friends-circle post / a shop sign), give a table of verdicts, “right / wrong / it depends on the occasion”.
::: solution
The prosecution's brief: wrong characters pollute the norms of the script, and mislead children at the stage of learning characters. The defence's brief: a homophonic pun is a figure of speech ([[rhetoric]]“仿拟”, 'imitation of a set expression'), and the life of a four-character idiom (*chengyu*) lies in its circulation. A model for the verdict table: public-service texts are held strictly, commercially creative texts are treated leniently and marked as such, and textbooks and examination halls are absolutely strict — **the true grammar of “right and wrong” is “fitness to the register”**. Say it clearly in one sentence, and the marks are given.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: is a faulty sentence an error or a change? {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. Starting from the regularisation of “凯旋而归” and the still undecided tug of war over “非常+名词” (非常时代), argue for your “boundary in a relativity of faulty sentences”: which faults are a want of ability (*performance* may be forgiven; *competence* is not yet in place), which are advance signs of change in the system, and which are purely a mismatch of style. Requirements: three cases, plus one “order of priority for the verdict” that you draw up yourself (of the norm, a feel for the language, and frequency, which comes before which), and a response to “Should the teacher of Chinese be the first to oppose change in the language?”.
::: solution
Points for the mark: the three cases are triaged accurately (an error of constituents nested like dolls counts as a case of *competence*; degree adverb plus noun is the forward edge of change; a homophonic advertisement is a case of register); the order of priority is consistent in itself and can be carried out; the reply to “teachers against change” is not extreme (a teacher's duty is **a grammar that tells occasions apart**, not to serve as an embalmer of the language); this lesson's historical point, the “moving boundary line”, is cited without error.
:::

::: summary
The six major types and fifteen subtypes of the faulty sentence are the genealogy; the three steps of diagnosis (contraction, leaf-reduction, logical review) are the procedure; the three virtues (preserving the true sense, minimality, leaving a trace) are the physician's manner. Seventy years of education in faulty sentences turned “writing it through” into a citizen's skill, and the regularisation of “凯旋而归” reminds us that the boundary is moving, but the moving has a ledger. The nine chapters of the whole course close the volume here — from “what grammar is” to “writing a sentence so that it comes through”, what you already hold is this: the ruler of distribution, the knife of layers, the lens of sentence patterns, and the lamp of the consulting room. Grammar is not a statute to be recited; it is the craft of making speech hold steady. The next course (rhetoric) teaches you to move that craft one inch further: to make speech come alive.
:::
