---
title: "Literature of the 1950s and 1960s"
---

The previous course ended in 1949; this lesson has to begin from something earlier than 1949 — **in 1942 someone, in a cave dwelling, asked a question: the question of "for whom" (为什么人); for the thirty-seven years after that, almost every formal feature of Chinese literature was an answer given to that question, and an answer continually revised**.

## Definition: a new institution {#def-jigou}

::: definition From the bookshop to the work unit {#def-danwei}
**Three things disappeared at once** (**the thread of the usual account: the nationalisation of periodicals, the system of publication licences and the system of writers' incomes, carried through one after another after 1949 —** the free market, private patronage and a living earned in fees for manuscripts left the stage together (the closing of the whole loop that runs through the economy of paying calls on patrons in Chapter 4, the economy of retainer households in Chapter 5 and the economy of the bookshops in Chapter 7: **the last time Chinese literature asked "on whom one depends to eat" (靠谁吃饭), a single institution answered the question at one stroke**)); **the shape of the wage** (**the usual account: a writer could join the Writers' Association, take a post on a periodical, and draw a wage and a grade —** once income no longer depended on the reader, the reader's opinion lost its veto (the strongest illustration, in Lesson 10, of "marking criteria shape the form of writing": **the literature of the Seventeen Years is not dishonest; its commissioning party had changed** (**the client (甲方) turned from a buyer into a task: and so the first reader of a novel was no longer the market, but the editorial office**))); **the formal consequences of "for whom"** (**the thread of the usual account: workers, peasants and soldiers were established as those to be served, and popularisation came before the raising of standards —** mark the technical meaning of this rule: **it requires an adult to read every work with a beginner's capacity to decode** (and so the rhetoric had to be transparent, the metaphors had to come from production, and a villain had to sound wicked at the first hearing (**"形象高大、语言易懂、立场分明" ('lofty images, plain language, a stance that is unmistakable') is not an aesthetic; it is a requirement of compatibility**))).
:::

::: definition The three characters "十七年" {#def-shiqinian}
**Where the name of the period comes from** (**the "Seventeen Years" (十七年, 1949–1966) are a stretch of time marked out after 1978 for convenience of narration —** an age was named, by those who came after, a "middle stage": **this itself is a demotion carried out by retrospective recognition** (the clause on retrospective recognition in Lesson 15: **its advantage is that it sets three stretches of literature on a single axis; its disadvantage is that it leads people to think literature had all along a "normal state", and that these seventeen years were a deviation** (**this lesson's attitude:** the Seventeen Years are not a departure; they are one complete experiment; they have their own internal logic, and their own underground stratum))).
:::

## Three dominant forms of writing {#def-wenti}

::: definition The full-length novel: revolutionary history {#def-xiaoshuo}
**A list of the conventions** (**the gist of the usual account: party struggle or war as the main line, growth as the skeleton, the hero at the centre, victory as the ending —** this is to run "memoir + policy document + romance" together in a single furnace (the institutionalising of 七实三虚, 'seven parts fact and three parts invention', from Chapter 3: **实 ('fact') answers for being credible, 虚 ('invention') for being readable, and "policy" for being defensible**)); **three sources** (** *The Hurricane* (《暴风骤雨》) was written by members of a work team out of the work of land reform (the usual account), *Red Crag* (《红岩》) was rewritten from the recollections of those who had escaped (the usual account), and *Tracks in the Snowy Forest* (《林海雪原》) turned the experience of suppression and fighting into a romance (the usual account) —** mark the difference among these three routes: the document, the testimony, and *píngshū* (评书, storytelling) (**how a novel is written depends on what it was first taken to be: a report, an appeal, or talk at night** (Chapter 1, "three origins of a genre"))); **the technique of *Teahouse* (《茶馆》)** (**the usual account: three acts span three ages, there is no protagonist and no event that runs through, "三幕戏写三个时代" ('Three acts write three ages.' (Our translation.)) —** this lesson calls it the dramatic version of "the list is a state that will not come apart" (a return to the Lu You 陆游 clause of Chapter 7: **Lao She 老舍 made a dynasty into a room: he tells no story; he only collects the accounts**); and its ending is in the manner of self-mockery — **he has the people in the play hold, for themselves, a memorial rite of scattering paper spirit-money** (the plot in the usual account): **this is the bravest act of self-classification in the literature of the Seventeen Years**).
:::

::: definition Model operas: the mechanics of an extreme form {#def-yangban}
**The grammar of the "three prominences" (三突出)** (**the gist of the usual account: among all the characters give prominence to the positive characters; among the positive characters give prominence to the heroic characters; among the heroic characters give prominence to the principal heroic character —** this is an instruction in composition, not an instruction in theme (the inversion of the language of the shot, in Lesson 12: **it fixed where the camera stands, and so it also fixed what cannot be seen** (**"the hero looms large" (英雄高大) is not a political slogan; it is a shot list** (**it translates moral rank directly into spatial co-ordinates: light, position, angle, shot**)))); **the cost** (**when the principal hero cannot have any weakness that has not been overcome, narrative loses its engine** (the "manner of death" clause in Chapter 2: **the manner of a character's death can come only from outside:** **the hero cannot be in the wrong, so the conflict can only be between enemy and own side, and cannot be inside the mind —** the real theme of the model operas (样板戏) is therefore not class, but "the proof of externality" (**every intrigue must be discovered in time, or the whole world is unsafe**))); **the technical legacy** (**the thread of the usual account: in the music, the dance and the stage blocking there was indeed innovation on a large scale (the forces of the orchestra, the remaking of metrical types (*bǎnshì* 板式), the actual performance of conventionalised movement)** this lesson's attitude is to read it as a history of form: **it proves that an extreme constraint can force out a very high density of craft, and it also proves that this density cannot buy a good play** (the second proof, in Chapter 8, of "constraint produces form, and does not produce quality")).
:::

::: definition New folk songs and the "collective I" {#def-minge}
**The folk-song movement of 1958** (**the thread of the usual account: during the Great Leap Forward, "new folk songs" were collected and imitated on a large scale, and their famous lines were taken into anthologies —** this is the only experiment in the history of Chinese literature in which the state set poetry to be produced (**its output is real; its authors are wrong: the great majority of the "folk songs" are imitations by cadres and men of letters** (the negative example for the algorithm in Chapter 3: **we attribute the cause to "the enthusiasm of the people", while the cause lies in the system of collection and the pressure of report-sheets** (**the "folk character" of a poem cannot be judged from its language; it can be judged only from the path by which it circulated** (the independent-source clause of Chapter 8))))); **the grammar of "I"** (**the thread of the usual account: lyric poetry of this period generally speaks as 我们 ('we') —** the first-person plural became the default subject (a return to the "four uses of 我们" in Lesson 3: **this is the fourth use of 我们: a collective subject that contains no concrete member** (**its rhetorical force is extremely strong; its structure of responsibility is zero:** **when 我们 does wrong, no single person's signature can be found**))).
:::

## The underground stratum {#def-dixia}

::: definition The drawer, the hand copy and the salon {#def-chouti}
**Three kinds of informal circulation** (**the thread of the usual account: unpublished manuscripts (drawer literature), hand copies and collections of copies, and, in a small circle, reading aloud and the answering of verses —** public literature and private literature coexisted in the same people (the circles of copies around *Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio* (《聊斋》), in Chapter 8, are played out again here: **a copy is still a social tie; only, this time, the social tie was illegal**)); **why they did not become the mainstream of literary history** (**not because they were badly written, but because they had no circulation, no prizes and no text in the textbooks** (Chapter 1, the format of distribution decides life and death: **the effect of "never published", inside literary history, is the same as that of "never born"** (**and when we read the Seventeen Years as "nothing but the models", we are in fact carrying out, on behalf of the distribution system of those years, one last censorship —** **this is the most important honesty column of this chapter**))); **the reliability of hand copies** (**a copy has no definitive text, and the annotators take part in rewriting it** (the contemporary version, in Chapter 3, of "the edition is the ideology") — **to cite them, one must state the level of the source:** **what we are reading is the text of editions put in order after the 1980s, not the original state of those years**).
:::

::: definition How one campaign changes a novel {#def-yundong}
***Keep the Red Flag Flying* (《红旗谱》) and revision** (**the thread of the usual account: when full-length novels were reprinted after the successive campaigns, they were revised, and the political placing of the characters was adjusted with that revision —** read facts of this kind as specimens of a system, not as gossip: **within the lifetime of one book the author was forced to reconcile the accounts again** (the reverse of "the commented copy is the definitive text", in Chapter 2: **this time it was not the commentator who exercised the rights of the author; it was the situation**)); **the cost of "writing the real" (写真实)** (**the thread of the usual account: a batch of short stories and reports of 1956–1957 that "intervened in life" (干预生活) became, more than a year later, evidence of a crime —** this lesson treats them as a natural experiment: **the same authors, the same set of techniques, two responses from the institution** (**the technique did not change; the tolerance did:** **from this one can measure where the boundary of public literature then lay:** **a boundary is not an abstract slogan; it is something a concrete sentence can strike** (the tragic version, in Lesson 1, of "falsifiability"))).
:::

::: example Turning policy into plot {#ex-fanyi}
Below are three tasks in rewriting the language of policy. (1) “依靠贫雇农，团结中农，中立富农” ('Rely on the poor peasants and farm labourers, unite with the middle peasants, and neutralise the rich peasants.' (Our translation.)); (2) “在正面人物中突出英雄人物” ('Among the positive characters, give prominence to the heroic characters.' (Our translation.)); (3) “普及第一，提高第二” ('Popularisation first, the raising of standards second.' (Our translation.)). For each, write a fragment of fiction or of drama in 100 characters, with this requirement: **use only scenes; the terms of the original must not appear**; then answer: **which sentence is hardest to turn, and why (a formal reason must be given; one may not say "for political reasons")**.
::: solution
**①** (**What is hardest is 中立 ('neutralise'):** in a policy, an item of classification that does not change its expression must, in a novel, become one person's concrete behaviour, and this person can be written neither as an ally nor as an enemy: **narrative has no grammar of 中立 ('neutrality')** (**a character in a novel must choose a side, or else he is only scenery:** **this is the formal reason why, in this period, the images of the rich peasant and the middle peasant were either beautified or caricatured —** **the blurred zone of a policy is the blank page of a novel**)).
**②** (**Technically the easiest, and the greatest in cost:** 突出 ('giving prominence') can be done by light, by position, by the length of a sung passage and by the silence of the supporting roles (**it can be taught, it can be copied, and it can be checked:** precisely because of this, it can write every play as one and the same play (**the difficult point is: once an audience is used to this spatial grammar, any character who is not at the centre is automatically read as unreliable —** **and so 突出 ('prominence') is raised from a technique to testimony**))).
**③** (**The difficulty is that 提高 ('the raising of standards') is deferred:** popularisation requires the standard to be met at one stroke, and so the standard cannot be raised; if the standard cannot be raised, the author loses any further step (**of the same type as "feel-good fiction has no cost" in Chapter 6:** a system that rewards only getting in the door will necessarily drive the best authors off to write something else (**the finest novelists of this period turned in large numbers to writing scripts, criticism and translations; this is a curve that can be checked against bibliographies** (Lesson 1, doing the accounts that other people leave out)))).
:::
:::

::: intuition The economics of positive depiction {#def-zhengmian}
**Why "brightness" is too dense in this period** (**the prevailing explanation is naivety or obedience; this lesson's explanation is the structure of costs:** the risk of writing darkness is borne by the author, and the gain goes to the reader; the risk of writing brightness is endured by the reader, and the gain is collected by the author (the mirror image, in Chapter 6, of the sharing-out of costs: **when a system rewards only one kind of subject matter, that subject matter inflates** (**the literature of the Seventeen Years does not lack people who could write darkness; the expression of darkness had been priced at the whole of what one has**))); **the right way to read it** (**do not ask "why did he not dare to write it"; ask "the part he did not dare to write — what did he rewrite it as"** (**all the excessive purity, the excessive coincidence and the excessive villains in the novels of this period are shapes left after a squeezing:** **they are evidence, not decoration** (the biology of the negative, in Chapter 8: the positive depiction of the Seventeen Years is another curve of the same lie detector))).
:::

::: history An institutional timeline of thirty-seven years {#sec-jigou}
**1949** The First Congress of Literary and Art Workers, and the establishment of the system of the Writers' Association (**the birth of a profession:** from then on, "writer" was a kind of post (**its side effect is that a post can be abolished**)); **1953–1956** socialist realism was established as the general norm, and the First National Congress of Literary and Art Workers and the *Talks* (《讲话》) were systematised (**the introduction of the very word "creative method" (创作方法) is itself worth noticing:** it granted that writing has a method, and that a method can be prescribed (the state version of the theory of procedures in Lesson 12)); **1956–1957** a brief loosening, and the Anti-Rightist Campaign that followed it: **the generation the literary world lost is the generation the 1980s would otherwise have had** (the "absent author" clause of Chapter 9); **1958** the new-folk-song movement (**the state as editor:** **for the first time, poetry had an annual target for output**); **1960–1963** an adjustment of policy, and a tightening once more (**the debate over "what to write" and "how to write" (the thread of the usual account):** **a question of form was treated as a question of political line, because form has always been a question of distribution**); **1963–1966** mass criticism entered the newspapers and periodicals (**the inflation of criticism:** when every piece of criticism charged the other party with opposing the Party, the charge lost its information content (the scene of the collapse of "refutability", in Lesson 3)); **1966–1976** public literary periodicals all but ceased publication, and model operas, together with a small amount of new writing, stood alone in the field (**an extreme concentration of a market:** when only one supplier is left, "best-selling" becomes the only concept of sales); **after 1976** the "Seventeen Years" were named anew (**retrospective recognition had begun:** and retrospective recognition itself is precisely the matter of the next lesson).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the Seventeen Years
**Disparaging the whole, or defending the whole** (**"turned into models" and "an age of innocence" are both products of anthologies:** one must judge work by work, and distinguish the conditions of production, the text, and the anthologies of later ages (Lesson 1, the list is a pair of spectacles: **these two ways of judging use one and the same list; they have only reversed the direction of the marks**)); **taking propaganda for a lie** (**propaganda is a form of writing, not a truth-value:** it has its own technical lineage, and its own compact with the reader (Chapter 6, a slogan is a compressed biscuit: **in reading it, ask whom it allows, and on what occasion, not to have to think**)); **forgetting the material conditions of a work** (**print run, paper, the range of distribution, fees for manuscripts, and translation:** a model opera's coverage of the whole country depended on magnetic tape, film and gramophone records (Chapter 1, the format of distribution: **if one does not write out the system of distribution, one can explain only half of the text**)); **taking "underground writing is truer" as the default** (**hand-copied texts likewise have self-censorship, personal temper and performativity:** the drawer, too, is a kind of stage (Chapter 8, independent sources: **an underground text needs a study of sources as strict as a public text does**)).
:::

::: exercise A compatibility audit {level=1}
Choose one outward-facing text of an institution you belong to (a recruitment notice, an annual report, a post on a public account), and in 100 characters point out: **① what capacity to decode it assumes in the reader; ② which words have been forbidden in order to accommodate that assumption; ③ which observable feature of the form of writing these prohibitions have produced**.
::: solution
Marking: **the list of forbidden words must be specific as far as word class** (**one may not say only "it cannot be too negative":** one must point out which class of syntax, or which names and attributes, it avoids — **this is the rhetoric of the Seventeen Years made general: transparency is the grammatical consequence of policy** (Lesson 1, "the connective is a customs post")).
:::
:::

::: exercise Policy into plot {level=2}
Take one modern rule you know (any one of a school rule, a company rule, or a platform's terms) and rewrite it as a fragment of fiction in 150 characters, with this requirement: **no term from the rule may appear, and every item of classification in the rule must be borne by one person's behaviour**; then point out: **which item of classification is hardest to bring to ground in the narrative**.
::: solution
Marking: **the number of items of classification and the number of persons must be checkable against each other** (**"doing the accounts that other people leave out":** to leave one item out is to leave out one moral experiment — **what is most often omitted is precisely the "intermediate state": narrative has no grammar of neutrality**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: literature that has changed its client {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**to study a literary system with only one kind of commissioning party makes the relation of literature and institutions clearer than to study a system with several kinds of commissioning party: not because the oppression in the former is more obvious, but because it concentrates in one place a cost that had already been dispersed — whoever pays is the first reader; whoever the first reader is, the form of writing grows according to that reader's capacity to decode; when this place is occupied permanently by an institution, every 'spontaneous form' of literature becomes a response to it, compliance included, a way round included, and later ages' total rejection of it included**". One must call on at least six items: **the income system and the replacement of the client**, **the requirement of compatibility**, **the full-length novel of three origins**, **the three prominences as a shot list**, **the fourth use of 我们 ('we')**, **the underground stratum's having no circulation**; and give a verdict: **"whether the achievement of the literature of the Seventeen Years is formal or political" — one must cite one work which one holds did, in this period, genuinely advance the Chinese language in technique (or state clearly that there was none), and bear the burden of proof**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**the client, compatibility, origin, composition, the plural subject, distribution**); the evidence (**directions for reference: the remaking of metrical types and of the orchestra in the model operas; the fusion, in writers of the kind of Liu Qing 柳青, of dialect and the vocabulary of policy; structural experiments in the full-length novel; the making of new opera into music-drama; the mark does not lie in which of these is chosen, but in this, that a "technical advance" must be inheritable by the next generation —** if a technique is scrapped together with the system, it is not an advance, it is a piece of equipment (**this criterion itself must be written out clearly: a return to "a programme is equipment" in Chapter 11: we call a thing an achievement only when the programme of the next period can use it**); if the conclusion can be written "**and so the most lasting influence of the Seventeen Years lies not in what they wrote, but in their having taught several generations of writers one thing: before setting pen to paper, first silently estimate the face of the first reader — after 1978 this reflex changed its name, and was called the market, and later the platform**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed under the institution: the client (**the wage replaces the reader: the first reader becomes the editorial office**), compatibility (**transparency is the grammatical consequence of policy**), the full-length novel (**three origins: the document, the testimony, and *píngshū***), composition (**the three prominences = a shot list: moral rank is translated into spatial co-ordinates**), the underground (**the drawer is also a stage, but it has no circulation**). The method in one sentence: **find the commissioning party first, then look at the form of writing: where a system concentrates its costs, there its works are deformed**.
