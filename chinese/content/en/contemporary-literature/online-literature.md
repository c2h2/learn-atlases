title: "Online literature"

In the eight chapters of this course that come before, the author at least knew for whom he was writing; the author of this chapter knows once every thirty seconds — **online literature (web fiction) is not 'literature that has gone onto the internet'; it is an institution of production that invents anew the storytelling prototype of the Chinese novel: payment reckoned by the thousand characters, an update every day, the plot altered according to readers' comments; this chapter reads it as a rare natural experiment: when selection pressure becomes both great and swift, what shape the Chinese-language novel will grow into**.

## Definition: an institution {#def-zhidu}

::: definition Three conditions {#def-santiao}
**Received account** (**① in the late 1990s, sites for original writing on the Chinese-language web, and serialisation on forums, appeared; ② around 2000 a batch of long novels had the web as their first publication; ③ in the mid-2000s paid reading and an institution for ranking writers took shape, and thereafter copyright was licensed on to film and television, to games and to animation —** omit any one of the three conditions and there is no today (**bandwidth decides whether one can serialise, payment decides whether one can eat, licensing decides whether one can grow large (the ultimate version of Chapter 1's 'the fee-charging room = can be relied on':** the paywall is not the opposite of literature; it is a new commissioning party (**and its unit of payment is the thousand characters, not the work —** this is the first hard nail of this chapter:** when payment is sold by the chapter, the ending becomes a sales slot (**the modern descendant of Chapter 9's 'the next instalment = a division of time'**.
:::

::: definition Type = a shelf {#def-leixing}
**Received account** (**_xuánhuàn_ 玄幻, _xiānxiá_ 仙侠, urban, historical, military, game, science fiction, infinite-flow (_wúxiànliú_ 无限流), system-flow (_xìtǒngliú_ 系统流) and the other categories make up the basic navigation of a platform —** this course's way of reading (**a type is not a school; it is a shelf (**it is harder than the 'schools' of literary history, because it directly decides where a work is exposed and which recommendation slot it occupies (Chapter 1, 'the catalogue is censorship':** here the catalogue simply is distribution (**at the same time it also lays down the reader's a priori expectations: a so-called 'formula' is the last success written out as this time's condition of entry (Chapter 11, a programme is equipment:** the platform's table of categories is the most effective creative programme of this age, and nobody needs to read it; one need only abide by it**.
:::

## Three new apparatuses {#def-zhuangzhi}

::: definition The golden three chapters {#def-sanzhang}
**Received account (the common claim of the trade's tutorials)** (**within the opening three chapters the protagonist's golden finger (_jīnshǒuzhǐ_ 金手指, a built-in advantage), goal and conflict must be set up, or else readers are lost and the recommendation falls through —** this is a miniature of Chapter 3's 'a spoiler in the first sentence = the management of expectation', compressed to within eight hundred characters (**more precisely:** it is the 'introductory tale (*rùhuà* 入话) + opening round (*tóuhuí* 头回)' of the storyteller's script (*huaben*) played over again under the pay system (the complete closed loop of the clause in Chapter 9:** the introductory tale is a moral advance notice, and the golden three chapters are a retention advance notice; both of them, in the second when the reader decides to leave, decide for him (**this course asks the reader to note one consequence:** when the first ten thousand characters of a novel are decided by sales indicators, those first ten thousand characters no longer belong to narratology; they belong to the conversion rate (**so the right question in judging a web novel is not 'is it well written?', but 'what did it promise in the first five minutes, and did it afterwards make good?' —** this is a question that can be checked item by item (the bestseller version of Lesson 2's test of ways of dying**.
:::

::: definition The daily update: turning the text into a river {#def-rigeng}
**Received account** (**frequency of update is pegged directly to the full-attendance reward, the charts and subscriptions; the author takes 'a daily update of several thousand to over ten thousand characters' as the norm, and a break in updating means falling off the charts (**three consequences for form (**①** the infinite text** (**the way a novel ends is often not a conclusion, but the stopping of the serial:** the boundary of the work is decided by the subscription curve (Chapter 5, 'the licensable universe':** a universe does not end; it only stops updating (**②** the fragmentation of structure** (**each chapter is a self-sufficient unit of sale, and so the long work is no longer made of 'plot' but piled up from 'grains of event' —** the reader may break off for three days and then come back:** this is at once a convenience and a disability:** it makes 'the shape of the whole' the rarest beauty in the web novel —** the fourth proof of Chapter 4's 'the excerpted scene (*zhézi xì* 折子戏) preserved it instead':** when one may enter and leave at will, the unit of the work changes from the whole piece to the single scene (**③** the author's visibility** (**remarks to the reader, notes on an update, a word on the single chapter, the author's words:** paratext becomes part of the main text —** the contemporary form of Chapter 1's 'a preface is an instruction manual':** it is now, as well, a customer-service window.
:::

::: definition The comment section as evolutionary pressure {#def-liuyan}
**Received account** (**in-chapter comments of the 'remarks on this chapter' kind put the reader's reaction on the same screen as the main text; the author can see which passage is being flooded and which passage nobody answers —** the core judgement of this course (**this does not amount to the reader taking part in the creation; it is selection pressure sped up: variation in the writing is still, as before, produced by the author, while the speed of elimination is shortened from a generation to a day (**this is the most important theoretical sentence of the chapter, and the place where it most deserves to be set beside Darwin rather than beside 'reception aesthetics (reception theory)' (**consequence one:** a 'point of satisfaction' is an adaptive device selected at high frequency, not a fall on the author's part (**it can pass the selection because, in a hundred thousand trials, it raises retention;** consequence two:** it is at the same time a kind of inbreeding:** rapid elimination wipes out every way of writing whose benefit shows only across a long time —** the slow burn, the leaving of blanks, complexity, and characters who are not lovable, are all in the batch that gets deleted (Chapter 6, selection is cutting and rewriting:** formerly the cutting and rewriting happened on the editor's desk; now they happen in the seven-hundredth comment (**consequence three:** 'restoring speech to a character already dead': readers' protest can make the author rewrite (**this was impossible in the age of print periodicals:** for the first time the author faces a commissioning party who can express a preference at once, and whose preference has already been proved by payment (**its nature, therefore, is not democracy; it is a contract.
:::

## Scale and recognition {#def-chidu}

::: definition Length is a structure of income {#def-changdu}
**Received account** (**web novels at the head often reach several million characters, and their income is pegged to how long the subscription runs, to tips, and to the licensing of copyright —** this course rejects two popular explanations (**neither 'the author is padding' nor 'the story is abundant' holds; what holds is:** in literary production sold by the chapter, length is a structure of income, not an aesthetic choice (**so length is not a defect; it is a price list (**to criticise it, one must criticise the way of pricing; to praise it, one can praise only its craft (**a corollary that can be checked:** the same genre, under different institutions of pricing, will show a different median length (**the final version of Chapter 4's 'four scenes are a physiological quantity' and of Chapter 7's 'length is decided by the celebration and not by the narrative':** this time the celebration is the monthly subscription, and the physiological quantity is the time of the commute (**this is why the first batch of abridged editions and of selected editions of web novels has not appeared to this day:** a summary would destroy the subscription, and the subscription is the work itself**).
:::

::: definition The four gates of recognition (the roster rearranged) {#def-sidao}
**Received account** (**web authors enter the Writers' Association, and receive national-level prizes and the assessment of professional titles; works enter libraries, and university classrooms, and academic articles; around 2011 the adjudication of the Mao Dun Literature Prize gave rise to a public discussion of 'whether the web novel counts as literature'; mainstream publishing houses bring out web novels, and adaptations for film and television go overseas —** this course sets these four gates in a row beside the four gates of the previous chapter (**the order is completely reversed:** this time the market first supplies the scale, and then the institution comes to discuss the price (**and so a strange posture appears:** the institution has recognised the works, and yet is still discussing whether this category deserves to be recognised (the final closed loop of Lesson 1's 'literature comes to set its seal' and 'popular fiction is reclassified' (**this course's verdict:** this time the seal cannot cover the paper — not because online literature is too strong, but because it is already no longer in the same system of distribution; a seal that can be stamped on paper is, for it, the equivalent of a commemorative stamp (**what it really needs is another matter:** the protection of copyright, the ratio of the split, and the clauses of the contract**) — this is the new answer this chapter gives to the old question of 'literature and the institution':** when a category is large enough to feed a thousand firms upstream and downstream, what it wants is no longer recognition; it is the law of contract.
:::

::: example Explain a formula with an 'adaptive device' {#ex-shiyingqi}
Choose one formula of the web novel (any one of the broken-engagement stream, the live-in-son-in-law stream, the check-in stream, rebirth and revenge, or system missions), and fill four columns: **① what it promised in the first five minutes; ② what it pays out per thousand characters; ③ which constraint of real life it handles for the reader; ④ if it is to be written as a 'good novel', what the author needs, at a minimum, to change**.
::: solution
**Example: rebirth and revenge** — **① the promise:** the reader knows at once that this time the same disaster will not be repeated (**an advantage in information is the protagonist's first weapon (**and it is also a kind of moral exemption: he need not be deceived again (**② the payout** (**one small victory per thousand characters: a retort, an identity revealed, an old debt cleared (**the satisfaction-fiction version of Chapter 3's 'pay out according to the state of the disaster':** the frequency of the payout is its rate of interest (**③ the real constraint** (**what it handles is 'there is no time to begin again':** the cost, in real life, of one error of allocation (a subject, a job, a marriage, an investment) is irreversible (**the cost of rebirth in imagination is extremely low, and its return in emotion extremely high, and so it becomes an adaptive device of the masses, and not because it is 'low' —** this is the way the chapter argues against a hierarchy of culture: explain the demand first, and only then judge the text**); **④ the smallest change** (**not compression, and not the adding of a literary tone; it is the adding of a cost to victory —** once victory begins to keep accounts, the frequency of the payout must fall, and to lower the payout is to commit suicide (**so a real remaking can only change the institution:** replace payment by the chapter with publication as a book completed at one go, or with film and television put out by the season (**this course's conclusion:** the problems of form in online literature are, in the great majority, problems of commercial clauses; a literary criticism of it that does not understand the licensing contract is therefore only praising, or rebuking, a contract it never signed.
:::

::: intuition A rare natural experiment {#int-ziyan}
**Why this chapter matters to the methods course** (**the sociology of literature has for a long time been able to do only retrospective research: first-hand sources, editions, print runs, diaries; online literature, by contrast, supplies data in real time (**retention, the subscription curve, the hot zones of the comments, the monthly-ticket chart (**so that, for the first time, one can observe:** how narrative features evolve under a known selection pressure (Lesson 1, 'doing the accounts other people skip':** not only has no one filled this cell in; it is even the first cell to be an experiment that has numbers —** but it has two limits that cannot be ignored (**① the data belong only to the platform, and the researcher cannot obtain the raw retention (**reproducibility is limited by the willingness of firms (Chapter 8, independent sources:** at present most judgements about online literature come from the public data and the self-accounts of a few platforms (**② the object under study is in the middle of revising its own definition (**any claim about the essence of online literature has a lifespan roughly equal to one cycle of technology (**so the suggestion below is more useful than a conclusion:** when one reads online literature, one should at the same time save screenshots of its interface (**the charts, the pages, the buttons, the character count —** because part of this literature is its shell, and the shell is changed every three years.
:::

::: history Twenty-five years {#sec-nian}
**The late 1990s** Original sites, and serialisation on forums (**received account:** publication became cheap again:** this is, after the newspaper supplements of the 1920s and the letterpress editions of the 1940s, the third time the Chinese language has undergone a sudden drop in the cost of publication (**the first two times produced a new literature and new readers; this time produced a new pricing (**); **2000** a batch of long works had the web as their first publication (**received account:** 'a first work can have a hundred thousand readers overnight' became, for the first time, an event one could expect (**what it changed was not literature, but the place of first publication (Chapter 1, the format of distribution**); **the mid-2000s** paid reading and the ranking system took shape (**received account:** the protagonist of this chapter comes on: not the author, but the billing meter**); **the 2010s** mobile reading, and the operation of copyright (**received account:** the work becomes the middle reach of an IP: upstream is inspiration, downstream is film and television and games, and the middle reach is the contract (**so 'written well' was for the first time weighed against 'the adaptation comes off', in the same meeting room (an upgrade of the previous chapter's clause on cinema as intermediary:** this time even translation was dispensed with; the image puts in its bid directly (**); **after 2015** 'going overseas', and machine translation, and afterwards communities that produce the translation by hand (**received account:** for the first time China became a net exporter of type-narrative (**and what was exported was precisely those parts criticised at home as formula (**this is the sharpest sentence of the chapter:** the translatability of a formula comes precisely from its not depending on a local context — what it skips is literature, and it has skipped literary history as well**); **the 2020s** short video and audiobooks draw the flow aside, and algorithmic recommendation concentrates it further (**received account:** the protagonist of the next chapter (if there is one) has already appeared:** it is not a website; it is the recommendation feed (**and the updating of a recommendation feed waits for no author's daily update.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this course
**'Inferior to traditional literature'** (**this is to compare two institutions of pricing with each other: first state which ruler you are using, and only then give the verdict (Lesson 1, where the ruler comes from**)); **'A democratic literature'** (**the paying reader is a commissioning party who can be measured, not a parliament:** a contract is not called a vote (**comments can affect the text, but the direction of the effect is prescribed by the retention curve (the clause on 'evolutionary pressure' above**)); **'Looking only at the text'** (**and not at the charts, the contract, the ceiling on the character count, the full-attendance prize and the algorithm for recommendation slots:** you will read an institutional phenomenon as a personal taste (Chapter 6, the analysis of Party A:** all through the text of this chapter a schedule of prices is stamped (**); **'Looking only at the data'** (**retention can explain why a formula survives; it cannot explain why a novel is great:** the two are not on the same level (**Chapter 8, independent sources + Lesson 3, the survivor clause:** the 'cases of success' that a platform displays are themselves a list of survivors, and the standard of that selection is a commercial one**).

::: exercise Short essay: the second birth {level=3}
In six hundred to a thousand characters, assess "**online literature is not a branch of the history of modern literature, but a return of the way the Chinese novel is born: it becomes once more a mixed oral—written form that charges per occasion, that has feedback on the spot, and that takes the renewing of a subscription as the condition of its existence; to treat the chain of these hundred years, 'publication in the press—publication as a book—prizes', as the natural state of literature is what is, historically, the exception. But a return does not equal a cycle: the storyteller faces a crowd of listeners, and the author faces a retention curve; listeners forget, the curve records, and the record will train the machine for whom to open the door the next time**". One must draw on at least six items: **the three conditions**, **type as a shelf**, **the golden three chapters and the introductory tale**, **the speeding up of selection pressure**, **length as a structure of income**, **the order of the four gates of recognition reversed**; and one must decide: **which of the 'return account' (a return to storytelling) and the 'exception account' (it is the modern literature of these hundred years that is the normal state) can better guide the practice of criticism — and one must give a concrete conclusion, for a book review, which one would on this account rewrite**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**conditions, the shelf, retention, pressure, pricing, order**); the decision (**for reference: the return account can better guide criticism, because it gives a system of analogy one can operate (**the introductory tale, the opening round, the next instalment and the suspense-clasp of the storyteller's script can all be matched directly**), whereas the exception account can only judge web fiction to be a 'fall'; but the risk of the return account is that one also carries over the storyteller's moral character (**the storyteller is under no obligation to 'be faithful to the story'; he is under an obligation only to be faithful to the subscriber**); the marking looks at whether it comes down to one concrete rewriting —** for example: where formerly you would have written 'the second half of this book collapses', you should now write 'after chapter X this book turns to sustaining updates; the aim of the narrative changes from drawing to a close to the renewing of the subscription, and so the word "collapse" does not hold; what holds is the cost of its having no ending' (**this is one real rewriting of the practice of criticism:** the language of evaluation has been replaced by the institution of pricing**); if the conclusion can be written, '**so the real question of this chapter is not whether online literature is literature, but that the very attention with which we read literature has itself already been sold per occasion; when we complain that it is watered, we are using a self we ourselves buy by the minute**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The second birth, filed: conditions (**bandwidth, payment, licensing**), the shelf (**the table of categories is a programme that cannot be read**), apparatuses (**the golden three chapters, the infinite text, in-chapter comments**), length (**a price list is not an aesthetic**), recognition (**the seal cannot cover the paper: what it wants is a contract**), method (**save the interface, because the shell is part of the main text**). Method in one sentence: **first look up the unit of pricing, and only then talk of the unit of narrative**. Eleven chapters have, with this, been read through: from the copper cash of the *gōulán* 勾栏 to the thousand characters of the platform, Chinese-language narrative has all along been given its shape by one and the same thing — **someone is willing to pay a little money for the next stretch**.
