---
title: "New realism and the 1990s"
---

The experimental literature of the previous chapter gave up the reader's immediate satisfaction; the literature of this chapter gives up something larger — **it gives up 'meaning' as a wholesale commodity: the literature of the 1980s fought over how to write, and the literature of the 1990s no longer even explains why one writes; and all of this happened so fast because the distribution system was changed once, and changed so thoroughly that the author's identity changed with it**.

## Definition: a quiet dividing of the household {#def-fenjia}

::: definition From supply to distribution {#def-fahang}
**The received account** (**from the mid-1980s on, publishing units gradually adopted enterprise accounting and, of their own accord, bore their own profits and losses; book distribution expanded from the main channel of state-run bookshops into many channels, collective and individual; in the 1990s book prices and manuscript fees were marketised — **the consequence of these three things:** the economic centre of literary production moved from the editorial office to the distributors and the bookshop counter (the fiercest fulfilment yet of Chapter 1's claim that distribution volume decides literary history: the previous chapter was still only a loosening of quotas; this chapter changes the client (**so the true object of study in this chapter is not the work, but a profit-and-loss account**)); **a writer's three incomes** (**the received account: a salary (a post and a grade inside the system), manuscript fees and royalties, and income from abroad and from lecturing — **three kinds of money correspond to three kinds of writing:** the salary feeds 'periodical literature', royalties feed the long novel and the bestseller, and money from abroad feeds 'the part that cannot be published at home' (the contemporary version of Chapter 5's 'three economic sources determine three literary styles': that the books a writer wrote after 1993 are longer than those of 1985 is first a fact of accounting, and only then a fact of aesthetics (**much longer, selling much more, and also much slower: it no longer has to meet a copy deadline; it has to meet the reader's commuting time**)); **the exit of criticism** (**the received account: in the 1990s the space for literary criticism in the newspapers and periodicals shrank, and reviewing turned towards the academy; at the same time the 'planner', the 'book-review advertisement' and the 'bestseller list' appeared — **the place of the critic and the editor was taken by a new profession (**the publishing planner:** what he reads is the market, what he signs is the contract, and what he says is 'readers like it' (the successor to Chapter 1's 'print is the invisible patron': **this time the patron revises the manuscript directly**.
:::

::: definition The programme of 'new realism' {#def-xieshi}
**The received account** (**from the late 1980s, works of the kind of Chi Li 池莉's *A Life of Vexations* (*Fannao rensheng* 《烦恼人生》), Fang Fang 方方's *Landscape* (*Fengjing* 《风景》) and Liu Zhenyun 刘震云's *A Ground of Chicken Feathers* (*Yidi jimao* 《一地鸡毛》) were grouped by the critics under the name 'new realism'; their gist is often summed up as writing 'the everyday existence of ordinary people', with the author withdrawing from judgement — **this lesson writes that out as three procedures one can carry out (**① choice of material: change from the event to the process (**going to work, the allocation of housing, a place for the child at a nursery, the price of hairtail**); ② point of view: change from judging to recording (**narration with no adjectives added = a moral stance:** 'I do not judge your lives; I only write them down' (**this is the core of this chapter:** it can be read at once as respect and as indifference**)); ③ structure: change from conflict to depletion (**there is no antagonist, so there is no victory:** and so the reader has nowhere to deliver a sense of justice (**this is the same reason it makes some people find it 'trivial', and others feel 'at last someone is writing about me'**.
:::

## Texts {#def-wenben}

::: definition *A Life of Vexations*: one day's balance sheet {#def-yitian}
**The received plot** (**one day in an engineer's life: queuing to squeeze onto the ferry, the workshop, the allocation of housing, the child, the bonus, supper, sleep — **this lesson calls it a chapter of 'accounts, and no poetry' (a return visit to the previous chapter's clause on *The Plum in the Golden Vase* (*Jin Ping Mei*): **it changes the novel's unit of measure back from 'event' to 'duration' — **when length is used to cover one day rather than ten years, the novel no longer claims that life can be summarised (**inside this one sentence sits the whole ideology of the 1990s:** the right to summarise was confiscated (**the other side of Chapter 6's 'a fine selection is a deletion and an alteration': new realism refuses to make a summary, and so it is at once the most honest realism and a complete withdrawal of its stake from the theory of 'typification' (**typification requires that one person stand for a kind of person; new realism says: a person stands only for the time he himself spends queuing**).
:::

::: definition *The Work Unit*: the system as climate {#def-danwei}
**The received plot** (**the allocation of housing, trust, and a little squeezing-out, inside one office; a newcomer learning to 'be sensible' — **its technical invention is:** to write the system as the weather (**no decision, no meeting, no document, only 'everyone does it this way' (**the continuation, after the Seventeen Years, of Chapter 6's 'the system cannot correct an error': **this time there is not even a fault one could appeal:** everything complies (**this is the aesthetic of 'wearing down':** punishment does not descend; it is apportioned; an appeal has no addressee, because no one, alone, has done a single thing that can be pointed to — **this is also why it is colder than scar literature (literature of the wounded):** scar literature has a defendant; the work unit has none (**and 'there is no defendant' is precisely the experience that readers in the 1990s could recognise most readily**.
:::

::: definition The belated lineage of *One Sentence Is Worth Ten Thousand* {#def-ding}
**The received account** (**Liu Zhenyun's long novel was written in the late 2000s and won an important prize in the country; its theme is a person looking for someone he 说得着 ('can really talk to') — **this lesson places it at the end of the chapter, because it is the longest result of that line of new realism (**①** it changed the unit of everyday narrative from 'things' to 'talk'** (**the earlier work writes about queuing; this one writes about having no one to talk to:** the same technical apparatus (a record that adds no judgement) at last comes to deal with the only thing that cannot be entered in the accounts (**loneliness**)); ②** it proves that the realist line need not stop at social description:** it can go as far as an ontology close to that of the folk storyteller (a return visit to the 'medical-record number' of *The Scholars* (*Rulin* 《儒林》) in Chapter 9: **Liu Zhenyun issued a medical-record number to 说不着 ('having no one one can talk to') as well**); ③** it is at the same time living evidence for this chapter's conclusion (**this novel could not have been written in the 1990s, not because the author lacked the ability, but because at that time 'a person cannot find anyone to talk to' was not yet as important as 'the allocation of housing':** the ranking of needs is rewritten as the system of allocation changes (Chapter 1, the hierarchy of needs: **literature is not a mirror; it is a table of priorities redrawn year by year**.
:::

## Debates {#def-lunzheng}

::: definition The great debate on the humanist spirit {#def-renwen}
**The received account** (**in 1993–1995, periodicals in Shanghai organised a debate on 'the loss of the humanist spirit': one side held that the market and secularisation had devalued the life of the spirit; the other held that this was an elitist complaint against popular taste — **this lesson does not award a win or a loss; it gives a structural analysis (**①** the evidence on both sides is second-hand** (**one side cites the book market and the bestseller lists; the other cites degrees and the distribution volume of periodicals:** both sides are using distribution data to prove a moral proposition (Lesson 3, 'a numerator with no denominator': **the sentence 'serious books will not sell', in those years when the total number of titles surged, is almost impossible to test**)); ②** the real disagreement is 'who ought to represent culture'** (**this is the third time court sits, after the power to define in Chapter 2 and the power to review in the previous chapter:** and this time the opponent is not the official side, but the market:** this is the new content of the intellectual's situation in the 1990s — **in the past they had to persuade power; now they have to attract attention (**persuasion is a kind of argument; attraction is a kind of design:** this is one piece of hard evidence in this chapter concerning 'change in language':** in the essays and the *zawen* (polemical essays) of the 1990s a great many words appeared about 'the margin', 'keeping watch' and 'stance' (**they are all names of posts taken up after persuasion has failed**.
:::

::: definition The place of Wang Shuo {#def-wangshuo}
**The received account** (**Wang Shuo 王朔's fiction and the 'Wang Shuo craze', the adaptations for film and television, and the debates, are one item that cultural discussion in the 1990s cannot get round — **this lesson's treatment (**①** his syntax is a technique of extrication** (**using the comic patter of the streets and self-deprecation to take apart the vocabulary of the sublime (**Chapter 8, 'writing in jest = a disclaimer', and the tradition in Chapter 1 of 'nothing but the truth':** lower yourself to the bottom first, and no one can demote you any further (**this is irony at zero cost: he never advocates anything, so he cannot be refuted (Lesson 4, the formula for transmissibility:** high emotion, low refutability — this is the grammatical reason he became a writer for the masses**)); ②** the value of the debate is that it exposes the premise the two sides share** (**the critics say he dissolves values; the defenders say he is honest; both camps take it as given that 'literature ought to carry values' (**this premise itself is the legacy of the 1980s (**the real change of the 1990s is that this premise quietly leaves the stage:** from then on literature could be taken as a profession, a craft, a kind of entertainment, and did not have to be a mission (**the most important sentence of this chapter:** new realism and Wang Shuo look opposed, but in fact together they completed one and the same thing — taking the post of 'speaking for others' off the writer**.
:::

::: example Two readings, both true {#ex-liangdu}
The received plot of *The Story of Qiu Ju* (*Qiu Ju da guansi* 《秋菊打官司》): the peasant woman Qiu Ju 秋菊 insists on a 说法 ('an explanation'), wins the case in the end, and yet the disposition she receives is not the kind she wanted. Please fill in: **① what she wants (one word), ② what the system can give (one word), ③ how the film's choices of form produce two opposite readings**; and answer: **why a text of this kind can be read at once as a 'hymn of praise' and as 'satire', while neither reading counts as a misreading**.
::: solution
**①** — **acknowledgement:** what she wants is for the other party to acknowledge that she, this person, has the right to be taken seriously (**the rural version of Chapter 2's 'the power to define is the power to allocate':** what she wants is one cell on the table of categories, not compensation**); **②** — **disposition:** what the system puts out is a set of standard actions (**its efficiency comes precisely from its not answering the question of 'acknowledgement' (Chapter 8, procedure; Chapter 6, the document that assigns responsibility:** what procedure gives is what can be carried out, not what can console**); **③ the choice of form** (**devices such as a documentary style + local actors + dialect press the 'official' part down to a minimum (**the same shots can be read as 'our villages are just like this, and the system really is in charge', and can also be read as 'a person asks for an explanation and does not get one in ten years':** the crux is that the film gives no words of judgement (**this is the judgement of this chapter:** when the narrator withdraws from judgement (procedure ② of new realism), the power to judge is handed to the audience, and the audience's political standpoint determines the nature of the film (**so the two readings are both true:** it is not that the work is ambiguous; its omission is precise enough that it lets each of the two audiences fill in exactly their own share**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The avant-garde and realism are in fact one thing {#int-yijian}
**One shared opponent** (**the avant-garde of the previous chapter and the new realism of this chapter look as if one seeks the strange and the other the ordinary, but what they take apart is the same thing:** the narrative of historical necessity (**the avant-garde takes it apart at the level of grammar (**causal sequence is suspect**); realism takes it apart at the level of experience (**life is, in itself, repetitive and without a climax**) (**this is why many writers, between 1989 and 1993, moved from the avant-garde to realism and did not feel it a betrayal:** they changed the apparatus; they did not change the enemy (**collateral evidence that can be checked:** both camps suddenly took an interest in 'chance' (**whether a story can hold no longer depends on whether it is 'typical', but on whether it could happen a second time (**the reverse of Chapter 3's algorithm clause:** the literature of the 1990s began to refuse to supply an explanation — this is the moment at which it most resembles the modern, and the moment at which it is least welcome**).
:::

::: history A timeline of the counter {#sec-guitai}
**1987** The advance of enterprise accounting in publishing units, and of many channels of distribution (**the received account:** this is the true opening event of this chapter: **more important than any novel**); **1989–1991** periodicals cut their format and their frequency; writers 'went into business' and changed trades (**the received account:** a professional group experienced the hiring market for the first time (**'not writing' becomes, here, a variable one can count (Chapter 4, keeping the accounts other people leave out:** the list of the silent in the early 1990s explains the look of literature in the mid-1990s better than a catalogue of works does**); **1993** the collision of humanist books with writing for the market, and the eve of the debate on the 'humanist spirit'; **1995** film and television linked with mass-market publishing (**the received account:** literature's first reader changed from the editor-in-chief to the 'planner' (**and so the meetings on what to publish were held in bookshops and on film sets, not in the editorial office**); **the late 1990s** writing by the 'post-70s' and writing of 'private speech' entered the periodicals (**the received account:** the body and intimate experience became newly legitimate subject matter: **this is another redrawing of the quota system — **last time, what was drawn was the boundary of politics; this time, what is drawn is the boundary of gender (**and the controversy called into question as 'consuming women' is precisely a new column on the same quota table (Chapter 3, survivors counted at eight-tenths:** the women's writing of that generation which we read is a sample left after screening, at the same time, by two editorial tastes**); **1997–2000** online literature (web fiction) gets under way (**the received account:** the next client is already on the line (**this chapter's closing words echo the previous chapter's study of structure:** the 'freedom' of each generation of literature is exactly equal to those few doors that, at the time, no one was guarding**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in this lesson
**Reading new realism as 'having no ideals'** (**'triviality' is its apparatus, not its failure:** to judge it, one must first say whether the theory of typification still held at the time (Lesson 1, where the measuring-rod was made**)); **explaining everything by 'marketisation'** (**the same market contains bestsellers that contradict one another:** so the market is a condition, not a cause (Chapter 3, the algorithm: **one must add three causal lines — generation, the staffing of periodicals, and translation and publishing — and rank them**)); **taking the debate on the 'humanist spirit' for a failure whose case is already closed** (**the question it raised has still been answered by no one:** when attention becomes a scarce good, who is responsible for supplying what does not sell so well (**the first note of Lesson 10's 'public' clause**)); **ignoring the writer's identity** (**in the 1990s a writer's contracts, royalties, share of film and television income, and place of residence all changed:** these documents get written into the sentences (Lesson 2, the mediation agreement between the production end and the interpretation end: **one need not replace the work with the life, but to pretend the life does not exist is to hand the economic variables to someone else for nothing**).
:::

::: exercise One day's accounts {level=1}
In 100 characters, write your own 'life of vexations': **only quantities, prices and lengths of waiting may appear; no evaluative adjective is allowed**.
::: solution
Marking: **clearing the adjectives to zero is a hard constraint** (**the moment 累 ('tired') and 烦 ('vexed') are allowed, the apparatus breaks:** these must be carried by the numbers (**the standard of a pass is: the reader himself grows tired (Chapter 6, 'give no conclusion; give the cost'**).
:::

::: exercise No defendant {level=2}
In 120 characters, narrate one 'harm in compliance' you have lived through (no one did anything wrong, and yet you were harmed). The requirement: **not a single person who could be blamed may appear**, and write **in which column your harm was in the end recorded**.
::: solution
Marking: **'in which column it is recorded' must be concrete** (**time, opportunity, credit, or health:** an abstract 'loss' scores nothing (**this is the technical core of *The Work Unit*:** writing punishment as an apportionment (Lesson 1, keeping the accounts other people leave out: **this column is usually counted on no institution's return**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the unguarded door {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing '**the most important lesson Chinese literature learned in the 1990s is that its social function can be withdrawn; when "speaking for others" is no longer a post a writer receives automatically, he either bids for new readers, or retreats into private experience, or simply changes jobs; these three options remain, to this day, the whole of the career paths open to a writer in Chinese, and it took the critics ten years to admit: this is not a fall, it is a repricing**'. You must draw on at least six items: **the change of the distribution system**, **three incomes corresponding to three kinds of writing**, **the three procedures of new realism**, **the climate-like system of *The Work Unit***, **the problem of evidence in the debate on the humanist spirit**, **Wang Shuo's zero-cost irony**; and adjudicate: **which of the two narratives, 'repricing' and 'decline', is the better — you must say who benefits from each (who obtains resources by way of which narrative of literary history)**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**distribution, three incomes, procedures, climate, evidence, irony**); the adjudication (**for reference:** neither narrative is fair, but the beneficiaries differ — **the 'decline' account gives the resources to the academy and the canon (it argues why only a few are worth reading); the 'repricing' account gives the resources to the trade and to cultural policy (it argues that this is an industry which can be fostered)** (**marking looks at whether 'the beneficiary' is pinned to a concrete institution: a chair, a prize, a fund, a book series, film and television rights (Lesson 1, 'literature comes to set its seal'; Lesson 15, recognition after the fact: **a narrative of literary history is a budget document**) — if the conclusion can be written '**so the hardest part of this chapter is not judging better and worse, but admitting that the marking sheet in our hands is also a product of this repricing: the reason we find "triviality" worth discussing is precisely that, after the 1990s, the everyday became the only subject matter no one fought over — and no one fought over it then because it did not sell**', that is an A.
:::
