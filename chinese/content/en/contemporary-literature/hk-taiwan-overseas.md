---
title: "Hong Kong, Taiwan and overseas Chinese literature"
---

The first seven chapters wrote Chinese literature as one whole with an editorial office; this chapter shows that it is in fact three — **one and the same set of square characters grew into three literatures inside three different payroll systems; the method of this chapter is to read 'Chinese literature' as a question at customs: who, in which year, was counted as one of our own, and how many times the scales were changed at that taking of delivery**.

## Definition: three systems {#def-xitong}

::: definition Who pays the writer's wages {#def-faxin}
**Received account** (**After the war, Hong Kong supported popular writing by newspaper serials and by the film and television industry; Taiwan supported modernism and academic writing by the universities, by the military's literary apparatus and by newspaper supplements; overseas, university writing programmes in the English-speaking world, publishing houses and prizes supported a group of authors writing in a second language —** every conclusion of this lesson is built on this payroll (an extension of the three kinds of income in Chapter 6: **it is not that the writer chooses the system; the form of the system first decides what can be written to the end** (**paid by the square, a serial therefore has the modern descendants of “且听下回分解” ('if you would know the rest, hear it set out in the next instalment') (Our translation.); paid by the term, a teaching post therefore has long poems that can be read in class; paid by the reader, a writing programme therefore has sentences that must first explain their cultural background**).
:::

::: definition Why it cannot be told as 'centre and periphery' {#def-bianyuan}
**One counter-example is enough** (**Received account: before the 1980s, Hong Kong, Taiwan and overseas preserved and carried on a number of ways of writing that had been broken off on the mainland — modernist technique, popular genres, notebook literature and the essay, and vernacular lyricism after May Fourth; after 1978 these ways of writing were brought back into the mainland (the merging of Chapter 2's 'drawer' and Chapter 5's 'borrowed mirror'):** **one cultural detour changed the grammar of the parent body more effectively than any manifesto** (**so the figure in this chapter is not concentric circles, but a map of the changed routes** (**a harsher point:** **of the 'modern fiction' that mainland writers read in the 1980s, a considerable part was knowledge at a second remove, passed on from translations and then again from criticism in Hong Kong and Taiwan** (**what is influenced is always the nearest textbook — and the textbook is sometimes someone else's**).
:::

## Hong Kong: the economics of the serial {#def-xianggang}

::: definition Jin Yong's three rewritings {#def-jinyong}
**Received account** (**From 1955 to 1972 Jin Yong 金庸 (Zha Liangyong 查良镛; also Louis Cha) wrote *wuxia* fiction (martial-arts fiction) in newspaper serials, and revised it many times afterwards; the text in circulation is the third revision; he was at the same time a newspaperman and a writer of editorials —** three technical consequences (**①** **the space was paid by the day** (**every day a suspense had to be left, and not too many people could die** (**readers would change their newspaper (the twentieth-century version of Chapter 9's 'the next instalment = a division of time'):** **a serial is not a genre; it is a subscription system** (**it explains every 'redundancy' in Chinese popular fiction: that redundancy is the cost of renewal**)); ② revision is an act of copyright (the contemporary closing of the loop upon Chapter 2's 'the commented copy is the definitive text' and Chapter 3's 'licence agreement for reuse': the author himself did three hundred years of marginal commentary, and so the question “哪个版本是真的” ('which version is the real one?') (Our translation.) was for the first time answered by the seller (the cost of it is: the side-branches cut out of the newly revised text are exactly the side-branches that old readers remember most deeply (and so there was copyright, and reception history was given one cut)); ③ the newspaperman's double identity (the hand that wrote editorials and the hand that wrote *wuxia* shared one political judgement (received account: the loyalty and duty in his fiction, and the Hua–Yi distinction, were adjusted as the stance of his newspapers changed (this is not gossip; this is evidence from the side that produces the work: one person's two genres alternated on the calendar of one and the same newspaper (the mediation paper of Lesson 2: the author is alive, both sides hold, and neither can prove which one it is that explains the other).
:::

::: definition A parade into literary history {#def-jinru}
**Received account** (**In the 1980s *wuxia* fiction reached mainland readers on a large scale; scholars such as Yan Jiayan 严家炎 publicly affirmed Jin Yong, and articles of the sort of Wang Meng 王蒙's 《上马末回头著稿代柬》 ('Once mounted, a look back at the end: a draft to stand in for a note') discussed Jin Yong and were widely cited; around 1994 a scholarly conference on Jin Yong was held in Shaoxing, and 'Jin studies' became a phrase of the newspapers; around 2000 his works entered secondary-school reading and the lists of texts chosen for textbooks, and set off a dispute —** this lesson calls this line 'a taking of delivery that lasted twenty years' (**①** **who took delivery first** (**the mass of readers and the secondary-school textbook, not literary prizes and scholarly monographs (a reordering of the four gates of canonisation in Chapter 7:** **this time the third gate (the textbook) opened before the fourth gate (translation out of Chinese, and international reputation)** (**the textbook is the least romantic, and the most effective, coronation**)); ② **the legitimising move of criticism** (**a mainstream scholar said something of the sort “金庸的多卷本，我一卷一卷读完了” ('Jin Yong's many volumes: I read them through, volume by volume') (the received account cites the gist) (Our translation.) —** **note the function of this sentence:** **it is not an evaluation; it is a ticket of admission** (**it lets a professor admit to having read them** (**this is the kind of event most common in the literary field, and least often put on record:** **authority issues a pass to itself, and in passing rewrites the catalogue for the whole profession (the contemporary Chinese scene of Lesson 1's 'peer review'**).
:::

## Taiwan: another timeline for modernism {#def-taiwan}

::: definition A generation in which the line did not break {#def-duandai}
**Received account** (**In the 1950s and 1960s Taiwan had debates and societies for modernist poetry and fiction (including periodicals of the sort of 《现代文学》 (*Modern Literature*)), and quite a few of their authors taught in universities; in the 1970s there was the debate on nativist literature (*xiangtu wenxue*); from the end of the 1970s on, the question of where 'Chinese literature' belonged was politicised on the island (received account) —** three observations of this lesson (**①** **technique was not lost, because there were teaching posts** (**when periodicals on the mainland closed, the syntax of modernism went on being taught in the Taiwanese classroom as 'the basic craft of the national literature' (the control group for Chapter 2's 'ten years':** **the same generation's drawer, and the same generation's lecture notes** (**this is the most important asymmetry in this chapter:** **one system turned literature into a drawer, the other turned literature into a syllabus; twenty years later, when the people in the drawer came out, they found that the syllabus had already arrived** (**this is the sociological reason why the revival of modernism on the mainland in the 1980s passed, in large part, through the hands of Hong Kong and Taiwan**)); ② **a debate is another way of writing a struggle over quotas** (**the struggle between the nativist and the modern is, in structure, of the same type as the 'brightness quota' of the previous chapter:** **what both fight over is 'who represents the reader' (Chapter 3, the right to define:** **the same lawsuit was opened separately on the two sides of the strait; the verdicts were opposite, and the reason on both sides was 'the people'** (**this is the cleanest lesson this lesson can give about 'literature and politics':** **the same reason and opposite verdicts show that the reason is decoration, and the verdict is a function**).
:::

::: definition Two readings of one story {#def-chuqing}
**Received plot** (**Work of the sort of Pai Hsien-yung 白先勇's 《永远的尹雪艳》 ('The Eternal Yin Xueyan') and 《游园惊梦》 ('Visiting the Garden, Startled in the Dream') writes dispersal out of old families and opera texts; 《台北人》 (*Taipei People*) is made up of many pieces, and the epigraph takes something of the sort of “旧时王谢堂前燕” ('swallows which, in old times, were before the halls of Wang and Xie') (Our translation.) (the received account cites the gist of the line) —** this lesson's technical verdict (**①** **it wrote 'root-seeking' in the reverse direction** (**the mainland writers of the previous chapter went back to the county to seek roots; the Taipei people packed the root onto one person's body and boarded the ship** (**Yin Xueyan is an ancestral hall:** **she is forever young, because memory does not allow her to grow old** (**this is why fiction of this kind was widely imitated on the mainland in the 1980s and seldom explained in this way (another chain of cause and effect for Chapter 5's 'time shattered':** **it is not that history is doubted; it is that history has been moved house** (**②** **on the mainland it was read as 'patriotic'** (**received account: *Taipei People* was accepted because it wrote of old people, old matters and nostalgia for home; and at the same time it is a lyricism of the complete loser** (**both readings hold:** **the former takes delivery by theme, the latter takes delivery by form** (**this lesson treats it as the specimen of this chapter:** **one and the same text, two customs tariffs** (**and the tariff is decided by that year's demand, 'do we need Taiwan or not' —** **this is exactly the contemporary version of Chapter 3's 'once a text leaves the country, the right to explain it belongs to customs'**).
:::

## Overseas: prizes, translation and 'the nationality that is cited' {#def-haiwai}

::: definition People who write in a second language {#def-ereryan}
**Received account** (**In 2000 Gao Xingjian 高行健 received the Nobel Prize in Literature; Ha Jin 哈金, writing in English, received the National Book Award of the United States at the end of the 1990s, and an interview of his on “作家的祖国是语言” ('the writer's homeland is language') (Our translation.) was widely cited; a considerable number of *Huayu* (Chinese-language) poets living abroad have kept going by bilingual periodicals and readings —** three mechanisms (**①** **the source of legitimacy is placed outside** (**when prizes and periodicals at home cannot, or will not, take delivery, a prize overseas becomes a passport in reverse (the fourth gate of Chapter 7:** **translation out of Chinese, and international reputation, can stand as security for the price set at home** (**and so a strange flow in two directions appears:** **a person is first banned or ignored at home, crowned in Sweden, and then published again at home** (**this lesson calls it 'detour coronation'** (**its cost is that the currency of evaluation has to pass through two conversions of exchange rate, and each conversion depends on translation and on a cut taken by departments of Sinology** (**this is why the question 'who represents Chinese literature' always has a third consignee**)); ② 'language is the homeland' is a situation, not a theory (for the person concerned, the sentence is a description (what he can move away is indeed only language); for the reader it is often taken as a programme (and so it becomes a new orthodoxy: as if one were honest only by leaving — the retrospective ratification of Lesson 15: twenty years after a sentence of self-description has been cited, it grows a meaning it never said)); ③ it changed the length of the sentence in Chinese (Chinese written in order to be translated into English will reduce allusions, shorten the modifiers and increase the actions that can be explained (received account: the Chinese of some overseas writing really is 'cleaner' (this lesson's attitude: this is both a loss and a gain: the loss is in the echoing between texts; the gain is in readability (and after 2000 readability became the most expensive commodity (Chapter 9's foreshadowing: in the next chapter it is the whole)).
:::

## The category itself {#def-fanchou}

::: definition 'Sinophone literature': a change of gauge {#def-yuxi}
**Received account** (**In 1972 the “中国（语）文学学会” ('Chinese (Language) Literature Society') was founded in North America; at the end of the 1970s 《联合报》 (*United Daily News*) set up prizes for fiction and the essay and went on promoting overseas Chinese-language writing; in the 2000s concepts of the sort “华语语系文学／Sinophone” ('Sinophone literature' / Sinophone) were proposed, meant to see the writing of the various *Huayu* regions as parallel systems rather than as centre and periphery —** this lesson's way of handling it (**①** **read the concept as a history of institutions** (**the moment at which each new name is proposed can be matched to one concrete change of institution: a society founded, a prize set up, a university course adjusted, a book series published (Chapter 3, 'the name is sovereignty':** **a new word is not a discovery; it is a proposal** (**②** **it solved a real problem, and it also made a new one** (**the real problem:** **writing from Malaysia, Singapore, Taiwan and Hong Kong was no longer treated as a 'local branch';** **the new problem:** **'*Huayu*' became the only item held in common, and so differences of politics, of institution and of language were flattened by a single line of language** (**this is what opponents call 'drawing the frontier by language'** (**this lesson's ruling:** **both are measuring-rods, and their uses differ: one is for sorting the shelves, one is for writing literary history; the step that mixes the two is the error (Lesson 1, where the ruler was made:** **first ask what this ruler is to measure**).
:::

::: intuition Customs' confession {#int-haiguan}
**The conclusion of this chapter is one sentence of method** (**the three words 'Chinese literature', '*Huayu* literature' and 'writing in Chinese' do not have the same boundary, and when they are used by turns in one and the same article with no explanation, the argument begins to leak** (**every 'so-and-so was written by a Chinese and so belongs to us' should be rewritten as:** **'under the standard of classification I am using, so-and-so is placed in this class; that standard was chosen in order to solve one concrete problem'** (**this is the final application of Lesson 1's 'a list is a pair of spectacles':** **the spectacles may be changed, but one may not fail to declare that one is wearing them;** **and the cost ought to be written down** (**bringing Hong Kong, Taiwan and overseas in often makes a narrative of literary history lose its institutional subject:** **'the modern experience of Chinese literature' must from then on have five different timelines** (**this is not a bad thing; it is honesty: the whole teaching of this chapter presses on this one sentence** (**a literary history of many centres is harder to write, but it will not tell one lie at each of 1949, 1966, 1978 and 2000**).
:::

::: history A map of the changed routes {#sec-gaidao}
**1950s–1960s** Hong Kong and Taiwan each completed the institutions of modernism (**lecture notes against the drawer**); **1972** the 'Chinese (Language) Literature Society' was founded in North America (**received account:** for the first time there appeared an organisational subject crossing regions (**thirty years before any theory, it was already treating '*Huayu* literature' as a field that could be administered (**a society is a customs house)); 1977–1979 the aftermath of Taiwan's nativist debate, and the establishment of the literary prizes of *United Daily News* (received account: prizes began to manufacture classics (and it later became one of the most stable 'standards of delivery' in the *Huayu* world (the southern version of the four gates of Chapter 7); 1980s *wuxia* and romance fiction entered the mainland book market, and modern works from Hong Kong and Taiwan changed hands through periodicals and anthologies (an import with no customs declaration — the most ironic chain: the mainland avant-garde took, from a relay station, the tools they used against realism (and the label of origin was torn off the tools); after 1989 a new tide of writing of dispersal (received account: three accents took shape: that of the 1950s, that of after 1989, and that of study abroad and of immigration (to call them all by the one name 'overseas *Huayu* literature' is the flattening this chapter criticises)); 2000/2012 two writers connected with *Huayu* received the Nobel Prize (one almost unreadable in the Chinese-language world, one setting off a revaluation of the price at home (both results are one and the same mechanism: reputation is brought in from outside; the price is set on the inside.
:::

::: warning Four pits in this lesson
**Treating '*Huayu*' as a community** (**it is a line of language, not a stretch of shared history:** **put the line in the place of the history, and differences of institution are read as differences of style (the clause of the ruling above**)); **treating Hong Kong and Taiwan as a museum that 'preserved the orthodox line'** (**what they preserved is what each of them chose to preserve, and at the same time they were carrying on experiments and a commercialisation that the mainland could not see** (**nobody kept the flame for anybody else:** **this is this chapter's reservation about every narrative of 'cultural China'** (**the conclusion implied by the metaphor of the flame is that the parent body has already gone cold** (**and it has not gone cold; it has only changed its fuel**)); **representing the whole by one Nobel Prize** (**a repetition of the clause in Chapter 7:** **and this time it is more absurd: most of those represented have never been translated**); **ignoring what the market does** (**'Jin Yong enters the textbook', 'the fever for Eileen Chang 张爱玲', 'the bestseller standing of Yi Shu 亦舒 and Sanmao 三毛' are all publishing events that come before literary judgement (Chapter 6, the planner:** **first the sales, then the essay of canonisation** (**told the other way round, it makes a false history of literature**).
:::

::: summary
The map of the changed routes, filed: payroll (**the serial, the teaching post, the writing programme: three lengths of sentence**), copyright (**the author himself takes on three hundred years of commentary**), asymmetry (**a gap of twenty years between the drawer and the syllabus**), import (**take only the equipment, not the manual**), coronation (**reputation and text can circulate uncoupled**), the name (**every new word answers to one change of institution**). The method in one sentence: **meet a 'we', and first check which goods it has bought, and which labels it has torn off**. Next chapter: literature on the screen — online literature.
