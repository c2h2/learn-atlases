---
title: "Root-seeking and the avant-garde"
---

The previous chapter moved the battlefield from content to form. This chapter opens two paths at once — **one goes back, to borrow a collective culture; one goes inward, to take narrative itself apart. The two paths look opposite, but they are in fact two solutions to one and the same 题材外逃 ("flight from subject matter")**.

## Definition: around 1985 {#def-chuangkou}

::: definition Why every textbook has this year {#def-nianfen}
**The received outline** (around 1985 there appeared programmatic essays of the kind of Han Shaogong (韩少功), "The Roots of Literature" (《文学的根》), novellas and short stories by Ah Cheng (阿城), Jia Pingwa (贾平凹) and others, and a batch of experimental fiction that later critics called "avant-garde" — **this lesson reads that year as a statistical phenomenon, not a phenomenon of genius:** the number of periodicals, the autonomy of editorial offices, the prize-giving rhythm of the Writers' Association system, and the release of the backlog in translation and publishing, four things peaking inside the same window at the same time — the confluence of Chapter 1's "distribution format" and the previous chapter's "quota": **when the quota loosened, what surged out in the first wave was not better works, but new categories** (because an author must first prove that "literature need not be written in only one way", and only then has time to write one piece well).
:::

::: definition The root-seeking programme {#def-xungen}
**The received account cites the gist of Han Shaogong's "The Roots of Literature"** (literature was to "回到自己的民族之根" ("return to the roots of its own nation"); folk culture and regional custom were treated as living carriers — **this is a typical call for tenders:** it has three items that can be carried out (**① shift the range of material from contemporary politics to custom and place; ② shift authority from policy texts to the field and to documents; ③ set "national character" up as a commodity one can compete over** (Chapter 11: the programme is the equipment: **all three can be taught, can be checked and can be copied — this is why it could become "a school" within two years**); **its hidden function** ("根" ("the root") is a client no one can point to (the previous chapter's clause on "flight from subject matter": **when subject matter taken from reality is under a limit, the author hauls in an entire Bronze Age; what was hauled in this time was the *Songs of Chu*, dialects, 巫傩 (wu shamanism and nuo rites), notebook literature (*biji*) and the county town** (**what it gained** is that literature recovered thickness and geography; **what it lost** is that the word "民族" ("nation") has not, in the twenty years since, been seriously questioned again as to its list of ingredients).
:::

::: definition Borrowing a mirror from outside {#def-jingzi}
**The received outline** (the translation and introduction of Latin American "magical realism", and the effect of the Nobel Prize, are the most important foreign ground of root-seeking argument; and the Latin American method itself comes from a compound of European modernism and an indigenous oral tradition — **and so a three-stage cycle occurs:** Chinese writers recognised, inside a foreign model, the things in their own house (Chapter 11's "以故为新" ("using the old to make the new") and the previous chapter's "the object of influence is always the most recent textbook": **the textbook may be one that has been translated** (**this lesson gives the phenomenon a name:** what the borrowed mirror lights up is not abroad, but those corners of one's own house that had all along gone unacknowledged).
:::

## Text one: Ah Cheng's minimalism {#def-acheng}

::: definition The technique of *The Chess King* {#def-qi}
**The plot as generally told** (sent-down youth go down to the countryside, a man obsessed with Chinese chess (*xiangqi*), one wheel-battle of game after game, and the shame of a single sentence, "死于谋食也" ("He died of scheming for his food.") (Our translation.) — **this lesson attends to three things** (**①** **a double displacement of subject matter** (the sent-down experience is written as "吃" ("eating") and "艺" ("the art"), and not as "冤" ("grievance"): **and so it goes round the previous chapter's quota on wounds** (Chapter 3, "legitimate slots for injury": **Ah Cheng did not cancel the trauma; he only changed the ledger heading — from the political account to the account of survival** (**this is the most successful strategy of root-seeking fiction, and the strategy most easily misread as "escape"**)); **② archaising the language** (his prose is generally judged to lie close to notebook fiction and to the short informal essays (*xiaopin*) of the Ming and Qing — **he uses a language that has already left the everyday in order to write the present** (**the effect is defamiliarisation; the cost is sterilisation:** sentences shifted towards Classical Chinese (*wenyan*) cannot carry an outcry; they can only carry silence (**this is why *The Chess King* (《棋王》) reads as "干净" ("clean"): cleanness is a chosen kind of visibility** (Chapter 8: what is left after the adjectives are deleted)))); **③ the demotion of the hero** (the person who is "为棋而生" ("born for chess") does not oppose the world; he simply does not take part (**this supplied a template for the "non-hero" of the mid-to-late 1980s** (the modern version of Chapter 10's Baoyu (宝玉) "refusing valuation": **what Wang Yisheng (王一生) refuses is not the system, but every way of setting a price**).
:::

## Text two: the avant-garde taken apart {#def-xianfeng}

::: definition Three devices {#def-zhuangzhi}
**① The narrator shows himself (metanarrative)** (the received outline: works of the Ma Yuan (马原) kind let the "I" appear inside his own story and intervene in the plot — **this puts Chapter 3's "the teller's credit" on exhibition** (**the effect:** the reader can no longer hand himself over to the narrator (**its political meaning is mild, and its grammatical meaning is radical:** a narrator who can lie is more unsettling than a character who lies (**because in the latter case the fiction still guarantees that there is a truth; in the former, the concept of truth is dismissed from its post**)); **② the shattering of time** (the received outline: the early short stories of Yu Hua (余华), Sun Ganlu (孙甘露) and Ge Fei (格非) often break up the order of cause and effect, and replace development with repetition and dislocation — **this is not a display of skill; it is a view of history** (**when a person cannot say clearly "from which step things began to go wrong", linear narrative becomes a lie** (**the form of avant-garde fiction and the questioning of reflection literature are in fact two answers to the same question:** reflection says "I want to look another ten years further back"; the avant-garde says "the act of enquiry is itself suspect" (**this is the most important contrast in the chapter: the two steps fork here into two genres**)); **③ excess of the body** (the received outline: violence, blood, torture and death appear at a high frequency in the avant-garde short stories of the late 1980s — **the reading in this lesson must give three causal lines at once** (**① aesthetics: once narrative has come apart, intensity of the senses is the only coordinate one can still rely on; ② the market and film: an adaptation needs scenes that can be seen** (the clause on "film as intermediary" below); **③ ethics: when the public part of language has been contaminated, the flesh is the last piece of ground left that can be written on** (**any one of these, told on its own, will read avant-garde fiction askew** (Chapter 3's three prescriptions: **one must compare which of them is the more refutable**.
:::

::: definition Film as intermediary {#def-dianying}
**The received outline** (adaptations of the kind of *Red Sorghum* (《红高粱》) and *Raise the Red Lantern* (《大红灯笼高高挂》) gained international prizes and a reputation at home from the late 1980s into the early 1990s — **and so the first mass readership of Chinese avant-garde literature was gained through the screen** (**three consequences:** **①** **bias in the selection**: a work that has been adapted gains a second life; a work that has not must wait for the academy before it enters literary history (Chapter 3, "the luck of the archive"); **②** **subject matter attesting to itself**: writers began to write in elements that were "filmable" — **colour, courtyard compounds, ritual, the body** (**this is where the 1990s criticism of an "Oriental spectacle" comes from, and it is also where that criticism is half right** (**a more accurate way of putting it: not writing for foreign jurors, but writing for a new channel of distribution** (Chapter 1, "circulation decides literary history"); **③** **the author's name becomes a brand**: Mo Yan (莫言) winning an international prize (the received outline: 2012) attributes a "root-seeking" line that had run for several decades to one person (Lesson 15's ratification after the fact: **naming a person is easier than naming a system, and the system is forgotten** — **this lesson's reminder:** **"root-seeking" is a programme with twenty authors; the "Nobel Prize" is one person and one name:** **the two ways of keeping the books cannot be set against the same measure**).
:::

::: definition Yu Hua's turn: a year as a case {#def-yuhua}
**The received outline** (the short stories of the late 1980s are known for violence addressed to the senses and for games with narrative; in the early 1990s, works of the kind of *To Live* (《活着》) turned to an extremely plain first person in order to tell history — often explained as a "spiritual turn" or as "the discovery of conscience". **This lesson gives an explanation that is colder, and also more checkable** (**a change of narrative device** (**the earlier stage:** the narrator manipulates everything, and the characters are pieces in his hand; **the later stage:** narrative authority is handed to a party to the events who does not understand, and the disaster is calmly retold by the victim (**this second device is extremely efficient:** it brings refutability down to a minimum (**the victim's own testimony cannot be refuted** — and so it gains, at one and the same time, the greatest transmissibility and the greatest controversy (Lesson 4, "transmissibility ≈ intensity of emotion / refutability": **from the experiments to *To Live* is not a passage from hard to understand to easy to understand; it is one repricing, from low transmissibility to high transmissibility** (**what the critics call "betrayal" and what readers call "maturity" are in fact two sides of one and the same act of pricing**).
:::

::: definition Why it ended in the late 1980s {#def-jieshu}
**Four causal lines, which this lesson ranks by refutability** (**①** **institutions and personnel** (changes in the editorial line of the periodicals, and a number of the principal authors leaving or laying down the pen — **the most checkable**); **②** **the market** (popular reading matter, and adaptation for film and television, draw the stream away, and purely formal experiment loses its distribution (Chapter 9's constraint of the box office); **③** **the generations** (the next wave of writing styles itself "the everyday", "the worldly" and "新写代" ("the new writing generation"), and the posture of opposing the avant-garde is itself a declaration of succession (the literary-field version of Chapter 3's "the anxiety of influence"); **④** **the law of fatigue** (**the least falsifiable:** once a technique is mastered generally, it is no longer a discovery but a format (the same curve as the history of metre in Chapter 11 and the history of slogans in the previous chapter — **the greatest enemy of the "avant-garde" is the second year in which it is imitated** — **this lesson's attitude: all four must be written down, and they must be ranked:** **the person who takes ④ as the only explanation is usually the person who does not check ① and ②**).
:::

::: example Two flights outward {#ex-waitao}
Fill in a table comparing "root-seeking" and the "avant-garde": **① who the client is, ② who the first reader is, ③ where the point that can be refuted lies, ④ what each gives up**; then answer: **why the same group of editors could accept both schemes in the same year**.
::: solution
**① The client** — **root-seeking: nation, region, culture** (a client who cannot be refuted, but who can be self-contradictory); **the avant-garde: language and narrative themselves** (a client who cannot speak for you, but who will not betray you either (**this is why root-seeking readily becomes a school, and the avant-garde readily becomes individual persons: the former has a programme, the latter only a procedure** (Chapter 11: **archaism is rhetoric; innovation is finance**)); **② The first reader** — **root-seeking: intellectual readers who care about "who we are"; the avant-garde: editors, critics and fellow writers** (**the avant-garde's first reader is a peer, which explains its density of technique and the lowness of its circulation** (the reverse of Chapter 1's "a room that charges = one that can be relied on": **writing that has no readers has its quality guaranteed by peers, and also limited by their scale**)); **③ Where it can be refuted** — **root-seeking, on the concept of "national character"** (**one counter-example is enough: the same customs can be written as progress or as backwardness**); **the avant-garde, on "whether there is only this one way of telling"** (**one counter-example is enough: the same event, told in linear narrative, might be equally good** — **and it is usually not tested in this way, because what it is judged on is "新不新" ("whether it is new"), not "对不对" ("whether it is right")** — **this is the evaluative bind of literature of form: it refuses to offer a proposition that can be tested, and so it can only be judged by age** ("新" ("new") is a word of time, not a word of value)); **④ What is given up** — **root-seeking gives up the complexity of contemporary experience** (it hands the present over for other people to write); **the avant-garde gives up the reader's trust** (it hands intelligibility over to the future); **editors who accepted both in the same year were in fact doing one and the same thing:** **moving literature off the subject matter that was under a limit, and away to two directions that nobody was regulating at the time — space, and grammar** — **this is the chapter's final explanation:** **the burst of form in the mid-1980s was not literature suddenly becoming clever; it was literature being squeezed up to two doors that nobody was defending**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The cost of experimental literature {#int-chengben}
**Experiment is an operation run on debt** (**avant-garde writing gives up three things: immediate recompense from the reader, immediate adaptation for film and television, and immediate recognition from prizes** (**what it lives on: mutual confirmation between unofficial journals and fellow practitioners, the academy's belated ratification, and a second certification by translation abroad** (**note that all three of these are slow money** (Chapter 1's distribution format: **if a kind of writing can survive only by delayed channels, its theme is often "delay" itself** — **this is the sociological explanation of why avant-garde fiction prefers memory, dreams and disordered time, and not Bergson alone** — a checkable inference: **if the subject matter of this same batch of works is counted according to "whether time is reversible", the proportion ought to be significantly higher than in realist works of the same period** (Lesson 1, keeping the accounts other people leave out: **this cell has never been filled in**).
:::

::: history One programme and three channels {#sec-qudao}
**1985** Programmatic essays, and the name "root-seeking" (**a school is founded the moment the name appears:** and at the same moment it begins to lose effect); **1985–1987** the high-output years of experimental short fiction (**the window for novellas and short stories:** their cycle of printing is short (**this is why this wave is not the full-length novel: a novel needs two years, and the window was open for only three**)); **1986–1987** major literary periodicals and book series "canonised" avant-garde works (**editors became the real organisers of the movement** (the contemporary Chinese version of Chapter 1's "peer review": **one editor-in-chief's term of office is the lifespan of a movement**)); **1988–1992** film and translation abroad supplied a second market (**authors began to have two reputations: a reputation with readers at home, and an academic reputation abroad, and the two are not in touch with each other** (**this split is, to this day, still the structure of the international standing of literature in Chinese**)); **the 1990s** the academisation of "avant-garde criticism" (**a group of critics arranged the procedures of the 1980s into courses that could be taught** (Chapter 11, on being made into textbook material: **once an experiment enters the syllabus, it becomes basic training** (**and so the "experiment" of a new generation of writers sets out, by default, from these page numbers**))); **2000–2012** international prizes (**the last ratification:** one person stood for a span of twenty years (**this lesson's reminder at the close: replace "代表" ("represented") with "被选中来代表" ("was chosen to represent"), and the sentence is an honest one**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this lesson
**Treating root-seeking and the avant-garde as two stages, one after the other** (**they belong to the same period, appear in the same journals, and are sympathetic to one another:** what they share is a transfer of subject matter; the difference is the direction of the transfer (**to tell them in order of time makes people take the avant-garde, wrongly, for a "development" of root-seeking** (**more accurately:** **they are two product lines opened by one and the same loosening of the bonds** (the relation, in the previous chapter, between "Ordination" (《受戒》) and reflection literature is the prototype)); **Taking "experiment" for a value** (**what is new is not the same as what is good:** "新" ("new") is a word of time (clause ③ above)); **Reading the writing of the body only as courage, or only as a commodity** (**the three causal lines must be set side by side, and ranked** (**to speak of only one of them yields two opposite distortions: a history of martyrdom, or a history of the market** (**whereas it is both of these at once**)); **Closing this chapter on a single Nobel Prize** (**that would attribute, to one person's genius, what the programme, the editors, the translators and film accomplished together** (Lesson 15: **a prize citation is the most condensed literary history, and also the least accurate**).
:::

::: exercise Change the ledger heading {level=1}
Rewrite, in 100 characters, a passage of "narrative of suffering" that you know. **Political vocabulary, and any wording of victimhood, may not appear; write only one heading under survival** (any one of eating, a craft, the body, or a travel pass).
::: solution
Marking: **the heading must be checkable** (**there is a quantity, a procedure and a price:** this is how "吃" ("eating") is written in *The Chess King* (**if one writes only "他很苦" ("he had a very hard time"), that is a change of words and not of the accounts** (Lesson 1, keeping the accounts other people leave out).
:::

::: exercise Let the narrator show himself {level=2}
Write 120 characters: **in the third sentence, have the narrator admit that he is making it up**. After reading to the end, the reader must be unable to judge whether the events are true or false, and must still want to know the next step.
::: solution
Marking: **"admitting that one is making it up" must have consequences** (**if the plot that follows is still credible just as before, the device is decoration** (**an adequate way of doing it: turn the trust that has been withdrawn into a new suspense — the question now is "他为什么要这样编" ("why he should need to make it up in this way")** (Chapter 3, the teller's credit: **change the object that can be argued over, rather than changing the tone**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the door nobody was defending {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim: "**A formal explosion in literature rarely happens when freedom is at its fullest, and often happens in that narrow window when freedom is only just sufficient, and yet still does not point towards mainstream subject matter. Writers then crowd towards two directions that nobody is defending — moving the subject matter into another time, and moving the narrative onto a new level. Both of these ways of moving can evade risk and can also win reputation, and so they look like the self-awareness of genius, when in fact they are the shape of the channel. But they only go so far as looking like it: because what is written after one has passed through those two doors is not decided by the doors.**" You must call on at least six items: **the four conditions of 1985**, **the three items of content in the root-seeking call for tenders**, **the borrowed mirror**, **the grammatical meaning of metanarrative**, **the three consequences of film as intermediary**, **the ranking by refutability of the four causal lines**; and you must adjudicate **where "channel determinism" (form comes from the channel) and "genius determinism" (form comes from the individual) each fail when explaining this period — for each, one phenomenon must be given that only that theory can explain, and that the other cannot**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**the window, the call for tenders, the mirror, metanarrative, the intermediary, the ranking**); the adjudication (**where the channel theory fails:** inside the same channel the technical gap between works is very large (**on the same periodical there are works that can travel and works that cannot**); **where the genius theory fails:** these people appeared almost at the same time, and faded almost at the same time, and the time of the fading matches the change in the channels (**marking looks at whether a division of labour is given for "what each of them explains":** **the channel explains why it is these two paths; genius explains why it is these two people** (**if the conclusion can be written as "therefore the correct way to narrate this period is double-entry bookkeeping: the upper level records the channel (the quota, the periodicals, film, translation and introduction), and the lower level records the procedure (point of view, time, register, the sentence); explaining on either level alone will write the literature of an era as the history of its distribution, or as its roll of famous names"**, that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The two doors, filed: the window (**four conditions peaked in the same year**), the call for tenders (**national character is a commodity one can compete over, and it is also a client no one can point to**), the mirror (**recognising, inside a foreign model, the corners of one's own house**), the device (**the narrator's showing himself dismisses the concept of truth**), the intermediary (**the screen supplied the first mass readers, and a second kind of reputation**), the ending (**four causal lines, which must be ranked; ④ must not be the only one kept**). The method in one sentence: **draw the channel first, then read the sentence; the channel explains why this path exists, and the sentence explains who went far**.
