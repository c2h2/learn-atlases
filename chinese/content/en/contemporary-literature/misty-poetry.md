title: "Misty poetry"
---

The writers of the previous chapter negotiated inside a quota; the group in this chapter changed the battlefield — **they no longer asked what they could write, and asked instead how to write; and so "cannot make it out" ceased to be an aesthetic complaint and became a political charge: because, in this dispute, clarity equals loyalty**.

## Definition: the victory of an insult {#def-mingming}

::: definition The origin of the word 朦胧 {#def-mingci}
**Received account** (the word 朦胧 ('misty'; also 'obscure') first appears in 1980, in a letter from the author of a poem, and is used to rebuke certain new poetry as too obscure and too hard to understand; in 1983 an article assessing three poems drove the debate to its height — **three steps are worth remembering:** ① a pejorative expression appears; ② the other side accepts it and uses it as a label; ③ the label becomes the title of a chapter in literary history (a return to "the name is sovereignty" in Lesson 3: **a nickname given to an opponent is the cheapest trademark transfer in literary history**); **why "clarity" was politicised** (**received account: both sides of the debate took it as given that poetry ought to "be understood by the broad mass of readers" —** and so a measurable index (how many people understand it) was treated as a moral index (which side one stands on) (**the methodological core of this chapter:** once degree of clarity becomes the credential of loyalty, the poet has only two choices left: **obey by speaking clearly, or disobey by complicating the structure** (**the latter is the formal origin of Misty Poetry (Obscure Poetry): obscurity is not a preference of style; it is the only veto**).
:::

::: definition Three events {#def-shijian}
**_Today_ (《今天》) (1978–1980, received account)** (**an unofficial mimeographed journal:** its significance lies not in what it carried, but in its having no approval from an editorial board (**the socialisation of the "drawer" of Chapter 2:** private writing shared one mimeograph machine for the first time (**the birth of a genre is often nothing more than a condition of printing that lets it appear, and keep appearing**)); **anthologies of the _Menglong shixuan_ (《朦胧诗选》) kind, and the public debate (around 1980, received account)** (**an anthology tied works scattered here and there into "a generation":** and so "Bei Dao 北岛, Shu Ting 舒婷, Gu Cheng 顾城, Jiang He 江河, Yang Lian 杨炼" became a unit that could be cited and could be opposed (the "four masters named together" clause of Chapter 1: **the camp is made by critics, the works are written by poets, and the opponents are the beneficiaries of both**)); **the slogan of the "three everythings" (received account: in the early 1980s, summaries of the kind "思考一切、怀疑一切、否定一切" ('think about everything, doubt everything, negate everything') were cited over and over, and their original source has passed through many retellings) —** this lesson uses it only as a specimen of the smell of the age: **it proves that the real object of the dispute then was not rhetoric, but "whether the individual has the right to judge before the collective does"** (the fourth use of "we" in Chapter 3 is cancelled here: **"I" is once again the subject of the sentence**.
:::

## Texts {#def-wenben}

::: definition Bei Dao: the manifesto as poem {#def-beidao}
“**卑鄙是卑鄙者的通行证，高尚是高尚者的墓志铭。**” (‘**Baseness is the pass of the base; nobility is the epitaph of the noble.**’ (Our translation.)) (commonly cited from ‘The Answer’ (《回答》)) — **this is a line of verse that can be quoted on its own, and to be quoted on its own is its function** (**the technique of the manifesto-poem:** write a judgement as a couplet, and then it can travel free of the whole poem (Chapter 6: the slogan is a compressed biscuit: **the sharpest thing about Misty Poetry is not that it is obscure; it is that it also produces the most condensed sentences one can quote** (**its readers are therefore of two kinds, those who can recite it and those who cannot make it out, and the number of the former decides its fame**)); negative sentence-forms of the kind “**我—不—答—应**” (‘**I—won't—a—gree**’ (Our translation.)) and closures of the kind “新的转机上星星闪烁” (‘stars glitter on the new turn’ (Our translation.)) (the gist of the commonly cited lines) — **this lesson calls this structure the twentieth-century version of "a judgement that refuses to be issued"** (the *Peach Blossom Fan* clause of Chapter 7): **it offers no alternative; it only withdraws recognition** (**this is exactly why, in the 1980s, it was read as a "spirit of doubt": a negation that offers no programme can neither be refuted nor be put into effect — **this is its strength, and also the reason it was criticised as "empty"**).
:::

::: definition Gu Cheng: the fairy tale as testimony {#def-gucheng}
“**黑夜给了我黑色的眼睛，我却用它寻找光明。**” (‘**The black night gave me black eyes, yet with them I seek the light.**’ (Our translation.)) (commonly cited from ‘A Generation’ (《一代人》)) — **the mechanics of two lines:** it writes trauma as a remaking of the senses (**not "I suffered", but "my organs have been refitted"** (the lyric version of "constraint grows an organ" in Chapter 8: **with the public archive absent, this generation's only credential is its own body**); **why it has only four lines** (**received account: short poems of this period were often the first to be taken into anthologies — **the irrefutability of the short poem:** it asserts no causation, and so it has no link that can be falsified (the ways-of-dying test of Lesson 2: **the three-word combination “黑夜—眼睛—光明” ('black night — eyes — light') can be neither confirmed nor falsified:** and so it became one of the most cited sentences of the 1980s: **the works with the highest rate of spread are often precisely the works with the lowest refutability** (**this sentence is not said in dispraise; it belongs to the study of distribution**.
:::

::: definition Shu Ting: writing "we" back into love {#def-shuting}
“**我如果爱你，绝不像攀援的凌霄花，借你的高枝炫耀自己。**” (‘**If I love you, I will never be like the climbing trumpet creeper, borrowing your high branches to flaunt myself.**’ (Our translation.)) and lines of that kind (commonly cited from ‘To an Oak’ (《致橡树》)) — **note what it is doing:** it does not negate the collective; it only asks for a union of equals (the twentieth-century sequel to Liniang 丽娘 and her “我情愿” ('I am willing') in Chapter 2: **Tang Xianzu 汤显祖 made feeling into a ground one could plead; Shu Ting wrote that pleadable form as a partnership** (**antithesis of the kind “根，紧握在地下；叶，相触在云里” ('Roots, gripping tight underground; leaves, touching in the clouds.' (Our translation.)) makes equality into grammar** (**this is why it was accepted by the public earlier than many works of the same years:** it attacked no dogma; it only changed the contract**.
:::

::: definition Yang Lian and the turn to the "cultural epic" {#def-yanglian}
**Received account** (in the early 1980s some poets turned to the long poem and to subjects drawn from civilisation (poetic sequences of the kind of *Lihun* (《礼魂》, 'rites for the soul')) — **this lesson takes this turn, and I read it as one "flight of subject matter abroad":** the quota and the debate had written personal lyric into a high-risk zone, and so "culture" became a third party at whom no one can point (the "programme" clause of Chapter 11: **when real-life subject matter is under a quota, the author brings the whole Bronze Age in as the client** (**what it gains:** poetry recovers bulk, and a sense of rite; **what it loses:** abstract nouns replace concrete people, and the reader loses the way in — **this laid the red carpet for "root-seeking" in the next chapter:** when poetry goes looking for the roots of culture, fiction will go looking too**.
:::

## The structure of the debate {#def-lunzheng}

::: definition A lawsuit that cannot be judged {#def-guansi}
**The original proposition** (**received account: new poetry was "too obscure", had "departed from the national tradition", "was misty" —** this is an aesthetic judgement; **the counter-proposition** (**received account: "poetry cannot require that everyone understand it at a glance", "the ambiguity of poetry has a tradition" —** this too is an aesthetic judgement; **the real issue** (**what appears again and again in the received account is, however, two other questions:** who has the right to represent the reader? who has the right to define "tradition"? (**both sides thought they were talking about rhetoric; in fact they were talking about the right to adjudicate** (a perfect case of "the right to define is the right to allocate" in Lesson 2: **no aesthetic argument in this debate could prevail, because from the beginning it was a dispute over qualification** (**this is why it went ten years without being settled, and in the end closed with "we argue no more":** the judgement was carried out by a replacement of generations (**a new generation was elected editors and professors, and so "Misty Poetry" entered the textbooks, and the opponents became "the readers of that time"**).
:::

::: definition The modernist-school debate: a second hearing {#def-xiandai}
**The remarks and the criticism of 1986** (**received account: the minutes of a symposium on the "modernist school" were published, and the speakers described the situation of the Chinese modernist school with "loss of roots" and "transplantation"; official literary journals then organised a discussion by a call for essays, and "may one write like this" became the question — **please take the two debates in order:** what the Misty Poetry debate disputed was "can one understand it?"; what the modernist-school debate disputed was "ought one to learn from abroad?" (**neither was a debate inside literature:** the first disputed the right to adjudicate, the second disputed cultural sovereignty — **and the effect they shared was this:** they turned the question of "how to write" into an offence one had to answer for — **and so, from then on, formal experiment in literature in Chinese has carried a permanent posture of answering a charge** (Chapter 6, the concession put first: **in the syntax of China's avant-garde literature there is, for a whole lifetime, a critic standing on the other side**).
:::

::: example A ranking by refutability {#ex-kefbo}
Rank the following four sentences (all of them commonly cited lines, or rewordings of them) by "**the degree to which they can be refuted**", from low to high, and explain: **why this ranking is roughly the reverse of their degree of spread**.
① 卑鄙是卑鄙者的通行证，高尚是高尚者的墓志铭 (‘Baseness is the pass of the base; nobility is the epitaph of the noble.’ (Our translation.))
② 黑夜给了我黑色的眼睛，我却用它寻找光明 (‘The black night gave me black eyes, yet with them I seek the light.’ (Our translation.))
③ 我如果爱你，绝不像攀援的凌霄花 (‘If I love you, I will never be like the climbing trumpet creeper.’ (Our translation.))
④ 在没有英雄的年代里，我只想做一个人（通说引句大意） (‘In an age without heroes, I want only to be a person.’ (Our translation.) (gist of the commonly cited lines))
::: solution
**From low to high (for reference)** — **② is the lowest** (**a combination of three words, and no causation: it cannot be falsified**); **① is the next lowest** (**a maxim-like universal judgement, but it contains a moral distribution one can test:** a single counter-example is enough to weaken it (“卑鄙者也有不通行的时候” ('there are times when the base, too, are not let through'))**); **④ is in the middle** (**it asserts a ranking of values, and one may press it on "what 'being a person' and 'being a hero' mean"; and "an age without heroes" is a historical statement one can dispute**); **③ is the highest** (**it proposes concrete terms of a contract of love, and so it can be refuted: “紧握在地下” ('gripping tight underground') may also mean that the two trees are felled together** (**once a plan is proposed, a plan that can be attacked has been proposed**); **why this runs roughly opposite to the degree of spread** (**to be quoted, a sentence needs irrefutability:** a refutable sentence draws discussion; an irrefutable sentence draws only an echo (the "refutability" clause of Lesson 3, and a mirror application of the ways-of-dying test of Lesson 6: **in literature, degree of spread ≈ 1 / degree of refutability × intensity of feeling** (**this formula is not a law; it is a rough description of a mechanism of selection — **it explains at once the slogans of the previous chapter and the famous lines of this one: the slogan and the famous line are two subspecies of the same species**).
:::
:::

::: intuition Form is the last technical means {#int-xingshi}
**A transferable law of explanation** (**when content is censored, subject matter is held to a quota, and evaluation is politicised, innovation moves across to the level of form: syntax, image (_yixiang_), punctuation, length, and whether or not there is quotation** (the same pattern as "*shiyu*" ('the poem's remainder') in Chapter 4 and "the programme is the equipment" in Chapter 11: **this is not an aesthetic preference; it is a feasibility analysis** (**evidence one can check:** the formal features in this chapter (no subject, rupture, synaesthesia, cultural images) are almost the same as the techniques of encryption in the underground writing of the previous chapter — **they did not come from France; they came from the drawer. Only after the mid-1980s were translations brought in, recognised after the fact, as an "external ground"** (Chapter 8: the object of an influence is always the most recent textbook: **this generation had two sets of textbooks, one the translations, and one the ten years in which one was not allowed to speak**).
:::

::: history Twenty years of citation {#sec-yinyong}
**1978–1980,** *Today* (**mimeograph:** a literature's minimum viable product**); **1980–1983,** naming and debate (**how a pejorative, inside three years, became the title of a chapter in a course**); **1986,** the modernist-school debate (**a second hearing:** the issue changed from readability to sovereignty**); **after 1989,** the "de-publicisation" of poetry (**received account: poetry stepped down from the centre of public debate and moved into unofficial journals, coterie writing and academic research — **this is not a failure; it is one more relocation of the system of distribution:** poetry became again what it had been after the Song, a subject of high repute and low cash (the closed loop of "the poem's remainder = a duty-free zone" in Chapter 4: **the "central genre" in the history of modern literature has never been a ranking by literary nature; it is a ranking by channel**)); **the 1990s to the present,** anthologies, textbooks, writing in Chinese that has won the Nobel Prize, and the public talk of "Bei Dao nominated year after year" (**the last step of a classic is to be quoted and no longer read** (Lesson 3: turning into a set phrase = the rate of circulation rises, and the meaning returns to zero: **the famous line detaches from the whole poem, and the poet detaches from the work** (**this chapter's closing reminder:** what university students today know of Misty Poetry is, for the most part, those three lines one can recite; **they are not to blame — **those three lines were written, in the first place, so that they could be recited**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this lesson
**Treating 朦胧 ('misty') as a style** (**it is a weapon inside a dialogue:** to take it as a category of poetics is to build a taxonomy out of an opponent's jeer (Lesson 1: a list is a pair of spectacles: **a better classification is by channel of publication and by features of syntax, not by the insult**)); **attributing the influence only to foreign countries** (**translations do matter, but the immediate cause of the form is the constraint on what can be written:** both causal chains have to be given (Chapter 8, independent sources: **influence research with only one source is a catalogue of translations**)); **passing over it in one phrase as "awakening / enlightenment"** (**this is language that a later age has recognised after the fact** (Lesson 15: **it presses the different choices of a group of people (the manifesto, the fairy tale, the contract of love, the cultural epic, flight abroad) into a single motive** (**this chapter's way:** five strategies correspond to five hypotheses about the reader, and not to one spirit of the age**)); **ignoring the social function of the debate** (**this debate made poetry a public topic again:** being criticised is a kind of print run (Chapter 1, "a list of banned books = a list of recommendations": **without those few articles of criticism, many of the poems would not have been read by hundreds of thousands of people within a year**).
:::

::: exercise The lifespan of an insult {level=1}
From a field you yourself work in, give a term (in any language) that went from a pejorative nickname to an official name, and in 80 characters set out clearly: **who said it first, why the other side accepted it, and what was lost after it was accepted**.
::: solution
Marking: **"what was lost" is the key column** (**a common answer: what was lost was the obligation to answer the original charge — **once the nickname has become a noun of the discipline, the pejorative it carries is institutionally forgotten** (Lesson 3, "the name is sovereignty": **this is why the title of this chapter is written "the victory of an insult", and not "Misty Poetry"**.
:::

Write a maxim-like couplet of no more than 20 characters, and it must be **quotable on its own**; then write one line on **what its irrefutability rests on**, and one further line on **which one person could pull it apart with a single sentence**.

Marking: **the sentence that pulls it apart must really be able to pull it apart** (**give a counter-example, or give the hidden premise:** writing "it is too absolute" scores nothing (the ways-of-dying test of Lesson 2**); **the aim of this question is to let the student feel the asymmetry in this chapter:** it is easier to write a lie that can travel than a truth that cannot (the mirror of the "false profundity" entry in Chapter 6**).

::: exercise Short essay: the causal relations of form {level=3}
Write between six hundred and a thousand characters, assessing "**the formal features of an age are often not learnt from the foreign exemplars it has read, but grow out of what it cannot say: and so the right question, in studying form, is not 'whom did they imitate?', but 'what was forbidden, and what did that make possible?'; yet this is by no means to say that form is a product of censorship — otherwise every technique becomes a footnote to politics, and once technique has become a footnote to politics, criticism never has to read a sentence again**". You must call on at least six items: **the three steps of the insult**, **clarity as the credential of loyalty**, **the minimum viable conditions of the mimeographed copy**, **the body as credential (Gu Cheng)**, **the contract of love (Shu Ting)**, **the flight of subject matter abroad (the cultural epic)**; and you must rule on **which of the two explanations, "form comes from constraint" and "form comes from foreign influence", is the easier to overuse, and why — and for each you must give a sentence showing how you would misuse it**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**naming, loyalty, channel, credential, contract, flight abroad**); the ruling (**for reference: both are liable to be overused, but the consequences of the misuse differ — **overusing the "foreign-origin account" writes itself out as a catalogue of translations (**its typical sentence of misuse: "the achievement of this group of poets was to import modernism"**); overusing the "constraint account" reads every technique as resistance, and cancels the whole of the author's responsibility for the craft (**its typical sentence of misuse: "this rupture is the direct result of censorship"**); the marking looks at whether the student can point out **the mistake the two share: explaining an observable technical choice by a single cause** (the algorithm clause of Chapter 3: **the better question is this: under the given constraints, and under the given conditions of reading, what extra did this way of writing secure, beyond other ways of writing; and then ask what it therefore gave up** (**if the conclusion can be written thus, "**form is neither the mirror-image of a prohibition, nor a letter from abroad; it is an engineering plan: the material is the prohibition, the drawing is the exemplar, and the one who accepts the work is the only new reader to appear in those ten years — a person willing to read it to the end even without understanding**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The battlefield, filed: naming (**pejorative → label → title of a chapter**), channel (**the mimeograph = the minimum viable product**), credential (**the black night gave the eyes: the body stands in for the archive**), contract (**Shu Ting rewrites the grammar of equality**), flight abroad (**culture as a third party at whom no one can point**), judgement (**a replacement of generations in place of an aesthetic conclusion**). The method in one sentence: **first ask what extra this way of writing secured, then ask what it gave up**.
