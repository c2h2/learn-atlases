---
title: "Exposition"
---

Expository writing is the **public service** of writing: it does not seek to move the reader, but to say the thing clearly — to explain a thing, a procedure or a concept so that a **reader with no foundation** can understand it. The professional ethic of this craft has only one article: **do not assume the reader understands it as you do**.

## Three neighbours, one boundary map {#sec-bianjie}

::: definition Exposition's household registration {#def-huji}
The three brother-modes of expression are often made to share a house: **description** wants the reader to **feel** ("月色如积水空明", 'moonlight like a sheet of clear standing water'), **exposition** wants the reader to **know** ("灯箱由面板、光源、底座三部分构成", 'a light box is made of three parts: a panel, a light source and a base' (Our translation.)), **argument** wants the reader to **believe** ("节能灯优于白炽灯，因为……", 'an energy-saving lamp is better than an incandescent lamp, because…' (Our translation.)). The test, in one sentence: **delete it — is what is lost atmosphere (description), information (exposition), or a stance (argument)?** — The commonest genre-crash in the examination hall is expression of feeling slipped into an expository piece ("啊，笔袋，你默默无闻奉献一生", 'ah, pencil case, you have given a whole life without recognition' (Our translation.) — **to personify an exposition is a lazy shortcut, not vividness**), and turning "介绍《海底两万里》" ('introduce *Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea*') into a retelling of the plot plus impressions after reading (**an account of a book must know that it is an account: author, setup, structure, edition, how to read it; a plot summary is only one cell of that**). Two subspecies of exposition: **exposition of things** (construction, function, process — manuals, recipes, notes on a map) and **exposition of principles** (concepts, principles, relations — why the mobile signal is poor on a high-speed train). They share only one measure: **the reader's retelling accuracy** — following your text, can the reader assemble that thing, or retell that principle?
:::

::: definition Four kinds of order and six tools {def-shunxu}
**Choose the order by the skeleton of the object**: time (a process of making, a history of change), space (a building, a map — "由南向北", 'from south to north', "自下而上", 'from the bottom upwards'; **words of direction are the traffic lights of exposition**), logic (from the outside to the inside, from cause to effect, from the chief to the secondary — the default gear for concepts), procedure (the steps of an operation — they may be numbered, they may be shown in a diagram, **and skipping a step is forbidden**). **The six-piece kit of methods**: **defining** (the concept defined = specific difference + genus concept — "正方形是四边相等且四角为直角的四边形", 'a square is a quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles' (Our translation.); **a definition must not be circular, must not be negative, and must not be a figure of speech**: "好奇心是一种饥饿", 'curiosity is a kind of hunger' (Our translation.), is description, not a definition); **classifying** ("图书按用途分为…", 'books are divided by use into…' — **the subclasses added together must equal the parent class, and only one criterion of classification is used at a time** — mixing a division by 开本 ('format', the size of the page) with a division by content = the crash site of taxonomy); **giving examples** (the visa on arrival of an abstract principle); **listing figures** (**figures must carry the courtesy of an approximation** — "约" ('about'), "上下" ('or so'), "左右" ('more or less'): writing a measured value as an exact value, false precision is less honest than vagueness); **comparing** (a new thing hitched to old experience — "这个水库相当于两万个标准泳池", 'this reservoir is the equivalent of twenty thousand standard swimming pools' (Our translation.)); **drawing a likeness** (**in an exposition a likeness is scaffolding**: it helps the reader over the first wall, and is taken down once the reader is over — a likeness does not count as evidence).
:::

::: example Rewriting an exposition: 'How to tie a shoelace' {#ex-xiedai}
Write, for new pupils in the third grade, a short exposition of "如何系鞋带" ('how to tie a shoelace'). Below is a first draft. Rewrite it by the three checks "顺序、可操作、零先验" ('order, operable, no prior knowledge'), and list the steps the draft skips:
“先把鞋带交叉拉紧，然后绕一圈，打个结，再抽出一个耳朵就好了，很简单的。”
'First cross the shoelaces and pull them tight, then loop them once, tie a knot, pull one ear out, and that is all; it is very simple.' (Our translation.)
::: solution
The three checks convict it. **Order** ("绕一圈", 'loop once' — after what state does this happen? The **preconditions** between the steps are entirely missing). **Operable** ("拉紧", 'pull tight' — in which direction, and with how many hands? Is "打个结", 'tie a knot', a single knot or a double knot?). **No prior knowledge** ("耳朵", 'ear', is the jargon of the trade — **for someone who does not know, every noun must be dispatched on the spot**; "很简单", 'very simple', is a privileged statement of the author, and **it sets the cost of the reader's difficulty at zero** — words of the forbidden class, "很简单" ('very simple') and "不言而喻" ('it goes without saying'), are banned). The list of skipped steps: the direction of the cross (the left lace pressing on the right, or the right on the left — **the finished article comes out different**); which way the lace is passed through (upwards or downwards decides whether the next step can stand at all); after the loop, which end goes in where and comes out where; how the two loops of the slip knot pass through each other; the direction of the tightening pull (pull the two loops on the diagonal, and one lift of a lace undoes it — **the draft's "抽出一个耳朵", 'pull one ear out', is exactly the cause of the knot's coming undone**). A model rewrite (an extract): “①两手持绳头，左右交叉，**左绳从下往上**穿过右绳形成的圈，两手同时向**斜上方**拉直——此时鞋面上是一个单结。②捏住左绳对折，形成一个环，食指顶住环根部……” — '① Hold a lace-end in each hand and cross them left and right; pass the **left lace from below upwards** through the loop formed by the right lace, and with both hands at once pull straight towards the **diagonally upward** side — there is now a single knot on the upper of the shoe. ② Pinch the left lace, fold it double to form a loop, and brace the root of the loop with the index finger…' (Our translation.) — **every step begins with a verb, one step does only one action, and words of direction are not left out**. The gold standard for marking: someone who has never tied a shoelace follows it (or imagines following it) and **asks no question** — **every "然后呢" ('and then?') the reader puts forward is a debt the author owes for the next step**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The courtesy of exposition {#int-lingmao}
Expository writing is **the humblest mode**. Its moral sense hides in the details. ① **Explain the term on the spot** (at the first appearance a plain equivalent must be given — "地幔（地球中间那层石头，像煮得半熟的鸡蛋的蛋清）", 'the mantle (that layer of stone in the middle of the Earth, like the white of an egg boiled until half done)'). ② **Figures carry body heat** (after "高 8848.86 米", '8,848.86 metres high', add a sentence "相当于从头楼顶到大楼底再爬两千八百多层", 'the equivalent of climbing from the roof of the building down to its foot, and then more than another two thousand eight hundred storeys' (Our translation.) — **a figure must give the body something to feel**). ③ **Uncertainty must turn itself in** ("约", 'about'; "目前所知", 'so far as is at present known'; "尚无定论", 'there is as yet no settled conclusion' — **honest vagueness is better than a pretence of precision**; this runs straight through to the ethics of popular science; see the tradition of leaving a doubt on record, of the [[modern-vocabulary/dictionaries]] kind). ④ **Design for the fool, and have the expert check** (the portrait of the reader comes first: writing for classmates and writing for the judges are two different manuals — **exposition has no "大众" ('general public') reader; it has only one kind of person**). Summed up in cold humour: writing that expresses feeling fears a reader who is unmoved; expository writing fears a reader who **asks "然后呢" ('and then?')** — and both fears are the right ones.
:::

::: history From the *Kaogong ji* to the expository lesson {#sec-kecheng}
China has long had a strong tradition of exposition: **the *Kaogong ji* 考工记 ('The Artificers' Record')** (a pre-Qin technical text of the handicrafts — "凡攻木之工七，攻金之工六", 'Of the craftsmen who work wood there are seven trades; of the craftsmen who work metal, six.' (Our translation.): classification, procedure and standards are all there, **a national-standard manual from two thousand years ago**); **the *Qimin yaoshu* 齐民要术 ('Essential Techniques for the Common People')** (the model of procedural exposition in farming: ploughing and the gathering of seed each have their season, and the entries are like code); **the *Tiangong kaiwu* 天工开物 ('The Work of Heaven and the Opening of Things')** (the Ming — an exposition of the whole process of "巧人巧器", 'ingenious people and ingenious devices', giving equal weight to illustrations and to data, and translated and introduced overseas); **the notebook tradition** (*biji*; the *Mengxi bitan* 梦溪笔谈 ('Brush Talks from Dream Brook') explains movable-type printing — **Bi Sheng's 毕昇 invention has come down wholly through Shen Kuo's 沈括 expository writing**: one passage of writing preserved one technique, the archival merit of exposition); **the modern period**: "说明文" ('expository writing') was established as a genre for the classroom together with textbooks in the vernacular (*baihua*) (in step with the writing of practical prose: **exposition is a demand for a kind of writing brought forth by modernisation and industrialisation** — manuals, compacts and encyclopaedias all need **people who can write clearly**); **from the 1950s** exposition entered the sequence of the textbooks (lessons of the kind 'The Stone Arch Bridges of China' (《中国石拱桥》) and 'A Record of the Peach-stone Boat' (《核舟记》) became, for several generations, the initiation into "说明" ('exposition') — **the model expositions Ye Shengtao 叶圣陶 wrote for the textbooks** remained in the selection for a long time, and the author and the genre made each other); **the present**: the gaokao (the university entrance examination) for a time examined texts of a practical kind as a hot topic (charts, manuals, letters of invitation — **the merging of "应用文" ('practical writing') and "说明文" ('expository writing')**), and afterwards, inside "任务驱动型作文" ('task-driven composition'), it continued to appear under the names of **a speech script, a letter and a broadcast script**: **exposition has never left the examination hall; it has only changed its work clothes**.
:::

::: warning Four great taboos of exposition
One, **a circular definition** (explaining "爱好" ('a hobby') by "兴趣" ('an interest'), then explaining "兴趣" by "爱好") — **a definition must set its foot on ground that is already known**; two, **a drifting criterion** ("书分为中国的、外国的、古代的、平装的", 'books are divided into Chinese, foreign, ancient and paperback' (Our translation.) — **one criterion of classification at a time**; mixing them = tipping three drawers into one basket); three, **personification that plays cute** ("小水滴周游世界", 'the little water-drop tours the world' (Our translation.), from one end of the piece to the other — **a likeness is scaffolding, not the main structure**; a figure of speech cannot stand in for a mechanism, and once the reader has finished laughing they have remembered nothing); four, **data left bare** (a string of figures stuck on with no account of the order of magnitude, the unit or the source — **a figure that is not translated might as well not have been said**; a figure whose source is in doubt is better not used: **the credit of expository writing is its only asset**).
:::

::: exercise Writing with no prior knowledge {level=1}
Write an exposition of 150 characters: ① "什么是月食" ('what a lunar eclipse is') (audience: the second grade of primary school); ② "如何折叠并封装一封信" ('how to fold a letter and seal it') (audience: someone who has never seen an envelope). Requirements: steps that begin with a verb, no term left unexplained, and at the end ask yourself "读者会问哪个‘然后呢’" ('which "然后呢" ('and then?') will the reader ask?') and supply it.
::: solution
Marking: ① must deal with "影子从哪来" ('where the shadow comes from') (first explain that a shadow = light being blocked — **the principle comes first; only then has the phenomenon a place to hang**); ② must have "开口朝哪" ('which way the opening faces') and "胶水还是水" ('glue, or water?') (a self-adhesive strip has to be explained: "撕开的那条纸皮下面有胶", 'under the strip of paper backing you tear off, there is glue' (Our translation.) — **this is what no prior knowledge means**); each patch for a "然后呢" ('and then?') is +1 mark, and if "很简单" ('very simple') appears the marks are taken straight back.
:::
:::

::: exercise Classification surgery {level=2}
Write one classificatory exposition of the school clubs: invent your own list of the twelve clubs there now are, divide them by a **single criterion** into two classes and three grades, write it as an exposition of 120 characters, and attach one sentence: "我放弃了另一种分法，因为……" ('I gave up another way of dividing them, because…' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
Marking: the subclasses added together = the parent class (nothing left out, nothing overlapping); the criterion is the only one, and it **serves the purpose of the exposition** (dividing by "是否要上台表演" ('whether they have to go on stage and perform') carries more information than dividing by the first letter of the name — **classification is for solving the reader's problem, not for looking tidy**); the "放弃说明" ('explanation of what was given up') shows metacognition (another criterion also holds, but it serves a different question — classification is a point of view, not the truth).
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: clarity is a kind of morality {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters. Someone says "把东西写清楚只是技术问题，把东西写美才是文学问题" ('Writing a thing clearly is only a technical problem; writing a thing beautifully is what makes it a literary problem.' (Our translation.)). Comment. Requirements: ① by way of one passage of classic exposition ('The Stone Arch Bridges of China' (《中国石拱桥》), 'A Record of the Peach-stone Boat' (《核舟记》), or the case of a manual), analyse "清楚的技艺" ('the craft of being clear'); ② in connection with **the information age** (rumours, copy for hawking goods, expositions generated by AI), discuss how "清楚" ('clarity') and "诚实" ('honesty') are bound together; ③ give your conclusion: are clarity and beauty opposites?
::: solution
Points for marking: able to unpack the craft contained in "清楚" ('clarity') (choosing the order = judgement, classification = a world-view, translating the figures = empathy); examples from the information age may be used ("科学配方" ('a scientific formula') with no data = dishonest; the fluency of a rumour is counter-evidence that **fluency is not a near-synonym of clarity**); the counter-thesis of the conclusion: clarity and beauty have the same source — **two forms of having the reader in mind**; anyone who closes by quoting, in the manner of Ye Shengtao, "写东西是给人看的" ('What one writes is for people to read.' (Our translation.)) may receive full marks.
:::

::: summary
Four items on the exposition workshop's inspection before a piece leaves: **household registration** (the reader is to know, not to be moved), **skeleton** (time/space/logic/procedure; words of direction are the traffic lights), **the six tools** (a definition is not circular, classification uses a single criterion, figures carry body heat, and a likeness is scaffolding and is taken down once it has been used), **courtesy** (terms explained on the spot, uncertainty turning itself in, the portrait of the reader coming first). The measure, as the piece goes out, is a single line: **the reader's retelling accuracy**. The next chapter tidies up punctuation and faulty language — the hygiene regulations of language.
:::
