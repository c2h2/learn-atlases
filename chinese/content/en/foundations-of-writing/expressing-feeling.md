---
title: "Expressing feeling"
---

Expressing feeling is the deposit business of writing: only as much of being moved as the perceptible detail saved up earlier may one dare to withdraw here. The list of negative examples in this chapter is long — essays that open with “啊！” ('Ah!'), slogans at the ending, class meetings in which everyone is moved — **the right way to write feeling is to let the reader be moved of their own accord**.

## Two channels, three pawnshops {#sec-qudao}

::: definition Direct and indirect {#def-qudao}
Expressing feeling has two channels: **direct expression of feeling** (“我很想它” ('I miss it very much.') (Our translation.) — that Chinese parasol tree which was cut down — in the manner of a shout: short, fierce, costly: **one place is worth thirty characters at most; any more, and it goes hollow**) and **indirect expression of feeling** (the feeling is lodged somewhere else, in three pawnshops): **expressing feeling through a scene** (“念天地之悠悠，独怆然而涕下” ('Pondering the vastness of heaven and earth, alone, in grief, I shed tears.') (Our translation.) — heaven and earth are the wholesaler of scene; the modern version: the stove in which no fire has been lit again since Grandmother died — **the moment the scene speaks, the tears are saved**); **expressing feeling through an object** (the whole piece lodged in one thing: the white poplar, the plum blossom, the kite — the object is the feeling's **real property**, and one flat to an essay is enough); **expressing feeling through narration** (**the channel with the highest interest**: *Xiangji xuan zhi* 项脊轩志 ('Record of the Xiangji Studio'), “庭有枇杷树，吾妻死之年所手植也，今已亭亭如盖矣” ('In the courtyard there is a loquat tree, planted by my wife's own hand in the year she died; now it already stands tall and spreading, like a canopy.') (Our translation.) — zero adjectives, zero exclamation marks, and time itself walks the whole course of the sorrow; the buying of the oranges in *Beiying* 背影 ('A view of his back') is of the same kind: **once the event is concrete, the feeling moves of itself**). The order of teaching, and the instinct of the examination, often take direct expression for a synonym of “感情真挚” ('sincere feeling') — **sincerity is not high decibels**. Loud is the loudspeaker; deep is the gramophone.
:::

::: example Case consultation: the ending of 《再见了，母校》 ('Farewell, My Old School') {#ex-muxiao}
The original: “六年过去了，我即将离开母校。啊，母校！您是我的摇篮，我的灯塔，我的第二个家！我一定会永远永远怀念您的！母校，我爱你！” ('Six years have passed, and I am about to leave my old school. Ah, my old school! You are my cradle, my lighthouse, my second home! I shall certainly cherish the memory of you for ever and ever! My old school, I love you!') (Our translation.) Please consult on the case (three diseases), and perform one operation of the “降分贝升当量” ('lower the decibels and raise the yield') kind.
::: solution
The consultation: **Disease one · parallelism inflation** (摇篮, 灯塔, 家 — 'cradle', 'lighthouse', 'home' — three expired metaphors fired off in a string, each diluting the next; **metaphors in a row are like prices called one after another; at the third call, no one believes**); **Disease two · addiction to calling out** (“啊，母校！” ('Ah, my old school!') (Our translation.) “母校，我爱你！” ('My old school, I love you!') (Our translation.) — direct expression overdrawn; love that is shouted out is an IOU, and only love that is carried out is a deposit); **Disease three · the hollow promise** (“永远永远怀念” ('cherish the memory for ever and ever') — six years of feeling settled in six characters; **what the stacking of adverbs multiplies is not the feeling but the want of confidence**). The plan of the operation: **reopen every pawnshop — delete the three metaphors, the two callings-out, and the one 永远 ('for ever')**; lodge it in **one scene plus one object**: “最后一次锁教室门，我把钥匙在窗台放了很久才推给老师。楼道里那幅中国地图，右上角被我贴过一面小红旗——那节语文课我讲南海。旗还在，我下周就交钥匙了。” ('The last time I locked the classroom door, I set the key on the windowsill and left it a long while before I pushed it across to the teacher. On that map of China in the corridor, I had stuck a little red flag in the upper right corner — that Chinese lesson, I spoke about the South China Sea. The flag is still there, and next week I hand the key in.') (Our translation.) — of the three characters of “我爱你” ('I love you'), not one is there, and the whole passage is those words. The operation is named: **lower the decibels, raise the yield**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Why restraint costs more {#int-le}
The economic explanation: the **scarcity pricing** of feeling. The reader keeps a steelyard in mind — write “我伤心极了” ('I am sad to the utmost.') (Our translation.), and you are **quoting a price**; write “爸爸把我的碗筷也洗了” ('Dad washed my bowl and chopsticks too.') (Our translation.) (the first meal after Mother died; no one in the family spoke), and you are **delivering the goods**. Quote a price too often, and credit goes bankrupt; deliver the goods, and the goods speak for themselves. Aesthetics in China and abroad converge here: China speaks of “**哀而不伤，乐而不淫**” ('Grieving, yet not wounded by it; joyful, yet not carried into excess.') (Our translation.) (the *zhōnghé* aesthetics of the *Analects* 《论语》 — *zhōnghé* 中和, 'balance and harmony' — not that feeling is forbidden, but that feeling must have a vessel); Western modernism speaks of the iceberg and of “**展示，不要告诉**” (show, don't tell — two dialects of one and the same curriculum standard). Three techniques of the vessel: **stop on the action** (draw to a close exactly at the highest point of the emotion, and let the action serve as the full stop: “我把试卷翻到背面，背面是空的” ('I turned the paper over; the back was blank.') (Our translation.)); **let the object speak for you** (“旗还在，我下周就交钥匙了” ('The flag is still there, and next week I hand the key in.') (Our translation.)); **a cap on the numbers** (in one essay, a quota of three exclamation marks, and a quota of one calling-out — **punctuation that has overspent will lie for you**).
:::

::: history Curricular history and the history of forms {#sec-kecheng}
**Classical stock**: poetry expresses intent (《尚书·尧典》, the 'Yaodian' 尧典 ('Canon of Yao') in the *Shangshu* 尚书 ('Book of Documents'): “诗言志，歌永言” ('Poetry expresses intent; song prolongs the words.') (Our translation.) — the birth certificate of the right to express feeling), the rhapsody (*fu*) “体物写志” ('embodying things and writing intent'), the song lyric (*ci*) from its place as the “艳科” ('amorous genre') until Su Shi 苏轼 and Xin Qiji 辛弃疾 **opened their mouths and seized ground from “诗” ('poetry')** (the birth of the *háofàng* school — *háofàng* 豪放, 'bold and unrestrained' — = a reform of the volume of expressed feeling); **the line of literary education**: after the 1950s, the “表达方式四分法” ('fourfold division of the modes of expression') (narration / description / exposition / argument) and the ambiguous standing of “抒情” ('expression of feeling') as **the fifth of the modes** (in some textbooks the division into four includes expression of feeling; in some it is listed on its own — **even within pedagogy they have not come to an agreement**); **the line of composition**: in the 1940s, Feng Zhi 冯至, *Shisihang ji* 十四行集 ('Sonnets'); in the 1980s, “朦胧诗” ('Misty Poetry', also called 'Obscure Poetry') (Bei Dao 北岛, “我不相信” ('I do not believe.') (Our translation.) — **a modern recasting of direct expression: a shout may be high-grade, on condition that every line carries a list of goods**), and Hai Zi 海子, “面朝大海” ('Facing the sea.') (Our translation.) (after 1989 it became a national template for expressing feeling, and writing in imitation of it played it to ruin — **once a rebellion made into a template grows popular, it becomes the new template**); **the classroom as it stands**: training in expressing feeling is concentrated on the stage of **elevation at the ending** (the last kilometre of “由真情到哲理” ('from true feeling to a philosophical point')), and thus is born a new disease of form: **“升华强迫症”** ('elevation compulsion') — even a snail climbing a wall must come to some great truth of life (this chapter maintains: **allow a piece of writing not to elevate, only to finish** — write one spell of longing clearly, and the merit is already complete).
:::

::: warning Three falsities of expressing feeling
One, **borrowed emotion** (writing “每读到这首诗我都泪流满面” ('Every time I read this poem, tears cover my face.') (Our translation.)) — **someone else's work is not an invoice for your feeling**; write what grounds it has, and do not write that you cried. Two, **expressing feeling for a festival** (the assembly line of “妈妈的爱” ('Mother's love') in Mother's Day essays: bringing an umbrella, carrying me on the back to the hospital, milk deep in the night — **public events passing themselves off as private memory**, and not one of Mother's real likings). Three, **fundraising on misery** (grave illness in the family, and poverty at home, fabricated to order in exchange for emotion — **trading misery for marks is begging with feeling**, the same case as “加戏癖” ('the habit of adding drama'); see [[foundations-of-writing/narration]], the honesty of narration). The accomplice of the three falsities: **taking the expression of feeling as the end** — expressing feeling is the interest on narration, not the target sum of a cash machine.
:::

::: exercise The delivery exercise {level=1}
From the following “报价句” ('sentences that quote a price'), choose any three, and rewrite each as a “交货” ('delivery') in no more than 40 characters (only actions, objects and numbers are permitted; nouns of emotion may not appear): ① 我很难过 ('I am very sad.') (Our translation.) ② 我很想爷爷 ('I miss Grandfather very much.') (Our translation.) ③ 我特别感动 ('I am especially moved.') (Our translation.) ④ 我们班都舍不得他。 ('No one in our class can bear to part with him.') (Our translation.)
::: solution
Samples, to show the direction: ② “爷爷的收音机还放在他常坐的椅边，电池早没了，我每周还是把它摆正一次。” ('Grandfather's radio is still set beside the chair where he used to sit; the batteries gave out long ago, and once a week I still set it straight.') (Our translation.) ④ “值日表贴出来，他的名字后面那格没人舍得排新活，一直空到学期末。” ('The duty roster was put up; no one could bear to enter a new task in the space after his name, and it stayed empty until the end of term.') (Our translation.) Marking: **nouns of emotion appear not once**, and the reader can reason back to the feeling; if the reasoning back fails = the delivery is insufficient.
:::
:::

::: exercise The lower-the-decibels operation {level=2}
Find a passage of expressed feeling in an old piece of your own (or a passage of calling-out in a model essay), and perform the same operation as the “母校结尾” ('the old-school ending'): delete the calling-out, delete the expired metaphors, and instead lodge the feeling in **one scene plus one object**; at the end of the piece attach a self-diagnosis of 30 characters: “原稿在喊，新稿在……” ('The original draft is shouting; the new draft is……') (Our translation.)
::: solution
Marking: the new draft **does not contain the three kinds of wording that were deleted** (啊 / 永远 / stock metaphors of the cradle-and-lighthouse kind); the scene must be **concrete enough to photograph** (the time, the place, what is held in the hand); the sentence of self-diagnosis is honest (“在摆证据” ('laying out the evidence'), “在闭嘴” ('shutting up'), both count as right). Paste the original passage and the operated passage side by side, so the scar is easy to inspect.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: expressing feeling and the public template {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: “为什么一到母亲节，全网都在转发同一句话？” ('Why is it that, the moment Mother's Day arrives, the whole internet is forwarding one and the same sentence?') (Our translation.) Taking festival copy, fan-support slogans and graduation posts on Moments (朋友圈) as the material, discuss **the convenience and the tyranny of public templates for expressing feeling**, and give your scheme for a “私人表达保留地” ('a reserve for private expression') (it must be something one can carry out; shouting slogans is not allowed).
::: solution
Points for marking: the mechanism is stated clearly (a template lowers the cost of expression, and at the same time **predetermines what is worth being moved by** — the tyranny lies in the predetermining, not in the convenience); this lesson's pawnshop model can be cited (a public template = futures that no one is there to inspect); the scheme is concrete (for example: whenever you are about to forward something, first write three lines of your own; send Mother **an in-joke that only the two of you understand**, rather than the blessing style). The pessimistic school is allowed: the template swallows everything, but **a private corpus is the last private plot** — whoever can show, on their own evidence, that they hold the title deed is marked A.
:::
:::

::: summary
The regulations of the firm of expressing feeling: **channels** (a quota of thirty characters on direct expression; three pawnshops for the indirect — scene, object, event, and the interest on narration is the highest); **pricing** (a quoted price goes bankrupt, a delivery rises in value — that Dad washed your bowl and chopsticks beats a thousand exclamation marks); **the vessel** (stop on the action, let the object speak for you, a cap on the numbers); **ethics** (allow a piece only to finish, and not to elevate — a snail may simply finish climbing the wall). The next chapter takes up the calmest kind of writing: exposition — to say a thing plainly is a harder courtesy than to say it beautifully.
:::
