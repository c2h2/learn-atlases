title: "Language and punctuation"
---

Faults of language have to be caught by the ear; faults of punctuation have to be cured by rules. This chapter is the writing course's **hygiene regulations**: the four characters 文从字顺 ('coherent in the writing, smooth in the words') are the baseline, and punctuation is the score of tone — once both have been practised into habit, the page speaks for itself.

## Coherent writing, smooth wording: the ear as quality inspector {#sec-wcn}

::: definition Four kinds of roughness and reading aloud {#def-bushun}
“**思想不集中，文章就不通。**” ('**If the thought is not concentrated, the writing will not be coherent.**') (Our translation.) — **writing and thinking are one and the same thing**; the root of a faulty sentence lies in the line of thought, not in a feel for the language. The faulty-sentence hospital and the grammar class triage (for a detailed diagnosis of the pathology of syntax, see [[modern-grammar/faulty-sentences]]), and this chapter takes four frequently seen patients home: **improper collocation** (“记起了过去的往事和同学们的音容笑貌” — 'recalled the past events of the past, and the voices, faces and smiles of one's classmates' (Our translation.) — “想起音容” will do, “想起笑貌” will not — **a coordinate structure must be put through a trial marriage one member at a time**), **redundant constituents** (“大约……左右”, “基本上完全” — 'about … or so', 'basically completely' — **words at both ends squeeze the middle to death**), **unclear reference** (“小明告诉小刚，他获奖了” — 'Xiaoming told Xiaogang that he had won a prize' (Our translation.) — three 他 scramble for one place), **a connective out of position** (when the subjects before and after differ, “不但” must go before the subject: “不但他去了，我也去了” — 'Not only did he go; I went too' (Our translation.); when the subjects are the same, the connective follows the subject: “他不但去了，还唱了两首” — 'Not only did he go; he also sang two pieces' (Our translation.) — one mnemonic: **same subject, after; different subjects, before** — when the subjects before and after differ, “不但” should come before “我” — this one mnemonic is enough). **The quality-check procedure**: when you have finished writing, **read it aloud** (silent reading cheats the ear; reading aloud does not — where the breath falls short is where the sentence is too long); **recheck by reducing the sentence** (pick out the trunk: who—does—what? Do the branches and leaves fit?).
:::

## Punctuation: the score of tone {#sec-biaodian}

::: definition The national standard and the everyday ledger {#def-guobiao}
The national standard **GB/T 15834—2011** *Usage of Punctuation Marks* (its forerunner is the 1995 edition): sixteen kinds, pause marks and indication marks (点号 and 标号), each discharging its own duty. The high-frequency items on the classroom ledger: **the enumeration comma versus the comma** (“我们跑步、跳远、投掷” — 'we run, long-jump and throw' (Our translation.) — **coordinate words and phrases** inside the sentence take the enumeration comma, and **coordinate clauses** take the comma: “我们跑步，我们跳远” — 'we run; we long-jump' (Our translation.) — **between the two “我们” there is nowhere for an enumeration comma to lodge**; once coordinate words and phrases carry a modal particle, change to the comma: “又香呢，又甜呢” — 'fragrant, mind you, and sweet, mind you' (Our translation.)); **book-title marks** (they govern 'works': books, articles, newspapers and periodicals, film and television, songs — “《读者》” is right; an activity, a theme or a column is not a work, so use quotation marks and not book-title marks: “读书节” opens, and do not write it as 《读书节》); **quotation marks and the point** (when the quotation stands on its own, the sentence-final point goes inside the quotation marks; when the quotation is a constituent of the sentence, the point stays outside the quotation marks — **quotation marks are the tenant, and punctuation is the landlord: only inside the house (the whole sentence) is the tenant's account reckoned**); **where to put “某某说”** (placed before the quotation, use a colon: 他说：“去吧。” — 'He said: go.' (Our translation.) Placed in the middle of the quotation, use a comma: “去吧，”他说，“天黑前回来。” — 'Go, he said, and come back before dark.' (Our translation.) — **a comma when “说” sits inside the quotation is a regular on the examination paper**); **the semicolon** (between coordinate clauses; **only a complex sentence that already has commas, and that seeks to make its levels clear, invites the semicolon out** — it is not a 'senior comma'); **levels of numbering** (the tradition of official documents: 一、（一）1.（1） — **in a composition, keeping one kind to one level is merit without measure**).
:::

::: intuition The check by copying and reading {#int-chaodu}
On the examination paper there is no teacher, only you yourself — the three-pass final check: **the first pass, listen** (aloud, or slowly in the mind; where it sticks, there is a faulty sentence — **the tongue is the cheapest doctor of grammar**); **the second pass, count** (count whether the paragraphs are out of balance, count whether each paragraph does only one thing, count whether the exclamation marks have overspent — **numbers will not join a feeling in a lie**); **the third pass, copy** (copy the characters that are easy to get wrong once onto scrap paper, then write them back in — the three pairs of enemies “的地得”, “在再” and “做作” are **named place by place**, and not by whether they take the eye). The punctuation first-aid box: **one comma all the way** (split a long sentence short; the full stop is a breath, not a surrender); **enumeration commas and no comma** (coordinate clauses given the enumeration comma by mistake — read it three times and you will hear it); **the punctuation of a quotation, each mark back to its own home** (inside, for a whole sentence; outside, for a constituent); **nothing superfluous at the end of a title, a book title or a question** (old books relied on *judou* 句读 (traditional pause marks); in the age when the teaching “读书略解义理，不以章句为事” — 'In reading, roughly grasp the sense and the principle, and do not make chapters and sentences your business.' (Our translation.) — was passed from master to pupil by word of mouth, *judou* was a private accomplishment and not a public instrument); **1919**, the Preparatory Committee for the Unification of the National Language passed the *Proposal Requesting the Promulgation of New-Style Punctuation Marks* (jointly signed by Ma Yuzao 马裕藻, Qian Xuantong 钱玄同, Zhou Bingchang 周秉常, Hu Shi 胡适 (Hu Shih) and others — **punctuation turned from a private accomplishment into a public instrument, on the same track as the New Culture Movement**); **1920**, the Ministry of Education ordered by circular that they be put into use; **1951**, the General Administration of Publication of the Central People's Government promulgated *Usage of Punctuation Marks* (the first time a government promulgated a unified usage); **1990**, the State Language Commission and the Press and Publication Administration revised and issued it; **1995**, the first national standard, GB/T 15834—1995; **2011**, the national standard now in force, GB/T 15834—2011 (it adds a three-way division of the connector, and provides that “！” and “？” may be omitted where there is no pause, among other detailed rules — **punctuation has version control too**); **the age of the internet**: punctuation enters a wild phase of pragmatics — **“好的”, “好的。” and “好的！” are three kinds of weather** (the cold of the full stop, the heat of the exclamation, the slack of no punctuation); the inflation of “！！！” devalues the single exclamation mark (the study of putting a cap on numbers, from the lesson on the expression of feeling, reappears here; see [[foundations-of-writing/expressing-feeling]]); the wave marks in “~~” are used to act cute, and the dash in “一” is pulled out into a horizontal line — **innovation in use is fun to play with; the examination paper does its business by the national standard**: both ledgers have to be ones you know how to keep.
:::

::: warning Four lapses of language and punctuation
First, **“的地得” running naked** (定性名, 状动地, 动补得 — 的 with attributive and noun, 地 with adverbial and verb, 得 with verb and complement; anyone can recite the mnemonic, and naming each place in turn is the real skill; the writing course's stop-loss line: **if you are not sure, read it three times**); second, **overuse of the question mark** (“我不知道他为什么不来。” — **a question inside a clause is not a question being asked**); third, **punctuation copied out of shape** (**not telling halfwidth from fullwidth is the wrong character of the electronic age**); fourth, **treating the page as a draft** (blobs of correction and insertion marks flying all over the sky — **the page is the reader's first impression, and the first duty of punctuation is visual order**).
:::

::: quiz
The grounds on which the enumeration comma is correctly used in “他每天跑步、游泳、打篮球，身体很好” are ( ).

- [x] Between coordinate words and phrases inside the sentence use the enumeration comma, and use a comma before the predicate joined on at the end
- [ ] The comma may replace the enumeration comma throughout

The enumeration comma for coordinate words and phrases, the comma for coordinate clauses — the difference of level between word and clause.

::: exercise The reading-aloud clinic {level=1}
Read the following faulty passage aloud and revise it (four faults): “他的家乡是山东人，出产苹果、山东的苹果特别甜、每年秋天，果农们忙着摘苹果、装苹果和苹果的运输。” ('His home town is a person from Shandong; it produces apples; Shandong apples are especially sweet; every autumn the fruit-growers are busy picking apples, packing apples, and the transport of the apples.') (Our translation.)
::: solution
The clinic slip: ① **improper collocation** — “家乡是山东人” → “他是山东人” or “家乡是山东” ('his home town is a person from Shandong' → 'he is a person from Shandong' or 'his home town is Shandong'); ② **levels of coordination mixed up** — “苹果、山东的苹果” repeats, and an enumeration comma joins clauses; ③ **one comma all the way / an enumeration comma used as a comma** — “特别甜、每年秋天” crosses a sentence; ④ **coordinate constituents of uneven structure** — “摘苹果、装苹果和苹果的运输”, a verbal item and a nominal item packed in together; change it to “摘苹果、装苹果、运苹果” ('pick apples, pack apples, and the transport of the apples' → 'pick apples, pack apples, transport apples'). A sample rewrite: “他是山东人。家乡出产苹果，秋天的时候特别甜，果农们忙着摘、装、运。” ('He is a person from Shandong. His home town produces apples, especially sweet in the autumn, and the fruit-growers are busy picking, packing and transporting.') (Our translation.) — **what is coordinated must be of the same level, and the same level must be of the same form**.
:::
:::

::: exercise A punctuation composition {level=2}
In no more than 60 characters, write a passage of dialogue which must contain all of the following at once: a colon cueing “说”, a “他说” inserted inside the quotation (with a comma), a question mark, and one sentence-final point inside the quotation marks and one point outside them. Mark, item by item, the 'landlord / tenant' identity of each punctuation mark.
::: solution
Sample: “你走哪条路？”她问，“导航说都在修。”——“远吗？”“不远，”她说，“一脚油门的事。” ('Which road are you taking?' she asked, 'the navigation says they are all under repair.' — 'Is it far?' 'Not far,' she said, 'it is the work of one touch on the accelerator.') (Our translation.) Marking: after an inserted “她说”, use a comma; the question mark of an independent question goes inside the quotation marks (the tenant settles the account); the comma in “不远，” **belongs to the quotation itself** (the original is “不远。” and the full stop is changed to a comma to follow the inserted form — the logic of the explanation: **the punctuation of a quotation follows its syntactic role**). Mark one place wrong but set the reason out clearly, and the full mark is not taken off.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the character of punctuation {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters: taking “好的。”, “好的!”, “好的～” and “好” in a chat as the examples, discuss **the four tone-prices of one and the same message**; then discuss whether a serious text and everyday usage need two sets of norms for punctuation, and how you switch between the two.
::: solution
Marking points: the price analysis of the four writings is properly done (the cooling of the full stop / the exclamation open for business / the wave playing cute / the casualness of the bare characters — **punctuation is the smallest sticker pack**); able to distinguish **the layer of norms (the national standard) and the layer of use (what a community agrees on)** and not favour one while slighting the other; the standard for switching is made clear (the person addressed and the occasion decide the ledger — sending a manuscript to an editor and replying to a close friend are two exchange rates), and able to point out: **using the ledgers in a jumble is more tiring than keeping to the old** (sending “好的～” in a formal text ≈ calling for help in a composition).
:::
:::

::: summary
The clinic settles the account: **the four sentence-fault cases** go back to the grammar hospital for a further visit, and this chapter supplies the examination by ear (reading aloud + reducing the sentence); **punctuation is entered in the ledger by the national standard** (the enumeration comma and the comma divide words from clauses; when “说” is inside the quotation, use a comma; for the point on a quotation, see whether it is the tenant or the landlord); **three passes of the final check**: listen, count, copy; **two ledgers for punctuation on the internet**: the cold of “好的。” and the heat of “好的！” are both legitimate — it depends on the occasion. Language and punctuation are the personal hygiene of writing — when they cost no marks, nobody praises them; when they cost marks, all the blame falls on them. The last chapter: revision — the procedure that turns 'finished writing' into 'written well'.
:::
