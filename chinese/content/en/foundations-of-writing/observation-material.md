title: "Observation and material"
---

“没得写” ('nothing to write') is the first intractable ailment of a writing course — this chapter exposes it: **it is not that you cannot write; it is that you have not seen**. Seeing is a muscle that is trained; recording is a habit that is systematised; only when the storehouse has goods does the pen have rice.

## Seeing, not recognising {#sec-kanjian}

::: definition The lazy governance of looking, and the reform {#def-kanjian}
The brain's default setting is **recognising** (“树” 'tree', “交警” 'traffic police', “地铁” 'the underground' — stick a label on and the job is done). What a writer wants is **seeing** (what Shklovsky 什克洛夫斯基 called “**使石头成为石头**”, '**making the stone into a stone**' — redeeming the thing from its label; the device of **defamiliarisation** has already been met in [[rhetoric/what-is-rhetoric]], and this chapter treats the craft at its **input end**). Three cuts of the training: ① **separate accounts for the five senses** (what is seen does not count as news; only what is **smelled, heard and touched** is fresh — the stock word for “早市” 'the morning market' is “热闹” 'bustling', while the observer's account carries “鱼腥混着炸油条的烟、秤砣磕案板的闷响、塑料袋在指环上勒出的白”, 'the fishy smell mixed with the smoke of frying dough sticks, the muffled thud of a steelyard weight on the chopping board, the white a plastic bag pinches out on a finger-ring'); ② **a compulsion for close-ups** (refuse the panorama: force yourself to film “一枚硬币在摊主油亮掌心里的旅行”, 'the journey of a single coin across the stallholder's oil-bright palm'); ③ **numbers entering the eye** (“人多” 'lots of people' is a lazy account; “排队的人从三号窗口拐了两个弯、第九个在刷手机第七分钟”, 'The queue turns two corners from window number three, and the ninth person is in the seventh minute of scrolling a phone.' (Our translation.), is a writable account). **Chekhov's lesson**: he taught his brother to look at the windowsill — “**你不必告诉我月亮在发光，让我看到碎玻璃上的一抹闪光**” ('**You need not tell me that the moon is shining; let me see a streak of glint on the broken glass.**' (Our translation.)) (the gist of the famous remark in the letter of 1888 to his brother Alexander Chekhov — **detail is the hard currency of the realm of description**).
:::

::: definition The three-column method for observation notes {#def-sanlan}
A pocket notebook (or a phone memo) is divided into three columns: **seen / inferred / felt** — training in “**事实与脑补分账**”, '**separate accounts for fact and mental fill-in**'. Example: seen, “她每隔半分钟看一次墙上的钟” ('Every half-minute she looks once at the clock on the wall.' (Our translation.)) / inferred, “在等人，且等得心虚” ('Waiting for someone, and ill at ease in the wait.' (Our translation.)) / felt, “我替她着急” ('I am anxious on her behalf.' (Our translation.)). The whole value of the three columns lies in **the separate accounts**: half of writing accidents is writing an inference up as fact (“他悲伤地走进来” 'He walked in sadly.' (Our translation.) — you only saw a lowered head, and speed); half is selling a feeling as a consensus (“感动的场面” 'a moving scene' — you yourself recorded no perceptible detail). **15 minutes a day × three entries**, and it takes effect in two weeks — this is a **prescription-grade assignment** of this course (set in parallel with the corpus in [[foundations-of-writing/language-punctuation]] as two daily lines).
:::

## Keeping the material storehouse {#sec-cangku}

::: definition Four stores of material and three grades of source {#def-siku}
Divided by **what is stored**, there are four stores: **people** (a portrait store: looks, catchphrases, habitual movements — “父亲的口头禅” 'Father's catchphrase' is a hundred times more writable than “父爱如山” 'a father's love is a mountain'); **events** (a plot store: a complete event + an unexpected turn); **ideas** (a store of views: quotable lines, disputes, judgements against common sense); **feelings** (an emotion store: the bodily reaction at the time — nervousness is “手滑在袖口上蹭了三次”, 'a slipping hand rubbed the cuff three times'). Divided by **where it comes from**, there are three grades, and each is marked with a **confidence**: **lived experience** (the highest, but with the blind spot of “只在此山中”, 'only within this mountain'); **hearsay** (oral accounts and second-hand material — note how many times it has changed hands; “听三姨说”, 'I heard it from Third Aunt', must be writable as from whom to whom); **imagination** (legitimate material, but the author must, in mind, **hang on it a placard reading “假一赔三”**, 'if fake, pay triple': invention is a right of fiction, not a pardon for the narrative composition — to write “合理想象” 'reasonable imagination' into a narrative composition is to admit fabrication). **The small craft of oral history** (the proper channel for stocking the hearsay store): open the sluice with “还记得什么” ('What do you still remember?' (Our translation.)), chase detail with detail (“那天早上你穿的什么？” 'What were you wearing that morning?' (Our translation.)), and **do not correct the other person's memory** (half the value of an oral account lies in “记成了什么样”, 'what it came to be remembered as'); one hour of oral account can yield three good compositions — **your own home holds an unmined mine of material**.
:::

::: example One morning market, three firms taking in stock {#ex-zaoshi}
Scene: a morning market, a fish stall. Please open one material card for **lived experience**, one for **hearsay** and one for **imagination**, and state the use and the red line of each card.
::: solution
**Lived-experience card** (the three columns seen / inferred / felt): seen, “他把翻肚的鲫鱼挑回盆里，手指插进鱼鳃试了几条，最后拎起两条最小的” ('He picked the belly-up crucian carp back into the basin, pushed a finger into the gills and tested a few, and at last lifted out the two smallest.' (Our translation.)) / inferred, “他在替人挑鱼——付钱的今晚不吃这条” ('He is choosing fish for someone else — the person who pays will not eat this one tonight.' (Our translation.)) / felt, “我忽然不敢看他笑” ('I suddenly dared not look at his smile.' (Our translation.)). Use: it goes straight into a narrative composition; the red line is that **the inference column must not be mixed into the body as fact**. **Hearsay card**: Third Aunt tells of the first time she went to the fish market — “以为鱼和菜市场一样讲价，结果人家按条报价，多问一句‘能挑吗’被白了一眼” ('I thought fish were bargained over just as at a vegetable market; it turned out they named a price per fish, and when I asked one thing more, "Can I pick?", I was shown the white of an eye.' (Our translation.)) — record the chain of transmission (Third Aunt → me), and note the date of the telling. Use: a non-fiction retelling; the red line is that **it must not be written as lived experience** (the three characters “我看见”, 'I saw', are an oath). **Imagination card**: the fish-seller's route for taking in stock at three in the morning (I have never been). Use: a legitimate wing of fiction/the essay; the red line is **packaging it as an interview inside a narrative composition** — imagination entering the right genre is called creation; entering the wrong genre is called falsification. Three cards, three legal identities — **the integrity of material begins with the grading**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Account thinking: why some people 'write more and more' {#int-zhanghu}
People with “没得写” treat writing as a **withdrawal** (when inspiration comes, withdraw a sum, and when it is withdrawn dry, let it drop); people who can write treat it as a **statement of account** (observation is a deposit, writing is the keeping of the books — between deposit and withdrawal there is **compound interest**: a person who has once recorded “鱼鳃试指”, 'a finger testing the gills', will next time automatically notice the knife-work at the meat stall — **training in observation changes the receiving standard of your perception**, and the more the storehouse is inventoried, the more alive it is). **The prescription of Ye Shengtao 叶圣陶 is the most honest: “养源” ('nurturing the source') — live life itself into a spring, and not into a hunting ground for raw material**. So the assignment in this chapter runs against intuition: **first live properly, and note things down as you go**. Someone asks how much noting-down counts as enough — only a person who asks about deposits would ask that.
:::

::: history From 'read ten thousand books' to the corpus {#sec-chuantong}
Chinese education in writing has always set store by “蓄材” ('storing up material'): **“读万卷书，行万里路”** ('Read ten thousand books, and travel ten thousand li.' (Our translation.)) (the words come from a Ming writer's theory of painting, and were later carried over to the study of literary composition — a famous saying has layers of provenance of its own) is the official formula; **Sima Qian 司马迁 netted the old accounts that had been scattered and lost** (*Bao Ren An shu* 报任安书, 'Letter in Reply to Ren An' — the founding master of using interview and archive together); **the brocade bag of Li He 李贺** (as recorded by Li Shangyin 李商隐: he rode a donkey out, and the moment he got a line he threw it into the brocade bag, and on returning wrote it up again — he lived “积累” 'accumulation' into a romantic idol); **Gu Yanwu 顾炎武 copying books on the carriage and at the horse's belly** (*Rizhi lu* 日知录 ('Record of Daily Knowledge') is the triumph of the excerpt-card system); **Qian Zhongshu 钱锺书 and notes in the manner of *Guanzhui bian* 管锥编 ('Pipe and Awl')** (taking cards and reading notes as the way of study, with the lecture notes piled into a hill); **modern times**: Ye Shengtao repeatedly required students to keep a diary and to set up notebooks (in his lecture notes, the diary and the notebook are standing subjects); **the present**: favourites folders, read-later lists, corpus apps — **the generation with the most material, and the fewest notes, in all of history** (collecting is an illusion of possession — **a memory that is not restated is not a memory**).
:::

::: warning Four illnesses of accumulation
One, **collecting mania** (three thousand clippings, and finished pieces at zero — **excerpting is not accumulation; retelling plus application is**; every saved item must be relayable in one sentence, otherwise delete it); two, **hunting-ground sickness** (you go out not to live, but only to “找素材” 'look for raw material' — **raw material is the interest on life; the principal must be paid in first**); three, **fabrication sickness** (the stock template “助人为乐” 'taking pleasure in helping others', and a made-up “外婆” 'grandmother' — **integrity in the narrative composition is the first virtue**: if you cannot write that you were moved, you may write “没感动” 'not moved'; the true stands higher than the correct); four, **expired-tin sickness** (the whole group shares the “万能例” 'all-purpose examples' of Sima Qian suffering the punishment of castration, Edison inventing the electric light, and Hawking sitting in a wheelchair — **it is not that these figures cannot be used; it is that direct wholesale, without observation and working-up of your own, is nothing but reciting them back**; a public figure, too, must be given **a reading of your own**).
:::

::: exercise Fifteen minutes, three columns {level=1}
Go to a crowded place (a canteen, a station, the gate of a residential compound), and in 15 minutes write three three-column notes. After submitting, review one another: in the other person's inference column, mark with △ the entries that are “证据不足” ('insufficient evidence').
::: solution
Marking: the seen column has **no privilege for adjectives** (if there is an adjective, it must bring the detail of its source); in the inference column **verbs must be countable** (“他在等” 'he is waiting' is insufficient evidence; “第三次看表” 'a third look at the watch' is evidence); the felt column allows “没有感觉” ('no feeling'). The person with the highest density of △ in the mutual review is precisely the next to improve the fastest — **being marked △ is good luck in this course**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Family oral history {level=2}
Interview one family member for 40 minutes (topic: choose either “你最后一次挨打” 'the last time you were beaten' or “你第一份工作的工资” 'the wage of your first job'), and write it up as a 600-character verbatim oral record. Requirements: the chain of transmission must be clear; keep one stretch of the original words in which he corrects his own memory; at the end of the piece attach one △ of yours (where you do not believe it).
::: solution
Marking points: ① traces of questions that **chase detail with detail** (at least one question of the grade of “那天你穿的什么” 'What were you wearing that day?' (Our translation.)); ② “记成了什么样” ('what it came to be remembered as') is **kept without correction** (where the value of an oral account lies); ③ the honesty of the △ (“他说‘你爸当时也在场’，但我爸说他那年不在家” 'He said, "Your dad was there at the time too," but my dad said he was not at home that year.' (Our translation.) — **contradiction is precisely historical source material**). This assignment is at the same time a family history — read again after twenty years, and the value doubles.
:::
:::

::: exercise A map of material lodes {level=3}
Using one week of three-column notes, make a lode map of your own: mark the three richest lodes (people / events / ideas / feelings), and for each list 3 writable topics + 1 pre-set central sentence + 1 question (what material is still needed).
::: solution
Marking: a lode comes from **repeated appearance** (a kind of raw material seen three times in three days = a lode; once = chance); the topic is specific (《奶奶的第二个手机》 'Grandma's second mobile phone' is not 《亲情》 'family affection'); the central sentence has a stance (a sentence that can be proved or overturned); the question column is honest (“需要：她第一次用它给谁打电话” 'Needed: whom she first used it to telephone.' (Our translation.) — **the next step of observation then has somewhere to land**). The three lodes on the map are your stock of compositions for this term.
:::

::: summary
This chapter has taken apart the consulting room of “没得写”: **looking** (recognising → seeing: separate accounts for the five senses, a compulsion for close-ups, numbers entering the eye — let the stone become a stone); **recording** (the three-column method: seen / inferred / felt, and separate accounts for fact and mental fill-in); **storing** (four stores and three grades: people, events, ideas and feelings kept in separate stores; lived experience / hearsay / imagination given separate legal identities); **taking** (fetch stock to the topic; a public figure, too, must have a reading of your own). The prescription is a single one: **fifteen minutes a day; first live properly, and note things down as you go**. The next chapter lays down a rule: one piece keeps only one master — theme and structure.
:::
