title: "Narration"
---

Narrative composition is the breath of the writing class: it hands events to the reader in order. This chapter does not teach "every single thing that happened". It teaches **the craft of carrying events** — a ledger of the six elements, the proportion of summary narration to scene, the shot that writes one second slowly, and one iron rule: **one event, one feeling**.

## The six elements: ledger and selection {#sec-liuyao}

::: definition The bookkeeping spirit of the 5W1H {#def-liuyao}
Time, place, person, cause, course and result (the classroom version of the 5W1H of journalism) — what they are is not a fill-in-the-blank template but a **ledger**: before the piece is handed in, check the account column by column. **An essay missing its "cause", and the reader patches it for you at random; an essay missing its "result", and the reader gets angry in your place.** Three rules of bookkeeping: ① **an element may be hidden but must not be missing** (the narration need not write the place, but the author must know it — knowing and not writing is called leaving a blank; not knowing, and writing it in by force, is called letting the trick show); ② **the course is double-entry bookkeeping** (the course of the event must be entered in full on a two-sided account of "action + reaction": 他递伞／她没接 ('he held out the umbrella / she did not take it') — **a one-sided account, written out, is a running list; only a two-sided account is a story**); ③ **the cause is worth one revision** (the root of most cases of "straying off the topic" lies in the cause: write the cause crooked, and everything after it is paving the road for the mistake — so, of the 8 minutes of planning in a 45-minute examination, **the cause takes four minutes**).
:::

## Summary narration and scene: the sketch and the livestream {#sec-changjing}

::: definition The study of proportion {#def-bili}
Narration has two speeds: **summary narration** (“暑假过得很快” ('The summer holiday went by very quickly.') (Our translation.) — one sentence runs across three months; that is **transport**) and **scene** (“井绳的凉气顺着指尖往上爬，西瓜在桶里撞着桶壁响” ('The cool of the well-rope climbed up along the fingertips; the watermelon struck the side of the bucket and sounded.') (Our translation.)) — that is a **livestream**. A good narrative composition is transport in the main, and a **livestream of the three key moments**: ① the moment the conflict is laid on the table ② the moment a person makes a choice ③ the moment your defences break — these three places are **slowed into scenes** (the action taken apart, dialogue brought on, the senses fully open); everything else, without exception, is summary narration hurrying on. The beginner's proportion is exactly the reverse: the running account of meals and of lodging is livestreamed at five hundred characters apiece, while the crucial scene is shipped across the border in the one sentence “我很感动” ('I was very moved.') (Our translation.) — **the lens has been taken for a souvenir camera; photographing everything = having nothing**. Three techniques of the slow shot: **splitting the action into frames** (“他把钱递给我” ('He handed me the money.') (Our translation.) → “他数出三张，指头在裤腿上蘸了两下，才捏着边角递过来” ('He counted out three notes, dabbed his fingers twice on his trouser leg, and only then pinched the corner and passed them over.') (Our translation.) — **splitting into frames is a close-up**); **bringing dialogue on** (words inside the quotation marks carry a verbal tic and pauses, and are ten times more alive than “他劝了我半天” ('He urged me for a long while.') (Our translation.)); **the senses cutting in** (in the scene, at least one non-visual line of the account: sound, smell, touch — see [[foundations-of-writing/observation-material]], the five senses kept on separate accounts).
:::

::: example Writing "he was very angry" as thirty seconds {#ex-shengqi}
A dead sentence common on the examination topic 《那一刻，我（他）明白了》 ('The moment I (he) understood'): “爸爸很生气。” ('Dad was very angry.') (Our translation.) Using the three-piece kit for a scene, rewrite it as a passage of no more than a hundred characters; the two characters 生气 ('angry') must not appear at any point; then say where, in the rewrite, the frame-splitting, the dialogue and the senses each sit.
::: solution
Sample: “爸爸没说话。他把卷子摊开，又对齐桌沿，对齐了再挪开半指。我妈从厨房探头，又把头缩回去。安静了三秒——他开口，声音平得不像他：‘这道题，教过你吗？’我以为要挨吼，结果只是很长的安静。” ('Dad said nothing. He spread the test paper out, then aligned it with the edge of the desk, and, once it was aligned, shifted it aside by half a finger's width. My mother put her head out from the kitchen, then drew her head back. Three seconds of quiet — he spoke, his voice so flat it did not sound like him: "This question: have I taught it to you?" I thought I was in for a shouting; what came was only a very long quiet.') (Our translation.) The three-piece kit, checked against the account: **frame-splitting** (spreading it out, aligning it, shifting it by half a finger — the bookkeeping of anger; the lighter the action, the greater the pressure); **dialogue** (“声音平得不像他” ('his voice so flat it did not sound like him') + a question that gives no answer — **the real anger is in the blanks of the lines**); **the senses** (the stir of a head put out from the kitchen, and the "sound" of that quiet itself, its 听感 ('how it falls on the ear') — **the quiet has taken on weight**). A mark for extra credit: the mother's head drawn back (a bystander's instinct raises the water level for the reader). The core of the marking: **do not write the name of the emotion; write the emotion's inventory of behaviour**.
:::
:::

::: intuition One event, one feeling: the digestive limit of the short piece {#int-yishi}
The iron rule of this chapter: **one piece narrates only one event, and sustains only one feeling** (in the teaching tradition, “以小见大”, '**seeing the large in the small**'; in the language of engineering, “聚焦即放大”, '**to focus is to magnify**'). Why this is not "writing about small matters" but "**writing one event**": the radius of a secondary-school student's life is not a weak point; it is an **advantage of resolution** — if you can write "forty minutes of one parents' meeting", you already have every procedure for writing "a battle" (the Battle of Chengpu in the *Zuo Tradition* (*Zuozhuan*) also takes its first stitch from the single action “退避三舍” ('retreating three *she*'); see [[pre-qin-literature/zuo-zhuan]]). The pathologies on the other side: **the "platter form"** (three blocks of material, three hundred characters each = three pieces broken off halfway) and **the "chronicle form"** (a running account from morning to night = plagiarising a diary). One cure for each: for the platter form, take the block that hurts most and **expand it into a scene**, and cut the rest away to serve as background; for the chronicle form, **cut off the head and the tail, and start writing from the middle of the event** (the technical name is *in medias res*, beginning in the middle of the event — **Homer, too, begins in the middle**).
:::

::: history The pedagogical lineage of training in narration {#sec-putong}
**1920s—1930s** In the National Language class (国语) the heading “记叙文” was established, in a three-way division with expository composition (说明文) and argumentative composition (议论文); **“记叙文” ('narrative composition') is a teaching genre, not a literary genre** — it takes in the parts common to the memoir, the travel account, the feature and the short story; **textbooks in the line of Ye Shengtao 叶圣陶 taught pieces under the heads of recording, narrating, the expression of feeling and reasoning, and the procedures of narration acquired their first formulation in a textbook;** **1950s** the Soviet system advanced three lines at once, “观察—想象—表达” ('observation — imagination — expression'), and training in narration stressed two points of knowledge, **the order of narration and person**; **1963/1978/1986/1992/2000** the syllabuses were revised in turn: from “把话说清楚” ('say it so that it is clear') to “**有中心、有条理、有一定详略**” ('**having a centre, having order, and having a due proportion of detail and brevity**') (the outline in these few characters is a constitutional article of training in narration); **from 1999** the *zhōngkǎo* and the *gāokǎo* ('the senior-high entrance examination' and 'the university entrance examination') set “文体不限/文体自选” ('genre not limited / genre of one's own choosing') — narrative composition was for the first time allowed **not to wear the school uniform**, and at once there appeared a new eight-legged formula in three steps, “**半命题 + 细节描写 + 结尾升华**” ('**a half-given title + detailed description + a lift at the end**'); **from 2016** the writing volume of the unified textbooks (“学会记事” ('Learn to record an event') and “文从字顺” ('fluent prose, words in good order') standing as units of their own — **the procedures of narration are taken apart and taught again**); **today**: teachers want “真情实感” ('genuine feeling'), the market wants “反转爽点” ('a reversal and a point of gratification'), and both sides are contending for the same young readers — this chapter takes a side: **first tell the event clearly, and only then talk of telling it so that it shines**.
:::

::: warning The four pests of narration
One, the **running account** (too much summary narration — the cure: set the whole piece a "scene budget", and in a thousand characters allow only two livestreams); two, the **compulsion to add drama** (artificial elevation of the “我扶着老奶奶” sort ('I was supporting an old lady by the arm'), and a sudden offering of charity at the end — **an inflation of feeling destroys credit faster than a deflation of detail**); three, **drift of person** (“我妈说我傻，你猜我是不是傻” ('Mum says I am silly; you guess whether I am silly.') (Our translation.) — a change of post among the narrative persons “我/他/你” ('I / he / you') must have a sentry: **the first person, held all the way through, is safest**); four, **leaving the full and the brief to adjectives** (“我非常非常难过” ('I was very, very sad.') (Our translation.) is an IOU for the emotion — **adverbs of degree are counterfeits of a scene**; it is enough to write “把冰棍吃完了，签子舔了三遍” ('I finished the ice lolly and licked the stick three times.') (Our translation.), and the sadness does not need you to say it).
:::

::: exercise A check-up of the ledger {level=1}
Audit the six elements in your most recent narrative composition: mark each column “明写／暗知／缺失” ('written on the page / known but not written / missing'), find the most serious "one-sided account" (an action and no reaction), and rewrite it as a two-sided account.
::: solution
Marking: the audit sheet is complete; the column "known but not written" must be something one can really answer (ask: what time was it that day? the author answers at once = a pass); a sample rewrite: “我摔门进屋” ('I slammed the door and went in.') (Our translation.) → “我摔门，门在框里跳了两跳才挂住；屋外的脚步声顿了一下，走了” ('I slammed the door; it jumped twice in the frame before it latched; the footsteps outside paused, then went off.') (Our translation.) — **a reaction is an echo, and the echo marks the size of the space**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Livestreaming the three moments {level=2}
For the topic 《那一次，我没有退缩》 ('That time, I did not flinch'): first list your "three key moments" (one line each for the showdown / the choice / the breach), write a scene draft only for those three moments (each ≤120 characters), and carry everything else past in a single sentence of summary narration — submit this "skeleton that contains only the crucial scenes".
::: solution
Marking: the three moments do not duplicate one another (showdown = the conflict shows itself; choice = a price is paid; breach = the feeling changes what it says); each moment uses at least one tool of the slow shot; **the moment of "choice" must make the cost visible** (the advantage of flinching is written by you in the open; only then does not flinching count as a choice — **a choice with no cost is a posed shot**). Once the skeleton passes, the whole piece is only a matter of filling in, on top of it, the mileage of transport.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the good faith of narration {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters: teachers require "genuine feeling", yet real life is often not "moving" enough. When "the true" and "a good story" conflict, which side should the narrative composition stand on? Requirements: ① analyse why “编一个更感人的真事” ('making up a more moving true event') is self-contradictory; ② give one model of narration you have read that **wins by its procedure and not by its plot**; ③ state the three remedies you allow when "the material is not enough", and the boundary of genre for each.
::: solution
Marking points: ① "a more moving true event" = invented — the key to the contradiction is that "true" is a contract of the genre, not a moral ornament; ② the standard for the model is not limited to famous pieces (concreteness of the kind in “奶奶的老年机每次都接” ('Grandma's phone for the elderly was answered every time.') (Our translation.), as in a classmate's composition, is enough, provided you can name its procedures: frame-splitting / dialogue / restraint); ③ a recommended list of three remedies: **change the angle and write yourself** (give the lens to a bystander), **compress the time** (write only ninety seconds), **change the genre** (go and write fiction — **put what is invented into a basket that admits it is invented**) — and state the red line of each.
:::

::: summary
The four procedures of narration: **checking the ledger** (the six elements: they may be hidden but must not be missing; the course is recorded as a two-sided account; the cause is worth one revision); **setting the pace** (summary narration transports, the scene livestreams, and the three crucial moments are slowed); **the three-piece kit of the slow shot** (splitting the action into frames, bringing dialogue on, the senses cutting in — do not write the name of the emotion; write the emotion's inventory of behaviour); **keeping the iron rule** (one event, one feeling; seeing the large in the small is not writing small, but writing deep). The next chapter enters the colour-mixing room of description: the fixed point and the moving step, plain line and fine brush, the division of labour among the five senses and their not connecting (synaesthesia).
:::
