title: "Description"
---

Description is narration's pause key: the story stops, and lets the reader **stand inside the scene**. A pause has to have a reason — what this chapter teaches is the reasons and the technique: how the senses divide the labour, how the camera is placed, and how the ink is spared.

## Division of labour among the senses, and placing the camera {#sec-ganguan}

::: definition Balancing the five senses {#def-wugan}
The first principle of description: **the reader can enter the scene only through your senses**. Three rules for balancing the five senses. ① **Demote sight** (a beginner's draft is 90% an eyesight report — an old hand first asks “这里有什么声音/什么气味/什么温度/脚底下什么触感” ('what sound is there here / what smell / what temperature / what it feels like under the feet'); smell is memory's shortcut, and “一碗馄饨的猪油味” ('the lard smell of a bowl of wonton') can take a person back to the alley better than ten lines of night scenery); ② **One scene, one leading sense** (for one scene, choose one sense to be the protagonist, and let the rest be the harmony — the leading sense is assigned by the **mood of the scene**: tension is given to hearing (a pendulum, an Adam's apple), desolation to touch (sand in the wind, dust on the seat of the chair)); ③ **Weigh the adjectives first** (“美丽的、漂亮的、迷人的” ('beautiful, pretty, charming') are three brothers of a devalued currency — **exchange them for nouns and verbs**: “她的头发像被雷劈过” ('Her hair looked as if lightning had split it.') (Our translation.) beats “她很凌乱” ('she is very untidy'); in “夕阳把楼房的西墙烫成铜色” ('The setting sun scorched the west wall of the building to a copper colour.') (Our translation.) there is not one adjective of feeling).
:::

::: definition Four placements of the camera {#def-jingtou}
**Fixed-point description** (stand fast in one place and sweep from a wide shot to a close-up — friendly to expository scenes), **the view changes with the step** (the person walks and the scene moves; the spine of a travel piece; the disease is writing it as a guide's script — **at each stop keep only one sight that shakes hands with the "theme"**), **high and low cameras together** (switch between looking down, “车像甲虫爬” ('cars crawl like beetles'), and looking up, “烟囱插进云” ('a chimney thrust into the cloud'), and the space is at once three-dimensional), **from far to near / from the surface to the inside** (the classic sequence of focus: distant outline → near detail → fine enough for “他领带上有个饭粒” ('There is a grain of rice on his tie.') (Our translation.) — **a close-up is foreshadowing**, and the grain of rice will speak). The discipline common to the four placements: **the camera is the place where the narrator stands, not the author's showing off** — whoever is looking at this scenery, write the part that person can see (a person in a bad mood cannot see the sunset clouds, and sees only “天像一块没洗的抹布” ('The sky is like a rag that has not been washed.') (Our translation.) — **the household registration of scene-words is entered under the character's name**; the technical name: see the reversed use of “触景生情” ('feeling stirred on meeting a scene') in [[rhetoric/figures-of-meaning]]: **there is feeling first, and only then that pair of glasses**).
:::

## Plain outline, fine brush, and synaesthesia {#sec-baimiao}

::: definition Two kinds of brush and ink, one bias {#def-bimo}
**Plain outline** (*báimiáo* 白描; borrowed from the terms of Chinese painting: no colour is laid on, only the line is drawn — shape is got from the fewest nouns and verbs: *Bei ying* 《背影》 ('The Back View') — a dissection in verbs, with zero adjectives; a hundred years on, it is still the most precious back) and **fine brush** (*gōngbǐ* 工笔; heavy colour and fine detail: the moonlight, the lotus scent and the tree shadows in *Hetang yuese* 《荷塘月色》 ('Moonlight on the Lotus Pond') are coloured layer on layer — **elaboration is expression of feeling on a budget; abuse it and it is inflation**). The standard of choice does not lie in how high or low the literary colour stands, but in **character and state of mind**: a person who has seen the world, or a restrained narrator, uses plain outline (few words = weight); a moment when feeling is so full that it is about to overflow is allowed the fine brush once (**the fine brush is fireworks; let them off every day and they are noise**). **Synaesthesia** (the star among figures of speech: “微风过处，送来缕缕清香，仿佛远处高楼上渺茫的歌声似的” ('Where a light breeze passes, it brings thread after thread of delicate fragrance, as if it were the faint singing from a high tower far away.') (Our translation.) — smell goes next door to call on hearing; “她的笑很甜” ('her smile is very sweet') — taste takes a second job): synaesthesia is not rhetoric showing off its skill; it is **the senses truly cross-wired inside an emotion**, and to use it accurately is worth more than to use it often.
:::

::: example Case notes: a passage of stock scenery {#ex-gongban}
The opening of the composition 《春》 ('Spring'): “春天来了，万物复苏，小草从土里探出头来，花儿露出了笑脸，小鸟在枝头唱着歌，小河欢快地奔向远方。” ('Spring has come, and all things revive. The little grasses poke their heads up out of the soil, the flowers show smiling faces, the little birds sing on the branch-tips, and the brook runs merrily off towards the distance.') (Our translation.) Open the case-notes book (at least three each for the name of the disease, the seat of the disease, and the prescription), and rewrite it as a paragraph of "你家楼下的春天" ('the spring downstairs from your home').
::: solution
Case notes: **Disease 1 · the photocopier of public material** (“万物复苏” ('all things revive'), “探出头” ('poke a head out'), “露笑脸” ('show a smiling face'), “奔向远方” ('run off towards the distance') are all **public stock** — personification copied from a dictionary; nobody has seen, downstairs from their own home, grass poking its head out); **Disease 2 · the whole cast visual + the whole cast cheerful** (the sounds of spring, its smells, its temperature, and the willow-catkin allergy on the legs are all absent; and the mood has gone first and stamped “欢快” ('merry'), before the senses have had time to vote); **Disease 3 · the verbs laid off as a body** (“来了、复苏、探出、露出、唱着、奔向” ('has come, revive, poke out, show, are singing, run towards') — half are cousins of the “是”字句 (the *shi* sentence), and half are **all-purpose actions turned into personification**; not one is an action that would happen only here). Prescription: **send the goods back and restock** — go downstairs (or to the window, or along the way to school) and, in 15 minutes of three-column notes, gather it again; try **one scene, one leading sense** once, with “春天先从什么声音/气味开始” ('which sound, or which smell, spring begins from'); **a special drill on verbs** — replace every all-purpose verb with an action that is “此处独有” ('found only here'). A sample rewrite: “春天是从塑料袋开始的。风把去年冬留的白袋子绑在栅栏上，抽得啪啪响，像没人接的电话；槐花的甜香翻墙进来，混在尾气味里，呛出一代人共同的鼻涕虫记忆——妈喊我回家收衣服，那被单上已经铺好一层细黄的花。” ('Spring begins from a plastic bag. The wind ties the white bags left from last winter onto the fence and whips them until they crack, like a telephone that nobody answers; the sweet scent of the pagoda-tree flowers comes over the wall, mixes with the smell of exhaust, and stings out a whole generation's shared memory of snot-nosed children — Mum calls me home to take the washing in, and that sheet is already spread with a layer of fine yellow flowers.') (Our translation.) — not one mention of all things reviving, and spring is present.
:::
:::

::: intuition Description is the interest on narration {#int-xifen}
The **only practical standard** for judging a passage of description good or bad: delete it, and does the narration collapse? **Description is the interest on narration** — when the principal (story and emotion) is present, description makes the reader rich; when the principal is absent, however splendid the description, it is **naked inflation** (two thousand characters of scenery in the “作文选体” ('manner of the composition anthology'), and the character has still not walked out of the door). So description has only one permit to start work: **a pause is needed here** — either to give emotion a place to set its foot (scene and feeling fused), or to give what follows a slot for foreshadowing (a close-up is a preview), or to give the reader a map (the setting establishes the scene). **Scenery written for the sake of scenery is called an illustration, not description**.
:::

::: history From the rhapsody to "training in description" {#sec-fuli}
Chinese literature has always had **a major tradition of description**: the **Han rhapsody** (*fu*), “铺采摛文，体物写志” ('Spread the colours and lay out the pattern; embody things and write the intent.') (Our translation.) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Quan fu' 《诠赋》 ('Explaining the Rhapsody') — the official age in which to describe a thing was to state intent, and elaboration was the orthodox form); **landscape poetry** (Xie Lingyun 谢灵运, “池塘生春草” ('The pond grows spring grass.') (Our translation.) — nature, entering poetry, obtained a household registration); **short notes of the Six Dynasties**, “风烟俱净，天山共色” ('Wind and mist are all clear; sky and mountains are one colour.') (Our translation.) (Wu Jun 吴均), shrink landscape to a postcard; the line of *jìngjiè* 境界 ('world; realm') in **Tang–Song ancient-style prose (*guwen*) and in poetry and song lyric** (“红杏枝头春意闹” ('On the red-apricot branch-tips the feeling of spring clamours.') (Our translation.) — Wang Guowei 王国维 prized this one character “闹” ('clamours') most — **refining a word is refining the senses** — the song lyric (*ci*) of Song Qi 宋祁 read in class together with Wang Guowei's *Remarks on Song Lyrics in the Human World*); **Qing theory of painting** passed the character “描” ('depict') down as the family heritage: the pattern-book teaching of *Jieziyuan huazhuan* 《芥子园画传》 ('The Mustard Seed Garden Manual of Painting'); **the modern period**: *Wenxin* 《文心》 ('The Heart of Writing') and the composition textbooks moved “描写” ('description') from painting theory into the Chinese class, and set it alongside “记叙、说明、议论” ('narration, exposition, argument') as the **fourfold division of the modes of expression** (an invention of teaching method; the same reminder as the previous chapter); **the modern and the contemporary**: Lao She 老舍, “北平的秋” ('autumn in Beiping'), and the Gaoyou salted duck eggs of Wang Zengqi 汪曾祺 (a modern demonstration of **plain outline + the sound of speech**: to write what one eats is to write one's native place); **today**: storyboards for film and television, and “种草文案” ('copy that plants a craving'), have become the new generation's textbook of description — **the boundary between the words of exposition and the prose of description will come to court in [[foundations-of-writing/exposition]]**.
:::

::: warning Three diseases of description
One, **heaping** (adjectives in a chain explosion: “绚丽多彩的金色灿烂的朝霞” ('gorgeous, many-coloured, golden, brilliant clouds of dawn') — **three adjectives equal not even one**; prescription: a quota of one adjective per sentence, and anything over the quota is taken away to be exchanged for nouns and verbs); two, **stock images** (the moon must be “如玉盘” ('like a disc of jade'), the eyes must be “黑葡萄” ('black grapes') — **a comparison is a consumer good; to use a comparison someone else has used = to stick on a barcode**; first check the comparisons in stock for yourself, then decide whether it is a homage or a return); three, **description uncoupled from the character** (the narrator is a lower-secondary student in a hurry, yet the scenery is as if an essayist had taken possession — **the glasses must be fitted to the character**: age, occupation and mood decide what he “看见什么” ('sees'); register one detail to the wrong household, and the whole passage of description has to be moved to the “风景明信片” ('scenic postcard') shelf).
:::

::: exercise Rewriting for the leading sense {level=1}
Take a passage of scenery you have written (or use straight off “暴雨前的教室”, 'the classroom before a rainstorm'), and rewrite it twice: pass A, leading sense = hearing; pass B, leading sense = smell. Each pass no more than 80 characters, and at most one adjective per sentence.
::: solution
Marking: the two passes **do not repeat the information** (the same scene hands over two different chains of evidence); outside the leading sense there is exactly one harmony (in the hearing pass, one touch of a smell); the special drill on verbs is passed (“砸、拍、挠” ('pound, slap, scratch'), each going back to its own place in the sound). After comparing the two passes, write a conclusion of 30 characters: which sense found you first, and why — **this is the report on your sensitive zone**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Fitting the glasses {level=2}
For the same scene, “商场中庭” ('the atrium of a shopping centre'), fit three pairs of glasses and write three passages of 60 characters each: ① an office worker waiting for someone and about to be late; ② an old person from the countryside entering a large shopping centre for the first time; ③ a security guard on the night round. Requirement: the three passages must not introduce an identity; identity shows only through **what is seen**.
::: solution
Marking: the office worker sees only the clock and the exit (scene-words = signposts of anxiety); the old person's list of verbs gives away the frame of reference (“厕所比家里客厅还大” ('The toilet is even bigger than the sitting room at home.') (Our translation.), “没人买东西他们为什么排队” ('Nobody is buying anything; why are they queueing?') (Our translation.)); the staffing structure of the guard's attention (fire hydrants, blind spots of the cameras, sockets) — **an occupation is the best pair of glasses**. Full marks where the three identities can be guessed, and not by way of a name-badge.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short paper: the inflation of description {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters. Take “作文选体风景段” ('a scenic passage in the manner of the composition anthology') as the specimen, discuss how “辞藻通胀” ('the inflation of diction') comes about (the over-issue of public comparisons, the reckless minting of adjectives, price control in which feeling goes first), and set against it the plain outline of one master (Lao She, Wang Zengqi, or 《背影》 ('The Back View'), choose any) to explain **how the currency was held steady**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the mechanism of the disease is made clear (stock comparisons = the over-issue of a public coinage; once everyone uses them, they are worthless to everyone); the master's example can be entered on the ledger of verbs and nouns (“他肥胖的身子向左微倾” ('His fat body leant slightly to the left.') (Our translation.) — how zero adjectives hold their value); the conclusion is allowed to be the conservative one (inflation has no solution; there is only a **personal austerity policy**: fewer adjectives, observe diligently, delete three times) — **an A goes to whoever can set down austerity clauses that they themselves can carry out**.
:::

::: summary
Goods drawn from the description workshop: the five senses balanced (sight demoted, one scene and one leading sense, adjectives weighed), four placements of the camera (scene-words registered under the character's name), three grades of brush and ink (plain outline is a dissection in verbs, the fine brush is fireworks in a limited run, synaesthesia is the senses cross-wired inside an emotion), and, kept at the bottom of the chest, that permit — **a pause is needed here**. In the next chapter we learn the calmest mode of all: exposition — to make words clear is a harder courtesy than to make words beautiful.
:::
