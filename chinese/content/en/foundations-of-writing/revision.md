title: "Revision"

This chapter teaches the most valuable lesson of a writing course: **parting from your own treasured draft**. Asked what the first draft of any serious writing is, Hemingway gave a coarse answer that has been quoted for half a century — **every serious writer owes a debt to the first draft, and becomes a writer by paying that debt**.

## Three layers of the knife {#sec-sandao}

::: definition The structural knife, the paragraph knife and the sentence knife {#def-sandao}
**Revision has three layers, and the order cannot be reversed** (before you set a hand to a lower layer, the upper layer must already have been brought to a stop — **in decorating, you cannot paint first and knock the wall down afterwards**): **① the structural knife** (tip the draft out into an outline: one sentence summarising each paragraph, pasted at the side — **the outline will make the shape appear**: paragraphs that wander off, paragraphs that repeat, and major scenes that are absent stand clear at a glance; then ask the three questions of the constitution: is the central sentence still that sentence? which paragraph serves two masters? have the three crucial moments been broadcast live?), **② the paragraph knife** (inside the paragraph, **one core, one matter**; let the first sentence stand as **sentry of the paragraph's point**; cleave a long paragraph into short ones; if two paragraphs are stuck together, add a signpost word; when you check a transition, check the door frames by which an inserted narration goes in and comes out), **③ the sentence knife** (reading aloud pins down the fault; delete three kinds of padding — **“进行了”“十分非常”“基本上其实”** ('carried out', 'very-extremely', 'basically-actually'); upgrading the verb takes priority over adjectives adding drama; **one protagonist to a sentence, and cut a long sentence into breaths**). **How the characters are portioned out also belongs to this layer**: first drafts commonly leave the protagonist short of scenes and crown the walk-on parts — **what the structural knife cuts away is often the opening, and what it brings back to life is often the ending**.
:::

::: example A three-layer operation on one paragraph {#ex-shoushu}
Case file ('Tug of War'): “大家都非常努力地拉着绳子，场面十分热烈，同学们都在呐喊，我也很激动，感觉团结的力量真大。绳子一会儿过去一会儿过来，最后我们赢了，明白了一个道理：团结就是力量。” 'Everyone was pulling very hard on the rope; the scene was extremely lively; the classmates were all shouting; I too was very excited, and felt that the power of unity was truly great. The rope went across for a while and came back for a while; in the end we won, and understood a principle: unity is strength.' (Our translation.) Please open a three-layer operation sheet and write the paragraph after the operation.
::: solution
**Structural knife**: the whole paragraph misses every shot of the "three moments of the major scene" (no showdown, no choice, no breach of the defence), yet snatches a shot of one elevating line (“团结就是力量” settles the account too soon) — the operation sheet: **cut the two sentences of commentary at the head and the tail, and expand the middle into one live broadcast**. Paragraph knife: one paragraph serves five masters (the pull, the shouting, what I feel, the rope, the moral) — split it into three short paragraphs, **one pull, one shout, one close**. Sentence knife: the three groups of degree adverbs “非常努力、十分热烈、很激动” ('trying very hard', 'extremely lively', 'very excited') are all **to be exchanged for a delivery list**; “一会儿过去一会儿过来” ('over for a while, back for a while') is a good observation — **keep it, but give it an exact subject**. The gold line for marking: **it is not that the elevation may not be written; it is that it may not be written for the reader** — drive the moral out of the composition, and let it grow up in the reader's mind.
:::
:::

::: intuition The law of cutting out what you love: the technique of parting from the treasure {#int-geai}
The greatest resistance in revision is not the eye, but **feeling** — “那段排比我很喜欢” 'I am very fond of that parallelism.' (Our translation.) “这个开头花了我二十分钟” 'This opening cost me twenty minutes.' (Our translation.) Three works of psychological engineering: **the cooling method** (leave the first draft overnight, then revise — **only the self who has become a stranger is a strict editor**; the compressed version for the examination hall: write the ending first, and come back to read five minutes later); **the transferred-account method** (a sentence you cannot bear to delete, **move it into a "spare-materials notebook"** — not a deletion, but a transfer of post; the materials notebook will prove it: a good sentence is worth more here than in the main text); **the reader-stand-in method** (ask the person at the next desk to read only your **opening sentences of the paragraphs**, and to retell the piece — the part they cannot retell is the words you thought you had written and in fact had not). Let famous lines be shareholders in the spirit: Cao Xueqin 曹雪芹, “披阅十载，增删五次” 'read through for ten years, added and deleted five times' (Our translation.), is revision made into capital; Wang Anshi 王安石, “春风又绿江南岸” 'Again the spring wind greens the southern shore' (Our translation.) (as recorded in Hong Mai 洪迈, *Rongzhai xubi* 容斋续笔, 'Further Notes from the Rong Studio', the character “绿” changed several times), is the top of the bill in the history of revising a verb; a Song notebook (*bǐjì* 笔记) passes down that those at the door of Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修 were set the topic “逸马践犬” ('a bolting horse tramples a dog'), and the good ones ran to only a handful of characters — **the craft of concision lies in the information, not in the number of characters**; Jia Dao 贾岛 and “推敲” ('push' or 'knock'), meeting Han Yu 韩愈, so that even the tone comes under revision's charge (a celebrated anecdote of Tang and Song remarks on poetry, *shīhuà* 诗话) — **the hand of revision, in the end, reaches to the sound**.
:::

::: history A teaching history of views of revision {#sec-jiaoxue}
Chinese literary theory long ago had a chapter devoted to "revision": in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Melting and Cutting' (《文心雕龙·熔裁》), “**句有可削，足见其疏；字不得减，乃知其密**” 'If a sentence can be cut, that is enough to show the looseness; if a character cannot be taken away, then one knows the density.' (Our translation.) (the definition of “密” — **density is not being short; it is that a character cannot be pulled out**); historians prized “简” ('concision'), and **“事溢于句外” 'the event overflows beyond the sentence' (Our translation.) is first-rate goods**; Jia Dao's “推敲”, with Han Yu settling on “敲” ('knock') (a celebrated anecdote of Tang and Song remarks on poetry — **even the tone comes under revision's charge**); Wang Anshi's “春风又绿江南岸” (as recorded in Hong Mai's *Rongzhai xubi*, **the character “绿” was changed several times** — the top of the bill in the history of revising a verb); in modern teaching, **Ye Shengtao 叶圣陶, “写完之后至少读两遍” 'After you have finished writing, read it at least twice.' (Our translation.)** became the classroom watchword for several generations; from the 1980s, discussion that “**作文批改重于精批细改**” '**marking a composition outweighs meticulous detailed correction**' (Our translation.), and that a teacher's corrections in red are not as good as the student revising it themselves (**the subjectivity turn: the right to revise belongs to the author**); the process approach to writing ([[foundations-of-writing/writing-process]]) separates revising/editing — **changing the content is not correcting wrong characters**; the nationally unified textbooks set activity lessons on “**文从字顺**” ('the writing flows and the words run true'), “**语言简明**” ('language concise and clear'), and on mutual review and revising one's own work; **today**: AI proofreading is on call whenever it is wanted, and human revision has for the first time "an actor to play the scene opposite" — but **judging which sentence is my voice is still something the author does with their own hand**.
:::

::: warning Four errors of revision
One, **correcting only 错别字** (characters wrongly written or wrongly used) (treating revision as proofreading — **the order of the knife runs backwards, and the wall you have painted is knocked down the next day**); two, **growing longer the more you revise** (each round only adding and never deleting = stuffing souvenirs into a suitcase; **open a deletion ledger: the characters deleted this round must not be fewer than half of those added**); three, **an addiction to tearing it down and starting again** (on the third pass, setting up the stove afresh once more = a legitimate evasion of the first three passes; **revision is carving one and the same piece of stone**); four, **saying polite formulas in peer review** (“写得很好，语句优美” 'Very well written; the sentences are beautiful.' (Our translation.) = zero information; peer review by a scale: **you must point to one fault plus one change that can be carried out**, and anyone who cannot write the change is counted as not having reviewed).
:::

::: exercise An outline that makes the shape appear {level=1}
Take a recent composition of your own and **tip it out into an outline** (one sentence summarising each paragraph); mark, with three kinds of symbol, paragraphs that serve two masters, major scenes that are absent, and paragraphs stuck together; then move only the **structural knife**, and list the operation sheet (containing no revision of any wording).
::: solution
Marking: an outline sentence must be **a summary in subject–predicate form** (not a copy of the paragraph's opening sentence); for each of the three diagnoses give at least one piece of evidence, or note “未见” ('not found'); items on the operation list begin with a verb (“砍” 'cut', “并” 'merge', “提前” 'bring forward', “扩” 'expand'). **If a single adjective appears in the list, that item is judged invalid** — the structural knife is not allowed to touch the rhetoric.
:::
:::

::: exercise Peer review on a scale {level=2}
Review each other with the person at the next desk, and hand in a form according to the scale: ① a copy of the central sentence (as the reviewer summarises it) plus the author's original sentence, set side by side; ② the sentence with the best selling look (quote the original); ③ the one place you most want shot again (state which "moment" is missing); ④ give one change **the author can carry out on the spot**; ⑤ one point of doubt, and ask the author to answer it.
::: solution
Points for marking: ① the comparison is the diagnosis (if the two central sentences do not match = the conception failed to get across); ④ is a hard requirement — **“这里要更生动” 'This needs to be more vivid.' (Our translation.) is judged invalid**; “把‘他很着急’改成他看表的三个动作” 'Change "他很着急" ('he was very anxious') into three actions of him looking at his watch.' (Our translation.) is judged acceptable; ⑤ a doubt must come down to a particular sentence. A receipt signed both ways: on each item the author writes “改／不改＋一句理由” ('revise / do not revise, plus one sentence of reason') — **not revising is a legitimate option; only not responding is a breach**.
:::
:::

::: exercise The final-draft battle: to revise is to rewrite {level=3}
Choose one draft; after three rounds of revision attach three operation sheets (for each round: the layer of the knife, the number of places the knife touched, characters deleted / characters added) plus a closing of 200 characters: which of the three rounds hurt the most, and why — **where the sentence “修改即重写” ('to revise is to rewrite') is rhetoric, and where it is fact**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the three rounds **must be different layers** (one round each of structure / paragraph / sentence; anyone who mixes layers is sent back); the counts of deletions and additions must be genuine (what grows shorter the more it is revised is usually closer to the truth); the standard for the closing: the structure round hurts in cutting away the part you like but do not need (**the ledger of feeling**), the sentence round hurts in admitting that the original prettiness was a layer of plating (**the ledger of narcissism**) — whoever can tell “**rewriting = tearing it down**” from “**rewriting = reconstructing**” and can bear witness with their own draft earns an A for this chapter, which is also the completion certificate for this course.
:::
:::

::: summary
The revision workshop settles its accounts: **the knife from the top downwards** (structure → paragraph → sentence; decorating does not paint first), **the mind must cool** (overnight, a transfer of post, invite a stand-in — parting from the treasure is a technique, not a temperament), **review must have a scale** (polite formulas = zero information; a method of revision is the currency), **the ledger must record deletions and additions** (“字不得减乃知其密” 'if a character cannot be reduced, then one knows the density' (Our translation.) — density is that it cannot be pulled out, not that it is short). Nine chapters now run through in one line: observation enters the store, laying down the law fixes the constitution, narration lays the rails, description lays on the colour, expression of feeling calculates the interest, exposition keeps the rites, punctuation takes off the dust, revision leaves the factory — **this hard labour of writing, from now on, has an assembly line in your hands**. Go and write; go and revise. What remains, hand to the next topic.
:::
