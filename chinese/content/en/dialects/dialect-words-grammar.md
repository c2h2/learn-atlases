title: "Dialect words and grammar"

The first six chapters say again and again, “词汇是天气、音类是气候” ("Vocabulary is the weather, and sound categories are the climate.") (Our translation.) This chapter lays those two things out as a list that can be checked — **and it gives the fieldwork lesson that this lesson holds to be the most important: a dialect you elicit by asking in Putonghua (standard Mandarin) is always dialectised Putonghua**.

## Definition: the lexical layer {#def-cihui}

::: definition Three kinds of difference {#def-sanzhong}
**① Word-form replacement** (**the same thing is said with an entirely different word**) (**for the action 走, some places say 行;** this agrees with the division of labour in earlier Chinese, and so is often taken as evidence of a stratum) (the received account) **② Semantic shift** (**the same word, with a different range of meaning:** a word for a child means, at some points, only a son, and a word for a father means, at some points, a grandfather) (**The received account:** this kind most easily causes misunderstanding across dialects, because on a word list it shows up as 'the same') (**This lesson's classification:** word-form replacement can be counted, and semantic shift cannot — **so any argument that rests on 'the proportion of words in common' must first say how it handles polysemy**) **③ Lexical gap** (**the place has no ready-made word, and so a phrase is used, or a word is borrowed from the common language**) (**What this lesson sets store by in a gap:** it exposes something that a conceptual system has never distinguished) (**differences of classification in kinship terms and in containers, and between grains of rice and cooked food, are the easiest examples to observe**) (**The received account:** this is one legacy that the anthropology of the lexicon has left to dialectology: a word list is not an inventory of labels, but a ledger of a local taxonomy) (**Course 2, 'Chinese characters rewriting a taxonomy', here acquires a spoken version.**
:::

::: definition Basic words and cultural words {#def-jiben}
**The received account** (**comparative research divides words into two layers: 'basic words', such as parts of the body, kin, numbers, actions and natural objects, and 'cultural words', such as artefacts, institutions, foods and abstract nouns**) (three uses) **① Basic words are used to judge how close a relationship is** (**they are replaced slowly, and so they carry more weight on a word list**) **② Cultural words are used to judge contact** (**a batch of cognate names for things spreads along the trade routes, and is more honest than the record in any history**) (**The received account:** **this chapter's suggestion:** make two lists, and report them separately; **to add them up into one 'percentage of similarity' is to merge two ledgers into one**) (the same thing was said when the previous chapter summed up its audit) **③ A word list must record the sound and must also record the use** (**a correct record of a word includes who will say it, on what occasion it is said, and its register colouring**) (**record the word and not the register, and old-school words are written down as words in general use, so that thirty years later someone takes your record as proof of a usage that never existed**) (**this is this lesson's application, in dialectology, of the point about 'archival luck':** your notes will be taken by later people as evidence, and you will be unable to set the matter straight for them) (**Chapter 10 will turn this sentence into a working procedure.**
:::

::: definition Four kinds of grammatical difference {#def-silei}
**① Function words** (**negation, aspect, place, the plural and sentence-final mood: each dialect has a set of its own;** this is where dialects are least 'equivalent') **② Constructions** (**the patterns of comparison, disposal, the passive, the dative and interrogation are each different**) (**The received account:** the order of the two objects, and the preposition of the comparative sentence, are the two points cited most often in the grammar of the dialects) **③ Word order and position** (**adverbs placed after, the position of the adverbial, and the way a number of dialects say 'do this first'**) (one item on Yue (Cantonese) in the previous chapter) **④ Stress and rhythm** (**the layer that has been studied least;** and it is precisely what an outsider can tell apart at a single hearing) (**The received account:** **this lesson gives one ranking:** the closer a difference lies to the skeleton of the sentence, the harder it is — **constructions are harder than function words, function words are harder than words, and words are harder than names**) (**this ranking is the working-out of that sentence in Chapter 1, “语法差异最有诊断力” ("Grammatical differences have the greatest diagnostic power.") (Our translation.);** **and the corollary is unsettling:** what we record most easily (the word list) is exactly what is least able to prove a relationship.
:::

::: definition Can grammar be borrowed {#def-jie}
**The received account** (**the traditional statement holds that grammatical structure is rarely borrowed, whereas, in the discussions of contact linguistics, areas that have long been bilingual do indeed show the copying of patterns**) (**this lesson joins the two statements into one proposition that can be tested**) (**the order of borrowing is: content words → function words → patterns; and for a pattern to be copied, three conditions are usually required**) (**a large bilingual population, a great disparity in the prestige of the common language, and two speeches whose types were already close**) (**in the later twentieth century these three were met almost entirely in the coastal dialect areas of China**) (**and so a new object appears:** a form of spoken language that carries dialect grammar while its vocabulary comes from the common language, usually called 'Such-and-such-place Putonghua') (**This lesson's definition:** it is not 'spoken short of the standard', but a variety in the course of formation: its phonology is local, its vocabulary is shared, and its grammar is mixed) (**please read this sentence together with the first six chapters:** where dialectologists used to describe dialects, they now need to describe this intermediate speech, and intermediate speech is the hardest to write down, because no one admits that it is a language) (**Chapter 9 will take this sentence as its main question.**
:::

::: example Designing a set of six items {#ex-liuti}
You are to examine aspect, and the pattern of comparison, in an unfamiliar dialect. Design six elicitation items, and for each say why it is designed in that way, and how to avoid an 'answer by translation'.

::: solution
**First, the method that fails** (**read a Putonghua sentence aloud to him, and ask him to 'say it once in your home-town speech': that is to ask him to translate, and the target of the translation is the sentence he thinks you want**) (**this lesson calls this 'inducing a normative answer', and it is the chief source of error in a survey of dialect grammar**) (**Six elicitation items**) (**① Continuous action**) (**show him a stretch of action in progress, and ask “他现在在做什么” ("What is he doing now?") (Our translation.);** this examines the durative marker) (**② Just completed**) (**ask “他刚才做完了什么” ("What did he finish doing just now?") (Our translation.);** this examines the perfective marker and its negative) (**③ An experience that has been had**) (**ask “他这辈子做过这件事吗” ("Has he done this at any time in his life?") (Our translation.);** this examines the experiential aspect) (**④ Ability and possibility**) (**ask “他做得动吗” ("Can he manage to do it?") (Our translation.) and “他能不能做” ("Is he able to do it?") (Our translation.);** this examines the potential complement and its negative form) (**⑤ Two things compared**) (**set two things out and ask him to compare them in size or in dearness and cheapness;** this examines the comparative pattern and the comparative marker) (**⑥ Giving someone a thing**) (**actually hand the thing over, and say a sentence;** this examines the dative, the order of the double object, and the verb of giving) (**Shared principles of design**) (**every item gives an observable scene and a question that has to be answered, so that the respondent is placed where he 'has to say something', not where the question is 'whether or not to translate';** and every item must be able to draw out both an affirmative and a negative form, because the negative is often irregular, and the most irregular place is the one with the greatest value for comparison) (**The received account:** this is a point that historical-comparative linguistics has long known: an exception is a window onto the system) (Marking) (if three or more of the six items are of the sort 'read a sentence and have him repeat after you', this question fails) (the remaining marks go to 'at the time of the record, also writing down the register and the speaker's age') (word list, ③, above: the same rule applies equally in a survey of grammar.
:::

::: intuition The accent is in the ear, the grammar is on the tongue {#int-tou}
**An everyday observation** (**hearing someone speak Putonghua with a local colour, we can often tell his home town, even when his tones are already very like Putonghua**) (**This lesson's explanation**) (**① What he has kept is the function words, the word order and the rhythm**) (**these things have no counterpart that could be put in their place, and they cannot be covered up by 'changing the word'**) (**phonology can be borrowed, vocabulary can be exchanged, and the skeleton of the sentence cannot move house**) (**② Grammar is therefore the visible face of the substratum**) (**in research on language contact, 'substratum' means the structure left behind by the language that has been replaced;** and in a person, the substratum is his grammar) (**this point answers the one in Chapter 1:** 'substratum' is an inference, and a person's grammar is what is observed; first record the differences in the latter, and only then discuss the causes of the former) (**③ A corollary that is of use to oneself**) (**what is hardest to change, when a person learns a foreign language, is precisely these three things;** this is why an adult's foreign language sounds as if it 'has a mother tongue', while the stock of words has long since gone beyond the textbook) (**this lesson writes the point here so that the technique of this chapter may be joined to your own body:** you are not describing a 'rustic speech'; you are describing something that everyone does, and that no one has been taught to do — **speaking with two systems**) (**in the next chapter this sentence becomes a question of policy.**
:::

::: history From word lists to constructions {#sec-cihui}
Around the first century BC (a handbook of local words took as its frame “同一件事各地叫什么” ("What does each place call the same thing?") (Our translation.); The received account: every method of lexical study in this chapter grows out of that one question) (② The 1950s) (the national survey of dialects used a unified word-list form, and for the first time made comparison across regions possible) (The received account: this lesson's assessment: the real contribution of a survey by word list is not the words, but comparability — it put one and the same set of questions to people from ten provinces) (in a social survey this is called the design of a questionnaire; in linguistics it is called luck) (③ The 1970s—1980s) (the study of dialect grammar gradually became a line of work; The received account: this lesson gives an institutional explanation: the study of grammar came late, because it requires the investigator himself to be able to judge whether a pattern is grammatical, and that needs a feel for the language together with training; writing the sounds down needs only an ear) (④ The 1980s—1990s) (monographs and articles on dialect grammar grew in number, and comparative studies appeared that were compiled by grammatical topic (aspect, disposal, the passive, interrogation)) (the received account) (⑤ The 2000s) (corpora and dialect databases; at the same time, dialect word lists on the internet were formed out of contributions from users, and both their quality and their coverage were extremely uneven) (The received account: one warning from this lesson: a word list contributed by users will take 'words someone is willing to write down' for 'words in local use', and what people are willing to write down tends to be the strange batch) (the rule of marking survivors down to eighty percent applies here in full) (⑥ The present and the future) (the Language Resources Protection Project collects long stretches of speech, and for the first time supplies natural data for research in syntax) (The received account: the testable prediction of this chapter: once long stretches of spoken data can be searched, several rows of this chapter's table on which 'constructions are the hardest' will be overturned; and that is a good thing — it shows that our table is temporary, and only a temporary table is a table that can be checked) (this is the sentence that this curriculum has written again and again in Chapter 2, in Chapter 6, and in Chapter 2 of this course.

::: exercise Short essay: what can be borrowed and what cannot {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “语言接触里有一种朴素的等级：东西先过来，名字跟过来，最后才轮到格式。这个等级是真实的，也是可被推翻的——它更像是对时间与双语人口数量的记录，而不是一条语法结构的天然禁区。二十世纪后期的中国沿海方言提供了一个大型实验：共同语通过屏幕进入每一个家庭，其强度超过历史上任何一次接触；结果不是方言被替换，而是方言被抽走句式、留下腔调。假如这个判断成立，那么将来我们能记录的‘方言’，将主要是口音的目录。” In language contact there is a plain hierarchy: things come over first, names follow them, and only at the last is it the turn of patterns. This hierarchy is real, and it can also be overturned — it is more like a record of time and of the number of bilingual speakers than a natural forbidden zone in grammatical structure. The coastal dialects of China in the later twentieth century supply a large experiment: the common language entered every household through the screen, at an intensity greater than that of any contact in history; the result was not that the dialects were replaced, but that the constructions were drawn out of the dialects and the lilt was left behind. If this judgement holds, then the 'dialects' we shall be able to record in future will be chiefly a catalogue of accents. (Our translation.) You must draw on at least six items: **the three kinds of difference**, **basic words and cultural words**, **the four kinds of grammatical difference and their ranking by hardness**, **the order of borrowing, and its conditions**, **intermediate speech**, **the bias of corpora and of contributed word lists**; and you must give a verdict: **how far this prediction is open to refutation (cite one study that you have not yet done, that you could finish within two years, and that would be enough, if it came out false, to overturn the prediction)**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms; the verdict (an acceptable answer must give one study that can be carried out, for example: ① a comparison, on the scale of a full-length novel, of natural speech from two age-groups in the same community (if it can be shown that the difference in constructions between the generations of 1990 and of 2015 does not exceed the difference in vocabulary, the prediction is weakened) (the crux is that it must be natural speech and not elicited sentences, because sentences got by elicitation will call the dialect grammar out); ② a comparison of the grammatical annotation of subtitles in dialect television dramas (if works of recent years keep the constructions and only change the words, then 'the screen draws the constructions out' does not hold; ③ a comparison of the same dialect in an immigrant community and in the home community (**if the immigrant community keeps the constructions and the home community loses them, the mechanism changes from 'intensity of contact' to 'intervention by the school', and the two can be told apart**) (**what the marking looks at is whether a mechanism that can be told apart has been proposed, not the attitude alone**); if the conclusion can be written “口音的目录是一部分真理；更准确的说法是：我们将拥有两种东西——一种用于亲密场合的、语法被削薄的变体，和一种用于公共场合的、带当地腔调的共同语。方言学要做的，是在削薄发生之前把完整的一层记下来，因为削薄不可逆，而记录可以等” ("A catalogue of accents is part of the truth; the more accurate statement is this: we shall have two things — a variety used on intimate occasions, whose grammar has been thinned, and a common language used on public occasions, carrying a local lilt. What dialectology has to do is to record the complete layer before the thinning happens, because thinning is irreversible, while a record can wait.") (Our translation.), that is an A.

::: summary
The accounts of the two layers: vocabulary (**replacement, shift, gap; shift is the one most apt to disguise itself as sameness**), lists (**basic words and cultural words reported separately, and never entered on one table**), grammar (**constructions > function words > words > names**), borrowing (**the hierarchy of content words, function words and patterns is in fact a function of time and of the number of bilingual speakers**), intermediate speech (**a local phonology, a shared vocabulary, a mixed grammar**), questioning (**do not let him translate; give him a question he cannot help but answer**).
