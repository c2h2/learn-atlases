title: "Cantonese and Yue"
---

The previous chapter said that whether a form of speech can be given a written form depends on whether there is an industry that bills in it; this chapter is the only complete case of that proposition — **Yue (Cantonese for the speech of Guangzhou and Hong Kong) is the only variety among the dialects of Chinese that, in the twentieth century, possessed records, films, subtitles and song lyrics, so its status was not given by policy, and for that reason it may at any time move away together with the industry**.

## Definition: the ledger of the tones {#def-shengdiao}

::: definition Nine tones, six values {#def-jiusheng}
**Mainstream account.** **It is often said of the speech of Guangzhou that it has "nine tones and six tone values".** This is not a play on words, but one act of bookkeeping. **① Each of the four ancient tones is divided, according as it is voiceless or voiced, into yin and yang, giving six categories of unchecked tone; ② syllables of the ancient entering tone (checked tone) are divided again into three classes by the length of the main vowel, or by the conditions on the coda, forming upper, mid and lower entering.** Mainstream account: **the pitches of the three entering tones are respectively the same as those of three unchecked tones; they merely carry one stop coda in addition.** So "nine categories and six pitches" are true at the same time. **This lesson's teaching point.** This is the dialect instance of that line from Course 3, "categories and phonetic values are two ledgers"; **people learning a dialect often go wrong here, because what they count is the pitch they themselves hear, whereas what dialectology counts is splits and mergers.** Remember this, and you can deal with any dispute of the form "how many tones does such-and-such a dialect have": **first ask the other party what they are classifying by, then ask whether what is being counted is tone categories or tone values.** This question is the first tool this chapter wants you to take away.
:::

::: definition Codas and vowels {#def-yunwei}
**Yue has kept the pairing complete.** **Nasal codas -m and -n stand, as a matched set, opposite stop codas -p and -t, and to these are added laryngeal endings, nasal and stop.** Mainstream account: **its historical significance is obvious at a glance:** other dialects have knocked out half the cells, and Yue has kept both halves. **This lesson at once adds two sentences of restriction, for otherwise it becomes the "fossil theory".** ① Keeping them does not mean they are as they were. Yue's vowel system, its compound vowels and its initials have each undergone their own changes; **some descriptions treat its vowels as an opposition of long and short, and this is an important step in the analysis, not a retelling of the "ancient sound" (mainstream account). ② Keeping them also has a cost.** **Keeping the stop codas makes the end of the syllable tense, and thereby affects connected speech and the way loanwords are adapted.** That law of equilibrium from Course 3 applies here: **Yue is not the more conservative; what it has done is to leave the complexity on the codas, whereas Mandarin (*guanhua*) has moved the complexity over to tone and prosody.** Hence the first comparison in this chapter: **Wu has dropped the codas and kept the voiced sounds; Mandarin has dropped both and developed the neutral tone and rhotacisation (*erhua*); Yue has kept both and raised the number of tones to nine —** not one of the three paths is "progress".
:::

::: definition Five grammatical points {#def-wudian}
**This lesson uses five short examples to show that "grammatical differences are harder than lexical differences" (that line from Chapter 1).** ① Aspect markers. Perfective, durative and experiential each have a marker of their own; **these look as if they corresponded neatly to the 了, 着 and 过 of Putonghua (standard Mandarin), but in actual use they do not coincide.** Mainstream account: **one lesson to be drawn: equivalence in translation is not equivalence in grammar. ② "先" placed after.** **The adverb that means "do this first" is placed after the verb.** This is a word order rare among the dialects of Chinese, and it is also one of the most obvious markers of Yue identity (mainstream account). ③ Comparison uses "过". Patterns of the type "甲高过乙" ('A is taller than B'); **its source is "超过" ('to surpass'), and it belongs among this chapter's cases of grammaticalisation (the dialect version of the history of Chinese in Chapter 9). ④ The dative and the double object.** **The order of the two objects of verbs of giving differs from Putonghua.** Mainstream account: **differences of this kind do not appear in a word list; they can be seen only from the whole sentence —** so an investigation must elicit whole sentences (③ above). ⑤ The plural marker. The personal pronoun is followed by a plural element. **It forms a contrast with the "辈", "等" and "们" of a number of dialects, and it is a point often cited in historical comparison.** Mainstream account. **This lesson gathers the five things into one sentence:** the difference between Yue and Putonghua runs deeper at the syntactic level than at the lexical level; **and differences at the lexical level are the easiest to see, and also the least valuable for classification —** that is the distance between dialectology and the "dialect joke".
:::

::: definition Dialect characters {#def-yongzi}
**Yue has a batch of characters used only in Yue.** **Common words that denote things, denote negation, denote place, denote the plural and denote aspect each have their character.** Mainstream account: **the social fact at this layer is the most interesting one in this chapter:** a language with no official standard has grown a folk orthography, and among its users it can be read and understood. **Three mechanisms.** ① Sound-borrowing characters. Take a ready-made character of the same sound and use it to write the word; the price is that the meaning of the word is dragged off elsewhere. ② Semantic-phonetic compounding. Coin a new character from a semantic component plus a phonetic component; this is the ordinary mode of production of the Chinese-character system. ③ Borrowing and gloss-reading (*xùndú* 训读). Write a common character close in meaning, and read it the Yue way. **Mainstream account:** these three are exactly isomorphic with the sound-recording characters, the phonetic loans and the popular characters taught in Course 1; **in other words,** characters used for a dialect are not an exception to the Chinese script, but the ordinary productive capacity of Chinese characters unfolding where a standard is lacking. **This lesson's verdict.** An outsider looking at written Yue feels that "字都写错了" ('the characters are all written wrong'). This reaction is an accurate grammatical judgement plus a wrong premise: it is not Putonghua written wrong, but the writing of another language; **and it "looks wrong" because there is no dictionary with the authority to declare it correct —** the next section sets out why that dictionary has, to this day, never appeared.
:::

::: definition Status given by industry {#def-chanye}
**Mainstream account.** **In the second half of the twentieth century, Yue acquired, by way of records, film and television, a kind of public existence that the other dialects of Chinese have not had.** This lesson's analysis in four layers. **①** Conventions for recording the sounds arose with the industry. Over several decades, subtitle teams, song lyrics and the usage of the newspapers formed a way of writing that can be used again: **this is a spontaneous orthography, and its normative force is the volume of sales. ②** Prestige can come the other way. Putonghua has absorbed a batch of Yue words and expressions. **Mainstream account: a number of colloquial words originating in Yue entered, around the 1990s, the layer in common use in northern colloquial speech.** This is a rare direction in the history of the Chinese language: **outside the axis "common language — dialect", there is also the axis "rich dialect — poor dialect".** The exchange rate of Chapter 8 is at its clearest here. **③ Song lyrics are a kind of language engineering.** **Yue has nine categories of tone and strict codas, so the lyrics must match tone to melody, and this forms the craft rule "依字上腔" (*yī zì shàng qiāng*, 'the tune follows the character').** Mainstream account: **the cultural consequence is that the phonological constraints of a form of speech become the standard of a craft;** a person who cannot tell the tones apart cannot write lyrics. **This is how industry turns Chinese historical phonology into a requirement of the job. ④** When the industry moves away, status moves with it. Mainstream account: after the turn of the century, the centre of the film, television and music industries moved north, the population structure of the Pearl River Delta changed sharply, and Putonghua became the common language of markets and factories. **This lesson's conclusion:** the public status of Yue is a by-product of industry, and not a gift of policy; **anything that is a by-product becomes a burden when the industry leaves —** this is a sentence that will be played out again when the next chapter discusses Min, except that the carrier there is not industry but the hometowns of overseas Chinese.
:::

::: definition A dispute around 2010 {#def-2010}
**Mainstream account.** **Around 2010, because of adjustments to the arrangements for the language of television and for the language of teaching in schools, Guangzhou saw public discussion and expression by its citizens on a fairly large scale.** This lesson does not comment on the justifiability of what each side maintained; it records only four structures. **①** The object of the dispute was the channel, not the pronunciation. What was fought over was public airtime and the language of instruction, that is, what the previous chapter called "陆军与海军" ('the army and the navy'). **② The participants were chiefly the urban middle class.** **They treated Yue as cultural capital rather than as a tool of communication;** this point is extremely important: **a movement in the name of "preservation", whose main force consisted of people who no longer needed this form of speech in order to find work. ③** The grammar of the demand exposes the mechanism. The slogans of the time were mostly of the sort "保护我们的语言" ('protect our language'); **and "我们的" ('our') is where its strength lies, and also its limit:** it defines a form of speech as the mark of a local identity, rather than defining the people who speak it as users who need a service. **The consequence: demands of this kind find it hard to win a budget of the bilingual-education sort, and can win only programme slots. ④** Neither side was satisfied with the result. The channels did not change; the attitude did: **a number of studies have noticed that thereafter young people's identification with the dialect rose instead, while their rate of use fell —** symbolic support and an actual shift of use can grow at the same time. **This lesson takes this as a law of the whole course:** when a language becomes cultural heritage, its everyday function has usually already come to an end; **heritage is something that is commemorated, not something that is used.**
:::

::: example Five words, or a grammar {#ex-wuge}
Below is a common piece of written Yue (**佢哋话冇嘢食，我只好在楼下买咗啲嘢；你放低啲嘢先去；呢度太挤，企唔到**). Complete three tasks: **① explain the function words and the pronouns word by word; ② point out the differences in it that belong to the grammatical level rather than to the lexical level; ③ explain why this passage cannot be counted as "Putonghua with the characters written wrong".**

::: solution
**① Word by word.** **"他们、说、没有、东西吃、我、只、在、楼下、买了些东西；你放下那些东西再走；这里太挤，站不住"** ('they, say, have not, things to eat, I, only, at, downstairs, bought some things; you put those things down and then go; it is too crowded here, one cannot keep standing') (Our translation.) — a meaning of that sort. Function words and pronouns each have a form of their own, and place words, too, are two separate sets. **Mainstream account. ② There are three differences at the grammatical level.** **First, locative pronouns and the demonstrative system form a set of their own, and cannot be derived from the Putonghua word list; second, the aspect marker after the verb, and the position of adverbs of the "再走" ('then go') type; third, the negative forms of ability and of the potential form are different.** Note that none of these three is "a different word was used"; each is "the same meaning was put into a different pattern" — **this is what Chapter 1 means by a grammatical difference being a hard difference. ③ Why it is not wrong characters.** **Because it can be translated sentence by sentence, it is internally consistent, it has a stable convention for which characters to use, and its grammar is not the grammar of Putonghua.** One criterion: **if you can replace every word, one for one, with a Putonghua word and need not change any word order, then it is only Putonghua carrying dialect words; this example, however, reads smoothly only when the word order and the function words are changed —** that is the writing of another language. **Marking.** If, of the three grammatical differences, you point only to vocabulary, you are marked as having passed; **if you write down the test "it is not smooth until the word order is changed", you are marked A** — **it is a tool that can be applied on the spot between any two varieties, and it is cheaper than any dispute about "whether or not it is a dialect".**
:::
:::

::: intuition Why some people learn a dialect from song lyrics {#int-geci}
**Observation.** **The most common path by which an adult learns Yue is not the classroom but song lyrics.** This lesson's explanation. **① Lyrics give whole chunks of material that can be memorised.** **And what language acquisition needs is precisely the whole chunk;** a dialect textbook gives a word list; the order of a textbook is the alphabet, the order of a song is feeling. **② Tone is locked in by the melody.** **A song whose tune is fixed requires the tones of the words set to it to match, and so the learner is forced to distinguish tone categories;** this is training in phonology that industry completes on the school's behalf. **③ Lyrics contain registers that ought not to go into a textbook.** **Colloquial function words, slang, love and complaint;** and it is precisely this layer that decides whether a person can speak at the dinner table. **From this the lesson draws a general conclusion.** The written language of a variety is often not in the dictionary, but on the carriers that are most looked down on: lyrics, subtitles, text messages and shop signs; **this holds both for Chapter 2 and for Chapter 9 —** so this course requires that, when you make a phonetic transcription, besides collecting stories told by elders you also collect the text messages of the young: the former give you the depth of the system, the latter the present state of the system. **The two often contradict each other, and the contradiction is itself the conclusion.**
:::

::: history From the port to karaoke {#sec-yue}
**The eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.** **The confinement of trade to a single port, and the opening of the ports that followed, brought the spoken language of the Pearl River Delta into foreign trade and into documents.** Mainstream account: **this lesson emphasises only one point:** the first to compile dictionaries and grammars for Yue were a group of people who needed to deal with merchants, and their genre has had its influence to this day. ② **After the middle of the nineteenth century.** **Chinese people went abroad to work and to reside overseas, and Yue became the common spoken language of the early Chinese communities of Southeast Asia and North America.** Mainstream account: community speech based chiefly on the accents of certain counties and cities of the Pearl River Delta continued for several decades in a number of countries. **Chapter 21, on Chinese speech overseas, joins this chapter here.** ③ The beginning of the twentieth century. Records of Cantonese opera, and films, appeared. **Mainstream account:** a form of speech acquired, in print and on the stage, a pattern that could be repeated, and from then on it had "a way of speaking that can be imitated". ④ **The 1950s–1970s.** **The other end:** Hong Kong became the centre of Yue film and television and of popular music. **Mainstream account:** this is the only time in the history of the dialects of Chinese that a dialect has possessed a distribution industry reaching across regions. ⑤ **The 1980s–1990s.** **Yue words and expressions entered northern colloquial speech along with audio-visual recordings, catering and commerce; at the same time, "learning Yue" became an investment in a career.** Mainstream account: **this lesson calls this a brief inversion of the exchange rate.** ⑥ The 2000s to the present. The centre of industry moved north, the population structure of the Pearl River Delta changed, public discussion turned on channels and the language of instruction, and there was the phonetic transcription carried out by projects for the protection of language resources. **Mainstream account.** This lesson's closing: **the history of Yue tells us that a dialect can gain status within fifty years, and can also lose it within fifty years, and that neither time was decided by scholars;** so the last word this course has to say on the protection of dialects is in Chapter 9, and it is not about recording.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in this chapter
**Taking "nine tones" to amount to complexity** (**first ask whether what is counted is tone categories or tone values (Nine tones, six values, above)**); **"Kept complete, and therefore a living fossil"** (**it has left the complexity in the codas, while the vowels have changed on their own (the two restrictions on the codas, above)**); **Taking written Yue for wrong characters** (**the criterion is whether one-for-one replacement is possible without changing the word order (③ of the example, above)**); **Taking protection to be commemoration** (**symbolic support and an actual shift of use can rise at the same time (④ of the dispute, above):** this point is this chapter's warning to every piece of work that sets out to protect.
:::

::: exercise A bookkeeping exercise {level=1}
Using material you know, list the tone categories and the tone values of Yue (or of the dialect of the place where you are), and explain why the two numbers differ.

::: solution
Marking: **the two words "tone category" and "tone value" must be distinguished**, and **one instance must be given of "same category, different value" or "same value, different category" (Nine tones, six values, above);** anyone who gives only one number is marked as not having mastered this tool.
:::
:::

::: exercise Find three patterns {level=2}
From the lyrics or the subtitles of Yue songs, find three grammatical patterns (any of aspect, comparison, and the position of an adverb), translate them into Putonghua, and write down the places you had to change.

::: solution
Marking: **"the places that had to be changed" must be written out as a change of word order or of function words**; **writing only that the vocabulary differs counts as a fail (② of the example, above);** this is the method of "asking three sentences" from Chapter 1, put to actual use.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: protection of the industrial kind {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**粤语的经验提出一种保护模式：不必先有政策，只要有一门话能挣钱的地方——唱片、影视、餐饮、直播。工业式保护的速度远快于学校，代价是它只保存工业需要的部分：好听的声音、可用的俚语、可以印在T恤上的身份；至于枯燥的语法、乡土的词汇与不能变现的语域，工业并不欠它们什么。因此用文化工业保护方言，本质上是一次编辑：它把一种语言剪成一个形象**". **The experience of Yue proposes a model of protection: there is no need for a policy first, so long as there is a place where a form of speech can earn money — records, film and television, catering, livestreaming. Protection of the industrial kind is far faster than the school; the price is that it keeps only the part that industry needs: sounds pleasant to hear, slang that can be used, an identity that can be printed on a T-shirt; as for dry grammar, the vocabulary of the native place, and registers that cannot be turned into money, industry owes them nothing. Using the culture industry to protect a dialect is therefore, in essence, an act of editing: it cuts a language down into an image.** (Our translation.) You must call on at least six items: **Nine tones, six values**, **Codas and vowels**, **Five grammatical points**, **Dialect characters**, **Status given by industry**, **the four structures of 2010**; and you must give a ruling: **faced with the price of being "cut into an image", should the recorders and the community accept this transaction or refuse it (state your conditions)**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms; the ruling (**you must not give fifty blows to each of the two sides;** an acceptable answer should be given in conditional form: **accept, but attach two obligations:** ① **the bias in the material industry selects must be made up by another activity** (**record the grammar, the word lists of the native place, and the registers that are not respectable;** this is the only way of working that can offset the editing, and it is also the reason for all of this lesson's training in phonetic transcription); ② people inside the community must be enabled to command the channels for turning it into money (**if a form of speech earns money only on someone else's platform, then the shape it is cut into is decided by the platform;** **when the paper is marked, what is looked for is whether this point has been thought of —** it puts "who benefits" inside the definition of protection. (**Refutability.** An excellent paper will also point out that the transferability of this model is limited: **Yue's condition is that it has a consumer population, abroad and abroad, in the tens of millions, and an autonomous system of distribution;** most dialects do not have this, so for them industry is not an option but luck. **This sentence is what stops the Yue case from being taken as advice for policy.**) If the conclusion can be written "**形象保护的是可听见的部分，记录保护的是不可让渡的部分；一门话要活下去需要两样：一个值得说的理由，和一个不必换语言也能活着的岗位——前者工业可以给，后者只有机会结构能给**" — "**What the image protects is the part that can be heard; what the record protects is the part that cannot be alienated; for a form of speech to go on living, two things are needed: a reason worth speaking for, and a post at which one can keep alive without changing language — the former, industry can give; the latter, only the structure of opportunity can give**" (Our translation.) — that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The Yue file: tones (**first ask what the classification is by**), codas (**the complexity is left at the ending**), grammar (**hard differences in five places; the word list is the level of the dialect joke**), characters (**an orthography with no dictionary**), industry (**status is given by the volume of sales, and it moves away with the sales as well**), the present (**being made into heritage, and shift of use, happen at the same time**).
