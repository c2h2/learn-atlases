The first eight chapters teach you to recognise and to record; this chapter asks a harder question — **if a variety is no longer used by anyone to earn a living, how much can all the goodwill about it amount to. This chapter's stance is to do the accounts before talking of feeling**.

## Definition: how language shift happens {#def-zhuanyong}

::: definition Three stages and one indicator {#def-sanjie}
**The received account** (**Language shift is usually described in three stages: ① bilingualism across the whole area, the two varieties each governing one set of occasions; ② the common language enters every public occasion outside the family, and the dialect (*fangyan*; topolect) is left only with the intimate register; ③ parents no longer pass the dialect on to their children (**The third stage is a measurable event (An international assessment framework that is often cited ranks 'intergenerational transmission' as the foremost factor in language vitality (The received account (This lesson gives it a sharper form (The fate of a variety does not depend on how many people speak it, but on how many seven-year-olds hear it used as the default form of address (Why this is harder than the number of users: numbers are inflated by the size of the population base; the home register of seven-year-old children is not (An optimistic statistic often misread ('There are still several tens of millions speaking it' and 'ten-odd years from now it will no longer be passed on' can perfectly well be true at the same time; this is what this chapter calls 'late-onset extinction' (After a variety has become heritage, it can usually live on for twenty years, long enough to finish a documentary and hold three meetings (This lesson requires that, when you later see figures of the kind 'so many tens of thousands are still speaking it', your first reaction is to ask: to whom they are speaking.
:::

::: definition Why giving up is rational {#def-lixing}
**This chapter rejects every account that attributes shift to 'backward ideas', and instead gives three mechanisms** (**① The exchange price** (**How much education, work and medical care a variety can be exchanged for determines its place in the household budget;** the price of the common language on this table is fixed by examinations and by recruitment, and neither of those two discusses the dialect (**② The switching cost is borne by the family alone** (**A switch that must be completed on entering school, and nobody reimburses it;** rational parents therefore buy it out in advance (**This is the most important point in this chapter:** it is not that the school prohibits the dialect, but that the school provides only one track, so the family is forced to subtract (**③ Prestige is indivisible** (**It is not that parents find the dialect no good; they are unwilling for the child to be recorded, in an assessment, as 'a person with an accent' (**Chapter 1 and Chapter 4 converge here (**Taken together, the three mechanisms give this chapter's judgement on policy:** any measure that cannot alter these three will not change transmission, and will only change the talk about transmission (**The next section turns this sentence into a table.**
:::

::: definition Documentation, symbol, domain and opportunity {#def-leixing}
**① Documentation** (**sound transcription, grammatical description, dictionaries, sound archives;** almost bound to succeed, because it requires nobody to change their behaviour; what it changes is the knowledge of people in the future, not the choices of people now (② Symbolic status (dialect programmes, signboards, the lists of intangible cultural heritage, metro stop-announcements in two varieties; it can raise attitude scores, and usually does not change use (This chapter's law (from the same source as the one in Chapter 5: symbolic support and actual shift can rise at the same time; when a variety is spoken only while it is being commemorated, it has completed its conversion into a cultural relic (③ Occupying domains (mother-tongue or dialect courses, interpreting in judicial and medical settings, local administration that permits its use; it does change use, but it needs concessions on budget, authorised establishment and assessment — this is its real cost, and also the reason it is often exchanged for ② (④ Changing the opportunity structure (making a variety a condition of entry to some kind of work: cross-border trade, local medical care, early-childhood education, and the local-production requirements of regional media; this is the only type which the historical record has shown can turn transmission around, and it does not usually come under the department in charge of language (This lesson's core judgement (The first three types change 'what is said about language'; the fourth type changes language; and the great majority of dialect policies belong to the first three, not out of indifference, but because the fourth type does not lie within the powers of the authority that issues the document (This sentence is the hardest to hear in this course, and the one most worth remembering: if a department cannot decide conditions of recruitment and the timetable, it can only state a position.
:::

::: definition Protection is not prohibition {#def-jinzh}
**A logical clarification** (**'Protecting dialects' and 'promoting Putonghua (standard Mandarin)' are not zero-sum;** what is to be reduced has never been ability in the common language, but the situation of having to be able to speak only one variety (**Three corollaries (**① Bilingualism is not an additive obstacle (The received account: mainstream research does not support the common worry that 'learning a dialect hinders learning the common language' (This lesson's neutral formulation: if it were true, we ought to be able to see systematic differences of academic attainment between dialect areas and the intensity of teaching in the common language, whereas the direction actually observed does not support it (② What is truly scarce is time (Cram two writing systems into sixteen hours a week, and one side is bound to be squeezed down to the spoken layer; this is a problem of timetabling, not a problem of ability (To call it a problem of ability is the kind of substitution this chapter opposes (③ Institutional asymmetry (The received account: the relevant laws and regulations of this country have special provisions on ethnic minorities' use of their own languages and scripts, while the dialects of Chinese are not among the objects of those provisions; thus 'contending for one course for the dialect' has, in the language of administration, no application item to correspond to it (This lesson's value judgement on this fact is zero: it is only a constraint; and any protection scheme that does not mention it will fail, in the first year, at 'having nowhere to apply'.
:::

::: example Auditing a protection scheme {#ex-fangan}
Below are the six items of a fictional 'implementation scheme for protecting the dialects' in some locality: **① set up a leading group for the work of protecting the dialects; ② on the television station, keep one dialect programme and increase its airtime; ③ compile a dialect dictionary and a reader; ④ in some primary schools, open a weekly interest class in local culture, which pupils attend voluntarily; ⑤ build a sound database of the dialect; ⑥ require taxi drivers to 'be able to greet passengers in the dialect, so as to improve the image of the city'.** Classify them by the four types of intervention, and for each item write what it changes, who pays the cost, and its typical way of failing.

::: solution
**Classification** (**① belongs to organisation rather than to intervention (**what it changes is the circulation of documents**); ② symbol; ③ documentation; ④ domain (**a weak form of domain**); ⑤ documentation; ⑥ symbol disguised as an opportunity structure (**③ and ⑤ are the two most honest items (they do not require other people to change their behaviour, and the results can be checked; their way of failing is that nobody uses them (The received view: the rate at which archives are used lies far below the cost of building them; this is inherent in the work of documentation, not a defect (The way ② fails is the most typical (the programme is retained while the audience ages: it serves people who can already speak, not the children who are being lost; and so what the budget buys is the sentence 'we have done it' (④ is the only item in this chapter that is worth more money, but its way of failing is written on the face of the question (the three words 'weekly', 'interest' and 'voluntary', taken together, equal zero compulsion, zero assessment, and zero link to progression into the next school; and Section 2 has already said that families allocate language according to assessment and recruitment; if it is to work, one of three things must be changed: put it on the timetable and assess it, tie it to some actual gain, or make the teaching post itself require ability in the dialect (these three correspond, respectively, to domain, opportunity and establishment (⑥ is this chapter's satirical target (it lays a linguistic duty on an occupational group with no power to bargain, and gives that group no resources to match; requiring other people to pay for your nostalgia is the commonest degenerate form of an intervention of the symbolic kind (The judgement on ① (a leading group is a paperwork device: it turns 'nobody is responsible' into 'somebody issues documents'; this lesson does not deny its use — it decides whether a budget can be allocated, which is to decide whether ③ and ⑤ can exist (Marking: whoever can sort the six items into two columns, according to 'does it change the language, or change what is said', gets an A for this chapter (and this way of sorting into columns can be carried straight across to any document on reading, writing, education or cultural policy — this is the general tool that this lesson gives to all ten lessons.
:::
:::

::: intuition Tuesday afternoon {#int-xingqi}
**This lesson gives one diagnostic, for testing any measure of language protection** (**whether it requires that someone, on some Tuesday afternoon, lose something because they cannot speak this variety**); **three explanations (**① Why Tuesday afternoon (because real language policy does not live in ceremonies, but lives in the attendance sheet: the job description, the timetable, the conditions of promotion, the procedure for receiving patients (② 'Losing something' must be concrete (a job, a consultation that is not interrupted, a service window at which one need not find a relative to interpret; what these three things share is that they have a cost (a measure without a cost is a gesture; the gesture is not in itself a bad thing, but it does not change transmission (③ This diagnostic is also a check on oneself (this course has you transcribe sounds, describe, and write papers; none of these will make anyone lose anything on a Tuesday afternoon; so the work of documentation is morally honest, and humble in its effect — this lesson holds that to admit as much is closer to the facts than to call documentation 'salvation' (Chapter 10 will give three things that are still worth doing under this humility (Let one sentence be set down here first: an archive is not a rescue; it is testimony.
:::

::: history Surveying and unifying at the same time {#sec-jinjin}
1950s (A nationwide survey of the dialects and the promotion of the common language started at the same time; the received account: this lesson's first judgement: this is not self-contradictory, but the two sides of one project: to know where to push, one must first know what there is (This explains why the institutionalisation of dialectology is the same age as the promotion of Putonghua (②1982 (The promotion of Putonghua for general use throughout the country was written into the Constitution (the received account (③1990s (A scholar raised, by the title of a book, the question “我们不知道为什么语言在消失” ('We do not know why languages are disappearing.') (Our translation.); from the 1990s, international organisations placed linguistic diversity on the agenda, and in the 2000s frameworks for assessing endangered languages appeared (the received account: this lesson takes the method and not the classification: this framework places intergenerational transmission first, which is exactly what turns policy from 'how many people speak it' towards 'to whom they are speaking' (④2000 (The Law on the Standard Spoken and Written Chinese Language was passed, establishing the legal status of the common language and retaining specified domains of use for the dialects (the received account (⑤2008—2010 (Public discussion of the language of television and of teaching appeared in Guangzhou and other places, the scale of it being wide-ranging urban expression in the manner of a movement (the received account: this lesson's two-point reading: first, those who took part were mainly of the urban middle class, which shows that the age of the dialect as cultural capital had already begun; second, the demands fell on channels rather than on posts and the timetable, which shows that it could not enter the fourth type of intervention (⑥2015 on (The Language Resources Protection Project made recordings at more than a thousand points; at the same time, urbanisation and the proportion of cross-regional marriages continued to rise (the received account: this chapter's closing judgement: the two things point in opposite directions, while the strength of both is increasing; so over the next twenty years we shall probably see archives unprecedentedly complete, and use unprecedentedly thin (This is not a prophecy; it is the mean that this chapter thinks the most likely (Chapter 10 discusses what an individual can do under a mean of that sort.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: what can be promised {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**Language protection is the only field of policy that openly admits it may fail: no government has ever succeeded in reversing a shift in the course of being completed, and the examples of failure are all counted by the century. Precisely for this reason, its ethics should not be built on 'it can certainly be kept', but on two things. First, commemoration must not be substituted for choice. Second, other people must not be made to pay the bill for your taste. What can still be done is to let those who are willing to speak have a place to speak, to let those who want to learn have the means to learn, and to record also the part that is unpleasant to hear**". One must draw on **the three stages and intergenerational transmission**, **the three mechanisms of giving up**, **the four types of intervention**, **protection is not prohibition**, **institutional asymmetry** and **archival fortune and testimony**, at least six items; and decide: **if you can persuade only one department, which do you choose, and what do you give up**.

::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms; the decision (one must not choose 'the Publicity Department adds one more programme', because that choice falls outside the second type and the fifth type of measure, and it also occupies the attention of the fourth type; a passing answer usually lands in one of two places: the education department (reason: it is the only department able to write a language into the timetable and into the requirements of teaching posts; the cost is to give up results of the symbolic kind, and to accept the political constraint 'the dialect does not enter the examinations'); or the human-resources and social-security authorities, and the departments in charge of the industries (reason: conditions of recruitment are real language policy; the cost is that the risk of discrimination must be accepted — once a given variety becomes a condition of a post, it becomes a tool for screening out people from elsewhere, and this is isomorphic with what you oppose (the marking looks at whether this backlash has been thought of**); what one gives up (**one must write explicitly that the aim 'make everyone love the dialect' is given up (**because love has already been shown not to bring transmission, while indicators of attitude conceal precisely this point**); if the conclusion can say '**So this chapter does not promise salvage, and promises only two things: not to lie (not to pass commemoration off as use) and not to shift the burden (not to let people with no power to bargain do your aesthetic judging for you). Within these two, documentation is honest, courses are expensive, and the opportunity structure cannot be asked for — and this is already the greatest sincerity a discipline can offer**', that is an A.
:::
