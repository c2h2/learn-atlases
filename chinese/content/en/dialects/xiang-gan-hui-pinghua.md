---
title: "Xiang, Gan, Hui and Pinghua"
---

The dialects of the previous chapters each have a base one can point to; the four areas of this chapter all live on a boundary — **the least stable strokes on a classification map are these, and the argument of this chapter is that every assumption of taxonomy shows itself at the boundary, so to summarise these four chapters is worse than writing them as four disputes**.

## Definition: why "between" is hardest {#def-zhijian}

::: definition Three treatments and their supporters {#def-sanzhong}
**For a place that lies between two groups, an atlas has only three ways of drawing it.** (**① Merge it into the nearer group** (**readers are satisfied, the units are fewer, and policy is easy to carry out;** the cost is writing transitional features down as "exceptions". (② **Set it up as a group of its own** (**this respects the feature bundle;** the cost is admitting that how many units there are depends on the researcher's preference, and that from then on one must defend the teaching materials and the funding for a group. (③ **Mark it as undecided** (**the most honest;** almost never adopted. (**This lesson's account of item ③ is worth remembering:** the readers of a map — textbooks, finance, local identity — all need a cell; **an empty cell is more unsettling than a wrong cell.** (**This is why the two characters 未定 ('undecided') are nowhere to be found on dialect maps, while research reports are full of the word.** (**Each of the four places in this chapter has had all three treatments applied to it in turn;** when you read one book assigning Huizhou speech to Wu and another listing it as a group of its own, what you are seeing is not a conflict in the data, but the three treatments in competition.
:::

::: definition Old Xiang and New Xiang {#def-xiang}
**Received account.** (**Xiang is often divided into two kinds, "old-style" (老派) and "new-style" (新派):** at a number of points Old Xiang (老湘语) retains a voiced pronunciation of the ancient fully voiced initials (全浊声母), or a pronunciation with a voiced colouring, while New Xiang (新湘语) has mostly devoiced, and of these Changsha speech is the closest to Mandarin (*guanhua*). (**Three readings.** (**① This is a Mandarinisation one can watch as it happens.** (**Over more than a hundred years the provincial capital has pushed its own dialect towards the Mandarin type, while the townships to the west and to the south have kept the old readings.** (**The previous chapter's line, "age replaces geography", here becomes "the provincial capital replaces the townships".** (② **Are the two words "old" and "new" a value judgement or a chronological judgement?** (**In dialectology they refer to features, not to relative merit;** but in everyday language 老湘语 sounds like "backward" — **this lesson requires you, when you write, to add one sentence, 此处老派指保留某特征 ('Here 老派 means that a certain feature is retained.' (Our translation.)). This is clarity at a very low cost.** (③ **The cause of the Xiang boundary.** (**The Dongting Lake plain and the Xiang River waterway are transport routes, not barriers;** repeated migration and commerce since the Qing have kept the dialects along the river aligning with Mandarin, while the mountain areas keep the old features. (Received account: this is the general rule of this chapter: the two sides of the water are alike, and the two sides of a mountain differ; so a dialect map read beside a topographic map has more explanatory power than one read beside administrative divisions. (Chapter 2's "lines coincide with mountains, rivers and provincial boundaries" here receives an answer that leans towards the terrain.
:::

::: definition The joining and splitting of Gan and Hakka {#def-gan}
**Received account.** (**Gan is distributed over central and northern Jiangxi and a number of neighbouring counties in Hunan, Hubei and Anhui; its relation to Hakka has long had two views, "join them as one group" and "each as a group of its own".** (**One feature often cited is this:** some characters with ancient fully voiced initials are now read aspirated, and the aspiration is not wholly constrained by the level-and-oblique condition (平仄). (**This stands in contrast with Mandarin's "aspirated in the level tone, unaspirated in the oblique tones".** (**Received account.** (**This lesson stresses three points.** (**① A single feature can set a group up, and can also take a group apart.** (**In the previous chapter Jin was set up on the entering tone (checked tone); if Hakka and Gan are merged on the condition of aspiration, the internal differences of the two groups have to be written as "subgroups" (片).** (② **Is the coincidence of a province's name and a dialect group chance, or is it method?** (**"赣" is the short name of Jiangxi;** when a dialect group is named after a province, scholars and the administration are working on the same map, and thereafter every formulation of "this province's dialect" treats the administrative boundary as a language boundary. (Read this against the Hakka of the previous chapter: Hakka refuses to be cut by provincial boundaries, whereas Gan is named by one provincial boundary. (③ The point on which Gan and Hakka part company is migration history. (Those who hold that they should be joined look at sound categories; those who hold that they should be split look at one emigration and settlement that can be ascertained; this is another performance of the three treatments from the opening of this chapter — and the audience is the local institutions applying for funds. They usually want themselves to be a group of their own.
:::

::: definition Hui: the dispute with the least of a base {#def-hui}
**Received account.** (**Hui refers to a number of dialects in the southern Anhui region, often described as "part like Wu, part like Gan, and with some innovations of its own besides"; whether it stands as a group of its own is one of the unsettled questions in Chinese dialectology.** (**Three things this lesson thinks worth remembering.** (**① Its features really are mixed.** (**Tone categories are many, the finals have a number of special types of nasalisation and of rhotacisation (*erhua*), and the aspect markers differ from those of the neighbouring dialects.** (**Received account: being mixed is not a shortage of data:** a region that has long served as a corridor and as a place where families divide ought, by nature, to be mixed. (② Huizhou once had an extremely strong consistency, and that consistency was not linguistic. (Since the Ming and Qing, networks of Huizhou merchants, lineages and contract documents have made up a highly self-consistent regional society; the quantity of popular documents handed down in this region stands out among regions of the same kind. (Received account: this lesson writes that down as the most counter-intuitive sentence of this chapter: a region with a developed documentary culture has not won its dialect a settled status in the classification; the documents are written in the language of contracts, and the dialect is only spoken. (Chapters 5 and 6, "papers decide fate", are here filled out by a counter-example: papers have legal force only if written in the common language (通用语) — Huizhou merchants were precisely the first people to master this. (③ Why the dispute will not be settled. (Because to rule it "independent", one must point to innovations that are its alone; and on a transitional belt innovations arise independently of one another, so that a common origin cannot be proved. (This is the limit, here, of the method of parallel splits and mergers. (This chapter requires you to write this sentence down: a question that a method cannot answer is not the method's failure; it is the boundary of the question.
:::

::: definition Pinghua: a name still pending {#def-ping}
Received account. (Varieties in a number of areas of Guangxi are called Pinghua (平话); their relations to Yue, to Mandarin, and to one another have long been unsettled. In one scheme of the atlas it was listed as a unit on its own, and the dispute has gone on ever since. (Three points. (① The origin of the name is itself muddled. (The received account holds that it is taken from a source of the sort of the local name for their own speech; when the name of a unit in the classification comes from a name used by others (他称) or a self-designation (自称), and the etymology is unclear, the field usually argues for half a century. (② Its position has determined its fate. (Guangxi is a provincial region where Mandarin, Yue, Pinghua, Hakka and several non-Chinese languages (Zhuang and others) interleave; in a contact zone this dense, any single criterion can find points that support it, and can also find points that oppose it. (**Received account.** (**This is the most concentrated scene of Chapter 9's "three ledgers of contact".** (③ **A case not yet closed has the greatest value for teaching.** (**You can count for yourself in the library: the same set of points is assigned to four units across four or five schemes.** (**This lesson sets this for everyone who feels that "classification is boring":** copy onto one page the various schemes of assignment for some one area, and then ask which school's evidence is the weakest — **this exercise will teach you what strength of argument is, better than the whole of this chapter's main text.** (**The third course and the course on the argumentative essay converge here.**
:::

::: example Four points on the boundary {#ex-bianjie}
The table below is a fictional but reasonable feature matrix (＋ means the feature is present):

| Point | Voicing retained for fully voiced initials | Fully voiced initials aspirated | Entering tone independent | Nasalisation of the final | Self-designation "本地话" |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 甲 | ＋ | － | ＋ | － | － |
| 乙 | ＋ | － | － | ＋ | ＋ |
| 丙 | － | ＋ | － | ＋ | ＋ |
| 丁 | － | ＋ | ＋ | － | － |

For each of the three treatments, draw the grouping once, and write who would object each time.

::: solution
**Merge into the nearer group.** (**Give 甲 to Xiang, 丙 to Gan, and 乙 and 丁 to two larger neighbouring groups;** those who object are local scholars and local institutions, and their grounds are usually not sound categories but identity. (② **Set up a group of one's own.** (**乙 and 丙 can be combined into one "transitional group"** (**they share the two items nasalisation and the self-designation**), and 甲 and 丁 are each assigned to a neighbouring group, or further groups are set up; those who object are the scholars who do historical comparison, because "sharing two items" cannot prove a shared innovation. (Note: nasalisation is a typological feature, and it can arise independently — this is what I mean by the limit of the method. (③ Mark as undecided. (The objectors are the most numerous, and also the most forceful: "undecided" cannot enter teaching materials, word lists or budgets; an editor will directly require you to write a name. (This lesson's judgement of this round. (Of the three treatments, only the third is epistemologically correct, only the first is institutionally workable, and the second is the most ambitious; any dialect report that does not state which one it has used is disguising its own vote as a fact. (Marking: anyone who can write "a typological feature cannot serve as evidence of common origin" receives an A for this chapter. (This point and Chapter 1's "similarity may be borrowing" are the obverse and the reverse of one and the same proposition, and they are also the adjudicating rule for every classificatory dispute in the whole course.
:::
:::

::: intuition Nobody says “我讲老湘语” ('I speak Old Xiang.' (Our translation.)) {#int-bendi}
A steady observation. (Names from the classification almost never appear in local people's descriptions of themselves; they say 本地话 ('local speech'), 城里话 ('town speech'), 乡下话 ('country speech'), 街上话 ('street speech'), or else the name of a county. (Four inferences. (① Local designations are an indexical system, not a classificatory system. (It ranks things by "how far from me" and by "whether we intermarry with them". (Chapter 8's axis of stance is here the classification native to the speaker. (② The content of the word 本地话 moves with the speaker. (To someone in the county town it is the accent of the county; to someone in a township it is the village; so any material that cites "the locals' self-designation" must mark who is speaking. (③ A school-district name is a slow violence. (When a word enters primary-school textbooks, the local designation retreats into the family within twenty years; this is the reason this chapter requires you, in fieldwork, to learn the local name of the speech first and to use the scholarly name only after that. (**Chapter 10 will give this an operating procedure.** (④ **One sentence of epitaph that this lesson gives taxonomy.** (**What a map records is the way we ask questions, not the way the land answers;** and these four chapters of "between" are worth writing because they expose the way of asking — **the centre never needs to defend itself; the boundary is always defending itself.** (**This sentence can be carried into any classificatory dispute in any discipline and used there.**
:::

::: history The ledger of the four places {#sec-zhang}
**Ming and Qing.** (**Two large-scale migrations, 江西填湖广 ('Jiangxi fills Huguang') and 湖广填四川 ('Huguang fills Sichuan'), changed the population of the middle reaches of the Yangtze;** **received account:** **this lesson stresses the direct effect of these two events on the classification:** the boundaries of Xiang, Gan and South-western Mandarin are the sediment of these two migrations. (② **Ming and Qing.** (**Contracts, account books and genealogies of Huizhou lineages and merchant groups have come down in great quantity;** **received account:** **the state of document preservation in this region stands out among regions of the same kind in the whole country.** (③ **1950s—1960s.** (**The national survey established the framework of seven groups;** the joining and splitting of Gan and Hakka, and units of the "Pinghua" sort, were already under discussion then. (Received account. (④ 1970s—1980s. (Systematic description of voicing in Xiang and of "old-style and new-style"; Hui was proposed as a group of its own; received account: what marks this period is that classification no longer looks only at a few features, but begins to look at the parallelism of splits and mergers in sound categories. (⑤ 1987／2012. (The three areas Jin, Hui and Ping entered the atlas, and the second edition redrew the lines. (Received account. (⑥ From 2015, and the future. (The Language Resource Protection Project has placed dense points on the transitional belts; this lesson's testable prediction: when the materials for these points are published, at least one of the four areas in this chapter will be broken up and redrawn. (This is the same prediction as that sentence in Chapter 2, "the denser the points, the more the groups scatter"; it can be proved wrong within five years — please write it down and check it. (A discipline that can offer a prediction open to being falsified needs no further defence.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in this chapter
**Treating a transitional belt as a shortage of data** (**mixture is the normal result of a corridor (Hui ① above)**); **using a provincial name to think the dialect group** (**"赣" is an administrative short name (Gan ② above)**); **using a typological feature to prove common origin** (**nasalisation can arise in each place on its own (example ② above:** this item is the adjudicating rule for the whole chapter)**); citing "the locals' self-designation" without stating who is speaking** (**the content of 本地话 moves with the speaker (intuition ② above:** the first formatting rule of a field note).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the boundary is more real {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**The centre is a result of classification; only the boundary is the actual state of the language: the centre is orderly because survey points are many, standardisation is strong, and the locality is willing to cooperate; the boundary is disorderly because nobody there is answering on the language's behalf. A good dialect gazetteer ought therefore to give the most space to those villages that least resemble this group. This suggestion sounds absurd. In fact it only carries a commonplace of historiography over into linguistics: the place with the richest archives is often the least representative of the dynasty.**" You must draw on **the three treatments**, **old-style and new-style, and Mandarinisation**, **provincial names and dialect groups**, **documents and the written industry**, **the position of Pinghua**, **the indexicality of the self-designation**, at least these six items; and adjudicate: **this suggestion is good for the researcher as an individual; is it good for a discipline? (State clearly what "centre" a discipline needs, and otherwise what it would lose.**

::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms; the adjudication (**a passing answer must reserve two things for the discipline:** **① comparability** (**if one studies only the boundary, the groups cannot talk to one another, because every paper is talking about exceptions;** the centre supplies the definition of the units and the stability of the terminology (**this is this chapter's real concession to the anti-centre position**); **② a judgement of representativeness** (**policy, education and medical translation need "how a given group generally says it", and this is a demand a discipline cannot refuse (**Chapter 8's "the standard language is infrastructure" may be cited**); a compromise scheme must then be given (**candidates: take the centre as the unit of description and the boundary as the unit of theory; or, in one and the same gazetteer, set up two sets of chapters, "typical points" and "transitional points", and require the latter to cite the former (**marking looks at whether the scheme can be carried out, not at whether it is pretty**); if the conclusion can write "**so this chapter does not oppose the centre; what it opposes is taking the centre as the truth of the language. A discipline may draw a centre for the sake of communication, so long as it also writes on the map: this is for convenience, not because this place is truer**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Boundary file: treatments (**the correct, the workable and the ambitious — the three cannot be had together**), Xiang (**the provincial capital pushes the dialect towards Mandarin, and the townships keep the old readings**), Gan (**a provincial name is not a dialect group; the focus of the dispute between Hakka and Gan is whether migration history counts as a basis**), Hui (**documentary culture does not bring a written industry**), Ping (**a case not yet closed is the best teaching material**), self-designation (**an indexical system is not a classificatory system**).
