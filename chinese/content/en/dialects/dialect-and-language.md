---
title: "Dialect and language"
---

The three courses before this one treated Chinese as a single object: one set of initials, one grammar, one stretch of literary history. This chapter begins by taking it apart — **you will find that “Chinese” is a storehouse holding ten spoken varieties that cannot understand one another, and that the nameplate on the storehouse was hung there by the hand that writes, not made out by the ear**.

## Definition: three boundaries {#def-jie}

::: definition Why mutual intelligibility is not enough {#def-hutong}
**An introduction to linguistics will give one criterion:** if the speakers of two ways of speaking can understand each other without learning, they are the same language (**Inside Chinese this criterion snaps at once into three pieces (**① the continuum (village A understands village B, village B understands village C, and village A does not understand village C; in that situation “mutual intelligibility” is a question with no answer, because it requires you to cut a gradient band into two segments (the mountain regions of Europe and of China both have this kind of cloth; it proves that the unit “language” cannot be defined by mutual intelligibility, because mutual intelligibility itself has no boundary (② one-way understanding (people in the northern dialect area can often understand Southwestern Mandarin (*guanhua*), but not necessarily the other way round; both sides understand a third party's Putonghua (standard Mandarin), and so each takes the other to “have an accent” (this lesson's reminder: mutual intelligibility is an asymmetric quantity, and to use it as a symmetric quantity is the first step of an error in classification (③ coverage by writing (speakers of Suzhou speech and speakers of Wenzhou speech share one set of Chinese characters, one written language and one literary history; and so the real ground of the judgement “the same language” is not the ear but the character (this is the core mechanism of the chapter: the Chinese character is a machine that files spoken varieties of very great difference into one and the same record (Course 2 and Course 10 meet here: when the script is unified and speech is split, the line between “language” and “dialect” (*fangyan*; topolect) is drawn by institutions, and the place where an institution draws the line is exactly the place it needs to answer for.
:::

::: definition The use of one maxim {#def-geyan}
**The received account** (**There is a Yiddish maxim often repeated at second hand, to the effect that “一种语言就是拥有陆军与海军的方言” ('a language is a dialect that possesses an army and a navy') (Our translation.) (**This lesson does not take it for a fine-sounding piece of cynicism, but for a statement that can be tested: **it predicts three things (**① **a variety that has a standard language is called a language** (it has schools, dictionaries, examinations and announcers; ② a variety without these is called a dialect (and so the speakers themselves will say “我们那边的方言土” ('the dialect over our way is rustic') (this is the institution stuffing the classification into the native speaker's mouth (③ the fastest way to change the classification is not to prove anything, but to found a school (All three predictions were fulfilled in the twentieth century, and the one fulfilled most thoroughly is Putonghua itself: before it was defined in 1955, it was a cover term for several kinds of northern speech; after it was defined, it became a language with a phonological table, with tests and with grades of accent (another name for Chapter 8, “the standard language is infrastructure” (From this the lesson gives an operational definition for the whole course: “language” is an administrative unit, “dialect” is an administrative unit under an administrative unit, and “accent” is one person's error; and what really exists by nature is only people speaking, and the share of one another's speech that they understand.
:::

::: definition Three levels: phonology, lexicon and grammar {#def-sancang}
① The phonological level (The number of tone categories runs from four in northern Mandarin to seven or nine in a number of southeastern dialects; as for codas, the north mostly has no entering-tone (checked-tone) codas, while some dialects of Yue (Cantonese, for the speech of Guangzhou and Hong Kong), Hakka and Min keep stop codas such as -p, -t and -k, and Wu keeps an entering tone of the glottal-stop kind, though the coda has already weakened (The received account: characters of one and the same Middle Chinese origin go to different results in each area, and this is the reason dialect classification looks mainly at the phonology (② The lexical level (Basic words fork: sets of the kind 站、瞧、瞅、看 and 儿子、囡、查某子 (verbs of standing and of looking; 'son', and words for a child); this lesson insists that only a fork in basic words (the body, kinship, numbers, actions) is a hard difference, while a fork in cultural words and in names of things is only a difference of lists (③ **The grammatical level** (**the level most underestimated, and also the one with the most diagnostic force:** negators, aspect markers, comparative sentences, word order and plural markers all differ; **grammatical difference matters because** loanwords happen every day, while grammatical structure is very rarely borrowed (**this line joins the present chapter to the “three ledgers of contact” in Chapter 9 (**A technique that can be tried on the spot (**To judge how far two varieties differ, do not ask about words; ask three sentences:** “我没有” ('I do not have it'), “被他打了” ('was hit by him'), “比我还高” ('even taller than I am') **—** look at how negation, the passive and comparison are used (**These three sentences are the lowest-cost tool in a dialect survey.**
:::

::: definition Where the differences come from {#def-sange}
① Separate inheritance (One and the same Middle Chinese source changes, in different places, at different speeds; the most typical case is the devoicing of the fully voiced initials, 全浊声母的清化: the north is basically devoiced, many places in Wu keep voiced sounds or a voiced colouring, and at a number of Mandarin localities some characters are read as voiced fricatives (The received account: from this follows the most important methodological point of the chapter: dialect difference is often not a matter of which variety is “ancient” and which is “modern”, but of different degrees of completion of one and the same change (The piece of common knowledge that “people who speak Putonghua retain little of the old pronunciation” is wrong; the correct way to put it is that the layer it retains and the layer the southern dialects retain are not the same (② Layers stacked one on another (Inside one dialect are stacked reading pronunciations of several periods: literary readings and colloquial readings (文读 and 白读) (the colloquial reading is everyday transmission; the literary reading is learnt from the system of reading pronunciations and from a more recent common language (Min is the extreme case of this phenomenon; Chapter 6 of this course sets it out (The explanatory power of the notion of layers is this: it turns “two systems of pronunciation inside one dialect” from a strange thing into a usual thing; and it is the evidence of stratification in a living *Qieyun* 《切韵》 ('Cutting Rhymes') (③ Contact (long-term borrowing from neighbouring non-Chinese languages, and substratum influence (The received account: from this arises a class of objects that are hard to deal with: several varieties around southern Guangxi and Guangxi whose relations to one another, and to Yue and to Mandarin, are all in dispute; the dispute itself is good news — **it shows that classification has never been a discovery, but the putting forward of a proposal that can be opposed (**This sentence is at the same time this lesson's return visit to the logic course and to the course on argumentative writing (**A classification has content only if it can be refuted.**
:::

::: example Three pairs of speech, three rulers {#ex-sandui}
Using the three rulers, judge separately whether each of the following pairs is “the same language” or “two languages”: **A: Suzhou speech and Shanghai speech; B: Putonghua and Northeastern Mandarin; C: Southern Min (闽南语) and Eastern Min (闽东话)**. Ruler one: mutual intelligibility; ruler two: historical origin (**degree of common origin**); ruler three: what local people say, and how institutions reckon it.

::: solution
**A** — **mutual intelligibility in the upper middle (**young people in the urban districts understand one another; between towns and townships it takes effort**); the degree of common origin is high (**both belong to Wu, and the splits and mergers of sound categories run highly in parallel**); institutions and public opinion reckon them “two localities of the same dialect” (**but Shanghai speech, on account of the city's standing, is treated as “一种话” ('a speech in its own right'), while Suzhou speech is called “乡下话” ('country talk') — two social fates of what is in fact one phonological system (② B — mutual intelligibility very high, the degree of common origin extremely high, and institutionally reckoned two accents of the same language; this is the only pair on which the three rulers line up, and it is also why people are willing to grant it (③ C — mutual intelligibility low (the vocabulary has correspondences, but the tones, the rhyme categories and the everyday words all differ); the degree of common origin is still high (both belong to Min, and the mechanism of literary and colloquial layering is the same); local people and institutions both reckon them “two dialects”, while international classifications often list each as a language (Marking (the form of the conclusion must be written out: “the three rulers do not agree at A and at C, so any answer must first declare which ruler it is using; this lesson holds that the honest wording is: “cognate in the phonology, unintelligible in speech, one language administratively” — to state the three things separately is the whole method of this chapter (The worst paper asserts “they are two languages to begin with” or “they are dialects to begin with”, because that is precisely to disguise the administrative ruler as the auditory ruler (A restatement, here, of the line in Chapter 8, “whenever someone says ‘好好说话’ ('speak properly'), ask who gets it free”: whenever someone says “那才是正经话” ('that is the proper speech'), ask who is holding an examination for it.
:::
:::

::: intuition An accent may be forgiven; a dialect is required to be corrected {#int-kouyin}
**First set up one distinction** (**an accent** (**the phonology differs, while morphology and syntax are shared:** “他的普通话有口音” ('his Putonghua has an accent') (② **a dialect** (**all three levels differ:** “我听不懂他说的话” ('I cannot understand what he is saying') (**This distinction is not a concept of linguistics; it is the line between politeness and punishment:** having an accent is a personal trait that is tolerated, while speaking a dialect is a public act that may be corrected (**Three observations (**① **tolerance runs along prestige** (the same difference, coming from the capital, is called “标准” ('standard'), and coming from a county town is called “土” ('rustic'); the “axis of embarrassment” of Chapter 8 has a parallel axis here: the axis of prestige (② the word “accent” puts the responsibility on the person (to say that someone “has an accent” implies a baseline; and the baseline does not exist by nature; it is kept up by the examination system and the broadcasting system (③ and so this chapter gives an attitude one can take away (on meeting a stretch of speech one cannot understand, the right first reaction is not “what dialect is this”, but “how much institution do he and I still not share”; understanding a half has never been a question of ability; it is a question of the amount of shared exposure (This sentence will be used again in the chapter on dialect surveying: the virtue an investigator most needs to train is to record, as it stands, the fact of not understanding, and not to explain it as the other person failing to speak clearly.
:::

::: history A word list, a map and a change of name {#sec-lishi}
**Around the first century BC** (**a book that collected everyday words from the various places arranged the speech of the various states by the glosses (**the received account attributes it to Yang Xiong 扬雄 (**This lesson reads it in two ways:** first, it proves that in the Han regional difference was already treated as an object of knowledge that could be collected; second, it is organised on the principle “what one and the same thing is called in different places”, and so it is by nature a word list and not a map — the dialectology of China began as a word-list tradition, and became a phonological tradition only in the twentieth century (② 601 (a rhyme dictionary states in its preface that it strikes a compromise over “南北是非、古今异同” ('the rights and wrongs of north and south, and the likenesses and differences of ancient and modern') (Our translation.) (The received account: this sentence is often quoted; this lesson stresses another meaning it has: as early as the seventh century, scholars already knew that difference of place and difference of period get tangled together, while a book in the service of the examinations has to pretend that the two layers can be straightened out (③ The seventeenth century (Europeans who came to China compiled character books and tables of sounds for the study and transmission of Chinese, and these included materials on Mandarin and on the various accents (The received account: this lesson takes only one point: among the earliest people to treat the Chinese dialects as objects that could be recorded, one group were foreigners, doing it in order to learn them (④ 1928 (a study of Wu described a number of localities by phonetic transcription and by questionnaire, and established the method (the received account attributes it to Zhao Yuanren 赵元任 (⑤ 1956/1987 (a nationwide survey of dialects was paired with a draft of seven areas; thirty years later a language atlas fixed ten areas, and the second edition moved the lines again (The received account (this lesson's conclusion: the dialect divisions of China are changed once every thirty years; this is not the failure of a science, but its normal state — new localities are filled in, and the boundaries are drawn again (⑥ From 2015 (a project for the protection of language resources set up recording points at more than a thousand places (The received account: this chapter points out, at the end, an ethic: when the reason for recording is “快没了” ('it will soon be gone'), the recording itself becomes a kind of consolation; this lesson requires you, after you have finished Lesson 10, still to remember this sentence (Chapter 9 sets it out.

::: warning Four pitfalls of this chapter
**Drawing the line by mutual intelligibility alone** (there is no line on a continuum; the person who draws the line already had an administrative unit (① above)); value judgements of the form “who retains the old pronunciation” (the correct statement is that the layers retained are different (origin ① above: this is the sentence for which marks are most often deducted in this chapter)); taking a difference of vocabulary for a hard difference (loanwords happen every day; one must look at basic words and at grammar (② and ③ of the three levels above)); explaining “not understanding” as “not speaking clearly” (understanding a half is a question of the amount of shared exposure (intuition ③ above: this one decides whether you can carry out a survey.
:::

::: exercise A survey in three sentences {level=1}
Ask someone near you who speaks a non-Mandarin dialect (or use dialect materials) the three sentences **“我没有”, “被他打了” and “比我还高”**, record the dialect forms, and say which of the three levels shows the greatest difference.

::: solution
Marking: **phonology, lexicon and grammar must be written as three separate things** (**writing them mixed together is a fail for this chapter (①, ② and ③ of the three levels above); if, in the target dialect, all three sentences use the same word order and only the function words are changed, writing that point down counts as good data that passes.**
:::

::: exercise Finding an isogloss {level=2}

::: solution
Marking: **the conclusion must be written in the form “the line coincides with some administrative or topographic boundary”** (and the limitation that the sample is too small must be stated (origin ③ above, and intuition ③); only drawing the line, and not writing the limitation, is a fail (The four strengths of evidence from Course 1 apply here in the same way.

::: exercise Short essay: naming is allocation {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**Whether a variety is called a language or a dialect is decided not by how it sounds, but by what it can demand: the language of instruction in school, translation in court, a slot of time on the radio, and whether children must give up their own pronunciation for it. For this very reason, taxonomy is always the first chapter of politics; and what linguistics can contribute is only a negative proof — no structural criterion can rank these varieties as higher and lower**”. You must draw on at least six items: **the three rulers**, **the continuum**, **two degrees of completion of a common origin**, **the stacking of layers**, **the line between accent and dialect**, **the ethic of recording**; and you must give a ruling: **when linguistics cannot prove higher and lower, why do tens of millions of parents still, of their own accord, give up passing the dialect on to the next generation (you must give the mechanism, not a moral judgement**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**rulers, continuum, degree of completion, layers, accent, recording**); the mechanism on the parents' side (**an answer that passes must be written as the economic arithmetic of the locality and the household, not as “观念落后” ('backward ideas'):** candidates: ① **the exchange price of language ability** (**Chapter 8: how much education and how much in wages a variety can be exchanged for decides its place in the household budget**); ② **the child's cost at school** (a child who starts school speaking a dialect must complete the switch, unaided, within a few years, and nobody pays this switching fee; parents buy it out in advance so as to avoid that cost (this is the point in the whole chapter that most needs to be written clearly); ③ the indivisibility of prestige (it is not that parents find the dialect no good; they are unwilling for the child to be labelled, in an assessment, as “有口音的人” ('a person with an accent') (the accent item above); ④ information asymmetry (most households do not know that a bilingual upbringing will not harm schoolwork; and the institution never tells them — so one of the mechanisms is that the duty to publicise is omitted); the direction of the conclusion (if the answer can put it as “so the real force driving language shift is not policy but the parents' budget sheet; any policy in the name of protection, if it does not at the same time lower the exchange price of the common language or compensate the cost of switching, is only morally reproaching people who have made a rational choice”, that is an A; this lesson requires a conclusion of this kind: it neither strikes a pose of nostalgia nor pretends to be neutral.
:::

::: summary
This chapter hands over four things: **the three rulers** (**mutual intelligibility, origin, institution — they will clash with one another, and you must say which one you are using**), **degree of completion** (**dialect difference is not a difference between ancient and modern; it is different stages of progress of one and the same change**), **layers** (**literary and colloquial variant readings are a living history of pronunciation**), **accent and dialect** (**the former is forgiven, the latter is corrected; this difference is social structure**). Next chapter: the division into areas — how the speech of China is drawn into ten pieces, and why those lines have to be changed every thirty years.
