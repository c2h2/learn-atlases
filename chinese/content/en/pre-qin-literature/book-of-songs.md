---
title: "The *Book of Songs*"
---

That a comprehensive anthology of songs became a "Classic" is the greatest accident, and the greatest necessity, in literary history: the Zhou people's collection of words for singing was prized by Confucius, established as a school of learning by the Han court, given new commentaries by writers of the Song, and restored by readers of our own time as poetry — **every generation has read, in these 305 pieces, the China it wanted**.

## Number and origin {#sec-shushen}

::: definition The file on the Three Hundred Poems {#def-dang'ang}
The full count: **305 pieces** (a further 6 *shēng* poems, 笙诗, have titles but no words, so some reckon 311). The dates run roughly from **the early Western Zhou to the middle of the Spring and Autumn period** (11th–6th centuries BC), and the territory spans the Airs of the Fifteen States — from the land of Bin to the Jiang and the Tuo, almost the whole map of the Zhou cultural sphere. **Musicality is the first attribute**: every piece was taken into the music and could be sung. The *Mozi* has “诵诗三百，弦诗三百，歌诗三百，舞诗三百” — 'Recite the three hundred poems, play the three hundred poems on strings, sing the three hundred poems, dance the three hundred poems.' (Our translation.) One poem, four ways of performing it. The three accounts of origin are to be filed together. **The collection theory**: He Xiu 何休, commenting on the *Gongyang Tradition*, recounts from earlier tradition “行人振木铎徇于路以采诗，献之大师” — 'Envoys shook wooden-tongued bells, went about the roads collecting poems, and presented them to the Grand Master of music.' (Our translation.) The 'Treatise on Literature' in the *Book of Han* also grants “古有采诗之官” — 'In antiquity there were officials who collected poems.' (Our translation.) **The presentation theory**: *Discourses of the States*, 'Discourses of Zhou', has “故天子听政，使公卿至于列士献诗” — 'Thus, when the Son of Heaven attended to government, he had everyone from the dukes and ministers down to the ranked officers present poems.' (Our translation.) A route by which the upper ranks offered remonstrance. **The deletion theory**: the *Records of the Grand Historian* has “古者诗三千余篇，及至孔子，去其重” — 'In antiquity there were more than three thousand poems; when it came to Confucius, he removed the duplicates.' (Our translation.) From the Tang onward many have doubted this (if he really did delete them, why are the poems left outside so few; from Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修 downward the attack is especially forceful). The settled point for teaching: **Confucius's relation to the *Songs* is teaching, revising and commending (“吾自卫反鲁，然后乐正，雅颂各得其所” — 'I returned from Wei to Lu, and only then was the music put right, and the Odes and the Hymns each found their place.' (Our translation.)), not necessarily deletion** — 'deletion' looks more like the compilation project of the myth of the uncrowned king (*sùwáng* 素王).
:::

::: definition Airs, Odes and Hymns: the ground of the threefold division {#def-fengyasong}
The standard of classification is **music, not content**. **Airs** (*fēng* 风) = the folk-song tunes of the vassal states (the Airs of the Fifteen States, 160 pieces, a radio of local airs; “风，风也” — 'Feng is feng.' (Our translation.) — a gloss by sound in the Mao Preface). **Odes** (*yǎ* 雅) = the orthodox music of the court in the royal domain (the Greater Odes, 31 pieces, mostly Western Zhou epics and admonitions — 'Shengmin' 生民 ('Birth of the people'), 'Gong Liu' 公刘, 'Mian' 绵 ('Continuing'), 'Huangyi' 皇矣 ('August'), 'Daming' 大明 ('Great brightness'), the "five-part cycle of the Zhou people"; the Lesser Odes, 74 pieces, half folk song and half songs of banquet and reward for toil, standing in the place of grievance and censure — the "changed Odes", *biànyǎ* 变雅). **Hymns** (*sòng* 颂) = dance-songs and musical songs of the ancestral temple (the three Hymns of Zhou, Lu and Shang, 40 pieces; “颂者，容也” — 'The Hymn is the bearing.' (Our translation.) — poems of the dance's bearing). An exegetical map of four schools and three strata: **the New Text schools of Qi, Lu and Han** (今文齐鲁韩; oral teaching in the early Han — the Qi Songs give primacy to calamities and anomalies, the Lu Songs are plain, and the Han Songs, 韩诗, survive in the *Outer Commentary*, *Waizhuan*) and **the Old Text Mao Songs** (古文毛诗; Mao Chang 毛苌's commentary, Zheng Xuan 郑玄's subcommentary, the only text to survive after the Han) — the "Book of Songs" in general use today is the **Mao Songs system**. In the Song, Zhu Xi 朱熹, in his *Shi jizhuan* 诗集传 (*Collected Commentaries on the Songs*), set up a stove of his own: the "Airs" of the *Songs* are mostly “里巷歌谣之作，所谓男女相与咏歌、各言其情者也” — 'works of the ballads of lanes and alleys, what is called men and women singing to one another, each speaking his or her own feelings' (Our translation.) (the gist of the preface to the *Shi jizhuan*). The moralists of the Learning of the Way were, against expectation, the honest ones: the politics of praise and blame (*měicì* 美刺) gave way to the study of human feeling. Evidential scholarship of the Qing, and the revival of New Text learning, reversed the verdict again. **Readers of our own time** restore the songs with literature as the standard. To read one Classic = to drill a borehole through four geological strata.
:::

::: example Between "praise and blame" and "love poetry" {#ex-meici}
'Guanju' 关雎 — “关关雎鸠，在河之洲。窈窕淑女，君子好逑。” 'Guan, guan cry the ospreys, on the islet in the river. The graceful, good lady is a fine match for the gentleman.' (Our translation.) The Mao Preface: “《关雎》，后妃之德也……乐得淑女，以配君子” — '"Guanju" is the virtue of the royal consort… Delighting in obtaining a good lady, so as to pair her with the gentleman.' (Our translation.) Zhu Xi's *Shi jizhuan* affirms its 'joyful but not licentious' (乐而不淫), but places it among the proper human relations 'between the bedchamber and the pillow' (闺房枕席之间). Yu Guanying 余冠英, in his *Shijing xuan* 诗经选 (*A Selection from the Book of Songs*), calls it 'a love poem' (一首爱情诗). Please give a verdict, and state the principle of the verdict.
::: solution
This case has no single verdict. ① **The textual layer**: the face of the poem writes of a gentleman seeking a lady, and the words "royal consort" do not appear. What the Mao Preface says is a **reading in the manner of the study of the Classics** (that poetry serves political instruction is the institutional premise on which Han readers read the Classics; it is not that their invention is wholly fabrication. It preserves the pre-Qin context of use, "chanting a poem to express intent", *fùshī yánzhì* 赋诗言志). ② **The layer of origin**: as a musical song of the Zhou, it is most likely an aristocratic wedding rite or a love poem (“钟鼓乐之琴瑟友之” — 'With bells and drums give her joy; with *qin* and *se* befriend her.' (Our translation.) — is an inventory of the instruments of rites and music, and may also be a happy imagining). ③ **The layer of reception**: two thousand years of the "praise and blame" reading have themselves become a fact of literary history (palace-style poetry avoids it; Chinese opera, *xiqu*, quotes it). The principle of the verdict is the same as in the lesson: **first ask which layer you are reading in** — a class on the Classics examines Mao and Zheng; a literature class examines the love poem; a cultural-history class examines how two thousand years of misreading were also a creation. Taking one layer for the whole is the mistake.
:::
:::

::: history The assembly line by which a body of songs was canonised {#sec-jingdianhua}
**Pre-Qin**: the *Songs* were a required course for the aristocracy and a tool of diplomacy — Confucius: “不学《诗》，无以言” — 'If you do not study the *Songs*, you have nothing with which to speak.' (Our translation.) “诗可以兴，可以观，可以群，可以怨” — 'The poems can stir, can observe, can draw people together, and can voice resentment.' (Our translation.) (*xìng*, *guān*, *qún*, *yuàn* 兴观群怨 = the ancestral outline of reception theory). **Han**: the Erudites of the Five Classics were established, and the *Songs* became a textbook of official ideology; the four schools, Qi, Lu, Han (韩) and Mao, competed for the posts; the three schools gradually died out and the Mao Songs alone survived (a struggle over the making of one book of poems). **Tang**: Kong Yingda 孔颖达's *Mao shi zhengyi* 毛诗正义 (*The Correct Meaning of the Mao Songs*) fixed the standard interpretation for the examinations. **Song**: Zhu Xi's new commentary shook the monopoly of praise and blame. **Ming**: the examinations took Zhu's *Commentary* as their authority, and candidates used “思无邪” — 'The thoughts are without depravity.' (Our translation.) — to feel their way into the speech of the Three Dynasties. **Qing**: Yao Jiheng 姚际恒's *Shijing tonglun* 诗经通论 (*A General Treatise on the Book of Songs*) and Fang Yurun 方玉润's *Shijing yuanshi* 诗经原始 (*The Book of Songs Returned to Its Origins*) counter-attacked the study of the Classics with a literary way of reading. **After the 1920s**: the *Gushi bian* school (*Gǔshǐ biàn* 古史辨, 'Debates on Ancient History') took apart the system of "praise and blame", and the people of the Literary Revolution invited the *Songs* back onto the first page of literary history. **Today**: “关关雎鸠” — 'Guan, guan, the ospreys.' (Our translation.) — appears in kindergarten textbooks and in the speeches at weddings. **Canonisation is not standing still after sagehood has been conferred; it is each generation claiming the work anew.**
:::

::: intuition The right posture for reading the *Book of Songs*: take it first as singing {#int-chang}
Every problem in the *Book of Songs* is in the end a problem of music. The four-character line in a two-and-two beat (“关关/雎鸠，在河/之洲”) is a drumbeat, not prose. Repeated stanzas and stacked lines are the refrain; changing a word is improvisatory variation (the next chapter sets this out in detail). Particles such as “兮”, “只” and “思” are a written record of the voice held on. That Han readers interpreted it as a text of political instruction is **a compensatory mechanism after the music had been lost** — unable to hear the melody, they could only search in every character for subtle words and a great meaning (*wēiyán dàyì* 微言大义). So the first step in a close reading of the *Book of Songs* is **to read it aloud to the rhythm** (even in modern pronunciation), and to put the ear back on duty; only the second step is the commentaries and subcommentaries (knowing on which line Mao and Zheng installed the virtue of the royal consort); the third step is to return to the scene of the song — one footnote, "poems collected and entered into the music" (采诗入乐), is the whole of its proper trade.
:::

:::

First, **the craving to prove the poem from history**: every poem must be pinned to some person and some event. The institution of collecting poems was vague to begin with, and the occasions (*běnshì* 本事) are mostly not transmitted; forcing oneself to be the one who understands is an old ailment of the writers of commentaries and subcommentaries — do not catch it. Second, **going too far in discarding the Prefaces**: Master Zhu's doubts about the Minor Prefaces (*Xiaoxu* 小序) opened a reading by human feeling, but praise and blame preserve the political history of how the pre-Qin age used the *Songs*; delete once, and both are left empty. Third, **failing to check the names of things**: “七月流火” — 'In the seventh month the Fire declines.' (Our translation.) — is the Great Fire star sinking in the west (a signal that it is turning cool); to use it of fierce summer heat is an accident over names and things. The “苹” in “呦呦鹿鸣” — 'You you, the deer cry.' (Our translation.) — is the plant *laihao* 藾蒿, not an apple: get one piece of hard knowledge wrong, and a full house of applause becomes a full house of shame. Fourth, **quotation that jumps a level**: splicing the three layers of statement — the Mao commentary, Zhu Xi and Yu Guanying — into "the original meaning of the *Book of Songs*". A citation must keep its layer; to mix the layers is to forge a commentary.
:::

Make a file card for 'Jianjia' 蒹葭 ('Reeds'): affiliation (state / musical category), the structure of stanzas and lines, a conjecture about the musical character, and one reading at each of two layers (origin / reception).
::: solution
The file: the Airs of Qin (the local tunes of the land of Qin — 'Wuyi' 无衣 ('No robe') and 'Xiaorong' 小戎 ('The small war-chariot') are martial; this piece alone is hazy and remote; the very placing is the riddle). Three stanzas in stacked singing; each stanza changes rhyme and changes words (苍／霜、唏／湄、长／沚 — to change a word is to vary; one sings and three sigh in answer). The layer of origin: a seeking-song of an autumn morning on the Wei. The layer of reception: “所谓伊人” ('the one spoken of as that person') was, by the tradition of instruction through the *Songs*, raised to a general metaphor for the ideal and for the worthy (a span of two thousand years, from the wares of feeling to the motif of seeking the worthy). Fill all four items on the card, and that is full marks.

::: exercise Reading the commentaries against each other {level=2}
'Taoyao' 桃夭, “之子于归，宜其室家” — 'This young woman goes to her married home; she will suit her chamber and house.' (Our translation.) The Mao Preface ties the poem to the age of King Wen, and sees in it a government of “男女以正，婚姻以时” — 'men and women kept in rectitude, and marriages made in season.' (Our translation.) Zhu Xi's *Shi jizhuan* follows its praise, while the tone returns it to human relations in daily use. Yu Guanying of our own time marks it straight off as 'congratulating a new marriage' (贺新婚). Compare the three views, and point out the type of ground each relies on.
::: solution
What each of the three relies on: **the logic of the arranged prefaces** (the study of the Classics — 'Zhounan' 周南 is “正始之道”, 'the Way of setting the beginning right', and the peach blossom in its blaze is read as a diagnosis of the age, that marriages came in due season); **the setting given by the names of things** (literature — “桃之夭夭，有蕡其实” — 'The peach is young and tender; abundant is its fruit.' (Our translation.) — simply is the stock of images on a wedding card); **the function of the folk song** (folklore — the proper trade of a musical song that adds to the joy of the wedding rite). The marks turn on being able to tell the "types of ground" apart, not on rushing to answer "the only correct one": each of the three governs one layer, and within its layer each is solid.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is the *Book of Songs* literature? {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. The *Book of Songs*, in its fourfold identity as "song / classic / textbook / literature": was the modern placing of it under "literature" a restoration or a narrowing? Requirements: ① cite one piece of material from each of Confucius, the Mao Preface, Zhu Xi, and one modern scholar; ② give one example to show that "a reading from the study of the Classics produced good criticism" (you may use the lineage of Bai Juyi's allegorical satire); ③ set out your scheme of classification, and its cost.
::: solution
What earns the marks: the four citations have no hard errors of fact; "the study of the Classics also produced literature" is shown in a concrete lineage (Chen Zi'ang 陈子昂's “风雅兴寄”, 'the Airs and Odes, stirring and lodging intent', and Bai Juyi 白居易's “文章合为时著” — 'Writing should be composed for the times.' (Our translation.) — are both endorsed by the authority of the study of the Classics: without the "Classic", where would the great banner of *fenggu* ('wind and bone'; vigour of feeling and structure) come from); the scheme of classification honestly marks its cost ("literarisation" throws away the materials of the view of rites and music; "classicisation" crushes the love poems to death).
:::

::: summary
Four entries in the file on the *Book of Songs*: the origin of the 305 pieces (the three accounts — collection, presentation and revision — filed together; deletion of the poems left in doubt); the Airs, Odes and Hymns of the musical threefold division; the **four geological strata** of the Mao commentary, Zheng's subcommentary, Zhu's commentary and modern selections; the history of use from diplomatic visits, through the examinations, to the kindergarten. It became a "Classic" by being claimed in politics, and it lives as "poetry" by being restored through human feeling — both things are true; do not let either cancel the other. The next chapter enters the technical workshop: the four-character beat, repeated stanzas and stacked lines, and *fu*, *bi* and *xing* (exposition, comparison, evocation) — how "technique" is still serviceable after three thousand years.
:::
