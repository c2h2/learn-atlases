---
title: "The *Analects* and the *Mencius*"
---

The debate among the Masters begins with the recording of what Confucius said, and it reaches its climax when Mencius, shouldering the 'kingly way' (*wángdào* 王道), stages a collision with the courts of the various states — the *Analects* is a teacher's classroom sketch, the *Mencius* a debater's live broadcast. The 'argumentative persona' of prose: these two books wrote it into the compilation.

## The birth of the recorded-sayings form {#sec-yulu}

::: definition The *Analects*: the polyphony of the classroom {#def-lunyu}
The *Analects*: Confucius's disciples and the disciples of those disciples **recorded it after the event** and compiled it (the text fixed at the beginning of the Warring States); twenty books; a mixed copying-out of hard debate, of replies, and of passing impressions — the form itself is the 'analects' (“语者，论难应答之言” — *Yu* means the words of hard debate and of reply. (Our translation.) He Yan 何晏, *Collected Explanations*; Huang Kan 皇侃, *Elucidation of the Meaning*; Xing Bing 邢昺, *Subcommentary*; Zhu Xi 朱熹, *Collected Annotations*: four layers of commentary). The literary value hides in the **gaps of the form**: ① **sketching the person**: “夫子喟然叹曰” ('The Master sighed and said'), “子之武城，闻弦歌之声——夫子莞尔而笑：割鸡焉用牛刀” — The Master went to Wucheng and heard the sound of strings and singing — the Master smiled a slight smile: why use an ox-cleaver to kill a chicken? (Our translation.) — smiles, slips of the tongue, being put in check on the spot by a disciple (“昔者偃也闻诸夫子” — In the past I, Yan, heard it from the Master. (Our translation.) — and the teacher owns up: “前言戏之耳” — What I said before was only in jest. (Our translation.)); **the *Analects* is the only pre-Qin book that keeps an embarrassment for the sage**; ② **the bare weight of the maxim**: “巧言令色，鲜矣仁” — Clever speech and an ingratiating face: rarely is there humaneness (*rén* 仁). (Our translation.) — no background, no argument, the conclusion running naked — the recorded-sayings form (*yǔlùtǐ* 语录体) is **the form of memory** (Liu Xie 刘勰, *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Discourse and Discussion': “圣哲彝训曰经，述经叙理曰论” — The abiding instructions of sages and the wise are called classics; expounding the classics and setting out principles is called discourse. (Our translation.) — the genealogy of “论” ('discourse') takes the *Analects* as the work that opens the line); ③ **the scene of speech**: the dispute of ancient and modern in “先进于礼乐，野人也” — Those who came first in ritual and music were rustics. (Our translation.) — and Zeng Dian's 曾点 statement of his intent in “莫春者春服既成” — In late spring, the spring clothes already finished. (Our translation.) — that sentence of Confucius, “吾与点也” — I stand with Dian. (Our translation.), is the **highest aesthetic judgement** the whole book records.
:::

::: definition Mencius: his own statement about loving to argue {#def-mengzi}
The *Mencius*, seven books (disciples such as Wan Zhang 万章 and Gongsun Chou 公孙丑 “次其语”, 'set his words in order' — Sima Qian 司马迁 says that Mencius “退而与万章之徒序《诗》《书》，述仲尼之意” — He withdrew and, with followers such as Wan Zhang, set in order the *Songs* and the *Documents*, and transmitted the intent of Zhongni. (Our translation.)), and the form **grew from the short chapters of the *Analects* into the scene of debate**: round after round of engagement with King Hui of Liang 梁惠王, King Xuan of Qi 齐宣王, Gaozi 告子 and Chunyu Kun 淳于髡, and the **long form of momentum** (*qìshì* 气势, 'rhetorical force') is opened for the first time. Mencius's own avowal: “予岂好辩哉？予不得已也” — Am I fond of arguing? I cannot do otherwise. (Our translation.) — **argument is a form the way of the age forced into being**. His three weapons: ① **knowing words** (*zhīyán* 知言): “诐辞知其所蔽，淫辞知其所陷，邪辞知其所离，遁辞知其所穷” — From one-sided words I know wherein they are blinded; from excessive words I know wherein they are sunk; from perverse words I know wherein they stray; from evasive words I know wherein they are exhausted. (Our translation.) — a pathology of four kinds of verbal tactic (a diagnostic table of argument that has failed, the remote ancestor of later classes in logic and rhetoric); ② **cultivating *qi*** (*yǎngqì* 养气; *qi* 气, 'vital energy'): “我善养吾浩然之气……其为气也，至大至刚” — I am good at cultivating my flood-like *qi*… As for this *qi*, it is supremely great and supremely firm. (Our translation.) — **the first bucket of gold in the theory of the *qi* of writing**: Han Yu's 韩愈 “气盛言宜” ('when *qi* is abundant, the words are fitting') and Su Shi's 苏轼 “行云流水” ('drifting clouds and flowing water') are both drawn off this store; ③ **the story-legion of pulling up seedlings to help them grow**: pulling up the seedlings, the man of Qi swaggering before his wife and concubine, Yi Qiu 弈秋 teaching the game, fifty paces laughing at a hundred, stealing one chicken a month — **parables used as the propeller of argument** (Zhuangzi's 庄子 parables are aeroplanes; Mencius's parables are evidence in court: each story does only one thing — it drives you onto the common sense you have already admitted).
:::

::: example King Xuan of Qi asked: “交邻国有道乎？” Is there a way to conduct relations with neighbouring states? (Our translation.) {#ex-qixuan}
Mencius replied: “有。惟仁者为能以大事小，惟智者为能以小事大” — There is. Only the humane can serve a small state by means of a large one; only the wise can serve a large state by means of a small one. (Our translation.) (*Liang Hui Wang*, lower, 'King Hui of Liang'); there is also the famous chain of questions “四境之内不治，则如之何？王顾左右而言他” — If all within the four borders is not in order, then what is to be done? The king looked to those on his left and right and spoke of other things. (Our translation.) (the same book). Analyse the **design of the opening** and the **design of the close** in Mencius's dialogue.
::: solution
The design of the opening: **cut in where no defence is set** — King Xuan asks about relations abroad, and Mencius answers “以大事小乐天、以小事大畏天” ('serve the small by means of the great and delight in Heaven; serve the great by means of the small and fear Heaven'), rewriting a question of policy as **a question of ranks of character** (humaneness / wisdom / courage); the ruler's vanity takes the order of its own accord. The design of the close — “四境不治如之何” — If the four borders are not in order, then what? (Our translation.): the questions before it (the wife and children, in the case of the minister; the officer of justice who does not govern his officers) were all verdicts of guilt pronounced by King Xuan himself, and when it comes to “四境之内不治” ('all within the four borders is not in order'), **the brush of the verdict is already in King Xuan's own hand** — he can only “顾左右而言他” ('look to the left and right and speak of other things'): the movement of flight is frozen by the recording brush, and becomes the most famous **close-up of silence** in the history of Chinese prose. The technical summary: Mencius does not wrangle with people's mouths; he wrangles with their 'admission' — first he tricks your “然” ('it is so') out of you, then he makes your “然” explode (the method of pressing with questions, a parallel device of the same type as Socrates' art of midwifery — filed side by side within the course, with no comparison of which is better).
:::
:::

::: history From the books of the Masters to the 'Four Books' {#sec-sishu}
**From the Warring States to the Han**: the *Mencius* was ranked among the Masters, its standing between Xunzi 荀 and Yang Xiong 扬; **the mid-Tang**: Han Yu established the transmission of the Way (*dàotǒng* 道统), and judged Mencius “醇乎醇” ('pure of the pure') ('On Reading Xunzi') — the foremost hand in the honouring of Mencius; **the Song**: Wang Anshi 王安石 and the two Chengs 二程 raised its standing, and **Zhu Xi's *Collected Annotations on the Chapters and Sentences of the Four Books* (1190)** welded the *Analects*, the *Mencius*, the *Great Learning* and the *Doctrine of the Mean* into a new classic for the examinations — from this point the centre of gravity of examination study moved from the Five Classics towards the Four Books; **the Yuan**: the court of Renzong 仁宗 fixed Zhu's *Commentary* as the set pattern for the examination hall; **Ming and Qing**: half the topics of the eight-legged essay (*bāgǔ* 八股) came out of the *Analects* and the *Mencius*, and the training in “代圣贤立言” ('speaking on behalf of the sages and the worthy') made the two books a memory of the whole people; **around May Fourth**: the target of ritual teaching (*lǐjiào* 礼教) was still being fired on, yet Lu Xun 鲁迅 and Zhou Zuoren 周作人 read the *Analects* as men of literature — **the Confucius of classical scholarship retired, and the Confucius and Mencius of literature came on duty**.
:::

::: intuition The emotional engineering of argumentative prose {#int-qingxu}
Read the *Mencius* by watching **how he gets angry**: he attacks Yang Zhu 杨朱 and Mo Di 墨翟 with “无父无君，是禽兽也” — To have no father and no ruler is to be a bird or a beast. (Our translation.) — the cursing reaches zoology; on seeing King Hui of Liang he opens with “王何必曰利” — Why must the king speak of profit? (Our translation.) — seizing the definition; when Gongsun Chou asks what is meant by the flood-like *qi*, he first answers “难言也” — It is hard to say. (Our translation.) — **first he grants that it cannot be said, then he says the whole of it in one breath** (a sublime control of volume). In Mencius's hands emotion is not noise; it is **the volume knob of the argument**: when a cold laugh is called for, “以邻国为壑” ('taking the neighbouring state for a drainage ditch'); when feeling is called for, “民为贵，社稷次之，君为轻” — The people are the most precious; the altars of soil and grain come next; the ruler is of least weight. (Our translation.) — **the two body-temperatures of Chinese argumentative prose (cold mockery and scalding gall) take their set form from here**: above, they inherit the mockery of the *Zhuangzi*; below, they open the temperature-control line that runs from Han Yu's 'On the Origin of the Way' onward, all the way to Lu Xun's *zawen* (杂文, the polemical essay).
:::

::: warning Three pitfalls in reading the *Analects* and the *Mencius*
One: **taking maxims for theorems**. The chapter “唯女子与小人为难养也” — Only women and petty men are hard to keep. (Our translation.) — has been disputed from ancient times to the present; the right reading is **an archaeology of context** (“小人” ('petty man') is by one account a household retainer, and the various accounts of the chapter's point are set out side by side), not a forced whitewash or a forced blackening. Two: **taking debate for historical fact**. Mencius's dialogues with the rulers of the various states were for the most part **polished into a fixed form** by the disciples (brisk lines of the sort “望之不似人君” — Seen from a distance, he did not look like a ruler of men. (Our translation.) — are literary private notes, not the minutes of a meeting) — the layer of historical fact and the layer of literature are kept on separate accounts. Three: **taking the people-as-root for democracy**. “民为贵” ('the people are the most precious') is **the limit of an ethics of responsibility** (when the ruler is disqualified, Mencius says “闻诛一夫纣矣，未闻弑君也” — I have heard of the putting to death of the fellow Zhou; I have not heard of the assassination of a ruler. (Our translation.) — a theoretical lightning-flash of the right to revolution); it is not an institutional scheme in which sovereignty lies with the people — if the ancients are to be put to a vote, it must be on the format of their ballot.
:::

::: exercise A close reading of the sketches {level=1}
The book *Xiang Dang* is known as “孔子起居注” ('a diary of Confucius's activity and repose') (“入公门，鞠躬如也” — On entering the duke's gate, he bent as if in a bow. (Our translation.) “席不正，不坐” — If the mat was not straight, he did not sit. (Our translation.)). Choose three items and show how plain description (*báimiáo* 白描), with no comment at all, shapes a persona.
::: solution
An example: “执圭，鞠躬如也，如不胜” — Holding the jade tablet, he bent as if in a bow, as if he could not bear it. (Our translation.) (the weighty object writes reverence); “升车，必正立执绥” — When he mounted the carriage, he would stand straight and grasp the strap. (Our translation.) (the bearing in the carriage writes ritual); “厩焚。子退朝，曰：‘伤人乎？’不问马” — The stable burnt. The Master withdrew from court and said: 'Was anyone hurt?' He did not ask about the horses. (Our translation.) (**an omission of two characters writes the person** — an astonishing stroke in the age of state horse administration). The principle: the whole of *Xiang Dang* never comments with so much as the one character “圣” ('sage'); it only lists actions — **habit is the fingerprint of a persona**. This method is the remote ancestor of later fiction's “不写之写” ('writing by not writing') (of one direct source with *A New Account of Tales of the World*). The three examples and the principle, half and half, will do.
:::
:::

::: exercise Dissecting the fifty paces {level=2}
The chapter “五十步笑百步” ('fifty paces laughing at a hundred') (*Liang Hui Wang*, upper): draw how Mencius dismantles the boast “寡人之于国也，尽心焉耳矣” — As for my relation to the state, I do but exhaust my mind, and that is all. (Our translation.) — into **a trap in the comparative**, and point out its logical strength and its hole.
::: solution
How to take it apart: King Hui of Liang boasts “寡人之于国也，尽心焉耳矣” — As for my relation to the state, I do but exhaust my mind, and that is all. (Our translation.) → Mencius sets a battlefield simile and lures him into pronouncing the verdict himself — the king said: “不可，直不百步耳，是亦走也” — It will not do. He merely did not run a hundred paces; this too was running. (Our translation.): **King Hui of Liang convicts himself with his own hand** (running fifty paces and running a hundred are of the same kind). The strength: a structure of analogy plus self-admission — once you have admitted “同类” ('the same kind'), you must admit that the government of Liang and that of the neighbouring states alike belong to “王道未成” ('the kingly way not yet achieved'), and the entrance to “王道之始” ('the beginning of the kingly way') is opened by his own mouth. The hole: **where the analogy fails** — deserters and civil administration are not of the same dimension; a difference of degree, once it crosses a threshold, is a difference of kind (can the difference between a yearly relief of thirty and of zero be set in analogy with “走” ('running') and “不走” ('not running')?). The point for marks: both to admire the fineness of the argument's design and to be able to point out the cost of 'letting a simile stand in for the argument'.
:::

::: exercise A short essay: two argumentative personas {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: the *Analects*' “温而厉” ('warm and yet stern') and the *Mencius*' “直而傲” ('straight and yet proud') — which is the better suited to be a public persona for argument? Requirements: ① cite one chapter of each as a specimen; ② in connection with one scene in contemporary media (a comments section, a debating contest, or a video in a knowledge section), analyse the two personas' chances of success and their distortion; ③ set your choice in the open and state the cost.
::: solution
Points for marks: the specimens are accurate (for example, the *Analects*, “子温而厉，威而不猛，恭而安” — The Master was warm and yet stern, majestic and yet not fierce, respectful and yet at ease. (Our translation.); the *Mencius*, “说大人则藐之” — When persuading a great man, look down on him. (Our translation.)); the analysis of the medium is concrete (the manner of the *Analects* suits the circulation of quotable lines and of signature blocks — the cost is being cut into excerpts where there is no context; the manner of the *Mencius* suits a long debate and a long video in a knowledge section — the cost is that the persona of “好辩” ('being fond of argument') attracts hostility); the conclusion does not sit on the fence, and the cost is one that can be stated.
:::

::: summary
The twin peaks of the *Analects* and the *Mencius*: by sketches and maxims the *Analects* preserves a **sage who can be embarrassed** — the recorded-sayings form is the form of memory, and a smile is literature's gap; the *Mencius* writes argument as a contest and a show of persona — knowing words is the diagnostic table, cultivating *qi* is the engine, the legion of stories is the propeller, and even the flight of “顾左右而言他” ('looking to the left and right and speaking of other things') is a shot in his storyboard. The people of the Song drew the two peaks inside the 'Four Books'; from then on there are, in Chinese people's mouths, these books' four-character idioms (*chengyu*), and under their brushes these books' tone. The next chapter turns where the path turns round the peaks: Zhuangzi comes on — when argument meets the hurricane of parable.
:::
