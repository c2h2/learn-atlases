---
title: "The *Analects* and the *Mencius*"
---

The contention of the Masters begins with the recording of what Confucius (孔子) said, and it reaches its climax when Mencius (孟子), shouldering the Kingly Way (*wángdào* 王道), goes and stages collisions with the courts of the various states — the *Analects* is a teacher's sketch from the classroom, the *Mencius* a debater's live broadcast. Of the "persona of argument" in prose, these two books were the ones entered.

## The birth of the recorded-sayings form {#sec-yulu}

::: definition The *Analects*: polyphony in the classroom {#def-lunyu}
The *Analects*: **recorded after the fact** by Confucius's disciples and by disciples of the next generation, and so compiled (the compilation fixed at the beginning of the Warring States); twenty books; a mixed copying-out of disputation, replies and occasional impressions — the form is precisely *lúnyǔ* 论语 (by *yǔ* 语 is meant words of disputation and reply; the four layers of commentary are He Yan (何晏), *Jijie* (*Collected Explanations*); Huang Kan (皇侃), *Yishu* (*Elucidation of the Meaning*); Xing Bing (邢昺), *Shu* (*Subcommentary*); and Zhu Xi (朱熹), *Jizhu* (*Collected Commentaries*)). The literary value hides in the **gaps of the form**: (1) **sketching persons** — “夫子喟然叹曰” ('The Master sighed and said.' (Our translation.)) “子之武城，闻弦歌之声——夫子莞尔而笑：割鸡焉用牛刀” ('The Master went to Wucheng, and heard the sound of strings and song — the Master gave a faint smile: why use an ox-knife to kill a chicken?' (Our translation.)) — smiles, slips of the tongue, being put in check on the spot by a disciple (“昔者偃也闻诸夫子” ('In the past I, Yan, heard it from the Master.' (Our translation.)) — and the teacher owns the account: “前言戏之耳” ('What I said before was only in jest.' (Our translation.))). **The *Analects* is the only pre-Qin book that keeps a sage's embarrassment.** (2) **The bare weight of maxims**: “巧言令色，鲜矣仁” ('Artful words and an ingratiating countenance: rare indeed is humaneness.' (Our translation.)) — no setting, no argument, the conclusion running naked — the recorded-sayings form (*yǔlùtǐ* 语录体) is **the genre of memory** (Liu Xie (刘勰), *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Discourse and Persuasion': “圣哲彝训曰经，述经叙理曰论” ('The constant teachings of sages and the wise are called classics; expounding the classics and setting out principles is called discourse.' (Our translation.)) — the genealogy of *lùn* 论 takes the *Analects* as its founding work); (3) **speech on the spot** — the dispute of ancient and modern in “先进于礼乐，野人也” ('Those of former times, in ritual and music, are rustics.' (Our translation.)), and Zeng Dian (曾点) stating what he aspired to in “莫春者春服既成” ('In late spring, when the spring clothes are already made.' (Our translation.)) — that sentence of Confucius's, “吾与点也” ('I am with Dian.' (Our translation.)), is the **loftiest aesthetic verdict** the whole book records.
:::

::: definition Mencius: his own statement on loving to argue {#def-mengzi}
The *Mencius*, seven books (disciples such as Wan Zhang (万章) and Gongsun Chou (公孙丑) "arranged his sayings" — Sima Qian (司马迁) says that Mencius “退而与万章之徒序《诗》《书》，述仲尼之意” ('withdrew and, with followers such as Wan Zhang, set in order the *Songs* and the *Documents*, and set forth what Zhongni meant.' (Our translation.))), and the form **grew from** the short sections of the *Analects* **into a scene of debate**: round after round of exchanges with King Hui of Liang (梁惠王), King Xuan of Qi (齐宣王), Gaozi (告子) and Chunyu Kun (淳于髡), and the **long form of momentum** was opened for the first time. Mencius's own statement: “予岂好辩哉？予不得已也” ('How could I be fond of disputation? I cannot help it.' (Our translation.)) — **disputation is a genre forced out by the way of the age**. His three weapons: (1) **knowing words** (*zhīyán* 知言) — “诐辞知其所蔽，淫辞知其所陷，邪辞知其所离，遁辞知其所穷” ('In one-sided words, know what screens them; in excessive words, know where they sink; in deviant words, know where they leave the path; in evasive words, know where they are at an end.' (Our translation.)) — a pathology of four kinds of talk (a diagnostic chart of argument failing, the remote ancestor of later classes in logic and rhetoric); (2) **nourishing *qi*** (*yǎngqì* 养气; *qì* 气, vital energy) — “我善养吾浩然之气……其为气也，至大至刚” ('I am good at nourishing my flood-like *qi* … As for this *qi*, it is of the utmost greatness and the utmost firmness.' (Our translation.)) — **the first pot of gold of the theory of the *qi* of writing** (*wénqì* 文气): Han Yu's (韩愈) “气盛言宜” ('when *qi* is abundant, the words fit') and Su Shi's (苏轼) “行云流水” ('moving clouds and flowing water') are both drawn from this stock; (3) **the story-legion of pulling seedlings up to help them grow**: pulling up seedlings, the man of Qi bragging to his wife and concubine, Yi Qiu (弈秋) teaching chess, fifty paces laughing at a hundred, one chicken a month — **using fables as the thrusters of argument** (fables in the *Zhuangzi* are aeroplanes; Mencius's fables are evidence in court: each story does only one thing — it forces you onto the common sense you have already admitted).
:::

::: example King Xuan of Qi asked: "Is there a way of dealing with neighbouring states?" {#ex-qixuan}
Mencius replied: “有。惟仁者为能以大事小，惟智者为能以小事大” ('There is. Only the humane can, being great, serve the small; only the wise can, being small, serve the great.' (Our translation.)) (*King Hui of Liang*, lower part); there is also the famous chain of questions “四境之内不治，则如之何？王顾左右而言他” ('If within the four borders there is no good government, then what is to be done? The king looked to left and right and spoke of other things.' (Our translation.)) (the same book). Analyse the **design of the opening** and the **design of the close** in Mencius's dialogue.
::: solution
The design of the opening: **cut in where no defence is set** — King Xuan asks about relations with other states, and Mencius answers “以大事小乐天、以小事大畏天” ('serving the small while great is delighting in Heaven; serving the great while small is standing in awe of Heaven' (Our translation.)), rewriting a question of policy as **a question of ranks of character** (humaneness (*rén* 仁) / wisdom (*zhì* 智) / courage (*yǒng* 勇)); the ruler's vanity takes the order of itself. The design of the close — “四境不治如之何” ('the four borders are not governed: what then?' (Our translation.)): in the questions before this (the wife and children, in the subject's case; the officer of justice not governing the officers) King Xuan himself speaks the condemnation, and by the time one reaches “四境之内不治” ('within the four borders there is no good government'), **the pen of judgement is already in King Xuan's own hand** — he can only “顾左右而言他” ('look to left and right and speak of other things' (Our translation.)): the gesture of flight is freeze-framed by the historian's brush, and becomes the most famous **close-up of silence** in the history of Chinese prose. Technical summary: Mencius does not fight people at the mouth; he fights their "admission" — first he tricks your “然” ('it is so') out of you, then he makes your “然” explode (the method of pressing questions, a parallel device of the same type as Socrates' art of midwifery — filed side by side within the course, with no comparison of which is better).
:::
:::

::: history From books of the Masters to the "Four Books" {#sec-sishu}
**From the Warring States to the Han**: the *Mencius* was ranked among the Masters, its standing between Xunzi and Yang Xiong; **the mid-Tang**: Han Yu established the succession of the Way (*dàotǒng* 道统), and judged Mencius “醇乎醇” ('pure of the pure') ('On Reading Xun') — the foremost promoter of exalting Mencius; **the Song**: Wang Anshi (王安石) and the two Chengs raised its rank; **Zhu Xi's *Collected Commentaries on the Chapters and Sentences of the Four Books* (1190)** welded the *Analects*, the *Mencius*, the *Great Learning* and the *Doctrine of the Mean* into a new classic of the examinations — from then on the centre of gravity of examination studies moved from the Five Classics to the Four Books; **the Yuan**: the court of Renzong (仁宗) fixed Zhu's *Commentary* as the set form for the examination hall; **Ming and Qing**: half the topics of the eight-legged essay came from the *Analects* and the *Mencius*, and the training in “代圣贤立言” ('writing on behalf of the sages and worthies') made the two books a memory of the whole people; **around May Fourth**: the target of the ritual teachings went on being hit, yet Lu Xun (鲁迅) and Zhou Zuoren (周作人) read the *Analects* as men of letters — **the Confucius of classical scholarship retired, and the Confucius and Mencius of literature came on duty**.
:::

::: intuition The emotional engineering of argumentative prose {#int-qingxu}
To read the *Mencius* one should watch **how he gets angry**: he denounces Yang Zhu (杨朱) and Mo Di (墨翟) with “无父无君，是禽兽也” ('To be without father and without ruler is to be a bird or a beast.' (Our translation.)) — the cursing arrives at zoology; the moment he meets King Hui of Liang he opens with “王何必曰利” ('Why must the king speak of profit?' (Our translation.)) — seizing the definition; when Gongsun Chou asks what is meant by flood-like *qi*, he first answers “难言也” ('It is hard to speak of it.' (Our translation.)) — **first admit that it cannot be said, then say the whole of it in one breath** (a sublime control of volume). Emotion in Mencius's hands is not noise; it is **the volume knob of the argument**: when a cold sneer is called for, “以邻国为壑” ('taking a neighbouring state as one's drainage ditch' (Our translation.)); when feeling is called for, “民为贵，社稷次之，君为轻” ('The people are the most precious; the altars of soil and grain come next; the ruler is of least weight.' (Our translation.)) — **the two body-temperatures of Chinese argumentative prose (cold mockery and scalding gall) were from then on fixed as the pattern**: taking up the mockery of the *Zhuangzi* from above, and opening the way from Han Yu's 'On the Origin of the Way' downward, all the way to the temperature-control circuit of Lu Xun's *zawen*.
:::

::: warning Three pitfalls in reading the *Analects* and the *Mencius*
First, **taking maxims for theorems**: the chapter “唯女子与小人为难养也” ('Only women and petty men are hard to keep.' (Our translation.)) has been contested from ancient times to the present; the right reading is **an archaeology of context** (“小人” is by some said to mean a household retainer, and the various accounts of the chapter's point are set side by side), not a forced whitewash or a forced blackening. Second, **taking the debates for historical fact**: the dialogues of the *Mencius* with the rulers of the various states were for the most part **polished into a settled form** by the disciples (brisk lines of the sort “望之不似人君” ('Looked at from a distance, he did not seem a ruler of men.' (Our translation.)) are literary private notes, not minutes of a meeting) — keep separate accounts for the layer of historical fact and the layer of literature. Third, **taking "the people as the root" for democracy**: “民为贵” ('the people are the most precious') is **the limit of an ethics of responsibility** (when the monarch is no longer fit for the role, Mencius says “闻诛一夫纣矣，未闻弑君也” ('I have heard of the execution of that fellow Zhou; I have not heard of the assassination of a ruler.' (Our translation.)) — the theoretical lightning of a right of revolution), not an institutional scheme in which sovereignty lies with the people — if the ancients are to vote, it has to be on the format of their ballot.
:::

::: exercise Close reading of the sketches {level=1}
The book *Xiangdang* (《乡党》) goes by the name of a "diary of activity and repose" for Confucius (“入公门，鞠躬如也” ('On entering the duke's gate, he was bent, as it were.' (Our translation.)) “席不正，不坐” ('If the mat was not straight, he did not sit.' (Our translation.))). Choose three entries and explain how plain description, with no commentary at all, shapes a personality.
::: solution
An example: “执圭，鞠躬如也，如不胜” ('Holding the jade tablet, he was bent, as if he could not bear it.' (Our translation.)) (a weighty vessel writes reverence); “升车，必正立执绥” ('On mounting a carriage, he would always stand straight and hold the strap.' (Our translation.)) (the bearing in the carriage writes ritual); “厩焚。子退朝，曰：‘伤人乎？’不问马” ('The stable burnt. The Master came back from court and said: "Was anyone hurt?" He did not ask about the horses.' (Our translation.)) (**an omission of two characters writes the person** — an astonishing stroke in the age of horse administration). The principle: the whole of *Xiangdang* never comments with the one word “圣” ('sage'); it only lists actions — **habit is the fingerprint of personality**; this method is the remote ancestor of "writing by not writing" in later fiction (directly of the same source as *A New Account of Tales of the World*). The three examples and the principle, half and half, will do.
:::
:::

::: exercise Dissecting the fifty paces {level=2}
The chapter “五十步笑百步” ('fifty paces laughing at a hundred' (Our translation.)) (*King Hui of Liang*, upper part): set out how Mencius takes the boast “寡人之于国也，尽心焉耳矣” ('As for my relation to the state, I do but exhaust my mind, and that is all.' (Our translation.)) and dismantles it into **a trap in the comparative degree**, and point out its logical strength and its hole.
::: solution
How it is taken apart: King Hui of Liang boasts “寡人之于国也，尽心焉耳矣” ('As for my relation to the state, I do but exhaust my mind, and that is all.' (Our translation.)) → Mencius sets a battlefield analogy and induces him to give judgement in his own mouth — the king says: “不可，直不百步耳，是亦走也” ('It will not do. He merely did not run a hundred paces; but this too was running.' (Our translation.)): **King Hui of Liang convicts himself with his own hand** (running fifty paces and running a hundred are of the same kind). The strength: a structure of analogy plus self-admission — once you have admitted "the same kind", you must admit that the government of Liang and that of the neighbouring states alike belong to "the Kingly Way not yet achieved", and the entrance to "the beginning of the Kingly Way" is opened by his own mouth. The hole: **where the analogy fails** — deserters and the civil administration are not of the same dimension; a difference of degree, once it crosses a threshold, is a difference of kind (can the difference between a yearly relief of thirty and of zero be analogised to "running" and "not running"?). What earns the marks: both admiring how fine the design of the debate is, and being able to point out the cost of "letting analogy stand in for argument".
:::

::: exercise Short essay: two personas of argument {level=3}
Write between eight hundred and twelve hundred characters: of the *Analects*' “温而厉” ('warm yet severe') and the *Mencius*' “直而傲” ('forthright yet proud'), which is the better suited to be the public persona of argument? Requirements: (1) cite one chapter of each as a specimen; (2) in connection with one scene in contemporary media (a comment section, a debating contest, or a video in a knowledge section), analyse the two personas' chances of success and their distortion; (3) set out your choice and state the cost.
::: solution
What earns the marks: the specimens are accurate (for example, the *Analects*, “子温而厉，威而不猛，恭而安” ('The Master was warm yet severe, awe-inspiring yet not fierce, respectful and at ease.' (Our translation.)); the *Mencius*, “说大人则藐之” ('When speaking to a great man, look down on him.' (Our translation.))); the analysis of the medium is concrete (the manner of the *Analects* suits being passed on as quotable lines and in signature blocks — the cost is being cut into excerpts where there is no context; the manner of the *Mencius* suits long debates and long videos in a knowledge section — the cost is that the persona of "loving to argue" attracts hostility); the conclusion does not sit on the fence, and the cost is one that can be stated.
:::

::: summary
The twin peaks of the *Analects* and the *Mencius*: by sketch and maxim the *Analects* preserves a **sage who can be embarrassed** — the recorded-sayings form is the genre of memory, and a smile is literature's gap; the *Mencius* writes argument as a contest and a show of personality — knowing words is the diagnostic chart, nourishing *qi* is the engine, the legion of stories is the thruster, and even the flight of “顾左右而言他” ('looking to left and right and speaking of other things') is a shot in his storyboard. The people of the Song enclosed the two peaks within the "Four Books"; from then on Chinese people have had these books' idioms in the mouth, and their tone under the brush. The next chapter turns as the path rounds the peak: Zhuangzi comes on — when debate meets the hurricane of fable.
:::
