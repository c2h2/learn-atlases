---
title: "Qu Yuan and the *Li Sao*"
---

Before him, the singers of the *Book of Songs* (*Classic of Poetry*, *Book of Odes*; *Shijing*) were mostly nameless; after him, Chinese poetry had its **first face**: a high crown, a long sword, and the haggard colour of one who walks chanting beside the marsh. Qu Yuan 屈原 wrote the "I" into the poem, and wrote his fate outside it — more than five hundred lines of *Encountering Sorrow* (*Li Sao*), at once an autobiography, a political testament, and the birth certificate of romanticism in Chinese.

## One person, two layers of record {#sec-renwu}

::: definition Qu Yuan's dossier {#def-quyuan}
**The layer of historical biography**: personal name Ping, courtesy name Yuan (in mutual gloss with the *Li Sao* line “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” — "He named me Zhengze, the Correct Standard; he styled me Lingjun, the Numinous Evenness." (Our translation.)), of the ducal clan of Chu; “博闻强志，明于治乱，娴于辞令” — "Widely learned and strong of memory, clear about order and disorder, practised in the art of speech." (Our translation.) (*Records of the Grand Historian* (*Shiji*), 'Biography of Qu Yuan and Master Jia') — **China's first biography of a poet**. An official career across two reigns: at first he had King Huai's trust and was made **zuotu** 左徒, “入则与王图议国事以出号令，出则接遇宾客应对诸侯” — "Within, he planned the affairs of the state with the king, so as to issue commands; without, he received guests and answered the lords." (Our translation.); later he was estranged and set aside (across the two generations of King Huai and King Qingxiang, the slander and jealousy of Grand Master Shangguan and *lingyin* Zilan 令尹子兰 as the warp, and the vertical and horizontal alliances of Qi and Chu as the weft), twice banished, and in the end **he drowned himself in the Miluo** 汨罗 (traditionally tied to the twenty-first year of King Qingxiang, in the time before and after Bai Qi took Ying). **The textual layer**: the circle of reliable works is fixed as the ***Li Sao***, the ***Nine Pieces*** (*Jiu zhang* 《九章》), the ***Nine Songs*** (*Jiu ge* 《九歌》) (some say they were made by reworking folk songs of sacrifice), ***Heavenly Questions*** (*Tian wen* 《天问》), and ***Summons of the Soul*** (*Zhao hun* 《招魂》) (Wang Yi's line attributes it to Qu Yuan; *Summons of the Soul* may be Song Yu's) — while *Yuan you* 《远游》 ('Far Roaming'), *Bu ju* 《卜居》 ('Divining a Dwelling'), *Yu fu* 《渔父》 ('The Fisherman') and the like remain disputed as to authorship. The first courtroom of the study of the *Songs of Chu* (*Chuci*) is forever trying the case "**who wrote it**".
:::

::: example The tetralogy of the *Li Sao* {#ex-lisao}
The *Li Sao* is the longest political lyric of ancient China (ancient and modern ways of dividing the lines do not agree, and this lesson will not force a count). Give a four-part outline and explain its **psychological structure**.
::: solution
Four parts (following the narrative–psychological line). **The autobiographical part** — “帝高阳之苗裔兮，朕皇考曰伯庸” — "I am a descendant of the high lord Gaoyang; my august father was called Boyong." (Our translation.) (a narrative of innate gifts in three strands: descent, birth-time, and a fine name; “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” — "He named me Zhengze, the Correct Standard; he styled me Lingjun, the Numinous Evenness." (Our translation.) — **the name is a political programme**: what is level and upright can be a model; what is impartial can make things even). **The political part** — the ideal of fine government in “乘骐骥以驰骋兮，来吾道夫先路” — "I mount fine steeds and gallop; come, I shall lead on the road ahead." (Our translation.), and the bitter ledger of apostasy in “众芳之芜秽” ('the many fragrant ones gone to weeds and rot') — the colleagues' defection written as **the withering and fall of the fragrant plants**. **The quest part** — three quests for the woman (Fufei, the fair maiden of Yousong, the two Yao of Youyu), knocking above at Heaven's gatekeeper, Lingfen's divination, Wu Xian's bringing the spirits down — **the political search for accord, written in the structure of a love affair** (the code of fragrant plants and fair ones running in full). **The part of resolve** — for a time he leaves the state and roams far (“国无人莫我知兮，又何怀乎故都” — "No one in the state knows me; why, then, should I long for the old capital?" (Our translation.)), yet at the moment of ascending to heaven, “仆夫悲余马怀兮，蜷局顾而不行” — "My driver grieves, my horses yearn; they hunch, look back, and will not go." (Our translation.) — even the horses will not go, and so he states his case and asks to die: “愿依彭咸之遗则” — "I wish to follow the rule Peng Xian left behind." (Our translation.). Summary of the psychological structure: **setback — sublimation — falling back — dying for his intent** — a report on dignity from the fourth century BC, the depth of whose circling leaves the outer shell of the rhapsody form (*fu*) as good as empty.
:::
:::

## Fragrant plants and fair ones: a political semiotics {#sec-daima}

::: definition A manual for deciphering the code {#def-daima}
“**善鸟香草，以配忠贞；恶禽臭物，以比谗佞；灵修美人，以媲于君**” — "Good birds and fragrant plants are paired with loyalty and steadfastness; vicious birds and foul things are likened to slander and flattery; the Lingxiu and the fair one are matched with the ruler." (Our translation.) (Wang Yi, 'Preface to the *Li Sao* Classic' — the formal publication of the master table of the code). The fragrant-plant series (兰, 蕙, 荃, 茝, 留夷, 揭车 — **a system for wearing the person**: “纫秋兰以为佩” — "I string autumn orchid to make a pendant." (Our translation.); “朝饮木兰之坠露兮，夕餐秋菊之落英” — "At dawn I drink the dew that falls from the magnolia; at dusk I eat the fallen blossoms of the autumn chrysanthemum." (Our translation.) — **to eat and to drink is moral conduct**). The fair-one series (lord, sovereign, the quest for the woman — the grammar of love loaded with the meeting of ruler and minister). The foul-plant series (萧 and 艾 filling the hall — “户服艾以盈要兮，谓幽兰其不可佩” — "Every household wears mugwort until it fills the waist, and says the secluded orchid cannot be worn." (Our translation.); **the reversal of fragrance and stench is the reversal of politics**). Of where this code comes from, three accounts are filed together: **the tradition in the *Songs* of comparing virtue** (a bloodline of classical learning); **the shamanic custom of the land of Chu** (the fragrant plants were in fact the implements by which sacrificial rites brought spirits down — a structure of love between human and god; the dedicated chapter [[pre-qin-literature/songs-of-chu]] will go through that ledger again); **the politics of the body among the *shi*** (cultivating the self = governing the state, the same structure; Mencius's “我善养吾浩然之气” — "I am good at nourishing my flood-like *qi* ('vital energy')." (Our translation.) is the parallel version from the north). Influence, in the history of the code: Cao Zhi's 'Banks of the Luo', Zhang Heng's 'Four Sorrows', Li Shangyin's 'Untitled', and the daughters of *Dream of the Red Chamber* (*The Story of the Stone*) who are made of water — **the master codebook of two thousand years of *jituo* 寄托 ('lodging one's meaning') literature is bound here**.
:::

::: history Two thousand years of a poet's lawsuit, and a modern taking of office {#sec-guansi}
**Han**: Jia Yi lamented him by the Xiang; Sima Qian wrote Qu's life (“余读《离骚》《天问》《招魂》《哀郢》，悲其志” — "I read *Encountering Sorrow*, *Heavenly Questions*, *Summons of the Soul* and 'Lament for Ying', and I grieved for his intent." (Our translation.) — **the Grand Historian himself checked in at the Miluo**); Liu An's *Li Sao zhuan* 《离骚传》 ('Tradition of the *Li Sao*') has “与日月争光可也” — "He may vie in light with the sun and the moon." (Our translation.) (quoted in the *Records of the Grand Historian*), and Ban Gu criticised him for “露才扬己” ('displaying his talent and exalting himself') (**the Han had already opened court: loyal minister, or unbridled gentleman?**); Wang Yi's *Chuci zhangju* 《楚辞章句》 ('The *Songs of Chu* in Chapters and Sentences') (Eastern Han) took the "loyalty and steadfastness" view as its authority and annotated the whole. **Song**: Hong Xingzu's *Chuci buzhu* 《楚辞补注》 ('Supplementary Commentary on the *Songs of Chu*'), and Zhu Xi's *Chuci jizhu* 《楚辞集注》 ('Collected Commentaries on the *Songs of Chu*') (reread through “愤懑不平” ('indignant and unreconciled'), a Neo-Confucian's sympathy). **Qing**: Jiang Ji's *Shandai ge zhu Chuci* 《山带阁注楚辞》 ('Commentary on the *Songs of Chu* from the Mountain-Girdled Pavilion') checks the course of his travels by geography. **The 1920s–50s**: in the aftermath of the *Gushi bian* 古史辨 ('Debates on Ancient History') there was, for a time, a discussion calling into question Qu Yuan's authorship and where to place him in his age; in 1953 the **World Peace Council** named Qu Yuan one of the four great cultural figures of the world (Guo Moruo's history play *Qu Yuan*, of 1942, had already staged the “雷电颂” ('Hymn to Thunder and Lightning') into a bugle of the nation — **the disputed case and the god they honoured ran side by side in the same century**). **Today**: the heritage inscription of Duanwu (in 2009 China's Dragon Boat Festival entered the United Nations list of intangible cultural heritage) shares the screen with online essays debating whether Qu Yuan is the first poet or the first poetry IP — **he is still a living contested heritage (a heritage in dispute)**.
:::

::: intuition Why "romanticism" {#int-langman}
Textbooks stick the label "romanticism" on the *Li Sao*; taken apart, it is really three techniques. **Space out of control** (“朝发轫于苍梧，夕余迈乎县圃” — "At dawn I set out from Cangwu; at dusk I press on to Xuanpu." (Our translation.) — dragons in harness and phoenixes as the outer steeds, ranging the whole heavenly realm, geography become a screen onto which the mind is projected). **Time folded** (looking ahead to Chonghua, a later tryst with Lingjun, conversing with the ancients as with colleagues). **Things taking part in government** (wearing *lan*, eating chrysanthemum, twining *hui* — the person externalised into a flora). The imagination of the *Book of Songs* in the north is **the scale of the market and the fields** (“送子涉淇，至于顿丘” — "I saw you across the Qi, as far as Dunqiu." (Our translation.)); the imagination of the south is **the scale of the cosmos** — not a matter of which is higher and which lower, but two psychogeographies. In later times Li Bai, going up to heaven and down into the earth, follows the southern school; Du Fu, who does not leave ruler and realm for a single meal, follows the northern school — **the two great circuits of feeling in Chinese poetry had their lines laid already in the pre-Qin**.
:::

::: warning Four precautions in reading Qu Yuan
First, **using one's understanding to meet the poet's intent, pushed too far**: pinning every line about a fragrant plant to some reign and some event (the game of hunting out whom “兰” points to and whom “女” points to — Wang Yi's table of correspondences is a commentary, not an archaeological report). Second, **taking the biography as a verbatim record**: the original biography of Qu Yuan in the *Records of the Grand Historian* draws on legends of Chu and of the Han, and **the traces of layer-on-layer accretion are plain** (episodes such as those of Zheng Xiu and Zhang Yi are at variance with the *Intrigues of the Warring States* and the 'Hereditary House of Chu') — "the Qu Yuan in the *Records*" and "the Qu Yuan in the *Songs of Chu*" are to be kept in two envelopes. Third, **co-opting him into a frame of loyalty to the sovereign**: Master Qu's tension lies precisely in the tear between "**loyalty to the Chu of his ideal**" and "**loyalty to the Chu of his blood**" (“既莫足与为美政兮，吾将从彭咸之所居” — "Since there is no one with whom it is worth making fine government, I shall go to dwell where Peng Xian dwells." (Our translation.) — fine government first, Peng Xian after); to reduce him to blind loyalty is to banish him a second time. Fourth, **drawing a straight line of equality with Duanwu**: the folk history of *zongzi* and dragon boats did not necessarily begin with Qu Yuan (there is the view that they were old customs of the Wu–Yue region); it is a living specimen of **commemoration's co-opting of folk custom** — the archaeology of the festival and the archaeology of the poetry keep separate accounts.
:::

::: exercise The politics of the name {level=1}
“名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” — "He named me Zhengze, the Correct Standard; he styled me Lingjun, the Numinous Evenness." (Our translation.) — is in mutual gloss with “屈原名平字原” ('Qu Yuan, personal name Ping, courtesy name Yuan'). Explain why this "exegesis of the name" is **a political programme in his own telling**, and, setting it beside a modern persona (an online name / a manifesto), write one sentence of analogy.
::: solution
The points: Zhengze = Ping (平 may be glossed as 正 and as 法 — fair, and fit to be a model); Lingjun = Yuan (原 is glossed as 均 — 均 has the two senses 平 and 治, 'even' and 'to order'). The father (and the father-teacher) names the descendant with **items of virtue that can be glossed**; naming = legislating (“皇览揆余初度兮，肇锡余以嘉名” — "The august one surveyed and measured my first degree, and at the outset bestowed on me a fine name." (Our translation.) — birth-time, appearance and a fine name make the three-piece set of the narrative of innate gifts). The analogy: where a modern account takes the name “明镜” ('Bright Mirror') or “天平” ('Balance'), the signature line is a charter. Marking: the exegesis is accurate, plus an explanation of the mechanism by which "naming is politics".
:::

::: exercise Imitating the code {level=2}
In the code of fragrant plants and fair ones, write a modern "quest for the woman" of two hundred characters: the protagonist is seeking a job or a project; the characters “求” and “职” do not appear at any point; the system of symbols is consistent in itself (foul plants = the talk of *neijuan* 内卷 ('involution'); fragrant plants = craft).
::: solution
Marking: (1) the system is self-consistent (one foul plant set against one fragrant plant; do not mix two sets of vehicles); (2) there is one turn of "withering and fall" (兰 and 蕙 changing into 萧 and 艾 = the scene on which the ideal goes bad); (3) the close learns a movement of the “蜷局顾而不行” kind ('they hunch, look back, and will not go') (hide the decision in a hesitation of the body, or of a thing). Sample direction: handing in an application = offering a pendant; the interview = knocking at the gate; an offer = winning the woman; a broken agreement = changing colour halfway.
:::

Points for marking: the material of the three stations is accurate (Jia Yi's *Diao Qu Yuan fu* 《吊屈原赋》 ('Rhapsody Lamenting Qu Yuan'); Guo's play *Qu Yuan* and the 'Hymn to Thunder and Lightning'; the World Peace Council in 1953). A conscious hold on the "two layers" (to grant the accretion of layers is not to deny the value — **being needed is itself a historical fact**). The reply to the argument from embarrassment: failure is precisely the source of his aesthetic and moral force — **the reverse-side monument which China has left for the "study of getting things done"**; an argument that can do this may be given an A at once.

::: summary
Qu Yuan's four columns of gallery labels: two layers of dossier for the person and the books (the historical biography draws on legend; the texts have lawsuits); the psychological tetralogy of the *Li Sao* (setback — sublimation — falling back — dying for his intent; "the name is the programme, and the horses' refusal to go is the verdict"); the code of fragrant plants and fair ones (a semiotics of loyalty and steadfastness, of slander and flattery, and of the meeting with the ruler; the master codebook of two thousand years of *jituo* literature); two thousand years of being claimed (from the Grand Historian's check-in to the world cultural figure). The next chapter draws the camera back: the shaman's hall and the singing-ground of the *Songs of Chu* as a whole, and Song Yu's “下东风” ('the east wind comes down') — an overview of the ecosystem of southern literature.
