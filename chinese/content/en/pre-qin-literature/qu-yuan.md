---
title: "Qu Yuan and the *Li Sao*"
---

Before him, the singers of the *Book of Songs* (*Classic of Poetry*, *Book of Odes*; *Shijing*) were mostly nameless; after him, Chinese poetry had its **first face**: a tall hat, a long sword, the haggard colour of one who paces and chants beside the marsh. Qu Yuan 屈原 wrote the "I" into the poem, and wrote his life outside the poem — *Encountering Sorrow* (*Li Sao*), in more than five hundred lines, is an autobiography, a political testament, and the birth certificate of romanticism in Chinese.

## One person, two layers of the dossier {#sec-renwu}

::: definition Qu Yuan's dossier {#def-quyuan}
**The historical-biography layer**: personal name Ping, courtesy name Yuan (the line in *Encountering Sorrow*, “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” — 'He named me Zhengze; my courtesy name he gave as Lingjun.' (Our translation.) — stands in mutual reference with this); of the ruling clan of Chu; “博闻强志，明于治乱，娴于辞令” ('Of wide learning and strong memory, clear about order and disorder, adept in official speech.' (Our translation.)) (the *Records of the Grand Historian* (*Shiji*), 'Biography of Qu Yuan and Master Jia') — **China's first biography of a poet**. An official career across two reigns: at first trusted by King Huai 怀王 and appointed **zuotu** 左徒 — “入则与王图议国事以出号令，出则接遇宾客应对诸侯” ('Within, he planned the affairs of the state with the king and issued commands; without, he received guests and answered the feudal lords.' (Our translation.)) — and later estranged and dismissed (two generations, King Huai and King Qingxiang 顷襄王; the slander and jealousy of Senior Grandee Shangguan 上官大夫 and Chief Minister Zilan 令尹子兰 as the warp, and the vertical and horizontal alliances of Qi and Chu as the weft); twice banished; and in the end **he drowned himself in the Miluo** 汨罗 (traditionally tied to the twenty-first year of King Qingxiang, around the time Bai Qi 白起 took Ying 郢). **The textual layer**: the works one can rely on are marked off as ***Encountering Sorrow***, the ***Nine Pieces*** (*Jiu zhang* 《九章》), the ***Nine Songs*** (*Jiu ge* 《九歌》; some say they were made by working up folk sacrificial songs), ***Heavenly Questions*** (*Tian wen* 《天问》), and ***Summons of the Soul*** (*Zhao hun* 《招魂》; the line that follows Wang Yi 王逸 attributes it to Qu Yuan, and *Summons of the Soul* may be by Song Yu 宋玉) — *Far Roaming* (*Yuan you* 《远游》), *Divining Where to Dwell* (*Bu ju* 《卜居》), *The Fisherman* (*Yu fu* 《渔父》) and the like still have their authorship in dispute. The first courtroom in the study of the *Songs of Chu* (*Chuci*) is forever hearing "**who wrote it**".
:::

::: example The four movements of *Encountering Sorrow* {#ex-lisao}
*Encountering Sorrow* is the longest political lyric poem of ancient China (ancient and modern ways of dividing the lines do not agree, and this lesson will not force a count). Give a four-part outline and explain its **psychological structure**.
::: solution
The four movements (along the narrative–psychological line): **the autobiographical movement** — “帝高阳之苗裔兮，朕皇考曰伯庸” ('I am a descendant of Emperor Gaoyang; my honoured late father was called Boyong.' (Our translation.)) (a threefold narrative of inborn gifts: lineage, time of birth, and fair name; “名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” — 'He named me Zhengze; my courtesy name he gave as Lingjun.' (Our translation.) — **the name is a political programme**: what is level and upright can serve as a standard; what is impartial can make things even); **the political movement** — the ideal of good government in “乘骐骥以驰骋兮，来吾道夫先路” ('I mount a fine steed and gallop; come, I will lead the way ahead.' (Our translation.)), together with the bitter ledger of defection in “众芳之芜秽” ('the many fragrances overgrown and fouled') — the colleagues' change of allegiance, written as **fragrant plants withering and falling**; **the quest movement** — three quests for the fair one (Fufei 宓妃, the peerless woman of Yousong 有娀, the two Yao of Youyu 有虞), knocking above at Heaven's gatekeeper, Lingfen 灵氛 divining, the spirit descending by way of Wu Xian 巫咸 — **the political search for a match, written in the structure of a courtship** (the code of fragrant plants and fair ones running at full capacity); **the movement of resolve** — for a time he leaves the state and roams far (“国无人莫我知兮，又何怀乎故都” — 'In the state there is no one who knows me; why, then, should I yearn for the old capital?' (Our translation.)), yet at the moment of rising into heaven “仆夫悲余马怀兮，蜷局顾而不行” ('My driver grieves, my horses yearn for home; they curl, look back, and will not go.' (Our translation.)) — even the horses will not go, and so he sets out his words and asks to die, “愿依彭咸之遗则” ('I wish to follow the model Peng Xian left behind.' (Our translation.)). The psychological structure, in sum: **setback — sublimation — falling back — dying for one's resolve** — a report on dignity from the fourth century BC, the depth of whose circling leaves the outer shell of the "rhapsody form" (*fu*) as good as empty.
:::
:::

## Fragrant plants and fair ones: a political semiotics {#sec-daima}

::: definition A handbook for deciphering the code {#def-daima}
“**善鸟香草，以配忠贞；恶禽臭物，以比谗佞；灵修美人，以媲于君**” ('Good birds and fragrant plants, to match the loyal and steadfast; evil birds and foul things, to figure slanderers and flatterers; Lingxiu and the fair one, to stand as the peer of the ruler.' (Our translation.)) (Wang Yi, 'Preface to the *Encountering Sorrow* Classic' (*Lisao jing xu*) — the formal publication of the master table of the code). The fragrant-plant series (兰 *lan*, 蕙 *hui*, 荃 *quan*, 茝 *chai*, 留夷 *liuyi*, 揭车 *jieche* — **a system by which character is worn**: “纫秋兰以为佩” ('I twine the autumn orchid and make it a pendant.' (Our translation.)), “朝饮木兰之坠露兮，夕餐秋菊之落英” ('At dawn I drink the dew that drops from the magnolia; at dusk I eat the fallen blossoms of the autumn chrysanthemum.' (Our translation.)) — **to eat and to drink is moral conduct**); the fair-one series (the ruler, the sovereign, the quest for the woman — the grammar of love, loaded with the coming together of ruler and minister); the foul-plant series (萧 *xiao* and 艾 *ai* filling the court — “户服艾以盈要兮，谓幽兰其不可佩” ('In every house they wear *ai* until it fills the waist, and say the secluded orchid may not be worn.' (Our translation.)), **to turn fragrance and stench upside down is to turn politics upside down**). The origins of this code are filed together under three accounts: **the tradition of virtue-comparison in the *Songs*** (a kinship with the study of the Classics); **the shamanic custom of the land of Chu** (fragrant plants were in fact the implements by which sacrificial rites brought spirits down — a structure of love between human and spirit; [[pre-qin-literature/songs-of-chu]] will go through that ledger again in a chapter of its own); **the body politics of the *shi* 士** ('men of service') (cultivating the self = ordering the state, built to the same structure; Mencius's 孟子 “我善养吾浩然之气” — 'I am good at nourishing my flood-like *qi* ('vital energy').' (Our translation.) — is the parallel version in the north). The influence of the code through its history: Cao Zhi's 曹植 'banks of the Luo', Zhang Heng's 张衡 'Four Sorrows', Li Shangyin's 李商隐 'Untitled', and, in *Dream of the Red Chamber* (*The Story of the Stone*), the girls are made of water — **the master code-book of two thousand years of literature of *jìtuō* 寄托 ('lodging one's intent in something else') is bound here**.
:::

::: history One poet's two-thousand-year lawsuit, and a modern inauguration {#sec-guansi}
**Han**: Jia Yi 贾谊 mourned him by the Xiang 湘; Sima Qian 司马迁 wrote the life of Qu (“余读《离骚》《天问》《招魂》《哀郢》，悲其志” — 'I read *Encountering Sorrow*, *Heavenly Questions*, *Summons of the Soul* and "Lament for Ying", and I grieve for his resolve.' (Our translation.) — **the Grand Historian himself checked in at the Miluo**); Liu An's 刘安 *Tradition of Encountering Sorrow* (*Li Sao zhuan*), “与日月争光可也” ('He may contend in light with the sun and the moon.' (Our translation.)) (quoted in the *Records of the Grand Historian*); Ban Gu 班固 blamed him for “露才扬己” ('parading his talent and exalting himself') (**the Han had already opened the court: loyal minister, or wild scholar**); Wang Yi's *Songs of Chu, Chapter and Verse* (*Chuci zhangju* 《楚辞章句》) (Eastern Han) takes its stand on the theory of "loyalty and steadfastness" and annotates everything. **Song**: Hong Xingzu's 洪兴祖 *Supplementary Commentary on the Songs of Chu* (*Chuci buzhu* 《楚辞补注》), Zhu Xi's 朱熹 *Collected Commentaries on the Songs of Chu* (*Chuci jizhu* 《楚辞集注》) (reread through “愤懑不平” ('indignant and unreconciled') — a Neo-Confucian's sympathy). **Qing**: Jiang Ji's 蒋骥 *Songs of Chu Annotated from the Shandai Pavilion* (*Shandai ge zhu Chuci* 《山带阁注楚辞》) establishes the course of the travels by geography. **1920s–50s**: in the aftermath of the Debates on Ancient History (*Gushi bian* 古史辨) there was discussion that called in question Qu Yuan's authorship and where to place him in time; in 1953 the **World Peace Council** named Qu Yuan one of the four great cultural figures of the world (Guo Moruo's 郭沫若 history play *Qu Yuan* had already, in 1942, turned the 'Hymn to Thunder and Lightning' 《雷电颂》 into a clarion of the nation — **the disputed case and the god one reveres ran side by side in the same century**). **Today**: the bid to inscribe the Duanwu Festival 端午 (in 2009 China's Duanwu Festival entered the United Nations list of intangible cultural heritage) shares the screen with the online-essay debate “屈原是第一个诗人还是第一个诗歌IP” ('Is Qu Yuan the first poet, or the first poetry IP?') — **he is still a living contested heritage (a disputed heritage)**.
:::

::: intuition Why "romanticism" {#int-langman}
Textbooks stick the label "romanticism" on *Encountering Sorrow*; taken apart, it is really three techniques. **Space out of control** (朝发轫于苍梧，夕余迈乎县圃 — 'At dawn I set out from Cangwu; by evening I had pressed on to Xuanpu.' (Our translation.) — driving dragons, with phoenixes as the side horses, and ranging over the whole realm of heaven; geography becomes a screen for the projection of the mind); **time folded** (looking ahead to Chonghua 重华, a later appointment with Lingjun, talking with the ancients as if with colleagues); **things taking part in politics** (the orchid worn, the chrysanthemum eaten, the *hui* twined — character externalised as a flora). The imagination of the northern *Book of Songs* is **the scale of the marketplace and the farmland** (“送子涉淇，至于顿丘” — 'I went with you across the Qi, as far as Dunqiu.' (Our translation.)); the imagination of the south is **the scale of the cosmos** — not a question of which is the higher and which the lower, but two psychogeographies. In later ages Li Bai 李白, going up to heaven and down into the earth, takes the southern line, and Du Fu 杜甫, not a meal away from ruler and realm, takes the northern line — **the two great emotional circuits of Chinese poetry had their lines laid already in the pre-Qin**.
:::

::: warning Four precautions in reading Qu Yuan
First, **carrying "using one's understanding to meet the poet's intent" too far**: pinning every line about fragrant plants, as hard fact, to some reign and some event (the game of hunting out whom "the orchid" means and whom "the woman" means — Wang Yi's table of correspondences is a commentary, not an archaeological report). Second, **taking the biography as a verbatim record**: the biography of Qu Yuan in the *Records of the Grand Historian* draws on legends of Chu and of the Han, and **the traces of layered accumulation (*céngléi* 层累) are plain** (episodes such as those of Zheng Xiu 郑袖 and Zhang Yi 张仪 do not tally with the *Intrigues of the Warring States* and the 'Hereditary House of Chu') — "the Qu Yuan of the *Records*" and "the Qu Yuan of the *Songs of Chu*" are to be kept in two envelopes. Third, **co-opting him under a frame of loyalty to the ruler**: the tension in Master Qu 屈子 lies exactly in the tear between "**loyalty to the Chu of the ideal**" and "**loyalty to the Chu of the blood**" (“既莫足与为美政兮，吾将从彭咸之所居” — 'Since there is no one with whom I can join to make good government, I shall go to dwell where Peng Xian dwells.' (Our translation.) — good government first, Peng Xian after); to simplify this into foolish loyalty is to banish him a second time. Fourth, **equating him directly with the Duanwu Festival**: the folk history of rice dumplings (*zongzi* 粽子) and dragon boats did not necessarily begin with Qu Yuan (there is the theory that they were older customs of the Wu–Yue region); it is a living specimen of **commemoration having co-opted folk custom** — the archaeology of the festival and the archaeology of the poetry keep separate accounts.
:::

::: exercise The politics of the name {level=1}
“名余曰正则兮，字余曰灵均” ('He named me Zhengze; my courtesy name he gave as Lingjun.' (Our translation.)) and “屈原名平字原” ('Qu Yuan, personal name Ping, courtesy name Yuan') stand in mutual reference. Explain why this "exegesis of the name" is **a political programme stated by himself**, and, setting it beside a modern persona (screen name / manifesto), write one sentence of analogy.
::: solution
Key points: 正则 = 平 ("平" may be glossed as "正" and as "法" — fairness can serve as a standard); 灵均 = 原 ("原" is glossed as "均" — "均" has the double sense of levelling and of governing). The father (and the father-teacher) names the next generation with **virtues that can be glossed**; naming = legislating (“皇览揆余初度兮，肇锡余以嘉名” — 'The august one surveyed and measured my first bearing, and from the start bestowed on me a fair name.' (Our translation.) — time of birth, appearance, and fair name make up the three-piece set of the narrative of inborn gifts). For comparison: where a modern account takes the name 明镜 ('bright mirror') or 天平 ('scales'), the signature line is a charter. Marking: the exegesis is accurate, plus an account of the mechanism by which "naming is politics".
:::

::: exercise Imitating the code {level=2}
In the code of fragrant plants and fair ones, write a modern "quest for the woman" of two hundred characters. The protagonist is seeking a job, or seeking a project; the two characters 求 and 职 must not appear at any point. The symbolic system must be consistent in itself (foul plants = the patter of *nèijuǎn* 内卷 ('involution'); fragrant plants = craft).
::: solution
Marking: (1) the system is consistent in itself (one foul plant against one fragrant plant; do not mix two sets of vehicles); (2) there is one turn of "withering and falling" (兰 and 蕙 turning into 萧 and 艾 = the scene where the ideal goes bad); (3) the close learns an action of the “蜷局顾而不行” kind ('curl, look back, and will not go' — hide the decision in a hesitation of the body, or of a thing). A sample direction: sending an application in = offering a pendant; the interview = knocking at the gate; an offer = gaining the woman; breaking the agreement = changing colour midway.
:::

Points for marking: the material at the three stations is accurate (Jia Yi's *Rhapsody Lamenting Qu Yuan* 《吊屈原赋》, Guo's play *Qu Yuan* and the 'Hymn to Thunder and Lightning'; the World Peace Council in 1953); an awareness of the "two layers" (to grant layered accumulation is not to deny the value — **being needed is itself a historical fact**); the reply to the embarrassment argument: failure is precisely the source of his aesthetic and moral force — **the negative monument China has left for the "learning of getting things done"**; an argument on these lines can be given an A at once.

::: summary
Qu Yuan in four exhibition labels: a two-layer dossier of the person and the books (the historical biography draws on legend; the texts have lawsuits); the psychological four movements of *Encountering Sorrow* (setback — sublimation — falling back — dying for one's resolve; "the name is the programme, and the horses' refusal to go is the verdict"); the code of fragrant plants and fair ones (a semiotics of loyalty / slander and flattery / the encounter with the ruler; the master code-book of two thousand years of literature of *jituo*); two thousand years of being claimed (from the Grand Historian's check-in to world cultural figure). The next chapter draws the camera back: the shaman's hall and the singing-ground of the *Songs of Chu* as a whole, and Song Yu's “下东风” ('the east wind comes down') — an overview of the ecosystem of southern literature.
:::
