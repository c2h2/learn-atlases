title: "Myths"
---

Greek myth has a complete set in the single account of Hesiod; the North has the *Edda* — the way Chinese myth survives is entirely different: **fragments of gold across the whole sky, late in appearing yet with a source, recast generation after generation**. This lesson does three things: get a clear grasp of the warehouses of the fragments, learn to give myth "a dating and a household-registration check", and see why the Chinese made myth live as morality and as engineering.

## Fragments of gold and the two great warehouses {#sec-cuijin}

::: definition How Chinese myth exists {#def-fangshi}
① **No systematic epic**: there is no ancient "theogony". The opening of the world, the flood, Yi, and the wars of Yan and Huang are scattered each through its own books, and **the books are later than the events** — the earliest complete version of Pangu opening heaven comes from **Xu Zheng 徐整, a man of Wu in the Three Kingdoms, in the *Sanwu liji* 三五历纪 (*Chronicle of the Three and Five*)** (search the whole of the pre-Qin corpus, and this piece of textual evidence is not there); that is to say, the "opening" we know is a latecomer. ② **Two great warehouses**: the ***Shanhaijing*** 山海经 (*Classic of Mountains and Seas*), in eighteen chapters (a shaman's book of geography: the inner lands, the regions beyond the seas and the Great Wilderness are nested, layer on layer, about the world of the four quarters, and it preserves the densest cluster of strange gods — the Queen Mother of the West, Xiwangmu 西王母, her form like a person's, a leopard's tail and a tiger's teeth — as different from the later noble lady of the Jade Pool, Yaochi 瑶池, as if they were two gods), and the ***Huainanzi*** 淮南子 (*The Masters of Huainan*) (a collection made by the retainers of the Prince of Huainan in the Western Han, which smelts the myths of the various northern schools into a cosmological narrative: Nüwa 女娲 mending heaven, Hou Yi 后羿 shooting the suns, and Gonggong 共工 in his rage striking Mount Buzhou 不周山 are all given written form in it). ③ **Leftover material scattered about**: "Heavenly Questions" (*Tianwen* 天问) in the *Songs of Chu* asks, in one breath, more than one hundred and seventy questions of myth (a precious snapshot of the versions); the *Zuo Tradition* (*Zuozhuan*) hides demonic gods who have been turned into history (the four demonic gods Hundun 浑沌, Qiongqi 穷奇, Taowu 梼杌 and Taotie 饕餮 are translated into four unworthy clans — myth is recruited as an official historian); and late books such as the *Liezi* 列子 and the *Diwang shiji* 帝王世纪 (*Genealogies of Emperors and Kings*) go on "filling in the file".
:::

::: definition The four great clusters of myth {#def-jiqun}
**Opening and the making of things** (Pangu is late to appear; in the classical archive Nüwa's main post is the mending of heaven, while the moulding of human beings and the instituting of customs belong to a later layered accumulation (*cenglei* 层累) — keep the two accounts in separate ledgers), **flood and disaster relief** (Gonggong stirs the torrent, Gun 鲧 steals the *xirang* 息壤 ('soil that swells of itself'), Yu 禹 dredges the Nine Rivers — a three-stage rocket: disaster, theft, and bringing under control), **cultural invention** (Suiren 燧人 fetches fire, Shennong 神农 tastes the herbs, Xuanyuan 轩辕 makes boats, and Cangjie 仓颉 creates characters, whereupon "天雨粟，鬼夜哭" ('Heaven rained millet, and ghosts cried in the night.' (Our translation.)) (*Huainanzi*, "Benjing xun" 本经训 ('Teachings of the Basic Canon') — the price of civilisation opens with ghosts and spirits losing speech)), **resistance and the unyielding** (Kuafu 夸父 chases the sun and dies of thirst, and "弃其杖化为邓林" ('Cast the staff aside, and it became the Deng Forest.' (Our translation.)); Jingwei 精卫 "常衔西山之木石，以堙东海" ('Ever carried in the beak the wood and stones of the Western Mountains, so as to fill the Eastern Sea.' (Our translation.)); Xingtian 刑天 "执干戚舞" ('Grasped shield and axe and danced.' (Our translation.)) — everyone who fails is entered in the register, and the posts of tragedy belong to the establishment too).
:::

::: example Checking the household registration of "Pangu" {#ex-pangu}
Please set out a chronological table of the documents for the Pangu myth, and explain how the method of "layered accumulation" is used here.
::: solution
**Household-registration table**: the pre-Qin classics have no Pangu; the Western Han *Huainanzi* discusses the evolution of primordial *qi* (*yuanqi* 元气) but is without the name Pangu; the earliest complete record that is extant and can be cited is **Xu Zheng of Wu in the Three Kingdoms, the *Sanwu liji*** (the original book is lost, and it survives because the *Yiwen leiju* 艺文类聚 (*Classified Collection of Literature*) quotes it: "天地混沌如鸡子" ('Heaven and earth, in chaos, were like a chicken's egg.' (Our translation.))); Ren Fang 任昉 of the Liang, in the *Shuyi ji* 述异记 (*Records of Strange Things Recounted*), further takes in the legends of Pangu's territory; the classified encyclopaedias (*leishu* 类书) of the Tang and the Song, relaying the quotation layer upon layer, become instead the ancestral text of the version current today. **How layered accumulation is used**: the methodology of Gu Jiegang 顾颉刚 in the *Gushi bian* 古史辨 (*Debates on Ancient History*) of 1923 — "时代愈后，传说的古史期愈长" ('The later the age, the longer the stretch of ancient history in the legends.' (Our translation.)): for the people of Zhou the most ancient was Yu; by the Spring and Autumn, Yao 尧 and Shun 舜 had been added; by the Warring States there were the Three Sovereigns; by the Han there was Pangu — **Pangu is not "an older belief that was recorded"; it is "a new demand for an opening of the world that was manufactured"** (why did the Eastern Han–Wu need a god who opens heaven? The mixing of lineage origins, and the cosmological engineering of Daoism, are both answers given by scholars). The gain from checking the household registration: a myth's **year of birth** is more worth asking about first than its **content**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The "civil-servant personality" of Chinese myth
Set against Greece: Prometheus stealing fire is tragic rebellion, while the Chinese Suiren shi 燧人氏 fetching fire is **the registration of an invention**; the Zeuses fall in love and stir up trouble, while the service records of the Chinese gods are, to a one, public works — Gun and Yu controlling the waters, Shennong tasting the herbs, Hou Yi removing harms (Yayu 猰貐, Zaochi 凿齿, Jiuying 九婴, Dafeng 大风, Xiushe 修蛇, Fengxi 封豨: one inventory of harms removed), and Changxi 常羲 bathing the moon while also keeping the calendar. This is not that primitive science was backward; it is **the victory of a culture of keeping accounts**: in ancient China myth was taken onto the books by the official historians' spirit of "esteeming use", and a god must **have done service to the people** before enjoying a place in the sacrificial canon (*Book of Rites* (*Liji* 礼记), "Jifa" 祭法 ('The Law of Sacrifices'): 以死勤事、以劳定国、能御大灾、能捍大患 — otherwise "不在祀典" ('dying in the diligent pursuit of one's duties, settling the state by one's toil, being able to ward off a great disaster, being able to withstand a great calamity' — if not these, 'not in the sacrificial canon'). (Our translation.)). And so the aesthetic core of Chinese myth is not fear and the sublime, but **bending to the task until one's strength is spent**: even if Kuafu dies of thirst, there must still be a turning into a forest; even if Jingwei drowns, the sea must still be filled — to read the myths is to read the system by which early China assessed the spirit.
:::

::: history From "the Master did not speak of them" to a national IP {#sec-ip}
A two-thousand-year history of how myth was treated: in the **pre-Qin**, the Confucians "不语怪力乱神" ('did not speak of prodigies, force, disorder or spirits' (Our translation.)), yet they requisitioned them without ceremony (the Masters discussed their learning by parable, and the shaman-books of the mountains and seas were used all the same); in the **Han**, pictorial stones depict Nüwa and Fuxi 伏羲 as human-headed and snake-bodied, facing one another (the pictorial archive grows greatly), and Wang Chong's 王充 *Lunheng* 论衡 (*Balanced Discourses*) audits the myths with the firepower of "疾虚妄" ('detesting the false and the baseless') (the Han already had fact-checkers); in the **Song and the Ming**, notebook literature (*biji*) adds further material but little system; in the **early twentieth century**, scholars tidy the heritage with modern disciplines (the divisions of the old learning are reorganised, and ballads and myth enter the gate of the new learning); in **1923**, the preface and examples of Gu Jiegang's *Gushi bian* sing out "层累地造成的中国古史" ('the ancient history of China built up layer upon layer' (Our translation.)), and the study of myth has from then on a scalpel for dating; in **1981**, Yuan Ke's 袁珂 *Zhongguo shenhua chuanshuo* 中国神话传说 (*Myths and Legends of China*), revised and enlarged several times from the middle of the last century, becomes the hub of a collected corpus of myth and of its popularisation; **from 2015**, *Xiyouji zhi Dasheng guilai* 西游记之大圣归来 (*Journey to the West: The Great Sage Returns*), *Nezha zhi Motong jiangshi* 哪吒之魔童降世 (*Nezha: The Demon Child Descends upon the World*) and others turn mythic material into box-office IP, and games and web fiction follow — **myth is the only civil-service establishment, in the Chinese world of narrative, that lives for ever and never grows old**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in using myth
One, **reading them as historical fact**: myth is not primitive news; it is a drama of ideas — in the study of myth, Yu the Great is first of all "the virtue of controlling the waters", not a report on water conservancy (between history and legend lies Gu Jiegang's layer of accumulation). Two, **using a late book to prove an early belief**: to take the *Sanwu liji* as proof that "Pangu was a belief of the Xia and the Shang" is the same as taking a biography of 2020 as proof of the folkways of 1920. Three, **taking a variant text as the original**: the Queen Mother of the West, from the tiger's teeth and leopard's tail of the *Shanhaijing* to the lady of the Jade Pool, is two thousand years of secondary creation; any "original version" is some one layer of the accumulation. Four, **one-way forced comparison**: see a flood and say it comes from Babylon, see Pangu and say he comes from India — the proper duty of comparative mythology is **the parallel study of influence**; when the chain of evidence is not enough, each being beautiful in its own beauty is the most honest provisional conclusion.
:::

::: exercise A chain of variant texts {level=1}
Build, for the "Queen Mother of the West", a four-stop chain of variant texts (*Shanhaijing* → *Mutianzi zhuan* 穆天子传 (*Account of Mu, Son of Heaven*) → *Huainanzi* → Han pictorial stones and the later plays of the Jade Pool), one sentence of features for each stop.
::: solution
Reference: the *Shanhaijing*, "其状如人，豹尾虎齿而善啸，是司天之厉及五残" ('In form like a person, with a leopard's tail and a tiger's teeth, and skilled at whistling; the one in charge of heaven's pestilence and the five destructions.' (Our translation.)) (a baleful god, master of pestilential plague) → the *Mutianzi zhuan*, a banquet at the Jade Pool with songs offered in reply (a half-humanised queen of the West) → the *Huainanzi*, "羿请不死之药于西王母" ('Yi asked the Queen Mother of the West for the drug of no-death.' (Our translation.)) (the female immortal in charge, who holds the drug of no-death) → Han pictorial stones and later works such as the *Han Wudi neizhuan* 汉武帝内传 (*Inner Biography of Emperor Wu of the Han*): a poised and dignified noble lady, the host of the Flat-Peach Gathering — the four stops are four hundred years of the running ledger of an audit of belief. Marking points: the features cited are accurate, and each stop indicates a "motive for the rewriting" (from fear, to friendship, to seeking immortality, to worldly feasting and pleasure).
:::

::: exercise An inventory of harms removed {level=2}
The "Benjing xun" of the *Huainanzi* records that in the time of Yao "十日并出" ('ten suns came out together') and the harms of Yayu, Zaochi, Jiuying, Dafeng, Fengxi and the long snake. Please arrange them as a table of "type of disaster — civil servant" (the ten suns = drought; Fengxi = a plague of beasts; the rest by the same reasoning), and explain why the history of monsters in Chinese myth is equivalent to a history of disaster reduction.
::: solution
Main points: ten suns out together (drought) — Yi shoots; Jiuying (a monster of water and fire) — kill; Dafeng (a beast of wind disaster) — drive off; Yayu and Zaochi (fierce beasts / formidable foes) — strike; Fengxi and the long snake (a plague of swine, a plague of snakes) — slay. The direction of the conclusion: the monster's name = the disaster's name; removing the harms = a **collective narrative of merit** in controlling floods, ending drought and driving out pestilence, parting ways, in the centre of gravity of the narrative, from Greek heroes who fight for honour and for love — the reading as a "history of disaster reduction" is the narrative version of "service done to the people". If the table holds and the conclusion stands to reason, that is full marks.
:::

::: summary
The three keys to Chinese myth: **the way it survives** (fragments of gold, two warehouses, late books — check the household registration before talking of belief), **the base colour of the spirit** (only service to the people gets one onto the establishment, and the posts of tragedy belong to the establishment too), **the method** (Gu Jiegang's knife of layered accumulation: a myth's year of birth is more worth asking about first than its content). Myth is not the prologue to literary history; it is its resident editorial office — in the three chapters that follow we enter the *Book of Songs*: the people of Zhou edited ballads into a classic, and Chinese literature had a canon for the first time.
:::
