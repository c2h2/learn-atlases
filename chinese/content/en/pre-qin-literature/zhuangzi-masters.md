---
title: "Zhuangzi and the other Masters"
---

Mencius writes argument as a debating match; Zhuangzi writes it as a dream — a butterfly, a great fish, Robber Zhi (Dao Zhi 盗跖) and a skeleton take the stage in turn. By the time Xunzi sits upright composing his books, and Han Fei grinds the knife and sends up a memorial, the prose of the Masters has run the whole course from 'speech' (语) to 'writing' (文) to 'law' (法).

## Zhuangzi's revolution in literary form {#sec-zhuang}

::: definition Nine-tenths parables (*yùyán* 寓言); goblet words (*zhīyán* 卮言) come forth daily {#def-yuyan}
The verdict on the whole book takes Lu Xun's 鲁迅 settled judgement in *Han wenxue shi gangyao* 汉文学史纲要 ('An outline history of Han literature'): “其文则**汪洋辟阖，仪态万方**，晚周诸子之作，莫能先也” — 'As for his writing, it is **ocean-vast, opening and closing at will, of myriad bearings**; among the works of the Masters of the late Zhou, none can come before it.' (Our translation.) — **the constitution of the imagination in Chinese prose**.
:::

::: definition The debate on the Hao bridge: a debate with no loser {#ex-hao}
“鲦鱼出游从容，是鱼之乐也。”“子非鱼，安知鱼之乐？”“子非我，安知我不知鱼之乐？” ('The minnows swim out at their ease; this is the joy of the fish.' 'You are not a fish; how do you know the joy of the fish?' 'You are not I; how do you know that I do not know the joy of the fish?' (Our translation.)) (《秋水》, 'Autumn Floods') — please analyse the **logical structure** of this wrangle and its **aesthetic conclusion**.
::: solution
A term thrown in for good measure: Hui Shi's 惠施 'ten theses on going through things' (历物十事) and the 'twenty-one theses' of the disputers recorded in 'The World' (《天下》) — including “一尺之捶，日取其半，万世不竭” ('A stick one *chi* long: take half of it away each day, and through ten thousand ages it is not exhausted.' (Our translation.)) — are the School of Names (*míngjiā* 名家) in formal dress; the stage costume of the *Zhuangzi* is outside and inside to that dress. One is charged with making problems captious, the other with making problems cosmic. A *chi* (尺) is a unit of length; its size changed over time.
:::
:::

## Xunzi and Han Fei: two ways of closing an argument {#sec-xunhan}

::: definition From eloquence to the essay to the knife and brush {#def-xunhan}
**The *Xunzi*:** thirty-two chapters, **the literary form of the individual collection embodied** — chapter titles that are titles of essays ('Encouraging Learning' 《劝学》, 'Cultivating the Self' 《修身》, 'A Discourse on Heaven' 《天论》) are themselves a revolution in bibliography. Two treasures of form: 'Encouraging Learning' **builds a tower out of copious metaphor and parallelism** (“青，取之于蓝，而青于蓝” 'Blue is taken from the indigo plant, and is bluer than the plant'; “不积跬步，无以至千里” 'If you do not accumulate half-steps, there is no way to reach a thousand *li*.' (Our translation.) — Mencius's army of metaphors is reorganised into a systematic project); the 'Rhapsodies' chapter (《赋篇》) lodges words in things and writes separately of five objects — ritual, knowledge, cloud, silkworm and needle — and is one of the pre-Qin specimens of the place where the rhapsody (*fu*) got its name; 'Chengxiang' (《成相》) writes a political textbook in folk song-words on the measure 'three, three, seven, four, seven' — **Xunzi is the only pre-Qin Master who deliberately worked at literary form**. A *li* (里) is a unit of distance; its length changed over time.
**The *Han Feizi*:** fifty-five chapters, the grand synthesis of the Legalist school (*fǎjiā* 法家) and **a stylist of scepticism**. 'On the Difficulties of Persuasion' (《说难》) writes of the danger of offering persuasion — “凡说之难，在知所说之心” 'In general, the difficulty of persuasion lies in knowing the mind of the one who is to be persuaded.' (Our translation.) — **China's first essay in the psychology of persuasion**. 'Refutations I' (《难一》), 'Refutations II' (《难二》) and 'Refuting Positional Power' (《难势》) form a set of **essays of refutation**, setting up the face of the question themselves and objecting item by item — a court from which the adversary is absent, the violent aesthetics of logic. The parables of the man of Chu who sold a shield and a spear ('Refutations I'), and of waiting by a stump for a rabbit ('The Five Vermin', 《五蠹》), are as cold and hard as annexes to a written judgement — **Zhuangzi's parables are kites; Han Fei's parables are exhibits**. The learning of Xunzi produced Han Fei and Li Si 李斯: the same teacher taught a man of letters and an engineer — the expansive parallelism of Li Si's 'Memorial against the Expulsion of Guest Officers' (《谏逐客书》) is precisely the prose method of Xunzi's school, the pre-Qin graduation rite of the memorial form.
:::

::: history Two thousand years of Zhuangzi {#sec-zhuangxy}
**Han:** an ancient text of fifty-two chapters circulated (the commentaries of Sima Biao 司马彪, Cui Zhuan 崔譔 and others); in the early Han, Huang-Lao 黄老 (the teaching of the Yellow Emperor and Laozi) held the state, and Zhuangzi entered the grove of the prominent schools; **Wei and Jin:** Guo Xiang's 郭象 commentary in thirty-three chapters was fixed as the single authority; **mysterious learning (*xuánxué* 玄学) honoured the 'Three Mysteries'** (三玄: the *Laozi*, the *Zhuangzi* and the *Book of Changes*); “庄老告退，而山水方滋” ('Zhuang and Lao withdraw, and mountains and waters begin to flourish.' (Our translation.)) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Elucidating Poetry' — verse of mysterious discourse ebbs, and landscape poetry comes ashore out of Zhuangzi's eyes); **Tang:** in the Tianbao era an edict conferred the title **True Scripture of Nanhua** (《南华真经》), and the Daoist system incorporated it; **Song and Ming:** explaining Zhuangzi by Chan and Chan by Zhuangzi became the mutual fashion, and men of letters honoured Zhuangzi as a spiritual back garden; **Qing:** Xuan Ying's 宣颖 *Nanhua jing jie* 南华经解 ('Explication of the Nanhua Scripture') and Liu Fengbao's 刘凤苞 *Nanhua xuexin bian* 南华雪心编 ('The Nanhua, compiled for a snow-pure heart') — the full account of **the prose-study way of reading** (the craft of the piece, of the sentence and of the word all demanded from Zhuangzi); **modern times:** Lu Xun's *Han wenxue shi gangyao* fixed the judgement “汪洋辟阖” ('ocean-vast, opening and closing at will'), and Feng Youlan's 冯友兰 (Fung Yu-lan) *A History of Chinese Philosophy* (《中国哲学史》) regards Zhuangzi as 'philosophy within philosophy' (“哲学中的哲学”); the academic line of Zhuangzi studies opens; **today:** 'Cook Ding carves the ox' (“庖丁解牛”) enters the epigraphs of science textbooks, and 'moistening one another with spit' (“相濡以沫”) enters the lyrics of popular songs — **Zhuangzi is the standby psychological power supply of Chinese culture**.
:::

::: intuition Why imagination is the hard principle {#int-xiangxiang}
In the contest of the Masters' prose, Mencius's weapon is momentum, Xunzi's weapon is system, and Han Fei's weapon is the knife — Zhuangzi's weapon is **to lead the opponent off the battlefield**. To speak of 'the small and the great' (小大), he does not argue; he first lets you see a great fish, several thousand *li* long, turn into a bird. To speak of 'death and life' (死生), he lets the skeleton itself open its mouth and, with courtesy, decline to return to the living. To speak of 'knowing' (知), he becomes a butterfly. **Argument persuades the brain; imagination rewrites the right to ask** — this is the greatest battle literature has won in the department of philosophy, and it is the scene where Chinese prose signed the covenant of 'thinking in images' (*yixiang* 意象) (the ancestral contract of [[rhetoric/figures-of-meaning]]).
:::

::: warning Three prohibitions in reading the Masters
First, **discarding the book on account of the person**: skipping the psychology of 'On the Difficulties of Persuasion' because Han Fei is 'harsh and scant of kindness' (刻薄寡恩), and missing the literary form of 'Encouraging Learning' because of Xunzi's 'human nature is evil' (性恶) — style and doctrine are kept on separate accounts. Second, **taking the parables for recorded sayings of Zhuangzi**: the crucian carp (鲋鱼), Robber Zhi and the noble son of Ren (任公子) are all **roles**; outside the seven Inner Chapters, much is expansion by later followers — 'Zhuangzi' is a family of texts, not a single person. Third, **reading the 'Refutations' chapters as a wrangle**: their method is the internal critique of 'attacking his shield with his own spear' (以其矛攻其盾) (the idiom 自相矛盾, 'spear and shield against oneself', comes from this); doubt is a method, not a position.
:::

::: exercise An imitation in goblet words {level=1}
Using the method of 'nine-tenths parable' (寓言十九), write a passage of three hundred characters. With a non-human role (a cloud, a steelyard or an old mobile phone — choose one), talk about 'the useful and the useless' (有用与无用). The role must have a character; the claim must be carried by the plot.
::: solution
Marking: (1) the role's logic is consistent in itself (the steelyard complains of the cost of being 'straight', 直); (2) the claim is not spoken for the role ('the use of the useless', 无用之用, is brought out by the story turning over; the role is not made to shout a slogan); (3) there is one loose sentence in the manner of goblet words — it suddenly runs off the subject and suddenly hits the mark (for example, “我年轻时称金，老了称葱——金轻葱重，人间秤知道” 'When I was young I weighed gold; grown old, I weigh spring onions — gold is light and onions are heavy, and the steelyards of the human world know.' (Our translation.)). One of these is a pass; all three count as an A.
:::
:::

::: exercise Judgement at the Hao bridge {level=2}
From the standpoint of logic, write a judgement on the debate at the Hao bridge. Are Hui Shi's two questions in 安知 ('how do you know') synonymous? Is Zhuangzi's close, 请循其本 ('please follow it back to the root'), an effective response, or a termination of the debate? Use only terms and wording from within the lesson (the disputers of names; uniting sameness and difference, *hétóngyì* 合同异). Do not cite works not yet read.
::: solution
Points for the judgement: Hui Shi's first question asks after the **grounds of knowing** (on the strength of what do you know); the second is a rhetorical question by way of counterattack (you are not I; how do you know my knowing) — the two questions do not have the same scope. Zhuangzi's last sentence, “我知之濠上也” ('I know it from here above the Hao.' (Our translation.)), re-reads 安知 as 'knowing it at what place', making use of the semantic elasticity of the interrogative; logically this is equivocation. 'Please follow it back to the root' (请循其本) draws the debate back to the **level of pragmatics** (when you first asked me, you had already presupposed that I knew — a counter-move that uses the questioner's presupposition). It is effective but not complete: it wins the dialogue, and it does not win the argument. The verdict: Hui Shi wins on logic, Zhuangzi wins on rhetoric — the text deliberately leaves the reader in a dilemma. This is the *Zhuangzi*'s way of setting the question.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the Masters and the *shi* take the stage {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters. From the question and answer of the *Analects* to the memorials of the *Han Feizi*, how does prose form record the rise, the division and the re-incorporation of the stratum of the *shi* 士? Requirements: (1) take the formal features of two writers as evidence (the triple jump of recorded sayings, single-author book and memorial); (2) take the gist of the order to burn the books as the reference for the close; (3) in the conclusion discuss 'the symbiosis of freedom of thought and literary form'.
::: solution
Marking points: the material of the triple jump is accurate (the *Analects* and the *Mencius* are records of private teaching; the *Xunzi* is an individual book; Han Fei leads on to the memorial — **from the classroom to the study to the court**); the order to burn the books is cited in its gist (非秦纪者皆烧之、博士官所藏除外 'Whatever is not a chronicle of Qin is to be burned, excepting what the offices of the Erudites keep.' (Our translation.)); the conclusion can point out that the moment when the literary forms of the Hundred Schools were most flourishing was precisely the moment when royal power was most fragmented, and that the bargain between unifying literary form and unifying thought was carried through from the Qin to the Han — **the next act of prose is called the rhapsody** (*fu*).
:::

::: summary
The four poles of the Masters: Mencius fits argument with volume, Zhuangzi fits it with a cosmos, Xunzi fits it with beams and pillars, Han Fei fits it with a knife-edge. The prose of the Masters begins in question and answer in the classroom (the *Analects*), flourishes in eloquence (Mencius), is transformed in parable (Zhuangzi), is settled in the single-author book (Xunzi), and ends in the memorial sent up (Han Fei) — the *shi* walks from schoolmaster to teacher of emperors, and then under the knife of the Qin court. The next chapter changes to the southern marshes: Qu Yuan 屈原 comes on, and poetry takes back the individual voice.
:::
