title: "Form and technique in the *Book of Songs*"

The previous chapter drew the file; this chapter goes into the workshop: why the drumbeat of the four-character line, across three thousand years, has returned to the stage only in archaising revivals; how repetition across stanzas uses the refrain to change the feeling out; ***fu*, *bi* and *xing*** (exposition, comparison, evocation) — the three oldest characters in Chinese literary theory, and the ones most quarrelled over.

## Four-character lines: the shortest line-length {#sec-siyan}

::: definition The golden age and the retirement of the four-character form {#def-siyan}
The *Book of Songs* is **mainly four-character** (a two-and-two beat: "关关/雎鸠——参差/荇菜", '*guan guan* / the *jujiu* — uneven / the water-plants'), several lines to a stanza, running on downwards in layered song. The four-character line was made to measure for Old Chinese: a language of mainly monosyllabic words, plus music and dance to a drumbeat, comes to one word a beat and a line made two and two; the beat is the unit of meaning ("桃之/夭夭", 'the peach / so tender'; the character 之 pads the sound and takes up no meaning). Its golden age is the whole of its life: the *Songs of Chu* (*Chuci*) broke it into lines of mixed length; the Han *raoge* 饶歌 imitated the outward look; **after the *Book of Songs*, the four-character line lived only twice** — Cao Cao 曹操, "东临碣石，以观沧海" ('Eastwards I come to Jieshi, to view the blue-green sea.' (Our translation.)), and four-character writing of the Mao Zedong 毛泽东 type in "江山如此多娇" ('The rivers and mountains are so full of beauty.' (Our translation.)) (one bold and deep, one political rhetoric), and in addition the inscriptions, encomia and temple-and-court musical pieces of successive ages (on full-time duty of solemnity). Why the retirement? The disyllabification of Old Chinese ([[modern-vocabulary/morphemes-words]]) made single-beat words gradually lame, and the four-character line could not swallow compound concepts — **the verse form changed generation; the revolution in word-formation came first, and the aesthetic came after**. The mnemonic for reading four-character lines: read aloud by the beat, voice the function words lightly, and let the drum do the punctuating for you.
:::

## Repetition across stanzas: the engineering of the refrain {#def-chongzhang}

::: definition Three ledgers of overlapping repetition {#def-futa}
Repetition across stanzas = the frame recurring from stanza to stanza, with particular characters changed ("采采芣苢：薄言采之→有之→掇之→捋之→袺之→襭之" — 'Gather, gather the plantain: 薄言 pick it → have it → pick it up → strip it → hold it in the skirt → tuck it in the sash.' (Our translation.) — six verbs, a montage of the actions of labour). Three ledgers: ① **the ledger of music**: the stanzaic structure of oral song set to music; to change a character is to vary the tune; the cost of memory is halved, and the life of the song in passing on is doubled (the memory-card format of an oral culture, echoing the intuition lesson [[classical-chinese-1/punctuating]]); ② **the ledger of feeling**: one singing and three sighs; overlapping repetition is **compound interest on feeling** — "知否知否" ('Do you not know, do you not know?' (Our translation.)) is still a Song writer's continuation on this same frame; ③ **the ledger of meaning**: the characters changed are the shots of the montage — in 'Plantain' (*Fouyi* 芣苢), from "采" to "有" to "掇" to "捋" to "袺" to "襭", the action grows greedier and the harvest fuller; **the whole poem has not one line expressing feeling; the progress bar is the feeling**. The repeated lines of later tune titles (*cipai*), the four-seasons tunes of folk song, and the refrains of popular song are all branch shops of this engineering.
:::

::: example Workshop dissection: 'No Clothes' {#ex-wuyi}
"岂曰无衣？与子同袍。王于兴师，修我戈矛，与子同仇。" ('Who says there are no clothes? With you I share a robe. The king is raising an army; I ready my dagger-axe and spear, and with you I share the foe.' (Our translation.)) (three stanzas of 'No Clothes' in the Airs of Qin, 《秦风·无衣》; 袍/泽/裳, 矛/戟/堂) — showing how a change of characters makes a progression inside the overlapping repetition.
::: solution
The frame of the three stanzas is not changed (同袍 → 同泽 → 同裳: from underclothes to the battle-skirt; the degree of sharing increases = the private self is yielded, grade by grade, to the bond of those who share a robe); the arms change characters (戈矛 → 戈戟 → 作堂: the drawing-up moves from weapons to the array; the machine of war heats up); each stanza closes on "与子同仇/偕作/偕行" — **同仇 (a consensus of feeling) → 偕作 (getting into action) → 偕行 (setting off for the front)**. Inside the shell of the overlapping repetition runs a straight line of the progress of mobilisation. The hardest poem in the *Book of Songs*, and yet the technique is the most "simple": the procedure is written into the refrain. **Repetition across stanzas is not repetition: the frame is conserved, and the shot is displaced.**
:::
:::

## *Fu*, *bi* and *xing*: a thousand-year court for three characters {#sec-fubixing}

::: definition The written judgement on the three meanings {#def-sanyi}
The designation comes from the "six poems" (六诗: 风赋比兴雅颂 — the Airs, exposition, comparison, evocation, the Odes and the Hymns) in the Offices of Spring (春官) chapter of the *Rites of Zhou* (*Zhouli* 周礼); the 'Mao Preface' (*Mao shi xu* 毛诗序) renamed them the "six principles" (六义) — **they were at root six subjects in the music officers' teaching, and a thousand years of literary theory read them as six techniques**. The verdict rests on three sentences of Zhu Xi 朱熹 (calibrated back and forth between the *Shi jizhuan* 诗集传, *Collected Commentaries on the Songs*, and the *Lunyu jizhu* 论语集注, *Collected Annotations on the Analects*): **赋者，敷陈其事而直言之** ('*Fu* means setting the matter out and speaking of it directly.' (Our translation.)) ("五月斯螽动股，六月莎鸡振羽" — 'In the fifth month the locust moves its legs; in the sixth month the katydid shakes its wings.' (Our translation.) — narrative laid out, and walking straight onto the stage); **比者，以彼物比此物** ('*Bi* means using that thing as a comparison for this thing.' (Our translation.)) ("手如柔荑，肤如凝脂" — 'Hands like soft young rushes; skin like congealed fat.' (Our translation.) — the figures of simile and metaphor); **兴者，先言他物以引起所咏之词** ('*Xing* means first speaking of some other thing, so as to lead in the words one sings.' (Our translation.)) ("关关雎鸠，君子好逑" — '*Guan guan* cry the *jujiu*; a good mate for the gentleman.' (Our translation.) — the bird sings off to the side, and the poem opens over here). The trouble is with "*xing*": the *jujiu* and love **do not fit in reason, but lock together in sound** — for two thousand years, interpreters of the *Songs* have either forced a link of meaning (Zheng Xuan 郑玄: *xing* is a moralised version of *bi*, "以善物喻善事", 'using a good thing to figure a good deed' (Our translation.)), or else have simply recognised only the opening impetus of sound and feeling ("*xing*" is close to an **instrument of atmosphere**: it starts the tune, sets the scene, and opens the door for feeling). Two modern schools: the **opening-formula theory** (*xing* = the opening routine of a song set to music; the study of formulas, oral-formulaic theory — "关关雎鸠" and "桃之夭夭" are a set of **ready-made modules for opening a *xing***) versus the **association theory** (thing and feeling really are connected in a hidden, minute way; the ethical legend that the *jujiu* is "挚而有别", 'devoted, and yet keeping apart', is exactly this line of thought). This lesson teaches the two files together: ***xing* is the hybrid rice of musical system and poetic meaning — when you reap the rice of musical rule, do not throw away the barnyard grass of poetic meaning.**
:::

::: definition *Bide*: the greatest dividend for later ages {#def-bide}
*Bi* and *xing* combine into one symbolic machine: the ***bide* tradition** ('matching things to virtues') — natural things acquire a moral rank: millets (黍稷) = the ancestral state ('Shu li' in the Airs of the Royal Domain, 《王风·黍离》: "行迈靡靡，中心摇摇" — 'On I go, slowly, slowly; my heart within is shaken.' (Our translation.) — the "黍离之悲", 'the grief of the millets', has had its template from then on); peach and plum = the household gate; pine and cypress = integrity; orchid and *hui* = the gentleman; the owl = one who seizes power ('The Owl' in the Airs of Bin, 《豳风·鸱鸮》: the mother bird sobs out her plaint; the first fable in a bird's words to break this ground). Once "赋比兴" had been given fixed form by the *Songs*, it became **the operating system of Chinese poetic method**: the laying-out of the Han rhapsody (the sense of *fu* expands into a genre); the fragrant herbs of the *Songs of Chu* (*bide* is enlarged into a system); the *xing*-images of Tang poetry (*xingxiang* 兴象; *xing* is promoted to *yijing* 意境, 'the realm of meaning'); the lodged intent of the song lyric (*jituo* 寄托; *bi* and *xing* fuse into one faint and boundless sorrow — Zhang Huiyan 张惠言, in the *Ci xuan* 词选, *Selection of Song Lyrics*, reads every line of Wen and Wei 温韦 as "《骚》自命", 'professing itself the *Sao*', that is *Encountering Sorrow*) — one workshop shipping the goods, and for three thousand years the supply has never broken.
:::

::: history The battle over the right to define "*xing*" {#sec-dingyiquan}
**Han**: Zheng Zhong 郑众, in his commentary on the *Rites of Zhou*, "兴者，托事于物" ('*Xing* means lodging the matter in a thing.' (Our translation.)) — the first shot of moralising; Zheng Xuan's note on the "six poems" classes *xing* as of the kind "美见" ('seeing what is fine'); **Song**: Zhu Xi's three-sentence definition settled it with one hammer-blow ("先言他物", 'first speaking of another thing'), and at the same time he admitted in his own mouth that "*xing*" is the hardest to explain — "那兴字最难看" ('That character *xing* is the hardest to make out.' (Our translation.)) (the frank admission in the *Zhuzi yulei* 朱子语类, *Classified Conversations of Master Zhu*); **Qing**: Yao Jiheng 姚际恒 and Fang Yurun 方玉润 used poetics to oppose Han Learning; from the middle of the last century on, linguistic anthropology and oral-formulaic theory entered China, and "套语和起兴" ('formulas and the opening *xing*') were put back in their place in the industry of musical song ("葛藟" and "日月", 'vines' and 'sun and moon', are each high-frequency modules); **the present**: the "conceptual metaphor" of cognitive linguistics has again opened talks to renew the contract for *bide* ("坚贞像松柏" — 'Constancy is like the pine and the cypress.' (Our translation.) — is a conceptual mapping, not rhetorical ornament). The history of defining the single character "*xing*" is a route-map of how Chinese literary theory set out from the study of the Classics, passed through aesthetics, called at cognitive science, and set out again.
:::

::: warning Three precautions in analysing technique
First, **the compulsion to stick on a category label**: every poem must be marked "*fu* / *bi* / *xing*" — the three are ideal types, not three drawers; hybrids of the "比而赋、兴而比" kind ('*bi* and yet *fu*; *xing* and yet *bi*') are what is normal on the ground (Zhu Xi himself also annotates in great numbers "比也", "兴也", and in addition "赋而比也" — 'it is *bi*', 'it is *xing*', 'it is *fu* and yet *bi*'); second, **taking the poem apart into spare parts**: once the technique has been explained, forgetting that 'No Clothes' makes one want to go on campaign, and 'Plantain' makes one want to join in and set to work — **technique is a subordinate clause in the service of why it is good**; third, **applying it across ages**: using the Song writers' "theory of *jituo*" to read everything (reading "关关雎鸠" out into an endless interlocking chain of "微言大义", 'subtle words and great principles') — in reading the *Songs*, ask first about the music of Zhou, then about the Han Confucians, and only at the end does the turn come round to the magnifying glass of the Song Confucians.
:::

::: exercise An experiment with the beat {level=1}
Rewrite one stanza of 'Reeds' (*Jianjia* 蒹葭) once as five-character lines and once as seven-character lines, read them aloud and compare, and explain where the four-character line cannot be replaced.
::: solution
A sample: the seven-character "芦苇苍苍秋露白，所谓伊人水一方" ('Reeds lush and green, the autumn dew white; the one of whom I speak is on one side of the water.' (Our translation.)) — add characters, and at once it becomes a commentary on the picture; the **scenes set side by side, a juxtaposition with zero verbs**, of the original "蒹葭苍苍，白露为霜" ('The reeds are lush and lush; the white dew becomes frost.' (Our translation.)) (two beats, four images: 蒹葭–苍苍–白露–为霜) is diluted by grammatical glue; the five-character line has the same illness. Where the conclusion should tend: the bleakness of the four-character line = juxtaposition and space left blank; simplicity of word-formation is precisely a weapon of atmosphere. The rewriting experiment is proof, on the spot, of "同义不互换" ('the same meaning is not interchangeable'), echoing [[rhetoric/choosing-sentences]].
:::
:::

::: exercise A search for *xing* modules {level=2}
In the *Book of Songs*, retrieve two examples each of the three modules "南山", "河水" and "桃李" ('the southern hill', 'the river water', 'peach and plum'), for opening a *xing* or as objects of comparison; judge whether the use in each poem is the same module (opening vs stirring feeling vs *bide*); set out your classification.
::: solution
For reference: for instance "陟彼南山" ('I climb that southern hill.' (Our translation.)) ('Cocklebur' / 'Grass-insects', 卷耳/草虫 — climbing high as an opening *xing*, plus anxious thought setting the scene); "河水清且涟猗" ('The river water is clear, and ripples besides.' (Our translation.)) ('Chopping the tan-wood', 伐檀 — a real river scene turning to argument, as an opening *xing*); "桃之夭夭" ('The peach, so tender and fair.' (Our translation.)) ('The peach so tender' / 'How rich the blossom', 桃夭/何彼秾矣 — a module of wedding festivity). Three grades of classification: a pure opening (sound), the stirring of feeling (atmosphere), *bide* (meaning); one poem may take two grades, with an explanation. Marking points: the lines cited are accurate, and the classification has a standard; do not strain to play the interpreter who must explain it all.
:::

::: exercise A short essay: three thousand years of one technique {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: choose one of "*xing*", "*bide*" and "repetition across stanzas", and write a biography of its components: ① the scene in the *Book of Songs*; ② one transformation in the *Songs of Chu* or the Han rhapsody; ③ a further transformation in Tang poetry and Song song lyric; ④ one use of your own (a composition, lyrics, or a bullet-screen comment will all do) — the conclusion answers: is a classical technique still alive in the modern age, or are only the terms left?
::: solution
Marking points: no hard errors at the four stations (if, for example, one writes "*xing*" all the way to the source, in landscape poetry, of "感物动情", 'feeling moved by responding to things', that counts as having made the link); terms correspond accurately to works; the station of one's own use is honest and concrete (a bullet-screen comment "蒹葭+1", 'Reeds +1', also counts as a specimen); the conclusion refuses both extremes (neither "活化石", 'a living fossil', nor "已死", 'already dead'), and lands on **a change of scene**: from musical song to text to the interface.
:::

::: summary
The workshop's collection slip: the four-character line = the retired champion, a drumbeat made to measure in the age of monosyllabic words (its returns to the stage depend on solemnity and on archaising); repetition across stanzas = the refrain-engineering of a conserved frame and a displaced shot (the progress bar of 'Plantain', the mobilisation order of 'No Clothes'); *fu*, *bi* and *xing* drift from the music officers' timetable into the operating system of poetic method; Master Zhu's three sentences are the precedent, and the right to define *xing* is still in court; *bide* enters natural things in the dictionary of morals, and has supplied the oil for three thousand years. Technique is a subordinate clause in the service of "why it is good" — the next chapter changes exhibition hall: historical prose, from the hard speech of the proclamations and charges of high antiquity, to the veiled words of the *Spring and Autumn* method, and on to the live broadcast of war.
:::
