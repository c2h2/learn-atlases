title: "The *Zuo Tradition*"
---

The previous chapter drew the archive; this chapter enters the theatre: five hundred and forty years of rise and fall are written as a long work with shot division, with foreshadowing, and with the characters speaking for themselves — Han Yu 韩愈 said “《春秋》谨严，《左氏》浮夸” ('The *Spring and Autumn Annals* are strict and tight; the *Zuo* is florid and extravagant.' (Our translation.)), and this 'florid extravagance' is precisely the victory of narrative.

## The book and the learning {#sec-shu}

::: definition The *Zuo* archive {#def-zuo}
The *Chunqiu Zuoshi zhuan* (*Zuo Tradition of the Spring and Autumn Annals*; also *Zuozhuan*): a *zhuan* 传 ('tradition') that explains the *Spring and Autumn Annals* by historical events (there are also the two New Text traditions, the Gongyang 公羊 and the Guliang 穀梁 — **the *Zuo* is strong on events; the *Gongyang* and the *Guliang* are strong on meaning**, and the first battleground of New Text and Old Text classical learning is right here). The record of events runs to the twenty-seventh year of Duke Ai of Lu (468 BC). It is of old ascribed to Zuo Qiuming 左丘明, Grand Historian of Lu. For two thousand years there has been no final verdict on the author; the field mostly regards it as the work of scholars of the **early Warring States**, stitching together the chronicle-notes of the historiographers of the various states and texts of the 'discourses' kind (*yu* 语). Its greatest literary legacy is **the writing of war and the speeches of envoys** (*xingren* 行人): the campaigns of Chengpu 城濮, Xiao 崤, An 鞌 and Yanling 鄢陵 are marvelled at age after age, and ranged after them are the political discourses of Zichan 子产, Yan Ying 晏婴 and Shuxiang 叔向, and hundreds of scenes of diplomacy and exile. Comparison of classic and tradition shows its character most clearly: the classic has the six characters “陨石于宋五” ('Stones fell in Song, five.' (Our translation.)), yet the world that goes with them in the tradition is a flood of visits and inquiries among the lords, of divination, of dreams and of debate at court — **the *Zuo Tradition* is not a footnote to the *Spring and Autumn Annals*; it is the entire household stock of pre-Qin narrative**. Han Yu's “《春秋》谨严，《左氏》浮夸” ('The *Spring and Autumn Annals* are strict and tight; the *Zuo* is florid and extravagant.' (Our translation.)): the 'florid extravagance' is in fact **the victory of narrative**.
:::

::: definition The four great weapons of narrative {#def-bingqi}
① **The whole board and the shot division**: a great battle first sets the chessboard, then gives the close-up — at the battle of Chengpu, it first lays out the ledger of gratitude and grievance in “退避三舍” ('withdrawing three *she*', stages of a march) and the linked chain of diplomacy before the fight; the camera then falls on the field, where separate routes join in the attack. At the battle of An the whole board is Jin and Qi contending for hegemony, but the close-up is a sentence in blood spoken on a war-chariot: “自始合，而矢贯余手及肘，余折以御，左轮朱殷，岂敢言病” ('From the first engagement an arrow has pierced my hand and reached my elbow; I snapped it off and drove on; the left wheel is dark crimson; how dare I speak of my wound?' (Our translation.)) (Xie Zhang 解张) — **macro-history and the micro-level person, in one frame**, the birth certificate of Chinese literature of war. ② **Prolepsis**: shell-and-stalk divination, dreams and verdicts on persons let the ending hang ahead of time — before the battle of Xiao, Jian Shu 蹇叔 weeps over the army: “吾见师之出，而不见其入也” ('I see the army go out, but I do not see it come in.' (Our translation.)) “勤而无所，必有悖心” ('If there is toil and nowhere to put it, there will certainly be a mind that turns against.' (Our translation.)) — the old minister's prophecy waits the whole way to be paid out, and what the reader watches is **how the process pays out fate**. ③ **To record speech is to record character**: Gong Zhiqi 宫之奇, with the commonsensical eloquence of “辅车相依，唇亡齿寒” ('The cheek and the jaw rest on each other; when the lips are gone, the teeth grow cold.' (Our translation.)), remonstrates against lending the road; Jian Shu, remonstrating by weeping, cannot hold back a hegemon's ambition — the rhetoric of the warning, and the face of not listening, are both forerunners of the technique of fiction. ④ **“君子曰”** ('The gentleman says'): the commentator who steps out once the narrative is over — the ancestor of the appraisal form (the line of “太史公曰” ('The Grand Historian says') in the *Records of the Grand Historian*, “赞曰” ('The appraisal says') in the *Book of Han*, and “臣光曰” ('Your servant Guang says') in the *Comprehensive Mirror to Aid in Government* (*Zizhi tongjian*) all acknowledge this ancestral grave).
:::

::: example The battle of Xiao: a four-act play {#ex-xiao}
The thirty-third year of Duke Xi: the Qin army makes a surprise attack on Zheng; Xian Gao 弦高 presents provisions to the army → Jian Shu remonstrates by weeping (“吾见师之出，而不见其入也” 'I see the army go out, but I do not see it come in.' (Our translation.)) → Jin annihilates the Qin army at Xiao, “匹马只轮无反者” ('Not one horse and not one wheel returned.' (Our translation.)) → Wen Ying 文嬴 asks for the three commanders; Xian Zhen 先轸, in anger, “不顾而唾” ('spat without turning to look'). Please analyse its structural art, and the hidden line of ritual (*li* 礼).
::: solution
**The aftershock act** — Wen Ying, mother of Duke Xiang of Jin (a woman of Qin), with one sentence has the three commanders released and sent back; Xian Zhen comes to court to ask what has happened, and says in anger “堕军实而长寇雠，亡无日矣” ('Cast off the army's fruits and build up the enemy, and ruin will not be a day away.' (Our translation.)), and at once “不顾而唾” ('spat without turning to look') — spitting the spittle onto the ritual between ruler and subject. The hidden line, summed up: the whole book takes 'ritual' as the judge of the narrative — Qin's defeat is first written as its “轻而无礼” ('frivolous and without ritual') (when the army passed the north gate of Zhou, those on left and right took off their helmets and dismounted, and those who leapt back onto the chariots came to three hundred chariots: a flippant review of the troops); Jin's victory is first reckoned in argument at court; and Xian Zhen's lapse of ritual in “不顾而唾” ('spitting without turning to look') answers, head to tail, his death in battle the next year, “免胄入狄师” ('took off his helmet and entered the Di army') — **character is fate, and ritual is structure**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Ritual: the unit of measure in the *Zuo Tradition*'s narrative {#int-li}
The causal chain of the *Zuo Tradition* is 'ritual — without ritual — defeat — the writing': the rise and fall of many states are strung on one measurable grammar of conduct — the order of seats at a feast, the weight of jade and silk, the humility or the arrogance of a speech, the bearing of an army as it passes a capital's gate. This is not the historian's moral obsession; it is **narrative physics**: without ritual = stress in the system, defeat = the fracture paid out, 'the gentleman says' = a flaw-detection report (a judgement of the brush on the pattern of “宋公无盟而讨，故书曰” 'The Duke of Song punished without a covenant; therefore the writing says:' (Our translation.)). The knack of reading the *Zuo Tradition*: **fix on every shot of 'without ritual', and wait for it to explode** — Ziyu's 子玉 extravagance at Chengpu in “玉缨琼弁” ('jade tassels and a jasper cap'), King Gong of Chu at Yanling taking up a bow and losing the proper bearing, Yanzi 宴子's “惟礼可以已乱” ('Only ritual can stop disorder.' (Our translation.)): all are different readings off the same instrument. This skill of 'forecasting rise and fall from details of behaviour' is what the later *Comprehensive Mirror*'s “臣光曰” ('Your servant Guang says') and the novel's “草蛇灰线” ('a snake in the grass, a line in the ash') both came to take as their scripture.
:::

::: history A thousand-year lawsuit over one book {#sec-guansi}
**Western Han**: the *Zuo* had no Erudite (*boshi* 博士); Liu Xin 刘歆 sent an official letter to the Chamberlain for Ceremonials (*taichang* 太常), and the gong opened on the great war of New Text and Old Text classical learning. **Western Jin**: Du Yu's 杜预 *Collected Explanations of the Classic and the Tradition of the Spring and Autumn* (*Chunqiu jingzhuan jijie*) created the combined edition in which 'classic and tradition are one'; the summing-up of the rules of meaning (*yili* 义例), “传以尽经” ('the tradition exhausts the classic'), is its opening preface, and it is the ancestor of the layout of the current *Zuo Tradition*. **Tang**: Kong Yingda's 孔颖达 *Correct Meaning* (*Zhengyi*) was fixed by official authority. **Song**: Confucians had many points to weigh where classic and tradition meet. **Qing**: Gu Yanwu's 顾炎武 *Record of Knowledge Gained Day by Day* (*Rizhi lu*) says of the *Zuo* “其文则史” ('As for its writing, it is history.' (Our translation.)); Liu Fenglu's 刘逢禄 *Evidential Study of the Zuo's Spring and Autumn* (*Zuoshi chunqiu kaozheng*) once more advanced “左氏不传《春秋》” ('The *Zuo* does not serve as a tradition upon the *Spring and Autumn Annals*.' (Our translation.)) (a New Text counter-attack). **Republic of China**: the Doubting Antiquity school, Qian Mu 钱穆 and others carried on the attack upon the theory of 'finalised in writing in the Warring States'; Yang Bojun's 杨伯峻 *Commentary on the Spring and Autumn and the Zuo Tradition* (*Chunqiu Zuozhuan zhu*, 1981) gathers the old explanations, the bronze inscriptions and the calendrical tables as the standard edition of today. **Further evidence from excavation**: the *Annals* (*Jinian*) from Ji Commandery 汲郡 in the Western Jin, its dates collated against the *Zuo*, opened a new phase in the collation of ancient books; the *Xinian* 系年 piece among the Tsinghua bamboo slips (made public in 2011) records events largely of the same origin as the *Zuo* — **another branch of the Warring States 'tradition of narrative by historiographers'**.
:::

::: warning Three things to guard against in reading the *Zuo Tradition*
First, **do not take the speeches for a transcript of what was said**: several hundred diplomatic speeches have passed through the historiographers' polishing of the wording (“记言半出拟构” 'What is recorded as speech is half a simulated composition.' (Our translation.) is common knowledge in the field — the difficulty of imitating speech lies in **imitating the voice of that time**, not in recording the sound). Second, **do not take divination and dreams for historical source-material**: entries of the proleptic kind are mostly an **arrangement of fulfilment after the event** (the historian's literary brush, and at the same time a device of fate) — trust their art of narrative; there is no need to trust their ledger of ghosts and spirits. Third, **do not take the *Tradition* for the *Classic***: scholars of the Gongyang school revile the *Zuo* as “不传《春秋》” ('not a tradition upon the *Spring and Autumn Annals*'), and scholars of the Old Text school defend the 'rules of meaning'; the modern closing of a lawsuit of two thousand years: **the *Zuo Tradition* is first of all a work of history and of literature from the earlier Warring States, and only secondly a classic** — the household stock of 'Spring and Autumn learning' and the household stock of '*Zuo Tradition* learning' are two ledgers, to be entered apart.
:::

::: exercise Shot division in war {level=1}
Choose the battle of Chengpu or the battle of An, and draw a table of detail and brevity for the three layers 'chessboard layer — camera layer — comment layer', explaining the measuring principle of detail and brevity in the *Zuo*'s narrative., and explaining the measuring principle of the *Zuo*'s 'being good at narrating army discipline' (“善序师律”).
::: solution
Example (An): Chessboard layer — Qi and Jin contending for hegemony, and the insult to the envoy before the battle (Xi Ke 郤克 was sent to Qi; the lady watched him from behind a curtain and laughed — the fuse by which one sentence opens a war, brought in as a footnote). Camera layer — Xie Zhang's three sentences in blood, “矢贯余手及肘” ('an arrow pierced my hand and reached my elbow'); the scene of 'the loyal servant saving his master', in which Feng Choufu 逢丑父 orders the Marquis of Qi “下取饮” ('Get down and fetch a drink.' (Our translation.)) and so lets him escape, below three circuits of the Hua Spring. Comment layer — “君子谓” ('the gentleman considers'): each is settled in his penalty and his name. Measuring principle: **where the moral point is at stake (ritual, good faith, greed, calumny), give detail; for troop numbers and the record of places, be brief** — what the *Zuo* writes is 'the victory and defeat of persons', not 'a table of the deaths of persons'.
:::

::: exercise The rhetoric of speeches {level=2}
In the twenty-sixth year of Duke Xi, Duke Xiao of Qi attacked the northern marches of Lu. The state was poor and the envoy's position weak. Lu sent Zhan Qin 展禽 (Zhan Xi 展喜) to greet the Qi army with provisions. A man of Qi asked: “室如悬磬，野无青草，何恃而不恐?” ('Your rooms are as empty as a hanging chime-stone; your fields have no green grass. What do you rely on, that you are not afraid?' (Our translation.)) Zhan Xi sets out in order King Cheng's charge and the covenant that Qi and Lu had kept generation after generation — please analyse the three layers of his **using history as a weapon**.
::: solution
Three layers: ① **Citing the archive**: the memory of the Duke of Zhou's enfeoffment, and of the covenantal charge that sons and grandsons, generation after generation, should not harm one another — using an old treaty as military force in the present (“恃先王之命” 'relying on the charge of the former kings'); ② **The tall hat of flattery**: holding up the ancestral manner of Duke Huan of Qi, “犹务柔诸侯、镇尔中国” ('still bent on conciliating the lords, and on steadying your central states'), and binding Duke Xiao to his ancestor's persona — what a contender for hegemony fears most is to be struck off by the historian's brush; ③ **Turning defence into attack**: the composure of “何恃而不恐” ('What do you rely on, that you are not afraid?') is itself deterrence — fear is the first currency on the psychological battlefield, and speeches are the nuclear weapon of the weak. Marking point: being able to present the three layers as the structure of a strategic game.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: Xian Zhen's spit {level=3}
Write eight hundred to twelve hundred characters: is “不顾而唾” ('spitting without turning to look') a lapse of ritual, or an upright minister's highlight? Discuss this together with the *Zuo Tradition*'s system of judging by 'ritual' and the political situation in Jin (reason only inside the text; do not force in history from outside): what does the historian mean to say by writing an 'anger contrary to ritual' into a narrative that is like the text of a classic? You may set it against Jian Shu's weeping (both are loyalty: one weeps, one spits, and the narrative's payment differs for each).
::: solution
Marking points: being able to see the narrative's **double bookkeeping** — the 'gentleman says' system keeps to ritual, while the narrative itself cherishes true temperament (Xian Zhen's anger is 'performed' in the text, not 'sentenced'); being able to read the death in “免胄入狄师” ('taking off his helmet and entering the Di army') as the closed loop of character (ritual's debt, repaid by himself); if the conclusion points out that "the *Zuo*'s 'ritual' is not a dogma but **an instrument for measuring tension**", the main point of the lesson has been reached.
:::

::: summary
Four lessons of the *Zuo Tradition*: shot division (the chessboard and the words in blood in one frame), prolepsis (Jian Shu's lesson in terrain, in the manner of a dirge), the recording of speech (character and speeches bearing each other out), and the causal engine of 'ritual' (without ritual — stress — fracture). It is the graduation ceremony of the annalistic form, and also the general ground on which later narrative draws its material — the wars of the *Records of the Grand Historian*, the comments of the *Comprehensive Mirror*, the novel's “草蛇灰线” ('a snake in the grass, a line in the ash'), all took their tickets from this theatre. The next chapter meets the twin jades of the Masters: the *Analects* and the *Mencius* — a turn from recording events to establishing words, and prose's 'personality of reasoned argument' opens its doors.
:::
