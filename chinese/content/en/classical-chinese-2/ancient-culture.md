title: "Ancient culture for readers"
---

By the time you have read this far in Classical Chinese II, what trips you is often no longer the grammar but the **configuration of a world-view**: months have the official names that belong to months, the stars have the establishment that belongs to the stars, and holding office has the verb-conjugation that belongs to holding office. This chapter issues you four maps — **heaven, earth, office and time**. Read them through, and the world in the old books turns from a stage set back into a real scene.

## Heaven has its establishment {#sec-tian}

::: definition The twenty-eight lodges, the twelve stations and '七月流火' {#def-tian}
**The twenty-eight lodges** = one street of inns for the moon (the moon takes about one lodge a day, and goes once round the sky in a month; seven lodges in each of the four quarters, and the four 'dormitory districts' — **Azure Dragon 苍龙, Vermilion Bird 朱雀, White Tiger 白虎 and Dark Warrior 玄武** — are the Four Images) — "**月离于毕，俾滂沱矣**" ('The moon attaches to the Bi lodge, and the rain will pour.' (Our translation.)) (*Book of Songs*, 'Jianjian zhi shi' 渐渐之石: the moon attaches to the Bi lodge, and a great rain is coming — **a plain weather station of stars and rain**). **The twelve stations and field allocation** (*fenye* 分野, 'the pairing of asterisms with lands'): twelve road signs on the ecliptic (Xingji 星纪, Xuanxiao 玄枵 … Shouxing 寿星, Dahuo 大火, Ximu 析木), then paired one for one with the provinces and states on the ground ("**星分翼轸，地接衡庐**" — 'The stars are apportioned to Yi and Zhen; the land adjoins Heng and Lu.' (Our translation.) — one line of Wang Bo 王勃 takes in astronomy and geography entire) — **an administrative division of the sky**: guest stars, comets and solar eclipses are all, by their field allocation, read as news from a locality; **a change in the stars is an alarm, and a sign in the heavens is the public mood**. **"七月流火"**: Fire = **the second star of the Heart lodge (the Great Fire star)**; "流" **is not heat so great that it flows, but sinking away to the west** — "**七月流火，九月授衣**" ('In the seventh month the Fire sinks westwards; in the ninth month clothes are given out.' (Our translation.)) speaks of **the heat retreating and the cool on its way** (授衣 = preparing for winter; **the sentence itself is the exhibit**), and its misuse as 'the weather is scorching' is a miscarriage of justice a thousand years old. In the *Book of Documents* (*Shangshu* 尚书), 'Canon of Yao' (尧典), "**日中星鸟，以殷仲春**" ('The day of medium length, the star Bird, so as to fix mid-spring.' (Our translation.)) — day and night of equal length, the Bird lodge at mid-heaven: **the stars are used to fix the spring equinox, and an observatory of high antiquity is written into the classic**.
:::

## Earth has its other names {#def-di}

::: definition The Nine Provinces, Jiang and He, and '江左' {#def-jiuzhou}
**Two editions of the Nine Provinces**: the 'Tribute of Yu' (*Yugong* 禹贡) draws the boundaries in the sentence pattern "**X惟Y**" ("**济河惟兖州**" — 'The Ji and the He delimit Yan Province.' (Our translation.) "**华阳黑水惟梁州**" — 'Huayang and the Black Water delimit Liang Province.' (Our translation.) — ruler-and-compass construction, carried out in geographical prose); 'Explaining the Lands' in the *Erya* (*Erya* 尔雅, 'Shidi' 释地) sets out another edition — **the province names of the two editions do not agree, and when an old book names a province one must first ask 'which edition of the Nine Provinces'** (to use a province name is to cite an edition of a map; **if the edition is wrong, the whole map is wrong**). **A history of 江 and 河 as proper names**: in high antiquity "江" and "河" referred specifically to the **Yangtze and the Yellow River** ("**将军战河北，臣战河南**" — 'The general fought north of the He; I fought south of the He.' (Our translation.) — 河 = the Yellow River; today "江河" is already a common noun — **a case of extension, common noun ⇄ proper name**; see [[classical-chinese-2/original-meanings]]). **Directional words for waters**: **江表** (seen from the court, beyond the Yangtze — the official point of view of Sun Wu 孙吴); **江左 = 江东** (an old map is oriented by **sitting in the north and facing south**, so that left is east and right is west — **'江左风流' is the image the Eastern Jin and the Southern Dynasties had of themselves**). **山东 and 山西** take the Xiao Mountains (崤山) and Mount Hua (华山) as their axis, and **are not provincial borders**; **中国–中原–海内–天下** ('the central states — the Central Plain — within the seas — all under heaven') are four concentric circles — **every circle is a political claim**.
:::

## Offices have their verb-conjugations {#def-guan}

::: definition Offices have their verb-conjugations {#def-zhiguan}
**The skeleton at a glance**: Qin and Han, the **Three Dukes and Nine Ministers** (Chancellor — Grand Commandant — Censor-in-chief; the Nine Ministers largely handled the private business of the imperial house — **a living fossil of family and state as one**) → Sui and Tang, the **Three Departments and Six Ministries** (the Secretariat (中书) drafts the edicts, the Chancellery (门下) seals or returns them, the Department of State Affairs (尚书) carries them out; Personnel (吏), Revenue (户), Rites (礼), War (兵), Justice (刑) and Works (工) = the administrative modules in use down to the end of the Qing) → the Song, the **Two Councils and Three Fiscal Commissions** (the Secretariat-Chancellery (中书门下) holds civil government, the Bureau of Military Affairs (枢密院) holds the army, the Three Fiscal Commissions (三司) hold the finances — **the chancellor's power is cut into three**) → the Ming abolishes the Chancellor and the work passes into the **Grand Secretariat** (内阁); the Qing has the **Grand Council** (军机处) (**a staff that kneels to take the brush and record edicts = the final shape of imperial power**). **A conjugation table for the verbs of appointment and removal**: **拜** ("**拜相如为上大夫**" — 'He appointed Xiangru Senior Grand Master.' (Our translation.)); **除 / 授** (除 = removing the old office and taking up the new); **辟 / 征** (when a senior official recruits on his own authority it is called 辟; when the emperor specially recruits a commoner, 布衣, it is called 征); **迁** (the default is a **promotion** — "**再迁为太史令**" — 'Promoted again, he became Director of the Grand Scribes.' (Our translation.)); **转 / 徙** (a sideways transfer); **出** (a capital official posted out); **左迁 / 放 / 窜 / 逐** (demotion and exile: **"左" means demotion** — "**一封朝奏九重天，夕贬潮阳路八千**" — 'At dawn one memorial went up to the ninefold heaven; by evening, banished to Chaoyang, a road of eight thousand *li*.' (Our translation.) The length of the *li* changed from period to period.); **罢 / 免 / 黜 / 废 / 夺** (leaving office, and deprivation); **致仕** (the formal name of retirement) + **乞骸骨** ('let these old bones go home' — **the bodily rhetoric of an application to retire**). **The slang of rank**: the Han simply put the salary grade where the office would stand — a commandery governor was called "**two thousand *shi***" — **the pay slip became a nickname**. The *shi* was the unit of that grain salary; its size changed by period.
:::

::: example A scalpel for one sentence of a date {#ex-riqi}
Take apart three sample dates: ① the 'Rhapsody on the Red Cliff' (*Chibi fu* 赤壁赋), "**壬戌之秋，七月既望**" ('In the autumn of the year *renxu*, the seventh month, after the full moon.' (Our translation.)), and, in the same piece, "**徘徊于斗牛之间**" ('lingering between Dou and Niu.' (Our translation.)); ② the 'Latter Rhapsody on the Red Cliff', "**是岁，十月之望**" ('That year, the full moon of the tenth month.' (Our translation.)); ③ the 'Preface to the Orchid Pavilion Collection' (*Lanting ji xu* 兰亭集序), "**永和九年，岁在癸丑，暮春之初**" ('The ninth year of Yonghe, the year being *guichou*, at the beginning of late spring.' (Our translation.)). Question: which calendrical layers does each sentence give? Which layer does each lack? Which day is "既望"? Why does "暮春" not write "三月" outright?
::: solution
**①**: the stems and branches record the **year** (*renxu* 壬戌 — **a stem-and-branch year does not carry a year of the common era with it, and must be converted by way of an emperor's reign-dating**) + an **ordinal month** (the seventh month of the Xia calendar) + "**既望 = after the day of the full moon, usually the sixteenth**" (望 = the fifteenth; 既 = already past; words of the same family: **朏** = the new moon around the third day, **魄** = the waning light after the sixteenth — **high antiquity kept its accounts by the moon, and a date came with its own diagram of the phase**); "徘徊于斗牛之间": the moon is moving along between the Dou lodge and the Niu lodge — **what Su Shi 苏轼 used to tell the time was a clock in the sky; the night scene is a star calendar**. ② "是岁，十月之望": **"是岁" points back to the year-dating of the previous piece — the two Red Cliff pieces share one coordinate system**; the fifteenth of the tenth month, the full-moon day of the first month of winter — **the latter rhapsody's "月白风清" ('the moon white and the wind clear') is signed and issued by the date**. ③ "永和九年，岁在癸丑，暮春之初": **reign title + that year's stems and branches + a seasonal division**, the three layers all present — **the standard format of a Chinese date after the Eastern Han**; "暮春" does not write "三月", but uses a **season-slicing word** (the last ten-day period of the third month of spring), and it also brings with it, of itself, the allusion-filter of the *Analects*, "**暮春者，春服既成**" ('In late spring, when the spring clothes are already made.' (Our translation.)) — **once a calendrical word has entered a classic, it forever carries one further layer of feeling**. A reminder of the missing layer: none of the three examples records the **double-hour** (*shichen* 时辰) — **only the histories are precise to the double-hour; men of letters leave it out as they please; annalistic writing and lyrical prose do not share the same precision of clock**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Common knowledge is a cross-line verifier {#int-yanzheng}
The real use of cultural common knowledge is not looking a word up in a dictionary; it is **cross-checking on four lines**: the astronomy in a single sentence (lodges, phases of the moon), the calendar (reign titles, new moon and full moon), the offices (the birth-years of office-titles) and the geography (the history of administrative regions) must **all hold at once** — if any one line fails to match, either you have read it wrong, or **the book has a problem**. This is exactly the courtroom logic by which Yan Ruoqu 阎若璩 exposed the forged *Old Text Book of Documents* (*Guwen Shangshu* 古文尚书): **internal evidence from the language** (the '市买' case in the lesson on function words, [[classical-chinese-2/particles]]) **plus the anachronism of offices, place names and the calendar, the four lines closing in — a full Manchu–Han banquet of the study of detecting forgeries**; Wang Guowei 王国维's 'dual evidence' is the archaeological version of the same principle. One precept comes as a by-product: **common knowledge is not to be a lone witness** — however hard the literal evidence for "七月流火" may be, it still has to be checked against the phenology of the whole piece ("九月授衣" 'in the ninth month clothes are given out' (Our translation.); "八月载绩" 'in the eighth month we begin to spin' (Our translation.)); **where common knowledge ends is still a check against the context, not a display of memory**.
:::

::: history A textbook history of common knowledge {#sec-jiaoke}
**Pre-Qin**: common knowledge grew inside institutions and poems ('The Seventh Month', *Qiyue* 七月, is a calendar of farm work; the 'Monthly Ordinances', *Yueling* 月令, are the government's table of what to do — **life was knowledge, and there was originally no need for a course called 'common knowledge'**); **Han**: the *Erya*, 'Explaining Heaven', 'Explaining Buildings', 'Explaining Implements', 'Explaining Music' (释天, 释宫, 释器, 释乐) — **the first time the world was compiled into categories**; the *Shiming* 释名 (*Explanation of Names*) divides its chapters by classes of meaning (provinces and states, mountains and waters, food and drink, clothing and techniques — **taxonomy is a world-view**); **Tang**: the *Yiwen leiju* 艺文类聚 ('Classified Collection of Arts and Letters'; compiled by imperial order in 624) — **a 'Tang search engine', with categories of matter first and poems and prose after**; **Song**: the *Taiping yulan* 太平御览 ('Imperial Reader of the Taiping Era'), one thousand *juan* (one of the four great classified books, *leishu*); **Ming**: the *Sancai tuhui* 三才图会 ('Illustrated Compendium of the Three Powers'; divided under the three powers, heaven, earth and human affairs) — **an illustrated encyclopaedia with the picture on the left and the history on the right**; **Qing**: the *Gujin tushu jicheng* 古今图书集成 ('Complete Collection of Books and Illustrations, Ancient and Modern'; completed in 1726), six compilations and thirty-two canons — **the limit of a database on paper**; **the Republic**: the 'national culture' units of the new-style textbooks — **common knowledge turned from the classified book into a civics lesson**; **the present**: the **'Database of Biographical Materials for Figures through the Chinese Dynasties' (CBDB)** joins the records of office, the kin and the circles of acquaintance of several hundred thousand historical persons into a network of relations — **the study of common knowledge has evolved from looking up an entry to running a graph**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of common knowledge
**The backwards misuse of the '七月流火' kind** (dressing today's heat in old words — **before quoting, first check the phenological testimony of the original line**); **gauging ancient territory by the place names of today** (seeing "河南" and drawing a provincial border — **"河南" in the 'Basic Annals of Xiang Yu' (*Xiang Yu benji* 项羽本纪) is south of the He**; the history of administrative regions is a dynamic layer, and the base map must be changed for a 'historical map'); **using office-titles as if they held across dynasties** (taking "尚书令" for a Qin office — **every office-title has its own year and month of birth**; see [[classical-chinese-2/ancient-culture]], the skeleton of this table); **taking common knowledge for the conclusion** (the correspondence of lodges with field allocation **is not** astronomy but political semiotics — **common knowledge tells you 'what this is'; you still have to press on and ask 'who is using it, whom it deceives, and why it has power'** — otherwise you have only recited the ancients' world-view until it has become your own).
:::

::: exercise Match the aliases {level=1}
Match the four groups, and for each invent one counter-example of **use in the wrong context**: 江左／江东, 桑榆／暮年, 桑梓／故乡, 杜康／酒.
::: solution
Marking: the four groups correspond without error (**'桑榆' is not an entry in a flora — "失之东隅，收之桑榆" ('Lose it at the eastern corner; gather it in at mulberry and elm.' (Our translation.)) and "日暮桑榆" ('the sun sets at mulberry and elm') corroborate each other in the two places** — the genealogy method for the character 与, from the previous chapter, is reused here: **a word's map has to be drawn twice before it holds**); the counter-examples are honest (the commonest crashes: taking 桑梓 for a garden, and using 杜康 as a personal name in the wrong slot — **an alias is a compressed sentence; use it wrong, and you have memorised the lesson wrong**).
:::

::: exercise Cross-line verification officer {level=2}
Invent one, or find in a collected works one sentence of a date or an office that contains **two or more layers** of cultural information (for example "**元和十年，予左迁九江郡司马**" — 'In the tenth year of Yuanhe, I was demoted to *Sima* of Jiujiang Commandery.' (Our translation.) — the preface to Bai Juyi 白居易's 'Song of the Pipa' (*Pipa xing* 琵琶行) ✓), and separate out where the reign-title layer, the office layer and the geography layer each point, in a year of the common era and in a place of today; then check again with any one tool (a chronological table, the *Historical Atlas of China*, *Zhongguo lishi ditu ji* 中国历史地图集), and note the process of the check.
::: solution
A reference answer: "元和十年" = 815 (a reign title of Xianzong 宪宗; looked up in the *Chronological Tables of Chinese History*, *Zhongguo lishi jinian biao* 中国历史纪年表); "左迁" = demotion; "九江郡司马" = a supernumerary sinecure (the Tang system of *zhou* and commandery offices: the *sima* was often a post in which to place officials under penalty — **"江州司马" became from then on the literary code for a demoted official** — the establishment behind the tears on the blue gown). Marking: **each of the three layers has a source**; a margin of error is allowed (muddle in the changing of a commandery's name must be marked as doubtful — **"not found" is also a verification report that passes**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the ancients' sky {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: "Field allocation, star divination and auspicious omens were the ancients' **system of political communication**, not a leftover of superstition" — take as your example one record of a celestial phenomenon (any one of a solar eclipse, a comet or a guest star; sample: the classic of the *Spring and Autumn* (*Chunqiu* 春秋), "**秋七月，有星孛入于北斗**" — 'Autumn, the seventh month: a comet entered the Northern Dipper.' (Our translation.) — the comet record of the fourteenth year of Duke Wen of Lu 鲁文公 (613 BC), regarded as one of the early observations of Halley's Comet), and analyse **how the phenomenon was read off, submitted to the throne and answered (withdrawing from the hall, reducing the meals, an edict seeking frank speech)**; and set beside it mechanisms of the same kind in modern society (opinion polls, satellite cloud charts, economic data), discussing **the dependence of an 'authoritative reading' on its medium**.
::: solution
Marking points: the canonical example is accurate (the classic of the *Spring and Autumn*, the fourteenth year of Duke Wen of Lu = 613 BC; "孛" = a comet — **one of the earliest records of Halley's Comet in the world**; the qualifier 'one of' may be kept); the mechanism is complete (the closed loop **celestial phenomenon → the discourse of calamities and anomalies → the emperor issues an edict / withdraws from the principal hall → seeks frank speech**, making clear that 'heaven' is the opposition's **court of final appeal**); the modern analogy holds (an authoritative reading depends on instruments / classics / models — **whoever holds the gauges defines the will of Heaven, or the data**); if the conclusion can land on "**to read the heaven of the old books is also to read the people who write heaven**" (Our translation.), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Closing the accounts of the map lesson: heaven (inns · road signs · field allocation + the case of "七月流火" reversed + the tense of the moon's phase), earth (two editions of the Nine Provinces; the proper names 江 and 河 rising and sinking; 'left is east', from sitting in the north and facing south; all under heaven in four circles), office (the living fossil of family and state → the closing line, of kneeling to receive edicts; the verb-conjugation table + the nickname method of "two thousand *shi*"), time (the standard format of reign title + stems and branches + ordinal + moon phase; the double-hour depends on the occasion). The method, in one sentence: **check across the four lines, and a lone witness does not establish the case; common knowledge is in charge of 'what it is', and you still have to ask 'who is using it'**. The next chapter learns the 'meta-ability' of reading a commentary edition: read commentaries and subcommentaries as a genre — a navigation lesson in bibliography, editions and the history of scholarship.
:::
