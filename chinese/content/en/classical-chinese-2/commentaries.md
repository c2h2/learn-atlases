title: "Reading commentaries"
---

Of one and the same *Analects*, you can buy a 'speed read' finished in two hours, or you can bump into a Qing writer's *zhengyi* (正义, 'correct meaning') of eight hundred thousand characters — **two thousand years of the publishing trade stand in between**. In the previous chapter you learned 'what is written in the book'; in this chapter you learn '**which production line the book came off**': catalogue, edition and selection, three factory slips. Only if you can read them will the annotated edition you buy not be a hardback trap.

## Three factory slips {#def-changdan}

::: definition Catalogue: the tour script of scholarship {#def-mulu}
The 'Treatise on Literature' in the ***Book of Han*** opened the way — the official résumé of Liu Xin (刘歆) and his *Seven Summaries* (*Qilüe* 七略) (the sixfold division into the **Six Arts, the Masters, Poetry and Rhapsodies, Military Books, Numbers and Techniques, and Recipes and Arts** — **a map of the disciplines from the first century BC**); the 'Treatise on Classics and Books' in the ***Book of Sui*** fixed the four-part frame of **Classics, Histories, Masters and Collections**, and for the next one thousand four hundred years Chinese books all lived in these four rooms; the work that gathers it all up is the ***General Catalogue of the Complete Library of the Four Treasuries*** (*Siku quanshu zongmu* 四库全书总目): each book has a descriptive note of a hundred characters, and **before each category there is a short preface on the lineages into which the learning divided** — **the category preface is the tour script**: "**预知某类学术史，先读某类小序**" ('To know in advance the scholarly history of a given category, first read that category's short preface') (Our translation.) (both Lu Xun and Zhang Zhidong (张之洞) issued reading lists of the sort 'first read a few pieces of the *General Catalogue*' — **the *Siku General Catalogue* is, before modern times, the most usable introduction to the disciplines**, and Zhang Zhidong's ***Shumu dawen*** (书目答问, 'Answers to Questions about Books') is its late-Qing popular version). Use the three moves of bibliography: **check a book's household registration** (whether it is entered, and under which category — **classification itself is a scholarly verdict**: does *Water Margin* get into the Collections division? the fiction-writers, *xiaoshuojia*, of the Masters division?), **check one age's reading list** (the 'Treatise on Literature' keeps the Han reader's desk; the 'Treatise on Classics and Books' keeps the Sui reader's bookshelf), **check the family stock of one kind of learning** (the splitting and joining of the ten categories of the Classics division = the fault lines of the history of the study of the Classics).
:::

::: definition Edition: the family history of a book {#def-banben}
Four great production lines for old books: **manuscripts** (the mainstream before the Tang; **every time a copy is made = a child is born**), **woodblock editions** (woodblock printing spread in the late Tang and the Five Dynasties, and blocks were repaired on from Song to Yuan to Ming to Qing — for the first time a book could be 'photocopied'), **movable type** (Bi Sheng (毕昇) of the Northern Song — **movable type, in the printing trade, caught up with the market share of woodblocks only in the Qing**), **photo-reproduction and typeset printing** (the twentieth century — ***shanben* walked out of the collectors' closed rooms onto the desk**). **The three senses of a *shanben* (善本, 'fine edition'): old, rare, and excellent** — it is not that only Song and Yuan copies may be called fine; **a Qing woodblock whose collation is careful and exact is a *shanben* too**. **A textbook case, *Dream of the Red Chamber***: the manuscript line (the '*zhiben*', more than ten of them extant, mostly titled *The Story of the Stone*, **bearing the comments of Zhiyan Zhai (脂砚斋) — traces of an age when reader and author were in the same room**) and the printed line (the Chengjia edition and the Chengyi edition put in order by Cheng Weiyuan (程伟元) and Gao E (高鹗) — set and printed in one hundred and twenty chapters from 1791) are read against each other — **the 'original appearance' lies between the two reincarnations, of copying and of block-cutting**; **the first law of the study of editions: the later the woodblock edition, the neater it is, and the further it may be from the author; the earlier the manuscript, the rougher it is, and the more it may preserve slips of the tongue**.
:::

::: definition Selection: the selector's private classroom {#def-xuanben}
**Comprehensive anthologies** and **selections** are two things. After the *Wenxuan* (文选, *Selections of Refined Literature*; **compiled by Xiao Tong (萧统) — China's first literary selection**: it does not select from the Classics, the Histories or the Masters; the boundary of 'literature' was drawn by it; **for the standard of inclusion, see the 'Preface to the *Wenxuan*'**), a selection is **a private verdict on literary history**: the ***Guwen guanzhi*** (古文观止, 'Ancient-Style Prose: Where the Viewing Stops'; a schoolbook of the Kangxi reign by the uncle and nephew **Wu Chucai (吴楚材) and Wu Diaohou (吴调侯)** — **for two hundred years, the national timetable of 'ancient-style prose'; the taste of the Tongcheng school passed into general use through elementary schooling**), *Three Hundred Tang Poems* (**"熟唐诗三百首，不会作诗也会吟" ('Have the three hundred Tang poems by heart, and you can chant even if you cannot write a poem') (Our translation.) — the foundation of the proverb is this book**) — **a selection makes the reader think that he is reading Tang poetry, when in fact he is sitting in the selector's class**. Three questions for judging a selection: **whom it selected** (the reproduction of the list of classics), **whom it did not select** (silence is louder than the texts selected — **of Tao's poems the *Wenxuan* records only a few, and the *Shipin* (诗品, 'Grades of Poetry') places him in the middle grade; the crowning as "隐逸诗人之宗" ('the patriarch of reclusive poets') comes after the Song**), **how it judges** (marginal comments = a transcript of the class; inside the study of the *Wenxuan*, **the thousand-year dispute between Li Shan's (李善) commentary and the Five Ministers' commentary — "李善注富，五臣注通" ('Li Shan's commentary is rich; the Five Ministers' commentary carries the sense') (Our translation.): between annotated editions, too, one is choosing one's courses**).
:::

::: example A selection case: 'Climbing the Stork Tower' {#ex-guanque}
白日依山尽， ('The white sun sets against the mountains,') (Our translation.) A poem of twenty characters, and yet **the author column has two work badges**: the line of comprehensive anthologies such as *Wenyuan yinghua* (文苑英华, *Finest Flowers of the Garden of Literature*) lists **Zhu Bin** (朱斌; also given as Zhu Zuori, 朱佐日); the editions in general use from *Tangshi gui* (唐诗归, 'Tang Poems Returned') and *Three Hundred Tang Poems* onward sign **Wang Zhihuan** (王之涣). Using this lesson's three factory slips, give a verdict: does this count as a 'forgery'? Whom should the textbook name? Write a 'verdict on the edition', in two hundred characters.
::: solution
**A model verdict.** **First, check the registration**: Song comprehensive anthologies such as *Wenyuan yinghua*, and the several books such as the *Yuefu shiji* (乐府诗集, *Collection of Yuefu Poems*), each enter it on their own; **the split in the signature has been so since the Song; it is not a sudden fancy of Qing writers**. **Second, check the family**: a quatrain of twenty characters was set to music and sung on — **musicians '各选所长' ('each choosing what he is best at') are a hotbed in which signatures drift** (one and the same poem, like the 'Weicheng Melody' (渭城曲), belongs to the 'performance market', and not to 'the author of a collected works'). **Third, check the classroom**: after *Three Hundred Tang Poems*, 'Wang Zhihuan' wins, and he wins by **the power of the selection**, not by a conclusion of collation — **a signature is a verdict of the history of transmission, not the final judgement of authorship**. Conclusion: **it does not count as a forgery; it counts as 'the history of property held in common'**. The textbook may sign Wang Zhihuan, but **a footnote must hand Zhu Bin back one work badge** — **the courtesy of the study of editions: write the disagreement into the notes, and hand the choice to the reader**. Marking: the verdict passes if the three checks are all there and there is one phrase that 'leaves it in doubt' (*cunyi* 存疑). The line to remember: **a signature is a steel seal stamped by the selection and the woodblock — the steel seal will rust; the negative is still there**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Three steps in buying an annotated edition: preface, editorial principles, collation notes {#int-mai}
**The first step is to read the preface**: see whether the annotator **has any sense of the catalogue** — does he cite the *General Catalogue*, does he mention editions, does he speak of the history of scholarship? **The preface is a book's display of its qualifications**. The second step is to read the editorial principles (*fanli*): **'this book takes such-and-such as its base text, and such-and-such editions as the copies collated against it' — is there any sense of a base text** (*diben*; if there is no base text one can speak of, that equals a report with no method); **the format of the notes** (the brief note of the form "某，某也" ('X is Y') against the full note that quotes the old commentaries — **fullness and brevity have no higher and lower; only whether there is a standard does**). The third step is to sample the **collation notes**: collation notes are the live broadcast of the study of editions — "某字，某本作某，今不从" ('for such a character, such an edition reads such, and we do not follow it now'); **an edited text of an old book with no collation notes is turning a stage play into a film and then deleting the end credits**. **The mnemonic for who sits where**: **first, collation (whether there are collation notes); second, basis (whether what is cited is specific down to the name of the edition); third, combining (whether the commentator 'honours one school alone' or "广采众说，下己意" ('gathers widely the several explanations, and then sets down his own view') (Our translation.) — the code of honour of Qing scholars annotating the Classics)**; as for **'authoritative'** and **'definitive text'** on the belly-band of the cover — **'definitive text' is a swear word in the study of editions: it pretends that collation is already dead** (and the spirit of Gu Guangqi's (顾广圻) "不校校之" ('collating by not collating') is here: **the best collation is** to let the reader see the risk of not collating).
:::

::: history From collating clerk to database {#sec-jiaoxi}
**Han**: the father and son Liu Xiang (刘向) and Xin **collated the palace books** — the work of checking in parallel, "一人备书本，一人持读" ('one person prepares the copies of the book, one person holds it and reads') (Our translation.) (the prefaces to *Guanzi*, *Yanzi chunqiu* (晏子春秋, *Spring and Autumn of Master Yan*) and others preserve the procedure — **the birth certificate of the study of collation**); the *Seven Summaries*' "条其篇目，撮其指意" ('set out the titles of their chapters, and summarise their purport') (Our translation.) (the general preface of the *Han* treatise — **the birth certificate of bibliography**). **Song**: **woodblock printing + the civil examinations = a society of books** (Yue Ke's (岳珂) **Xiangtai Book Studio** was famous for cutting books with a collation careful and exact — **the contest of honour among family woodblock editions**); under the book titles in You Mao's (尤袤) ***Catalogue of the Suichu Hall*** (*Suichu tang shumu* 遂初堂书目) he **notes variant editions and collated editions** (**a pioneering act in the recording of editions** — a catalogue for the first time records 'different appearances of one and the same book'). **Qing**: **the study of editions and catalogues becomes a discipline of its own** — Huang Pilie (黄丕烈) and his '**besotted with the Song**' (*ningsong* 佞宋) (every time he obtained a Song edition he announced it with a sacrifice, and cut a facsimile — **the cult of editions and the science of editions were born at the same time**); Gu Guangqi upheld '**collating by not collating**' — he did not lightly change characters, and he kept the old appearance: **the principle of humility in collation**; Ruan Yuan's (阮元) ***Collation Notes on the Thirteen Classics with Commentaries and Subcommentaries*** (十三经注疏校勘记) — **a census of editions on the project scale of the Collections division**; the completion of the *General Catalogue of the Complete Library of the Four Treasuries* (1782) — **the capstone of catalogues compiled by the state**. **The Republic**: the ***Sibu congkan*** (四部丛刊, *The Four Divisions Series*) (1922), by **photo-reproducing old editions of the Song and the Yuan**, took on hundreds and thousands of bodies — **the antonym of 'spurious edition' is the photocopy**. **1958–1978**: **the punctuated and collated 'Twenty-Four Histories'** — **old books enter the standard man-hours of the state** (the edition in general use from then on had the collective identity of a 'contemporary *shanben*'). **The present**: Jihe Net (籍合网) / the Zhonghua Classics Ancient Books Database (中华经典古籍库), CADAL, and local **digital gazetteers and full-text databases** — **'editorial principles' become search queries, and collation notes become plug-ins for comparing editions**; the new scarcity is no longer the book, but **eyes that can read collation notes**.
:::

::: warning Four traps in using an annotated edition
**Choosing only what is thick** ('eight hundred pages of notes must be authoritative' — **thickness may be a water-wall of old notes copied across**; see whether the editorial principles state one's own contribution); **choosing only what is old** ('a thread-bound book of the Republican period must be good' — **a photo-reproduced Song edition also has the wrong characters of the Song**; age is a price-tag, not a conclusion); **skipping the collation notes** ('I am not doing research anyway' — **what the collation notes happen to tell you is that this poem need not originally have been like this** — the uncertainty of literature is free); **taking the base text for the original work** ('what I am reading is *Dream of the Red Chamber*' — **what you are reading is one concrete manuscript or printed edition, carrying its wrong characters, its revisions and its cuts** — to read a classic = to read **one edition** of it, and this awareness is worth more than any note).
:::

::: exercise A census of household registrations {level=1}
For the *Analects*, *A New Account of Tales of the World* and *The Story of the Western Wing*, look up once each the 'catalogue household registration' (the corresponding entry and classification in the *Han* treatise / the *Sui* treatise / the *Siku General Catalogue*), compare how the classification of one and the same book shifts in the catalogues of different ages, and write a short essay of 150 characters, 'The verdict of household registration'.
::: solution
For reference: the *Analects*: the *Han* treatise enters it under the Six Arts epitome, and the *Sui* treatise enters it under the *Analects* category of the Classics division; *The Story of the Western Wing*: the main volumes have almost no place for it — the *Siku* admits variety plays only in the **song-lyric and *qu* category** of the Collections division, and mostly as titles retained in the catalogue (between the *Siku*'s titles-retained and its bans and destructions — **the chain of discrimination by which variety plays do not enter the orthodox** — the historical fact that the *Siku* does not record fiction and drama). Marking: **a change of classification must be explained up at the level of ideas** (the migration of '*qu*' from the entertainment quarters to the desk to the main hall of literary history = **a coup in the ranks of genres**); when you cite a catalogue you must mark the name of the category — **bibliography in practice: a category name is harder to fake than a book title**.
:::

::: exercise A verdict by the three checks {level=2}
Choose for yourself one case of a Tang poem's signature, or of a variant text (for example the variant in Du Mu's (杜牧) 'Mountain Walk', "白云生处／深处"), and, following 'check the registration — check the family — check the classroom', write a verdict of two hundred characters, noting the tools you used.
::: solution
Marking: the three checks are complete, and **each has one source** (the tools are not limited: the Database of Chinese Basic Ancient Books (中国基本古籍库), the *Siku General Catalogue*, any authoritative collated and annotated edition); **the conclusion uses 'more likely / the evidence is not enough' rather than 'it just is'** — the register of a verdict is a judge, not a fan; the point of the case "白云生处/深处": 生 = a dynamic springing forth, 深 = a secluded distance in space — **a variant is not an error; it is the eyes of two dynasties** (if, for each, you can find one use in a Tang poem that supports it — the bonus marks take off).

::: exercise Short essay: the original in the age of the photocopy {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters: "Photo-reproduction and databases let every manuscript of *Dream of the Red Chamber* '化身千百' ('take on hundreds and thousands of bodies') — **does the concept of the original still exist?**" — you must draw on one historical fact from this lesson (any one of the Zhi copies / the Cheng editions, the *Sibu congkan*, the punctuated and collated Twenty-Four Histories, and CBDB), and discuss **how technologies of reproduction rewrite the production of the 'authoritative text'**; and draw an analogy with one contemporary experience (any one of a scanned article, the version history of a shared cloud document, and an AI collation plug-in).
::: solution
Points for marking: the historical fact is drawn on accurately (the '**taking on hundreds and thousands of bodies**' of photo-reproduction brings at the same time a **new dictatorship** — the most convenient photo-reproduced edition becomes the new original in general use — **the democracy and the monopoly of reproductive technology are delivered together**); a position is given on the concept of 'the original' (one may answer '**the original state of a text is a river; collation is taking a sample of the water**' — but this metaphor must be paid for with argument); the analogy must line the mechanisms up (the version history of a cloud document = the making ordinary of collation notes — the three questions, **who changed it, when it was changed, and whether the original version can be rolled back**, have the same structure); if the conclusion lands on '**what we read has never been the original work, but one dated state of the original work**', that is an A — the debt of this sentence is to be repaid by the next chapter's practical class on editions.
:::

::: summary
Closing the account of the factory inspection: catalogue (six epitomes → four divisions → the category prefaces of the *General Catalogue*; **to classify is to judge**), edition (three production lines, of copying, of block-cutting and of printing; the first law, **the neater, the further, and the rougher, the nearer**; 'definitive text' is a swear word), selection (the *Wenxuan* draws the boundary of literature, the *Guanzhi* sets out the national timetable, **silence is louder than the text selected**), inspection of the goods (collation, basis and combining; the preface = a display of qualifications, the collation notes = a live broadcast). The method in one sentence: **before reading any annotated edition, first read its editorial principles; before trusting any text, first ask after its edition**. The next chapter puts the three tools to use together: choose one annotated edition, and read it through once, complete — the 'end-of-term project' of the class on commentaries and subcommentaries.
:::
