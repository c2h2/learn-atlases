title: "Books and editions"
---

The last chapter returns to **matter**: what the characters are written on, what shape they are bound into, and from which pavilion they have been scattered — these three things decide why every character, on the page you open today, stands where it does. When you have finished this lesson, you will be able to do one thing on your own: **for any old book, match it with the right annotated edition, and say clearly why that one**. This is the certificate of completion for Classical Chinese II (*wenyan*), and the ticket to every course that follows.

## The material history of the book {#sec-wuzhi}

::: definition Materials, binding and the posture of reading {#def-wuzhi}
**Three generations of material**: oracle bone and bronze (**cast** characters, so that the number of characters is limited — **the whole body of writing held up by the hundred thousand oracle-bone pieces from Yinxu is no match for a single *Zuo Tradition***), **bamboo and wood, and silk** (*jianbo* 缣帛) ("**killing the green**", *shaqing* 杀青: the bamboo slips are roasted over a fire until the sap comes out like sweat, which keeps the worms off and makes the slips easy to write on — **汗青 ('sweat-green'), 篇 ('piece'), 卷 ('roll'), 编 ('to string') and 删 ('to cut', altering characters with a knife) are all verb-fossils of my writing characters by hand**; 韦编三绝 = the leather thongs snapped by turning the slips), **paper** (widespread in the Eastern Han; **four hundred years of the manuscript age, a thousand years of the woodblock age — the default format of the "book" decides every habit of layout we recognise**). **Five changes of binding**: bound slips → **the scroll** (hence "**juan**" (卷) as the unit of a part; a scroll has a roller at the head and at the tail, and reading = unrolling towards the left — **the physical reason for writing from right to left**) → **the sutra-fold** (*jingzhe* 经折) (Buddhist scriptures were troublesome to unroll and to roll up again, so they were changed into an accordion — **one pull and they open, the forerunner of the age of looking things up**) → **the butterfly** (woodblock editions of the late Tang and the Five Dynasties, **and when the page is turned the two half-leaves turn the same way, like butterfly wings**) → **wrapped-back and thread-binding** (*baobei* 包背, *xianzhuang* 线装) (**叶 ('leaf'), 页 ('page'), 书口 ('book-mouth'), 版心 ('block centre') and 鱼尾 ('fish tail') are all terms of block-carving — every photofacsimile of an old book in your hands is the rubbed image of a single Song or Yuan printing block**). The key inference: **binding decides the manner of annotation** — in the age of the scroll one wrote "**side notes**" (*cezhu* 侧注) in the seams within the scroll; in the age of the woodblock edition one wrote "**interlinear notes**" (*jiazhu* 夹注) beneath the block centre, and "**eyebrow comments**" (*meipi* 眉批) in the upper margin — **the format of the commentary of Li Shan 李善, "commentary below and subcommentary in front" (注下疏前), and the fierce comments of Jin Shengtan 金圣叹 in the upper margin, are both a sitting posture decided by the furniture**.
:::

::: definition A quick inventory of the four divisions {#def-jiadi}
**Classics**: the smallest closed loop = the ***Thirteen Classics with Commentaries and Subcommentaries*** (*Shisan jing zhushu* 十三经注疏) (the collated woodblock edition of Ruan Yuan 阮元 is the base copy in ordinary use; **of the reading orders through the ten classes of the Classics division, entering by the *Mao Shi* (*Mao Poems* 毛诗) and the *Zuo Tradition* is the least dry**); recommendations, work by work: for the *Analects*, **Yang Bojun 杨伯峻, *Translation and Commentary* (*Yizhu* 译注),** goes ahead, and Liu Baonan 刘宝楠, *Correct Meaning* (*Zhengyi* 正义), brings up the rear; for *Mencius*, **Jiao Xun 焦循, *Correct Meaning***; for the *Book of Songs*, **Cheng Junying 程俊英, *Translation and Commentary*,** set side by side with **the Mao commentary, Zheng's annotations and Kong's subcommentary** (*Mao zhuan* 毛传, *Zheng jian* 郑笺, *Kong shu* 孔疏); for the *Zuo Tradition*, **Yang Bojun, *Translation and Commentary*** (one of the ceilings of the modern *Zuo Tradition*). **Histories**: the **punctuated and collated *Twenty-Four Histories*** (*dianjiao ben* 点校本; Zhonghua Book Company, 1958–1978, a collective project — **the editorial principles (*fanli* 凡例) of one set of books are a history of contemporary scholarship**); for the way in, do not read the histories entire, but read the **three commentaries** (*sanjia zhu* 三家注) **on the *Records of the Grand Historian*** together with Hu Sanxing 胡三省's commentary on the ***Comprehensive Mirror for Aid in Government*** (*Zizhi tongjian* 资治通鉴) (**Hu's commentary: 通鉴之学，经纶措置，规模本末 ("The study of the *Mirror* is planning and disposition, scale and root and branch." (Our translation.)) — the strategic adviser of those who read history**). **Masters**: for the *Laozi*, **Chen Guying 陈鼓应, *Commentary and Translation* (*Zhuyi* 注译),** together with Wang Bi 王弼's commentary; for the *Zhuangzi*, **Chen Guying, *New Commentary and New Translation* (*Jinzhu jinyi* 今注今译),** together with Guo Xiang 郭象's commentary and Cheng Xuanying 成玄英's subcommentary; for the *Xunzi*, Wang Xianqian 王先谦, *Collected Explanations* (*Jijie* 集解), and for the *Han Feizi*, Chen Qiyou 陈奇猷, *Collected Explanations* — **with the books of the Masters, first get the text clear and only then talk about the thought; if the spurious chapters are not sorted out, the case one builds is crooked through and through**. **Collections**: for the *Songs of Chu*, **Hong Xingzu 洪兴祖, *Supplementary Commentary* (*Buzhu* 补注)** (**the format of a "supplementary commentary": first set out Wang Yi 王逸's commentary, then add one's own investigations — a textbook-grade posture of 接着说 ('taking it up and going on')**), the *Wenxuan* (*Selections of Refined Literature* 文选) with **Li Shan's commentary**, for Du Fu 杜甫 **Qiu Zhao'ao 仇兆鳌, *Detailed Commentary* (*Xiangzhu* 详注)**, for Su Shi 苏轼 **Wang Wengao 王文诰, *Compiled Commentary* (*Jizhu* 辑注)**, for Li Bai 李白 **Qu Tuiyuan 瞿蜕园 and Zhu Jincheng 朱金城, *Collation and Commentary* (*Jiaozhu* 校注)**, and for *Dream of the Red Chamber* read together **a photofacsimile of the Gengchen copy (*Gengchen ben* 庚辰本) and the collated and annotated edition from People's Literature** (*Renmin wenxue* 人民文学) — **enter the Collections division by the general collections (*zongji* 总集) (the *Wenxuan*, *Blossoms of the Garden of Letters* (*Wenyuan yinghua* 文苑英华), *Complete Tang Prose* (*Quan Tang wen* 全唐文)); read one or two individual collections (*bieji* 别集) closely, and for the rest it is enough to turn through the list of contents**.
:::

::: example Choosing in practice: four ways in for one reader {#ex-ruku}
The scene: a reader in the sciences or engineering, having finished this lesson, wants within one year to "truly finish one old book". Four candidates: ① the *Records of the Grand Historian* as a complete history; ② *A New Account of Tales of the World* (*Shishuo xinyu* 世说新语); ③ the seven Inner Chapters of the *Zhuangzi*; ④ *Dream of the Red Chamber*. For each, assign one **edition + annotated edition + schedule + standard of acceptance**, and say why it is not the other three.
::: solution
**The ruling**: ① for the *Records of the Grand Historian*, **advise against reading the whole** (one hundred and thirty *juan*; without a teacher the rate of giving up halfway is extremely high) — assign instead **a selected reader plus excerpts from the three commentaries**, and for acceptance: "**read fifteen biographical chapters closely, and be able to draw one diagram of how the people are related**"; ② *A New Account of Tales of the World* is **the best way in** (the items are short, the language is plain, the aftertaste is long; **3 *juan*, 36 categories, more than 1,130 items** — **three items a day, and it comes to a year and more**) — for the edition, assign **Yu Jiaxi 余嘉锡, *Annotations and Subcommentary* (*Jianshu* 笺疏)** (**a model of the *jianshu* form: using history to verify the fiction — the coronation by which notebook literature (*biji* 笔记) is raised to the rank of a historical source**), and for acceptance: "be able to point out which items were copied off into the *Book of Jin* (*Jin shu* 晋书) and had their tone of voice changed"; ③ the seven Inner Chapters of the *Zhuangzi* are **the first choice for a reader of thought** (**the quantity of text is the smallest and the density the greatest; Cheng Xuanying's subcommentary is too hard, and it is enough to set Guo Xiang's commentary beside a modern commentary**), and for acceptance: "**copy out the whole of 'Free and Easy Wandering' (*Xiaoyao you* 逍遥游) and of 'On the Equality of Things' (*Qiwu lun* 齐物论) once each, and be able to read the plain text (*baiwen* 白文)**"; ④ *Dream of the Red Chamber* has **the most complicated editions, and is not fit for the first battle** — but once it has been read through, **it is as good as sitting in on a whole class in the study of editions** (the differences between manuscript copies and printed editions are visible to the naked eye). **The common rule: choose a book one "can finish" — a famous work one cannot finish is the greatest sunk cost in learning Classical Chinese.**
:::
:::

::: intuition Editorial principles are a book's constitution {#int-fanli}
The editorial principles of the punctuated and collated "**Twenty-Four Histories**" from the Zhonghua Book Company lay it down: choose the best as the base text, collate every copy throughout, and **if a character is changed, a collation note must be entered** — from then on the three characters of 点校本 ("punctuated and collated edition") had the force of law; the other way about, **if a prepared edition (*zhengli ben* 整理本) writes no editorial principles, it is the same as governing a state without establishing a constitution and yet calling the government a great order**. This is also the methodological core of the lesson: **the first layer of understanding, in reading old books, is to understand "why this copy looks like this"** — one and the same line, 床前明月光 ("The moonlight is bright before the bed." (Our translation.)), and the difference between the Song printed edition of the *Collected Works of Li Taibai* (*Li Taibai wenji* 李太白文集) and *Three Hundred Tang Poems* is not a thousand characters, but **a thousand years of editorial stance** ([[classical-chinese-2/commentaries]] — the three checks formally take up their posts here). One layer deeper: **the spirit of editorial principles can be carried across to every secondary material** — looking at an article, first find its "editorial principles" (the statement of method and materials): what the base text is (the version of the database), what was collated throughout (how far the survey of the literature covers), and whether a changed character is entered in a collation note (whether a quotation is copied as it stands) — **the study of editions is the classical form of critical thinking**.
:::

::: history Keeping and scattering {#sec-cang}
**Han**: a wide gathering-in of the books left from the Qin, and **the establishing of a policy for collecting books** (the Shiqu and Tianlu pavilions — **the Rome of the state's book-collecting**), while Liu Xiang 刘向 and his son made the collation of books into a procedure; **Wei and Jin**: Xun Xu 荀勖 put the *Zhongjing* (中经) in order, **and so made the forerunner of classification by the four divisions** — in an age of war, the official collections three times gathered and three times scattered; **Tang**: the **four repositories of imperial books** (the Academy of Assembled Worthies, *Jixian yuan* 集贤院, copying books, and the wrapper-colours of the four divisions, "**jia, yi, bing, ding**" (甲乙丙丁) — **the four divisions acquired a colour code**), and the sealing away of the sutra-cave at Dunhuang (around the year 1000) — **a single cave held all the negatives of the manuscript age (discovered in 1900; in 1910 what survived the plunder entered the capital — one and the same curve of keeping and scattering)**; **Song**: **three systems of official book-carving: the Directorate of Education (*Guozijian* 国子监), the prefectures and commanderies, and private carving and bookshop carving** (**woodblock editions of the Liangzhe Circuit were called "first among Song editions" — the earliest "certification of origin"**), and the books of the four divisions in the Chongwen Institute (*Chongwen yuan* 崇文院) went from thirty thousand to fifty thousand *juan* — **printing turned the collecting of books from a marvel into the ordinary state**; **Ming**: the ***Yongle Encyclopaedia*** (*Yongle dadian* 永乐大典) (completed in 1408; **用韵以统字，随字以载事 ("Use the rhyme to order the characters, and under the character record the matter." (Our translation.)) — a giant classified encyclopaedia (*leishu* 类书) indexed by rhyme; nine-tenths of the original book was scattered in the wars, and a little more than four hundred volumes survive today** — **the lot of a classified encyclopaedia: the compilers wanted it to be a storehouse, and history made it a tombstone: lost books come back to life by means of it (Qing scholars extracted from the *Encyclopaedia* a great quantity of Song and Yuan texts)**); **Qing**: the ***Complete Books of the Four Storehouses*** (*Siku quanshu* 四库全书) (completed in 1782; the Wenyuan Pavilion and the others, **seven pavilions** — **a book copied seven times over still vanished, pavilion by pavilion, in fire and in war; "book calamities" (*shu'e* 书厄) are the ordinary weather of the history of book-collecting**); **the Republic**: the **four great book-collecting houses of the late Qing** (**the Lu family's Biku (陆氏皀库), the Ding family's Eight-Thousand-*Juan* Tower (丁氏八千卷楼), the Yang family's Haiyuan Pavilion (杨氏海源阁), the Miao family's Yifeng Hall (缪氏艺风堂)**) — the gathering and scattering of the four families' fine Song and Yuan editions (*shanben* 善本) is the history of how old books circulated in modern times — **the Biku (皀库) went to the Iwasaki family of Japan, and the Eight-Thousand-*Juan* Tower went to the Jiangnan Library: private houses scatter, and public collections take them in**; **the *Collectanea of the Four Divisions* (*Sibu congkan* 四部丛刊), photofacsimiled into hundreds and thousands of embodiments, together with libraries everywhere accepting donations — the age of the private house ended, and the age of the public collection began**; **the present age**: ***Chinese Rare Books Reproduced*** (*Zhonghua zaizao shanben* 中华再造善本) (a photofacsimile project from 2002 on — **the philosophy of "reproducing": turn the unique copy (*guben* 孤本) into what a thousand households have, and turn risk into zero**), the return of Chinese books from overseas, and images in high definition (**in a database there are no national borders, but there is copyright**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in choosing books
**Worship of the reading list** ("a hundred titles one must read", and when you have memorised the lot you are still at zero — **a reading list is a map, not the walk; the "thirty titles" of this lesson are fit only to serve as a shoe cupboard**); **buying only annotated editions that sell** (sales have nothing to do with the quality of the collation — **inspect the goods by 一校二据三兼 ('first the collation, second the basis, third the holding of both')**); **one good copy conquers all** (Yang Bojun's *Analects* and his *Zuo Tradition* are both excellent work, but **an annotated edition's capacity has a boundary**: for the *Book of Songs* one must go to Cheng Junying and to the Mao commentary and Zheng's annotations, and for *Mencius* one must go up to Jiao Xun — **using the right tool is more reliable than putting one's faith in the right master**); **taking pride in "having finished"** (to read the opening thirty *juan* of the *Comprehensive Mirror for Aid in Government* closely is far better than to flip through all two hundred and ninety-four *juan* — **the standard of acceptance must be "able to draw the diagram / able to recite the plain text / able to give an account of the people", not the check-in of "already finished"**).
:::

::: exercise My smallest book-collecting house {level=1}
Choose one title from each of the Classics, the Histories, the Masters and the Collections (**you may not copy the sample list in this lesson**; you may consult *Answers to Questions on the Book List* (*Shumu dawen* 书目答问) or an authoritative guide to reading). For each, write two sentences of reasons for the recommendation, plus one specific annotated edition **which you have looked up** (the book's title and designation, and the author).
::: solution
Marking: **one work in each of the four divisions, Classics, Histories, Masters and Collections, with no class repeated** (a common one-sidedness: four works in Collections — Histories left empty — **the Histories division is the arsenal of the Classical Chinese lexicon, and it must be there**); the recommendation of each work must **contain one sentence of "why this one is for you"** (for example, "the *Discourses on Salt and Iron* (*Yantielun* 盐铁论): in the form of the minutes of a meeting, the language of dispute comes closest to speech"); **the information on the edition must be specific as far as the person who prepared it and the year of publication** — the spirit of editorial principles, beginning from one's own reading list.
:::
:::

::: exercise Assessing annotated editions {level=2}
For one and the same book, choose **two annotated editions** (for example Yang Bojun on the *Analects* against some noted scholar's translation and commentary; the collated and annotated edition of *Dream of the Red Chamber* from People's Literature against a photofacsimile of the Gengchen copy). Following the three steps "preface — editorial principles — collation notes", write an assessment of 300 characters, and give **recommendations split by situation** (the examination / research reading / collecting).
::: solution
Marking: each of the three steps has **evidence at the level of a page number** ("page 2 of the editorial principles says that such-and-such a copy is taken as the base text, yet in the collation notes no citation of that authority is to be seen"); the recommendations by situation do not slurry the differences together (**the collecting situation is allowed to recommend "expensive but fine"**); the language of the assessment keeps to the book — **the ethics of the study of editions: compare only the evidence, and do not pass judgement on taste**.
:::
:::

::: exercise The completion project: a complete file for one book {level=3}
Choose one old book you have already read (poetry, the song lyric and *qu* are allowed too), and make **a set of five**: ① a statement of the editions (materials — copying and carving — the genealogy of prepared editions, one page); ② an assessment of annotated editions (two or more); ③ the plain text plus your own commentary (choose a passage of no more than 10 sentences, and write a "commentary" in imitation of the format of Chapter 6); ④ one reading note (in a single paragraph deal with one **problem of a variant reading or of the name signed to the piece**, in imitation of the "three checks" of Chapter 8); ⑤ a reflection of four hundred characters: "along this road, which of the things I 'thought were the original work' have I changed".
::: solution
Points for the mark: the set of five **cite one another internally** (the commentary in ③ must be in dialogue with the annotated editions chosen in ②; ④ must use the information on editions from ① — **what the course asks for is not five pieces of homework, but one system**); ⑤ must record at least one **concrete disillusion** ("I thought that 举头望明月 ('I raise my head and gaze at the bright moon.' (Our translation.)) was the original text; in fact the Song edition reads 抬头望明月 ('I lift my head and gaze at the bright moon.' (Our translation.)) — **even the posture of gazing at the moon has been changed by the edition**" — a real discovery of this kind is worth more than a hundred sentences of impression); a whole file that passes = **completion of Classical Chinese II: you are no longer a reader of Classical Chinese, but one of its editors**.
:::

::: summary
The lesson on matter closes its accounts: materials ("killing the green" and "sweat-green", verb-fossils), binding (the scroll → thread-binding, **the furniture decides the sitting posture**), the stock of the four divisions (for the Classics, the *Commentaries and Subcommentaries*; for the Histories, the punctuated and collated editions; for the Masters, get the text clear; for the Collections, enter by the general collections), keeping and scattering (**"book calamities" are the ordinary weather of the history of book-collecting**; reproduced rare books = turn the unique copy into what a thousand households have), method (**editorial principles = a constitution; the study of editions = the classical form of critical thinking**). Classical Chinese II comes together here: **original meaning — synonyms — pronouns, numerals and classifiers — function words — parallel prose and the rhapsody — commentaries and subcommentaries — common knowledge — reading commentaries — books**, nine chapters on one line: **an old book is a commodity laid down in layers; to read it with understanding = to understand its production line**. Next stop: the literature of the two Han dynasties, the Wei and Jin, and the Northern and Southern Dynasties — you take the full set of tools with you, and walk into the scene of literature's self-awareness.
:::
