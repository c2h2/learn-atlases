---
title: "Parallel prose and rhapsodies"
---

Pre-Qin prose goes on foot; from the Han onward, parallel prose and the rhapsody are **walking on stilts** — and they must dance as they walk. This chapter teaches you two things: **to see clearly the symmetry compulsion of parallel prose (*pianwen*)** (why Chinese people cultivated parallelism (the parallel couplet) into an aesthetic instinct), and **to see clearly the mania for spreading display in the rhapsody (*fu*; also rhyme-prose, poetic exposition)** (why description may swell and still be entirely in the right). They are not the opposite of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*); they are its **coming-of-age rite**: once form had become conscious of itself, the Chinese language for the first time knew that **it could be merely good to look at**.

## Parallel prose: the grammar of symmetry {#def-pian}

::: definition Three parts of the parallel sentence: parallelism, sound, allusion {#def-sanjian}

::: definition Four-six: a genre's identity number {#def-siliu}
The name 'four-six' (*siliu*) begins in the late Tang — **Li Shangyin 李商隐, *Fannan siliu jia yi ji* 《樊南四六甲乙集》 ('Four-Six Prose from Fannan, Collections A and B'): the genre has its document number authenticated backwards from the title of a collected works**; the Song carried it on as **the formal dress of practical writing**: memorials (表), letters of presentation (启), edicts and patents (制诰), and thanks for grace (谢恩) — whatever said a respectable thing to the emperor was four-six (**memorials and letters fill the volumes in the collected works of Su Shi 苏轼 and Wang Anshi 王安石 — the 'official-document skin' of Tang and Song writers**). The sentence pattern takes **the alternation of four-character and six-character lines as its bone** (four beats: four-four, six-six, four-six, six-four); fitted to it are **lead-in words** (“且夫、若夫、至若、已而” — the engine of a paragraph) and **hinge phrases** (“是以、然则、是故” — the connectors are also a matter of ceremonial courtesy). The household register of genres in the Six Dynasties: **“今之文章，盖古辞之流；有韵者文也，无韵者笔也”** ('The writings of the present are, broadly, a current of the phrasing of antiquity; what has rhyme is *wen*, and what has no rhyme is *bi*.') (Our translation.) (the gist of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (*Wenxin diaolong*), 'Zongshu' — **whether or not one rhymes, and whether or not one gives voice to feeling, was at that time the entrance examination for the subject '*wen*'**: the Classics, the Histories, and memorials and records were '*bi*'; only “吟咏风谣，流连哀思” ('chanting the airs and ballads, lingering in sorrowful thoughts') (Our translation.) (Xiao Tong 萧统, Preface to the *Wenxuan* 文选, 'Selections of Refined Literature') counted as '*wen*' — **parallel prose is the gold-medal event in the subject '*wen*', and the debate over *wen* and *bi* is its certificate of origin**).
:::

## The rhapsody: a history of description swelling {#def-fu}

::: definition The rhapsody's three changes of form {#def-sanbian}
**Where the name comes from**: **“赋者，铺也，铺采摘文，体物写志”** ('As for the rhapsody: it is spreading. It spreads colour and plucks patterned words; it embodies things and writes of intent.') (Our translation.) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Quan fu' — **the definition brings its own licence with it: spreading display is the proper trade**); whereas the *fu* of the 'six poems' (the 'Mao Preface', 《毛诗序》) means **stating the matter outright** — **first a method of the *Songs*, afterwards a genre: a technique is confirmed in post as a literary form**. Xunzi 荀子, in the 'Fu pian' 《赋篇》, writes of rites, clouds, the silkworm and the needle in riddling language — **the earliest 'rhapsody' is riddle literature**: the features are laid out, and at the end the answer is uncovered, **descriptive writing and the riddle as twins**. **The great Han rhapsody**: **it opens with a dialogue of guest and host** (Zixu 子虚, 'Unreal'; Master Wuyou 乌有先生, 'Master Non-Existent'; Lord Wangshigong 亡是公, 'Lord No-Such-Person' — **the names are themselves the declaration: fictional persons are set to hold a discussion meeting**), **laying-out in space** (mountains to the left and waters to the right; east, west, south and north, a price quoted layer by layer), **the closing section returns to remonstrance** (a sentence patched on at the end, “于是省边界、退奢乐” ('thereupon he reduced the frontiers and withdrew from extravagant pleasures') (Our translation.) — and the result is **a hundred persuasions to one remonstrance**: Yang Xiong 扬雄 confesses, “**童子雕虫篆刻，壮夫不为**” ('A child's carving of insects and engraving of seal script; a grown man does not do it.') (Our translation.) (*Fayan* 《法言》, 'Model Sayings', 'Wuzi') — **the spreading display gave the emperor a pleasant read, and the remonstrance had no one to sign for it**). **The short lyric rhapsody**: after the Eastern Han, the domain of the great rhapsody shrank to a corner of field and garden — Zhang Heng 张衡, 'Rhapsody on Returning to the Fields' 《归田赋》, “**于是仲春令月，时和气清**” ('And so, in the fair month of mid-spring, the season is mild and the air is clear.') (Our translation.) — **the 'inward turn' begins**. **The parallel rhapsody**: the Six Dynasties dressed the rhapsody in four-six formal clothes (《洛神》, 《别》, 《恨》 — 'The Goddess of the Luo', 'Parting', 'Resentment'). **The regulated rhapsody**: the Tang recruited its officials by the rhapsody, **limiting the prescribed rhymes, tightening the parallelism, fixing the lines of the pattern — the examination edition of the rhapsody, the formula at its peak**. **The prose rhapsody**: Song writers **used prose to make the rhapsody** — Du Mu 杜牧, 'Rhapsody on the Epang Palace' 《阿房宫赋》, sounded the first note; Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修, 'Rhapsody on the Sounds of Autumn' 《秋声赋》, and Su Shi, 'Rhapsody on the Red Cliff' 《赤壁赋》, became the models: “**耳得之而为声，目遇之而成色**” ('The ear gets it, and it becomes sound; the eye meets it, and it becomes colour.') (Our translation.); antithesis (对偶; parallel phrasing, covering both likeness and contrast) steps back into a supporting role, and function words breathe again; the last section, “洗盏更酌……**不知东方之既白**” ('We washed the cups and poured once more…… **not knowing that the east had already grown white**.') (Our translation.) — **the rhyme words leave the stage, and the scene takes over the shift: the rhapsody moves back to the border of prose**.
:::

::: example The allusion lift in the 'Preface to the Pavilion of the Prince of Teng' {#ex-tengwang}
“**酌贪泉而觉爽，处涸辙以犹欢**” ('I drink at the Spring of Greed and feel refreshed; I dwell in a dry rut and am glad even so.') (Our translation.) “**北海虽赊，扶摇可接**” ('Though the Northern Sea is far, the whirlwind is within reach.') (Our translation.) “**东隅已逝，桑榆非晚**” ('The eastern corner is already gone; among the mulberries and elms it is not late.') (Our translation.) “**钟期既遇，奏流水以何惭**” ('A Zhong Qi having been met, why be ashamed to play "Flowing Water"?') (Our translation.) — four groups of sentences, each to be taken apart: the original piece of the allusion → the original meaning → **the direction in which Wang Bo 王勃, after refashioning it, carried the sense**; and explain why a factual parallel that uses 'water' twice in a row (贪泉, the Spring of Greed; 涸辙, the dry rut) does not commit the taboo of '*fanfu*' (犯复, 'falling into repetition').
::: solution
**The Spring of Greed** (贪泉): in Guangzhou there is a spring in the rock; tradition says that whoever drinks of it conceives a desire for gold — **an ancient experiment in 'environmental determinism', and the control group is Wu Yinzhi 吴隐之** (his biography in the *Book of Jin* 《晋书》: Yinzhi drank of the Spring of Greed and yet strove to keep his conduct pure, and the bounds of Yue grew solemn) — Wang Bo turns his hand the other way and uses it as a declaration: drink of the Spring of Greed and feel refreshed, **and environment cannot veto one's disposition**; **the dry rut** (涸辙): the perch in a dry rut in *Zhuangzi*, 'Waiwu', **待升斗之水** ('waiting for a *sheng* or a *dou* of water' — small measures of capacity; their size changed over time) — the original text is **a call for rescue**, and Wang Bo remakes it as **being at ease where one is** (“以犹欢”, 'and glad even so' — **an optimism at which Zhuangzi himself would give a start**); the two matters belong to the same family of water, **the same in form and different in direction**: one is drinking and one is dwelling, one a refreshment and one a gladness — **antithesis does not forbid subject-matter that lies close; it forbids only a meaning that is the same**; **the Northern Sea and the whirlwind**: 'Xiaoyao you', “鹏之徙于南冥，**抟扶摇而上者九万里**” ('When the *peng* travels to the Southern Ocean, **it wheels on the whirlwind and rises ninety thousand *li***' — a *li* was a traditional measure of distance, and its length changed.) (Our translation.) — '赊' (far) set against '可接' ('can be reached'): **distance in space against height of cultivation, a compression of “路远怕什么，翅膀够大”** ('The road is long — what of it, if the wings are big enough?') (Our translation.); **the eastern corner and the mulberry and elm**: the biography of Feng Yi 冯异 in the *Book of the Later Han* 《后汉书》, “**失之东隅，收之桑榆**” ('**Lose it at the eastern corner; gather it in among the mulberries and elms.**') (Our translation.) — the original text is the settling of a battle's account, and Wang Bo turns it into **an accounting head for age** (losing the road in youth — the days ahead are long: **a self-consolation that one use of the dictionary is enough to complete**); **Zhong Qi and flowing water**: in *Liezi* 《列子》, 'Tang wen', Boya 伯牙 played the *qin* with his mind on flowing water, and Zhong Ziqi 钟子期 recognised it — **the word '惭' is the hinge of the whole piece: every feast in the text before this sentence is background sound, and from this sentence on he is looking for 'someone who listens'** — when the lift of allusion reaches this floor, **it changes from 'showing off one's deposits' to 'looking for a payee'**. The sentence to mark by: **an allusion in parallel prose is a compressed biscuit, and the commentary is hot water — only after it has been steeped open do you know the dignity, and the wretchedness, of an author hurrying the road on an empty stomach**.
:::

::: intuition Symmetry is an aesthetic, and it is also power {#int-duichen}
Why did the civilisation of the Chinese language turn parallelism into an **institution** (matching couplets, the regulated poem (*lüshi*), four-six, pillar couplets, and the titles of chapter-novels)? Three layers: **the physiological layer** — the disyllable plus the square character, naturally like **two doors that can be shut** (the word is a building block; the sentence is mortise and tenon); **the cognitive layer** — setting things in pairs is **emphasis at the lowest cost**: said once, it is a statement; said twice, and in the same shape, it grows to look like the truth (maxims are braced entirely by parallelism: “满招损，谦受益” ('Fullness invites loss; humility receives benefit.') (Our translation.)); **the political layer** — the **aesthetic of symmetry** in court ritual: hall, gate, gate-tower and couplet drawn up in a single line, **and parallelism in language is the copywriter's version of parallelism in architecture**; the examinations tested poetry and the rhapsody, and diplomacy submitted four-six — **symmetry is a test of obedience: can you, wearing shackles, dance a dance the authorities recognise**. Read as far as the political layer, and then read parallel prose again, and the sympathy changes: **those seemingly slick runs of parallel phrasing are a kneeling rite and a drum for rising, made into features of a genre — half the author's beauty is light thrown back off the shackles**.
:::

::: history The ranks of genres, reshuffled again and again {#sec-xiti}
**Warring States**: the rhapsody = a hybrid of riddle and laying-out (Xunzi) + **the art of persuasion of the *zongheng* strategists** (the dialogue of guest and host was a standing routine of pre-Qin argument — **the rhapsody is the debate contest made literary**); **Han**: the great rhapsody = **an imperial propaganda project** — 'a hundred persuasions and one remonstrance'; Yang Xiong drew a line between two kinds of 'beauty' (丽): “**诗人之赋丽以则，辞人之赋丽以淫**” ('The rhapsody of the poets is beautiful and keeps to the rule; the rhapsody of the men of phrasing is beautiful and runs to excess.') (Our translation.) (*Fayan*, 'Wuzi' — **splendour has to have a red line of proper measure; this is the written confession the rhapsodists themselves handed in**); **Wei and Jin**: **the independence of *wen*** — Cao Pi 曹丕, 'Lun wen' in the *Dianlun* 《典论·论文》 ('A Discourse on Literature', in 'Normative Discourses'), “**诗赋欲丽**” ('Poetry and the rhapsody want beauty.') (Our translation.) ('beauty' becomes an official index of taste for the first time), and Lu Ji 陆机, 'Rhapsody on Literature' 《文赋》, “**赋体物而浏亮**” ('The rhapsody embodies things, and is clear and bright.') (Our translation.) (**'embodying things' (体物) is entered on the register, and becomes a defining word of the rhapsody**); **Southern Dynasties**: parallel ornateness is capped, and 'Lizhi' ('Parallel phrasing') and 'Shilei' ('Allusion') in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* **supply parallel prose with the institutions of a discipline**; **Tang**: **the *guwen* movement (ancient-style prose) against parallel prose** (“**惟陈言之务去**” ('Let it be one's business only to be rid of stale phrasing.') (Our translation.) — **what was opposed was the set routine, not form**) + **the regulated rhapsody enters the examinations** (the formula at its peak); **Song**: **four-six stands on its own as a subject** (the shared skin of the examinations in edicts and patents; at the same time Ouyang Xiu and Su Shi 'used prose to make the rhapsody' — **one person, two skins, each clocking on to its own shift**); **Qing**: **a revival of parallel prose** — Ruan Yuan 阮元 held up '*wenyan*' (taking the *Wenxuan*'s “**事出于沉思，义归乎翰藻**” ('The matter comes forth from deep thought; the meaning comes home to the ornament of the brush.') (Our translation.) as his yardstick) in order to contend for orthodox standing on behalf of parallel prose, and Li Zhaoluo 李兆洛, with the *Pian ti wen chao* 《骈体文钞》 ('An Anthology of Parallel Prose'), established a selection and fixed the house rules — **parallel prose was invited back into the main hall, and ancient-style prose gave up the single-plank bridge**; **modern times**: Zhang Taiyan 章太炎 marked the bounds of literary study by “文字著于竹帛” ('writing set down on bamboo and silk') (Our translation.), and **parallel and free prose were no longer ranked above and below one another — the modern armistice in a thousand-year civil war of genres**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of parallel prose and the rhapsody
**See a parallel sentence and call it padding** ('parallelism that is nothing but empty talk' — **first check whether there is a gap in information inside the parallel phrasing**: the line between antithetic parallelism and 'direct parallelism is the inferior' lies in **whether or not there is a difference of potential** between the two lines); **see a rhapsody and assume that it rhymes** (the prose rhapsody need not rhyme — **'Red Cliff' is a prose poem under the name 'rhapsody'**, and the title is the declaration of genre); **the cult of allusion** (writers today paste on four four-character idioms (*chengyu*) and pass them off as parallel fours and ornate sixes — **an allusion should be 'salt in the water' (水中着盐), not 'salt sprinkled in the soup' (汤里撒盐): whatever you can pour out as grains is not the flavour; it is stock in hand**); **the purism that honours free prose and despises parallel prose** (“八股之害的源头是骈文” ('The source of the harm done by the eight-legged essay is parallel prose.') (Our translation.) — **a genre does not take the fall for an institution**; the eight-legged essay has its own account to settle; a lesson carried over from the lesson on function words: **before you criticise a genre, first check which post it is employed at**).
:::

::: exercise Taking factual parallels apart {level=1}
In the selection from the 'Preface to the Pavilion of the Prince of Teng' 《滕王阁序》, find three further groups of factual parallels (“**怀帝阍而不见，奉宣室以何年**” ('I yearn for the emperor's gate and do not see it; in what year shall I attend at the Xuan Room?') (Our translation.) “**冯唐易老，李广难封**” ('Feng Tang grew old all too easily; Li Guang could hardly be enfeoffed.') (Our translation.) “**杨意不逢，抚凌云而自惜**” ('I did not meet a Yang Yi; I touch "Lingyun" and pity myself.') (Our translation.) — choose any two groups): the candidates are '怀帝阍—奉宣室', '冯唐—李广', '杨意—钟期'); set them out as a list: source of the allusion → original situation → direction of transfer → dose of emotion.
::: solution
Marking: the **original situation** is specific (the Xuan Room = Emperor Wen of the Han and Master Jia's 贾生 reply on 'ghosts and spirits' — **what was asked was ghosts and spirits, not the common people; Wang Bo did not draw on Li Shangyin's account in advance** — extra marks for anyone who can string this into a story of the two Tangs); the **direction of transfer** is put into verbs ('难封', 'hard to enfeoff', moves Li Guang 李广 into **the head office of unrecognised talent**); the dose of emotion is honest (Feng Tang 冯唐 and Li Guang are **a placebo at double strength**: 'I have company in my failure' — **the ranked parallels of parallel prose are often the grammar of psychological healing**).
:::

::: exercise Taking down the stilts {level=2}
Rewrite “**老当益壮，宁移白首之心；穷且益坚，不坠青云之志**” ('Old, one should be all the more stalwart — would one shift the heart of white hair? Poor, and all the firmer — one does not let fall the ambition of the blue clouds.') (Our translation.) as prose of no more than 60 characters (drop the antithesis, drop the allusions; metaphor may be kept), and then, in the other direction, 'parallelise' a passage of vernacular motivational writing into two four-six couplets.
::: solution
Marking: after the rewriting as prose, **a self-assessed rate of semantic fidelity** (whether the force of '宁移' and '不坠', the rhetorical question and the verb, has been kept — **the information in a parallel sentence hides in the function words**; delete the function words and the air leaks); the two parallelised couplets: **the level-and-oblique endings comply (oblique on the upper line, level on the lower) + no ready-made allusion is used** (an experiment of one's own in 'salt in the water'); the standard for marking one another's work: **before and after the rewriting, do they look like two outfits of the same poem**.
:::

Write from six hundred to one thousand characters: “骈文与律赋都是**戴着镣铐跳舞**，但镣铐有时也是舞的一部分” ('Parallel prose and the regulated rhapsody are both **dancing in shackles**, but the shackles are sometimes part of the dance as well.') (Our translation.) — take one institution from this lesson (any one of the regulated rhapsody in the examinations, four-six memorials and letters, and diplomatic parallel prose), and discuss **how the constraint of form produces content in reverse** (a forced reserve, a forced concision, a forced pun); and respond: **in the old tug-of-war between “言之无文，行而不远” ('If words have no pattern, they do not go far.') (Our translation.) and “辞达而已” ('Let the words convey, and that is enough.') (Our translation.), whose turn is it today?**
::: solution
Points for marking: the example of a mechanism is concrete (memorials and letters of thanks: **when one must tell a respectable lie, allusion = a moat against being scolded** — an argument of the kind “**形式是弱势者的修辞铠甲**” ('**Form is the rhetorical armour of the weaker party.**') (Our translation.) meets the mark); 'forced production' has its path written out (a limit on the rhyme forces ingenuity into the open — **the house rules of the regulated rhapsody are the ancestor of later 'competitions on a set theme'**); the tug-of-war is given a position and does not sit on the fence ('words that convey' is the ideal, 'words without pattern' is the reality — **an acceptable conclusion: every age's 'conveying' owes a debt to form; the only difference is the rate of interest**); if one quotes “**繁采寡情，味之必厌**” ('**Ornament in abundance and feeling in little: taste it, and one is sure to weary.**') (Our translation.) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Qingcai', 'Feeling and ornament') — **the theory of parallel prose comes with its own anti-formalist alarm: cite that against the abuses parallel prose ran into, and it is full marks**.

::: summary
Settling the account at the gallery of the twin jades: parallelism (the four-line score + **the difference of potential between direct and antithetic parallelism**), sound (four-six is the outer garment; level and oblique tones at the end of the line are the skeleton — it is here that the umbilical cord of the regulated poem is cut), allusion (the transfer of authority + salt in the water), the rhapsody's three changes of form (riddle → imperial project → inner monologue; the regulated rhapsody puts the shackles on, the prose rhapsody takes them off); one sentence for the political layer: **symmetry is court ritual turned into language**. One sentence of method: **reading parallel prose, look for the difference of potential; reading the rhapsody, look for where the 'spread' (铺) is headed — once the spreading is done, to whom it was given**. The next chapter enters the workshop of commentaries and subcommentaries: how a Classic is wrapped, layer on layer, in commentary (注), subcommentary (疏) and collected explanations (集解, *jijie*) — the infrastructure of 'deep reading' for the traditional Chinese reader.
:::
