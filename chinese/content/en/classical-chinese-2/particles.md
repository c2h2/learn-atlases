---
title: "Function words in depth"
---

The previous course handed out an introductory card of function words (其之而以于为所者); this course **takes them apart and puts them back together**. Every high-frequency function word is in fact a **shell company holding several posts at once** — to check its household registration you have to trace all the way back to its **verbal ancestors**. The flesh and blood of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*) are the content words; **the breathing is entirely in the function words**. When Lu Xun 鲁迅 wrote Kong Yiji 孔乙己, with nothing but the four pronouns “之乎者也” he nailed a sour pedant to the page — **a function word is the fingerprint of a style, and also the growth ring of period-dating**.

## Two assembly lines {#def-liushuixian}

::: definition The twin machines of nominalisation: 者 and 所 {#def-zhesuo}
**者** = predicative material enters the factory and a noun leaves it: “**学者**” ('a person who studies'), “**勇者**” ('the brave'), “**耕者**” ('those who till'), “**观者如堵**” ('onlookers packed like a wall'); **the registration stamp of a postposed attributive** (“**马之千里者**”, 'a horse that does a thousand *li*', “**荆州之民附操者**”, 'those of the people of Jingzhou who went over to Cao', “**求人可使报秦者**”, 'seeking a man who could be sent to answer Qin' — **head + 之 + postposed attributive + 者**, one grammatical password that disposes of inversion in full); **marking the mood of a topical pause** (“**陈胜者，阳城人也**” — 'Chen Sheng 陈胜 was a man of Yangcheng 阳城.' (Our translation.) — the first beat of the nominal-sentence pattern). **所** = **a machine that extracts the object of a transitive verb**: “**所见闻**” = what is seen and what is heard (put “所” before the verb, and the verb becomes a “patient noun”); **the passive “为…所”** (“**若属皆且为所虏**” — 'The lot of you will all be taken captive by him.' (Our translation.) — **the passive marker “所” is still in use today: “为人所骗”**, 'deceived by someone'); **“所以”** does double duty: **marking instrument or means** (“**师者，所以传道受业解惑也**” — 'A teacher is the means by which the Way is transmitted, instruction is received and doubts are resolved.' (Our translation.) — the thing used to…), **marking cause** (“**此所以败也**” — 'This is why they were defeated.' (Our translation.)) — **ancient and modern uses fork from a common source**. The shared lineage of the two machines: the ancestor of **者** is modal or demonstrative, the ancestor of **所** is the noun “**处所**” ('place') (“**入之甚寒，问其所从来**” — 'On entering, it was bitterly cold, and he asked the place they had come from.' (Our translation.)) — **place → an abstracted relation**. Almost every function word has once had an “origin in real estate”.
:::

::: definition The ladder of grammaticalisation: 以, 其 and 焉 {#def-yiti}
**以**: the verb “to use, to take” (**the “以” of “以其子妻之” still carries a full lexical sense — “把”**, 'taking'. 'Taking his daughter, he gave her to him as wife.' (Our translation.)) → **preposition** (instrument or means: “**以子之矛**”, 'with your spear'; cause: “**不以物喜**”, 'not rejoicing on account of things'; time or place: “**以是**”, “**以故**” ('for this reason' — therefore — **the corridor along which a preposition becomes a conjunction**) → **conjunction** (“**作《师说》以贻之**” — 'He composed 'On Teachers' in order to present it to him.' (Our translation.) — marking purpose, on the way to “来”, 'so as to')). **其**: the demonstrative pronoun “that” (the Old Chinese home of “**其为人也**”, 'that way of his as a person') → **the personal genitive “他的/他们的”**, 'his / their' → **a modal particle of estimation** (“**其恕乎！己所不欲，勿施于人**” — 'It is, probably, reciprocity! What you yourself do not want, do not impose on others.' (Our translation.) — from “that” to “probably”: **a sense of distance worn down into an uncertain grasp**; “**其皆出于此乎**”, 'Does it all, perhaps, come from this?' (Our translation.), “**尔其无忘乃父之志**”, 'You must not forget your father's resolve.' (Our translation.) — the two faces of surmise and of the imperative). **焉**: the fused word “**于之/于是**” ('in it / in this') (“**心不在焉**” — 'the heart is not on it', on this; “**三人行，必有我师焉**” — 'Where three walk together, there is sure to be a teacher for me among them.' (Our translation.) — among them) → **bleached into a modal particle** (“**寒暑易节，始一反焉**” — 'Only when cold and heat had changed their seasons did he turn back once.' (Our translation.) — only the breath of “呢” is left). The common track of grammaticalisation: **content word ＞ (syntactic levers: the serial verb, the pending case) ＞ function word ＞ modal particle**; **bleach it any further and you have to change to a new vehicle**: thereupon “于” is laid off, “於” takes over the post, and “乎” makes a guest appearance, while the counter for “之” is forever busy — **a function word is a fossil of the language's old manufacture; in every layer are buried the remains of a content word** (**what Wang Li 王力 called “a grammatical employee's retirement and re-employment” — never mind the terminology; the route is the same**).
:::

::: example One character, three cuts: “而” {#ex-er}
“而” is not translated; it only **wires the connection** — for each sentence below, decide how it is wired, and say why it is not wired otherwise: ① “学而时习之” ('Study, and in due time practise it.' (Our translation.)); ② “人不知而不愠” ('Others do not know one, and yet one is not resentful.' (Our translation.)); ③ “蹴尔而与之” ('Give it to him with a kick.' (Our translation.)); ④ “子路率尔而对” ('Zilu 子路 answered abruptly.' (Our translation.)); ⑤ “万钟则不辩礼义而受之” ('Come ten thousand *zhong*, and he accepts them without sorting out ritual and rightness.' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
**① Continuation (sequential linking)**: “学” and “习” stand in a before-and-after running account — **read it as “然后”** ('and then'); **② adversative**: “不知” and “不愠” pull against each other — **read it as “却”** ('yet'); **③ modification (linking an adverbial)**: “蹴尔” is the **manner** of “与” — **read it as “着/地”** ('-ing / -ly') (“蹴尔” = the look of a kick — **manner enters the factory, a verb leaves it**); ④ **modification of the same type**: “率尔” is the attitude of “对” (**a retest of the same type** — to answer “sequential continuation” shows that the rule has not been learned firmly: **the manner-description is at the back, the verb brings up the rear, and what the character “而” carries is entirely the reading of the character “地”**); ⑤ **adversative and satirical at once**: “不辩礼义”, and yet he goes and “受之” — inside this “而” sits a cold laugh from Mencius (**read it as “竟然就”**, 'and thereupon actually'). The line to mark by: **“而” itself has zero meaning; it is the sentence's adaptor — sequential connection, a switched connection, a step-down (modification), all depending on the voltage difference between the two ends**. Any script that translates every “而” uniformly as “而且” ('and moreover') is treating every switch as a lamp.
:::
:::

::: intuition Function words are the breathing organs of a piece of writing {#int-huxi}
Read it aloud once and you understand: **the character “也” is drawn out into affirmation, “矣” closes off what is already so, “乎” rises into a question, “哉” lets feeling out, “夫” clears the throat and opens a topic, “盖” coughs first and only then sets up the argument** — a function word is **the temporal shape of mood** (in the *Analects*, “**予欲无言**”, 'I would rather not speak.' (Our translation.), and Zigong 子贡 asks in haste, “子如不言，则小子何述焉？”, 'If you, Master, do not speak, then what are we pupils to transmit?' (Our translation.) — **a single “焉” closes the line, and leaves blank the anxiety of “where am I to take this down”**). The experiment of deleting function words is the most serviceable surgical operation in appreciation: **change “其恕乎” into “恕”** — the point is still there, **but that beat of hesitation and of consoling advice is entirely gone**; delete the “焉” of “积土成山，风雨兴焉” ('Heap earth into a mountain, and wind and rain arise there.' (Our translation.)) — **the sense collapses from “in this place” into “comes about”**. Therefore: translation may sacrifice function words, but **appreciation must make offerings to them**; if in your writing you want an antique elegance, do not stick “之乎者也” on at random either — **a function word used wrongly gives you away more than one not used**. It is a footnote to the dialect, not a pleat in a stage costume.
:::

::: history Function words: from the house rules of a feel for the language to the statistical court {#sec-lishi}
**Han:** when masters of the Classics orally transmitted *judou* 句读 (traditional pause marks), they set the greatest store by function words (“**之乎者也，所以志其断绝**”, '之, 乎, 者 and 也 are the means by which one marks the breaks.' (Our translation.), a summary of that kind from later ages — **punctuation was not yet born; function words went first**); **Song and Yuan:** books of remarks on prose taught the tone of voice of ancient-style prose (*guwen*) (Chen Kui 陈骙, in *Wenze* 文则 (*The Rules of Writing*), discusses the mood of writing by way of “**辞之道**”, 'the way of wording' — **the study of function words turned into rhetoric**); **Qing:** **Liu Qi 刘淇, *Zhuzi bianlüe* 助字辨略 (*Brief Discriminations of Auxiliary Words*)** (“**助字**”, 'auxiliary words', became a field, ranging in explanation over the function words of the Classics and the Histories), **Wang Yinzhi 王引之, *Jingzhuan shici* 经传释词 (*Explaining Words in the Classics and Commentaries*)** (treating function words by **phonetic loan through sound** (*tongjia*) and by **induction from examples** — the author's preface to *Jingzhuan shici* takes as its own charge “**前人所未及，余都录之**”, 'What those before me did not reach, I have recorded it all.' (Our translation.); the whole book sets variant texts in the Classics and commentaries against the old commentaries of Han writers as mutual evidence, and is a textbook of the method of induction from examples); **the Qing tribunal for discriminating forgeries:** **Yan Ruoqu 阎若璩, *Shangshu guwen shuzheng* 尚书古文疏证 (*Evidential Analysis of the Ancient-Text Book of Documents*)**, taking a chronological gap in wording as evidence of the crime — **Yan found that the phrases of the late-appearing *Guwen Shangshu* 古文尚书 (the ancient-text *Book of Documents*) largely copy older books, and that the vocabulary does not fit the Three Dynasties, and he opened the precedent of “dating by language”** — the evidence room of the same court as the previous chapter's “**市买” is Eastern Han language**” — **this is what later ages call “internal evidence of linguistic chronology”**; **the Republic:** *Mashi wentong* 马氏文通 (*Mr Ma's Grammar*) set up four volumes on “虚字” ('empty words') (**prepositions, conjunctions, particles and interjections — Western-style drawers measuring a Chinese-style garment for the first time**); **Yang Shutang 杨树棠, *Ciquan* 词诠 (*Explaining the Words*)** (1928) arranged the function words of the Classics and commentaries into a **grammatical dictionary** (“a job title for every word”); **the present:** **contemporary quantitative stylistics** (frequency vectors of function words used to measure style — **wording can be imitated, habits are hard to fake**; a modern case in point is the separation, by clustering function words, of the first eighty chapters of *Dream of the Red Chamber* from the last forty **“modern quantitative stylistics takes the frequency of function words as the steadiest fingerprint of style (wording can be imitated, habits are hard to fake), and it has been used in research on the authorship of novels — such as the distinction between the first eighty chapters of *Dream of the Red Chamber* and the last forty”** (Our translation.)).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls with function words
**The compulsion of “one word, one meaning”** (translating every “以” as “use” — **several posts for one word are the norm; the post is judged by position**); **over-extending “焉 = fused word”** (splitting every 焉 you meet into “于之” — **a 焉 that is to be read as a modal particle, forcibly split, will be split into a faulty sentence**: “始一反焉”, 'only then did he turn back once', has no “于” and no “之” that can be split out); **a missed check of the five posts of the character 之** (“**何陋之有**” = the marker of a fronted object, 'What crudeness is there?' (Our translation.); “**师道之不传也久矣**” = between subject and predicate, cancelling the independence of the sentence, 'That the way of the teacher is not transmitted has long been so.' (Our translation.); “**蚓无爪牙之利**” = the 之 of a postposed attributive, 'an earthworm lacks the advantage of claws and teeth' — **three “之”, three operations of inversion; miss one knife and you will not recognise the patient**); **the trap of imitation** (writers of ancient-style prose in the Ming and Qing used function words more pre-Qin than the pre-Qin — **dating by frequency can guard against unintentional forgery, not against deliberate archaising**; it must be investigated on the same docket as character forms, the names of objects, and the avoidance of tabooed names).
:::

::: exercise Grammatical slices {level=1}
For the function word in bold in each of the following, write a “post specification” (word class + post + one equivalent in modern Chinese): ① “**以**其无礼于晋，且贰于楚也” ('Because they were discourteous to Jin, and moreover of divided allegiance towards Chu.' (Our translation.)); ② “若不阙秦，将焉取**之**” ('If we do not diminish Qin, from where shall we take it?' (Our translation.)); ③ “积土成山，风雨兴**焉**” ('Heap earth into a mountain, and wind and rain arise there.' (Our translation.)); ④ “身死人手，**为**天下笑” ('He himself dies at others' hands, and is laughed at by the world.' (Our translation.)); ⑤ “吾所以为此**者**，以先国家之急而后私仇也” ('The reason I do this is that I put the state's emergency first and a private feud after.' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
Reference: ① 以 = conjunction · marking cause (“因为”, 'because' — **note that what follows it is a sentence, whereas a preposition is followed by a phrase: to judge the post, see how heavy a load it carries**); ② 之 = pronoun · object case (“它”, 'it' — standing for a city or for a thing; **an object-case 之 always points to the nearest text above**); ③ 焉 = fused word (“于此”, 'at this' — the **live-broadcast position** of the mountain and of the wind and rain); ④ 为 = preposition · passive (“被”, 'by' — **the forerunner of the “为所” pattern, taking off in the Han**); ⑤ 者 = **a modal topical pause + an interface of explanation** (“…的原因”, 'the reason why…' — the **hinge** of a causal complex sentence: the nominal sentence, the hypophora, and the causal sentence are three beds, and it can sleep in any of them). Marking: name the post with its grammatical term, and also give a modern Chinese equivalent — **terms with no modern equivalent are spinning idle; a modern equivalent with no term is mysticism**.
:::

::: exercise Authenticating an imitation {level=2}
Forge a short piece (40–80 characters) in a “pre-Qin manner” (you may use at least three of the function words “盖、其、夫、焉、耳”); with the person beside you, appraise each other's work: find the **anachronistic giveaways** (collocations of function words that the pre-Qin did not have; later grammar mixed in), and give three criteria consistent with this lesson's “frequency court”.
::: solution
Marking: the giveaways in the imitation **can be stated as rules** (for example, in pre-Qin Chinese “也” is not used as a mid-sentence topical pause in a sentence that is not nominal; the suffix “们” is a giveaway; “**于是乎**” arises late — all of these are supported by evidence from frequency, and **a “it feels wrong” for which you cannot state a criterion does not count as an authentication**); a check in the other direction: **your own habits of modern Chinese (“进行”, “对于”), once mixed into Classical Chinese, give you away just the same — the blind spot in a feel for the language is oneself**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: function words and personality {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: “The function words a person likes to use expose his cognitive stance.” Choose any two from the hesitation of “其…乎”, the opening cough of “盖”, the dismissive close of “耳”, and the blank left by “焉”; with an instance from a work (any one of the *Analects*, the *Zhuangzi*, the *Records of the Grand Historian*, or a verbal habit of a modern writer), write a stylistic analysis, and answer: **statistics say “wording can be imitated, habits are hard to fake” — do you agree, to this extent, that “personality is the distribution of function words”?**
::: solution
Points for marking: a **description of the mechanism** linking mood and stance (“其恕乎” = giving the standard answer while putting the teacher's rod away — **the pedagogy of estimative mood**; “耳” = a close of “no more than this” — **contempt grammaticalised**); instances must bring the original text; on “personality is a distribution”, give **a position with reservations** (a personal style really does exist (a library of authorial fingerprints can be used), but **imitation, the application of a ready-made style, and the language-data of an age all dilute an explanation by personality** — to agree as far as 70 marks will do, but you must write who was given the remaining 30); if the conclusion arrives at “**to understand a person's function words is to understand where his hesitation stops the pen**”, that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The workshop settles its accounts: the twin machines (者 = nominalisation + the registration stamp of inversion; 所 = extraction of the object + a component of the passive; “所以” does double duty), the ladder (以: take → use → because; 其: that → his → probably; 焉: at this → 呢 — **in every layer are buried the remains of a content word**), the adaptor (而: sequential connection, a switched connection, a step-down, depending on the voltage difference at the two ends), the court (the frequency fingerprint + the counter-example of imitation: the case of Yan Ruoqu is on file). The method in one sentence: **judge a function word's post by its position, take the evidence for period-dating from frequency, and bring appreciation back to life by reading aloud**. The next chapter changes the age: parallel prose (*pianwen*) and the rhapsody (*fu*) — when Chinese characters begin **to take parallelism as their nature and rhyme as their craze**.
:::
