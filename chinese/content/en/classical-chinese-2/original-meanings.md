---
title: "Original and extended meanings"
---

The previous course taught you to read a sentence through; this course teaches you to read **one character thick**. That the character “一” is listed in a dictionary as far as an eighth sense entry is not a compiler with nothing to do — it is the history of one sememe gathering a crowd over two thousand years. This chapter issues you three tools: **a searchlight for original meaning, a genealogical study of extension, and a demon-revealing mirror for the phonetic loan (rebus)**.

## The lineage of one word {#def-jiazu}

::: definition Original meaning, basic meaning, extended meaning {#def-benyi}
**Original meaning** = the oldest word-meaning that the graph can prove — note the qualifier: **what the graph can prove**. It is therefore the dual product of "structural meaning + textual corroboration" (the oracle-bone form of 为 is the shape of a hand leading an elephant in labour; Xu Shen 许慎 had not seen the oracle-bone script, and his explanation misses the facts — ***Shuowen jiezi* (*Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*) is the largest dictionary of original meanings, and also the largest dictionary of original meanings awaiting collation**); **basic meaning** = the hub of word-frequency and of the use of senses in one age (the graph of 兵 is formed as 斤 holding 戈; the original meaning is a weapon, yet in Warring States texts the basic meaning has already leaned towards soldiers and military affairs — **the original meaning is what one digs up; the basic meaning is what living people use**); **extended meaning** = a new sememe radiated out from the hub sense, by three great routes of radiation: **proximity** (兵 → weapon → the person who holds a weapon → war), **similarity** (綱, the main rope of a net → the guiding essentials of a thing → the making of 纲纪 into a verb), **contrastive association** (臭 originally covered both the fragrant and the foul — “其臭如兰” ('its fragrance is like the orchid's.' (Our translation.)) — and later fed exclusively on the foul sense: **word-meaning will feed on one side and shrink**). In a well-trained mind, a polysemous word is not a table of sense entries; it is a **tree with its rootlets**: to look a sense up, find the root first; to assign a sense, watch which way the branches go.
:::

::: definition The usual directions of extension {#def-fangxiang}
Extension does not grow by wild paths. It has a countable few "phototropisms": **concrete → abstract** (道: road → method → doctrine; 理: working jade until the grain shows → ordered pattern → the principle of Heaven); **one word for both giving and receiving** (the oracle-bone form of 受 covers both conferring and receiving — the two ends of one transaction at first shared a single word; 贷, 借 and 假 are tangled in the same way); **thing → action → agent** (the original meaning of 盖 is 苫 — a covering woven of grass → to cover → to overwhelm); **common noun ⇄ proper name** (江 and 河 were stewed from the proper names of the Yangtze and the Yellow River into common nouns for water — **half the history of the directions of extension is the history of Chinese names for things**). A sense of direction is worth more than a table of sense entries: when a new word comes wearing a familiar face, you can guess **where it came from and where it is going**.
:::

::: example A genealogy of the character 与 {#ex-yu}
Draw up a genealogy for the character 與 (与). *Shuowen jiezi* explains 與 by “一施一” ('one bestows on one') — **giving is its semantic hub**. Starting from "giving", sort the seven examples below into sememes and mark the route of extension: ① “与之斗卮酒” ('gave him a dou-zhi of wine.' (Our translation.)); ② “与嬴而不助五国” ('sided with Ying and did not aid the five states.' (Our translation.)); ③ “失其所与，不知” ('to lose those with whom one is allied is unwise.' (Our translation.)); ④ “吾非斯人之徒与而谁与” ('If I am not to keep company with people of this kind, with whom shall I keep company?' (Our translation.)); ⑤ “吾与汝毕力平险” ('I, together with you, will bend all our strength to level the obstacles.' (Our translation.)); ⑥ “无乃尔是过与” ('Is it not, rather, your fault?' (Our translation.)); ⑦ “与闻国政” ('to take part in the hearing of state affairs.' (Our translation.)).

::: solution
The genealogy's verdict: **① giving = the hub sense** (the verbal root that is still alive); **② adherence** = the abstraction of giving (goodwill, even a state, is sent out — Qin's “与嬴” is this branch); **③ ally** = action → role (“所与” = the recipient of the giving, congealed into a noun — “失其所与”); **④ accompanying and assenting, two branches dancing together** (“徒与而谁与” = with whom to accompany and follow — **the same character 与 carries both the branch of accompanying and the branch of assenting; the translating brush has no choice but to take both in**); **⑤ 与 = 和 (a preposition)** = a further bleaching of accompanying (accompaniment → a marker of coordination — **the standard steps of grammaticalisation: verb ＞ preposition ＞ conjunction**; 共 and 同 are on the same ladder); **⑥ modal particle** = phonetic loan (interchangeable with 歟 — **a character borrowed for the same sound, nothing to do with the genealogy; open a separate household register**); **⑦ taking part** = an inverse extension of the sense "to enter into the midst of it"; dictionaries list it as an extension of a sense entry, and we follow the dictionaries. The genealogy in full: **giving — (abstraction) adherence — (role) ally ｜ giving — (accompaniment) accompanying — (bleaching) preposition and conjunction ｜ accompanying — (entering in) taking part ‖ 歟 = a loan-character add-on**. Scoring: draw five branches and one loan, and you pass; **spotting the two branches dancing together in ④ counts as a distinction**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Word meaning is a net; a dictionary is drawers {#int-wang}
Lining a polysemous word up as "①②③④" is the tyranny of printing: **sememes are in fact a net, not a queue** — neighbouring sememes seep into one another (the accompanying and the assenting of 与 are alive together in a single sentence); when an old sense leaves the stage it often leaves a title deed behind (idioms are a fossil-store of word-meaning: **the 走 of “走马观花” ('viewing flowers from a galloping horse') is still mounted on the ancient sense 奔, 'to gallop'**). So the right posture for looking a word up is: **first fix the contextual sense, then chain back to the hub** — like claiming a lost pet, and seeing which member of the household it resembles. One step further: **extension has a place of origin, and it also has coups** — 经济 was originally a compressed branch of 经世济民 ('ordering the age and relieving the people'); in the modern period it was made the translational equivalent of economy, **and was conversely honoured as the original meaning of the new word: extension can stage a coup; grafting is not growth**. One word, one coup — enough for you to savour.
:::

::: history From "explaining a character by looking at the picture" to "sound passing through the wall" {#sec-lishi}
**Pre-Qin**: the *Zuo Tradition* and the *Han Feizi* had already set the precedent of **explaining the sense by taking a character apart** (“止戈，武也” — 'To stop the dagger-axe is 武.' (Our translation.) — **the first bucket of gold in exegesis (glossing) was political rhetoric**); **Han**: *Shuowen jiezi*, with more than nine thousand characters, opened a general ledger of "explaining meaning from the structure of the graph" ([[chinese-characters/six-categories]]), and the contemporary *Shiming* 释名 (*Explanation of Names*) passed through the wall by **sound glosses** (“某者某也” ('X is Y') — the first nationwide survey of etymology, though most of its conclusions belong to **folk etymology**); **Qing**: Duan Yucai 段玉裁 established the discipline for sense entries that "**original meaning and extension are to be told apart**" (the gist of the editorial principles of *Shuowen jiezi zhu* 说文解字注 (*Annotated Shuowen jiezi*)), and the preface to Wang Niansun 王念孙's *Guangya shuzheng* 广雅疏证 (*Evidential Commentary on the Guangya*) boldly declares that **the aim of glossing resides in sound** (‘诂训之旨存乎声音.’ (Our translation.)) — characters are broken on the strength of sound, and **the sound gloss was refitted from metaphysics into a method that can be checked**; **Republic of China**: semantics entered the department, and the study of "meaning" gained a chair; **the present**: cognitive linguistics issued extension a theoretical licence (**radial categories, extension in a chain, and "family resemblance" entered the syllabuses of departments of Chinese — "a network is not a queue" acquired an academic place in the household register**), and the *Hanyu da zidian* 汉语大字典 (*Great Dictionary of Chinese Characters*) and the *Guxun huizuan* 故训汇纂 (*Compendium of Old Glosses*) filed two thousand years of sememes.
:::

::: warning Three defences of word-meaning
**Guard against "reading a meaning off the shape"** — splitting a character the moment you see it: 道德 is one word today; in the pre-Qin period it walked on two legs (道 and 德 each had a household register of its own); **there is a chronology to a disyllable's combining into a word: you may explain the parts apart, but if you take the combination as the real thing you must look it up**. Guard against **"ruling the past by the present"** — reading old books with modern senses: 衣 is not read yì (the 衣 of 衣冠禽兽 is "to wear"), and 雨 is not read yù (the 雨 of 天雨墙坏 is "to fall" — **word-meaning and pronunciation are tried in the same case; a changed reading is the ticket of admission**). Guard against **"extension can do anything"** — meeting every character by finding an original meaning and then assigning a story: **extension must have textual piers**; a branch with no written evidence is an unauthorised private building; a graft must be declared (the case of 经济 is on file, and cannot go unexamined).
:::

::: exercise Drawing meaning-trees {level=1}
For each of 兵, 向, 解, 理, 举 and 绝, draw a tree of original meaning and extended meaning (mark the hub sense, the three phototropisms, and at least one example sentence that serves as a "textual pier"), and between any two of the characters find one **isomorphic branch** (the same route of extension in different characters, such as "concrete → abstract").
::: solution
Scoring: each tree has an example sentence as a pier (a branch with no written evidence is snapped off and drawn again); a hit on isomorphic branches between a pair means your understanding has passed (理 and 纪 both climb from **the grain of a thing** to **the order of an affair** — abstraction is the coming-of-age rite of every word); extra credit: for 解, point out the **parting of sound and sense** in “解剖—瓦解—懈怠（解邂）” ('to dissect — to fall apart — to slacken (解 and 邂)') (when a graph's burden is too heavy, the household divides — the prelude to ancient and modern forms of a word).
:::

::: exercise An open case in the genealogy {level=2}
信: ① “信言不美” ('Trustworthy words are not beautiful.' (Our translation.)) ② “愿陛下亲之信之” ('I wish Your Majesty would draw them close and trust them.' (Our translation.)) ③ “信义著于四海” ('Good faith stood renowned throughout the four seas.' (Our translation.)) ④ “烟涛微茫信难求” ('In the faint haze of mist and waves, it is truly hard to seek.' (Our translation.)) ⑤ “低眉信手续续弹” ('With lowered brows she let her hands pluck on and on at will.' (Our translation.)) ⑥ “自可断来信” ('One may simply turn away the messenger who comes.' (Our translation.)). Locate the sememe of each of the six examples (honest → believe → truly → at will → envoy/matchmaker — five stations), and decide: is ⑥ an extension or a phonetic loan?
::: solution
For reference: ① honest (the words are true); ② trust; ③ trustworthiness (a quality made into a noun); ④ truly (from "not deceiving" to "not unfounded", made into an adverb); ⑤ at will, as one lets it be (from "at one's own discretion" to "casually" — a living fossil in **信手**, 'a hand left to itself'); ⑥ envoy/matchmaker (**the one entrusted** — 信 originally has the sense "messenger": a tally counts as the token → the person who holds the credential — **the extension takes the associative road "credential → the one who holds the credential"**, and is not a phonetic loan). Scoring: the verdict on ⑥ must give a **route of evidence** (a dictionary sense entry + written evidence); "I feel that" is not allowed.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the tyranny of the dictionary {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters. Taking any one polysemous word from this chapter as your example, discuss "**how a sequence of sense entries disguises itself as the essence of word-meaning**" — is the standing of the first sense entry given by history (inquiry into the original meaning, and a claim staked there) or by use (the basic meaning)? Whose cheese did the reform of modern dictionaries of the language, "matching sense to sound, and ordering by frequency", move? You must use at least one case of **semantic grafting** of the "经济 / coup" kind.
::: solution
Points for scoring: the concepts are clearly distinguished (original meaning, basic meaning, and the first sense entry are not the same thing, and **the arrangement of a dictionary is itself a stance in exegesis**); the case is concrete (经济 is the translational counterpart of a Japanese translation-term, and is conversely honoured, in modern common knowledge, as the "original meaning" — **a veritable record of usurpation on the table of sense entries**); the discussion can reach a self-awareness of method (the order of consulting a dictionary = look up the family first, then the household register — **sense entries are drawers; word-meaning is a net**); the conclusion may provoke, but it must pay in evidence — only a dilemma on the scale of "**should a dictionary be ordered by the frequency of its senses, or ordered historically**" counts as finding the door.
:::

::: summary
The meaning-tree lesson, accounts closed: original meaning has evidence of two kinds (structure of the graph + texts; *Shuowen jiezi* is both merit and fault); the hub sense drifts with the age (the 兵 that one digs up, as against the one living people use); three phototropisms (proximity · similarity · association; concrete → abstract is the coming-of-age rite); the phonetic-loan add-on (same sound, different sense, a separate household register; parting from extension depends on "whether there is a textual pier"); word-meaning can stage a coup and be grafted (the case of 经济). The next chapter strolls through the grove of synonyms: why 视, 瞻, 望, 见 and 观 are not five words for 看 ('to look') — **synonymy is the most refined ritual system of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*)**.
:::
