---
title: "Pronouns, numerals and forms of address"
---

The three most "empty" things in a sentence — **who, how many, and how to address someone** — are all heavy industry in Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*). For a pronoun, look at **position**; for a numeral, guard against **watering**; for a form of address, check the **household register**. This chapter is the manual to the ancients' system of interpersonal navigation. Read one cell wrong, and it is not an embarrassment; it is an **accident**.

## Pronouns: the grammar of who {#sec-dai}

::: definition Three squads of pronouns {#def-dai}
**The self-reference squad** (first person): 吾, 我, and 予/余 (in the *Analects*, **the old theory, from high antiquity, of a division of labour between 吾 and 我: 吾 is mostly nominative and 我 mostly accusative — in the *Book of Documents*, "**予及汝皆亡**" ('I and you shall both perish.' (Our translation.)) brings even "汝" into the same boat**), 印 (a fossil of self-reference preserved only in old books; the dictionaries label it "我", and examples are seldom met with), **朕** (**before the First Emperor of Qin, anyone could call himself this** — Qu Yuan 屈原's "**朕皇考曰伯庸**" ('My honoured late father was called Boyong.' (Our translation.)) has not a breath of imperial air; **from 221 BC it was nationalised into a designation reserved for the emperor — the history of one pronoun's change of ownership**), 小子/仆/牛马走 ("**太史公牛马走司马迁**再拜言" — 'Sima Qian 司马迁, Grand Historian, a servant who runs like ox and horse, bows twice and speaks.' (Our translation.) — self-deprecation that enrols one in the servants' company), 妾 (the departmental term by which a woman refers to herself), 臣 (the moment one sees the ruler, one is "臣" — **the situation of an office automatically converts it into a pronoun**); the plural has no word-form of its own, and depends on pieces added after: **曹, 属 and 辈** ("**若属皆且为所虏**" ('You and your sort will all be taken captive.' (Our translation.)) and "吾曹" ('our lot')). **The squad that points to people**: 女/汝, 尔, 而 and 若 ("**若入前为寿**" ('You, go forward and pledge a health.' (Our translation.)), the "you" of the Hongmen banquet; "**若属**" ('your sort') is of the same family), and 而 (*Zuo Tradition* (*Zuozhuan*), the fifteenth year of Duke Xuan: "**余，而所嫁妇人之父也**" — "我是你所嫁妇人的父亲", 'I am the father of the woman you married.' (Our translation.) — one sentence that says all there is to say of both the personal score and the grammar
::: definition Forms of address: name, courtesy name, posthumous title, and the study of steps {#def-chenghu}
**名** (the given name) is what one is given in childhood, and **字** (the courtesy name) is what is added at the capping ceremony at twenty — "**幼名，冠字，五十以伯仲，死谥，周道也**" ('A name in childhood, a courtesy name at the capping, at fifty the terms of birth order, and at death a posthumous title: such is the way of Zhou.' (Our translation.)) (*Book of Rites*, "Tan Gong": four sets of identity papers across a life); **for referring to oneself, one uses the given name** (self-abasement is the other half of politeness), and **peers, elders and superiors address one another by the courtesy name** (to call a person straight by his "名" = a ruler to a minister, a superior to an inferior, a **demotion strike** — in the *Analects* the ruler of the state calls him Kong Qiu 孔丘, while the disciples, recording their teacher, call him "夫子" ('Master')); after death there is the **posthumous title** (谥) — **a marking system once the coffin is closed** (the "Shifa jie" of the *Yi Zhoushu* (*Lost Book of Zhou*): "道德博闻曰文、克定祸乱曰武" — 'Broad in virtue and wide in learning is called Wen; able to settle disaster and disorder is called Wu.' (Our translation.) — **"文正" is a civil official's full-marks paper**); an emperor's three sets of after-sales labels run side by side, **temple name / posthumous title / tomb name** (Taizong, Emperor Wen, Zhaoling — one man, three designations). **Honorifics for the person addressed are the study of steps**: 足下, 阁下, 殿下 and 陛下 — the original meaning of each is **not daring to point straight at the person, but calling the attendant below his steps to pass the word on** (足下 is the most plebeian; 殿 and 陛 are architectural elevations). The self-deprecation matrix: **孤, 寡人 and 不谷** ("**人之所恶，唯孤寡不谷，而王公以为称**" — 'What people loathe is only to be orphaned, bereft and without grain, and yet kings and dukes take these as their titles.' (Our translation.) — the *Laozi* (*Daodejing*) has seen through this rhetoric of **taking an ill-omened name upon oneself**), **不佞, 不才 and 不肖** (the "without ability" line), **窃 and 私** ("窃以为" ('I venture to think') — the opinion first demotes itself by half a grade), **贱息** ("**老臣贱息舒祺，最少，不肖**" — 'This old servant's humble offspring Shu Qi is the youngest, and unworthy.' (Our translation.) — Chu Long 触龙, speaking to the Queen Dowager of Zhao: even his son is a "贱息" ('humble offspring')). There are, besides, four retrieval systems running in parallel: **address by courtesy name, by office, by ancestral commandery, and by birth-order rank** (Wang Youjun, Liu Hedong, Bai ershi'er — **a Tang address book is indexed in three columns: the official register, geography, and elder against younger**).
:::

::: definition Numerals: real numbers, empty numbers, and ancient fractions {#def-shu}
**Three formulae for self-defence against empty numbers** (虚数): common knowledge among Qing scholars — **when the ancients stated a number, three, nine, a hundred, a thousand and ten thousand were often loan-characters for "many"** (Wang Yinzhi 王引之, in *Jingyi shuwen*, raises examples of this kind again and again): "**三仕三已**" ('Three times he took office, and three times he was dismissed.' (Our translation.)) need not be ticked three times in the ledger; "**九死其犹未悔**" ('I would die nine deaths and still not regret it.' (Our translation.)) is not an application for insurance; "**将军百战死，壮士十年归**" ('The general dies after a hundred battles; the stalwart comes home after ten years.' (Our translation.)) is a double act of reciprocal phrasing (*huwen*) together with empty reference. **Hidden pits among real numbers** (实数): **再 = the second time** ("一鼓作气，再而衰" — 'At the first drum the spirit is raised; at the second it declines.' (Our translation.) — the second job of "又" ('again') was hired later); **"倍" is often a phonetic loan (*tongjia*) for "背"** (to turn against, to betray — a phonetic loan is squatting among the numbers too). **Han-style fractions**: "十死二三" and "亡十二三" = two or three tenths (**the casualty register of the *Records* and the *Han*: real numbers**); "**什一**" = a tax of one part in ten (**"什一之税"** ('a tax of one in ten'), Mencius's archaeology of the tax rate). **The age before classifiers were mass-produced**: "**乘韦先，牛十二**" (*Zuo Tradition*, the thirty-third year of Duke Xi; Xian Gao 弦高 provisioning the army: tanned ox-hides go first, and twelve head of oxen follow — **postposing the numeral is the regular parking space in Old Chinese**); "带甲百万、车千乘" ('a million men in armour, and a thousand chariots') is a strategist's quotation for armaments. Before reading the pre-Qin, first install this intuition: **a number = a thing, its position is free, and the classifier has not yet been born** (匹, 头 and 个 are talents who arose later).
:::

::: example Interpersonal navigation in a letter between states {#ex-guoshu}
A simulated edict and reply in the manner of the *Intrigues of the Warring States*: "**寡人不足承宗庙，夫人与诸大臣谋不谷——不谷不敏，敬诺。夫子行矣，为寡人谢齐王：‘弊邑之主，愿为兄弟，岁输皮币。’**" ('I, the inadequate one, am not fit to undertake the ancestral temple. The lady and the ministers have deliberated for me, the one without grain — I, without grain, am not quick of mind, and I respectfully consent. Go, Master; on my behalf, the inadequate one, speak to the King of Qi: "The ruler of our poor city wishes to be as brothers, and will send hides and silk each year."' (Our translation.)) Circle every form of address and every self-deprecation, turn them into modern diplomatic language, and point out **two places of deliberate self-abasement** and **one place that borrows another person's step**.
::: solution
**The self-deprecation matrix**: 寡人 / 不谷 (the two self-abasements are **diplomacy's buffer** — Laozi had long since seen through this rhetoric of **taking an ill-omened name upon oneself in search of good fortune**);
:::
:::

::: intuition Pronouns are empty; forms of address are full {#int-kongman}
The philosophy of this chapter: **a pronoun's meaning draws almost its whole salary from grammatical position** ("之" is nothing in itself; only when it stands in the object slot does it draw the wages of "他/它" — **in modern Chinese, "他" still holds concurrently the posts of "它" and of empty reference**); **the meaning of a form of address, by contrast, is entirely a density poured in by institutions** (one and the same "you": 尔 = a level gaze, 足下 = a step laid underneath, 陛下 = the great hall standing between — **the history of the Chinese second person is a long inflation of polite formulae**: the upgrade "你老→您" was completed only in the Ming and Qing, and the character "您" was originally "你们的" — **a plural turning into an honorific number is the standard path of inflation**, and it is the same across languages). The reading strategy therefore splits in two: **meet a pronoun, and check its position** (case and word order: which post it stands at in the sentence); **meet a form of address, and check the institution** (who is on the dais, how many steps, whose name is avoided). A hint for writing, in passing: **today's "贵司, 敝处, 在不" are all living fossils of this system** — "在不" ('are you there?') has zero steps, and so it is fit only for a friend.
:::

::: history From oracle-bone inscriptions to the character "您" {#sec-lishi}
**Oracle-bone inscriptions**: the system of numerals is complete, and the hunting inscriptions record captures in itemised real numbers; the emptying of "三" and "九" is a phenomenon of the stratum of transmitted texts (**real numbers in the divination records, empty numbers in the *Songs* and the *Documents* — two ways of keeping the books**); **Zhou**: "a name in childhood and a courtesy name at the capping" becomes a rite ("Tan Gong"), and bo, zhong, shu and ji enter the household register (**Zhongni 仲尼 was second in the birth order**); **Qin, 221 BC**: "朕" is nationalised, and 陛下 is made an institution — **the first reform of property rights in the history of pronouns**; **Han**: commentators on the Classics handled personal pronouns by sound-gloss equations of the type "**而，犹女也**" ('而 is as 女.' (Our translation.)) — **exegesis moonlighting as the study of grammar**; **Qing**: *Mashi wentong* (1898) establishes a category "代名字" ('words that stand in for names') — **for the first time the drawer of Indo-European grammar was stuffed with Classical Chinese pronouns, and for the first time someone measured whether they fitted**; **the modern age**: research on the history of Chinese by Wang Li 王力 and others shows that "**您**" is a fusion and contraction of "你们"; the form appears in the Song–Yuan vernacular (*baihua*), and northern Mandarin (*guanhua*) of the Ming and Qing completes the upgrade to an honorific — **a rare inflationary upgrade of the second person in the history of Chinese**; **the present age**: excavated bamboo and silk show that one and the same sentence commonly uses different pronouns in Warring States copies and in the transmitted text — **pronouns are the most easily "levelled into the common language" in copying and transmission, and bamboo and wooden slips are therefore the live recording of the history of pronouns**.
:::

::: warning Four accidents of address
**The accident of using the given name** — to call an elder or a superior straight by his "名" was, in the old sense, the same as ripping off his staff badge in public; **referring to oneself by one's courtesy name likewise overturns the cart** (one makes one's bow while holding oneself up); **the character 朕, travelling in time** — before the Qin, to call oneself "朕" was not unconstitutional; after the Qin, for anyone outside the imperial house to take it upon himself to say "朕" was evidence of the crime of plotting rebellion (**a double grip, for dating a text and for detecting forgery**); **reading a numeral as real** — entering "三仕三已" as a running account, and weeping over "亡者十二三" as though everyone had been wiped out (**empty numbers in the lyrical zone, real numbers in the zone of the histories — ask the register before you set brush to paper**); **the blind spot of name taboo** — to see "常山" and not think of "恒山" is **to recognise a person from an identity card whose name has been changed**.
:::

::: exercise An audit of forms of address {level=1}
For the *Chu shi biao* ('Memorial on setting out with the army'), the *Chu shi biao*, an extract (from "**臣本布衣，躬耕于南阳**" ('I was originally a commoner, and I ploughed in person at Nanyang.' (Our translation.)) to "**临表涕零，不知所言**" ('Coming to this memorial, my tears fall, and I do not know what I have said.' (Our translation.))): list every term of self-reference and every term for another person, sort them into the grades "humble / honorific / neutral", and keep the rhetorical account of how many times "先帝" occurs.
::: solution
Marking: inside the memorial, admit only words that actually occur: 臣 (self-reference · humble) / 先帝 (reference to the dead ruler · honorific + a posthumous title conferred afterwards) / 陛下 (address to the other party · the study of steps); "先帝" occurs six times in all — **each mention invites the founding partner up to the seat of honour to serve as a witness** (**the departed spirit of Former Emperor Liu is this memorial's strongest enforcer**); the "尔" of "尔来二十有一年矣" ('Since then it has been twenty-one years.' (Our translation.)) = **那** ('that'; demonstrative, not personal — **an on-the-spot check of one pronoun holding several posts**).
:::

::: exercise The officer who translates numbers {level=2}
Translate, and mark empty or real: ① "三人行，必有我师焉" ('Where three people walk, there is sure to be a teacher for me among them.' (Our translation.)); ② "亦余心之所善兮，虽九死其犹未悔" ('It is what my heart holds good; though I died nine deaths I would still not regret it.' (Our translation.)); ③ "再拜献大王足下" ('Bowing twice, I present this to you, Great King.' (Our translation.)); ④ "行路之余，十才二三" ('Of what remains of those on the road, barely two or three out of ten.' (Our translation.)); ⑤ "地方千里，带甲百万" ('The territory is a thousand li square — the li (里) changed in length from age to age — and the men in armour are a million.' (Our translation.)).
Requirement: for every empty number, state the rhetorical motive of "borrowing the character to stand for many".
::: solution
Reference: ① 三 = an approximate number / empty (a loan for many people; **it makes sense to set "三" against "师众" ('teachers in plenty')**) ② 九 = extremely many (**dying as many as nine times is the hyperbolic grammar of loyalty**) ③ 再拜 = bowing twice (**a real number of the rites: how many times the act is performed is fixed by institution, and is not empty**) ④ 十二三 = two or three tenths (**a Han fraction, a real-number register**) ⑤ "带甲百万" need not have been counted out — **the show-front of numbers: the strategists' zone is the emptiest (a quotation for armaments), and the ritual zone is the most real (再拜 = bowing twice; the act has an institution behind it)**). Marking: **empty or real is not settled by how large the number is, but by register** (real in the ritual zone, empty in the lyrical zone, emptiest in the strategists' zone — **suit the number to the person before you; first ask the occasion**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the inflationary history of honorifics {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: from "尔汝" to "您", and from "足下" to "亲" (the "亲" of customer service in online shopping today), on **the cycle of "inflation — devaluation — upgrading again" in second-person honorifics**. You must draw on one historical fact from this lesson (any one of the study of steps behind 陛下, a plural becoming an honorific number, and the fusion chain "若属→你属→你们→您"), and you must predict: **which honorific will be the next to be eliminated?**
::: solution
Marking points: the mechanism is clear (**the lifespan of an honorific = the speed at which it is overused** — **the old meaning of "哥" was "兄", and the old meaning of "爷" was "父" — every word in the system of address has a history of falling and of drift**; the materials are all there in Liang Zhangju 梁章钜's *Chengwei lu* ('Record of appellations')); the historical fact is used accurately (fusion / contraction is an instance of **sound put at the service of politeness**); the prediction stands to reason (the inflationary path of "宝宝 / 老师 / 师傅" — **"老师", from the calibre of a master of the Classics to the calibre of a livestream, is a living specimen of the present**); if the conclusion is that "**the end of an honorific is to become an intimate name again**", this lesson awards an A.
:::

::: summary
Settling the account of the navigation system: three squads of pronouns (position pays the salary — two laws of fronting, by case-slot and by word order), four certificates of address (given name, capping, courtesy name and posthumous title, plus the study of steps, plus the matrix of self-reference and self-abasement; the history of who owned "朕"), the empty and the real among numerals (再 = two times, Han fractions, the postposed parking space in the age before nominal classifiers were mass-produced), and one entry on inflation (你们→您; **an honorific is the currency that devalues the fastest**). The method, in one sentence: **for a pronoun, look at position; for a form of address, check the institution; for a number, ask the occasion**. The next chapter returns to the deep water of function words: a grammar-course taking-apart of 其, 之, 而, 以 and 焉 — the load-bearing wall of the grammar of Classical Chinese.
:::
