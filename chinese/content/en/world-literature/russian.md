title: "Russian literature"

In the previous chapter the novel learned, in France, to conceal its view; in Russia in the same period, the novel wrote the view as a character — **this chapter's explanation is one sentence only: France had four public forums, Russia had only one and a half (periodicals and the theatre, plus half the church); when debate cannot hold a meeting, the claim (thesis) has to come on stage disguised as a role**.

## Definition: the periodical is a political party {#def-qikan}

::: definition The thick journal and the substitute parliament {#def-hou}
**Received account** (**In Russia in the middle and later nineteenth century, one monthly carried at once fiction, criticism, political commentary, book notices and a satirical column; the print run was counted in the thousands, and those who read it in copied form far outnumbered the subscribers; a novel, because it was carried in a given issue, became a public event (**Three institutional consequences (**① **Criticism is a speech in parliament** (**Received account: critics of the sort of Belinsky and Chernyshevsky had their articles recited in whole paragraphs, and their judgement decided whether a work could be discussed (**This is not the weak proposition that 'criticism influences creation', but a strong proposition: **in a society lacking representative institutions, the critical periodical performed the function of a representative institution (**Of the three forums in Chapter 4, only one is left here, and so that forum's power is magnified several times over (**② **The writer is the only public speaker** (**Received account: when a writer died, tens of thousands joined the funeral procession, and the funeral became an assembly held without permission (**This lesson's interpretation: **the funeral of a literary figure was then the only form of political demonstration that could be held in public (**Note the direction of the cause: it is not that people loved literature, but that there was no other activity one could join (**③ **The 'meaning of the work' is signed on its behalf by the critic** (**Received account: a noun for a literary type was coined by a critic, and a character in a novel became that noun's defining term (**This is the point that matters most in this lesson: **the words 多余人 ('superfluous man') and 虚无主义者 ('nihilist') did not come from life; they came from the polemics in the periodicals; **they in turn taught several generations how to tell one person from another (**Compared with the previous chapter's 'naming after the fact', what happened in Russia was **naming at the same time, and the person named at once acted according to the name**.
:::

::: definition Censorship and oblique writing {#def-qu}
**Received account** (**The empire maintained a formal office for the censorship of books and the press, and in addition a power of further penalties against plays and periodicals; authors and editors developed a set of techniques of evasion (**Three layers of technique (**① **The cut edition is the norm** (**Received account: important works often appeared bearing cuts; an author's manuscript and the first printed edition could differ; copied manuscripts therefore still had a value in circulation (**This lesson's analogy: **this is the present chapter's 'the edition is the ideology' (**The second course and Chapter 1 recur here: **the 'established text' we read is often the result of a negotiation between the censorship and the author (**② **Oblique writing (*qǔbǐ* 曲笔, 'the oblique brush') is a rhetoric** (**Received account: in the Russian critical tradition there is a proper name for this way of writing that 'says half and lets the reader supply the other half' (**the received usage commonly calls it 'Aesopian language'**) — **it is exactly the opposite of the previous chapter's free indirect speech: **the French technique is that no one claims it; the Russian technique is that everyone understands and no one says it outright (**Thus the same tool grew into two shapes under two systems: **in France, leaving it unsaid is in order to escape liability; in Russia, leaving it unsaid is in order to publish (**③ **The outlet abroad** (**Received account: some works were printed abroad and then smuggled back to circulate at home (**From this the lesson gives in advance one exchange rate from Chapter 11: **a book written in the language of its own country, typeset in a neighbouring country and distributed by smugglers — its number of readers cannot be inferred from the quantity printed at home — **any statistic of 'the Russian reader' must first subtract the uncertainty of this channel (**Lesson 3: a numerator without a denominator**.
:::

::: definition The superfluous man: a kind of seat in the assembly {#def-duoyu}
**Received account** (**In the narrative poems and long poems of the first half of the nineteenth century there appears a clever young nobleman: educated above his society, his strength of will lower than his self-consciousness, ending at last in a duel, in idleness, or in an exit; later ages gathered this series of characters under one type-noun (**This lesson's way of reading (**① **This is a post, not a temperament** (**What it describes, in an aristocratic society where serfdom and bureaucracy coexisted, is the condition of the heir educated in Western Europe who has no office he can take up: **capital, cultivation, and no post (**② **Its historical function** (**Each generation of writers rewriting this character once is equivalent to the Russian intelligentsia making a self-assessment once every ten years (**This lesson calls this 'the genre of the periodic self-inspection' (**Its English analogue is the sentimental traveller; its Chinese analogue is the line of *língyúzhě* 零余者 ('the person left as a zero remainder') around the nineteen-twenties (**This comparison is left for Chapter 11, where the exchange rate is worked out (**③ **The way he dies exposes the system** (**This character's ending is never that he finds work, but a duel, death from illness, or disappearance: **literature cannot supply a post for structural unemployment; it can supply only prestige (**Received account: this type was gradually replaced, in the later nineteenth century, by the 'new man' (**This change of person is itself political history (**because the 'new man' means that society began to believe there was a kind of person who could get things done.
:::

::: definition The little man: one quota event {#def-xiao}
**Received account** (**A short story about a copying clerk and an overcoat brought into literature a grade of office that had never been written as a protagonist; writers of later ages have a remark that is often quoted, to the effect that 'we all came out of this story' (**This lesson treats the famous saying as the legend layer (**Its content that can be checked is this fact: **for the half-century after, copying clerks, ninth-grade civil servants and poor students became the regular protagonists of the Russian novel (**Three points (**① **The quota comes before sympathy** (**Morally, everyone speaks of 'care for the lower orders'; the literary fact is that the list was changed for another set of people (**Chapter 6's 'quota revolution' has here one clean demonstration (**② **The coat is the only important prop in the whole book** (**One garment constitutes the entire plot of a novel: **this is the limit case of the economics of realism: **a character so poor that he can own only one object, so that the object is his whole life (**③ **The price of being mocked** (**Received account: this subject drew two kinds of criticism at once, one finding fault with it for exaggerating suffering in order to play on the feelings, one finding fault with it for avoiding the system (**This lesson's use of it: **when a work writes only the suffering and not the cause, it receives both these penalty notices at once; **and its speech for the defence is always 'I let him be seen first'.
:::

::: definition The idea-character: a shelter for the claim {#def-sixiang}
**Received account** (**The Russian long novel of the second half of the nineteenth century often has a character who comes on with a whole world-view; his function is to turn a social doctrine into a person who can be loved and who can also be refuted (**This lesson's analysis of the mechanism (**① **Why it is not an essay** (**An essay would be banned; once the claim becomes a character it gains a layer of legal exemption: **the novel can say 'I did not maintain this' (**The reverse, here, of Chapter 6's 'if it can be located, it can be convicted': **a claim that cannot be located cannot stay hidden, because the moment the character opens his mouth everyone knows who is speaking (**Hence the Russian solution: **let the claim have a given name, a surname and a temper, and when he dies there is still someone to weep (**② **Polyphony is a title bestowed afterwards** (**Received account: a researcher in the twentieth century described this structure as 'polyphony' (复调); between the writing of his book and its republication lay more than thirty years (**This lesson takes only one conclusion from it: **the voices in a novel may withhold approval from one another; **but the timing of this theory must be noted: **it appeared after the Russian novel had been canonised, and therefore it is both a discovery and an appointment (**Chapter 6's 'naming after the fact' recurs here in full (**③ **It changed how later ages read 'thought'** (**Received account: readers in the twentieth century took the most disputed passage in a long novel and discussed it as the core of the whole book (**This lesson's technical observation: **that passage is an insertion wholly at odds with narrative necessity, its length counted in tens of pages, and the plot does not advance before or after it; **its existence itself proves this chapter's claim: **in a country with only one and a half forums, a philosophical speech can become the most famous part of a book.
:::

::: example A comparison of two kinds of syntax {#ex-jufa}
In the same period, the French novel developed free indirect speech, and the Russian novel developed the idea-character and the long argument. In 200 words set out a testable explanation: **① what institutional problem each of the two techniques solved; ② give a year or a fact that could overturn your explanation (you must state what kind of material it comes from**).

::: solution
**① The institutional problem** — **France: publication can be prosecuted, so the technical aim is to make the view impossible to locate; **Russia: publication can be prohibited, so the technical aim is to have the view present and yet not something the author must answer for — **disguising it as a person is the least troublesome exemption (**Both techniques deal with 'an opinion the author does not want to sign'; the difference is the type of risk: **the risk of a lawsuit breeds a blurred syntax; the risk of a ban breeds embodiment in a character (**② A condition that could overturn it** — **An acceptable form of answer looks like this: 'If, in a year when the censorship was plainly relaxed, direct argument in Russian periodical fiction decreased in step, then this explanation is weakened; if, on the contrary, the argument did not decrease, or turned towards new subject-matter, then other variables must be introduced (such as the composition of the readership, or the system of payment for manuscripts)' **); **the material must be checkable**: the years in which the censorship regulations were revised, the circulation of periodicals, the distribution of topics among critical articles, and the number of pamphlets of political commentary in the same period (**Marking (**This chapter marks not by the conclusion but by the design: **you must write down one piece of evidence that would put you in difficulty (Lesson 3, the ranking by refutability; Lesson 1, the strength of evidence: **an explanation that does not intend to lose does not deserve to win**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The Russia we read is shorter than Russia {#def-jian}
**Received account** (**Today most foreign readers meet the nineteenth-century Russian novel through translations; the early translations often had cuts, and what was cut was often the historical argument and the lines of secondary characters (**This lesson blames no translator (**It only lists three losses that follow of necessity (**① **Cut the argument, and you have cut this chapter's 'forum'** (**In the original a novel is half a work of political commentary; **the translation turns it into a story purely of love and of fate (**This is the commonest 'refitting' in the circulation of world literature: **in order to fit the forum-structure of the importing country, the political part of the exporting country's work is dismantled (**② **Length is an institution** (**The thickness of the long work comes from publication by instalments in periodicals and from the patience of subscription; **an abridgement cancels this experience of time, and with it cancels the social function (**The version, for translation, of Chapter 6's 'first ask by what unit it is sold' (**③ **The travel of terms** (**A few Russian words acquired a wider extension in the target language, and by the time they returned to Russian they had already changed shape (**This lesson's conclusion: **to read a translation of a foreign novel is to read an import list; **on the list there are cuts and alterations, and there is also a markup (**Chapter 11 will turn this sentence into the summary of the whole course**.
:::

::: history Sixty years and one expulsion {#sec-liushi}
**1825** (**An unsuccessful mutiny, and a group of nobles exiled or put to death; a poet, and his sympathisers who remained in the capital (**This lesson's reading: the first modern division of labour between literature and politics is completed here — **some went to Siberia, and some wrote poems (**② **1830s–1850s** (**The novel in verse, satirical comedy, and the naming of the 'Natural School' (**The type-noun was coined by an opponent in the polemic, and the party under criticism afterwards accepted it as a programme (**This is the first demonstration, on the spot, of the travel of a word's meaning (**③ **1847/1855–1860s** (**An open letter of polemic circulated outside the censorship; a critic died (**Received account: **the twenty years after were the golden age of the 'thick journal' (**The place of criticism was before the novel, after the play, and inside every topic (**④ **1860s** (**One novel produced a political noun; a novel written in prison became an operating manual for several generations of people who took action, and sixty years later another revolutionary paid tribute with the same title (**Received account (**This lesson's judgement: **this is the most extravagant 'textual consequence' in the history of world literature — **a novel acquired the surname of a political party (**⑤ **1870s–1880s** (**The years of maturity of two great works, a controversy in the form of a trial over whether a novelist ought to write about sex, and a reaction after a regicide (**Received account: the writer's late works circulated at home in a restricted manner, and at the same time were printed abroad in great numbers (**And so this chapter's irony reaches its peak: **a writer was struck off the rolls by his own church, and the notice of the striking-off did his salesmanship for him across the whole of Europe (**The verdict as an advertisement, the strongest variant of Chapter 1's 'literature comes to set the seal' (**⑥ **1901/1905/1917** (**Excommunication, one failed revolution, and the beginning of exile (**Received account: **in the twentieth century 'Russian literature', as a discipline of world literature, had half its materials in Paris, Berlin and New York (**This sentence will be read, in Chapter 18, side by side with Chinese-language literature abroad.

::: exercise Short essay: the number of forums determines the syntax {level=3}
Write between six hundred and a thousand words, assessing "**A country has as many kinds of literary form as it has places where one can debate in public; a society with only one such place will stretch that place until it deforms, until it is at once court, theatre, parliament and church. Therefore the long arguments, the idea-characters and the endings of sudden silence in the Russian novel are not an emanation of national temperament, but a proof that forums are scarce; conversely, a society with too many forums produces professional forms that cannot understand one another, and calls that failure to understand a division of labour**". You must call upon at least these six: **the thick journal**, **censorship and oblique writing**, **the superfluous man as a seat**, **the little man's quota**, **the idea-character and the after-the-fact naming of polyphony**, **translation and exile**; and you must adjudicate: **whether the proposition that 'the number of forums determines literary form' is itself a kind of environmental determinism (you must design one counter-proof for it, and state, if the counter-proof holds, what variable could take its place**.

::: solution
Points for the marking: the six mechanisms (**periodical, oblique writing, seat, quota, naming, translation**); the adjudication (**an acceptable answer must grant that this is a weak determinism: **it claims only that the number of forums is one of the necessary conditions of literary form, and it must give a concrete form for the counter-proof — **for example, two opposite literary forms appearing under the same forum-structure (in the same decade there is both oblique writing and direct statement**), or the number of forums changing while the literary form does not (**a year and a periodical must be specified**); acceptable candidates for a substitute variable: payment for manuscripts and the subscription system, whether or not a translation market exists, the intensity with which censorship is carried out rather than the text of the regulations, and the composition by social origin of the body of writers (**the marking looks at whether 'censorship' is split into two layers, the text of the regulations and the carrying-out of them** — this is the technical high point of the chapter, because the Russian text of the regulations was sometimes tight and sometimes loose, while the carrying-out was always wilful, and oblique writing is precisely an adaptation to 'wilful carrying-out' and not to 'harsh regulations'**); if the conclusion can be written "**so what this chapter teaches is to translate literary form into a list of places: do not ask how a nation thinks; ask where they can speak, what happens if they once speak wrong, and whether there is a second road to take**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed by forum: the periodical (**one forum performs four functions**), censorship (**the text of the regulations can change, and the carrying-out is wilful; oblique writing is the adaptation to that wilfulness**), the seat (**a type-noun is a post, not a temperament**), the quota (**being seen comes before being met with sympathy**), the claim (**the cheapest shelter is to hide it on the person of a character**), the translation (**dismantling the political part of the importing country**). The method in one sentence: **when you read a famous work, first count how many places there were, in its time, where a meeting could be held**.
