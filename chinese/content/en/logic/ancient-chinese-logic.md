title: "Logic in ancient China"
---

The last chapter, as the course closes, returns to the home ground — **the Warring States already had a complete study of disputation: its terms were called "名、辞、说、故" ('names, phrases, explanations, grounds'); its textbook was called the *Mohist Canons* (*Mòjīng* 墨经); its teaching staff argued their way to the white horse, hard and white, and "the flying arrow does not move". This chapter neither boasts "we had it long ago" nor laughs that "it never became a science": it only reads the material as a Chinese original of logic — and, along the way, answers the question that has still not been handed in: a spark this creditable, why did it go out?**

## Definition: names, phrases, explanations and grounds {#def-siceng}

::: definition The four-layer architecture {#def-jiagou}
**The *Xiaoqu* 小取 ('Lesser Selection'): "以名举实，以辞抒意，以说出故" ('By names one raises the object; by phrases one expresses the thought; by explanations one brings out the ground.') (Our translation.)** — **the contents page of a logic is this one line: a name = a word (raising the object: lifting the object out of the world); a phrase = a proposition (expressing the thought: setting the judgement on the scales of a truth-value, Chapter 3); an explanation = an argument (bringing out the ground: entering the relation of support in the ledger, Chapter 1); a ground = the reason itself (the 'Upper Canon', *Jing shang* 经上: "**故，所得而后成也**" ('A ground is what one obtains, and only then is the thing accomplished.') (Our translation.) — once one has it, the matter holds: the Mohist "ground" is precisely the post of the "premise" in logic, and it is further divided into the "greater ground" (when the conditions are complete, it must occur) and the "lesser ground" (given it, not necessarily; without it, necessarily not) — the former is like a sufficient condition, the latter like a necessary condition: these two definitions in plain words are almost of the same age as the analysis of "necessity" in the *Prior Analytics* (the genealogy in Chapter 4 recognises kin here: two households, with the Aegean Sea and the Huai River between them, were copying the same ledger of inference)**).
:::

::: definition Four patterns and one slide {#def-sishi}
**The four weapons that the *Xiaoqu* issues to disputation**: ***pì* 辟** ('illustration') ("**举他物而以明之也**" ('Raise some other thing, and by that make it clear.') (Our translation.): explaining by an example), ***móu* 侔** ('parallel') ("**比辞而俱行也**" ('Set the phrases side by side and let them proceed together.') (Our translation.): changing the sentence pattern in parallel — "白马，马也；乘白马，乘马也" ('A white horse is a horse; riding a white horse is riding a horse.') (Our translation.) — **the same object slides on the two sides of the word "是" ('is'): white-plus-horse is a horse; is riding white-plus-horse "riding a horse"? The fineness of inference in the *mou* pattern, and its danger, are of one and the same origin**), ***yuán* 援** ('citing') ("**子然，我奚独不可以然也**" ('You take it to be so; how may I alone not be permitted to take it to be so?') (Our translation.) — **if you may, so may I: the legal edition of analogy, the founding master of the art of demarcation in Chapter 6**), ***tuī* 推** ('inferring') ("**以其所不取之同于其所取者，予之也**" ('Take what he does not accept, which is the same as what he does accept, and set it before him.') (Our translation.) — **set before you the item of the same kind that you are unwilling to claim: the engine shared by induction and by reduction to absurdity**). **The disclaimer on the same page (the puzzlement, in the *Xiaoqu*, over "此物之所然而不然" ('that by which this thing is so, and yet is not so') — the received account calls it "the obscuring of *mou*")** — the Mohists were highly alert to the slide in "也是" ('is also'), and the received illustration of the line of defence they set for themselves is: "**爱人，待周爱人而后为爱人；乘马不待乘所有马而后为乘马**" ('Loving people counts as loving people only when one has gone on to love people universally; riding a horse does not wait upon riding every horse before it counts as riding a horse.') (Our translation.) ———— the same word "是", the same verb, and the conditions of quantification entirely different: **"loving people" requires universality; "riding a horse" does not require riding every one: this is the earliest note, in the world, of a "lexical logic" — a quantificational registration of the semantics of verbs** (the inventory of Chapter 2 and the square of Chapter 3 flow together here).
:::

## Gongsun Long 公孙龙: three thought experiments {#def-gongsun}

::: definition The white horse, pointing and things, hard and white {#def-baima}
**"白马非马" ('A white horse is not a horse.')** (not mad talk, but the opening of the problem of universals: "马者，所以命形也；白者，所以命色也。命色者非命形也，故曰：白马非马" ('A horse is that by which one names a shape; white is that by which one names a colour. What names a colour is not what names a shape. Therefore it is said: a white horse is not a horse.') (Our translation.) — the rendering on the received account: "horse" indicates the class of shape, "white" indicates the class of colour, the colour-class is not equal to the shape-class, and "white horse" is a new name in which the two classes overlap — the kernel, in it, that is a true proposition: the extension of the class-name (horse) and the extension of the species-name (white horse) differ, and the word "是" is ambiguous (belongs to / is identical with): 'a white horse is a horse' says 'belongs to', while 'a white horse is not a horse' bites on 'is identical with' (the extreme Warring States edition of Chapter 2's "agreeing on this word is not agreeing on the same thing": push the ambiguity until it dazzles, and force the whole room to look again at the word "是"). 'On Pointing and Things' (*Zhiwu lun* 指物论) — "物莫非指，而指非指" ('Of things, none is not a pointing; and a pointing is not a pointing.') (Our translation.) (there is no object that does not enter language by way of "pointing it out", and the pointing-out itself is not the object pointed at: a theory of the relation between sign and thing, a birth certificate from two thousand years ago; people of the Jin talked of pointing and things as they talked of *xuánxué* 玄学 ('mystery learning'), whereas to read it today is an introduction to semiotics (the meta-level at which the triangle of Chapter 2 is synchronised: the "pointing" of word—mind—thing is precisely that broken line)). 'Hard and White' (*Jianbai* 坚白) — the gist on the received account: "坚、白、石不相离" ('Hard, white and stone do not part from one another.') (Our translation.), while another party chants "离也者，天下故皆离" ('As for separation: the world, on that account, is in every case separate.') (Our translation.) — the classic opening of the analysis of attributes: "hard" depends on touch, "white" on sight: each of the two kinds of perception gets one of them — so where is "the stone itself"? Outside the bundle of sensations, is there a "one"? (Hume's bundle theory of perception, and Kant's thing in itself, both have drafts on the bamboo slips of the Warring States — the ultimate version of the further question in Chapter 3, "the circumstances of being true").
:::

::: definition The method of the three tests: the earliest rules of evidence {#def-sanbiao}
**The "three tests" of 'Against Fate' (*Feiming* 非命)** — **three procedures for testing what is said: rooting it ("考之" ('examine it') against the deeds of the ancient sage-kings — historical precedent); sourcing it ("察百姓耳目之实" ('Inspect the reality before the ears and eyes of the common people.') (Our translation.) — witness by experience); using it ("发以为刑政，观其中国家百姓人民之利" ('Set it forth as penal administration, and observe wherein lies the benefit to the state, the common people and the populace.') (Our translation.) — a test by actual effect)**. **How a logic course reads it** (**this is the earliest Chinese version of turning "on what grounds do you say so?" into a procedure: the three each take charge of one kind of evidence (canonical authority, direct witness, efficacy — the pre-Qin branch of the four questions about sources in Chapter 8; its shortcoming is equally clear: the first places authority above experience, and is precisely the entrance, in later ages, to "taking a doubtful transmission as something believed" — the method of the three tests ought itself to be put through the three tests: the first, "the deeds of the sage-kings", was already being used as a ticket at a time when whether the sage-kings existed still awaited investigation (Chapter 2, "the stipulated inventory": tradition is goods that someone else has already stocked, and you put them straight on the shelf**)).
:::

## Rectifying names, knowing words, and equalising things: three faces for three arenas {#def-sanchang}

::: definition Institution, symptom, adjudication {#def-zhengming}
**'Rectifying Names' in Xunzi 荀子 = the institutional face** ("**制名以指实**" ('Institute names in order to point to the objects.') (Our translation.) — **the king institutes names: the right to name is taken back into official hands (the political edition of the "stipulative definition" in Chapter 2); an audit classification of the "three confusions" (the received account: 惑于用名以乱实 ('confused by using names to disorder objects'), 惑于用实以乱名 ('confused by using objects to disorder names'), 惑于用情欲以乱是非 ('confused by using passions and desires to disorder right and wrong') — rewriting the facts with the meanings of words, rewriting the meanings of words with particular cases, passing off what one likes as right and wrong: three kinds of "disorder" that, to this day, are still the standard symptoms on a certificate for appraising rumours**); **Confucius's "必也正名" ('what is necessary is to rectify names') = the face of a stance** — **名不正则言不顺 ('If names are not correct, then speech does not follow.') (Our translation.): the problem of definition is taken directly as a political problem (the opening address, on commencing business, of the "resetting of the inventory" in Chapter 2**); **Mencius's "knowing words" = the face of symptoms** — "**诐辞知其所蔽，淫辞知其所陷，邪辞知其所离，遁辞知其所穷**" ('Of one-sided phrases, know what obscures them; of excessive phrases, know where they have sunk; of deviant phrases, know where they have departed; of evasive phrases, know where they have come to a dead end.') (Our translation.) (**of biased speech, know what has covered it; of unrestrained speech, know how deep it has sunk; of distorted speech, know where it has turned away; of dodging speech, know where its reasons have run out: the first "symptomatology of fallacies" in history — it lists no charges, and only reads the signs of illness: the Chinese-medicine edition of the roster in Chapter 9 (the looking, the listening, the asking and the pulse-taking of critical thinking — what it diagnoses is the complexion of "speech": evasive phrases are the physiological signal that 'it will not hold up' — earlier than any catalogue of fallacies, and less teachable than any catalogue: the intuition is seasoned, the procedure is absent — and this is precisely the omen of the two-thousand-year fate of *míngbiàn* 名辩, 'the study of names and disputation'**)).
:::

::: definition Zhuangzi's problem of the adjudicator {#def-qiwu}
**'On Equalising Things' (*Qiwu lun* 齐物论), in Zhuangzi 庄子** — "**既使我与若辩矣，若胜我，我不若胜，若果是也，我果非也邪？我胜若，若不吾胜，我果是也，而果非也邪？其或是也，其或非也邪？其俱是也，其俱非也邪？我与若不能相知也，则人固受其暗。吾谁使正之？使同乎若者正之，既与若同矣，恶能正之！使同乎我者正之，既同乎我矣，恶能正之！使异乎我与若者正之，既异乎我与若矣，恶能正之！使同乎我与若者正之，既同乎我与若矣，恶能正之！然则我与若与人俱不能相知也，而待彼也邪？**" ('Suppose that I have disputed with you. You defeat me, and I do not defeat you: are you in fact right, and am I in fact wrong? I defeat you, and you do not defeat me: am I in fact right, and is the other in fact wrong? Is one of us right, and one of us wrong? Are we both right, or both wrong? If I and you cannot know it of each other, then another person is certainly left with its darkness. Whom shall I have set it right? If I have someone who agrees with you set it right, then, already agreeing with you, how can he set it right? If I have someone who agrees with me set it right, then, already agreeing with me, how can he set it right? If I have someone who differs from me and from you set it right, then, already differing from me and from you, how can he set it right? If I have someone who agrees with both me and you set it right, then, already agreeing with both me and you, how can he set it right? This being so, I and you and the other person all cannot know it of one another — and are we to wait for that one?') (Our translation.) — **the earliest "infinite regress of the adjudicator" in the history of logic: the one who wins is not the one who is right (winning is not being right, the meta-level edition of "quarrelling eliminates evidence" in Lesson 1); all four routes of adjudication are rejected (one who agrees with you, one who agrees with me, one who differs from both parties, one who agrees with both parties — every route has already presupposed a position: a joint hearing, two thousand years earlier, of "the power of final review of the three parties" in Lesson 15)**. **It is not sophistry; it is a sceptical argument** — the Mohist reply is hidden in the character "*dàng* 当" ('fitting') in the *Canons* and the *Xiaoqu* (the Mohist reply, within the broad lines of the received account of the *Canons* and *Explanations* line: "谓辩无胜，必不当" ('To say that disputation has no victory is necessarily not fitting.') (Our translation.) — the very sentence "debate has no such thing as winning or losing" is itself a debate that must be lost; "或谓之是，或谓之非，当者胜也" ('Of them, one calls it right and one calls it wrong; the one that fits is the winner.') (Our translation.) — between the two alternatives there must be one that "does not fit": right and wrong have an objective adjudication ("fitting"); it is only that human disputation need not reach it — this line of divergence from Zhuangzi is the encounter, in the history of Chinese philosophy, that comes closest to "the correspondence theory of truth against the coherence theory").
:::

::: example Two boys debate the sun: the closing speech {#ex-liangxiaoer}
A page transmitted as from the *Liezi* 列子, 'Tang's Questions' (*Tang wen* 汤问): the first boy takes as his evidence **日初出沧沧凉凉、及其日中如探汤** ('when the sun first comes out it is chill and cool, and when it reaches noon it is like putting a hand into hot water'), "**日初出远，而日中时近也**" ('when the sun first comes out it is far, and at noon it is near') (Our translation.) (the sense of the sentence restated after the current text); the second boy takes as his evidence **日初出大如车盖、及日中则如盘盂** ('when the sun first comes out it is as large as a carriage canopy, and at noon it is like a plate or a bowl'), "**日初出近，而日中时远也**" ('when the sun first comes out it is near, and at noon it is far') (Our translation.). Confucius could not decide. **The task: (1) write the two boys' arguments in standard form; (2) at which step the problem of each lies; (3) why, in the context of the study of names and disputation, "Confucius could not decide" may be a virtue rather than a joke**.
::: solution
(1) **The first boy**: P1 if near then hot, if far then cool (a regularity of felt heat); P2 far at dawn and near at noon (the phenomenon: cool at dawn, hot at noon); ∴ far at dawn and near at noon. **The second boy**: P1 if near then large, if far then small (a regularity of vision); P2 large at dawn and small at noon; ∴ near at dawn and far at noon — **each of the two is one legitimate MP (Chapter 4! the structures are both valid)**; (2) **the lesion lies entirely in the first premise: both "regularities" hold only inside their own sensory channel (the sense of heat, vision), and neither has excluded intervening variables (the atmosphere, contrast with the background — visual illusion and intensity of radiation are two sets of physics: data of sensation cannot directly exclude one another across channels (nobody has signed the "no-confounding policy" of Chapter 7: the two parties each take a single instrument to measure a system of two variables**); (3) **the logical virtue of Confucius's being unable to decide** (where there is no authority to adjudicate, one does not hand down a judgement — in the shop-talk of the study of names and disputation, this is "not daring to set up a 'ground' at random": each of the two boys is valid on his own, and uncertain on his own: "valid" and "sound" are kept on separate accounts (the two offices of Chapter 1), and the court opened for this case two thousand years ago; the joke version of 'Two Boys Debate the Sun' mocks the idea that "even a sage has one thing he does not know" — the judgement of this lesson: knowing the boundary of "not knowing" is precisely the starting point of logic as a whole (Socrates' knowledge of his own ignorance shakes hands with Confucius here: one legislates by question and answer, the other sets the example by not answering — the founding acts of East and West have the same structure (the edition, in the history of the sages, of "both sides may be wrong" in Lesson 1).
:::
:::

::: intuition Why the study of names and disputation went out {#int-xile}
**The "three losses" on the received account** (**(1) no professional market: disputation was not how officials were selected (the examinations selected people by prose and rhapsody; "question—answer—refutation" had no place on the establishment — the law courts and the public square of ancient Greece needed advocates and sophist teachers, and after the Warring States this kind of profession did not grow up (Lesson 9's "the format of distribution decides life and death", applied to the discipline of logic itself: if a genre has no professional distribution, it has only genius, and no tradition**); **(2) commentaries and subcommentaries took the place of argument: the hermeneutics of the classics was too mature, and the time of thought was spent on "transmitting" rather than on "discriminating" (the other face of "the boundary of interpretation" in Lesson 15: explanation overwhelms proof**); **(3) the debate on words and meaning turned about: language was treated as something to be gone beyond (the received account's adaptation of "得意而忘言" ('get the meaning and forget the words') (Our translation.)), and not as something to be examined — the one-way lift of Lesson 15 starts work here: *xuanxue* took over Zhuangzi, and the switch of the study of names and disputation was pulled down (scepticism can be revered, and it can also be used to shut the door**)). **A statement of measure** (**the three losses are an explanatory hypothesis, not a verdict: the survival of famous pieces depends on multiple contingencies of text, institution and lines of scholarly affiliation — "not going out" takes however many conditions, while "going out" needs only some; beware, besides, of three states of mind: the forced parallel of "the Six Classics are a commentary on me" (the sticker-game of "the Mohists = ancient Greek logic + pragmatism"), the moralising of "a tradition cut off in the middle is a shame" (logic is a method, not a national report card), and reading "disputation has no victory" as "there is good reason to blur the difference" (what Zhuangzi is speaking of is the problem of the adjudicator, not "stop quarrelling": this is the sentence in the lesson that least ought to be misread**).
:::

::: history Two thousand years of the study of names and disputation: the power connected twice {#sec-liangdu}
**4th–3rd centuries BC**, the Hundred Schools period of the School of Names and of Mohist disputation (**the Jixia scene of Gongsun Long, "困于齐楚" ('hard pressed in Qi and Chu'); the later Mohists wrote the *Canons* and the *Explanations of the Canons* and joined the fight (the "因" ('reason') of *yīnmíng* 因明 entering China, in Chapter 5, had long since sprouted on native ground**); **3rd–2nd centuries BC**, a period of silence (**'Rectifying Names' in the *Xunzi* and 'Difficulties of Positional Power' (*Nan shi* 难势) in the *Han Feizi* 韩非子 took "disputation" over into the arts of ruling; Sima Tan 司马谈: "名家使人俭而善失真" ('The School of Names makes people constrained, and apt to lose what is true.') (Our translation.) (the received account of 'On the Essentials of the Six Schools', *Lun liujia yaozhi* 论六家要旨 — the verdict of the official historian has outlived the debates of the schools: the library defeated the public square**); **3rd–7th centuries**, *qīngtán* 清谈 ('pure conversation') and *yinming* side by side (**those who talked of "pointing and things" and of "words and meaning" went on adding notes; Buddhist *yinming* (the three members, and the three characteristics of a reason, of Chenna 陈那 and Facheng 法称) was translated in by Xuanzang 玄奘 — a logic from outside came onto the field a second time (the scene, in Chapter 5, of "the example taken onto the establishment"**); **the 1890s–1930s**, the first connecting of the power (**the putting in order of Mohist learning along the line of Kang Youwei 康有为, Liang Qichao 梁启超, Zhang Taiyan 章太炎 and Sun Yirang 孙诒让, plus Hu Shi (Hu Shih) 胡适, *Xianqin mingxue shi* 先秦名学史 ('A History of the Pre-Qin Study of Names'; English original, 1919), rewriting the history of philosophy as an "ancient study of names", plus the relay of collation along the path of Liang Qichao's *Mojing jiaoshi* 墨经校释 ('Collation and Explanation of the Mohist Canons') — "the art of bringing Mohist disputation back to life: use this new key, logic, to open a lock from two thousand years ago; how many wards of the lock have opened (of what fineness is the paired rendering of "故／理／辨" with premise／logic／dialectic), and the whole of the twentieth century has been doing the work again**); **1937 to the present**, the second connecting of the power (Jin Yuelin's 金岳霖 *Philosophy of Logic* (*Luoji zhexue* 逻辑哲学) places the study of names and disputation on the map of comparative logic (the bibliography of Chapter 1 is met with here); Shen Youding's 沈有鼎 symbolic rereading of the texts of Mohist disputation and of the School of Names (the line of the received account of *The Logic of Mohist Disputation*, *Mobian de luojixue* 墨辩的逻辑学, in the 1940s — reading "pointing" and "horse" as a notation of classes and members); bamboo slips and silk manuscripts unearthed in the present age, and newly arranged editions, keep rewriting the timeline: "the study of names and disputation is a living philology, not a museum".
:::

::: warning Four pits in the study of names and disputation
**The forced parallel of "the Six Classics are a commentary on me"** (**"Newtonian mechanics / the syllogism / set theory are there in the Mohist Canons" — in every such sentence one can find two or three characters that look alike, and none of them survives a close reading of the text: a forced parallel is the cheapest nationalism, and also the least polite way of elevating the ancients (lifting the ancients into a seat where one need not understand them**); **worship of vocabulary** (**"故 = premise, 理 = logic" — pairing terms across languages is surgery, not a tattoo (a return visit to the history of terms in Chapter 2, in which "logic" defeated "the study of names": to borrow a word is to transplant, and a transplant meets rejection**); **shame at a tradition cut off** (**treating the history of learning as a medal table: in many civilisations logic has gone on and off — Greek logic also slept for several centuries in the Latin West, and when it woke what it saw was Arabic translations — "the relay" is the normal condition of humankind, and "running alone" is the myth**); **reading Zhuangzi as blurring the difference** (**"disputation has no victory" gets cited as "whatever the quarrel, all of it is right" — what the original text asks, bitterly, is the qualification to adjudicate: what he takes apart is the vending machine of "winning = being right", not advice that you shut your mouth (the commonest version of a misreading of Zhuangzi, and of exactly the same model as the thing he opposes**).
:::

::: exercise A check-up in the *mou* pattern {level=1}
Give five sentences of "比辞而俱行" ('setting phrases side by side and letting them proceed together') a check-up in the *mou* pattern (what extension stands on each side of "是" ('is')? has it slid, or not?): (1) "白马，马也；乘白马，乘马也。" (2) "舟，物也；乘舟，乘物也。" (3) "盗，人也；杀盗，杀人也。" (4) "书，物也；读书，读物也。" (5) "诗，文也；读诗，读文也。"
::: solution
Marking: **check, sentence by sentence, the "quantificational conditions" of the verb** ((1) the Mohists permit it (riding does not require riding every one — "乘马不待乘所有马" ('riding a horse does not wait upon riding every horse')); (2) permitted, but comic ("乘物" ('riding a thing') sounds absurd because "物" ('thing') requires a higher class? In fact the range of the object of "乘" ('ride') has been widened — the syntax is licit, and one's feel for the language raises an alarm: **an alarm is not the same as a breach of the law**); (3) a high-incidence stretch of the obscuring of *mou* (the transmitted proposition of the School of Names, "杀盗非杀人" ('Killing a robber is not killing a person.') (Our translation.), is this very pattern: the received account has the Mohists argue over what "盗" ('robber') belongs to — when one says "杀盗" ('killing a robber'), "盗" is a restriction of status, and is not the same as denying that he is a "人" ('person'): "是" changes gear between "belongs to" and "is identical with", and ethics and logic part onto separate tracks here — **an answer need only point out: the extension is the same, the normative force is different: a legal subject and a rhetorical subject are not the same thing**); (4) and (5) **"读" ('read') requires an intentional object (reading the *Book of Songs* is not reading the paper): the meanings of the words are rearranged with the verb — the verb is the gavel of the sentence (a return visit to Lesson 1: the verb is the gavel)**).
:::

From the Mohist standpoint (the gist, on the received account, of "辩也者，有不可胜者也；两也者，有不当者也" ('As for disputation, there are cases that cannot be won; as for a pair, there are cases that do not fit.') (Our translation.)), write **a reply of two hundred characters**: what to do when one grants that knowledge is limited, and an opposition to letting "no victory" slide into "no right"; and state clearly what the criterion of the character "**当**" ('fitting') is inside the brief (fallible? testable? who is to do the fitting?).
::: solution
Marking: **grant the problem of the adjudicator, and refuse a nihilism of verdicts** (**once "winning" and "being right" are on separate accounts, the way out is to replace the adjudicator with a procedure: hearing both sides, the three tests, and watching the effect (the Mohist answer: the adjudication does not lie in the person, but in a test of the after-effect, "the benefit to the state, the common people and the populace" — the pre-Qin prototype of the table of efficacy in Chapter 6 and of traceability in Chapter 8**); the criterion of "fitting" (**marking looks for honesty: "corresponding to the facts" requires that one be able to state the circumstances of being true (Chapter 3); "coherence" requires an external anchor (the warning about immunisation in Chapter 9); "actual effect" has the problems of lag and of attribution — each of the three has its own account, and whichever one chooses, one must write down the conditions under which it fails**).

::: exercise A short essay: the question that has not been handed in {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim: "**the going-out of the study of names and disputation was not an accident of intellect but an accident of profession: a civilisation has the learning that matches the kind of person it pays — ancient Greece paid advocates and commentators on the classics, and the Song paid candidates for the eight-legged essay; in whichever dynasty logic draws a wage, in that language it tells the truth**". One must call on at least six items: **the architecture of names, phrases, explanations and grounds**, ***mou* and "the obscuring of *mou*"**, **the white horse, or pointing and things (choose one to read in depth)**, **the method of the three tests, and the lesion in its first article**, **Zhuangzi's problem of the adjudicator**, **the "three losses" on the received account**; and adjudicate: **"if the tradition of Mohist disputation had not been broken off, would China have invented modern logic on its own?" — write a responsible counterfactual history (which three conditions must hold at the same time; if any one is missing, the going-out is played out again)**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**architecture = the contents page; *mou* = quantification over words; the white horse = the ambiguity of the word "是"; the three tests = a procedure of evidence, plus the illness at the entrance in the sage-king article; adjudication = "winning" / "being right" on separate accounts; the three losses = the market, commentaries and subcommentaries, and a theory of transcendence**); the counterfactual adjudication (**three conditions, for reference: (1) a professional market (any institution, among law courts, deliberation on policy, and religious adjudication, that needs professional advocates); (2) an institutionalised genre of "question—answer—refutation" (a private-school system of set opponents in debate, a system of degree disputations, and the like); (3) a professional interest in language itself, rather than in the meaning beyond the words (a scholastic tradition of technique with texts). Lack (1) and there is no wage; lack (2) and there is no transmission; lack (3) and there is no question — the three losses of the study of names and disputation are the lack of all three (marking looks at who has argued "lack any one of the three, and the going-out is played out again" into the character of a system, rather than as three accidents set side by side; write the counterfactual as a logic exercise in conditionals, not as a nostalgic parallel universe**); if the conclusion can be written thus, "**the parting words at the close of this lesson: logic has been born twice in China — once, in the Warring States, it was not connected to the power; once, in 1905, the wires were joined from inside translations — and both times it was for the same thing: to give 'speaking by reasons' an objective standard, without looking to see whose seat is higher. The legacy of the study of names and disputation is not the passbook of 'we had it long ago'; it is the examination paper of 'why it went out' — this question has still not been handed in, and the one who hands it in is every ordinary person who speaks clearly and keeps the accounts distinct**", that is an A.

::: summary
The original, filed: the architecture (**names—phrases—explanations—grounds, plus the greater ground and the lesser ground, = the embryo of the sufficient and the necessary**), four patterns and one slide (**illustration, parallel, citing and inferring, plus the quantification over words in "loving people waits upon universality; riding a horse does not wait"**), Gongsun Long (**the white horse = the ambiguity of "是"; pointing and things = semiotics; hard and white = the analysis of attributes**), the three tests (**canonical authority, direct witness, efficacy: the sage-king article comes with a back door of its own**), the three arenas (**Xunzi's institution, Mencius's symptoms, Zhuangzi's problem of the adjudicator — the one who wins is not the one who is right**), the going-out (**the market, commentaries and subcommentaries, a theory of transcendence: the received account is a hypothesis, not a written verdict**). The method, in one sentence: **read it as an original; do not claim it as kin**. All ten lessons are done: from the smallest skeleton of an argument to the archaeological site of the study of names and disputation — a logic course closes with only one sentence: **your conclusion is never better qualified than the premises; and setting the premises up so that they are worthy of the conclusion is a lesson for a whole life**.
