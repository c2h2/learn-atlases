---
title: "Propositions"
---

The previous chapter had charge of the names of the accounts on the ledger; this chapter has charge of **the entries themselves** — which sentences can be booked as true or false, and as how true. **Logic's first set of scales is here: a proposition = judgement-content that can go on the scales**.

## Definition: qualification for the scales {#def-shangcheng}

::: definition Propositions, sentences and statements {#def-mingti}
**A proposition = the judgement-content of a sentence** (one and the same proposition has countless sentences to wear: “明天下雨” ('It will rain tomorrow') (Our translation.), “明日有雨” ('There will be rain tomorrow') (Our translation.), “雨将明日而至” ('Rain will arrive with the morrow') (Our translation.) — **synonymous sentences are the clothes of a proposition; the other way about: one sentence with many "skins" is ambiguity — “他谁都不认识” has two stocks (他不认识任何人 'he does not know anyone' (Our translation.) / 没人认识他 'nobody knows him' (Our translation.)): the proposition splits in two — the two parties to a dispute may each be wearing one outfit**). **Four kinds that do not go on the scales**: **questions** (“关门了吗？” 'Is the door shut?' (Our translation.)), **imperatives** (“把门关上！” 'Shut the door!' (Our translation.)), **exclamations** (“关得真好！” 'How well it is shut!' (Our translation.)) and **sentences of presupposition-collapse** (see section 5) — **“请把窗关上” 'Please shut the window' (Our translation.) is neither true nor false, yet it is a major client of the cosplay of argument: advertisements are all illegitimate children of the imperative (“买它！” 'Buy it!' (Our translation.) has no truth value, and therefore cannot be refuted either: that is precisely the design intent — words that cannot be false do not deserve to be true either: the "unfalsifiability" of an advertising slogan is its entire trading licence**). **Why there has to be such a thing as a truth value**: **a sentence with no true or false can take part in the staging of an argument (commands, threats, pleas), but cannot take part in the bookkeeping of inference (the ledger of Lesson 1) — deciding whether it "goes on the scales" is the first door that turns a problem of rhetoric into a problem of logic**.
:::

::: definition Truth conditions as the first principle of semantics {#def-zhizhi}
**“理解一个句子，就是知道它为真的情形” ('To understand a sentence is to know the circumstances in which it is true') (Our translation.) (the received gist of the *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*)** — **this remark turns "meaning" into a check one can carry out: if you can say what the world must be like for “天下雨” ('rain is falling') (Our translation.) to be true (some place, some time, water coming down), you count as understanding it; if you cannot say, you are only making a sound**. **From this comes a "check-up on disagreement"**: **before contending over "whether X is true", first synchronise "what each of us has set as the 'circumstances in which X is true'" — a great many public disputes have already parted company at this second step (“AI 有创造力” 'AI has creativity' (Our translation.): for one side, the circumstances of being true = the output surprises an insider; for the other, the circumstances of being true = the output proceeds from consciousness — each set of circumstances can be checked, and the quarrelling parties have often not checked at all**). **The near and the far of verification**: the circumstances of being true may be checked on the spot (“冰箱里有牛奶” 'there is milk in the fridge') (Our translation.), may take an instrument (“这 U 盘是 64G” 'this USB stick is 64G') (Our translation.), or in principle be such that no one can check them (“银河系外存在智慧文明” 'an intelligent civilisation exists outside the Milky Way') (Our translation.)** — whether a proposition is qualified is not settled by whether it can be checked on the spot, but **a proposition that for a long time no one can check ought to have its credit limit in an argument lowered automatically (laid in beforehand for the evidence lesson of Chapter 8)**.
:::

## The square: the opposition business of the four categorical propositions {#def-fangzhen}

::: definition The four relations of A, E, I and O {#def-duidang}
The four of standard form: **A, the universal affirmative (所有 S 都是 P 'all S are P') (Our translation.); E, the universal negative (所有 S 都不是 P 'no S are P') (Our translation.); I, the particular affirmative (有 S 是 P 'some S are P') (Our translation.); O, the particular negative (有 S 不是 P 'some S are not P') (Our translation.)**. **Four relations**: **contradiction** (A↔O, E↔I: **necessarily one true and one false: a mutual-destruction bout between the universal and the particular negative — if “都及格” 'all of them passed' (Our translation.) is false, it takes only one who did not pass**); **contrariety** (A↔E: they may be false together and may not be true together — “都爱他” 'everyone loves him' (Our translation.) and “都恨他” 'everyone hates him' (Our translation.) can both be rumours at once); **subcontrariety** (I↔O: they may be true together and may not be false together — “有人满意” 'some people are satisfied' (Our translation.) and “有人不满意” 'some people are not satisfied' (Our translation.) are often true together: **the two legs of an opinion poll: any “有人说……所以……” 'someone says…, therefore…' (Our translation.) is stealthily selling a subcontrary relation as a contradictory**); **subalternation** (A→I: if the universal is true, the particular is true; the converse inference is invalid — **“有的同学习惯熬夜” 'some students are in the habit of staying up late' (Our translation.) remains true even if not a single person is an exception — **"particular" = "at least one", and does not include "not all": ordinary language, hearing “有的” 'some' as “只有一些” 'only some', is the first lesson of the square**). **Reinforcement from the Chinese department**: **“不都是” ('not all are') ≠ “都不是” ('none are')** (“奖金不都是他拿的” 'the bonuses were not all taken by him' (Our translation.) = an O proposition; “都不” ('none') = an E proposition: a difference in the sentence passed, of a single character (“成绩不都是老师给的” 'the marks were not all given by the teacher' (Our translation.) concedes a part; “都不” turns hostile — the *gàngjīng* 杠精 ('compulsive quibbler') and the diplomat share the square, each taking one cell).
:::

::: definition Qualifiers: the bank account of an argument's strength {#def-xianding}
**"necessarily — usually — possibly — exactly once — barring a few"** — **qualifiers (the everyday variants of modal words and quantifiers) are the strength account of an argument: the Toulmin model gives them a line to themselves (a front-line preview in Chapter 1), because nine-tenths of what is right or wrong in everyday argument happens at the qualifier**: "**Smoking causes lung cancer" (the statistical sense) → "Smoking may cause lung cancer" (the individual sense, a conservative loan) → "Smoking makes a person necessarily contract lung cancer" (an overdraft beyond the limit)**; **two standard frauds**: **upgrade fraud** (reading "research finds that X is correlated with Y" as "X causes Y", and then as "X necessarily Y" — the three-stage rocket of popular-science reposts); **downgrade fraud** ("I am never late", punctured by a single accident — disguising "almost always" as "all", so as to be able to renege at any moment — **flexibility in the qualifier is left to the other party; rigidity is kept for one's own conclusion**). **The auditor's formula: find the qualifier in the conclusion sentence, then go back into the premises and see whether it has a deposit — if the premises have only opened "possibly", the conclusion cannot draw "necessarily" (Chapter 4: validity is a truth-preserving conveyor — an overdraft on a qualifier is guilty of the same offence as invalidity)**.
:::

::: example Empty subjects and fictional accounts: the king, Daiyu and the ex {#ex-kong}
Three hard cases for the scales: **① “法国现任国王是秃头” ('The present King of France is bald') (Our translation.)** (Russell's tiger of 1905; the original form is kept here); **② “林黛玉死于心碎” ('Lin Daiyu died of a broken heart') (Our translation.)**; **③ “我前任从不夸我” ('My ex never praised me') (Our translation.)** (the words of someone after a breakup). Please: decide whether each can be true, by whose stock it is to be judged, and give an **auditable rewrite**.
::: solution
① **Presupposition-collapse** (the sentence **asserts "there exists a present King of France" together with "he has, or has not, some property" — the first half is already false. Russell's proposal: the subject of the surface grammar is not in fact "the name of a thing", but the existential quantifier in disguise: "there exists a unique x such that x is King of France and…" — **it is false, not meaningless: this is the first operation in taming empty terms (“当今法国的国王” 'the King of France of the present day' (Our translation.) is false; whereas “当今法国的总统留胡子” 'the President of France of the present day wears a beard' (Our translation.) may be true or false: the grammar is close, the logic is differently built — Russell's value for teaching: making "a subject under which no thing can be found" show its true form)**); ② **fictional discourse** (**“林黛玉死于心碎” has nowhere to be booked on the ledger of reality (there is no such person) — the audit rewrite: “在《红楼梦》里，黛玉泪尽而逝” ('In *Dream of the Red Chamber*, Daiyu died when her tears were spent') (Our translation.): supply the sentence with an **index**, and the fictional proposition at once acquires an objective truth value ("in the book" is its adverbial of time and place: fiction is not talking nonsense; it is a change of ledger — "in …" has three legitimate accounts: the original work, *tóngrén* 同人 ('fan work'), and my imagination: the first two cells can be audited, the third can only be kept back for oneself — **the logical pathology of fan wars: the three parties each reconcile the books on a different account**)**); ③ **a universal over a private history** ("never" = an E proposition, plus a sample consisting only of "what I remember": **the qualifier-leakage disease of memory: the brain automatically edits "for the most part there were none" into "never" — the rewrite: “与我相处的全部年头里，我记得的夸奖次数为零（未记得的不计）” 'In all the years I was with them, the number of praises I remember is zero (those I do not remember do not count)' (Our translation.): one sentence, and the quarrel cannot get started (the degree of falsifiability rises, the power to wound falls: an honest formulation is often awkward to wield — the intimate-relationship edition of Chapter 2's “精确不是美德，分配诚实才是” 'Precision is not the virtue; apportioning honesty is') (Our translation.)**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The world does not sit in two cells, but the books use only two {#int-liangge}
**Two-valued logic is a budget** (two cells, true and false; **vague predicates (“高” 'tall', “富” 'rich', “堆” 'heap') and future contingents (“明晚会下雨吗” 'Will it rain tomorrow evening?') (Our translation.) each, in the twentieth century, reared many-valued logic, fuzzy logic and tense logic — they do not overthrow the two values; they open separate account headings for a world that is hard to manage: the main ledger is still two-valued, because an audit of everyday inference needs only that**). **The distance between judgement and belief**: **one and the same proposition can be believed, doubted, hoped, or commanded to be believed — a proposition is an atom of content; a propositional attitude (I believe that p / I am worried that p / a demand that p be ascertained) is the shell of an attitude: an audit of argument only pierces the shell to inspect the content inside; nine-tenths of everyday quarrels happen in the shell layer (“你竟然担心这个！” 'You would worry about that, of all things!') (Our translation.) — a war launched against the attitude, the content exempt from inspection the whole way through — the attitudinal version of Lesson 1's "attacking the person"**). **A sense of measure**: **the dichotomy a logic class draws on the world is thin: it says only "every sentence that can be booked must occupy one cell", and never says "the world has only two cells" — to read two-valued logic as a two-cell view of the world is to take the accounting software for a life**.
:::

::: history Several revisions of the table for the concept of truth {#sec-gaibiao}
**The fourth century BCE**: Aristotle, *On Interpretation* (**the definition of a proposition, and the dispute over the truth value of “明天将发生海战” 'a sea battle will take place tomorrow' (Our translation.): the first dossier of the future contingent — the first separating of accounts between determinism and logic (the received reading of chapter 9 of *On Interpretation*)**) + the Chinese learning of *xíngmíng* 刑名 ('punishments and names') and the Mohist *yǐ cí shū yì* 以辞抒意 ('to set forth one's intent by a *ci*') (*cí* 辞 = the layer of judgement: of the four layers *míng* 名, *cí* 辞, *shuō* 说 and *gù* 故 — names, judgements, explanations and grounds — this chapter has charge of the second); **the Middle Ages**: the correspondence theory of truth becomes canonical (**the scholastic settling of the correspondence theory (the sentence-form "truth is correspondence" passes into general currency) + the embryo of "reference / sense" (the theory of suppositio: which thing a word points to in a sentence — the medieval version of the stock problem in Chapter 2**); **1879**: Frege (**the concept-script: the content of a judgement (Gedanke) detaches from the grammatical shell — the modern birth certificate of this chapter's "a proposition ≠ a sentence"**); **1905**: Russell, 'On Denoting' (**surgery on empty terms: the theory of descriptions; in 1908 the theory of types, while it was about it, repaired the Liar — self-reference was fitted with a restraining strap (an echo, in Chapter 10, of *míngbiàn* 名辩, 'the disputation of names')**); **1921**: Wittgenstein, *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (**the received gist: a proposition is a picture of reality, and a compound proposition is a truth-function — the truth tables of the connectives are registered here (the key to the counting-house in Chapter 4)**); **the 1930s**: Tarski's definition of truth (**“雪是白的”为真，当且仅当雪是白的 — '“Snow is white” is true if and only if snow is white' (Our translation.) — the T-schema: the word "true", which the household has been using for three thousand years, is sent through the customs of formalisation: object language and metalanguage keep separate accounts (“这句话是假的” 'This sentence is false' (Our translation.) cannot have a case opened under the separate-accounts regime)**); **the 1960s to the present**: possible-world semantics (Kripke: "being true" is extended to "being true in some worlds" — modal logic acquires a sand-table; fuzzy logic enters household appliances (the "rather cool" setting on an air conditioner is Aristotle's square put to civilian use: a rare mass-production landing for logic)**).
:::

::: warning Four pits of the proposition
**"Where there is a subject, there is an object"** (**“圆的方” 'a round square', “现任国王” 'the present king', “孙悟空的金箍棒” 'Sun Wukong's golden-hooped staff' (Our translation.) — the grammar is complete, the stock is empty: Russell's scalpel is kept ready: the subject position may be a quantifier in disguise**); **hearing “有的” as “只有一些”** (**logical “有 S 是 P” 'some S are P' (Our translation.) = at least one, and does not exclude "all": a person who reports “部分项目达标” 'some of the projects met the standard' (Our translation.) may have had all of them meet it — do not add a qualifier on someone else's behalf, and do not take one off on your own**); **wars of attitude** (**“你怎么能这么想！” 'How can you think like that!' (Our translation.) — crossfire in the shell, the content exempt from inspection: dragging the quarrel back to the truth conditions of p is the only antidote**); **two-way fraud with qualifiers** (**upgrading (correlated → causes) and downgrading (almost always → never, the easier to renege): the audit checks only one thing — whether the conclusion's qualifier has a deposit in the premises**).
:::

::: exercise Rewriting news sentences {level=1}
Rewrite five vague sentences as **propositions that can be checked** (mark the truth conditions and the qualifier account): ① “专家认为该方案总体可行” ('Experts consider the scheme feasible on the whole') (Our translation.); ② “多数网友表示支持” ('Most netizens express support') (Our translation.); ③ “新产品显著提升睡眠质量” ('The new product markedly improves the quality of sleep') (Our translation.); ④ “相关措施有望近期落地” ('The measures in question have a prospect of being put into effect before long') (Our translation.); ⑤ “没有证据表明它无效” ('There is no evidence to show that it is ineffective') (Our translation.).
::: solution
A sample for marking: ① **"专家" ('experts') has no singular or plural, no field of qualification, no citation (who? how many? are they inside the relevant field?) — rewrite: “内分泌学会三位作者在 2024《…》中认为该方案在其假设下可行” 'Three authors of the Endocrine Society hold, in a 2024 《…》, that the scheme is feasible under its assumptions' (Our translation.): the qualifier “总体” ('on the whole') must be brought down to "applies to population X under conditions Y"**; ② **“网友” ('netizens') = a sample drawn from "people who will speak up": the truth condition is in fact "of M relevant posts on platform N, X% express support (time window T)" — a letter of advance notice for the sampling lesson in Chapter 7**; ③ **“显著” ('marked', 'significant') lacks a test (three columns: sample, control, effect size; an answer is required only to write "the number of minutes by which the average time taken to fall asleep fell, compared with the control group")**; ④ **“有望” ('there is a prospect') = a shell of disclaimer in which nobody answers for "not being put into effect" (downgrade fraud with a qualifier) — the rewrite must name the party who undertakes it, and the timetable**; ⑤ **“没有证据表明无效” ('there is no evidence to show that it is ineffective') ≠ “有证据表明有效” ('there is evidence to show that it is effective'): a cell of the burden of proof (an evidential vacuum for a negative proposition cannot be rewritten as an affirmative — the scene of a misuse of the subcontrary of O and I on the square: it excludes only "known to be ineffective", and does not exclude "unknown" or "harmful")**.
:::

::: exercise Reallocating the qualifier account {level=2}
For one real assertion (of your own choosing), write **five grades of strength of the same proposition** (necessarily — usually — in most cases — possibly — on one occasion), and mark **the evidence threshold of each grade**; then, in one sentence, point out **at which grade, in everyday discourse, people usually take their evidence, and at which grade they cite it**.
::: solution
Marking: **thresholds that rise monotonically and can be pointed to** ("on one occasion" needs only one witness; "necessarily" needs a mechanism that rules out counter-examples — the induction of Chapter 7 and the deduction of Chapter 4 shake hands here); **a diagnosis of the mismatch** (evidence is usually taken at "on one occasion", while citation is usually promoted to "they are all like that": **the standard exchange rate of folk argument: buy at the price of an anecdote, sell at the price of a law — what this lesson has to do is paste the table of rates on everyone's forehead**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: two-valued logic is a form of politeness {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**'What is not true is false' is not the world's resolution, but the bookkeeper's self-discipline: it promises that 'your objection is qualified to be entered as true or as false' — this is logic's greatest politeness towards dissent**". You must call on at least five things: **qualification for the scales (an imperative does not go on the scales)**, **the art of synchronising truth conditions**, **the opposition relations of the square (used at least twice)**, **the qualifier account**, **index surgery on fictional sentences**; and adjudicate: **whether "both true and false" (two-faced truth) can be entered in the books — is bringing in many-valued logic a liberation, or a fleeing of the accounts?**
::: solution
Points for marking: the five mechanisms (**qualification = the precondition of an objection's being auditable; synchronising = a check-up on the disagreement; the square = the difference in how far the firearms of contrariety and of contradiction will reach; qualifiers = double-entry bookkeeping of strength; the index = changing ledgers rather than not keeping books**); the adjudication of two-faced truth (**two lines to consult: the fleeing-the-accounts view — if "both true and false" cancels the principle of explosion (from a contradiction anything at all can be derived), the sentence loses every inferential force = an account with a zero deposit: it cannot take part in any argument, and only ornaments a posture; the liberation view — theories of truth-value gaps (of the Kleene kind) deal with a third state, "the evidence is not yet settled", not with "true and false": the naming has to be honest — the third cell is a new account heading for "undetermined", not permission to cook the books**); if the conclusion is written "**the two values of logic are not an arrogant verdict, but a commitment one can be held to: every sentence you utter dares to claim a cell — the impolite one is the person who refuses to fill in the form: and to fill in a cell and then have it crossed out is precisely what the whole of science means**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed on the checklist for the scales: qualification (**only what can be fixed as true or false enters the ledger; imperatives and commands only set the scene, and do not keep the books**), synchronising (**if you cannot state "the circumstances of being true", you are only making a sound**), the square (**contradictories kill one another, contraries may be false together, subcontraries are often true together, subalternation travels only one way; “不都” ≠ “都不”**), qualifiers (**the bank account of strength: the conclusion's allowance must have been deposited in the premises**), empty terms (**the king is made to show himself, Daiyu is supplied with an index, the ex edits memory: the scalpel is kept ready**). The method in one sentence: **first ask for this sentence's truth conditions, then ask for the balance on its qualifier**. Next chapter, onto the machine: deduction — the transport science of the passage from true to necessarily true.
:::
