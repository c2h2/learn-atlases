---
title: "Induction and analogy"
---

Deduction is in charge of "what must follow"; this chapter is in charge of "what is likely" — **almost all of humanity's new knowledge, the whole of experience, and the decision that you will eat rice again tomorrow, are signed on these two kinds of reasoning. What they have in common: the conclusion always goes one step further than the premises; that step is called risk**.

## Definition: two kinds of induction, one loan {#def-liangzhong}

::: definition Complete induction and simple enumeration {#def-juhe}
**Complete induction (enumeration)** (**examining every object: "all 42 people in the class have been checked one by one, and all passed" — the premises are exhaustive, and the conclusion has in fact never left the house: this is a conjunction wearing the skin of induction (Chapter 3's ledger: all its work is to put 42 facts into one pocket — safe, and of zero added value**); **simple enumeration** (**examining a part and asserting the whole: "every swan I have seen is white" — Mill's legislation for it is a single sentence: if no counter-example has been met, pay out the loan — the reliability of this rule is limited by the history of encounters (the loan contract: the principal is experience, the interest is luck, and the default clause is only four characters, "下一只天鹅" ('the next swan') — a black swan waited a thousand years in Australia for the Europeans: a species lost face to a single voyage**)). **Why there must be induction**: **deduction never leaves the house; induction runs the errands — "the sun rises" holds in no logic; it holds across 20 billion sunrises: this chapter does not teach you how to avoid risk (it cannot be avoided); it teaches you how to price risk**.
:::

::: definition Hume's return visit: can induction defend itself? {#def-xiumen}
**The circle in the standard defence** ("**induction has always worked in the past, so induction will work in the future" — using the proposition to be proved as the engine: this is the cosmic-scale version of Chapter 1's "tautological repetition" (a zero-interest loan lent to oneself**). **Hume's verdict** (**induction is groundless in logic and necessary in psychology: what supports it is not reason, but habit — "customism": reason can give no reason; nature decides for you: this is an honest failure, not a cover for a failure**). **This lesson's stance** (**do not pose as deduction, and do not kneel to deduction: the product of induction is a "risk quotation", not a "certificate of guarantee" — a person who can calculate probabilities neither curses Hume (he is right) nor plays dead (then no one would dare use induction): the right posture is to state the quotation sheet — how large the sample is, how wide the range is, what would overthrow me: in one sentence, turn the loan into an open account**.
:::

## Two disciplines of sampling: quantity and geography {#def-caiyang}

::: definition The sampling frame and the square {#def-gezi}
**Discipline one (the sampling frame before the sample size)** (**"survey 100 university students; 90% support extending the library's late-night opening" — at the foot of whose building did you stop them? The library door ten minutes before closing = counting the faithful inside a church (the sample is not a question of quantity, but of geography: on whichever square you hand out the questionnaire, your conclusion belongs to that square — the two characters 总体 ('population') are the first page of the study of sampling: first draw "who is eligible to be counted in", then talk about proportions**); **discipline two (the absent will speak)** (**survivorship bias — wartime statisticians stared at the bullet holes on the planes that had returned and recommended reinforcing the places with the most holes; a staff officer killed the proposal in one sentence: the planes that did not come back are not on the bullet-hole diagram (the commonly told version of Wald's scheme) — "what needs repairing is not in the holes, but on the people who did not come back: the first step in arranging the data is not to calculate what one already has, but to call the roll of those who did not turn up — questionnaire studies, rich lists, success studies, dropout statistics, analysis of bad reviews (a sampling frame of good reviews), start-up interviews (only the living accept an interview: the list of failures needs another archaeological team) are all the same question**)). **A link with Chinese rhetoric** ("**你身边的人都……**" ('the people around you all…') — "身边" ('at one's side') is the square: "我认识的体制内的人都后悔辞职" ('everyone I know inside the system regrets resigning') = the geochronology of the third floor of an office building (the foundation of Chapter 8's assessment of information, poured in advance).
:::

::: definition Mill's five methods: sorting the suspect list {#def-wufa}
**The method of agreement** (**the single shared exposure in several cases of illness: the canteen changed only one kind of soya sauce — "seek the one across the many cases: the sole common factor is automatically promoted to prime suspect"; the weak spot: what counts as "in common" depends on how finely you cut (they have all eaten soya sauce, and they have all breathed the same stretch of air: the relevant background is endless; which one you pick as the suspect depends on prior medical knowledge — Mill's five methods are not magic; they are a hypothesis-searcher**); **the method of difference** (**the same field, half fertilised and half not — a unique difference is the suspect: the philosophical pedigree papers of the controlled experiment, the ancestor of the gold standard; the weak spot: the "same" in "the same field" is metaphysics (soil, the aspect of the slope, the routes of flocks of birds: controlling variables = an application to simplify the universe, and the application is never written long enough**); **the joint method of agreement and difference** (**looking, in a positive group of cases and a negative group together, for the common factor: the classical prototype of the placebo control**); **the method of residues** (**deduct the known items, and open a separate account for the remainder — "where the accounts do not balance, there is a new star": the method by which Neptune was discovered (Newton's equations had finished with the known planets, and Uranus's orbit was still a little off: that difference reared the eighth planet); it is also the daily round of a criminal-investigation section (you were not there, the money is short, only he has the key) — the force = the force of the premises' being exhaustive: a remainder that has not been cleared out is all suspect (a missed entry and a ghost have the same face**); **the method of concomitant variation** (**what changes along with it may be a partner: dose and blood pressure covary — the logic of the thermometer, the forerunner of correlation; the weak spot: the crowing of cocks and sunrise have covaried hundreds of millions of times (the problem of direction and the problem of a third party (covariation only supplies a list of suspects, not a verdict — Chapter 7's court of causation takes the case**).
:::

::: example Soya-milk makers, solar cookers and school districts: three audits of analogy {#ex-leibi}
**① The soya-milk-maker case** (**"this chain's soya-milk makers work well; the Beijing shop's works well; ∴ the one at the place where I am staying on a business trip also works well" — break it into the form of an analogy and assess it**); **② the solar-cooker case** (**"the Moon has no atmosphere, and the difference in temperature between day and night is extreme; a desert field station hopes to save money on cooling, and so covers the expedition cabin with a reflective layer, simulating conditions on the lunar surface" — which step is the analogy, and which step is the test**); **③ the school-district case** (**"School A did well in last year's gaokao under the 'county-school model' (县中模式); School C need only copy it across" — list three reasons they are "not of the same kind", and give a stronger alternative inference**).
::: solution
①**The form of the analogy** (**School A has attributes abc + works well; this shop has abc; ∴ this shop works well — the assessment rests on "the relevance of the points of similarity to d (works well)": a shop's quality control, the water quality and the batches of beans are all relevant to "works well"; "the decoration looks alike" is not relevant — conclusion: an analogy of medium-to-low strength (being the same chain raises the sameness of kind of the machines and of the beans: the brand is the credit endorsement of the analogy — the whole of the logic that advertising buys**); ②**analogy is responsible only for "producing a bet"** (**the lunar surface and the desert are alike in being "dry, shedding heat by radiation, cloudless", and from this one infers that "a highly reflective layer can lower the temperature" — that is the analogy; the truth table: once it is fitted, measure the temperature — that is the test — the mark of a good analogy is precisely that it can be tested (a metaphor is analogy's sticker-pack; a model is an analogy: the contract writes down which attributes are relevant, and where the boundary of failure is: Rutherford's atom being like the solar system is likewise an analogy; its merit is not that it is true, but that it points out "what ought to be measured"**); ③**three distinctions** (**the entrance scores of the intake differ (the main cause of the outcome variable lies in the selection, not in the model), the age structure of the teachers differs, and the ecology of resitting differs — the relevant attributes are mismatched: copying only carries off "the actions one can see"; it cannot carry off "the conditions that produce the actions"**); **a stronger alternative inference** (**the method of difference first, and analogy afterwards: find a school whose intake is in the same frame as School C, and which also uses School A's model, to serve as a positive case, then run a small-scale pilot (controlling variables comes before transplanting the whole — "transplant one experimental plot first; do not transplant the whole school: the best way to use an analogy is to demote it to a hypothesis**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The turkey's thousand and first day {#int-huoji}
**Russell's turkey** (**the farmer fed it for 1,000 days; the inductive curve climbed all the way, and "the farmer loves me" was all but necessary — day 1,001 is Thanksgiving: the deepest place in induction is either laziness or thanksgiving (the material unchanged, the conclusion changes dynasty; it needs only the feeder to change heart once — for "a regularity to fail" the world need not turn bad; it needs only that you did not put "the farmer" into the table of variables**). **Three points for teaching**: **① defeasibility** (**an inductive conclusion is born with a revocation clause: this is not its disgrace; it is its way of working — "of all known reasoning, only that which is willing to leave a window for 'countermanding the order' deserves the name of experience**); **② bringing the mechanism into the sample** (**the turkey's way out (if it had a logic class): do not only count "the number of feedings"; also study "what the feeder lives on" — induction can be upgraded: from a surface regularity up to a hypothesis of mechanism (Chapter 7, causation: mechanism is induction's bulletproof vest — an induction that can say "why it must be so" takes a beating better than "it has always been so"**); **③ the scientist's self-cultivation** (**Popper's prescription (a further entry in the ledger of Chapter 4's inverse law of tautology): science does not start the count from confirmations; it starts the count from attempts to overthrow — "evidence that supports me sets me at ease; evidence that can overthrow me makes me progress: this sentence will not go into a book of inspirational quotations; it is methodology**).
:::

::: history A chronology of the techniques of the probable {#sec-biannian}
**5th century BC** the Hippocratic corpus, *Epidemics* (**"观察今日之病人以预告明日之病人" ('Observe today's patients so as to foretell tomorrow's patients.') (Our translation.): the inductive programme of empirical medicine + the Chinese "**物以类聚，方以类明**" ('Things gather by their kind; methods are made plain by their kind.') (Our translation.) (the impulse to classify in the "Xici" 系辞 of the *Book of Changes* (*Zhouyi* 周易) — kind is the family tree of *xiangshu* 象数 ('images and numbers') and of analogy (an advance drawn on Chapter 10's *mingbian* 名辩, 'the debate over names'**)); **1620** Francis Bacon, *Novum Organum* (**the title collides head-on with the *Organon*: the speech for the inductive method's opening for business — "idols of the cave and illusions of the race" (the cave of the person and the filter of the race: a systematic nomenclature of prejudice — an upgrade from "you should beware of prejudice" to "prejudice is of four kinds, each with an address": the first zoned census in the theory of fallacies**); **1739** Hume (**took the problem of induction out of theology's hands and nailed it down as a permanent sentence**); **1843** Mill, *A System of Logic* (**the five methods are made a canon (Yan Fu 严复 in 1905 translated it in as *Mule mingxue* 穆勒名学 ('Mill's Logic') — a sister case to the history of terminology in Chapter 2: both transfusions of a modern Chinese "feel for logic" travelled the inductive road**)); **1930s–50s** probability and induction (**Keynes's *A Treatise on Probability* (1921) mathematised the "probable"; Reichenbach's "straight rule" (直推) and Carnap's inductive logic — "the twentieth century tried to price the loan (degree of confidence, the prior, Bayesian updating) — Hume is still there; he has only changed over to collecting interest**); **1950s–the present** the paradox of confirmation and the Bayesian revival (**Goodman's "grue" paradox (绿蓝; the invention of the new predicate "grue", 绿蓝): even "which predicate the evidence supports" depends on linguistic habit — termites have been found under the last floorboard of induction as well; afterwards Bayesianism became one of the mainstream dialects of statistics: "safety" was abandoned outright, and "updating" took over the business**).
:::

::: warning Four pits of probable reasoning
**Taking a degree of confidence for truth** (**"repeated 99 times" is not equal to "necessary": frequency gives people comfort; mechanism gives people prediction — only a record of the feedings, and no portrait of the farmer**); **the square fallacy** (**using the sample at one's side to assert the whole country, and using the volume of voices online to assert the silent majority: "a sampling frame out of place" is the first way public-opinion reporting dies (a regular on the headlines of Chapter 8**)); **analogy exceeding its authority** (**using analogy in place of argument: "治大国若烹小鲜" ('Governing a great state is like cooking a small fish.') (Our translation.) is both an insight and an exemption from blame — a good analogy produces a hypothesis; a bad analogy ends the discussion (how to recognise it: after an analogy, does a "how to test" follow; if none follows, it is rhetoric**)); **the survivor's report** (**autobiographies of success studies: the failures as a whole cannot get a book published — before reading any "what do they all have in common", first ask "has a failure of the same model as me ever published a book?" — data will be silent, and silence is not consent**)
:::

::: exercise The quotation sheet {level=1}
For each of three inductive conclusions, open a **risk quotation sheet** (sampling frame / sample size / the scene most likely to overthrow it / the confidence grade you recommend): ① "after the canteen raised its prices, the rate of leftovers rose; students are sensitive to price"; ② "the proportion of this school's graduates taking the civil-service examination has risen for three years running, and will rise again next year"; ③ "I have used this notes app for two weeks, and my efficiency has clearly improved".
::: solution
A sample for marking: ① **confounders: whether, over the same period, the menu was changed / the epidemic limited the flow of people / the weather (variables other than price covarying with the leftover rate — use the method of difference before concluding)**; ② **three dead-end roads of extrapolating a trend (an abrupt change of policy, the calibre on which establishment posts are counted, the sample by graduating cohort: the sample size of "three years" = 3: a trend is not a law (hearing "rose three times in a row" as "must rise" = the turkey's third day**); ③ **a sample of oneself, n = 1, no control, novelty mixed in, publication bias (you remember only the times it worked well — the correct use of a single case: to generate a hypothesis, not to verify one ("two weeks" is the literal sense of a trial period: for the app, and also for your own conclusion**).
:::

::: exercise A distinguishing brief {level=2}
In a field you know, write a **"distinguishing opinion" of two hundred characters**: one side cites a precedent or an analogy in support of a claim; you write three reasons they are "not of the same kind" (naming the relevant attributes), then give one stronger case of the **same kind**.
::: solution
Marking: **"not of the same kind" must land on relevant attributes** ("the cities differ" does not count; "the structure of the intake differs" does — the technical content of distinguishing: from among a thousand differences, pick out "those few differences that bear on the outcome" (the muscle of case law, the muscle of the oral defence, isomorphic)**); the substitute case must state in which column the sameness of kind lies** (at least two of the three items: brand, mechanism and constraint — **the right to rewrite the analogy is in your hands: this is the lesson's attack and defence beating each other**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the poverty of safety and the richness of adventure {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**deduction is the safe poor relation; induction is the wealthy gambler; the family business of science is run jointly by two people: the bookkeeper is in charge of not making a loss, the gambler is in charge of turning the tables — every quarrel happens in the two persons' disagreement over 'whether this stake ought to be placed'**". You must call on at least six items: **the zero added value of complete induction**, **Hume's circle and the risk quotation**, **the sampling frame / the survivor**, **at least two of Mill's five methods**, **analogy = a testable bet**, **the turkey's day 1,001**; and adjudicate: **"is a large AI model the extreme of induction, or a new species of reasoning?" — give your appraisal in this lesson's vocabulary (hint: is training = super-enumeration? is prediction = writing on in probabilities? is "emergence" a black swan, or the turkey's Thanksgiving?)**.
::: solution
Points for marking: six mechanisms (**exhaustiveness is conjunction; the circle is an honest failure; geography decides the household registration of the conclusion; the five methods are a hypothesis-searcher; analogy's merit is in pointing the way, not in judging the case; the Thanksgiving clause**); the AI adjudication (**reference routes — the extreme-enumeration view: super-covariation statistics over a vast corpus; the absence of an explanation by mechanism is the turkey's risk (it will say "it has always been so"); the new-species view: the model has learned representations that can be reused across tasks; analogy and deduction are only a probabilistic machine compressed into the parameters; marking looks at the "conditions of failure" ("the moment a test question goes beyond the training distribution, it shows its true form: the evaluation set is that day 1,001 — so the higher the benchmark score, the more expensive the logic class: to write this sentence out is the standard of an A: it gives offence to both sides at once, and is right about both**); if the conclusion is written "**the truth of the family business: the bookkeeper cannot manage turning the tables, and the gambler cannot manage bankruptcy — a mature person does not pick a side, but knows whom to send on at this moment, and after the show they settle the accounts together**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed under the probable: two kinds of induction (**exhaustive, of zero added value; enumeration pays out the loan on the history of encounters**), Hume (**the circle + habit: turn the loan into an open account**), sampling (**first ask which square; first call the roll of those who did not turn up**), Mill (**the five methods sort the list of suspects; they are not a verdict**), analogy (**relevant attributes decide the strength; what can be tested is a model, and what cannot be tested is a sticker-pack; distinguishing is the counter-attack from the defence**), the turkey (**leave a window for revocation; write the mechanism into the sample**). One sentence of method: **when you draw a conclusion, attach your way of dying (what would overthrow me)**. The next chapter examines one kind of conclusion in particular: "A causes B".
