title: "Su Shi"

The previous chapter ended by saying that the "I" in the song lyric (*ci*) of the early Song was a post that could be lent on; this chapter writes of the man who wrote a birthday into a lyric — **Su Shi 苏轼 did not make the lyric larger; he moved himself in and lived there: taking poetry as the lyric, taking prose as the lyric, taking experience as the tune title (*cipai*), so that from then on the lyric's credit statement had one column more, "the author himself"**.

## Definition: the life is commentaries and subcommentaries {#def-shengping}

::: definition One route of demotion = one chronology of the works {#def-luxian}
**Meishan 眉山 — Bianjing 汴京 — Hangzhou 杭州 — Mizhou 密州 — Xuzhou 徐州 — Huzhou 湖州 — Huangzhou 黄州 — Huizhou 惠州 — Danzhou 儋州 — Changzhou 常州** (**Lay the route map over the collected works, and the rate of overlap is frighteningly high: 'Jiangchengzi: Hunting out at Mizhou' (《江城子·密州出猎》) was written at Mizhou in 1075, the two pieces 'Red Cliff' (《赤壁》) were written at Huangzhou in 1082, 'Eating Lychees' (《食荔枝》) was completed at Huizhou, and 'Crossing the Sea on the Night of the Twentieth of the Sixth Month' (《六月二十日夜渡海》) was completed on the return north from Danzhou —** this is not coincidence; it is a mechanism: the Song system of places of demotion pulled the author out of the central machine of official documents and stuffed him into the rivers and lakes, into disasters, into the rice fields and onto the sea: the service record became a bank of topics (the standard Song case, in Lesson 16, of "position produces the text": demotion is the invisible editor-in-chief of Song literature**). The Crow Terrace Poetry Case (乌台诗案, 1079)** (**imprisoned on account of poems: the writing was interrogated line by line (the usual account: the Censorate cited his poems as slander) —** one event taught him two things: first, a poem can serve as evidence produced in court (thereafter his "pieces in which something was intended by the writing" (有意于文字者) moved across into the leftover of poetry, *shiyu* 诗余, an older name for the song lyric, and into letters, *chidu* 尺牍); second, the lyric's tradition of exemption from liability could catch the poem's debts (**after the accident, the duty-free zone became a refuge from creditors: the legal status of a genre decides what it can hold**).
:::

## Huangzhou: the author's apparatus {#def-huangzhou}

::: definition Rewriting "minister" as "guest" {#def-ke}
**The double voice of host and guest in 'Rhapsody on the Red Cliff' (《赤壁赋》)** (**the guest blows the xiao (洞箫), as if grieving, as if longing, and Master Su (苏子) answers with the distinction of water and the moon —** the concession is turned into an institution: the strongest argument for the void in the whole piece is spoken by the "guest" first, spoken to the end and spoken to the full, and only then is it the host's turn to take it apart (the distant ancestor, in the form of the rhapsody (*fu*), of the concession placed first in Lesson 6: the guest is the seat of his opposing side, kept for ever inside his own house**); “逝者如斯，而未尝往也；盈虚者如彼，而卒莫消长也”** ('What passes is like this, and yet it has never gone; what waxes and wanes is like that, and yet in the end it neither shrinks nor grows.') (Our translation.) — **the same water, two ledgers (the course that changes and the course that does not): the "discoursing on principle" (说理) in Song poetry is not the shouting of slogans; it is putting the same thing into two sets of statements and checking one against the other (a return to the lesson on Song poetry**); **the lone crane of 'Later Rhapsody on the Red Cliff' (《后赤壁赋》)** (**“道士顾笑，予亦惊寤”** ('The Daoist turned his head and smiled, and I too woke with a start.') (Our translation.) — **the ending does not settle the account: for the first time a rhapsody closes on a suspense (the advanced use of the unsettled point in Lesson 15: he left the ending of the rhapsody as a door standing open**); **'Busuanzi: The Wild Goose' (《卜算子·雁》)** — “**拣尽寒枝不肯栖，寂寞沙洲冷**” ('It picks over every cold branch and will not roost; the lonely sandbank is cold.') (Our translation.) (written at Huangzhou, the usual account) — **a self-portrait of character does not paint the face; it paints a bird's problem of where to lodge: the highest compression of the situation at a place of demotion (不肯 = the stance, 沙洲 = the situation, 冷 = the feel of the body: three lines complete an autobiography**); **the legend of 'Linjiangxian' (《临江仙》)** (“**小舟从此逝，江海寄馀生**” ('A little boat is gone from this moment; what remains of my life I entrust to the rivers and the sea.') (Our translation.) — **Song notebooks (*biji*) record the story “郡守大惊叩门” ('the prefect, in great alarm, knocked at the door') (Our translation.); it need not really have happened, and yet everyone is willing to believe it (the automatic supplement written by reception history: the reader shoots the sequel for the author: the legend is the work's dividend**.
:::

::: definition Dingfengbo: rewriting the accident as weather {#def-dingfengbo}
“**莫听穿林打叶声，何妨吟啸且徐行。竹杖芒鞋轻胜马，谁怕？一蓑烟雨任平生。**” ('Do not listen to the sound, through the wood, of rain striking the leaves. What harm in chanting aloud and walking slowly on? A bamboo staff and straw sandals are lighter than a horse. Who is afraid? One coir cape, and the mist and the rain: I let them be as my whole life will.') (Our translation.) (1082, Huangzhou; the preface “沙湖道中遇雨，雨具先去” ('On the road at Shahu I met with rain; the rain gear had gone on ahead.') (Our translation.) — **the preface of a lyric for the first time records the coordinates of a small matter: meeting rain, companions on the road, knowing of it beforehand — none of these is a traditional topic of the lyric (a small topic + a date + a place = plugging the lyric into the official-document lineage of the diary form**); **a technical taking-apart** (**① “莫听／何妨” ('do not listen' / 'what harm') = two active reallocations of attention (it is not that the scenery has changed; the headings in the books have changed); ② “穿林打叶” ('through the wood, striking the leaves') = translating the sound of political wind into weather (the frontal use of the indirect stroke, *qubi* 曲笔, in Lesson 16: not that he dared not say it, but that he translated the whole level of danger**); ③ **the keen mountain wind and the slanting light — the body is reconciled before the mind is (**“归去，也无风雨也无晴” ('Go back: there is neither wind and rain, nor any clear sky.') (Our translation.) is not a song of triumph at sudden enlightenment; it is the statement balancing after wet clothes have dried: what is great in Su Shi is that he is cold first, and thinks it through afterwards (experience first, philosophy after: this is the most superficial likeness to, and the deepest difference from, Zhuangzi's “安时而处顺” ('be at ease in the time, and dwell in accord') (Our translation.).
:::

## The jurisdiction case of taking poetry as the lyric {#def-guanxia}

::: definition Four projects of expansion {#def-kuorong}
**① The system of prefaces to the piece** (“**乙卯正月二十日夜记梦**” ('On the night of the twentieth of the first month of the *yimao* year, a record of a dream.') (Our translation.) “**丙辰中秋，欢饮达旦，大醉，作此篇兼怀子由**” ('Mid-autumn of the *bingchen* year: drank merrily until dawn, dead drunk, wrote this piece and with it thought of Ziyou 子由.') (Our translation.) — **date, personal name and the matter in hand enter the cover of the lyric: a text for singing becomes, for the first time, a text that records events (a rewriting of the genre contract in Lesson 1: the lyric's addressee is changed from "the singer" to "history" (the elegy for the dead to the tune *Jiangchengzi* 《江城子》, “**不思量，自难忘**” ('I do not set myself to think of it, and of itself it is hard to forget.') (Our translation.): the lyric begins to take on the business of "private standing" (私人名分) that only poetry had had**)); **② The invasion by topics from poetry** (**elegy for the dead, meditation on the past, discoursing on principle, pastoral questioning —** the lyric's list of subjects is opened across the board to poetry; 'Niannujiao: Red Cliff' (《念奴娇·赤壁》) turns the "Three Kingdoms" into material for contemplation: “**故国神游，多情应笑我，早生华发**” ('In spirit I roam that former kingdom; the one of much feeling should laugh at me, that my hair has greyed so early.') (Our translation.) — **Zhou Yu 周瑜 is used by Su Shi, inside the lyric, as a mirror, and the mirror is held the wrong way round: the hero's youth is set against his own hair greyed early: meditation on the past is not a tribute; it is a fault-finding check of the accounts (“人生如梦，一尊还酹江月” ('Human life is like a dream; one cup I still pour out to the moon in the river.') (Our translation.) is a transfer made over to the river moon: losses that cannot be written off are posted to a public account**)); **③ Argument enters the lyric** (“**人有悲欢离合，月有阴晴圆缺，此事古难全**” ('People have grief and joy, parting and reunion; the moon has cloud and clear weather, waxing and waning; this matter has from of old been hard to make whole.') (Our translation.) — **the conclusion is written straight into the words of the song: people of the Song disliked it for saying too much; people now praise it for being too exact:** between the lyric's “要眇宜修” ('delicate, and fit to be adorned') and poetry's “说尽” ('saying it all'), he chose to break the contract on both sides (the question in the preface to 'Shuidiao getou' (《水调歌头》), “不知天上宫阙，今夕是何年” ('I do not know, of the palaces in heaven, what year this night is.') (Our translation.), is precisely taking the "moon" as an object of political argument: heaven is asked and does not answer; the lyric answers for heaven**)); ④ The dispute over *qingxiong* and according with the music** (**Chao Buzhi 晁补之 and Li Qingzhao 李清照, in one line of criticism, mocked him for “多不谐音律” ('mostly not according with the music') and for “句读不葺之诗” ('poems whose *judou*, the pause-marks, have not been put in order') (names assigned by the usual account) —** this is not a conservatism of taste; it is a protest over jurisdiction: expansion = changing the asset structure of the genre: **when the "author" moves in, the singer gives up the tenancy: the dispute over the rent and the right of signature is the title deed of this case**.
:::

::: definition The late years: turning suffering into sightseeing {#def-wannian}
**“日啖荔枝三百颗，不辞长作岭南人”** ('Each day I eat three hundred lychees; I would not refuse to be, for long, a man of Lingnan 岭南.') (Our translation.) ('Eating Lychees') / **“九死南荒吾不恨，兹游奇绝冠平生”** ('Nine deaths in the southern wilds, and I do not regret them; this journey, surpassingly strange, crowns my whole life.') (Our translation.) ('Crossing the Sea on the Night of the Twentieth of the Sixth Month') — **note the pattern of the sentence: he does not write “恨” ('regret'); he writes “不恨” ('do not regret'); he does not write “奇” ('strange'); he writes “冠平生” ('crowns my whole life'):** suffering is rewritten as a journey that yields a return (turning it into sightseeing is not numbness: numbness is the absence of narration; he seized the right of narration back (set against the early Song lyric's "not giving the accident": Su Shi in his late years is "retitling the accident" — the same technique of the blank, its use changed from covering up to saving himself); Zhaoyun 朝云 (Wang Zhaoyun 王朝云, the concubine who attended him at Huizhou, died, and a lyric mourns her ('Xijiangyue: Plum Blossom' 《西江月·梅花》, “高情已逐晓云空” ('The lofty feeling has already followed the dawn clouds into the void.') (Our translation.), the name assigned by the usual account) — the number of times in his life that he wrote a private relation into a public genre had no one before him: the lyric's "private permission" is a patent he applied for (a return to Lesson 1: the lyrical "I" changes from a post into a real person, and the work starts from him); the Cold Food manuscript (《寒食帖》) (a draft of poems at Huangzhou in 1082: crossings-out, and character-forms that slant, become part of the text (a return to the calligraphy lesson: the "traces of revision" in a manuscript are literature's duplicate copy: the myth of a definitive text of the lyric goes bankrupt here: literature is at its best precisely when it has not yet been copied out fair.
:::

::: example Reading in parallel on one theme: two cities in the rain {#ex-duizuo}
Read together 'Dingfengbo' (meeting rain on the road at Shahu 沙湖) and Liu Yong's 柳永 'Yulinling' (《雨霖铃》, 寒蝉凄切, 'the cold cicada's cry is sharp') (Our translation.): both write "rain / a desolate scene + a journey". Please make a comparison table in three columns, **① who is speaking, ② who the companion is, ③ where the rain falls in the end**; then answer: **why Liu Yong's rain falls inside a parting, while Su Shi's rain falls inside the body**.
::: solution
**① The speaker** — Liu: **the tone of a male singer speaking by proxy (saying, to the person in the "orchid boat" (兰舟), “执手相看泪眼” ('we hold hands and look at each other with eyes full of tears') (Our translation.); the person is a post that can be lent on (Lesson 15)**; Su: **a person who comes with a name and a date (the preface reports in first: “雨具先去，同行皆狼狈” ('The rain gear had gone on ahead, and everyone travelling with him was in a wretched state.') (Our translation.) — the witness's identity is given before the testimony**); **② The companion** — the person present in Liu is **the beloved (a private relation = a bank of feeling)**; the persons present in Su are **the crowd of "everyone travelling with him was in a wretched state", plus one self (a social relation + a meeting held inside: for the first time the lyric has its extras)**; **③ Where the rain goes** — Liu's rain **is in the imagining after the parting (“杨柳岸晓风残月” ('willows on the bank, the dawn wind, a waning moon') (Our translation.) = the rain falls on an account drawn in advance against the future)**; Su's rain **falls, from start to finish, on skin and flesh (“料峭春风吹酒醒，微冷” ('The keen spring wind blows the wine awake; a slight chill.') (Our translation.): the body pays first, and the heart gives the change afterwards)**; **the verdict** — **what Liu Yong writes is rain inside a relation (parting is a kind of identity); what Su Shi writes is rain inside a situation (relegation is a kind of climate):** the former lyric borrows human feeling; the latter lyric passes through my own body: the whole cost of carriage from "post" to "real person", Su Shi paid off over one life.
:::
:::

::: intuition Su Shi is the public personality of the Song {#int-gongwu}
**"Su Shi" was already a cultural idol in both the Northern and the Southern Song** (**poetic exchanges, poems made by assembling others' lines, and verses written on portraits of Su Shi (people of the Song already had an industry of "commentaries on Po's poems" (坡诗注) and a vogue of gatherings for "matching Su" (和苏) (the thread of the usual account) —** the mechanism that produces an exemplar: people of the Song were already consuming Su Shi; becoming an idol is not a matter for after death; it is the present continuous); his imitability is extremely high, and his replicability is zero (those who study Su get his *kuang* and lose his art: for this "ease" (旷, the short form of *kuangda* 旷达, 'expansive ease') is not a temperament; it is a whole set of techniques for handling a thing (reallocating attention, taking back the right of narration, meditation on the past by checking accounts) — the correct posture for learning Su Shi is to copy his procedure, not to copy his mood (what Jin Shengtan 金圣叹 called the "method of taking one's pleasure" (行乐法) (a return to the reception history in Lesson 15); “一肚皮不合时宜” ('a whole bellyful of not fitting the times') (Our translation.) (the usual account in the notebooks of Song writers: Zhaoyun recognised in him “一肚皮不入时宜” ('a whole bellyful of not entering what suits the times') (Our translation.)) — what suits the times is an institution; not fitting them is a position: Su Shi's greatness does not lie in his having risen above factional strife (he did not: both sides demoted him), but in his having demoted both sides into material (this is a privilege of genre: only when literature's ledger is large enough can a political loss be settled on another set of books.
:::

::: history A chronology of Su (selected points) {#sec-nianbiao}
1037 born in Meizhou 眉州; 1057 passed the *jinshi* examination (Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修 was chief examiner that year — teacher and pupil met inside a scheme of marking: the rite by which the ancient-style prose movement, the *guwen* movement, passed on its place (the other timeline, in Lesson 1, of the second year of Jiayou); 1071–74 controller-general (通判) of Hangzhou (the industrialisation of West Lake (西湖): “水光潋滟晴方好” ('The light on the water, rippling, is at its best when the day is clear.') (Our translation.) ('Drinking on the Lake, Clear at First, Then Rain' 《饮湖上初晴后雨》, the usual account) — a landscape is taken in, by a single line of verse, as public property: from then on the city advertises for tenants according to the poem); 1075 Mizhou (two lyrics to the tune *Jiangchengzi*: hunting out, and recording a dream — the same year and the same place wrote two futures for the lyric); 1076 'Record of the Terrace of Chaoran' (《超然台记》) together with 'Shuidiao getou' (the two characters “超然” ('rising clear of it') are his early brand name: he used Zhuangzi to outsource the career of office); 1079 the Crow Terrace Poetry Case; 1082 the two rhapsodies at Huangzhou, 'Niannujiao', 'Dingfengbo', the Cold Food manuscript (a miracle year for one author — more exactly: the yearly interest on one spell of being left idle by the institution); 1084 'Inscribed on the Wall at West Forest' (《题西林壁》) (“不识庐山真面目，只缘身在此山中” ('I do not know the true face of Mount Lu 庐山, and only because I am myself inside this mountain.') (Our translation.) — one of the peaks of Song writers' discoursing on principle: epistemology written as a tourist's complaint); 1089–91 prefect of Hangzhou (the Su Embankment (苏堤): a work of administration lives longer than the essays: literature and infrastructure share one and the same author's name); 1094 Huizhou; 1097 Danzhou (he set up a school, and carried books across the sea: the place of exile pushed the boundary of Song culture out to an island in the sea); 1101 on the return north, he died at Changzhou (after his death: from "Yuanyou partisan" (元祐党人) to "Wenzhong Gong" (文忠公), and on to cultural idol: the speed of rehabilitation could not catch the speed at which the persona reproduced).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in Su Shi
**The sticker of expansive ease** (**pressing the whole of the work down into the two characters "optimism" (乐观):** expansive ease is the result of the procedure, not the premise of the work — first take the cold of 'Busuanzi' and the bitterness of the Cold Food manuscript as the cost, and only then is there the profit of 'Dingfengbo' (to read the profit and not the cost is an investment scam)); the textbook habit of the "bold-style school of the lyric" ("the Su–Xin school of the bold style" (苏辛豪放派) is a bin marked out by later people (the legacy of lyric criticism in the Ming and the Qing, and of modern textbooks): Su Shi himself never registered a school, and people of the Song are not seen operating a "school of the bold style" (豪放派) — to explain Su Shi by schools is to travel by a map: most of Su's lyrics lie between *qingxiong* (清雄, 'clear and heroic') and texts for singing; they do not lie inside the "bold style" (豪放))); taking the legend for history (the door-knocking story of “小舟从此逝” ('a little boat is gone from this moment'), the year of the elegy “十年生死” ('ten years, the living and the dead'), the dialogue with Zhaoyun about "a bellyful" (一肚皮): the layer of Song notebooks — a notebook is not a fabrication; it is taking an explanation for news: a citation has to mark its layer (the layering of sources in Lesson 1)); reading the technique as genius ("he was by nature open and easy" (生性豁达) cancels the whole history of the craft: prefaces to the piece, checking the accounts, translating the accident, leaving a suspense — every one of these is a technique that can be taught (this lesson teaches Su Shi so that you too can do what he did: not in order to become him, but in order to learn his procedure.
:::
