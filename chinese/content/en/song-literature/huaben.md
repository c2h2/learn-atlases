---
title: "Storytellers’ scripts"
---

The first eight chapters were all set in the study, at the banquet and in the yamen; this chapter goes downstairs, into the *wǎshè* 瓦舍 ('entertainment quarters') — **what the storyteller's script (*huàběn*) taught Chinese narrative is three things, later called, respectively, point of view, serialisation and reader response; vernacular fiction (*báihuà*) was not born out of Classical Chinese (*wényán*), it walked out of a room that charged admission**.

## Definition: a trial that has stayed open for a hundred years {#def-ting}

::: definition The three-layered unsettled case of "what a storyteller's script is" {#def-tingan}
**Material layer** (the so-called storyteller's scripts of Song writers, as they have come down, depend for the most part on Ming compiled texts and revised texts (the line of *Jingben tongsu xiaoshuo* 京本通俗小说 ('Popular Stories from the Capital Edition') and of *Qingping shantang huaben* 清平山堂话本 ('Storyteller's Scripts from the Hall of Clear Peace')), and the authenticity of the compiled texts is itself a modern unsettled case (the received account: some have judged *Jingben tongsu xiaoshuo* a forgery) — let us admit this first: the 'Song *huaben*' which this chapter reads are a version jointly compiled by three parties, the storytelling grounds of the Song and the Yuan, the bookshops of the Ming, and modern scholars (a textbook case of Chapter 8's 'text and wording')); **definition layer** ('*huaben*' may refer to: ① the base text used by the storyteller, ② a record of the scene of telling, ③ a later composition imitating the tone of the telling ('imitation *huaben*', *nǐhuàběn* 拟话本) — the substance of the quarrel among the three accounts: we have in hand almost nothing of ② (records made on the spot are extremely scarce), and so, as between ① and ③, which is right and which is wrong can be settled only by internal evidence (**a discipline's quarrel over its definition is often the helplessness of the inventory of what it holds**)); **naming layer** (of the 'four schools of storytelling' (*shuōhuà* 说话), both the number and the names are in question: Song records diverge (*Ducheng jisheng* 都城纪胜 ('Record of the Splendours of the Capital'), *Mengliang lu* 梦粱录 ('A Record of the Millet Dream') and *Chuogeng lu* 辍耕录 ('Notes on Resting from the Plough') differ in what they say, the received account); Lu Xun 鲁迅, in *A Brief History of Chinese Fiction*, sets up such classes as '*xiaoshuo*' ('fiction') and '*jiangshi*' ('telling of history') (the received account) — clear classes do not mean that people in the Song lived by them (the inversion of Lesson 15's 'classification before the works': here the classification comes eight hundred years after the works, and several of the boxes are sorted wrong)).
:::

## The *washi*: China's earliest literary box office {#def-washe}

::: definition The selection pressure of a single ticket {#def-piao}
**Place and economy** (the *wǎzi* 瓦子 and the *gōulán* 勾栏 ('railed theatres') of Bianjing 汴京, and of Lin'an 临安 under the Southern Song (the received account: the bustle recorded in *Dongjing menghua lu* 东京梦华录 ('Dreams of Splendour of the Eastern Capital') and in *Mengliang lu*); there were also the 'roadside pitches', *lùqí* 路歧 — free pitches, or pitches taken as they came, going up and down the streets and lanes (the received account): **a fixed *goulan* = a fixed audience = a reputation that can be accumulated: for the first time, literature had the concept of the 'return customer'** (Chapter 4's making into a venue of Liu Yong's 柳永 well-water transmission — an upgrade from 'it can be sung as far as' to 'buy a ticket and listen')); **the structure of income** (selling tickets, begging a booking and seeking money, a signboard with one's name (the received account: Luo Ye's 罗烨 *Zuiweng tanlu* 醉翁谈录 ('The Old Drunkard's Record of Talks') records the word-of-mouth standing of the famous performers of the time, and the division into the classes 'telling of history' and 'fiction') — **reputation is an asset that can be turned into cash: and so the ground of telling has brands, has styles, and has plagiarism and imitation** (**the performer's name entered the text; for the first time, Chinese narrative had a market value for a 'signed style'** (the mirror image of the words of recommendation in Chapter 9: the audience writes the recommendation with its own copper cash))).
:::

::: definition The grammar of performance: entry tale, main tale, next instalment {#def-yufa}
**Entry tale (*rùhuà* 入话; the head round, *tóuhuí* 头回)** (first tell one short little story, or adduce several poems; wait until the stream of people has filled the place, try the voice, and set the tone — the functional account: the entry tale is at once a warm-up and a 'moral preview'; it tells the listeners by which set of values the things that follow will be judged tonight (the oral version of Lesson 5's 'show the stake at the opening': the stake may be the one sentence 'good is repaid with good')); **the narrator shows himself** (“说话的，但见……” ('the teller: and one only sees ...'), “看官听说……” ('honoured audience, now hear ...') — the exception to zero focalisation: he coughs, he urges you, and tomorrow he still has to go on (Chapter 4's theory of point of view: from the first day, Chinese vernacular narrative does not pretend to be a camera; it pretends to be a person (the distant ancestor of Lesson 16's 'write the channel into the grammar': **the storyteller writes “我在讲” ('I am telling') into the syntax**))); **'honoured audience' (*kànguān* 看官)** (a fictitious listener summoned again and again: it assembles random passers-by into a community (the oral source of Lesson 7's 'four systems of "we"': this 'honoured audience' does not take morality hostage; it only reminds you that you are listening, and that you have the right to leave)); **break-off and close** (a break-off of the kind “要知后事如何” ('If you would know how matters turned out afterwards.') (Our translation.), and a closing formula of the kind “话本说彻，且作散场” ('The storyteller's script is told through; for now, let the company disperse.') (Our translation.) (the quotation follows the received account) — “且听下回分解” ('Just hear the next instalment break it down.') (Our translation.) = the commercial contract of serialisation (time paid by instalments: attention is priced, and the story comes to have stock and a debt (the worldly version of Chapter 7's 'To My Son': the next instalment, too, is a future tense which only someone else can reach))).
:::

## The anatomy of two works {#def-jiepou}

::: definition *The Carved Jade Guanyin*: the craftsman and the regulations {#def-niangu}
**Plot skeleton** (Qu Xiuxiu 璩秀秀 and Cui the *dàizhāo* 待诏 ('artisan attendant'): two marriages, one elopement, one 'ghost marriage' (the received account: the face of the compiled text as it survives) — what most deserves notice, in the structure, is this: the story is told to the end, and then told once again (the edition with the dead)); **technical verdict** (the 'ghost wife' of the second half is not an ornament of superstition; it is a second audit by the narrative: **the same pair of lovers fails, among the living, by the algorithm of human feeling, and succeeds, among the dead, by the algorithm of narrative — the *huaben* is therefore more honest than the moral system it inhabits** (Lesson 6's 'concession passage' turned into a story: I admit that rites and law won the reality; please allow me to turn the tables in another ledger)); **jargon and regulations** (the jade-polishing attendant, the temporary imperial residence (*xíngzài* 行在), the assignments and the rewards and punishments of the commandery prince's mansion (the received account: this piece keeps a great deal of the vocabulary of Song court bureaux and of the artisan system) — a townsman audience can hear the difference 'inside the establishment and outside it': **only when a place in the social order is written into the detail is there 'just as if it were real'** (the narrative version of Lesson 3's study of evidence: credibility lives in the jargon)).
:::

::: definition *The Wrongful Beheading of Cui Ning*: the economics of contingency {#def-cuozhan}
**Plot skeleton** (a misjudgement and a wrongful killing set off by fifteen strings of cash, *guàn* 贯 (the received account: the surviving compiled text takes 'a jest comes true' as its trigger) — the engine of the whole piece is one joke and one coincidence, not anyone's malice); **thematic mechanism** (the real meaning of 'fate', inside the *huaben*, is **information asymmetry**: once the gaps in the witnesses, the stolen goods and the confession have been filled in together, a person dies (**Song people said “造恶惹罪过” ('Doing evil brings the fault upon you.') (Our translation.); what we say today is a systemic misjudgement: two ways of keeping the books for one and the same event (the ancient scene of Lesson 5's regression to the mean and of Chapter 8's base rate: a wrongful case is not a bad person committing a crime; it is a low-probability event caught by the narrative**)); **the storyteller's comment** (again and again the piece has exhortations to the world (a feature on the received account) — these are often dismissed as 'feudal preaching'; this lesson reads them as a technique of performance: the comment is a stop-loss service for the listeners (the story is too frightening; someone has to tell you that tonight you can still sleep (the functional account of Lesson 15's 'moral tail': **preaching is often a painkiller for narrative anxiety, not the work's moral grade**))).
:::

::: definition Telling of history: the history book has the names, the storyteller's script has the plot {#def-jiangshi}
**A two-track system** (telling of history must follow the history books (the received account: 'telling the books of history' takes a work of the *Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance* kind (*Zizhi tongjian* 资治通鉴) as its skeleton, and elaborates that skeleton into a piece), and so the contract of its genre is: **great events may not be changed; small events, every one of them, must be supplied** (the extreme version of Lesson 1's 'genre = agreement': it signs, at the same time, for the official historian and for the audience)); **the mechanism of 'popularisation'** (translate the cause and effect of the annals into human motives and into scenes: **the first mass readers of history are students of history, and their teacher coughs** (**the cost of popularisation is not distortion; it is the pressing of 'many causes' into 'someone': this is the permanent risk of telling history, and also its permanent market (the boundary, drawn in Lesson 2, between 'factual claims' and 'value claims' is here levelled by the trade**)); **downstream** (old printings of the *Xinkan Wudai shi pinghua* 新刊五代史平话 kind ('Newly Printed Plain Tale of the History of the Five Dynasties') (the received account), and the final form which writers of the Ming gave to the *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* (the received account: accumulated into a book) — **'accumulated across generations' means that there is no author, only several generations of editors** (the opposite direction from Chapter 3's 'citation inflation': **collective writing, in popular literature, is not a defect; it is a mode of production**)).
:::

::: example Taking a performance apart {#ex-changyan}
Suppose you are the storyteller in the *wazi* of the silk market at Lin'an. Tonight's listeners: half are guild shop-assistants (they have come for the bustle), half are serving-women sent by their husbands (they have come to hear the moral). Using *The Wrongful Beheading of Cui Ning*, do a 'reworking of the show': **change the entry tale for another, cut one section of the main tale (*zhènghuà* 正话), and add one sentence of comment**, and say which risk to the listeners each change answers.
::: solution
**① Entry tale** — replace it with a neat little tale of 'a jest pays a debt' (shorter than the original entry tale, and funnier): the shop-assistants are buying rhythm, the serving-women are buying cause and effect: **the laugh sits the first kind down, and retribution makes the second kind nod** (the two-audience version of Lesson 5's 'show the stake at the opening': the stake has to be a draw and a preview at once); **② cut one section** — cut the excessively detailed scene of judicial torture (**keep the injustice of the verdict, and cut the technical detail of the cruel officers:** cruel detail is an affront to the serving-women ('retribution' fails, because the listeners begin to pity the concrete pain of the victim rather than to fear fate), and for the shop-assistants it is a cheap stimulus — you lose at both ends (Lesson 3's 'tears are a receipt, not an invoice': if you do not fit the tears with a pipe, emotion floods the argument)); **③ add one sentence** — add “看官：这十五贯钱，若不曾在桌上多放了一贯，便是十个人也不至于死” ('Honoured audience: these fifteen strings of cash — had one string more not been set on the table, even ten people need not have died.') (Our translation.) (**one sentence that assigns a cause hands the listeners something they can use:** it translates 'fate' into 'the management of detail', and they can use it as soon as they go home tonight (preaching becomes a service here, and service is another name for paying the fee again)); **overall verdict** — the 'edition' of oral literature is not an error of copying; it is a market test, once each night: **the 'Song *huaben*' we read today are those nights which the audience approved** (the extreme case of Chapter 4's 'the listener is the reviewer').
:::
:::

::: intuition The bias of the surviving sample {#int-shengcun}
**Storyteller's scripts and notebook literature are two formats of distribution** (notebook literature (*bǐjì* 笔记) depends on manuscript copies and on keeping them at home (the author preserves them himself); the *huaben* depends on performance and on the bookshop (commercial preservation) — both are lost in great numbers, but the manner of the loss differs: notebooks are lost because no one copies them, *huaben* are lost because no one prints them (the Song branch of Lesson 16's 'distribution format decides life and death')); **the corpses in the drawer** (the gulf between the ten-odd Song *huaben* that survive today (the order of magnitude on the received account) and the bustle of performance at the time — the sample on which we judge 'the level of Song narrative' is the little that leaked through the eyes of two sieves (the ancient version of Chapter 8's 'publication bias': **any literary history that argues from survivors must, in its conclusion, mark itself down to eight-tenths**)); **the water table of the language** (“我们、你、我、且、只、却” ('we', 'you', 'I', 'moreover', 'only', 'but') enter the main text: the true legacy of the *huaben* is pronouns, modal particles and the set phrases of talk (**the vernacular is not a simplification of Classical Chinese; it is another spoken language written down: its grammar grew up in the *goulan*, and Classical Chinese is only a coat it borrowed to wear (the prehistory of the *baihua* movement in Lesson 17: nine hundred years ago it had already succeeded once, and what it used was copper cash**)).
:::

::: history An undervalued hidden thread {#sec-anxian}
**1100s—1200s** the *washi* of Bianjing and Lin'an (**the received account: *Dongjing menghua lu*, *Mengliang lu* and *Ducheng jisheng* record the bustle each in its own book:** the density of the urban population, the institution of the night market, and money to spare, are the whole of the means of production of the *huaben* (the narrative version of Lesson 1's peacetime interest)); **Luo Ye's *Zuiweng tanlu*** (**the received account: it records 'the opening up of fiction' (*xiaoshuo kaipi* 小说开辟) and the houses of historical telling, and speaks along the lines of “小说名件至为夥……足以矜持座，应付才情” ('The named pieces of fiction are exceedingly many ... enough to command the sitting and to meet wit.') (Our translation.) (the quotation follows the received account) —** the earliest Chinese outline of an aesthetics of fiction came from the hands of writers of *qu* (*qǔjiā* 曲家) and of bookshops, and not from literary collections (the genre of criticism sinks in the Song (the sister event of Lesson 1's '*shihua*' 诗话, 'remarks on poetry'))); **1290s—1360s** historical plain tales (*pínghuà* 平话) between Song and Yuan, and old printings of the *Xinkan Wudai shi pinghua* kind; **between Hongwu 洪武 and Jiajing 嘉靖 in the Ming** the Qingping Mountain Hall compiled and printed *Liushi jia xiaoshuo* 六十家小说 ('Stories of Sixty Houses') (**masters of the bookshops became the patrons of popular literature:** booksellers of the Ming handed in the homework on behalf of the performers of the Song (the commercial reversal of Lesson 1's 'a catalogue is a censorship': a catalogue is also a solicitation)); **the Tianqi 天启 years** the 'Three Words' (*Sanyan* 三言) were compiled (the received account: **the line of Feng Menglong 冯梦龙 arranged and adapted the short pieces of the Song, the Yuan and the Ming — from this point the imitation *huaben* stood as a genre of its own (**the traces of collation are its birth certificate: popular fiction has never had a definitive text (the reverse of Lesson 2's *Hanshi tie* 寒食帖 ('the Cold Food manuscript'): the value of a manuscript is its uniqueness; the value of a *huaben* is that it can be changed**))); **1920s** Lu Xun's *A Brief History of Chinese Fiction* and the case over whether *Jingben tongsu xiaoshuo* is genuine or forged (**the entrance of modern scholarship: the *huaben* changes from 'crude fiction' into 'townsman literature' (**every generation invents the Song *huaben* over again, according to whom it wants to claim as an ancestor**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the storyteller's script
**The automatic bonus of 'townsman literature = progress'** (giving the *huaben* extra marks because it uses the vernacular and writes about ordinary people: **the rank of a language is not the rank of morality, nor the rank of technique** (Lesson 1's linguistic answer to 'Classical Chinese / the vernacular': this chapter adds one further answer, from the study of literature)); **taking a compiled text for a Song edition** ('What is in *Jingben tongsu xiaoshuo* simply is the Song's' — while the case of genuine or forged has not been closed, any 'of the Song' is a hypothesis (a citation has to be written 'the face of the compiled text as it survives' (the strict carrying-out of Lesson 1's layering of sources))); **writing it off in one stroke as 'feudal preaching'** (an exhortation to the world, at the end of a piece, is material, not dross: **first ask what it is doing inside the performance, and only then judge what use it still has for us today** (Lesson 15's historicism clause)); **founding an empire on ten-odd pieces** (using the ten-odd pieces now extant to infer that 'Song vernacular fiction had already reached a mature stage' — the sample frame is too narrow: **maturity is the visible end of a long accumulation, not the total of what survives** (Chapter 8's 'a numerator without a denominator is rhetoric')).
:::

::: exercise Write an entry tale {level=1}
For one modern scene (a takeaway delivery, working overtime, or a matchmaking meeting — any one of these), write an 'entry tale' of 100 characters: one little story plus one sentence of moral preview. You may not bring in the persons of the main matter.
::: solution
Marking: **the little story must stand on its own** (**it can be told to the end by itself, and be funny, or frightening, by itself**); **the moral preview must be capable of being broken by the main matter** (**the function of the preview is to produce expectation and contrast:** say “吃亏的是老实人” ('It is the honest person who loses out.') (Our translation.), and what comes next must have an honest person who does not lose out (the oral version of Lesson 5's 'route of requital')).
:::
:::

::: exercise Rework a telling of history {level=2}
Rewrite a passage of a history book (any one or two hundred characters of the *Comprehensive Mirror in Aid of Governance*) as 200 characters in the tone of 'telling of history'. The requirements: ① the great events do not move; ② supply one scene; ③ supply one motive; ④ add one 'honoured audience' comment. Self-assessment: **of each of your two additions, how many tenths are historical fact, and how many tenths are inference**.
::: solution
Marking: **the list of great events can be checked** (**year, place, names of persons and outcome must not be changed**); **the column of tenths in the self-assessment is the point of this question** (**mark inference as inference:** the honesty of popularisation depends on footnotes, and the storyteller has no footnotes, so today it is you who write them (Chapter 3's 'a sentence with its source given in full carries a premium')).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the room that charged admission {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**Literary history is in the habit of writing the *huaben* as the victory of 'the folk', but the folk is not a place of origin; it is a constraint: money has to be taken, people have to be kept from leaving, and tomorrow there still has to be someone who dares to come. These three constraints explain the shape of vernacular fiction better than any aesthetic programme can — it must make the narrator credible, make time divisible and make morality predictable, and then it occasionally breaks the appointment**". You must draw on: **the three-layered unsettled case of the definition**, **the box office and the return customer**, **the functional chain of entry tale / main tale / next instalment**, **the second audit in *The Carved Jade Guanyin***, **the information asymmetry in *The Wrongful Beheading of Cui Ning***, **the bias of the surviving sample**, at least these six; and you must decide: **whether 'the constraint of charging shapes the genre' is, today, strengthened or has lost its hold — you must take one contemporary form (any one of updates in web fiction, serialised comics, or a short-video serial) as a control experiment, and point out the constraints it shares with the *goulan*, and one key difference**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**unsettled case, box office, grammar, second audit, misjudgement, bias**); the control experiment (**for reference, the strengthening view — a daily update of web fiction and 'hear the next instalment break it down' are both a collecting of money by instalments of time, and the comment section is the cough of the *goulan*; the key difference — the *goulan* has a physical capacity and a refund on the spot, the platform has unlimited stock and sunk costs (the rate of reading to the end has replaced the cough), so the cost structure of 'breaking the appointment' has changed:** the storyteller does not dare to leave a rotten ending, because tomorrow he still has to appear in the same doorway (marking looks at whether 'constraint' has been written as something that can be measured: frequency of update, retention rate, refunds, and not a vague 'the market influences creation')); if the conclusion can be written "**the greatest credit of the room that charged admission is not that it made literature vulgar, but that it forced literature to be something one can rely on: a reader can forgive a genius for being obscure, and can hardly forgive tomorrow's absence**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filing it on the way downstairs: the unsettled case (**base text, record and imitation stand together as three layers; first admit that what we are reading is a joint compilation by three parties**), the box office (**the return customer = an asset of reputation: the audience writes the recommendation with copper cash**), the grammar (**the entry tale previews, the narrator shows himself, the next instalment is paid by stages**), the works (**the ghost marriage is a second audit; the wrongful case is information asymmetry**), the bias (**the ten-odd pieces in the eyes of two sieves: mark yourself down to eight-tenths**), the water level (**one victory, nine hundred years ago, for pronouns and modal particles**). One sentence of method: **first count tonight's tickets, and only then discuss the merits of the writing**. The nine chapters end here: this lesson's route has walked from the banquet of *yànyuè* 燕乐 ('banquet music') to the door of the *washi*; in the next chapter (Yuan, Ming and Qing) we need not go downstairs again — literature has moved into the city on its own.
:::
