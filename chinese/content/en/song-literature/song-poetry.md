---
title: "Song poetry"
---

The balance sheet of the Tang ancestral model (唐宗) is laid out before the eyes: for one thousand four hundred years the poets have all been auditing it — **Song poetry is often written up as a stretch of failure (“有唐诗然后有宋诗” — 'Once there is Tang poetry, only then is there Song poetry.' (Our translation.) — read as “唐诗之后只剩下不如” — 'After Tang poetry, all that is left is falling short.' (Our translation.)); this chapter reads it as a feasible solution: once one kind of perfection has become the standard, those who come after have only two roads left: to reproduce it, or to redefine what may enter a poem**.

## Definition: three assets = three breaches {#def-sanxiang}

::: definition Yan Yu's indictment {#def-yanyu}
 *Canglang shihua* (《沧浪诗话》, *Canglang's Remarks on Poetry*)  (the passage as commonly cited: “唐人以性情为主，宋人以文字为诗，以才学为诗，以议论为诗” — 'Writers of the Tang take temperament and feeling as the chief thing; writers of the Song make poetry by means of words, make poetry by means of talent and learning, make poetry by means of argument.' (Our translation.) — this is the most famous indictment in the history of Chinese literary criticism; this lesson first reads it as a set of accounts: the three breaches are precisely the three assets of Song poetry); ① Making poetry by means of words (turning "how to write" itself into the content of the poem: the polishing of words, the collecting of lines, the matching of rhymes, the embedding of characters, the medicine-name form, the number form — the subjects of poetry expand from "the world" to "poetry itself": this is metacriticism (criticism about poetry) appearing in the form of a poem (Lesson 15, "the self-reference of genres")); ② Making poetry by means of talent and learning (the deployment of allusions, rhymes that have a source, syntax modelled on the ancients — the institutional basis: printing, classified books (*leishu*), academies: for the first time, writers of the Song could practise "retrieval-style writing" (printing is the invisible patron of Song poetry: the possibility of looking things up changed the rhetoric (the technical sequel, in Lesson 1, to transmission by well water)); ③ Making poetry by means of argument (the dialect of the politics of the censors and the remonstrators, and of debate on the meaning of the Classics: the everyday speech of the scholar-official simply is argument; if poetry does not learn it, that is to abandon the true voice of this stratum (a return to Lesson 4, "making the song lyric (*ci*) in the manner of prose": one and the same invasion by grammar, two battlefields). Note who brings the indictment (Yan Yu 严羽 himself did not take office, and his doctrine became current after the fall of the Song — **the indictment became canonical only two hundred years after the case: "the prestige of Tang poetry" is a monument raised jointly by people of the Song and of the Yuan, the Ming and the Qing (Lesson 15, "legislation by recognition after the fact": Yan Yu is one of the lead drafters**.
:::

::: definition Four ways out {#def-sichu}
**① Do what it did not do (an expansion of subject matter)** (tea, ink, sleep, illness, poverty, small children, servants and slaves, the price of rice, monks' lodgings, the reading of collected writings — Mei Yaochen 梅尧臣, 'The Potters' (《陶者》): “陶尽门前土，屋上无片瓦。十指不沾泥，鳞鳞居大厦” — 'The soil before the gate is pottered out; on the roof there is not a tile. Ten fingers never touch the mud; scale upon scale they dwell in great mansions.' (Our translation.) To write, as a poem, what one "would not stoop to write" is a position taken in the market, not a degeneration (an eleventh-century rehearsal of Chapter 4, "doing the part of the accounts that others leave out")); ② Turn learning into technique (the Jiangxi road) (Huang Tingjian 黄庭坚's “点铁成金” ('turning iron into gold') and “夺胎换骨” ('seizing the embryo and changing the bones') (the wording of the letters, on the common account) — not a theory of plagiarism, but a licence protocol for reuse: fit citation with a processing procedure, and from then on "using old materials" and "stealing" have a technical boundary (Chapter 3's theory that legitimises Qingzhen 清真's "dissolving salt into water")); ③ Turn argument into structure (the signpost system of Song poetry) ("because — therefore — however — do not suppose": the turn in the second half of the quatrain (*jueju*) is written so as to resemble the wording of a verdict ('Written on the Wall at Xilin', 《题西林壁》: “不识庐山真面目，只缘身在此山中” — 'I do not know the true face of Mount Lu, only because I am myself inside this mountain.' (Our translation.) — a choice between placing the conclusion first and placing it last; writers of the Song almost all choose the conclusion: because what they want is not an illusion, but a consensus (a breach of the reserve clause in Lesson 12 — *hanxu*, 'meaning held past the words' — and also a repricing of that clause)); ④ Rectify from inside (the living method) (Lü Benzhong 吕本中's theory of the "living method" (*huofa*) (the common account): a patch proposed by the Jiangxi school from within its own house — the "living method" is a rectification of the "dead method": “规矩备具而能出于规矩” — 'The rules are all in place, and yet one can pass beyond the rules.' (Our translation.) (the poetics version of Lesson 2, "learn the procedure, not the mood")).
:::

## Ouyang and Mei: a scheme for dividing the work {#def-oumei}

::: definition The reform of poetry and prose, and 'meaning that is not exhausted' {#def-oumei2}
**Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修, *Liuyi shihua* (《六一诗话》, *Liuyi's Remarks on Poetry*)** (**China's first "remarks on poetry" (*shihua*): poetry discussed in the form of the notebook (*biji*), opening a new subject among the genres of criticism (the file was already opened in Lesson 1)**); **words of Mei Yaochen, “**状难写之景，如在目前；含不尽之意，见于言外**”** (recorded in *Liuyi shihua*, the common account) — 'Describe a scene that is hard to describe, so that it is as if before the eyes; hold a meaning that is not exhausted, so that it appears beyond the words.' (Our translation.) — note who proposed it: the theoretical quota of Song poetry was not all spent on "argument": the defence that writers of the Song made for "reserve" is finer even than Tang poetry's; they merely placed it in the remarks on poetry, and not in the poems (a self-aware division of labour among genres: poetry is responsible for stating the principle; remarks on poetry are responsible for giving an account of why one does not state the principle)); what Ouyang and Mei in fact achieved (the first wave of the turn to ancient-style prose (*guwen*), of writing in the manner of prose, and of making poetry in the manner of prose (*yi wen wei shi*): they remade the perilous strangeness of Han Yu 韩愈 into the everyday of the Song manner: to change “奇” ('the extraordinary') into “常” ('the ordinary') is a very inconspicuous revolution (the extraordinary is easy to imitate; the ordinary is extremely hard: the maturity of Song poetry lies exactly in its knowing how to write a morning on which "nothing happens")).
:::

## The Jiangxi school of poetry: a scene of founding the school {#def-jiangxi}

::: definition School, genealogy, and 'emerging from the embryo' {#def-tuotai}
**The *Chart of the Jiangxi School* (《江西宗派图》)** (records by people of the Song give a list of one line of eighteen persons (the common account; Xu Fu 徐俯, Hong Chu 洪刍 and others are named): this is the literary event of the Song with the strongest sense of organisation: a genealogical chart drawn from the ties of teachers and friends (Huang Tingjian as the ancestor, later disciples as the school (Lesson 15, "a school = a map after the fact"; the Jiangxi school is one of the few cases of "having the map beforehand")); Huang Tingjian, 'Sent to Huang Jifu' (《寄黄几复》): “桃李春风一杯酒，江湖夜雨十年灯” — 'Peach and plum, spring wind, one cup of wine; rivers and lakes, night rain, ten years of lamp.' (Our translation.) — the two lines set nouns side by side, and almost give no verb: two slides of memory, the blank between them filled by the ten years (the most minimal version of the triple-stacked tense in Chapter 4; also the high point, in Song poetry, of the "juxtaposition of images" (*yixiang*) in Lesson 12)); 'Climbing Kuai Pavilion' (《登快阁》): “朱弦已为佳人绝，青眼聊因美酒横” — 'The vermilion strings have already been cut for the fair one; the dark of the eye, for the while, turns level for fine wine.' (Our translation.) — two allusions packed into the parallelism (Boya 伯牙, Ruan Ji 阮籍): allusion is not ornament but compression: one layer of allusion = one line of monologue left out (Lesson 7, "to cite the Classics and pile on allusions is to borrow, and the debt must be repaid": Huang Tingjian can repay the debt, because he requires that the reader, too, be able to recite from memory (this is the true threshold of the Jiangxi school: it raises the reader's qualification along with the rest).
:::

::: definition Houshan and 'poetry from poverty' {#def-houshan}
**Chen Shidao 陈师道** (the practical wing of Huang Tingjian's method in poetry; his family was poor, he resigned office, and he froze to death in an incident over court dress (the common account: he went in the winter procession of insignia, obtained clothes, and died — plain poverty was written into the curriculum vitae, and so “穷而后工” ('skilful only after being poor') is no longer rhetoric (Ouyang Xiu, 'Preface to the Collected Poems of Mei Shengyu' (《梅圣俞诗集序》), “愈穷则愈工”, the common account — 'The poorer, the more skilful.' (Our translation.)): writers of the Song packaged a sociological cause and effect as a law of aesthetics (Lesson 15, the case of translating "mechanism → value" across)); the common account: his own report of composition by bitter toil (*kuyin*; records of the kind “诗句一年得数联”, 'a few couplets of verse got in a year') and the inferior latter current of the Jiangxi school, the current of "seeking lines behind a shut door" (the lines of Yuan Haowen 元好问, commonly cited: “闭门觅句陈无己，对客挥毫秦少游” — 'Behind a shut door, seeking lines: Chen Wuji 陈无己; facing a guest, sweeping the brush: Qin Shaoyou 秦少游.' (Our translation.)) — Yuan Haowen's contrast is a lesson in method: seeking lines and sweeping the brush are two systems of production (the former relies on a stock of time, the latter on a flow at the scene), not two grades of genius (the positive model, in Chapter 9, of a bad review given to a contrast-set.
:::

## The living method and Chengzhai: an internal revolution {#def-huofa}

::: definition Yang Wanli's 'suddenly, as if he understood' {#def-yangwanli}
**His own account of three changes in learning poetry** (**first he studied the Jiangxi school, then he studied the late Tang, and at last “**忽若有悟” ('suddenly it was as if he understood') and he gave them up (the common account, the line of self-accounts in 'Chengzhai's Own Preface', 《诚斋自序》) — what the "understanding" contains is in fact very plain: put the eyes back on the scene; skip the nature that sits in the piles of books, and go and observe directly — the antonym of "seeking lines behind a shut door" is "opening the door to look at the flowers": the antidote to the Jiangxi school comes, surprisingly, from the road in Song poetry that is least regarded: direct observation (the Song-poetry version of the writing in Lesson 12)); 'The Little Pond' (《小池》): “小荷才露尖尖角，早有蜻蜓立上头” — 'The little lotus has only just put out its sharp tip; already a dragonfly stands upon it.' (Our translation.) — and 'Leaving Jingci Temple at Dawn to See Lin Zifang Off' (《晓出净慈寺送林子方》): “接天莲叶无穷碧，映日荷花别样红” — 'Lotus leaves meet the sky, a green without end; lotus flowers in the sun, a red of another kind.' (Our translation.) — the technique of the "living line": write the movement of an instant as one meeting of thing with thing (the strongest moments of Song poetry are often not where it reasons the most, but in the half-second in which it forgets to reason (Lesson 3: the exception in which the conclusion is placed last is the good poem); the reputation of the "Chengzhai style" (accessible, humorous, good at mocking oneself: to change "the solemn poet" into "an interesting old man" is the literary conversion of the late-life character of the Song scholar-official (the sister-operation of Lesson 4, "writing unemployment into the sobriquet": writing old age as a comic bit).
:::

::: example Two politics of the same fruit {#ex-lizhi}
Du Mu 杜牧, 'Passing Huaqing Palace' (《过华清宫》): “一骑红尘妃子笑，无人知是荔枝来” — 'A single rider, red dust; the consort smiles; no one knows that it is the lychees that come.' (Our translation.) And Su Shi 苏轼, 'Eating Lychees' (《食荔枝》): “日啖荔枝三百颗，不辞长作岭南人” — 'Three hundred lychees to eat each day, and I would not refuse to be a man of Lingnan for good.' (Our translation.) Bai Juyi 白居易, 'Preface to the Picture of the Lychee' (《荔枝图序》): “若离本枝，一日而色变，二日而香变，三日而味变” — 'Once they leave their own branch, in one day the colour changes, in two days the fragrance changes, in three days the taste changes.' (Our translation.) Three writings on the lychee: point out **the post each poet holds** (remonstrating official / banished minister / investigator of things), and explain **why the same fruit is proof of a crime in Tang poetry and a consolation in Song poetry**.
::: solution
**① Du Mu: the remonstrating official's shot** (“一骑红尘” ('a single rider, red dust') set beside “妃子笑” ('the consort smiles'); not one word of judgement, and the judgement is already finished — the satire of Tang poetry relies on montage, and on the reader passing the verdict himself (reserve is firepower: the blank that is left is loaded by the reader's anger)); ② Su Shi: the banished minister's rescue of himself (“不辞长作岭南人” — 'I would not refuse to be a man of Lingnan for good.' (Our translation.) — writing the special product of the place of exile as something the human world can count worth having: this is changing the title of an accident (the clause in Lesson 2): the argument of Song poetry is here not a matter of reasoning, but of valuing anew); ③ Bai Juyi: the manual of the investigator of things (colour, fragrance and taste, three changes in three days: first make a record of observation; on this matter poetry and prose have not yet divided the household — this line explains Song poetry exactly: that the Song wrote "exposition" into poetry is the natural extension of this prose habit of the mid-Tang (the remote ancestors of making poetry in the manner of prose are in Han and Bai, not in the Song (the "breach" of Song poetry in fact holds Tang registration)); the overall verdict — the fruit does not change; the post does: in Tang poetry the poet stands opposite the empire (satirising, 讽); in Song poetry the poet stands inside his own situation (settling there, 处): from the position of critique to the position of bearing it — this is the real content of the difference between Tang and Song — not a higher or lower level, but a migration of the seat from which one speaks.
:::
:::

::: intuition The view of time of two poems {#int-shijian}
**“诗到宋换了一种时态”** — 'When poetry reaches the Song, it changes tense.' (Our translation.) (Tang poetry prefers "for ever" (“此恨绵绵无绝期” — 'This regret stretches on and on, and has no term at which it ends.' (Our translation.)); Song poetry prefers "today" (who shares the cup today; rain last night; leaving Jingci Temple at dawn) — Song poetry is literature in the form of a diary: it gives up the eternal and re-signs as a long-term contract: a whole life written out as ninety-four volumes (advance notice of the chapter on Lu You in Chapter 7)); the cost of "making poetry by means of talent and learning" (retrieval-style writing brings a density of intertextuality, and it also brings a class-stratification of the threshold the reader must cross: the ideal reader of the Jiangxi school is a person who can likewise retrieve — the flourishing of the genre and the shrinking of the community happen at the same time (the reverse of "the dividend of elegance" in Lesson 15: the song lyrics of the cultivated retainer and the poems of the scholar-official both raise the unit price in a shrinking market — note: this is a change in distribution, not a change in value).
:::

::: history Four testimonies of Song poetry {#sec-zhengci}
**1040s—1070s** Ouyang and Mei (the joint name “苏梅” ('Su and Mei'): a joint name is the way people of the Song made public opinion (people of the Song had already turned the contrast “苏豪梅瘦” ('Su bold, Mei lean') into the marketing of a persona; the thread of the common account)); 1070s—1100s the joint name Su–Huang, and Huang Tingjian founding a school (“苏黄” ('Su–Huang') and “苏辛” ('Su–Xin') were alike bound into one set by later ages (Lesson 4 has already broken this once) — the difference between Su and Huang is finer than that between Su and Xin: Su Shi is innate gift and argument; Huang Tingjian is technique and rules (the former anyone can appreciate; the latter requires training: one sells a finished product, the other sells a course)); 1100s the *Chart of the Jiangxi School* (a consciousness of genealogy: poetic schools of the Song have rolls of names: the roll is the standard of marking (the organisational version of the discussion of anthologies in Lesson 1)); 1130s—1190s the Four Masters of the Revival and the "living method" (the dispute over the order of Zeng Ji 曾几, Lü Benzhong, You Mao 尤袤, Yang Wanli 杨万里, Fan Chengda 范成大 and Lu You 陆游 is itself the self-awareness of Song poetry: a tradition that can quarrel inside itself shows that it has already taken its own methodology as an object of study (people of the Song compiled a history of criticism on their own behalf: this is a hard indicator of literary maturity)); 1240s *Canglang shihua* (legislation that takes the High Tang as its model: when Song poetry had already existed for two hundred years, it needed an outside by which to judge itself: Yan Yu supplied that outside, and in passing produced the concept "Tang poetry" (Lesson 15: a classic recognised after the fact is stronger than a classic as it was written).
:::

::: warning Four traps in Song poetry
**The stock phrase “宋诗不如唐诗”** — 'Song poetry is not as good as Tang poetry.' (Our translation.) (measuring people of the Song with a ruler that people of the Song themselves cast (*Canglang*) — the standard of evaluation itself has a pedigree: first ask who made this ruler, when, and for what (Lesson 15, "taste is legislation": the Tang ancestral model is a policy of the Southern Song through the Ming, not an eternal law of nature)); Treating “理趣” (*lǐqù*, 'the savour of a principle') as exempt from inspection (to reason is not to be good: the worst specimens of Song poetry are conclusions broken into lines (the criterion: whether the principle of the line can be drawn out by the image itself (the principle of 'Written on the Wall at Xilin' grows out of observing the mountain; the principle of "poems of nature and principle" is carried over from recorded sayings))); Treating allusion as a contest of learning (reading the Jiangxi school by checking only the source and not the function — the source is a question for the dictionary; the function is the poem (Lesson 4, "ask the function first: ammunition, precedent, a talisman of acquittal")); Ignoring the difficulty of subjects on which "nothing happens" (reading Song poetry and faulting it as trivial: to write one sleepless night takes more technique than to write a battle, because a battle brings its meaning with it, while sleeplessness has to earn its own (this is the most important gift of Song poetry to later ages: the everyday gained a capacity to be poetry — modern poetry owes it a signature fee).
:::

::: exercise An acceptance test for *liqu* {level=1}
Take any one line of "Song poetry that states a principle" and any one modern maxim broken into lines, and make an acceptance table: **can the principle of this line be drawn out by the image itself** (list the images); if it cannot, mark the items that are missing.
::: solution
Marking: **if the list of images is empty, judge it a "conclusion broken into lines"** (**then set it against 'Written on the Wall at Xilin': observation of the mountain → the limit of knowledge, with one step of reasoning between them that can be restated (the check by the "chain of mechanism" in the induction of Lesson 5)**); **samples of the modern maxim follow the same rule** (**the aim of the exercise is to gain a skill for life: recognising false profundity (the poetry version of the fake profundity in Lesson 10)**).

::: exercise A Jiangxi-school rewriting {level=2}
Take one line of plain expression of feeling that you have written (for example “我很想你”, 'I miss you very much') and, by the procedure of "turning iron into gold", rewrite it as two lines: ① borrow a trace of one earlier writer's phrasing (you may note the source); ② remake one part of its structure; ③ the verb of the original meaning must not appear.
::: solution
Marking: **the source can be identified** (**"a change with no source" is only rewriting: the core of the Jiangxi method is to leave a trace of the processing that can be checked (intertextuality is collaboration, not shame)**); **what is accepted is the extent of the remaking** (**to change only the words = collecting lines; only when the structure of the reasoning has been changed is it called seizing the embryo (the operational version of dissolving salt into water in Chapter 3)**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: a feasible solution {level=3}
In six hundred to one thousand words, assess “**宋诗的全部困境与全部荣誉是同一句话：唐诗太成功了。成功是一种封锁：它规定了什么算好，直到有人决定重写这条规定——宋人于是把学问、议论、日常与关于诗本身的思考都写进诗里，代价是被后世骂了两百年，收益是中国文学从此有了第二种成熟**” — '**The whole predicament of Song poetry, and the whole of its honour, are one and the same sentence: Tang poetry succeeded too well. Success is a blockade: it lays down what counts as good, until someone decides to rewrite this rule — writers of the Song then wrote learning, argument, the everyday, and thinking about poetry itself, all into the poem. The cost was to be reviled by later ages for two hundred years; the gain was that Chinese literature from then on had a second kind of maturity.**' (Our translation.) You must draw on at least six items: **the double reading of Yan Yu's indictment**, **the four ways out**, **the licence protocol of turning iron into gold**, **the rectification by the living method**, **the difficulty of subjects on which "nothing happens"**, **the migration of the seat (Tang satirises, 讽; Song settles, 处)**; and give a ruling: **in the case of Song poetry, is "the anxiety of influence" a driving force or an excuse — you must at the same time hand a verdict to Yang Wanli (he gained by opposing the Jiangxi school) and to Huang Tingjian (he gained by establishing the Jiangxi school), and explain why the two verdicts do not contradict each other**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (indictment, ways out, protocol, patch, difficulty, seat); the ruling (for reference: they do not contradict, because the object of the "anxiety" may be an earlier age, and it may also be one's own age: Huang Tingjian is anxious at the success of Tang poetry, and therefore develops linguistic resources inward; Yang Wanli is anxious at the success of the Jiangxi school, and therefore develops the resources of the scene outward (the same mechanism changes direction once it changes its opponent: **the real pressure of tradition does not come from the Tang period, but from the thirty years just past (“影响的对象总是最近的教科书” — 'The object of influence is always the most recent textbook.' (Our translation.): this explains why every dynasty both restores antiquity and innovates — restoring antiquity is rhetoric, innovation is finance (Lesson 15: to borrow money from antiquity is the cheapest financing**); if the conclusion can be written as “**每一个被奉为巅峰的时代，都是后来者替它办的追悼会：宋诗没有参加唐的葬礼，它只是另立了户口**” — '**Every age that is revered as a summit is a memorial service which those who come after hold for it: Song poetry did not attend the funeral of the Tang; it only registered a household of its own.**' (Our translation.), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The feasible solution, filed: the indictment (**three breaches = three assets; first ask the pedigree of the ruler**), the ways out (**subject matter, learning, argument, the living method**), Jiangxi (**a licence protocol for reuse, plus a threshold on the reader's qualification**), Chengzhai (**open the door and look at the flowers: the strongest moment of Song poetry is the half-second in which it forgets to reason**), the seat (**moved from opposite the empire into one's own situation**). One sentence of method: **do not ask why Song poetry is not as good as Tang poetry; ask what problems it solved that Tang poetry did not meet**. Next chapter: after the crossing to the south — Lu You, Yang Wanli, Fan Chengda and Liu Kezhuang 刘克庄.
