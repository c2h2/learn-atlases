title: "Song prose"
---

The prose movement of the previous course is usually ended with a name — Ouyang Xiu — and a claim — that it succeeded. **This chapter treats the success as an institutional mechanism: the ancient-style essay won because it was adopted as the grading standard of the examination, and what that did to Chinese argument is the story, including two essays that show the technique at its best and one that shows it at its worst.**

## The institutional victory {#def-zhidu}

::: definition Winning on the marking scheme {#def-pingfen}
**The standard account**: in the 1050s a man hostile to the "elegant" recent style was in charge of a provincial or metropolitan examination round, and the candidates who wrote plain, pointed prose in the ancient manner were passed; the reform of taste took one cycle and was never reversed. **Read the event as a mechanism rather than as a verdict. ① A style wins when it becomes the criterion, not when it becomes the fashion**: the examiner does not have to be right, only consistent, and consistency in marking is a force no manifesto can answer. ② **The candidates' adaptation was rational**: an essay that states a thesis, gives three grounds and closes with a policy recommendation is easier to rank than an allusive parallel composition, so both sides gained from the new norm — **the standard of an examination shapes the syntax of a literature, which is the applied-writing course's claim three centuries early**. ③ **The residue is visible today**: the five-paragraph habit, the demand to "state your point first", the distrust of ornament in school prose, are the sediment of that marking reform. **This lesson's rule: when you admire a period's clarity, ask first who was grading.**
:::

::: definition A list of genres is a list of career skills {#def-qingdan}
**The standard account**: prose of the period is classified into memorial, letter, preface, "record" (记), discussion (论), inscription, elegy, and history commentary, and every writer of note leaves samples of most. **The list is a skill chart, and each item has an owner in real life: the memorial is for office, the letter is for faction, the preface is for the anthology, the record is for a rebuilt hall, the elegy is for a colleague's family.** **Three consequences. ① Genre determined what could be said and in what person**: a private grievance is sayable in a letter and unsayable in a memorial, which is why biography has to read the two together. ② **The best essays are genre violations**: the "record" of a mountain that becomes an argument about method, the reply letter that refuses to reply, the preface that argues with the dead author. ③ **The canon is a curriculum of these violations**, which is why a textbook's table of contents looks like a list of exceptions — **and why any student who asks "why is this famous" should be told: because it used the wrong form and got away with it.**
:::

## Anatomy of four model essays {#def-jiepou}

::: definition "On Parties": a fight over a definition {#def-pengdang}
**The standard account**: a defence of the word "party" was written when opponents were accusing reformers of league and faction, and the essay's move is to accept the word and split it — there is a gentleman's party and a petty man's party, and only the first is a party in the sense that matters (**standard account**: it is addressed to a throne, and its argument is a definition). **The technique in three steps. ① Do not contest the accusation; contest the criterion**: the reader is invited to agree that association for benefit is condemnable, which costs the author nothing, and then to notice that the test of benefit has been substituted for the test of loyalty. **② History is used as a database**: dynasties fell when the ruler could not tell the two parties apart — a claim that cannot be falsified and does not need to be, since the examples are selected in front of a reader who shares the canon. ③ **The conclusion flatters the addressee into the role of judge**, which is the rhetorical equivalent of a testable prediction: if you accept the distinction, you have already chosen a side. **This chapter's verdict: it is a superb essay and a bad argument, and both facts matter — the course's oldest rule, that a definition is an allocation, is here practised at the highest level** (the argumentative-writing course's "definition-power is distribution-power").
:::

::: definition "On the Six Kingdoms": the portrait of an argument {#def-liuguo}
**The standard account**: an essayist of the mid-eleventh century argued that the six states perished not through inferior arms but through the policy of conceding territory, and the piece is taught as the model of Chinese argumentative prose. **Take it apart as a specimen of the genre's possibilities and its vices. ① The thesis is deliberately overstated** — the states did not in fact all fall for that reason, and the author knows it; **the point of a 论 is not a balanced account but a single blade pushed as far as it will go** (**standard account**: the genre's convention licenses the extreme). **② The evidence is arranged by function, not by date**: concession, then the consequence, then the failure to try the alternative, then the present-day sting — and the final paragraph, which turns to the reader's own court, is the reason the essay was written. **③ It is an essay about the present wearing a historical costume**, which was the only safe form for its argument; **this is not a defect of Song prose but one of its two great techniques, the other being the refusal to wear the costume.** **Warning for the student who imitates it: the genre's confidence requires a shared canon and an addressee who can be addressed indirectly; imitated in a modern seminar it reads as special pleading, which is why the modern essay-course insists on a stated criterion instead.**
:::

::: definition "Reply to Minister Sima": a short answer to a long letter {#def-sima}
**The standard account**: a chief councillor answered a long remonstrance from an opponent with a very short letter, and the letter's method is to decline the substance. **Three moves worth learning. ① The terms are restated in the author's own frame** — the accusations (adding office, rejecting the old ways, seeking profit, defying opinion) are re-described as ordinary administration, **which is the definitional struggle again, run in the defensive**. **② The volume of disagreement is converted into a statement about obligation**: "your letter had to be answered, and here is the reason for brevity" — a refusal that pays the cost of courtesy in advance. ③ **The final sentence offers no reconciliation**, and the essay is famous for that: **it is a document whose purpose is to end a debate by refusing the terms on which it was opened** (**standard account**: contemporaries read it as a declaration of policy as much as a reply). **The chapter's observation: the technique of declining to argue on the opponent's ground is legitimate and dangerous; in the hands of a man with the emperor it is called principle, and in the hands of a man without one it is called arrogance, which is a lesson in how little rhetoric can do without a constituency.**
:::

::: definition "Record of a Trip to Baochan Mountain": travel as self-audit {#def-baochan}
**The standard account**: a travel record recounts a half-failed descent into a cave, notes that the writer stopped because others stopped, and generalises: with the means to go in, one should not emerge by following the timid; **and if one has done one's best and still failed, one cannot be laughed at**. **Why the piece is the best short essay in the language, in three points. ① The evidence is a trivial event**: a muddy hole, a borrowed torch, a few inscribed stones — **and the argument is built from what the writer did rather than from what the world is**, which is the "record" genre pushed toward ethics. **② The structure is an audit**: means, will, others, and the question of blame are listed like a case file, and the moral is extracted item by item (**the chapter's argument that Song prose's real inheritance is procedural — see the intuition below**). ③ **The turn from cave to scholarship is honest but not quite earned**: the writer moves from "do not stop early" to "careful inquiry" without noticing the gap between effort and method. **That gap is the essay's most useful feature: it shows a thinker performing a conclusion, and it is where a teacher can catch a student learning to argue.**
:::

## Reversal, verification, and entries {#def-fanan}

::: definition The reversal essay as an audit of sympathy {#def-fanan2}
**The standard account**: a genre of very short essays takes a celebrated historical figure and overturns the common judgment — the famous four-sentence dismantling of a lord famous for attracting talent is the model (**standard account**: it is a 读 history-reading piece, and its brevity is the point). **The method in three moves: ① locate the ground of the sympathy — the man is praised because he saved a friend in danger; ② show that the ground is a smaller fact than the claim made on it — what he saved was one body, while a thousand mouths went unaided; ③ conclude that the praise was earned by the wrong deed.** **Three cautions. The reversal depends on the canon: without a settled sympathetic reading, there is nothing to overturn, so the genre is a symptom of a culture with an established past** (the previous chapter's "writing after"). **It is unfalsifiable in the ordinary case: the essayist selects the one standard against which the hero fails.** And **it teaches a real skill anyway — auditing your own sympathy for its grounds, which is what the critical reader does all day** (**the logic course's steel-man and death-floor tests, run on a historical figure**).
:::

::: definition The empirical spirit: "Record of the Stone Bell Mountain" {#def-shizhong}
**The standard account**: a writer who doubted a place-name's etymology went at night by boat beneath the cliffs, heard the water strike the hollow stone, and wrote a record ending with a rhetorical question about whether it is acceptable to decide that something exists or not without hearing and seeing it for oneself. **Three things to note. ① The move is genuinely a test in the philosophical sense: the writer identifies a competing explanation (a scholar's theory), arranges an observation that discriminates, and reports the result — with one honest admission that the observation itself was strange and frightening** (**standard account**: the piece is usually read as an argument against bookish inference). **② The conclusion is about method and not about the name**: what is attacked is inference without access — the same sentence the academic-writing course spends ten chapters on. ③ **And yet: later scholars found that the sound can be explained in other ways, and one of them pointed out that the author's own night experiment was conducted at a season and water level that do not generalise** (**standard account**: the record has its own critics). **So the piece is a masterpiece of the empirical gesture and a warning about a single observation — a pair of claims this course has never managed to separate.**
:::

::: definition Entry form: narrative without a plot {#def-tiaomu}
**The standard account**: alongside the essay, Song literati produced notebooks of entries — technical observations, philology, anecdotes, administrative detail, and one famous book of a polymath's findings which records things like movable type, optical and magnetic phenomena, and construction methods (**standard account**: the Shen Kuo volume is the case usually cited). **Why the form matters: it has no thesis, no arc and no addressee, and therefore it can record what an essay could not use. ① Its ordering is alphabetical-by-accident or thematic-by-mood, so a reader can enter anywhere — the opposite of the essay, whose whole power is that it goes somewhere.** ② **Its authority comes from specificity**, not from argument: measurements, quantities, materials and the name of the person who did it, which is why modern historians of technology treat it as a source and literary historians treat it as a style. ③ **It is the literature's equivalent of the search log: an instrument against forgetting, and it is better at preserving failures than any essay is.** **The lesson generalises from the criticism course: the value of a genre lies in its accumulation and correction mechanisms rather than in the quality of individual items — and the entry form, with its lack of arc, is the strongest accumulator in the period.**
:::

::: example A complaint and a definitional debate {#ex-liangpian}
Two texts (**invented for this lesson, in the shape of real ones**). **A**: "Since the new water tax was imposed, the households of the eastern market have decreased by a third; the port that once took four hundred boats now takes fewer than a hundred and fifty; and the reason is that the levy is collected before the goods are sold, so that a merchant must borrow at any price." **B**: "What is called a tax is that by which the ruler nourishes the people; what the present levy lacks is not the amount but the name, for a levy that precedes the sale is not a tax but a fine." **Identify each text's genre, its standard of proof, and the one move that makes B stronger and weaker than A at the same time.**

::: solution
**A is a memorial's evidence section: quantities, a comparison, a mechanism, and no claim about principles; its standard is that a reader could check the numbers at the office.** **B is a definitional debate: it moves the question from the amount to the name, and its standard is the canon of what a tax ought to be.** **The single move: B attacks the category, so it does not have to argue about the numbers A supplied — which is why B is stronger (it escapes A's evidence entirely) and weaker (it can be answered by anyone who shows that the canon permits a pre-sale levy, and because it cannot be implemented: the name has no column in the ledger).** **A memorial that stops at B loses; one that stops at A loses the argument about legitimacy; and the successful Song argument is the pair, which is why the period's model essays are so often structured as evidence-then-principle.** **Marking: whoever says that B is the kind of move that wins at court and loses in administration takes an A** — and that observation is the chapter's politics in one sentence.
:::
:::

::: intuition Procedural rationality {#int-chengxu}
**The chapter's overall claim about Song prose: its enduring contribution is not a style but a set of procedures for arguing.** Five of them, each visible in the essays above: **① state the point first** (the examination norm); **② separate the ground from the accusation** (definition work); **③ list the conditions of a judgment** (the cave audit's means, will, others); **④ test by observation when observation is possible** (the stone bell); **⑤ admit the limitation and record it** (the notebook and the essay's closing doubt). **Why this matters more than beauty: these are the ancestors of the modern paper's method section, and the lineage runs through the examination system rather than through Europe** (**standard account**: the claim about direct descent is not made; the claim about functional analogy is). **And the accompanying risk, which the essays also show: procedure can be used to make a weak thesis look audited** — a list of conditions is not the same as a check of them, and "on the three grounds above" is still the commonest fake in official prose. **The rule for your own reading: count the procedures that were actually performed, not the ones announced**, which is the same arithmetic the academic-writing course applies to a literature review.
:::

::: history A timeline of Song prose {#sec-shijian2}
**Late tenth to early eleventh century**: voices call for a return to plain prose against the balanced fashion; the model is the eighth-century masters, discovered by reading rather than by inheritance (**standard account**). **1050s**: the examination reform and the circle around Ouyang Xiu; the new generation — his pupils and their pupils — fill the offices and the anthologies (**standard account**). **1060s–1080s**: the great age of the essay, and with it the factional letter and the political 论; **the greatest prose style of the century is also the century's most effective political weapon** (**standard account**: several of these essays were read by contemporaries as policy documents, and some of their authors were punished by the same mechanism that had rewarded others). **Twelfth century**: the commentaries and the school anthologies fix the pieces as models, and a school of prose technique begins with rules about how to open, turn and close (**standard account**). **Thirteenth century**: the examination's new orthodoxy installs a commentary school as the state's reading list (**standard account**). **Fourteenth to eighteenth centuries**: later "ancient prose" schools re-run the same texts as a canon, and the period's essays become exercises in a method (**the criticism course's "a lineage is built backwards"**). **The reading: prose became literature by becoming a syllabus, and the syllabus is why we still read these pieces in the order this chapter has just followed — a sequence invented by examiners.**
:::

::: warning Four traps in this chapter
**Reading a 论 as history** (**its licence is the single blade**); **praising clarity without asking who graded** (**the marking mechanism**); **mistaking an announced procedure for a performed one** (**count what was checked**); **treating the notebook as inferior literature** (**no arc, hence no forgetting**).

::: exercise Run a reversal {level=2}
Choose a figure your own field praises and write a four-sentence reversal: locate the ground of the sympathy, show it is smaller than the claim, conclude, then write the sentence that would refute you.
::: solution
Marking: **the refuting sentence is compulsory**, and the exercise fails without it — a reversal that cannot be answered is rhetoric, not method (the reversal essay).

::: exercise Essay: procedure and its fakes {level=3}
In six hundred to a thousand words, discuss the claim that **the Song essayists invented a Chinese version of arguing-by-rule, and their inheritance is ambiguous in exactly the way all procedural inheritance is: a rule that lets a weak claim be defeated also lets a weak claim be dressed. Every technique in this chapter can be used to audit or to simulate an audit, and the difference is visible only to a reader who knows what would have to be checked.** Deploy at least six of: **the marking mechanism**, **genres as career skills**, **the definitional struggle**, **the 论's licence**, **the refusal to argue on the opponent's ground**, **the cave audit**, **the empirical gesture and its limits**, **the entry form**, **the five procedures**. Then adjudicate: **is teaching argument through model essays better or worse than teaching it through criteria, and what does the choice assume about the student?**

::: solution
Marking: six mechanisms; the adjudication (**state the assumption about the student**). **Models** teach tone, pacing and the feel of a turn, and they assume the student will imitate and then depart — **the assumption is apprenticeship, and its risk is that the student learns the costume** (which is exactly what the eighteenth-century manuals produced). **Criteria** are teachable, checkable and transferable, and they assume the student can act on a rule before acquiring a taste — **the risk is prose that passes every rule and moves nobody, which is what your department's least-read papers are made of**. **Highest marks for the composite answer: teach the model, then teach the criterion that the model satisfies, then ask the student to break it — which is what the four essays above allow, since each one violates its own genre in the way the chapter marks; and the assumption is that a student can learn rules fastest from a text that has already broken them.**
:::
