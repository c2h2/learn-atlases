title: "Looking at calligraphy"
---

The nine chapters have come to the last. What is issued is not a diploma, but **a pair of eyes**. The skill of writing characters, trained in the hand, is brought up to ten; the skill of looking can recognise it for you as a hundred — because, in this art, **looking is the forerunner of writing; collecting, judging, the practice of copying, and aesthetic judgement are all staked on the ticket of 'being able to see'**.

## Five questions for reading a model {#def-wuwen}

::: definition Five questions for reading a model {#def-duwu}
Facing any work of calligraphy, do not begin by looking at the exhibition label. Do five minutes of **structured face-to-face**, and ask five things: **① what was written** (the text: a transcription, and the use — a draft, a letter, a stele or an epitaph, a plaque or a couplet, a contract — **the genre of the text decides its decorum**); **② what script** (seal, clerical, regular, running and cursive, and the mixed gears among them — the proportion of running to cursive is this work's **speedometer**); **③ how it was written** (read the lines as a **video recording**: the reverse entry and the forward entry of the opening stroke; in the middle section of the moving stroke, the fullness and the thinning, the evenness and the vulgarity; the pause and the release of the closing stroke; **the dry and the wet of the ink colour ＝ a timestamp** — the ink gradation within one stroke is one breath, and how many times the brush is dipped within one column is an edit point); **④ why it looks good** (translate 'feeling' into the language of structure: the central palace (中宫), the centre of gravity (重心), yielding (揖让), the *qi* of the column (行气), the distribution of white (布白) — **a general drill in the terminology of the whole course**); **⑤ whether looking good equals being good** (the last question is left to the testimony of character and to the documents of an age — the value of the *Draft of a Memorial to My Nephew* (《祭侄稿》) is not 'beauty', but **the sob in the lines**; some characters look good by design, and some look good by **survival**). When the five questions have been gone through, only then does looking **turn from consumption into conversation**.
:::

::: definition Three layers of looking: composition, the single character, line quality {#def-sanceng}
**Look from a distance at the composition** (from three metres away, look at **the distribution of black and white**: whether the columns are straight or slanted, where groups of characters break and join, the position of the signature inscription and the seals — **the *qi* of the column is the traffic conditions of the characters**; in the *Orchid Pavilion Preface* (《兰亭序》), the *qi* of the column is, contrary to expectation, clearest where the writing has been blotted and corrected — **that is the original recording of a breath**); **look from close at the single character** (thirty centimetres: the framework (间架), the yielding, the dominant stroke — the study of the framework is cashed in here); **look microscopically at the line quality** (within ten centimetres, or in a high-definition reproduction: **the fullness of the middle section is the medical report on brush method** — pretty at both ends and sunken in the middle = a copyist's hand; the plain-language verdict on line quality is six characters: **薄、死、滑／厚、活、涩** ('thin, dead, slippery / thick, alive, resistant') — the 'roof-leak trace' (屋漏痕: a rain-trace winds down a wall, lingering rather than slipping), 'an awl drawing in sand' (锥画沙: the ridges of sand rise of themselves on both sides — the force is in the middle), and the other mnemonics drawn from looking at things, are all **teaching touch to the eye**).
:::

::: example Looking at one work in three minutes {#ex-sanfenzhong}
You are given a photograph of a running-cursive hanging scroll with no exhibition label (fictional). Within three minutes produce **an impression of two hundred characters**. The requirement: use at least two items from each of the three-layer way of looking and the five questions. Four all-purpose empty words are forbidden: 大气, 有气势, 行云流水, 漂亮 ('grand air', 'full of momentum', 'moving clouds and flowing water', 'pretty').
::: solution
**Specimen answer**: "Far more white than black; the axes of the columns sway left and right but do not break (composition seen from a distance: the traffic is a country lane, not a motorway); in the third column there is one obvious point where the brush was dipped, and the ink gradation runs from flooded to dry along the whole of one long vertical — **the speedometer shows a sprint here** (line quality + timestamp); the vertical hook of the character 事 stores a crouch and then kicks out, on the same path as the trailing thread from the previous character, and the stroke order conforms (character method); throughout the piece the central palace is sometimes tight and sometimes loose — where it is tight one sees the manner of Ou 欧, where it is loose it comes near the method of Dong 董 — **the catalogue of styles has read out two phrases of dialect** (the language of structure). Where it falls short: the characters of the signature are smaller than the main text, and faster, and the line quality turns thin — **evidence of a courtesy piece finished in a rush** (this item does not take marks off the ancients; it only asks you to notice: famous works, too, have their moments of fatigue)." Marking: the empty words occur zero times; after every description **a bracket of evidence follows** — the aesthetic grammar of this lesson: **"I feel" must be followed by "because, look"**.
:::
:::

::: intuition Looking while copying a model: three gears in which the eye precedes the hand {#int-lin}
Copying a model is not photocopying; it is **a handshake in the dark across time and space** — so looking is divided into three gears: **copying facing the model** (对临: the model on the left, the characters on the right; **one glance and one stroke is shutter practice** — what is trained is 'the temporary store of the eye'; the advanced requirement: **look at the whole character and write it closed-book**, the muscles storing it for two seconds); **copying from memory** (背临: close the model and write from memory — **a test of whether you have really looked**; every self-deception of 'looking like the model' shows its true shape here); **creating by gathering** (集创: with the characters in the model, gather a couplet or gather a poem; characters outside the model are **made by the method of the model** — **the only legitimate passage from copying to creation**: the bank of characters is one you have saved up yourself). Two accompanying methods of the eye: **reading the model comes first** (before the brush moves, read the route in silence — **the upper limit of hand speed = eye speed**); **comparison comes afterwards** (when the writing is finished, **play it back overlapped** against the original model: a phone photograph laid on semi-transparently is the modern person's demon-revealing mirror — **the list of differences is tomorrow's model**). When 'looking' has been trained until it can **predict** the way the brush will go (the next stroke has not yet landed, and you already know whether it will be square or round), copying a model counts as **having entered the gate** — the view inside the gate: a character is no longer a shape; it is **a string of decisions you can recognise**.
:::

::: history A history of the equipment of 'looking': from double-hook outline filled with ink to high-definition scanning {#sec-zhuangbei}
The tools of looking have never been neutral: **Tang**: facsimile reproduction by the palace storehouse, in double-hook outline filled with ink — the chance to see an original belonged to emperors; the copies belonged to favoured ministers; **Song**: the publication of engraved model-books (《淳化阁帖》, the *Chunhua ge tie*, 'Model-Books of the Chunhua Pavilion' — **looking was downgraded to rubbings, and the audience expanded ten-thousand-fold**) and the cataloguing in 《**宣和书谱**》 (the *Xuanhe shupu*, 'Catalogue of Calligraphy of the Xuanhe Reign': a court inventory = the bibliography of looking); **Ming**: the vogue for authentication and collecting flourished (**the seals of the family of Xiang Yuanbian 项元汴 fall like rain — the strongest violence of looking: possession by seal-stamp**; Dong Qichang 董其昌, theorising by the 'Northern and Southern schools', sat in the judge's seat of appraisal — **beyond the looking, appraisal itself became power**); **Qing**: the palace storehouse's 《**三希堂法帖**》 (the *Sanxitang fatie*, 'Model-Books of the Hall of Three Rarities'; engraved by imperial command in 1750 — the imperial sum of looking) and, among the people, **catalogues of metal and stone** (looking at a stele = looking at a paper rubbing + looking at the colophons — **a layer of scholarship was stacked on the looking**); **the early twentieth century**: collotype reproduction and the Youzheng Book Company (有正书局) — **famous works entered the studies of townspeople, and 'looking' was equalised for the first time**; **the 1960s**: the Palace Museum's display of calligraphy through the ages stood open for the long term — **the exhibition hall became a new institution of looking**; **the present**: **reproductions by Nigensha (二玄社) and high-definition digital libraries** (magnified ten times, a fine hair can be seen — **'microscopic looking at line quality' has entered the age of popular rights**: the 'roof-leak trace', which in the past only the appraisers of the palace storehouse could see, a secondary-school student can now see by enlarging it on a tablet); **AI assistance**: the recognition of style, and the scoring of forgeries — **the outsourcing of looking**; this lesson does not oppose the tools, and only declares a bottom line: **the 'three-minute face-to-face' must be yours**. The history of equipment being upgraded = the history of looking being democratised: **each time precision drops one grade, the audience expands one grade; each time it expands one grade, the right to judge sinks one grade** — today it is your turn.
:::

::: warning Four prohibitions in viewing calligraphy
**Do not recognise fame alone** (the exhibition label, and not the characters; staring for three days at a forged Wang Duo 王铎 — **first ask 'what was written, and how it was written', and then look at the signature and the price**); **do not judge high and low by 'whether it looks alike'** ('These characters do not look like printed ones' is the outsider's first sentence — **calligraphy is not the photocopying trade of character-shapes**: likeness is not value; **what does not look alike, and yet is consistent with itself, is where it begins**; if you really must judge by likeness, go and praise a printing house); **do not puff it with mysticism** ('This piece has an immortal air', 'That piece is thick with the air of killing' — **every adjective must be able to produce its evidence** (line, white, speed, weight); otherwise please withdraw to the '大气' ('grand air') club; this lesson does not issue membership cards); **do not look in order to trample** (the *guange* manner (馆阁), the *taige* style (台阁体), modern ink-images (墨象), the old-cadre style (老干部体) — **first understand their institutions and their functions, and only then judge aesthetically**: the *guange* style is an examination uniform, not fashion — **classify before you criticise**; taxonomy is the painkiller the whole course issues again and again).
:::

::: exercise A five-minute monologue {level=1}
Choose any one high-definition image of a stele or model-book from this lesson, do five minutes of face-to-face by the five questions, and write a monologue of 150 characters (four empty words are forbidden: 大气, 气势, 行云流水, 漂亮 — 'grand air', 'momentum', 'moving clouds and flowing water', 'pretty'). Swap with the person at the next desk, and for each of the other person's judgements mark the **type of evidence** (composition / line quality / character method / timestamp).
::: solution
Marking: the four empty words occur zero times (if they occur, add time and do it again); in the monologue **at least one sentence brings its own bracket of evidence**; the annotations after the swap show that the person judging is also reading the model (**looking at someone else looking at characters is the resit of looking**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Copying a model in the demon-revealing mirror {level=2}
Choose one eight-character line of a couplet (one that can be gathered from a model). Complete the three steps, copying facing the model → copying from memory → overlapped comparison; submit three images and a **list of differences** (for each difference note which item it is: opening / moving / closing / framework / ink gradation); and from the list draw up for yourself a **two-character prescription** for the next practice.
::: solution
Marking: the three steps are all present, and the copying from memory really is writing from memory with the model shut (anyone found copying by sight has the **prescription doubled**); the list of differences is pinned to the movement (at the level of 'the pause of the hook in the third character did not crouch — the third beat of 趯 (*tì*) is missing'; the terminology of the eight methods is cashed in a second time here); the two-character prescription must **train only the lesion** (do not copy the whole model; make the incision precisely); add one line to the end-of-term self-assessment: **how much the eye has progressed beyond the hand this time**.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the democracy of looking {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters: from 'the appraisers of the palace storehouse' to 'enlarging on a tablet', choose one **actual encounter between you and a famous work** (any one of a printed model, in person at a museum, a high-definition library, or a short-video explanation), and argue "how a change in the technology of looking changes **who is qualified to judge**"; and respond: **when AI can also give a report on reading a model whose evidence is complete, what is left of the 'three-minute face-to-face'?**
::: solution
Points for marking: the chain from technology to qualification is concrete (for example, collotype → townspeople of the Republic → the social basis of the standard-cursive movement of Yu Youren 于右任; or short video → character-count challenges → the war of public opinion over 'ugly calligraphy' (丑书) — **the sinking of the right to judge also brings an inflation of verdicts**); the personal encounter is not empty (write up one **slip of the eye**, and the mechanism of correction; extra marks); answers to the question about AI are allowed to vary, but the hard requirement is: **the part of the 'face-to-face' that cannot be outsourced must be pointed out** (choosing what to look at, being willing to be changed, taking the judgement into one's own hands — **aesthetic judgement is a responsibility, not knowledge**); if the conclusion is "**the course has ended; the scroll has not: looking is a lifetime's work**", this lesson's A is issued all the same.
:::

::: summary
The closing lesson settles its accounts: the five questions (from consumption to conversation), the three layers (composition from far, line quality from near, **teaching touch to the eye**), the three gears (copying facing the model, copying from memory, creating by gathering, **the upper limit of hand speed = eye speed**), the history of equipment (each time looking is equalised once, judgement sinks once), the four prohibitions (fame, whether it looks alike, mysticism, trampling at random). **The nine chapters of *Calligraphy* conclude the course here**: stroke order, the framework, the pen, the brush, regular script, running-cursive, seal and clerical, the historical outline, looking — three thousand years of the craft now each have one drawer in your hand. What you take away is not characters, but eyes. The homework left behind is only one line: **face one good piece of calligraphy for three minutes every day**, until your eyes start to charge a fee.
:::
