---
title: "A history of calligraphy"
---

In the 1970s an archaeological team dug up a dumb show: the occupant of the tomb had his head pillowed on an **inkstone**, and beside his hand a **brush-rest** was stuck in at a slant — **a man who clutched a brush even in his sleep** — yet the tomb epitaph gave neither name nor surname; while the biggest name of the same dynasty was worth a thousand in gold for a single stroke, and the traces of his writing were buried by the emperor, to go with him, in the Zhao Mausoleum. The history of calligraphy is the ledger of these two kinds of person: **the nameless, who slept on an inkstone, supplied the "change"; the biggest names, kept in the histories, supplied the "method"**. This chapter gets three thousand years of serial chapters by heart as a four-line doggerel, then teaches you how to doubt it.

## The four-line mnemonic {#def-koujue}

::: definition Valuing resonance, valuing method, valuing intent, valuing manner {#def-shang}
The Qing periodised in four sentences: "**晋人尚韵，唐人尚法，宋人尚意，元明清尚态**" ("People of the Jin valued resonance; people of the Tang valued method; people of the Song valued intent; the Yuan, Ming and Qing valued manner.") (Our translation.) (a form of words summed up by Qing writers; the wordings that have come down differ slightly; modern histories of calligraphy keep it as an outline — **it is a telescope, not a ruler**): **resonance** (*yùn* 韵) (Wei–Jin: the great clans' "字如其人" ('the writing is the man') first came online — the sons of the Wang and the Xie wrote as they waved a fan or wiped a tear, and **cultured panache was capital** (*fēngliú* 风流); the *Lanting* 兰亭 ('Orchid Pavilion') was the homework of an elegant gathering, not a project of the halls of state); **method** (*fǎ* 法) (Tang: selection for office by "**身、言、书、判**" ('body, speech, writing and judgement'); "writing" meant the method of regular script, vigorous and beautiful — **calligraphy was counted in the civil-service assessment**; Ouyang Xun 欧阳询, Chu Suiliang 褚遂良, Yan Zhenqing 颜真卿 and Liu Gongquan 柳公权 set up the standard portraits; Huairen 怀仁 gathered Wang, and the Kaiyuan era made copies and rubbings — the state put the classics in order); **intent** (*yì* 意) (Song: **in 992 the *Chunhua ge tie* 淳化阁帖 ('Model-books of the Chunhua Pavilion') was engraved**, and an official model was produced; yet Su Shi 苏轼, Huang Tingjian 黄庭坚, Mi Fu 米芾 and Cai Xiang 蔡襄 sang "**我书意造本无法**" ("My writing is made from intent, and was never based on method.") (Our translation.) — **rebelling against method while clutching a model-book; valuing intent was an insurrection on the establishment**); **manner** (*tài* 态) (Yuan, Ming and Qing: Zhao Mengfu 赵孟頫, returning to the ancient, opened a "**bibliography of styles**" — the styles of every age could all "resemble" some master, and **calligraphy entered a post-style age**; Dong Qichang 董其昌 put on a show with pale ink; Fu Shan 傅山's "四毋" ('four do-nots') issued a licence to the school of ugliness; the Qing stele school seized the class — **once "manner" had been involuted to the limit, they changed tables**). The right use of the mnemonic: it is not the truth, but **four diagnostic sheets** — on seeing any calligraphic style, first ask: does it want resonance, does it want method, does it want intent, or is it performing manner?
:::

::: example Time-travelling examiners: four interviewers {#ex-mianshi}
You take one and the same vertical scroll in running-cursive, travel to four ages, and apply for the post of "a person who writes": the great clans of the Eastern Jin, the Department of State Affairs in Chang'an, the institutes and archives of Bianjing, and the bookshops of Suzhou (the mid-Ming). Each age asks one question. Write **their marking sheet** and your **pledge of allegiance** (what to change, and what to keep).
::: solution
**Eastern Jin**: they ask "足下门第师承？" ("Sir, what is your family rank, and whose teaching do you inherit?") (Our translation.) — marking sheet: **resonance, bone, and the family method** (people of the time judged writing by "家法人物" ('family method and the person') — **the forms and momentum of the Wang and the Xie were the pass**); pledge: drop the ornamental display of skill, and add "不经意" ('not trying') (the altered places in the *Lanting* — **do not wash them clean**; those are the pores of resonance).
:::
:::

::: intuition Steles and model-books: a map war of three hundred years {#int-beitie}
The essence of the revolution of the Qing stele school was **a change of dynasty among the objects one took as models**: the model-book school = taking the **model-books of the Two Wangs** (engraved model-books, and ink traces) as the classics — the line = resonance on paper; the stele school = taking **stele carvings of the Han and the Wei, and seal and clerical script**, as the classics — the line = bone on stone (Ruan Yuan 阮元, with *Nanbei shupai lun* 南北书派论 ('On the Northern and Southern Schools of Calligraphy') and *Beibei nantie lun* 北碑南帖论 ('On Northern Steles and Southern Model-books'), drew the map first; Bao Shichen 包世臣, with *Yizhou shuangji* 艺舟双楫 ('Two Oars of the Boat of Art'), handed out the prizes; Kang Youwei 康有为, with *Guang yizhou shuangji* 广艺舟双楫 ('An Expansion of Two Oars of the Boat of Art'), issued the manifesto "魏碑十美" ('ten beauties of the Wei steles') — **esteeming steles and suppressing model-books turned from scholarship into a movement**). The technical causes of the war are more honest than the aesthetics: **a thousand years of retracing engraved model-books lost fidelity** (the original edition of the *Chunhua* was closed to rubbing, was burned, and was re-engraved again and again — the classics of the model-book school grew thinner with every carving), while **newly unearthed steles filled the screen with fresh pixels**; and, besides, scholars of the civil examinations **wanted a new canon with which to seize the interpretation** — the "struggle over the orthodox canon" in calligraphy has always been **the study of editions plus power**. Today's terms of reconciliation: **the bone of the stele, the soul of the model-book; the tools are neutral** — after the Deng school, no one forces you to take sides between Ouyang and the *Zhang Menglong* 张猛龙, and **taking sides is itself treated as the mark of an outsider**.
:::

::: history Institutions, media and identity {#sec-zhidu}
**Wei–Jin**: writing = an identification system for the great clans (letters were social calling-cards; **the family method was capital**); **Tang**: the tests for appointment, and teaching at the Hongwen Academy — **calligraphy became, for the first time, a technical standard of the state**; **Song**: publication of the pavilion model-books + an upgrade of the stationery (Su Yijian 苏易简, *Wenfang sipu* 文房四谱 ('Four Treatises on the Scholar's Studio')) + the turning of scholar-official life into elegant gatherings — **calligraphy changed from a duty of office into cultivation**; **Yuan**: Zhao Mengfu made the return to the ancient into a reform; **"calligraphy back to the Jin" became a slogan** — archaism was in office for the first time; **Ming**: the trade in engraved model-books, and the commodity economy (the small regular script and the running script of the Wen family were what the bookshops took as their model; Suzhou and the "Wumen" brand; Dong Qichang founded a school on "淡" ('pale') and "生" ('raw') — **writing became a religion of "the pale"**; his "Northern and Southern schools" belong in origin to the theory of painting, and must not be mis-cited); **Qing**: the *guange* style at its extreme, and the rebound of the stele school — Ruan, Bao and Kang fired three times in a row; **Deng Shiru 邓石如, with a "consummate art of seal and clerical", opened the way in practice — the stele school was written, not talked**; **1905**: the civil examinations were abolished, and **calligraphy lost the rice-bowl it had held for a thousand years** — the crisis of identity began; **1917**: the Calligraphy Research Society of Peking University was founded — **calligraphy was registered for the first time as a "society", and not as a "line of teachers"**; **1941**: the Chinese Calligraphy Society was founded in Chongqing (set going by Shen Yinmo 沈尹默 and others) — **the birth-pangs of a modern consciousness of calligraphy: at the very moment the brush was leaving the stage, calligraphy was rescued as "art"**; **1963**: the Zhejiang Academy of Fine Arts was the first to open a programme in calligraphy and seal-carving (the beginning of higher education in calligraphy); **1981**: the Chinese Calligraphers Association was founded; **2009**: calligraphy entered the intangible cultural heritage of the United Nations; **the present**: undergraduate, master's and doctoral programmes in calligraphy, and the "**modern question**" — art (the exhibition hall), craft (intangible heritage), cultivation (the interest class), or sport (ground-writing at the morning exercises)? **All four answers are open for business at once**; that is the state of the history of calligraphy in the twenty-first century.
:::

::: warning Four ills of the outline
**The evolutionism of "one age, one script"** (telling seal, clerical, regular, running and cursive as a "chain of progress" — **script types are a differentiation of function, not a Darwinian tree**: after the clerical change, seal script still lived on in seals; after regular script was established, cursive on the contrary grew wilder); **the moral intuition that "the writing is the man"** (the ink of Cai Jing 蔡京, Yan Song 严嵩, Zhang Ruitu 张瑞图 and Wang Duo 王铎, "**贰臣**" ('ministers who served two dynasties'), is hung all the same and studied all the same — **binding the quality of the writing to the quality of the person is an ethical invention from the Song onward**; one may use it to examine oneself, but it gives no grounds for judging another person; "**心正则笔正**" ("If the heart is upright, the brush is upright.") (Our translation.) is a remonstrance, not a forensic report); **the discrimination by physique in "the model-book school = effete, the stele school = virile"** (Kang Youwei's promotional copy taken for a medical report — **Deng Shiru was the brush of the stele school, Bao Shichen its mouth**; a mouth easily goes too far); **the illusion of ritual in "a heritage bid = protection, and entering the schools = a revival"** (intangible heritage is a project of **visibility**, not a project of **volume of use** — **the life-history of calligraphy is a history of use; the keyboard will not give up its seat for it, but it can resume its post in a new medium**: handwritten-style font libraries, bespoke title-slips, courses in psychological healing — **every tradition that is alive has changed its trade**).
:::

::: exercise A new commentary on the mnemonic {level=1}
For each of the four lines of the mnemonic, find **two counter-examples** (for instance a keeper of method in the age of "resonance", or one who reaches intent in the age of "method"), and write a "bug report on the mnemonic" of 200 characters.
::: solution
Marking: the counter-examples must come down to particular works (a keeper of method in the age of "resonance" — **Zhiyong** 智永, a seventh-generation descendant of the Wang house, who across the Sui and the Chen kept the family method, stroke by stroke, as one keeps a city; an extreme valuer of method in the age of "intent" — **the slender-gold script of Song Huizong** 宋徽宗, which wrote the rules out to the limit of the craft) — to point out "what misreading it makes easy" is a high mark.
:::
:::

::: exercise The periodising examiner {level=2}
From a character library or a stele-and-model-book app, draw at random 8 unsigned reproductions (covering seal, clerical, regular, running and cursive), and periodise them on the two axes "resonance, method, intent and manner" plus script type (give the grounds of the judgement: the format of the paper, taboo characters, the inscribed signature, the mounting — **formal evidence takes priority over temperament**), then mark yourself against the answers.
::: solution
Marking: of the 8 pieces, 3 or more **use formal evidence** (of the order of "avoiding the last stroke of the character ‘玄’ = Qing" and "the elevation of the upper dedication = the system of honorific address"; the instinct for bibliography in the materials lesson [[classical-chinese-1/punctuating]] is used again here); a wrong period loses no marks, and **fabricated evidence is penalised in full** (if you cannot see it clearly, write "cannot be judged" — **the first virtue of the history of calligraphy is that it can be falsified**); the line with which you mark yourself: "**我看的是字，断的是制度。**" ("What I was looking at was the characters; what I periodised was an institution.") (Our translation.)
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the modern question for calligraphy {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters on this: "When the brush withdraws from everyday life, what is left of calligraphy that cannot be replaced?" Choose any two of **bodiliness** (the hand and the breath), **temporality** (the line that cannot be undone) and **testimony of the person** (the signature and writing in one's own hand), develop them, and **answer one objection** (for instance, whether it holds that, as photography stands to painting, art is free only after practical use has left the stage).
::: solution
Points for marking: the two arguments do not overlap (body × time = two kinds of "what cannot be digitised"; testimony of the person, and the psychology of a "calligraphy fever"); the reply to the objection must **put that objection at its strongest, and only then reply** (the hole in the analogy with photography: the everyday function of calligraphy is dead, but **the skill in which reading and writing have one source is not dead** — this is the greatest difference from photography); if one takes as an example the **avant-garde calligraphy** of Japan in the second half of the twentieth century (few-character writing), that gains marks; a pessimistic conclusion is allowed, but it must be **concrete** (for example, "书法将如围棋：竞技与修养的窄门" ("Calligraphy will be like *weiqi*: a narrow gate of competition and of cultivation.") (Our translation.) — **a narrow gate is still a gate**).
:::

::: summary
The chronology lesson settles its accounts: the four-line mnemonic (resonance–method–the person, method–institutions, intent–learning, manner–the market — four diagnostic sheets), one war of steles and model-books (the struggle over the orthodox canon, of the study of editions plus power; today's reconciliation: the bone of the stele and the soul of the model-book), one institutional line (a living from selection for office → out of work in 1905 → forming societies in 1917 → a heritage bid in 2009 — **from rice-bowl to identity card to exhibition hall to interest class**), and a warning of four ills (evolutionism, moral intuition, discrimination by physique, the illusion of ritual). The last chapter brings the course to its close: how to **look** — a method of viewing for the reading of model-books, packing the whole of this course's knowledge into one three-minute meeting of eyes.
:::
