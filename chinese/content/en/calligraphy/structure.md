---
title: "The structure of characters"
---

The same eight strokes: arrange them, and the arrangement is a character; arrange them at random, and they are loose sand. The structure of the character studies **the architecture of parts inside the square**. This chapter first lays down three laws of physics, then strolls through the ancients' thirty-six house rules, and at last cures four common illnesses.

## Three laws of structural physics {#sec-wuli}

::: definition Centre of gravity, main stroke, distribution of white {#def-wuli}
**① Centre of gravity** (a square character has an invisible **drop-point** — the whole secret of structure is to let the strokes **circle it without crushing it**: **the centre of gravity is alive only when it is steady and does not sit dead centre** — the 中 of the Yan style (颜体) is upright without being stiff, the 中 of the Ou style (欧体) is precarious without falling; the same drop-point, two ways of standing); **② Main stroke** (a character is allowed only one hero: the right-falling stroke of 大, the lower horizontal of 天, the vertical of 十 — **the main stroke opens out, the remaining strokes draw in**; releasing two strokes as main strokes = two people grabbing the microphone); **③ Distribution of white** (the advanced view of structure: **what one looks at is not the black line but the white block** — 口 encloses one even breath, 川 lines the gaps up as three rooms; Deng Shiru 邓石如 of the Qing, "**疏处可以走马，密处不使透肤，常计白以当黑，奇趣乃出**" ('Where it is sparse, a horse may pass; where it is dense, do not let the skin show through. Constantly reckon the white so as to stand for the black, and a singular interest then appears.' (Our translation.)) (transmitted as recorded in Bao Shichen 包世臣, *Yizhou shuangji* 艺舟双楫, 'The Twin Oars of the Boat of Art') — **the distribution of white is the musicality of structure**).
:::

::: definition The Thirty-Six Methods and the central palace {#def-sanishi}
The *Thirty-Six Methods* (三十六法), transmitted as the work of Ouyang Xun 欧阳询 of the Tang (in fact arranged and put in sequence by people of the Song; attaching a work to a founding master's name was the ancients' usual practice), is the first compilation of 'methods of structuring': **piling and stacking** (the three-storey pavilion of 鑫), **avoiding and approaching** (components make way for one another — you extend, I draw in, as in 林, where the left-hand right-falling stroke becomes a dot), **wearing on the head** (when the top is heavy and the bottom light, it must be worn so that it sits steady), **support** (中 and 十, one pillar holding up the sky), **bowing in salutation** (a character of several bodies pays its own courtesy: the three parts of 翼 stand with hands cupped), **enclosing** (the outer frames of 国 and 园 are neither square nor round — **the frame is a gesture, not a matchbox**), **threading through** (the vertical strokes of 中 and 申 run right through)… There is no need to memorise them all. **Remember the spirit of the taxonomy: a problem of structure is named first, and solved afterwards** — what this way of talking trains is precisely 'seeing why it looks good'. The matching mnemonic: **a character stands as a person stands** (a single-body character must have a stance), **the four sides are evenly poised while the central palace is compact** ('**central palace**', *zhōnggōng* 中宫, = the tightened zone at the core of the square — both the pull inward and the pull outward need a centre to the circle; Huang Tingjian 黄庭坚, long spear and great halberd and yet with the central palace extremely tight, is a dancer who **tightens the core and then flings the four limbs**).
:::

::: example A clinic for four characters {#ex-zhen}
Hard-pen case notes: 想 written as three storeys pulled apart, 区 written as a matchbox, 森 written as three trees of equal size, the slanted hook of 成 soft and collapsed like a noodle. For each character, issue a **structural prescription** (move only the framework; do not touch the quality of the strokes).
::: solution
**想**: 相 and 心 stand in a **relation of bowing in salutation** — the illness is that 相 is too wide and the 心 at the bottom is too far away, so that the three characters become a scene of moving house; prescription: **compress the 相 component horizontally by three tenths, and let the 心 at the bottom, by its lying hook, hold up the centre of gravity of the right half of 相** (the philosophy of top-and-bottom structure: the lower part is a base, not a footnote). **区**: the outer frame is a **gesture** — the upper horizontal tilts slightly upward, the left vertical arches slightly, the bottom horizontal takes an oblique impetus and leaves a 'breath-mouth', and the × inside the frame sits towards the top and the right (the enclosing method's 'the space inside is alive only when it is uneven'). **森**: piling and stacking is not photocopying — **the three pines must be one general and two soldiers**: the upper 木 is the central army, its main stroke opened out; the lower left draws in a shoulder, the lower right yields a foot (three of the same form stacked, the '品-character' principle of the centre leading, the left yielding and the right drawing in; and so for 晶 and 鑫). **成**: the slanted hook is **at once the main stroke and a load-bearing wall** — at the start of the stroke, plant the feet; as the stroke travels, press gradually and carry an arc (arched, but not collapsed); before the hook comes out, stamp the foot and then kick; the other four strokes all **gather towards the slanted hook** (only when the central palace is tightened can the four limbs be released — what has gone soft was never the hook; it is the surroundings, too scattered, that set it off as isolated). The standard for marking: **every sentence of a prescription must land on a 'relation'** (who yields to whom, who supports whom, who is primary and who is secondary). Whoever says 'practise more' is judged a quack.
:::
:::

::: intuition The central palace: the politics of a geometric concept {#int-zhonggong}
The 'central palace' was originally a term of astronomical star-divination (the seat of the Heavenly King). Borrowed into treatises on calligraphy, it became **a politics of structure**: a tightened core = power concentrated, limbs opened out = power delegated — **the Ou style has a central palace tight and precarious (the style of an able minister who seeks stability within danger), the Yan style has a central palace broad and full (the style of a prime minister, poised and self-sufficient), and Li Yong's 李邕 running script (semi-cursive) stands upright within the slant (the style of a general)** — to read a model as far as this layer is to see through **the constitution of a style**.
The counter-evidence is in the case notes: **four corners braced full = anarchy** (each stroke governs on its own, and the character becomes a balloon); **the centre piled dead = an excess of concentrated power** (no white on any of the four sides, and the character becomes a cake of ink). A good history of structure is **a history of the swing between delegating power and drawing it back**: people of the Jin prized resonance (a relaxed central palace), people of the Tang prized method (a strict central palace), people of the Song prized intention (a central palace that follows the occasion) — the next lesson reads the history of styles; first set this centre of the circle into the eye.
:::

::: history A thousand-year ledger of the study of structuring {#sec-zhangben}
**Eastern Han**: Cai Yong 蔡邕, *Bilun* 笔论 ('On the Brush'), "**为书之体，须入其形……纵横有可象者，方得谓之书**" ('For the body of a writing, one must enter its forms…… Only when, vertically and horizontally, there is something that can be imaged, may it be called writing.' (Our translation.)) (the first declaration of a consciousness of structure: a character must **have the power of an image**); **Jin**: the *Bizhen tu* 笔阵图 ('Diagram of the Battle Array of the Brush'), transmitted as the work of Lady Wei (卫夫人), likens dots and strokes to a battle array ("横如千里阵云" — 'A horizontal is like clouds of the array across a thousand *li*.' (Our translation.) — **each stroke is one deployment of a battle**); **Tang**: the compilation, under an attributed name, of Ouyang Xun's *Thirty-Six Methods*, and the prizing of method by Yan and Liu, pushed structuring to a **statutory dose** ('Yan and Liu' are two pharmacopoeias of the method of structure); **Song**: Huang Tingjian's *Lunshu* 论书 ('On Calligraphy') sets store by "字中有笔" ('there is brush within the character') while the central palace is tightly gathered — **prizing intention does not change the geometry**; **Yuan**: Li Puguang 李溥光 (Shi Puguang 释溥光), in *Xue'an ziyao* 雪庵字要 ('Xue'an's Essentials of Characters') and kindred works, turned structuring into chanted formulae and **into something of the marketplace** (the textbook layer of ordinary writing classes); **Ming**: a publishing fever for *Zihui* 字汇 ('A Compendium of Characters') and *Liushu jingyun* 六书精蕴 ('The Essential Purport of the Six Categories'); structure was put to the service of **type design** (the skeleton of the Song typeface was fixed in this century); **Qing**: Deng Shiru's "计白当黑" ('reckoning white as black'; the theory of distributing white is capped) together with the record transmitted in Bao Shichen's *Yizhou shuangji* — **white was entered on the register for the first time as a proprietor**; **the modern period**, the 1930s, the debate over structure within **the controversy over 'reform of Chinese characters'** (the Latinising party found it cumbersome; the party of keeping what was established defended itself by **art** — the problem of structure was politicised for the first time); **the present**: the age of font engineering — **every character you read on a screen has been designed by the study of structure** (font libraries such as Founder (方正) and Hanyi (汉仪) make 'a relaxed central palace' and 'a high or low centre of gravity' into differences between commodities; the quarrel over the Palace Museum typeface (故宫体) and the Slender Gold typeface (瘦金体) = a battlefield of public opinion in the aesthetics of structure).
:::

::: warning Four myths of the framework
First, **the misunderstanding of "字无百日功"** ('There is no hundred days' achievement in a character.' (Our translation.)) (muscle memory can be formed in a hundred days; **the eye has no hundred days** — a hand that does not practise reading the model only copies errors; **copying the model a hundred times is not as good as reading the model once and then copying it once**); second, **grid-dependence** (leave the rice-character grid (米字格) and it falls apart — **the grid is an exoskeleton; the central palace must grow into the body itself**: three steps in withdrawing the grid: copying in the grid — on the threshold — in mid-air); third, **an impersonation show of star scripts** (going straight to Slender Gold, or to the Qi style (启体) — **style is a second-order product; first establish structure of the first order**: every excellence of Slender Gold is built on Ouyang Xun's foundation; decorate before the foundation is laid, and what you learn is 'matchstick style'); fourth, **mixing the structures of running script and regular script (standard script)** (carrying the logic of linked writing that belongs to running script over into the components and the stroke order of regular script — regular script has a regular-script construction, running script has the methods of running script — **to borrow structure across scripts, first look up that script's own rules for the character**, or else it is neither of the two).
:::

::: exercise Reading the model and taking the house apart {level=1}
Choose four characters each from Ou's *Jiucheng gong* 九成宫 ('Jiucheng Palace') and Yan's *Duobao ta* 多宝塔 ('the Duobao Pagoda') (Ou: "九、成、宫、泉"; Yan: "大、千、宝、氏"), and for each character mark: whether the centre of gravity sits high or low, which stroke is the main stroke, whether the central palace is loose or tight, and where the largest block of white lies.
::: solution
Marking: the columns for the four characters are complete, and **the comparison of Ou and Yan is made in pairs** (one and the same character 九: Ou's central palace is lifted and gathered under risk, Yan's central palace is broad, ample, level and solid — the marks must let a person who has not got the model **write it from memory off the diagram** in roughly that style); if 'both look quite nice' appears, the mark is zero — **this lesson accepts only the language of the study of structure**: high and low, loose and tight, gathering and releasing, bowing and yielding.
:::
:::

::: exercise Surgery on one character of the 品 type {level=2}
From 晶, 鑫 and 森 choose any two: first write each once on the pattern of 'three trees of equal size' (the case of illness), then redesign and adjust it by the 品-character principle of 'one general and two soldiers' (draw a sketch of how each part yields its place), and attach an explanatory note of 100 characters.
::: solution
Marking: the relations of yielding are clear (the upper one opens out, the left one draws in a shoulder, the right one yields a foot — or a variant according to the shape of the character; **it is enough that the account holds together**); the note uses three terms to the full: **piling and stacking, avoiding and yielding, centre of gravity**; extra credit: discovering the difference between 鑫 and 森 (金, with few strokes, yields by **making the form small**; 木, with many strokes, yields by **contracting the strokes** — one principle, two budgets).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the spirit of the age in structure {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: "颜体是中唐的，瘦金体是北宋末的，馆阁体是明代的" ('The Yan style is of the mid-Tang, Slender Gold is of the end of the Northern Song, and the *guange* style is of the Ming.' (Our translation.)) — choose one style, argue the connection between its structure and **the temperament of the age, or its institutions** (the civil-service examinations, the study of metal and stone, court taste, printing), and set, for this method of argument, one **guard-rail against over-interpretation**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the argument of connection is concrete (the 'broad generosity' of the Yan style flows together with the cultural self-confidence of the High and mid-Tang and with the worship of personality, and the layer of **retrospective recognition by people of the Song** must be mentioned (Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修 prized Yan) — a style is often 'appointed anew' by a later age); the institutional side has evidence (the small regular script of the examinations → the 'black, square and glossy' of the *guange* style, 馆阁体); the guard-rail is up to standard: **that structure reflects the age is a rhetorical hypothesis, not a causal law** — give one counter-example and keep hold of honesty (several calligraphic styles coexist in one and the same age).
:::

::: summary
The structure workshop settles its accounts: three laws of physics (the centre of gravity stands alive; the main stroke is limited to one; white, once distributed, stands for black), one set of house rules (name first, then solve — the Thirty-Six Methods are a taxonomy, not a statute), one centre (the gathering and releasing of the central palace = the constitution of a style), four illnesses ('practise more' = a quack; **the prescription must land on a relation**). You now have both the parts and the house. The next chapter's turn is the tools: first the most underestimated of them, the fountain pen — the modern main force of writing — and then it leads you to the door of the brush.
:::
