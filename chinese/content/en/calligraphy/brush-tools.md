title: "The brush and the four treasures"
---

A brush is a **soft spring with a temper**: press it and it makes no sound; hang it and it will not stand; leave it unwashed for three days and it falls to pieces. The four implements that wait on it (brush, ink, paper and inkstone) the Chinese call, together, the **four treasures of the study** (文房四宝), and they have even conferred offices on them: **笔中书君** ('the brush, Gentleman of the Secretariat'), **墨侯** ('Marquis of Ink'), **纸寿千岁** ('paper, a life of a thousand years'), **砚田** ('the inkstone field'). This chapter is the entrance lesson: meeting the family, completing the set, forming the habits, and, besides that, desensitising — washing away the four characters 不敢下笔 ('not daring to put the brush to paper').

## The brush: a user's manual for the soft spring {#sec-bi}

::: definition The four virtues of the brush head, and the specifications {#def-side}
The standard for choosing a brush has not changed in a thousand years: **pointed** (尖; the tip of the hairs like an awl — the lifeline of closing a stroke), **even** (齐; pinch it flat and look at whether the hairs are even in length — only what is even gives even force), **round** (圆; neither flat nor bald, the waist and belly full), **resilient** (健; the hairs have spring, and come back when pressed — goat is soft, wolf is firm, mixed hair takes the middle). The specifications are two numbers: **tip length** (出锋; the length of the hairs — a long tip is lively, and also hard to tame) and **diameter** (口径; the thickness, which decides the size of character). “写多大的字，用多大的笔” ('For characters of a given size, use a brush of that size.' (Our translation.)) is common sense. **Writing large characters with a small brush depends on the technique of loading ink and adjusting the tip; writing small characters with a large brush is only a cramped makeshift.** A roster of materials: **goat hair** (羊毫; soft, stores a great deal of ink, a dumbbell for training the strength of the wrist — only someone who can bear it is fit to speak of the thickness of a line), **wolf hair** (狼毫; made from the tail of the yellow weasel, firm and dear; most "wolf hair" on sale is adulterated with rabbit hair as a substitute — when you buy a brush, go by the shop and not by the name), **mixed hair** (兼毫; a layered build with soft hair as the coat and hard hair as the core; **the beginner's *dabaiyun* 大白云 is mixed hair, and the most obedient**), **long-tipped *lanzhu*** (长锋兰竹; a bamboo shaft and hemp hair, a painting tool that also writes, one tube of watery vapour).
:::

::: definition Opening, moistening and keeping the brush {#def-yangbi}
A new brush is fixed fast with glue: **soak it for two or three hours in cold or warm water (hot water melts the glue and ruins the tip) until the root goes soft**, then pinch lightly to bring the point out — **opening a brush is like putting an edge on a blade; it cannot be rushed**. Three rules for ordinary days: **moisten it through before use** (dip a dry brush into ink, and the ink starves on the surface while the tip will not gather — once it is moistened through, **turn it two circles on absorbent paper to take off the spare water, then dip**; the measure is that the tip 吃墨八分, 'takes ink to eight parts'); **wash it as soon as you have used it** (ink left behind crusts at the root, and a single night can make a bald brush — **in the water, pinch with the lie of the hairs; do not stroke against them**); **hang it to dry in the shade** (the brush rest is its sickbed; the cap is its warrant of death: **storing it wet under the cap = a rotten root**). Length of life is reckoned in "kilometres of writing". A splayed tip need not be retired at once — **demote it to writing large characters, and to practising ink on backing paper; using a thing to the full is a scholar's virtue**.
:::

::: definition Ink, paper and the inkstone: a triangle of black, white and hard {#def-mozhiyan}
**Ink** (pine-soot is black and has little sheen, and suits writing; oil-soot is dense and bright, and suits painting and regular script; **glue** is the vital point of ink: too much glue and the brush drags, too little glue and the ink loses its spirit — good ink "releases quickly, has little glue, and does not stick". The old maxim “磨墨如病夫” ('Grind ink as an invalid does.' (Our translation.)) speaks of **using force lightly and evenly**, not of truly being ill. If you are in a hurry, bottled ink does no harm: **choose ink for calligraphy and painting, and avoid carbon ink meant for the office (it clogs the brush)**; ink left overnight stinks — pour it away). **Paper** (**raw xuan** 生宣: it drinks water and the ink bleeds; a stroke goes down and there is breath in it — the home of running script (semi-cursive) and cursive script, and of freehand painting (*xieyi* 写意); **sized xuan** 熟宣: processed with alum, it does not bleed — the bed of small regular script and of meticulous painting (*gongbi* 工笔); **half raw and half sized** 半生熟: the middle way, **a friend to the beginner**; “纸寿千年” ('paper lives a thousand years') is xuan paper's boast about itself (Jingxian's recipe of blue sandalwood bark, 青檀皮, plus rice straw). **For practice, use maobian paper or yuanshu paper** (毛边纸/元书纸) — xuan paper is paper for finished work; what lets a beginner dare to pay the tuition is maobian). **Inkstone** (Duan stone 端石 and She stone 歙石, the two great houses; what is prized is **bringing the ink forth without using it up**; if you have no inkstone, use a white porcelain dish — **anything with a fine glaze can serve as an inkstone for the time being**: the "treasure" in the four treasures lies in performance, not in a famous kiln).
:::

::: example The first housewarming: a three-hundred-yuan list for the study {#ex-qingdan}
On a budget of three hundred yuan (renminbi), assemble the whole kit with which an adult begins a year of practising regular script, and for each sum of money state "what performance is being bought".
::: solution
**Brush** (60: two mixed-hair *dabaiyun* at 15×2 — **what you are buying is obedience**; one long-tipped goat-hair at 30 — what you are buying is the homework still to come; a further 15 yuan for a brush rest and a brush washer, **plastic will do; what you are buying is "a place to hang them" — the rate at which brushes are properly kept doubles**); **ink** (20: one bottle of ink for calligraphy and painting, plus an ink dish or a small white porcelain condiment dish — **what you are buying is a brush that does not clog**); **paper** (120: one knife of maobian paper at 60 — what you are buying is **daring to write** (not grieving over waste paper is the most precious psychological subsidy a beginner gets); a half-raw, half-sized practice book at 60 — what you are buying is **bleeding within measure**); **pad** (30: one felt for calligraphy and painting — **what you are buying is a buffer**: the give of the felt lets you dare to press); **models and grids** (40: one set of water-writing cloth ruled with the *mi*-character grid 米字格, plus a regular-script copybook to your liking — the water-writing cloth **buys the right to write it again at no cost**: write with clear water, and when it has dried begin again; the dumbbell of the world of calligraphy); **contingency** (70: a first brush that is a little better (including a reserve under the **demotion system**) plus the cost of printing a stele rubbing or model-book once, at original size). The discipline of the list: **the inkstone is left out for now** (a porcelain dish goes on duty), **xuan paper is left out for now** (buy it on the day of a finished piece) — **the first principle of equipment: spend the money on "frequency of practice", not on "the atmosphere of a study"**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The worship of tools, and its antidote {#int-gongju}
The second thing that kills a beginner is **anxiety about equipment** (the first is not daring to press): three months of saving to buy Huizhou ink and a Duan inkstone, and then **setting them up as offerings and not writing**. Three sentences of antidote: **the value of a tool = frequency of use × difference in performance** (a mixed-hair brush at thirty yuan, used every day to copy a model, beats a Huzhou brush at three hundred yuan set on the offering table month after month); **"the brush does not choose its paper" is an illusion; "the paper must choose its brush" is everyday fact** (settle the practice paper first, then match the brush — maobian with mixed hair, raw xuan with goat hair); **tools of collector's grade are goods on the edge of calligraphy, not the ticket of admission**. Those who have gone further, on the contrary, use simpler things the more they use them: **calligraphers at their practice mostly use ordinary brushes, and only when making a work bring out the brush they love** — the rank-order of tools ought to be levelled by **the amount of use**. To test which stage you are at, one question is enough: **how many brushes on your rest are "in use"?** More than three in use is diligence; not a single one is interior decoration.
:::

::: history A short archaeology of the four treasures {#sec-kaogu}
**Brush**: bamboo-shafted brushes have already come out of Chu tombs of the Warring States. **Meng Tian 蒙恬 made the brush**, as found in the *Gujin zhu* 《古今注》 ('Notes on Things Ancient and Modern') and the *Bowu zhi* 《博物志》 ('A Record of Manifold Things') — it is **a legendary crediting of an improvement in brush-making (the layered method of a core, 笔柱, and a coat, 笔被)**; that it arose from nothing, archaeology does not allow. **Han**: Xuan brushes (Xuancheng in Anhui) gradually made a name. **Tang**: the chicken-spur brushes of Xuanzhou, and purple-hair brushes, entered the tribute; Bai Juyi 白居易's *yuefu* (Music Bureau poem) 'The Purple-Hair Brush' 《紫毫笔》 spoke for them. **Song**: brush-makers moved south; the embryo of the Huzhou brush of Wuxing. **Yuan**: brush-makers of Wuxing such as **Feng Yingxing 冯应星 and Zhang Wenjin 张文进** became known to their age by their brushes (as transmitted in the notebook writings, *biji*, of people of the Yuan and the Ming); the reputation of the Huzhou brush broke out of its circle together with the writing style of Zhao Mengfu 赵孟頫 — **the style of the characters and the style of the brush are each the cause of the other**. **Ming and Qing**: the town of Shanlian became the "capital of brushes"; the ***Tiangong kaiwu*** 《天工开物》 ('The Exploitation of the Works of Nature') records the crafts of brush hair and of the stuff of paper. **Ink**: at the end of the Tang the Yishui ink-maker **Xi Chao 奚超** crossed to the south, and the Southern Tang granted him the surname Li — the ancestor of **Huizhou ink**; in the Qing, famous makers such as Cao Sugong 曹素功 and Wang Jinsheng 汪近圣, and the ink offices of Huizhou. **Paper**: in the Tianbao era of the Tang, Jingxian (泾县) was known for its paper; in the **Xuande era of the Ming** the court supervised, at Jingxian, the making of large-vat paper; in **2009** the craft of making xuan paper, and calligraphy, entered in the same year the UNESCO list of intangible cultural heritage. **Inkstone**: people of the Tang already prized Duan and She; in the Song, **Mi Fu 米芾's *Yan shi* 《砚史》 ('A History of Inkstones')** established a field of study for the inkstone; records that the various pits of Duanxi were **quarried out and the pits sealed** run on through the ages — the history of exhaustion is more honest than the history of tribute. The guiding line of the history of the four treasures: **first a revolution in performance, then a brand of place of origin, and only at the end the legends of the literati** — look at any advertisement for the "scholar's study", and check this chain backwards.
:::

::: warning Six beginner's accidents, one after another
**Opening a brush with boiling water** (the glue dissolves and the tip collapses — hot water is the crematorium of the brush head); **putting a wet brush away under its cap** (a rotten root and a bald head; storing a brush in a wet cap rots the root even faster — **once it is washed, it must be hung**); **thinning bottled ink with tap water** (electrolytes in unboiled water spoil the glue — **a few drops of warm water** are enough; better thick than thin; thinned, it bleeds on the paper and bleeds in the heart as well); **small regular script on raw xuan** (the paper eats the brush, and the more you write the more you doubt your life — **for small regular script use sized xuan or half-sized paper**); **dipping a new brush straight into ink and starting to practise** (a dry tip wrenched by force; one stroke finishes off a tuft of hair — **moisten first, then dip**, a four-character rite for the life of the brush); **a copybook laid on felt and not fixed** (paper that slips = a lawful excuse for a shaking hand — **two paperweights, or masking tape at the corners**; physical steadiness is the foundation of steadiness of mind).
:::

::: exercise Equipping a brush of your own {level=1}
On a budget of no more than two hundred yuan, for the writing of the stage you are at now (choose one of: regular script for a beginner, speeding up in running script, copying sutras in small regular script), fit out three brushes. For each, state the hair, the tip length, the diameter and the division of tasks, and after one month hand in a "service report" (including how far the tip has splayed).
::: solution
Marking: the set matches the tasks (for example mixed hair for large regular script as the main work, + wolf hair for copying sutras in small regular script, + one long-tipped goat-hair brush as the homework of "training the wrist"); **the service report has figures** (how many times it was used each week; whether the brush was washed on the same day); a self-diagnosis appears, of the kind "the more I write, the less I love it" (glue too heavy? the brush too soft? — **only an ear that can describe the discomfort counts as having come in**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Forty minutes on the water-writing cloth {level=2}
Using only water, and cloth for writing with clear water: complete the opening–travel–close of the horizontal stroke 横 one hundred times, divided into four groups of ten repetitions, and before each group silently call up one essential (group 1, conceal the tip and enter in reverse; group 2, press the middle section solid; group 3, turn the tip back and close the stroke; group 4, the three together, and pick up speed). Attach a self-observation of 80 characters: after the water-mark has dried, which stroke is "still there"?
::: solution
Marking: the hundred were truly completed (the merit of the water-writing cloth = a review that dries quickly); the observation is specific to **a part of the stroke** (“第二组的垂肩都写出来了，第五组开始抢拍” 'In the second group the drooping shoulders all came out; from the fifth group I began to rush the beat.' (Our translation.)); the answer to what is "still there" lands on the feel of the wrist — **the water-writing cloth trains the movement; maobian paper trains the result**; both are wanted, and the former saves money.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the economics of the scholar's study {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters: "How the four treasures of the study turned from implements into symbols of identity". It must contain one **observation of the present** (any one of selling ink by livestream, inkstones as a way of managing money, and the gift economy of a "three-piece set for the study"), and, with this lesson's reverse-check chain of "performance — place of origin — legend", take apart the advertising language of one real commodity.
::: solution
Points for marking: the historical layer has evidence to it (tribute brushes and tribute paper — the marks of famous makers — the records of connoisseurship and collecting — contemporary IP; **every step of the turn into a symbol has someone pushing**: the court, collectors, booksellers, platforms); the observation of the present is not cynical (the gift economy is not wholly a fraud; it supports the **survival of the intangible-heritage artisans** of the place of production — those who write this tension gain extra marks); the taking apart of the advertisement is exact down to the phrase (“纯手工青檀” 'wholly handmade blue sandalwood', “古法松烟” 'pine soot by the ancient method' — **which phrase is performance, which is place of origin, which is legend**); if the conclusion can tell "**buying tools for practice**" from "**buying props for an identity**", it meets the mark.
:::

::: summary
The study opens, and the account is settled: the brush (the four virtues, plus a start on mixed hair, plus the three rules of moistening, washing and hanging), ink (bottled ink is enough; office ink is refused entry), paper (maobian, so that one dares to write; raw and sized each keep their own office), the inkstone (a porcelain dish takes the post for the time being). The list at three hundred yuan proves that **admission need not be bankruptcy**; the reverse-check chain proves that **legend does not stand in for performance**. The objects are in place. The next chapter enters the standard course: hands-on practice of the eight methods of the character 永 — from "recognising the strokes" to "writing a horizontal that can stand".
:::
