---
title: "Seal and clerical script"
---

When the modern styles — regular script (standard script), running script (semi-cursive) and cursive script (grass script) — have been written until they are familiar, it is time to turn back and recognise the ancestors. **All the parts** of the character-shapes written today lie buried in two geological layers: **seal script** (the age of the line) and **clerical script** (the age of the stroke). The fault between them is called **the clerical change**, the only structural break in the history of the Chinese character — **recognise it, and only then can you be said to know what you are writing**.

## Geological layer one: seal script {#sec-zhuan}

::: definition Large seal, small seal, and 'the graceful and the continuous' {#def-zhuan}
The family of **seal script**: oracle-bone inscriptions (incised), bronze inscriptions (cast), **large seal script** (*zhouwen*; the Stone Drum inscriptions — a solid, weighty sample of Qin-lineage script), and on to **Li Si's small seal script** (Li Si 李斯), which completed the first state aesthetic (later ages described its line as “画如铁石，字若飞动” ('Strokes like iron and stone, characters as if in flight.') (Our translation.) — the words come from a Tang appraisal of Li Si; jade-chopstick seal and iron-wire seal are both lines descended from it: **even thickness, even speed, a pure centred tip (*zhongfeng*)**). The master-formula of brush method, in six characters: “篆尚婉而通” ('Seal script values the graceful and the continuous.') (Our translation.) (Sun Guoting 孙过庭, *Shupu* 书谱 ('Treatise on Calligraphy')) — **graceful** (no edges and no corners; the turn passes unseen — the circle is the breath of seal script), **continuous** (the *qi* (vital energy) does not break; one stroke is like a thread of silk drawn out). The act of writing: the brush set down vertically, the centred tip at an even speed, the head concealed and the tail protected — **there is almost no 'action', only 'motion'**. Why practise seal script? **Seal script is the basic training in singing for the centred tip.** Every problem of 'line quality' in the travelling strokes of regular script and the controlling turns (*shizhuan*) of cursive (flat, floating, thin) has its root in the side tip (*cefeng*) taking the steps instead — **one year of seal script is one year of core training for the line**.
:::

## Geological layer two: clerical script {#sec-li}

::: definition The clerical change: cutting off the two Zhou and joining the modern script {#def-libian}
**The clerical change**: over the four hundred years from the late Warring States to the Eastern Han, the clerical script of Qin, step by step, **straightened, flattened, abbreviated and merged, and split into separate strokes** the seal script — round turns were changed to squared breaks, what had been continuous was cut into strokes, and components were conflated and reshaped (the upper parts of “春、泰、秦” have from then on failed to recognise one another) — **ancient script (meaning by the form; one can guess by looking at the picture) from this point became modern script (spelt with signs; without study, one cannot read it)**. One sentence that fixes its nature: **the clerical change is the only irreversible break in the history of the Chinese character — before it, 'speaking from the picture'; after it, 'reading by the code'**. The Han themselves accounted for that speed: “秦时官狱职务繁，初有隶书，以趣约易” ('In Qin times the duties of the offices and the prisons were burdensome; clerical script arose for the first time, so as to hasten brevity and ease.') (Our translation.) (Xu Shen 许慎, the Preface to *Shuowen jiezi* (*Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*)); tradition has it that **Cheng Miao** 程邈 of the Qin put the clerks' script (*zuoli*) in order — of a piece with Meng Tian 蒙恬's making of the brush: **the legend assigns the credit; that it was put in order is fact**. The paired sentence of the *Shupu* records the temperament of the brush method: “篆尚婉而通，隶欲精而密” ('Seal script values the graceful and the continuous; clerical script desires the precise and the dense.') (Our translation.) — the graceful is the breathing; the dense is the bone.
:::

::: definition Silkworm head and wild-goose tail, and one wave with three turns {#def-canrou}
Three entries on the identity card of clerical script: **① silkworm head and wild-goose tail** (the horizontal enters in reverse, round and blunt, like a silkworm's head; at the close one gradually presses, then thrusts out a flicked foot, like a wild goose's tail); **② wild geese do not fly in pairs** (**within one character the wild-goose tail is allowed only once** — apart from the principal horizontal, the foot is in every case drawn in; two tails contend with each other for space, and the character is as if in a brawl); **③ horizontally spreading impetus** (the square of seal script is pressed into a flat square, and the flicked feet part to left and right, back to back — for the name **bafen**, teaching takes the one account “体势如八字分散” ('the posture of the form disperses like the character 八'), and the other accounts are noted alongside as coexisting). The technical hinge: **where seal turns, clerical breaks** — at the turn, lift the brush and pause, change face, and only then go on; **a squared break as if cut by scissors is the scissor-method of clerical script**; the 'bent hairpin' (*zhechigu*), which passes by a supple turn, is left to seal script.
:::

::: example A compass of four styles {#ex-panpan}
The four famous inscriptions of Han clerical script — the *Zhang Qian Stele* (《张迁碑》), the *Cao Quan Stele* (《曹全碑》), the *Ritual Vessels Stele* (《礼器碑》) and the *Shi Chen Stele* (《史晨碑》) — are each likened to one movement of the body, one musical instrument and one season, and written out as four executable 'prescriptions for copying a model' (stating which fault of writing each is suited to treat).
::: solution
**Zhang Qian**: standing post + bass drum + deep autumn — square strokes chopped clean, the structure flat and thick, skill hidden inside clumsiness; **treats slickness** (one who has written running script and whose hand itches turns honest after copying a hundred characters); **Cao Quan**: dance + flute + spring water — elegant, easy and fluent, the wild-goose tail opened out, even and clean as if writing a Spring Festival couplet; **treats stiffness** (the antidote for sufferers of the *guange* manner, the polished official hand; but covet its prettiness and you catch a sweet vulgarity); **Ritual Vessels**: knife-work + the *guqin* + clear frost — the line thin yet strong, lean and hard and through to the spirit, a model of spacing the white as in 'counting the white as black'; **treats the ink pig** (the puffy and the boneless); **Shi Chen**: hands cupped in salute + the *xun* + daybreak — balanced, mellow and orthodox, the rules all in their places, the positive specimen of '*guange*' within clerical script (rules are not a crime; turning rigid is); **treats those who run wild and lose the form**. How to use the compass: **first diagnose the illness, then choose the stele** — the four steles are not four dishes, they are four medicines; copy each of the four ways of writing the same character “于” ten times, and the fingers will remember the differences on the eyes' behalf.
:::
:::

::: intuition Seal script is a mute riddle; clerical script is a briefcase {#int-yama}
The **structure of the audience** of the two ancient styles determines their temperament. The audience of seal script is **gods and ancestors** (questions put by divination, merit set down in an inscription, stone incised — hence slow, round, symmetrical, decorative; the line itself is a rite). The audience of clerical script is **the yamen and the account book** (business urged through, copies made, notice given on a stele — hence fast, square, abbreviated and merged, readability first; **the form was pressed out by the quantity of official documents**). This explains the 'division of hand-feel' between the two: **to write seal script one must lower the heart rate** (long lines at an even speed; one gasp and the tip is exposed), **to write clerical script one must carry a rhythm** (silkworm head and wild-goose tail = the beat of one breath in and one breath out; an even speed is, on the contrary, dead and stiff). The practical return of practising ancient seal and clerical script is very concrete on a modern writing desk: **reading** (seals and inscribed boards use seal and clerical script in quantity; not being able to read them is embarrassing), **the origin of characters** (recognise the clerical change and you can explain why “春” is “三＋人＋日”, and you will no longer miswrite the middle of “泰”), **the appreciation of type design** (the thin horizontals and thick verticals of the Song typeface are **the printing legacy of the clerical change** — every day you are using a design from two thousand years ago).
:::

::: history One rise, one extinction and one revival of the two geological layers {#sec-fuxing}
**Qin**: Li Si made the small seal script; the Mount Tai and Langya **stone inscriptions** — the state aesthetic went onto stone for the first time (the original stones do not survive; what survive are Song rubbings and later traced recuttings — **even the 'code' is a copy; the history of script forms survives by transcription**); **Eastern Han**: clerical steles come to full maturity (the Xiping Stone Classics were written in red in clerical script; the holograph of Cai Yong 蔡邕, and the viewing and copying at the Imperial Academy); **from the Wei and Jin on**: clerical script turned into a 'full dress for the register' in the carving of steles (the headings of tomb tablets are mostly in clerical script, the main text in running-regular — **the division of full dress and everyday dress**); **Tang**: the iron-wire seal of Li Yangbing 李阳冰 took up again the lost music of Qin and Han — **three hundred years of seal script as a sole copy**; **Yuan and Ming**: seal and clerical both declined; Zhao Mengfu 赵孟頫 brought seal-script headings onto steles, yet the inherited method grew gradually thin; **Qing**: the stele school arose, and seal and clerical were **summoned back to life** — **Deng Shiru 邓石如 (1743–1793)** wrote seal and clerical with a long-tipped goat-hair brush, — Bao Shichen 包世臣, in *Yizhou shuangji* 艺舟双楫 ('The Paired Oars of the Boat of Art'), transmits the ranking, and holds him to be the completer **not seen for a thousand and several hundred years**; **Yi Bingshou 伊秉绶 (1754–1815)** made clerical script into **architecture** (horizontals level and verticals straight, like setting beams; the minimalist school of Han clerical); **He Shaoji 何绍基 (1799–1873)**, wrist turned back and held high, **brought seal, *zhouwen* and running script into *fenli*** (in a lifetime he wrote the character “寿” by the hundred and the thousand; the reversed wrist was his mark); **Zhao Zhiqian 赵之谦** recast seal and clerical in the manner of the Wei steles; **Wu Changshuo 吴昌硕** took the *Stone Drums* as the stock of his house, **melting seal, clerical and bronze-and-stone in one furnace** — the market of the Shanghai school and the mechanism of exhibitions made seal and clerical the protagonists of the exhibition hall for the first time; **the twentieth century**: oracle bones (from 1899), the Dunhuang Han slips, the Juyan slips, the Mawangdui silk manuscripts, the Guodian slips and the Shanghai Museum slips, the Tsinghua slips (entered the collection in 2008)… **excavated materials turn the 'clerical change' from a conclusion back into a process** — **the 'clerical' on the Qin slips of the Warring States is written all the way through to the 'clerical' on the steles of the Eastern Han, and the links between are no longer a blank**; the study of seal and clerical enters the century in which 'the rubbing gives place to the artefact'.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of the ancient styles
**Letting the brush do as it pleases and calling it seal script** (not knowing the *Shuowen*, and so 'drawing seal by feel' — **seal script has a method of characters received from a master** (the 9353 of the *Shuowen* and the system of radicals (section headers), [[chinese-characters/six-categories]]); a combined graph (*hewen*) must have a provenance; **write a wrong character into a seal, and once it is stamped it is a forgery**); **treating 'wild geese do not fly in pairs' as an exemption** (“那我只写一个雁尾其他全躺平” ('Then I shall write only one wild-goose tail, and let all the rest lie flat.') (Our translation.) — **horizontals other than the principal stroke must still have their rise and their fall**; one goose in flight does not equal eight chickens); **a superstitious faith in reprints of the copybooks of famous steles** (the text of a stele passes through three hands, **the chiseller, the rubbing-maker and the recutter**; a Song rubbing and a Qing rubbing differ as sharply as two steles — **a beginner must choose a good rubbing, photographically reproduced at the original size**; a saw-toothed edge to the stroke is mostly the fault of recutting, not the brush method of the ancients); **using modern script to guess at ancient script** (among ancient graphs, those close in form are extremely many (mixing up “戊己巳” and “口凵” is a beginner's common knowledge); **to recognise a character one must look it up and must remember it; guess, and the cart will certainly overturn**).
:::

::: exercise Long lines of the centred tip {level=1}
Small-seal practice: draw long lines in the shape of “之” (the basic training of jade-chopstick seal), forty characters. The line must be of even width, the turns must pass unseen, and the brush must travel without shaking. Test yourself on the **breathing method** — do you change breath every three strokes, or hold your breath until you have written it out?
::: solution
Marking: if the width of the line fluctuates by more than two tenths, **back into the furnace, at a slow speed** (the shake is not the error; **a change of speed is the true author of the shake**); if a sharp corner appears at a turn, practise **passing unseen** (while turning, twist the shaft of the brush slightly, and do not let the tip leave the original line); the record of the breathing must be honest (holding the breath = the heart rate rises = the chain of cause behind the shaking hand is caught by oneself; once it is caught, the aim of the teaching has been reached); extra credit: draw the same once more with a hard pen — and come to feel that **the principle of the centred tip has nothing to do with the tool**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Observer of the clerical change {level=2}
Choose the five characters “春、秦、泰、年、奉”; from a compendium of ancient script, or from a dictionary, copy out the seal form of each; then mark the exact place where, after the clerical change, a component was **conflated or reshaped**; write a 120-character 'accident report' (which stroke was straightened, which two pieces were merged).
::: solution
Marking: the sources of all five characters are fully labelled (the book of graphs / the number); the accident report **locates the matter down to the stroke** (at the level of “艸部三笔并为一横加一撇” ('the three strokes of the 艸 component are merged into one horizontal plus one left-falling stroke') (Our translation.)); one can explain why, after the clerical change, 'without study, one cannot read' (the components no longer look like a picture — **they have become signs**); a sample golden sentence: **“春”不是三人日，是草芽、太阳与舂捣之杵在隶变后的遗容** ('春 is not three people and the sun; it is the surviving face, after the clerical change, of a grass shoot, the sun, and the pestle of pounding, 舂.') (Our translation.)
:::

::: exercise Short essay: who killed a wild goose {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters. Taking the 'clerical change' as the subject, discuss **how efficiency in writing rewrote the writing system**. One 'counter-evidence guard-rail' is required (an exception that efficiency cannot explain, such as the retention, in *bafen*, of the intent of seal script, and the archaising decorative strokes in clerical script); and, by analogy with one contemporary change in writing (simplified characters on the internet, emoji, or abbreviations in Pinyin — choose any one), predict its 'degree of clerical change'.
::: solution
Points for marking: the mechanism is clear (**the clerical change was not one person at one time**: three stages, everyday fast writing → convention → official confirmation; a figure of the Cheng Miao type is a 'confirmer', not an inventor); the guard-rail meets the mark (one can cite the resistance of **archaism / ritual institutions / decoration** to efficiency — for example the *Xiping Stone Classics*, which set the refinement and regularity of clerical script against vulgar writing left unchecked); the analogy is kept in proportion (is 'yyds' a turning into signs? The prediction is allowed to be wrong, but the **criteria** must be written — the people who can read it, whether the structure has been given a rationale, whether a dictionary has taken it in); if the conclusion can settle on '**today's way of forming characters is the bill for the last clerical change**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The lesson in recognising the ancestors, the bill settled: seal script (the graceful and the continuous; the singing lesson of the centred tip; the art of lowering the heart rate), the clerical change (the break under the pressure of official documents; from the picture → to the code), clerical script (silkworm head and wild-goose tail, **wild geese do not fly in pairs**, the horizontal spread of *bafen*; the art of carrying a rhythm), the four steles (**four medicines, not four dishes**), use today (the deposit of two thousand years, in seals, inscribed boards and the Song typeface). With the two geological layers trodden solid, the next chapter climbs: three thousand years of script-types and calligraphic styles packed into a single chronological table — the panorama of the history of calligraphy, and a mnemonic for the periods.
:::
