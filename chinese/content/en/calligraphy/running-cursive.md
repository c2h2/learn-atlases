title: "Running and cursive script"
---

Regular script (standard script) has taken its stand; the next chapter teaches the body to move forward. “**楷如立，行如走，草如奔**” ('Regular script is like standing, running script (semi-cursive) like walking, cursive script (grass script) like a gallop.' (Our translation.)) — these three verbs come from an old saying in writings on calligraphy (the *Shupu* 《书谱》 (*Treatise on Calligraphy*) has “**真以点画为形质，使转为情性；草以点画为情性，使转为形质**” ('In regular script the dots and strokes are the form and substance, and the turns (*shǐzhuǎn* 使转) are the temperament; in cursive script the dots and strokes are the temperament, and the turns are the form and substance.' (Our translation.)), and it also says “**行近乎小真，所谓草不兼真，殆于专谨；草不通行，故非鸿伟**” ('Running script comes near to a small regular script; so-called cursive that does not also take in the regular comes near to mere scrupulousness; cursive that does not get through to running script is therefore not grand.' (Our translation.)) — the original wording differs; the general sense is the lineage of “行真草”, running, regular and cursive); this section splits walking and running into movements that can be learnt, and along the way clears a miscarriage of justice a thousand years old: “龙飞凤舞” ('dragons flying and phoenixes dancing').

## Walking: running script {#sec-xing}

::: definition Running script: between regular and cursive {#def-xing}
Running script was born of **regular script's need to be speeded up**: official documents written in regular script (真书) were too slow, and cursive script took too little account of anyone else — and so a rapid hand that was “真之小讹” ('a slight corruption of the regular') took shape as the second script of everyday use (tradition has it that **Liu Desheng** 刘德升 of the Later Han was the ancestor of running script — **the founding master is a figure of the legendary grade**, and, like Meng Tian 蒙恬 making the brush, it belongs to a historiography of retrospective recognition). Two branches: **running-regular** (*xíngkǎi* 行楷; close to regular script: 笔断意连 ('the brush breaks off, the intention continues'); the safe speed for official documents and letters — the territory of the three methods of linked strokes in the previous lesson) and **running-cursive** (*xíngcǎo* 行草; close to cursive script: linked strokes bunch into strings, and both feeling and speed are half a grade higher). Two model-books fixed the constitution of running script for all time: the ***Lanting Xu*** 《**兰亭序**》 (*Orchid Pavilion Preface*) — a story told of Emperor Taizong: after the wine had worn off, “**更书数百百本，终不能及**” ('he wrote it out again, several hundred copies, a hundred copies, and in the end could not equal it.' (Our translation.)) (the matter is in He Yanzhi 何延之, *Lanting ji* 《兰亭记》 (*Record of the Orchid Pavilion*); the manner is that of Tang fiction, and people of the Song already doubted it) — **the reason it cannot be surpassed is not that skill fell back; it is that the breeze, the wine and the friends of that evening cannot be bought back**; the ***Huairen ji Wang Shengjiao xu*** 《**怀仁集王圣教序**》 (*Preface to the Holy Teachings, with Wang's Characters Collected by Huairen*) — the monk Huairen 怀仁 **spent more than twenty years collecting the running-script characters of Wang Xizhi** 王羲之 and trace-copied and carved them onto stone (“集字” ('collecting characters') = picking out, character by character, the surviving traces of a master already dead, and piecing them into a new text — **the Tang ancestral edition of character-library retrieval**); for the thousand years after that, those who learnt running script passed one copy down in the family.
:::

## Galloping: cursive script {#sec-cao}

::: definition Clerical cursive, present-day cursive, wild cursive {#def-cao}
**Clerical cursive** (*zhāngcǎo* 章草; the rapid-writing layer of clerical script: it keeps the wild-goose tail, and every character stands alone — tradition has it that Shi You 史游 made the ***Jijiu zhang*** 《**急就章**》 (*Chapters for Quick Use*); it came out of a literacy primer; on the bamboo and wooden slips of the Han and the Jin this is its everyday face) → **present-day cursive** (*jīncǎo* 今草; the Two Wangs took away the wild-goose tail and increased the turns; the heads and tails of characters join into one *qi* (气, vital energy) — **from this point cursive has ‘characters’**: cursive symbols have a system handed from teacher to pupil, and the *Caojue* 《草诀》 (*Keys to Cursive*) and *Caoyun* 《草韵》 (*Cursive Rhymes*) of age after age set the symbols into rhymed lines and passed them on in copies) → **wild cursive** (*kuángcǎo* 狂草; Zhang Xu 张旭 and Huaisu 怀素: several characters in one stroke, the gaps between columns invading one another, the whole sheet cloud and mist — **a threshold experiment in the turn from ‘reading’ to ‘looking’**). Two iron rules: **cursive has its cursive method (*cǎofǎ* 草法); not one character may be invented for oneself** — the same cursive symbol often answers to more than one present-day character-form, and to read it one must consult a *Caoshu zidian* 《草书字典》 (*Dictionary of Cursive Script*); **to coin a cursive character in private is to alter a codebook in private**. Sun Guoting's 孙过庭 hard words: “**真以点画为形质，使转为情性；草以点画为情性，使转为形质**” ('In regular script the dots and strokes are the form and substance, and the turns are the temperament; in cursive script the dots and strokes are the temperament, and the turns are the form and substance.' (Our translation.)) “**草不兼真，殆于专谨；真不通草，殊非翰札**” ('Cursive that does not also take in the regular comes near to mere scrupulousness; regular script that does not get through to cursive is far from being proper correspondence.' (Our translation.)) — **the abandon of cursive is settled at the exchange rate**.
:::

::: example A pair of notes asking for leave {#ex-qingjia}
The same sentence — “昨夜风雨大作，瓦落庭中，晨起扫叶，遂迟半日” ('Last night the wind and the rain rose in force; tiles fell in the courtyard; at dawn I got up and swept the leaves, and so I was half a day late.' (Our translation.)) — written once as running-regular (addressed to the academic office) and once as wild cursive (a piece sent as a gift to a fellow enthusiast); assess each piece yourself: can the other person read it? If it cannot be read, whose fault is it?
::: solution
**The running-regular piece**, the standard for marking: linked strokes no more than three-tenths of the character, the connecting filament (*qiānsī* 牵丝) finer than the body of the stroke, each character's centre of gravity standing on its own — **that the recipient can read it with no training in calligraphy** is a hard requirement (cannot be read = the messenger has failed in the duty); **the wild-cursive piece**, the standard for marking: **the source in the cursive method can be checked character by character** (spot-check five characters in a *Dictionary of Cursive Script*; one place privately invented and it is sent back), the line has a **rhythm of dry and wet** (one load of ink all the way to the end is like cycling on the flat, with no slowing down and no sprint), the *qi* of the column runs through and **the character-groups breathe** (a group of three characters alternates with a character that stands alone). The core of the mark is in **the second question**: that wild cursive cannot be read **is not the reader's fault, and not the writer's fault either; it is the contract of the genre** — what wild cursive delivers is **a symphony of brush-traces**, and the readability of the text counts only as the accompaniment (**but a letter is not a symphony**: sending wild cursive to the academic office = ringing the doorbell with a symphony orchestra). A reflection for extra marks: before writing the cursive piece, ask yourself — **For whom is this piece written to be seen? How many characters? How large is the wall?**
:::
:::

::: intuition Wild cursive: the abandon that most keeps to the rules {#int-kuangcao}
“龙飞凤舞” ('dragons flying and phoenixes dancing'), in the speech of the public, is **a term of abuse** (= scrawling); in writings on calligraphy it is **a compliment of the highest grade** — provided the flight has method and the dance has a beat. The list of disciplines for wild cursive: **cursive method** (in the system of symbols not one character may be privately invented — **be a scholar of the script first, and a god of wine only after**); **turns** (Zhang Xu, “**听江声然后笔法始**” ('He listened to the sound of the river, and only then did his brush method begin.' (Our translation.)), and he also learnt from **Lady Gongsun** 公孙大娘 **dancing the sword** (*jianqi* 剑器), and so “神气抖擻，草书入神” ('his spirit was roused, and his cursive entered the divine.' (Our translation.)) (the preface to Du Fu's 杜甫 'Ballad of Watching a Disciple of Lady Gongsun Dance the Sword', 《观公孙大娘弟子舞剑器行》) — **the line is a dance of time, not a scribbling-over of space**); **composition** (章法; the **black** over the whole sheet must queue, and the **white** must queue even more — character-groups, the *qi* of the column, and density and openness, like the entry list of the parts in a symphony); **drawing in and letting go** (wild, but not howling: **one point of explosion, and the whole piece holds the ranks** — Huaisu's *Zixu* 《自叙》 (*Account of Myself*), even where the wildness reaches its extreme, still has a signature in small regular script; behind the god of wine stands an accountant). The moral for teaching: **abandon is the highest tax bracket of ability, not the giving-up of paying the tax** — whoever says “我写字随意但好看” ('I write as I please, but it looks good.' (Our translation.)), please first hand in the cursive-method tax return.
:::

::: history A short history of rapid writing: from the files to the exhibition hall {#sec-kaixie}
**Qin and Han**: an old account of the Han has it that “秦时官狱职务繁，初有隶书以趣约易” ('In Qin times the duties of the offices and the law courts were heavy, and clerical script first arose so as to tend towards the concise and the easy.' (Our translation.)) (recorded in the postface to the *Shuowen jiezi* 《说文解字》 (*Explaining Graphs and Analysing Characters*)) — **rapid writing is the excrement of administration**: the orthodox form copes with court assemblies; only the cursory hand can eat up the backlog; **Han**: Shi You taught the *Jijiu zhang* to children, and clerical cursive became **the first cursive script at the level of a literacy primer**; **Eastern Han**: a social fever for cursive script; **Zhao Yi's** 赵壹 ***Fei caoshu*** 《**非草书**》 ('Against Cursive Script') bitterly attacks the people of his time for “师尚杜、崔” ('taking Du and Cui as honoured masters'), practising until they were possessed in the manner of “夕惕不息” ('vigilant at dusk and never at rest'), and Zhao Yi rebukes them with “临事无意” ('faced with the task, they have no intent') (the general sense of the original) — **the first trial record in history of 'the practical party vs the expressive party'**; **Wei and Jin**: the present-day cursive of the Two Wangs took its settled form; **Tang**: Zhang Xu checked into the history of calligraphy by way of drunkenness (Du Fu, 'Song of the Eight Immortals of the Wine Cup', 《饮中八仙歌》: “**张旭三杯草圣传，脱帽露顶王公前，挥毫落纸如云烟**” ('Zhang Xu, three cups, and the sage of cursive is passed on; he takes off his cap and bares his head before princes and dukes; he swings the brush, and it falls on the paper like cloud and mist.' (Our translation.))), and Huaisu's ***Zixu*** collects contemporary comments from Yan Zhenqing 颜真卿, Dai Shulun 戴叔伦 and their fellows — **wild cursive obtained its first contemporary five-star compilation**; **Song**: Mi Fu 米芾, “**臣书刷字**” ('Your servant's writing brushes the characters.' (Our translation.)) (*Shushi* 《书史》 (*History of Calligraphy*)) — **for the first time, speed announced its own name**; **late Ming**: Xu Wei 徐渭 and Zhang Ruitu 张瑞图 — wild cursive became **the psychological fiction of an age of disorder**, and swelling ink (涨墨) and the twisted brush-tip (绞锋) are the brain-waves of the age; **Qing**: Fu Shan 傅山, “**宁拙毋巧，宁丑毋媚，宁支离毋轻滑，宁直率毋安排**” ('Rather awkward than clever, rather ugly than seductive, rather fragmentary than light and slippery, rather direct than arranged.' (Our translation.)) (*Zuozi shi erzhi* 《作字示儿侄》 ('On Writing Characters, Shown to My Sons and Nephews')) — **a licence pre-issued for every avant-garde of ugly posture**; **modern times**: the fountain pen and the keyboard, one after the other, took over rapid writing, and **wild cursive withdrew entirely into the exhibition hall** — the practical post of 'fast' disappeared, and only **the aesthetic of fast** was left.
:::

::: warning Four mines of running and cursive
**Using “龙飞凤舞” ('dragons flying and phoenixes dancing') as praise** (words with which an outsider compliments someone; an insider hears them as “无政府” ('no government') — **if you praise someone's characters as good to look at, please point to the particular place**: “这行枯笔痛快” ('the dry brush in this column is keenly satisfying.' (Our translation.)) beats “有气势” ('it has momentum') ten thousand times over); **plotting cursive when regular script has not come off** (“我楷书太板，不如练草” ('My regular script is too stiff; better to practise cursive.' (Our translation.)) — **a change of script by way of escape** — the stiffness is the muscles of the hand not having learnt to adjust the tip (调锋); cursive will only magnify it into a shake; the verdict in plain words: **if regular script does not form the person, cursive is sure to slide**); **stretching one stroke and passing it off as ‘one-stroke writing’** (一笔书) (true continuity depends on **turns**, not on **dragging** — where the middle of the line is limp, length only exposes the length); **taking the signature as graduation in cursive** (a signature is a **personal trademark**, not a **public text** — the trademark is lawful; cursive script faces the accounts).
:::

::: exercise Fifty questions on the cursive method {level=1}
Consult a *Dictionary of Cursive Script*, and for each of the first ten characters of “天地玄黄、宇宙洪荒” ('Heaven and earth, the dark and the yellow; the universe, a primeval wild.' (Our translation.)) find **one** form in present-day cursive; copy them out and mark the source of the symbol; then write the ten characters from memory, and check yourself: to how many tenths do they agree with the dictionary?
::: solution
Marking: all ten characters have a source (a page number, or the name of the model-book, is the ‘tax return’); any form invented by oneself is sent back (‘more or less’ means a great deal less — a cursive symbol read wrong); for anyone whose writing from memory agrees to less than eight-tenths, **next week the quantity is halved and the number of times through is doubled** — the foundation of rapid writing is rote memory.
:::
:::

::: exercise One sentence, three ways {level=2}
Write “学然后知不足” ('One studies, and only then knows what one lacks.' (Our translation.)) in each of three ways: regular script (sent to an exhibition), running-regular (written on the blackboard), running-cursive (given to a friend); attach a manual of 120 characters converting ‘speed—formality’ (at every place where strokes are linked or reduced, state why that is allowed at that gear).
::: solution
Marking: the **difference of gear among the three pieces can be ranked by an outsider** (even without the explanation, the higher and lower in degree of formality can be told apart); every linked stroke in the manual has a reason (“行草件此二字牵丝，因笔顺同路” ('on the running-cursive piece these two characters have a connecting filament, because the stroke order takes the same road.' (Our translation.)) = a pass; “感觉顺” ('it feels smooth') = a half-pass, with a further question on stroke order); if the **notion** “草书=乱写” ('cursive script = scrawling') appears, there is zero tolerance — a wrong notion is marked down harder than a wrong stroke.
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the ethics of speed {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters: from “the anger of Zhao Yi's *Fei caoshu*” to the contemporary “dispute between the electronic signature and the handwritten signature”, choose either end, and discuss “who pays the **readability tax** brought by the raising of the speed of writing”; the ‘contract’ framework of this lesson must be used (different genres = contracts at different rates of tax).
::: solution
Points for marking: historical fact and argument have a clear division of labour (Zhao Yi is **evidence**, not the **claim**; one must deal with the structure of ‘the practical party versus the expressive party’, rather than taking a side); the contract framework is used accurately (a ranking, and the reasons, of the ‘readability tax rate’ of four genres: the official document, the private letter, the exhibit and the trademark); if one writes of the present day, one must distinguish the **dispute over efficiency** from the **dispute over aesthetics** (wild cursive's withdrawal into the exhibition hall and the ugly writing of doctors are **not the same topic** — whoever talks of them mixed together loses marks for logic).
:::

::: summary
The lesson on speed settles its account: running script (two branches, two model-books for a constitution, the Orchid Pavilion cannot be bought back), cursive (three ranks, the cursive-method tax return, Sun Guoting's exchange rate), the wild (three disciplines, one point of explosion and the whole piece holds the ranks), the mines (do not flee regular script, do not drag the line, do not take the signature as graduation). The legs can walk; the next chapter turns back to acknowledge the ancestors: seal script and clerical script — **the geological stratum of the character-forms of today**, and that quiet revolution, the clerical change.
:::
