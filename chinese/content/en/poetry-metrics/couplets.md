---
title: "Parallelism and couplets"
---

The previous chapter said that parallelism (对仗, the parallel couplet) is "assigned to the two middle couplets", like the format in which an official document enters its proofs; this chapter opens a separate case for it. **Parallelism is the bare-form art of Chinese — it does not rely on morphological change, nor on loanwords from foreign languages, but can raise a symmetrical building on word order and juxtaposition alone** ([[modern-grammar/sentence-constituents]]: Chinese grammar gives weight to word order, and parallelism pushes that trait as far as legislation in aesthetics). Its independently issued edition is called the **couplet** (对联): the smallest literary unit the Chinese have, and the classical genre with the highest rate of survival — at the Spring Festival, households that have never read Li Shangyin 李商隐 paste couplets all the same.

## Three lines of acceptance {#def-sanxian}

::: definition Category, level and oblique, syntax {#def-yanshou}
Parallelism has to pass three scales at once. **(1) Category** (the domain of meaning) — celestial things match celestial things, geographical things match geographical things, plants and trees match plants and trees, colours match colours, numbers match numbers. The practical classification used by Tang writers is finer than word class (the tradition of matching by "doors" divides all the way down to "birds and beasts, flowers and trees, dress and ornament" … ***Shenglü qimeng* 声律启蒙 ('A Primer of Tones and Rhymes'): "云对雨，雪对风，晚照对晴空" — "Clouds match rain, snow matches wind, the evening glow matches a clear sky." (Our translation.) The real textbook of Chinese elementary schooling is a database of matching pairs: literacy, common knowledge, and level and oblique tones, three in one; at seven one begins to memorise the index**). **(2) Level and oblique in opposition** (the answering line stands opposite at each rhythmic point: this is the "opposition" (对) clause of the regulated poem (*lüshi*), projected onto the side of parallelism — **meanings contract a marriage, and tones pick a fight: that is the normal state of parallelism in regulated verse (recent-style poetry)**). **(3) Syntactic isomorphism** (subject–predicate matches subject–predicate, verb–object matches verb–object) — note: **isomorphism of syntax is not sameness of meaning**; sameness of meaning is an offence (see 合掌, "clasped palms"). The three grades of strength, from the bottom up, are **well-formed (level and oblique) → strictly neat (category) → strict and alive (syntax and momentum)** — whoever checks only the first two layers is a couplet printer.
:::

::: definition Four ways of matching, and one charge {#def-sizhong}
**正对 ("parallelism of the same sense")** (the two lines complement each other in the same meaning) is the steadiest, and also the most dangerous — Liu Xie 刘勰, fifteen hundred years ago, had already delivered the hard saying: "**反对为优，正对为劣**" — "Opposed matching is superior; matching of the same sense is inferior." (Our translation.) (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, "Parallel Phrasing" (丽辞) — **repetition of the same sense is treated, in aesthetics, as plagiarism**). **反对 ("opposed parallelism")** ("**蝉噪林逾静，鸟鸣山更幽**" — "Cicadas din, and the wood is the stiller; birds sing, and the mountain is the more remote." (Our translation.) — writing stillness by means of noise: the two lines are opposed and yet complete each other, two exposures of one photograph). **串对／流水对 ("the linked couplet" / "the flowing couplet")** (the meaning passes through the couplet as water does: **"即从巴峡穿巫峡，便下襄阳向洛阳"** — "Straightway from Ba Gorge, through Wu Gorge; then down to Xiangyang, and on towards Luoyang." (Our translation.) — **four place names plus four verbs racing the whole way; every stop-board of parallelism is in place, and the long-distance coach does not stop**; and again "**欲穷千里目，更上一层楼**" — "If the eye would exhaust a thousand li (里, a traditional measure of distance; the length varied by period), climb one storey higher." (Our translation.) — **a conditional sentence broken into two halves, one couplet loaded with an "if … then …": the flowing couplet is the extreme sport of parallelism, running the straight line of prose in the shackles of symmetry**). **借对 ("the borrowed match")** (a character borrowed for the occasion: borrowing the sound — "**清**" may be borrowed as "**青**"; borrowing the sense — when "**少**" in "**少年**" is read in the departing tone, it is entitled to match "**长**": one character holding two jobs on the premises — **the shadow-employee system inside parallelism**). The charge: **合掌 ("clasped palms")** ("**天地**" matched with "**乾坤**" — "heaven and earth" with "qian and kun" — and, on top of a letter from home that "抵万金" ("is worth ten thousand in gold"), pairing it again with "值千金" ("is worth a thousand in gold") — two sentences wearing one and the same coat: of an eight-line estate, not an inch may be given away for nothing — note: the "**三**" and "**万**" in "**烽火连三月，家书抵万金**" — "Beacon fires have gone on for three months; a letter from home is worth ten thousand in gold." (Our translation.) — are indefinite numbers in a rising hyperbole; in teaching they are often counted as a loose match (宽对) and allowed to pass, not as clasped palms; **the red line of clasped palms is "the meanings coincide completely", not "numbers appearing together"**).
:::

::: example A match in four layers: a specimen of parallelism in the quatrain {#ex-siceng}
"**两个黄鹂鸣翠柳，一行白鹭上青天。窗含西岭千秋雪，门泊东吴万里船。**"

Two yellow orioles sing in the emerald willows;
one line of white egrets climbs the blue sky.
The window holds the snow of a thousand autumns on the western ridge;
at the gate are moored boats from Eastern Wu, ten thousand li away.
(Our translation.)

— some say this is "a poem spliced together from two couplets" (a disputed piece: the four lines form pairs each with each, and there is no 起承转合, "opening, development, turn and closure"). Please: (1) take the parallelism apart layer by layer (number, colour, animal, time and space); (2) answer: **why is a display of parallelism with no "turn" still a masterpiece — has the quatrain's (*jueju*) law of the "turn", from the previous chapter, failed here?**; (3) the position and the function of the two verbs in "窗含" and "门泊"; (4) what "西岭—东吴" and "千秋—万里" are matching.
::: solution
(1) Number (两个／一行; 千秋／万里) + colour (翠／青) + animal (黄鹂／白鹭) + time and space (**time matched with space, the two couplets locking each other: in the third line the time is "千秋", and the space is what "窗含" holds; in the fourth line the space is "万里", and the time hides in "东吴" (a memory of the Three Kingdoms) — **the highest grade of parallelism: beyond noun matching noun, the two couplets are mirrors of each other in time and space**). (2) The law of the turn has not failed; **it has been exchanged for a law of framing**. The quatrain turns by time; this poem relies on **the direction of space** — one scene to every two lines, from near to far, from movement back to mooring. The fourth line's "门泊" draws the whole back from flight to the gateway: **the equivalent of the turn = the camera's last pull-back** (**the charge of "a poem made of two couplets" is rebutted by this: Du Fu 杜甫 is using parallelism to make a montage — symmetrical fragments cut into a record of a journey that never got away**. The poem was written at the time at the thatched cottage; there was hope the disorder would subside, and the date of sailing was hard to fix. "门泊万里船" = **the answering line of wanting to leave and stopping where one is**). (3) "含" and "泊" both stand in the third-character position (a stressed beat of the rhythm): **one is still, one makes fast — the mountain is packed into the window, and the distance is tethered at the gate: the verbs are the only drivers of these two couplets**. (4) Geography matching geography + age matching age + **direction matching orientation (west matching east)**: **the four walls of one and the same thatched cottage are opened by parallelism onto four directions — a strict match (工对) is not a shackle; it is the sapper who opens the windows**.
:::
:::

## The couplet: an independent issue {#def-duilian}

::: definition The smallest literary unit {#def-zuixiao}
The couplet = parallelism leaving the parent body to trade on its own: **the upper line ends on an oblique tone, the lower line ends on a level tone** (a door-closing sound above, a drawn-out sound below — **"oblique to begin, level to close" (仄起平收) is the ear's question and its answer**; look back to the prolonged sound and the cut-off sound of the first chapter). Door couplets, birthday couplets, elegiac couplets, wedding couplets, trade couplets, and quotation couplets (lines picked out of old poems and paired — **fan writing in the age of copyright**). The anatomy of famous pieces: **"海纳百川，有容乃大；壁立千仞，无欲则刚"** — "The sea takes in a hundred rivers, and capacity makes it great; the cliff stands a thousand ren (仞, a traditional measure of height; the length varied by period) sheer, and freedom from desire makes it firm." (Our translation.) (Lin Zexu 林则徐) — **a self-inscription by a couplet of maxims: the "use" of the couplet is promoted from decoration to a motto given a body**; the long couplet of the Grand View Tower in Kunming (Sun Ranweng 孙髯翁; the received text is one hundred and eighty characters) — **Han towers, Tang pagodas, Song steles … climbing to gaze, mourning what is past, and the expression of feeling, all packed into a single couplet: the long couplet proves that a small genre can also be expanded into a container for prose** (the title "the first long couplet under heaven" is less an aesthetic judgement than **a Guinness record in an arms race of character-counts**). **The trade couplet is the advertising slot of earlier times** (the pharmacy's "**但愿世间人无病，何妨架上药生尘**" — "Let there be no illness among the people of the world; what harm if the medicines on the shelves gather dust?" (Our translation.) — **parallelism completes a two-line financing of brand values**; a playful rewritten version in circulation turns the wish entirely upside down into its opposite — **rewriting a couplet = a folk second creation, and a reply to a bad review**).
:::

::: intuition Symmetry is the mother tongue of Chinese {#int-duichen}
Indo-European languages align a sentence by changes of word form; Chinese relies on **juxtaposition**: which of subject and predicate sits next to which, and how many syllables stand against how many syllables — **before a child has ever got a "matching pair" (对子) right, they can already say "你一拳我一脚", "再来一次没再来两次"** ("a punch from you, a kick from me"; "come once more, not come twice more." (Our translation.)). **Parallelism is not a subject on the rhetoric course; it is the developing fluid of grammar** ([[rhetoric/figures-of-form]], the chapter on antithesis (对偶): four-character idioms (*chengyu*) are almost the fossil bed of parallelism — "山高水长" ("mountains high, waters long"), "眼高手低" ("eyes high, hands low"): **in the lexicalisation of Chinese, symmetry is a powerful mould**). Hence the cultural cost of parallelism in the regulated poem is extremely low, and its acceptance extremely high: **it turns "it reads smoothly" into "one can see that it is fair" — symmetry is the feel, as form, of justice**. This also explains why the couplet is proof against every poison: **poetry changes its genre, and can die out; symmetry does not. It only changes its coat — from the parallel fours and coupled sixes of parallel prose (*pianwen*) to the advertising line "怕上火，喝×××"** ("Afraid of internal heat? Drink ×××." (Our translation.)). **Parallelism has never left the stage; it has only stopped signing its name**.
:::

::: history The ledger of symmetry {#sec-zhangben}
**In the *Book of Songs*, "昔我往矣，杨柳依依；今我来思，雨雪霏霏"** — "Long ago I went, and the willows trailed; now I come, and the snow falls thick." (Our translation.) — **the genes of parallelism were already dominantly expressed in the age of the four-character line**; the serried parallels in the elaborate display of the **Han rhapsody** (*fu*), and **parallel prose** with its "俪采百字之偶" — "pairing the ornament of a hundred characters into couples" (Our translation.) (*The Literary Mind*, "Parallel Phrasing") — **parallelism promoted from a figure of rhetoric to a genre**; **Tang**: the regulated poem is fixed as an institution in which "**the two middle couplets must be parallel**"; **Song**: lines of antithesis go independent as collections of anecdotes of "**clever matches**" (巧对) (Ouyang Xiu 欧阳修's *Guitian lu* 归田录, "A Record of Returning to the Fields", records many); **Ming**: Zhu Yuanzhang 朱元璋 promotes spring couplets (**peachwood charms (桃符) give way to "spring couplets": official promotion sends parallelism down to the countryside** — **the shortest genre survives on a large scale by administration and commerce, not by anthologies of poetry**); **Qing**: **the study of parallelism rises to a methodology (Ruan Yuan 阮元's theory of *wenyan*, "Classical Chinese", takes coupled sound as the making of patterned writing — a scholar of the Classics issues parallelism a licence in classical learning) + the golden age of the long couplet (the Grand View Tower and the Yellow Crane Tower each claim first place)**; **from the Republic on**: calling for matches (征对), and "absolute" pairs (绝对子), are a long-running amusement of newspaper supplements and of the wit of the streets (the old account of the cleverness of matching "独角兽" with "比目鱼" lies in this: the character "比" is not a number, and yet the fish's name carries a number of its own — **parallelism run as a riddle business**); **today**: **spring-couplet contests, couplets seeking a spouse, the antithesis of clickbait headlines ("听我说完，先别投票"** — "Hear me through; do not vote yet." (Our translation.)) — **the A/B testing of parallelism in the digital age**; *Shenglü qimeng* and *Liweng duiyun* 笠翁对韵 ("Rhymes for Matching by the Old Man of the Straw Cape") retreat from elementary schooling to the playlist of prenatal education — **the age of the readers of parallelism textbooks keeps moving downward: once a genre stops producing new works, it begins to occupy the kindergarten**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of parallelism
**Matching for the sake of matching, so that every line is clasped palms** (**parallelism of the same sense = a typesetting accident; the ground parallelism stands on is for opening windows, not for looking in a mirror**); **the superstition that a strict match = a good match** (**strictness is the floor, not the ceiling: masters of the High Tang mostly use loose matches, and yet their *qi* (vital force) runs high; in the Late Tang strict matches grow denser, and the compass of that *qi* turns small — **strictness and abundance are two axes, and a high-scoring script is often strict without being conscious of it**); **treating a borrowed match as a metrical fault** (**borrowing a sound and borrowing a sense are lawful subjects, not wrong characters — if the pair can be found in a *leishu* (a classified compendium), do not start by marking the script**); **forcing the level-and-oblique rules of the couplet onto the regulated poem** (**a couplet requires only that the final characters answer each other, oblique then level; a long couplet is free in its pauses (*judou*): the regulated poem is a statutory marriage, the couplet a de facto marriage — different laws apply**).
:::

::: exercise A database of matching pairs {level=1}
In the format of *Shenglü qimeng*, compose a small set of contemporary matching pairs (starting at the level of "屏对键，码对云", "screen matches key, code matches cloud"): **they must contain one class of "things of technology" matched with one class of "things of nature"**, and the category of each must be marked. In peer review: whose pairs would a seven-year-old child memorise?
::: solution
Marking: **the category labels are accurate** (do not mix such headings as number, colour and direction); **the cross-boundary match holds** ("屏对窗", "screen matches window": both are squares that give off light — **the charming point of parallelism is "so they were the same class"**); **a test of memorability** (smoothness on the tongue = a by-product of getting level and oblique right; read it aloud to a third person, and if they can repeat it, it passes) — **the KPI of elementary primers has not changed to this day**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Taking advertising parallels apart {level=2}
Collect three modern advertisements, headlines or slogans that use antithesis, and take each apart into **the category layer, the level-and-oblique layer and the syntactic layer**, and say whether it is clasped palms; then, for one of them, write a rewritten couplet in a "folk bad-review version" (in imitation of the second creation practised on the trade couplet).
::: solution
Marking: **each of the three layers can be checked on its own** (you may not say, in a general way, "it rhymes very well"); **the judgement of clasped palms gives a reason** ("品质超群，卓越非凡" — "quality beyond the crowd, excellence out of the common." (Our translation.) = clasped palms; "多快好省" ("more, faster, better, more economical") = a coordination of four terms, and does not count as parallelism); the satirical logic of the rewritten couplet holds, and the parallelism is not broken (**a bad review, too, must pass the lines of acceptance — this is what a national education in parallelism has produced**).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: symmetry is justice {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim that "**parallelism is the Chinese formal training in 'fairness': two sentences equal in status, symmetrical in force, each yielding half a step**". You must draw on at least these four: **the taxonomy of categories**, **the precedent that opposed matching is superior**, **the "concession" of the flowing couplet**, **the couplet's oblique beginning and level close**; and you must deal with one opposing case: **parallelism manufactures a false equality (setting unequal things out as a parallel match is rhetorical manipulation)** — you may answer it with a case of "advertising parallelism".
::: solution
Points for marking: the four mechanisms (**category = the myriad things are equal before a taxonomy with the force of law; opposition = the opposing side is seated at the same table; the flowing couplet = the sentence patterns yield place to each other; oblique and level = one sound asks and one sound answers**); the handling of the other side (**concede the point: parallelism can indeed issue an equal uniform to those who are not equal (the rhetorical parallelism (排比) of "与某某共命运", "sharing a fate with so-and-so") — and therefore the three lines of acceptance in this lesson are at the same time tools for seeing through it: check for clasped palms (emptiness), check for a category that exceeds its powers (claiming kinship), check for an asymmetry of force (a match of one solid and one empty = the tone of propaganda)**); if the conclusion can be written "**formal equality is not real equality, but without the training of formal equality, real equality does not even have a room in which to rehearse**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The case for symmetry is opened and closed: acceptance (**three lines, category / level and oblique / syntax: well-formed → strictly neat → strict and alive**), ways of matching (**the thousand-year precedent that opposed matching is superior, the headlong run of the flowing couplet, the shadow employee of the borrowed match**), the specimen (**the four lines of the thatched cottage = using parallelism to open windows; the verb is the only driver**), the couplet (**the question and answer of oblique-to-begin and level-to-close, a container of one hundred and eighty characters, the advertising slot of the reply to a bad review**), the intuition (**symmetry is the mother tongue of Chinese: it has not left the stage; it has only stopped signing its name**). Method, in one sentence: **on seeing a match, first check for clasped palms, then look for the window**. The next chapter loosens the bonds: ancient-style verse — how the negative teaching-text of the regulated poem, working in reverse, kept Li Bai 李白 and Du Fu fed.
:::
