title: "Imagery and allusion"
---

The first eight chapters measured the container: level and oblique tones, rhyme, line form, the pattern. This chapter opens the box: **why “月” ('the moon') is cold with no need of explanation, why “柳” ('the willow'), the moment it appears, means a plea to stay, why one slap on “栏杆” ('the railing') and the person is in sorrow** — the image (*yixiang*) is the public ledger of poetry in Chinese, and allusion is the adding of a record to that ledger; **only when these two are understood does the course on metre turn from acoustics into economics**.

## The public ledger {#def-zhangben}

::: definition The image: a shared object with its meaning pre-installed {#def-yixiang}
The image is not a figure of speech; it is a **protocol**: generation after generation of famous pieces write a meaning into a thing, and later writers, when they cite it, cite the meaning along with it. “月” was registered by Li Bai and those like him as the account for “homeland and time” (边月, 'the frontier moon'; 秦月, 'the moon over Qin'; 明月几时有, 'how long has the bright moon been there'); “柳” puns on “留” ('to stay'), and the breaking of a willow at Baqiao became, from the writers of the Tang on, the pre-installed program for a farewell; “雁” has carried a letter (the allusion of Su Wu's letter by wild goose stored the postbox inside the bird for good); “栏杆／高楼” = the posture of looking and not seeing (Wen Tingyun, “独倚望江楼…斜晖脉脉水悠悠，肠断白蘋洲” — 'Leaning alone, I gaze from the river tower… The slanting light lingers with feeling, the water runs on and on, and my heart breaks at White-Duckweed Isle.' (Our translation.) — **four items bound at the factory: leaning alone, the tower, the water, the broken heart**); “长亭” = the official table of distances turned into a unit for measuring parting (a long pavilion every ten *li* — the traditional mile, whose length changed from age to age: **the post-road system requisitioned by lyric feeling**). Three steps: **object-image (the moon) → verbal image (the poeticised spelling of “明月”, 'bright moon') → the image turned into an allusion (“月” comes with a homesickness field already installed)** — read as far as the third step, and **to read one line is to decompress a cabinet of texts from the past**: the image is the object library of Chinese poetry, and allusion is its reference count (the “compression” of this lesson has a formal audit in the example).
:::

::: definition Four operations of allusion {#def-si-caozuo}
**Direct use** (正用: citing according to the original case: “持节云中，何日遣冯唐” — 'Holding a tally at Yunzhong — on what day will they send a Feng Tang?' (Our translation.) — Su Shi adopts himself into the story of Feng Tang, a memorial-style profession of loyalty that seeks reinstatement); **contrary use** (反用: extraditing the allusion into the dispute now in hand — the same document, signed with the opposite annotation: Wang Anshi, 'Song of Mingfei' ('Song of the Bright Consort'), “君不见咫尺长门闭阿娇，人生失意无南北” — 'Have you not seen: within a foot, Changmen shuts Aijiao in; the disappointments of a life have no north or south.' (Our translation.) — one line tears up the thousand-year script of “resenting the painter / longing for the Han palace” — disappointment has nothing to do with geography); **overturning the case** (翻案: reopening the court on the original verdict: “胜败兵家事不期，包羞忍耻是男儿。江东子弟多才俊，卷土重来未可知” — 'Victory and defeat, in a soldier's affairs, come unlooked for; to pocket shame and bear disgrace is to be a man. The youth of Jiangdong are many and able; whether they will raise the dust and come again is not yet known.' (Our translation.) — Du Mu appeals Xiang Yu's self-defence up to the Tang court of public opinion; Li Qingzhao, “至今思项羽，不肯过江东” — 'To this day I think of Xiang Yu, who would not cross to the east of the river.' (Our translation.) — overturns it back again: **the same stretch of the Wu River: what the people of the Song wanted was shame, what the people of the Tang wanted was mettle — allusion is a hearing that crosses dynasties, and each generation testifies anew**); **unmarked adaptation** (化用: citation without a trace = the highest grade: “碧云天，黄花地” — 'A sky of blue-green cloud, a ground of yellow flowers.' (Our translation.) — buys up the whole of Fan Zhongyan, and nobody shouts to have the thief caught; “明月几时有，把酒问青天” — 'How long has the bright moon been there? Wine in hand, I ask the blue sky.' (Our translation.) — an old theme of the Tang, newly made — **the legal reading of unmarked adaptation: cite until nobody brings a suit, and it is the public domain**).
:::

::: example Xin Qiji's compression ratio: *Yongyu le* · 'Remembering the Past at Beigu Pavilion in Jingkou' {#ex-yongyule}
“千古江山，英雄无觅孙仲谋处……想当年，金戈铁马，气吞万里如虎。元嘉草草，封狼居胥，赢得仓皇北顾。四十三年，望中犹记，烽火扬州路。可堪回首，佛狸祠下，一片神鸦社鼓。凭谁问：廉颇老矣，尚能饭否。”

'Rivers and hills of a thousand ages; a hero, and nowhere to seek Sun Zhongmou… I think of that year: spears of gold and horses of iron, a spirit swallowing ten thousand *li* like a tiger. Yuanjia, hasty and careless — the rite of *feng* at Langjuxu — and what was won was a panicked look back to the north. Forty-three years: within this gaze I still remember the beacon fires on the road to Yangzhou. How can one bear to look back? Beneath the shrine of Foli, a stretch of spirit-crows and drums of the altar. On whom can one rely to ask: Lian Po is old — can he still eat?' (Our translation.)

Please: ① under a time limit, and with no notes, read off **the polarity of each allusion towards the context of its own day** (direct / contrary / overturning the case) — Sun Quan, Liu Yu, Liu Yilong, Foli and Lian Po, each speaking which sentence on whose behalf; ② “凭谁问” ('on whom can one rely to ask') **comes to rest on Lian Po**: why it must be an **allusion in the form of a question**, of the kind “尚能饭否” ('can he still eat?'); ③ confiscate the notes entirely: does this poem still “live”? — run an operational acceptance test on *gé* 隔 and *bù gé* 不隔 ('a barrier', and 'no barrier'; Wang Guowei: “语语都在目前，便是不隔” — 'If every phrase stands there before the eyes, that is no barrier.' (Our translation.)).
::: solution
① A table of polarity: **Sun Quan / Liu Yu = direct use, and it contains satire** (nowhere to seek a hero — what is praised is Jingkou, what is cursed is Lin'an: borrowing the ancients **to run a blank-control experiment**); **Liu Yilong = a warning by contrary use** (“元嘉草草”, 'Yuanjia, hasty and careless' — borrowing the disastrous northern expedition of Emperor Wen of Song, and pre-writing the verdict on Han Tuozhou's northern expedition: **the textbook of saying the straight thing by way of its opposite**); **the shrine of Foli = the fiercest image and the fiercest allusion at once** (the shrine of the enemy ruler has its incense flourishing — **the people of the occupied region are sacrificing to the invader: one line of picture is worth ten memorials**); **Lian Po = likening himself, and defending himself** (an old general with slanderers ringed about him, watching — putting “for the war” and “cast aside” on the books at the same time). ② An allusion in the form of a question = **handing the power of decision to the question**: allusion by statement is a claim of title; **allusion by question is a bid** — “尚能饭否” gives no answer; the answer is for the court to give: **the highest diplomatic form of allusion**. ③ The acceptance test: once the notes have been confiscated, this song lyric (*ci*) is **half alive** — “神鸦社鼓” ('spirit-crows and altar drums'), “金戈铁马” ('spears of gold and horses of iron') and “廉颇老矣” ('Lian Po is old') survive on the feel of the language and on the picture (**the part that is *bu ge***); “元嘉” ('Yuanjia') and “佛狸” ('Foli'), the moment the notes are lost, lose contact ( *ge* ). The verdict: Xin's song lyric bets that the audience's public dictionary is still there — it stakes the part that meets a barrier on the age, and collects payment first on the part that meets none (picture, tone, structure); the bet was won for a thousand years, until the dictionary was issued in a new edition (teaching notes today = fresh entries issued for a dictionary that has fallen out of repair). *Ge* and *bu ge* are not properties of the work; they are the difference between the work and the reader's stock (“Allusion is a compression algorithm: a shared dictionary = lossless; an expired dictionary = encryption” — the conclusion of the audit in the example).
:::

::: intuition The speech for the defence of the book-bag {#int-shudai}
The testimony on the other side is long as well. Zhong Rong's *Shipin* 诗品 (*Grades of Poetry*) judges literature by “自然英旨” ('a natural excellence of purport'), and records “补假” ('patching by borrowing'), the borrowing of other people's set phrases, as a fault of writing — **the first charter against allusion**. From Yuan Haowen on, critics have again and again attacked the line of the “獭祭” ('the otter's sacrifice') — **they divide allusion into two grades, “使事” ('deploying a matter') and “掉书袋” ('shaking the book-bag'), and the verdict looks entirely to whether the allusion is still doing work**. On the side of the defence: Huang Tingjian's “点铁成金” ('touching iron and making it gold') and “脱胎换骨” ('shedding the embryo and changing the bones'), as recorded by Huihong in *Lengzhai yehua* 冷斋夜话 (*Night Talks from the Cold Studio*) — **reading defined as a raw-materials industry: the question is not whether one uses it or not, but whether one transforms it or not**. The *shenyun* school of Wang Shizhen (*shenyun*, 'spirit and resonance') goes to the other extreme — **the fastidiousness of “不着一字” ('not a single character set down') is in fact allusion as well (negative allusion: taking the refusal to cite as itself a citation, and pretending one has never read a book)**. **The truth is a compromise: density is not the crime; running empty is — an allusion either carries a picture (Xin), or carries irony (Wang), or carries musical sound (Li Shangyin's “庄生晓梦”, 'Master Zhuang's dream at dawn' — the allusion used as a musical instrument); only what carries nothing but learning is called a book-bag**.
:::

::: history A history of the annotation industry {#sec-zhushi}
**Tang**: classified compendia (*Yiwen leiju* 艺文类聚, *Collection of Literature Arranged by Categories*; *Chuxue ji* 初学记, *Notes for Early Learning* — **the raw-materials supermarket of the writing supply chain, the age of “allusion = to cite is to be legitimate”**, [[tang-literature/early-tang]]) + the *Wenxuan* 文选 (*Selections of Refined Literature*), “熟精《文选》理” ('Master thoroughly the principles of the *Wenxuan*.' (Our translation.)) (Du Fu's words) — **volume one of the shared dictionary of the literati**; **Song**: the Xikun style read Li Shangyin as the model of the “otter's sacrifice” (**Yuan and Bai, plain, against Xikun, dense: two lines of distribution**); the false saying “杜诗无一字无来处” ('Not one character in Du Fu's poetry is without a source.' (Our translation.)) was in vogue — **the annotators of the myth were more industrious than the author of the myth: readers began to trace every character back to its household register**; **the Jiangxi school turned allusion into a method (touching iron, snatching the embryo — promoted from a practice to a pedagogy)**; **Ming and Qing**: **the annotated-commentary trade at its height (the system of Li Shan's commentary on the *Wenxuan* recut; Feng Hao's annotated commentary on the poems of Yuxisheng — the industrialisation of hermeneutics: the higher the compression ratio, the more developed the annotation industry — and this is an iron law of literary economics)**; Wang Yuyang's *shenyun*, going short, and the evidential scholarship (*kaoju*) of Zha Shenxing and his fellows, going long, are two sectors of the same market; **from the mid-Qing on**: the Song-poetry movement stages a restoration by “以学问为诗” ('taking scholarship as poetry') (the Tongguang style: a kind of courage that shifts the pressure of annotation onto the reader); **from the Republic to the present**: **the dispute over *ge* and *bu ge* (Wang Guowei judged the allusions of the Southern Song song lyric to be “如雾里看花”, 'like looking at flowers through mist'), the radical years of “allusion = feudal dross” — a revolution in the right to annotate; today, the sales of annotated editions are often higher than those of the collections of poems — the modern form in which classical poetry survives is its footnote** (this lesson's position: **a footnote is not an autopsy report; it is a consent form for organ donation — it lets an old text go on working in the body of a new reader**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of allusion
**Taking density as the mark of what is high** (**the definition of shaking the book-bag: the allusion runs empty — the source has been looked up, and yet one cannot look up why it is this one; the sentence for the acceptance test: “If this allusion were changed for another, what would the poem lose?”**); **letting the commentary stand in for the poem** (**the annotated commentary digs out ten matters, and the poem has still not been read — read it as a poem first, and as an archive afterwards; reverse the order, and the poem is read into a commentary**); **supposing that the ancients used allusion everywhere** (**the *yuefu*, the Music Bureau poems and ballads, Bai Juyi, and the “natural” line of the song lyric are all on the side against allusion; allusion is the dialect of the scholar-official, not the Putonghua, the common standard, of earlier Chinese**); **taking *bu ge* for the vernacular** (**what Wang Guowei measured is immediacy, not the degree of colloquial speech — “天若有情天亦老”, 'If heaven had feeling, heaven too would grow old.' (Our translation.): every character is recognised, and yet for everyone there is a barrier; no barrier = the picture comes first, and the vernacular (*baihua*) is only one of the shortcuts**).
:::

::: exercise An archaeology of the image {level=1}
Make an **account card** for “雁” ('wild goose') or “柳” ('willow'): ① list four lines of verse that contain it (lines already studied will do); ② for each, mark what it is doing there (carrying a letter / bearing witness / figuring oneself / setting the scene); ③ write a prediction of thirty characters: **in the AI poetry of twenty years from now, what meaning must this character still want to keep, if it is not to sink into decoration**.
::: solution
Marking: **the functions labelled do not repeat, and at least two lines can be looked up in a poem** (“鸿雁长飞光不度” — 'The wild geese fly far, and yet do not cross the light.' (Our translation.) — = the failed version of carrying a letter — **even the geese cannot fly across; the moonlight arrives before the news**: close reading of this kind is what scores the point); **figuring oneself must have a sense of position (the northern wild goose = a seasonal wage-worker)**; the prediction tests whether the mechanism is understood: **so long as someone still uses it to carry the account of “distance + news”, the image is alive; use as pure scenery is the early sign of a bad debt**.
:::

::: exercise A laboratory for contrary use {level=2}
Choose one “allusion with a standard answer” (愚公移山, 'the foolish old man moves the mountains'; 囊萤映雪, 'a bag of fireflies, and the light off the snow'; 刻舟求剑, 'notching the boat to seek the sword'; 守株待兔, 'waiting by a stump for a rabbit' — any one), and write four to eight lines: **first restate its “official moral” in two lines, then rewrite two lines by “contrary use or overturning the case”**, and finally give two sentences of “minutes of the hearing” (how a person of the Tang would judge, and how you would judge).
::: solution
Marking: **an overturning of the case must have a target** (if the official moral of “刻舟求剑” is merely restated as it stands, no offence has been committed); **the old version and the new must share the particulars** (“剑”, 'the sword', “舟”, 'the boat', and “标记”, 'the mark', stay at the scene — **the rule of evidence for contrary use: the same exhibit, a different statement**); if the minutes of the hearing show a conscious sense that “the verdict is revised as the age changes” (an analysis of what is gained and lost on the theory of victory and defeat / the theory of holding on), that is this chapter's point paid out at full marks.
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the life-and-death register of allusion {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, appraising the claim that “**the rise and fall of allusion is the rise and fall of the readers' market: when the shared dictionary is thick, an allusion is a lever; when the dictionary is thin, an allusion is a tax**”. You must draw on at least four of these: **the three steps of the image**, **one example of each of the four operations**, **the case of Xin Qiji's compression ratio**, **the history of the annotation industry (the Song's mythic commentary / Qing annotated commentary / the sales of annotated editions today)**. And answer: **in the present age of the “new rhymes” (based on Putonghua) and of new poetry (modern verse), do poets still “use allusion”? — if a 梗 ('meme') is today's allusion, are the tests by which the two are accepted the same?** (you are required to give the mechanism of the difference between the speed at which a meme fails and the thousand-year life of an allusion).
::: solution
Points for marking: the logic of the market (**the distinction between lever and tax = whether or not there is a shared stock: Li Bai uses Feng Tang and supplies no note, because a copy of the dictionary is in every hand; use the same allusion today, and half the readers pay a tax**); the four operations (**direct use, contrary use, overturning the case and unmarked adaptation, accepted by the test of “whether they are doing work”**); the case of the compression ratio (***ge* and *bu ge* = the difference between the work and the reader's stock**); the question of the meme (**the same structure: a meme = an allusion depreciating at high speed — the difference of lifespan is in the medium: an allusion has its contract renewed year after year by the institution of education, a meme is settled every day by platform traffic; so a meme that enters a poem is by nature quick to meet a barrier, unless the meme is written as a picture that can be understood without the meme (that is: the upgrade from meme to image is completed)**); if the conclusion can say “**all allusion is singing in chorus with people who are not there: while the dictionary is there, the voice is there**”, that is an A.

::: summary
Open the box and close the books: the ledger (**moon, willow, wild goose, railing, long pavilion — shared objects with their meaning pre-installed; read one line and decompress a cabinet**), the operations (**direct use claims title, contrary use annotates, overturning the case opens the court, unmarked adaptation goes on until nobody prosecutes**), the audit (**Xin Qiji bets on the public dictionary: he stakes what meets a barrier on the age, and collects first on what meets none**), the industry (**mythic commentary, Qing annotated commentary, the sales of annotated editions today — the higher the compression ratio, the more developed the annotation industry**), the intuition (**density is not the crime; running empty is**). The method, in one sentence: **on seeing an allusion, first ask which sentence it is speaking, and for whom; on seeing an image, first check the age of its account**. The last chapter strings twelve things on one rope: how to read a poem.
:::
