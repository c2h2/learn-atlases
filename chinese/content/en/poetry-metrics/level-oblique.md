---
title: "Level and oblique tones"
---

The whole edifice of metre is built on a binary code: **level and oblique** (平仄; also "level and deflected"). Level is "a sound that can be drawn out"; oblique is "a sound that cannot be drawn out" — **the rise and fall of sound in Tang poetry is, in essence, an engineering problem of whether the breath will last**. This chapter first makes that ruler, including a ruler that Putonghua (standard Mandarin) has already lost, and that has nevertheless left its marks everywhere: **the entering tone** (checked tone).

## Binary encoding {#def-erzhi}

::: definition Level and oblique: the bit compressed out of the four tones {#def-pingze}
The four tones of Middle Chinese (the system of the *Qieyun* 切韵, 'Cut Rhymes'): **level, rising, departing and entering** (平、上、去、入). Recent-style poetry (regulated verse) does not use the four tones directly. It first makes a **lossy compression**: rising, departing and entering are packed together as "oblique", and set against "level" — **four states compressed into one bit, so that the rules can be simple enough to set in an examination** (following on from [[han-to-nanbeichao/landscape-yongming]]: the Yongming poets worked with the four tones themselves; after Shen Quanqi 沈佺期 and Song Zhiwen 宋之问 the operating system changed over to level and oblique: **changing a four-colour brush for black-and-white printing was, on the contrary, a victory for the art of typesetting**). Why cut it here? **The property of level is "level and long" (the note can be drawn out); the property of oblique is "short and abrupt, or turning" (it cannot be drawn out) — the "rise and fall" of a regulated poem is the alternation of long sounds and short sounds: reading level and oblique as the "sustained note and the detached note" of music comes closer to the performance itself than reading them as a pitch contour**.
:::

::: definition Putonghua's converter for level and oblique {#def-zhuanhuan}
To judge level and oblique from Putonghua, a reader today must pass through three translations: **① the first tone and the second tone (阴平 and 阳平) = level** (both mā and má can be drawn out); **② the third tone and the fourth tone (上 and 去) = oblique**; **③ the entering tone = a missing person** — the `-p/-t/-k` codas dropped in the northern dialects and merged into the other three tones ("the distribution of the entering tone among the other tones", following [[phonology/to-mandarin]]), so that "**白, 石, 竹, 月, 雪, 力, 笔 and 国**" are today read as second tone, third tone and fourth tone alike — **any that are read as second tone are the most dangerous: every old entering-tone character distributed into the second tone passes itself off as a level tone quite good enough**. Three tools for the case: **dialect witnesses** (Yue (Cantonese, for the speech of Guangzhou and Hong Kong), Hakka, Min, Wu and Jianghuai Mandarin keep the entering tone: read "六, 七, 八 and 十" through once in the colloquial pronunciation, and the shortness is testimony given in court); **traces of the coda** (anything that ends in -b/-d/-g is one hundred per cent entering tone — a Cantonese speaker carries a detector); **phono-semantic clues** ("各" is entering tone, so "洛, 络 and 骆" are all entering tone: the phonetic component (phonophore) is a family register, and the family register records the offence).
:::

::: example Reading 'Quiet Night Thoughts' by level and oblique {#ex-jingye}
The level-and-oblique string (by the Pingshui rhymes): "床前明月光" ('Before my bed the moonlight is bright.' (Our translation.)) = **平平平仄平** (明 is level, 月 is entering); "疑是地上霜" ('I take it for frost on the ground.' (Our translation.)) = **平仄仄仄平** (地 and 上 are both departing); "举头望明月" ('I raise my head and gaze at the bright moon.' (Our translation.)) = **仄平仄平仄** (月 is entering again); "低头思故乡" ('I lower my head, thinking of home.' (Our translation.)) = **平平平仄平** (低 is level, 故 is departing). Please do three things: ① in the string, circle every entering-tone character; ② explain what rhythmic action each of the two appearances of "月" performs; ③ prove, from the accounts of opposition and adhesion (对 and 粘, the tonal links between lines), that **this is an ancient-style quatrain, not a regulated quatrain**.
::: solution
① One written form in the entering tone, and two appearances: **"月" ×2** ("地, 上, 故, 望 and 是" are all departing tone — **a departing tone draws out long; an entering tone can hold the door shut; you know which is which the moment you hear it**). ② In the first line, "明月光": **level + short-and-abrupt + level — the entering tone cuts a small step in the middle of the musical phrase**; at the close of the third line, "望明月": **the whole line stops short on a short sound, and the instant the head is raised is covered by the braking sound of "月"** — **the entering tone is the rhythmic punctuation of a regulated line**. ③ The accounts: **the second line, "平仄仄仄平", belongs to none of the four standard patterns of a regulated line** (four oblique and one level; the run of oblique tones on "地" and "上" stamps the rhythm dead), and **lines two and three fail in adhesion** — **a five-character ancient-style quatrain, with rhyme but without regulation**. A distinction thrown in: Li Bai 李白 could write regulated poems (he had brought 'Climbing Phoenix Terrace at Jinling' (登金陵凤凰台) to a high finish) — **to be able, and not to carry it through, is a capacity; to be unable, and yet to carry it through, is a defect; the "breach of the rules" that the ruler of the regulated poem measures in 'Quiet Night Thoughts' (静夜思) is precisely the identity of its genre** ([[tang-literature/li-bai]]).
:::

::: intuition The bodily test {#int-shenti}
The alternating skeleton of what is level and long has survived the change of pronunciation ([[phonology/to-mandarin]]) — **the building is still there; only the paint on the walls has been changed three times**.
:::

::: history A chronology of the tones {#sec-biannian}
**480s–510s**: Zhou Yong 周颙 distinguished the four tones, and Shen Yue 沈约, discussing literature, wrote "前句有浮声，则后句须切响" ('If the preceding line has a floating sound, the following line must have a cut sound.' (Our translation.)) (*Song shu* 宋书, 'Discussion of the Biography of Xie Lingyun' — **the order to start work on bringing the four tones into literature**); **501**: Liu Xie 刘勰, in *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (*Wenxin diaolong*), 'Sound and Metre', wrote "异音相从谓之和，同声相应谓之韵" ('Different sounds following one another is called harmony; the same sound answering is called rhyme.' (Our translation.)); **601**: Lu Fayan 陆法言's *Qieyun* — **the rhyme dictionary gave the four tones a dictionary**; **700s**: Shen and Song fixed recent-style poetry on the binary of level and oblique; **806**: Konghai 空海 (Kūkai) compiled, in *Wenjing mifu lun* 文镜秘府论 ('A Treatise from the Secret Repository of the Literary Mirror'), the Tang study of faults of sound; **1030s–1070s**: the examination rhyme dictionary *Libu yunlüe* 礼部韵略 ('Concise Rhymes of the Ministry of Rites') was promulgated; **1324**: the *Zhongyuan yinyun* 中原音韵 ('Rhymes of the Central Plain') distributed the entering tone among the other three tones, and **northern *qu* (song or aria) set up a separate tonal system (the level tone was split into yin and yang; the entering tone was distributed into the rising and the departing)**; **1919**: Zhao Yuanren 赵元任 (Yuen Ren Chao) composed 'A History of Mr Shi Eating Lions' (施氏食狮史) — **a homophone text was a stress test which the age of the vernacular (*baihua*) ran on Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*), and in passing it exposed the hidden cost of abolishing the entering tone**; **1958**: Wang Li 王力, *Hanyu shilüxue* 汉语诗律学 ('A Study of Chinese Poetic Metre'): a thousand years of mnemonic formulae became a discipline of tables.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in judging level and oblique
**Forcing a judgement of an old entering tone out of the modern pronunciation** (to read "白" in "白首", and "月" in "明月", as level, or as a long drawn tune, is already a breach of the rules — if you cannot judge, look it up in a table, and before you look it up find a dialect witness); **treating "一三五不论" ('the first, third and fifth need not be fixed') as a universal exemption from inspection** (**it holds only inside a small range, and it cannot rescue the boundary cases of the lone level tone and of three oblique tones at the end of a line — the graduations of this ruler are checked finely in the next lesson**); **taking level and oblique to be pitch** (**the "tone value" of Putonghua is a pitch contour; the first property of level and oblique is length, and whether the syllable is open or closed — taking them for a simplified version of the four tones comes closer to the truth than taking them for a five-line staff**); **forgetting that the forms divide the work** (**ancient-style verse, the song lyric (*ci*) and the *qu* each use their own tonal system: to measure 'The Road to Shu Is Hard' (蜀道难) with the ruler of the regulated poem is to mark Li Bai with an examination paper**).
:::

::: exercise Vocal-cord surgery on a piece of doggerel {level=2}
Perform vocal-cord surgery on the sentence "我是真好看" ('I really am good-looking.' (Our translation.)): first mark the level and oblique of the five characters ("看", in a regulated poem, has both a level reading and a departing reading; note which you choose), then revise it into an acceptable regulated line: **at least two different revisions, one by "substituting a character" and one by "reordering the words"**. In peer review: which change loses meaning, and which only changes the paint.
::: solution
Marking: **the level-and-oblique marking** (我 oblique, 是 oblique, 真 level, 好 oblique, 看 level/departing); **revision one (substitution)**: change the characters and keep the sense (something of the kind of "渠自是无双" ('He is indeed without a match.' (Our translation.)), compliant in level and oblique, and with the meaning undamaged); **revision two (reordering)**: reassemble by position ("真, 看, 好, 我 and 是" each look for a place in the two target formats "仄仄平平仄" and "平平仄仄平") — **the answer is open, but the target format of the regulated line must be declared before the characters are filled in: the drawing first and the building afterwards is the only hard discipline of this lesson**.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: are level and oblique grammar or rhetoric? {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing the claim "**level and oblique are the formal grammar of Chinese poetry: to violate them is not to be ugly; it is another kind of sentence**". You must draw on at least four items: **the compression from the four tones to level and oblique**, **the limits of "一三五不论" ('the first, third and fifth need not be fixed')**, **the division of labour between ancient-style and recent-style verse**, and **the separate tonal systems which the *ci* and the *qu* set up for themselves (a preview)**; and you must answer: **when the new-rhyme movement proposes "to legislate afresh, with Putonghua as the standard", what are its opponents defending?** (Hint: not merely nostalgia — the long-and-short property of the entering tone, the common market of the dialects, and the compatibility protocol with Tang poetry.)
::: solution
Points for marking: the distinction between grammar and rhetoric (**the rules of level and oblique are like grammar: what not keeping them produces is "another form of verse" (ancient-style verse, the *ci*, or free verse), not a "bad poem" — "ugliness" and "another kind of sentence" are the watershed of this lesson**); the logic of compression (**the cost of a rule: the easier it is to learn, the more widely it spreads, and the more widely it spreads, the more rigid it grows — every loosening, whether irregularity and compensation or a *ci* tune-pattern (*cipu* 词谱), is a response to a new situation**); "一三五不论" must be treated as **a distortion of a folk mnemonic**; the dispute over the new rhymes counts three ledgers at once (**the auditory ledger: a reader of Putonghua really cannot hear a breach of the rules; the compatibility ledger: the cost of translating back and forth with the whole of Tang poetry; the market ledger: the cost, for dialect areas, of withdrawing — a question of legislation has always been a distribution of interests**); if the conclusion can write "**a rhyme dictionary is a font file: you may change the font, but do not claim that the original edition was printed wrong**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The ruler is made: the four tones compressed into level and oblique (**the typesetting victory of black-and-white printing**), three tools for investigating the entering tone (**dialect witnesses / traces of the coda / the phono-semantic family register**), the bodily test (**sustained notes and detached notes taking their turn: the throat is the referee's seat**), the chronology (**from Shen Yue's order to start work to Zhao Yuanren's homophone stress test**). The method in one sentence: **measure length first, and only then talk of pitch; check the dialects first, and only then memorise the tables**. The next chapter steps onto the scales: rhyme and the rhyme books — the case of "falling outside the rhyme" (出韵) over which poets have been quarrelling for a thousand years.
:::
