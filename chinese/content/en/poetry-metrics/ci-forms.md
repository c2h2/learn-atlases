---
title: "The forms of the *ci*"
---

Poetry is a genre one 读 ('reads'); the song lyric (*ci*) began as a genre one 唱 ('sings') — **this difference of origin decides that the whole metrical study of the *ci* is a transcription of a musical score**. The previous chapter said, "词无定体之霸，只有按谱打工的填词人" ('The *ci* has no overlord of a fixed form, only writers who fill in words as hired work to a score.') (Our translation.) This chapter teaches you to read a *ci* score, and explains a reversal: **the rules of *ci* metre later grew stricter even than the rules of poetic metre — because what they had to serve was not the ear alone, but an instrument as well**.

## Reading the score {#def-dupu}

::: definition The tune title = the score plus a contract of format {#def-cipai}
The *ci* score prescribes four things, down to the character. **A fixed count of characters and lines** (how many characters to a line, how many lines in the whole piece; even 叠字 ('reduplicated characters') and 句读 (*judou*, the traditional pause-marks) are written in as fixed — 'Pusa man' 菩萨蛮 is 44 characters, 'Yingti xu' 莺啼序 is 240, **and the specifications differ by five and a half times: one tune title (*cipai*) is one custom-built machine**). **Level and oblique tones (also called level and deflected) fixed in position** (finer than in the regulated poem (*lüshi*): **a given character may be required to be departing tone, a given character required to be rising tone** — because the rise and fall of the melody has to bite onto the tone of the character). **Rhyme positions fixed in place** (where to rhyme, whether the rhyme is level or oblique, and at which line the rhyme changes — 'Pusa man' is a shuttle run of 'a change of rhyme every two lines, level-tone rhymes and oblique-tone rhymes by turns': count by the line "小山重叠金明灭" ('Little hills overlap; the gold flashes and goes out.') (Our translation.), and the rhyme words of each group of five lines are like the chorus marks of a change of song). **The structure of the *pian*** (a single tune is one section; a double tune has an upper and a lower *pian*; three repeats, four repeats — ***pian* 片 = the repeat sign in music: the lower *pian* changes the opening (*huantou*) but not the melody, and the *ci* is therefore the literature of the strophic song**). A demonstration of how to read the score: **look first at the rhyme positions (how many changes of rhyme = how many acts), then at the line-forms (long and short = breathing), then at the lead characters (*lingzi*; see the next section), and only at the end at level and oblique** — level and oblique are the fussiest of all, and yet they are often the battlefield of the *ci* writers' involution.
:::

::: definition Grades of capacity: *ling*, *yin*, *jin*, *man* {#def-rongliang}
The Qing division into the short lyric (*xiaoling*, within about 58 characters), the medium tune, and the long tune (the slow lyric, *manci*, from 91 characters) is **a filing by character-count after the event**; the real thread of the development is only one: **the music was growing longer**. The Tang and the Five Dynasties were single tunes and short songs ('Yi Jiangnan' 忆江南, one little piece of 27 characters). **In the reign of Song Renzong 宋仁宗 the 'new sounds of the marketplace' competed, and Liu Yong 柳永 expanded the short lyric into the slow lyric — 'Yu lin ling' 雨霖铃, at 140 characters, is the 12-inch extended version of a Tang popular song: a prelude of extended narration, a chorus that circles back, a drawn-out close at the end** (the place of "多情自古伤离别，更那堪、冷落清秋节" ('Since ancient times the heart of much feeling has grieved at parting; how much less can it bear the bleak node of clear autumn.') (Our translation.) falls exactly on the climax track of the lower *pian*). *Yin* and *jin* are transitional forms with added beats, lying between *ling* and *man* (**all you need remember is this: *ling*, *yin*, *jin* and *man* are four edited versions of one and the same tune along the axis of duration**). **Capacity is structure**: the short lyric is one exposure (the quatrain, as studied in the previous chapter); the slow lyric is one **film** — extended narration, the lift and the pause, and circling back are all techniques of the long take. **The first driving force in the history of the *ci* form was not the poet, but the length of nightlife**.
:::

## The independent constitution of the *ci* {#def-xianfa}

::: definition 'A house of its own': Li Qingzhao's criteria for acceptance {#def-bieyi}
Li Qingzhao's 李清照 'Discourse on the Song Lyric' (*Cilun* 词论) ran an acceptance inspection, in the flesh, of the famous writers of her own day. Of Liu Yong, "**词语是矣**" ('as for the words, they will do') (Our translation.); of Liu Yong in the musical rules, "**犹有未合**" ('there is still that which does not fit') (Our translation.) — extended narration, however gorgeous, does not pass review in any case. The likes of Zhang Xian 张先, Song Qi 宋祁 and Huang Tingjian 黄庭坚 are "**皆句读不葺之诗尔，往往不协音律**" ('they are all merely poems whose *judou* has not been put in order, and they often fail to accord with the music') (Our translation.) — the *ci* of famous writers were sentenced on the spot and sent back as 'poetry'. Almost the whole company of the *ci* world of the Eastern Capital (东都) was given marks, and not one asked for leave. She then established the constitution: "**词别是一家**" ('the *ci* is a house of its own') (Our translation.) — what is formidable about this standard is **the qualification of the person who judges**. Everyone named was a man at the very top, and the person who set the questions was one of the women who wrote *ci* best at the end of the Northern Song — **the first battle in the history of *ci* studies over the right of acceptance, and the way it was won was by producing a more professional ear** ('song words require "the five notes, the six pitch-pipes, the five tones and the eight faults" (五音六律五声八病); poetry and prose do not require them: the threshold of the *ci* is to take up the post with both certificates'). The reversal comes after: **the line of 'making elegant' (*yahua*) in the Southern Song pulled the *ci* towards poetry (Xin Qiji 辛弃疾 writing the *ci* in the manner of prose, 以文为词); the metrical school (Zhou Bangyan 周邦彦 — Wu Wenying 吴文英 — Jiang Kui 姜夔 — Zhang Yan 张炎) pushed the musical rules as far as the four tones of the single character — the ends of the two roads collided around the year 1200, and the rules of *ci* metre became the strictest metre in the whole of China**.
:::

::: definition The lead character and the entering tone: baton and brake pad {#def-lingzi}
**The one-character lead** ("**对**潇潇暮雨洒江天", '**Facing**, the driving evening rain sprinkles river and sky.' (Our translation.) "**渐**霜风凄紧", '**Gradually**, the frosty wind grows bleak and keen.' (Our translation.) "**望**长城内外", '**Gazing**, within the Great Wall and outside it.' (Our translation.) — **one character conducts all of the three or four four-character lines that follow: the lead character = the conductor's baton; the score marks 一字豆 ('a one-character pause'), and in the singing it raises a leading note**). **Two-character leads and three-character leads** work in the same way (of the order of "恰似一江" ('just like a whole river') and "最喜燕山" ('what I love best is Yanshan') — **the lead character lets the *ci* grow an aerial out of regular line-forms, to receive a tone of voice from outside the line**). **The stock turn of entering-tone rhyme** (the entering tone, also called the checked tone): 'Yi Qin e' 忆秦娥, "西风残照，汉家陵阙" ('West wind, the last of the light; the mounds and towers of the house of Han.') (Our translation.), and the temperature chart of the lesson on sound and metre — **tune titles have temperaments: 'Man jiang hong' 满江红 uses entering-tone rhymes throughout (歇, 烈, 月, 切 — the roar, of hair bristling so as to thrust the cap, dubbed with a 'dental shutting of the door'); the 'Willow' tune (柳) of 'Lanling wang' 兰陵王 is in three repeats, matched with the entering tone, and a farewell is written out to the length of an encore** — **choosing a tune title = choosing a prefabricated panel of emotion; the first multiple-choice question for the writer who fills in the *ci* is not what to write, but 'which song to use': the tune title's 'memory of subject matter' will speak for you ('Gui dian qiu' 桂殿秋 is short and brisk, fit for dreams; 'Yingti xu', at more than two hundred characters, is fit for setting out a whole life)**.
:::

::: example A complete reading of the score of one 'Pusa man' {#ex-pusaman}
Wen Tingyun 温庭筠, 'Pusa man': "**水精帘里颇黎枕，暖香惹梦鸳鸯锦。江上柳如烟，雁飞残月天。　藕丝秋色浅，人胜参差剪。双鬓隔香红，玉钗头上风。**"

Within crystal curtains, a pillow of glass;
Warm fragrance stirs a dream: mandarin-duck brocade.
On the river, willows like mist;
Geese fly in a sky of the waning moon.
Lotus-root silk, a pale autumn colour;
The *rensheng* cut to uneven lengths.
Fragrant red divides the two temples;
Wind at the tip of the jade hairpin.
(Our translation.)

Please read the score in the order of this lesson: ① mark the rhyme positions and the changes of rhyme (how many acts has it drawn?); ② count the characters in each line, and explain the boundary of the *pian*; ③ find any character that can be called a 'lead' (if there is none, explain why this tune does not need a lead character); ④ read the two lines of the upper *pian* at "江上" against the two lines of the lower *pian* at "双鬓", and **explain how the 'language of the camera' in the *ci* differs from the division of labour in the syntax of regulated verse** (recent-style poetry) (and how Wen Tingyun's 'no person' is maintained here by the rhyme positions).
::: solution
① Rhyme positions: **枕／锦 (rising tone) → 烟／天 (level tone, the *xian* rhyme 先) → 浅／剪 (rising tone) → 红／风 (level tone, the *dong* rhyme 东)** — **two journeys out and back between level and oblique; four groups of rhyme = four changes of light**: the upper *pian* cuts from indoors (pillow, brocade) to a great long shot out of doors (river, willows, geese, moon), and the lower *pian* cuts back to a close-up of adornment — **the 'shuttle-run rhyme positions' of 'Pusa man' force the scene to jump back and forth: this tune title's temperament is the camera's pushing in and pulling out, turn and turn about**. ② 44 characters, eight lines, two *pian* of four lines each: **the lower *pian* = a varied repeat of the melody of the upper *pian* (a changed opening)** — the structural unit of the *ci* is the *pian*, not the couplet. ③ **No lead character**: every line, of five characters or of seven, is a unit by itself, and this tune does not conduct long lines — **the lead character is equipment of the slow lyric (only the long tune needs a baton to direct the long takes; in the short lyric even the breathing is short, and a conductor is a superfluous courtesy)**. ④ The language of the camera: **the two lines "江上柳如烟，雁飞残月天" are pure scene, with no person, unlike the 'subject–verb–object line of expressed feeling' in regulated verse; "双鬓隔香红" lets the ornament serve as the subject — the syntax of the *ci* is cut to pieces by the singer's pauses, and what the juxtaposition of nouns joins is the beat of the music, not the sentence of grammar** (the previous chapter's 'Wen's camera' comes to land in the metre: **a change of light at the rhyme position + juxtaposition on the beat = the two-piece hardware set of *Huajian* ('Among the Flowers') aesthetics**).
:::
:::

::: intuition Metre is music delivered late {#int-yanchi}
After the tunes' scores were lost, what did the *ci* score become? **The commentary on a score that cannot be run: level and oblique are the fossils of melody, rhyme positions are the fossils of the beat, the entering tone is the fossil of some stretch of throat-resonance** — those who filled in *ci* after the Southern Song were in fact **copying music by tracing the fossils**; by the Ming and the Qing, even the copyists were no longer singing — **metre had become, outright, 'a backup in pure writing of auditory memory'** (this explains why the rules of the *ci* seem 'strict without a reason': where a departing-tone character stands, there was once a downward slide; the writer does not know it, and only copies — **form is the deposit slip of content; when the interest falls due one can still draw the money on the slip, only no one remembers what was deposited**). Set against poetry: the level and oblique of the regulated poem remain coherent through the change of pronunciation (the alternation of long and short is still at work); **the level and oblique of the *ci* contain a great deal of 'pure residue of the theory of music'**: and so the business of 'filling in words to the score' is, in its nature, **writing a grammar for a dead language** — and it turns out to work very well, because the backup is complete enough.
:::

::: history From the songbook to the book of characters {#sec-geben}
**Tang to Five Dynasties**: *quzi ci* (曲子词, 'words for tunes') = songbooks (the Dunhuang 敦煌 'quzi' have no titles; the *Huajian* calls them "**诗客曲子词**" ('*quzi ci* of the poets') — **the author is a supplier**, [[tang-literature/five-dynasties-ci]]); **Northern Song**: Liu Yong expands what is published (the long tune); **Su Shi 苏轼 'writes the *ci* in the manner of poetry' (以诗为词) — the first breach of contract: writing song-words as if they were poems** (disciples and former subordinates protest in a body: **the independent constitution of *ci* metre had only just been set up, and already someone was tearing the wall down**); **the 1100s**: **Zhou Bangyan was supervisor of the Dasheng Bureau (大晟府), collated the old tunes and created new melodies — the age when the *ci*'s royal academy of music fixed the scores (official musicians doubling as the national standards committee)**; **Southern Song**: Li Qingzhao establishes the constitution; Xin Qiji writes the *ci* in the manner of prose (the second breach of contract); **Jiang Kui composes tunes of his own and notes *gongche* notation (工尺谱) beside them — the last time a *ci* writer also served as composer (the notation-characters beside *Baishi daoren gequ* 白石道人歌曲, *Songs of the White Stone Daoist*, are the last certificate of betrothal between music and literature)**; **at the turn of Song and Yuan**: Zhang Yan's *Ci Yuan* 词源, and the matching of rhymes involuted as far as "**依四声**" ('following the four tones') (the final chapter of the arms race of exchanging poems: **reproducing the opponent's tones character by character**); **Yuan**: the *qu* flourished and the *ci* died as music — **the *ci* moved into the pure-text trade**; **Qing**: **the *Qinding Cipu* 钦定词谱, in more than eight hundred tunes, and the *Cilü* 词律, which collated the *judou* — filling in *ci* became 'the typography of a classical font': in the Qing revival of the *ci*, what the revival revived was the documents, not the music**; **today**: the industry of song lyrics takes the baton of filling in words (Fang Wenshan's 方文山 'plain rhyme' and Lin Xi's 林夕 'according with the notes' — **Cantonese song keeps the living procedure of 'filling in words to the melody' (依声填词); lyrics in Putonghua (standard Mandarin), for the most part, only rhyme on a roughly similar rhyme — the minimum living allowance of *ci* metre is issued, on its behalf, by popular song**).
:::

::: warning Four traps in reading the metre of the *ci*
**Judging a *ci* to be out of metre by the metre of poetry** (**each *ci* has its own score: 'Xi jiang yue' 西江月 has level-tone and oblique-tone rhymes answering one another; 'Qingping yue' 清平乐 changes rhyme halfway through the piece — to be 'out of metre' is mostly to be out of the metre of *shi* poetry: first find its song**); **taking the tune title for the subject** (**'Yong yu le' 永遇乐 is not 'forever meeting with happiness': a tune title is a tune-name plus a format, and the sense of the theme is given in a note of one's own, or marked apart — to misread the tune title = to take an MP3 file name for the content of the lyrics**); **ignoring entering-tone characters** (**where the *ci* score specifies an entering tone, picking the character by Putonghua is sure to be wrong: in 'Yi Qin e', "阙、月、绝" are hard requirements — the entering-tone investigation of the first chapter is, inside the *ci*, the proof one gathers in order to take up the post**); **supposing that 'filling in words to the score = safety'** (**the score governs the written surface and does not govern the feeling of the sound (*shengqing*): fill a tune title of the heroic manner (*haofang* 豪放) with matter of the delicate and restrained manner (*wanyue* 婉约), and the instrument will return the audience's tickets for them — the verdict of 'a poem whose *judou* has not been put in order' still applies, nine hundred years later, to the KTV**).
:::

::: exercise The score-reader {level=1}
Take one *ci* score ('Ru meng ling' 如梦令 or 'Bu suan zi' 卜算子, either), and **make the score in reverse**: take a *ci* written to this tune (of your own choosing), mark level and oblique and the rhyme positions character by character, and summarise that score's 'act table' (how many acts, the rhyme-type of each act, where it pauses). Check one another's work: whose act table can predict, from the symbols alone, how many characters the next line has?
::: solution
Marking: **how far the act table can predict** (being able to predict = having grasped the period of the musical phrase — **the rhyme position is the full stop of the musical phrase**); **the rhyme-type labels include '平—仄—平' ('level—oblique—level') or 'how many changes of light'** ('Bu suan zi' rhymes on the even lines, in an oblique-tone rhyme — **two changes of act**); mark the entering-tone characters in the *ci* and give a judgement, by ear, of 'what would happen if they were changed for level-tone characters' — **the score-reader's ear = a mouth that can read a score**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Choosing the tune {level=2}
Take a stretch of real feeling of your own (within 150 characters; do not state 悲伤 ('sorrow') or 快乐 ('joy') outright), **match it with a tune title**, and give your reasons: argue from the three items 'capacity (*ling* / the slow lyric), rhyme positions (how many changes of light), and the feeling of the sound (whether or not there is an entering tone; the temperature of the rhyme group)'; do not fill in a *ci*; hand in only a 'sheet of the tune chosen'.
::: solution
Marking: **one technical reason for each of the three items** (of the order of 'I choose "Ru meng ling" because it is so short that one can only hold the feeling in; I would choose "Man jiang hong", but I have not breath enough, so I change to "Que qiao xian" 鹊桥仙 — the rhythm of "**金风玉露一相逢**" ("The metal wind and the jade dew meet this once.") (Our translation.) suits the slow saying of heavy words'); **anyone in whom there appears a sense of 'using the memory of subject matter that the tune title brings with it'** (to write homesickness to 'Zhegu tian' 鹧鸪天 is to inherit 'the effect of an old song already on the playlist' — **a tune title is a prefabricated panel, and also the echo of earlier pieces**) receives full marks.
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the music first, or the metre {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing '**the rules of *ci* metre are the legal heritage of a lost music: they prove that form can go on legislating after the content has disappeared**'. You must call on at least four of: **the four-piece set of the *ci* score**, **the development of capacity in the slow lyric**, **'a house of its own' in the 'Discourse on the Song Lyric'**, **the residue, in the instrument, of the lead character and the entering tone**, **the involution of exchanging poems by following the four tones**; and you must deal with one opposing side: **since the music is gone, every 'according with the metre' in the filling-in of *ci* today is a pretence — better to go over entirely to new rhymes and new forms (writing for the music, for example song lyrics)**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the four mechanisms (**the transcription from music to metre (the fossil backup); duration driving genre; the right of acceptance, and the ear; following the four tones = involuting, into social currency, sound-symbols that cannot be verified**); the handling of the opposing side (**it may be half accepted: song lyrics are indeed the orthodox heir of the '*ci*' — 'writing for the music' has never stopped the line, and has only changed its carrier; but to keep one school of 'reading the *ci*' is also legitimate:** when metre becomes a voiceprint constraint of pure literature, what it provides is no longer music but the friction of 'resisting what runs smoothly off the tongue' — the value of the *ci* lies exactly at the stumble (the place where the prose sentences of the Xin school fight the musical phrase)**); if the conclusion is written as '**we are not replicating a sound; we are using writing to keep a seat for the sound — so long as human beings still need the feeling of "just short of singing it out", the *ci* does not count as dead', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Four files, archived. The contract (**characters and lines, level and oblique, rhyme positions, the division into *pian*: one tune title, one machine**); the duration (**Liu Yong's 12-inch extended version; the first driving force in the history of the *ci* form is the length of nightlife**); the constitution (**'a house of its own': if the instrument has not signed, it does not pass review — a battle over the qualification to judge**); the fossil (**the lead character is the baton, the entering tone is the brake pad; metre is music delivered late**). The method, in one sentence: **first find the song, then read the metre; first the act table, then level and oblique**. The next chapter goes on to the next-generation product of the *ci*: the *qu* — the revised system that stuffs padding words, colloquial phrases and the whole marketplace into the metre.
:::
