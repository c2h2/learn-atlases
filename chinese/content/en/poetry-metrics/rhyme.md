---
title: "Rhyme and the rhyme books"
---

The previous chapter made a straight-edge of level and oblique tones (also called level and deflected); this chapter steps onto the scales: **rhyme**. Rhyming looks perfectly natural and right, but in fact there are three cases outstanding: **By what is a rhyme defined? Why are there 106 rhyme groups and not 30? Why can an off-rhyme (*chūyùn* 出韵, 'using a character outside the permitted rhyme') ruin a career?** — the answers point, respectively, to **the rhyme dictionary, administration, and the examinations**.

## The anatomy of rhyme {#def-jiepou}

::: definition Rhyme groups: filed by the rhyme dictionary, not by the ear {#def-yunbu}
The "rhyme" of verse rhyme = **the same nucleus and the same coda (the medial does not count, the initial is irrelevant, and the tone must be the same)** — 关 and 还 are the same rhyme and may be rhymed together; 间 and 简 differ in tone and may not (**level, rising, departing and entering — the entering tone is also called the checked tone — each rhyme only with their own: a regulated poem (*lüshi*) keeps one rhyme to the end, and uses only the level tone**). The crux is "**filing according to the rhyme dictionary**". Rhyme dictionaries in the *Qieyun* 《切韵》 ('Cut Rhymes') line were in origin **a compilation of Sui pronunciation** (Lu Fayan 陆法言, “欲更开塞音韵” — "I wish further to distinguish and to merge the sounds and rhymes" (Our translation.) — **what it records is not the living colloquial of Chang'an, but the minutes of a joint meeting of the reading pronunciations of the gentry of north and south**; see [[phonology/rhyme-books]]). The officially fixed Tang *Tangyun* 《唐韵》 ('Rhymes of the Tang') raised the number to 206 rhymes, and **used-together and used-alone** (*tóngyòng* 同用, 'licensed to be used together'; *dúyòng* 独用, 'to be used alone') were a notice pasted up by the authorities (a given rhyme "used alone" = to rhyme across it is a foul; certain rhymes "used together" = a specially permitted merger). At the turn of the Song and the Jin the Pingshui system formally merged the cases of those marked for use together, and **the *Pingshui rhymes* 《平水韵》, in 106 rhymes, became the de facto standard for the rhyming of regulated verse (recent-style poetry) in the Yuan, Ming and Qing** — **the history of the rhyme groups is half a history of speech sounds and half a history of official paper: that 东 and 冬 are not used together is not because the ear cannot tell them apart; it is because the notice says they must not be mixed**.
:::

::: definition Three instruments of punishment for rhyme {#def-xingju}
**① Off-rhyme**: using a character from a neighbouring rhyme = being struck off the provincial examination. "**东 and 冬 do not count as kin, do they, merely by being called 'the first, 东' and 'the second, 冬'?**" — Right, they do not: **the examiner brings only the rhyme dictionary into the hall; the ear is not on the establishment**. **② A special pardon for borrowed rhyme** (*jièyùn* 借韵, 'a neighbouring rhyme licensed in a set place'): the first line may use a neighbouring rhyme (the pattern "**孤雁出群格**", 'the lone wild goose leaving the flock') — **a side door which the metrical system leaves where one comes in: the first line receives the guests, and does not count as the main hall**. **③ The ban on a repeated rhyme word** (*chóngyùn* 重韵, 'using the same rhyme word twice'): a regulated poem may not use the same rhyme word again — **in a little room of twenty characters one may not hang two copies of the same picture**; ancient-style verse, the song lyric (*ci*) and the *qu* (song or aria) are a separate discussion (the rules of the *qu* in the *Zhongyuan yinyun* 《中原音韵》 ('Rhymes of the Central Plain') line even let level, rising and departing rhyme across one another, and a rhyme word may be repeated — **every rhyme dictionary comes with a code of law of its own**). Annexed to these, one famous case still open: **matching the rhyme words in order** (*cìyùn* 次韵, 'a response that copies the original's rhyme words, and in the same order') — Yuan Zhen 元稹 and Bai Juyi 白居易 turned it into an arms race; Su Shi's 苏轼 'Snow at the Hall of Gathered Stars' (《聚星堂雪》) goes further and stacks on a ban on the stock words for the thing described (*jìntǐ wùyǔ* 禁体物语: to write of snow and yet not be allowed the characters “玉、月、梨、梅” and the like) — **a difficult rhyme (*xiǎnyùn* 险韵, 'a rhyme with few characters to choose from') plus banned characters, and those who answer are swept aside: "rhyme" is promoted from a rule to a difficulty mode, and the writers of the Song fitted extra weights onto the shackles**.
:::

::: example Post-mortem on the rhyme group of one poem {#ex-yanshi}
Liu Zongyuan 柳宗元, 'Climbing the City-Wall Tower at Liuzhou and Sending This to the Four Prefectures of Zhang, Ting, Feng and Lian' (《登柳州城楼寄漳汀封连四州》): “城上高楼接大荒，海天愁思正茫茫。惊风乱飐芙蓉水，密雨斜侵薜荔墙。岭树重遮千里目，江流曲似九回肠。共来百越文身地，犹自音书滞一乡。”

On the city wall a high tower meets the great wilderness;
grief toward sea and sky stretches vast.
A fierce wind wildly shakes the lotus water;
dense rain slants in upon the wall of climbing fig.
Trees on the ranges, layer on layer, block a view of a thousand li;
the river's course bends like an intestine coiled nine times.
Together we have come to the tattooed lands of the Hundred Yue,
and still the letters stall, each held in its own place. (Our translation.)

① Mark the rhyme words and look up their Pingshui groups; ② if this poem had 荒, 茫, 墙, 肠 and 乡 all replaced with characters from the 东 and 冬 rhymes, how much of the feeling would drop away; ③ the three rules of regulated verse, "one rhyme to the end, no repeated rhyme word, and rhyme only in the level tone" — how is each made good in this poem?
::: solution
① The rhyme words: **荒, 茫, 墙, 肠, 乡** — all belong to the lower level-tone section, the seventh, 阳 (下平七阳) (**墙 is in the 阳 rhyme too: ang runs straight through**): **zero off-rhymes, zero repeated rhyme words, the level tone to the end**, all three rules made good. ② How much the feeling drops — set up one minimal contrast. A model rewriting: “接大**东**／正朦**胧**／密雨斜侵薜荔**栊**／江流曲似九弯**中**” ("meets the great **east** / is just now a **haze** / dense rain slants in upon the climbing-fig **lattice** / the river bends like the **midst** of nine turns") (Our translation.). **The 阳 rhyme (-ang) has the mouth at its most open and the nasal resonance at its longest = an amplifier for sorrow; Dong–Zhong (东钟, -ung) draws the mouth in, and what one hears is dim = the imagery of one and the same poem shrinks at once from “海天茫茫” ("vast sea and sky") to “烟雨朦胧” ("mist and rain in a blur")** — **rhyme is not wrapping paper; it is orchestration** (this lesson's "study of the timbre of rhyme": -ang is open and broad, -i is thin and narrow, the closed-mouth rhyme in -m (闭口韵) is a sob: see the warning). ③ The 一 of “滞一乡” is an entering tone (**inside the line it serves as a mark of rhythmic punctuation; see the previous chapter**) — **the entering tone does not go into the rhyme word (a regulated poem rhymes in the level tone), but at any moment it brakes inside the line: level and oblique govern the beat, the rhyme word governs the answering sound, the entering tone governs the pause — three offices of sound, and none of them holds another's post**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The corpse of the closed-mouth rhyme {#int-bikou}
The groups 侵, 覃 and 盐 of the Pingshui rhymes (**the lower level-tone section, the twelfth, 侵: “吟、深、寻、心”; the fourteenth, 盐: “帘、尖、淹”**) had, from Old Chinese through Middle Chinese, an **-m coda** (“三、南、心、金” were read like sam, nam, sam, kam — Yue is still like this; see [[phonology/to-mandarin]]); **the -m coda merged into -n in the northern dialects around the turn of the Yuan and the Ming**, and yet the *Pingshui rhymes* froze the two specimens “侵寻” and “廉纤” in the dossier — **from then on the poet rhymed a sound he could not himself produce: the two hundred years by which the rhyme dictionary outlived the spoken language are the specimen of "metre lagging behind pronunciation"**. Hence the rhyme words of “帘卷西风，人比黄花瘦” ("The west wind rolls the curtain; she is thinner than the yellow flower.") (Our translation.) and “深巷明朝卖杏花” ("In the deep lane, tomorrow morning, they sell apricot blossom.") (Our translation.) were, in the original speaker's mouth, a lock falling shut with the lips closed: **the auditory archaeology of reading a poem often digs up a dead sound**.
:::

::: history The changes of regime of one rhyme dictionary {#sec-zhengquan}
**601**, Lu Fayan's *Qieyun* — **“论韵尊陆法言”** ("When rhyme is discussed, Lu Fayan is the authority.") (Our translation.): **the minutes of a household salon on phonology (Yan Zhitui 颜之推 was present) became the official rhymes for three hundred years**; **from 658**, the annotations of Zhangsun Neyan 长孙讷言, and Sun Mian's 孙愐 *Tangyun*, officially compiled and expanded — **the rhyme dictionary nationalised**; **from 1008**, the *Guangyun* 《广韵》 ('Expanded Rhymes') and the *Jiyun* 《集韵》 ('Collected Rhymes') — **"a character store + an index + a digest of decided cases" in 206 rhymes** (a rhyme dictionary that is at the same time a dictionary: **looking a character up by its rhyme = querying on a primary key: China's first successful relational database**; see [[phonology/rhyme-books]] and [[chinese-characters/origins]]); **the 1100s–1200s**, the practical edition for the examination hall, the *Libu yunlüe* 《礼部韵略》 ('Concise Rhymes of the Ministry of Rites'), and the Pingshui merger into 106 — **the market cut the official rhymes down to an examination-only model**; **the Yuan**, the *Menggu ziyun* 《蒙古字韵》 ('Rhymes in the Mongol Script') in eighty-one rhymes, and Zhou Deqing's 周德清 *Zhongyuan yinyun* in nineteen rhymes — **northern *qu* enacted a constitution of their own: the level tone was split into yin and yang, and the entering tone was distributed among the three tones, so that the rhymes of the song lyric and the rhymes of poetry thenceforth stood side by side in three sets**; **Ming and Qing**: the imperially commissioned *Peiwen shiyun* 《佩文诗韵》 ('The Peiwen Poetic Rhymes') — **the old code brought back by the civil-service examinations: cases of off-rhyme were still being heard on the palace-examination ground down to 1904**; **1941**, the *Zhonghua xinyun* 《中华新韵》 ('New Chinese Rhymes') in seventeen rhymes (the Gwoyeu Romatzyh camp, 国语罗马字), the revision of 1965, and **today's "Zhonghua xinyun" (fourteen rhymes)** — **the new rhymes are not meant to abolish the old; they issue a driving licence to the Putonghua (standard Mandarin) ear** (the Ministry of Education's 2012 industry standard, the *Zhonghua xinyun*: **the two systems are registered in parallel: negotiation of a compatibility protocol is still under way**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of rhyming
**Using today's pronunciation to convict the ancients of an off-rhyme** (“远上寒山石径斜（xiá），白云生处有人家” — "A stone path slants far up the cold mountain; where the white clouds are born, there are people's homes." (Our translation.) — **in the old pronunciation 斜 belonged to the 麻 rhyme: it is not the old poem that has gone out of tune; it is you who moved house and changed accent**; if you are not sure, look up the Pingshui rhymes before you conclude); **treating the new rhymes and the old rhymes as a war of good against evil** (**both sets are conventions: the old rhymes are compatible with the whole corpus of Tang poetry, the new rhymes are compatible with Putonghua hearing — a question of which to choose; do not open a struggle session**); **ignoring that the song lyric and the *qu* have codes of their own** (**the rhymes of the song lyric are wider than the rhymes of poetry (rising and departing may cross, and the rhyme may be changed); the rhymes of the *qu* follow the *Zhongyuan yinyun*: measure Li Qingzhao 李清照 with the Pingshui ruler, and everything that comes out is "off-rhyme"**); **a blind faith in the beauty of "rhyme positions fixed once and for all"** (**"in a seven-character line, the second, fourth and sixth are distinct" is a mnemonic of later people: the real units of the beat are the foot and the pause — read it aloud first, then check it against the table**).
:::

::: exercise Orchestrating with rhyme {level=1}
Take one and the same meaning (for example “我想你”, "I miss you"), and for each of the three sets of rhyme words **-ang, -i, -u** write two lines (colloquial or Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wenyan*), with no restriction), and compare them: **which set is the loudest, which the most private, which the most muffled**, and explain how the degree to which the mouth opens "cheats" its way into a feeling.
::: solution
Marking: **each of the three sets of rhyme words uniform within itself** (-ang: 窗, 灯, 长; -i: 你, 雨, 期; -u: 路, 雾, 暮级); **a report on how far the mouth opens** (-ang, open = a broadcast; -i, flat and thin = a whisper; -u, rounded and drawn in = under the quilt — **half of what makes a rhyme "pleasant to hear" is the acoustics of the vowel**); full marks to anyone who can once own up to "changing the meaning for the sake of the rhyme word" (a live experience of rhyme-word determinism).
:::
:::

::: exercise Retrial of the off-rhyme case {level=2}
For the verdict "‘远上寒山石径斜’ does not rhyme in Putonghua; the poet made a mistake" (‘远上寒山石径斜’按普通话不押韵，诗人犯了错误), write a **judgment on retrial** (150 characters): **you must cite the clauses on validity in time of two "codes"** (the Pingshui rhymes / the *Zhonghua xinyun*).
::: solution
Marking: **an analysis of jurisdiction** (a work of the Tang is governed by the rhyme dictionary in force in the Tang: **Pingshui (of the *Tangyun* line) is the law at the time of the act, and the new rhymes are the law at the time of judgment — any discussion of following the old and also following the new must first declare which system applies**); **reject the claim of "a mistake"** (the old sound of 斜 is the 麻 rhyme, the same group as 家 and 花: **it does rhyme**); bonus marks for a practical tail of the sort "in a teaching setting one may note 'read xiá, or, by the modern sound, explain how ancient and modern differ'" (教学场合可注‘读 xiá 或依今音说明古今异’) — **a judgment has to be one that can be carried out**.
:::

::: exercise Short essay: is a rhyme dictionary a database or a cage {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**the rhyme dictionary freezes language into an archive, and the poet gets by on thawing and on smuggling**". You must call on at least five things: **the nature of the *Qieyun* as "minutes of a joint meeting"**, **used-together and used-alone, and the Pingshui merger of cases**, **the special pardon for borrowed rhyme**, **the -m rhyme groups dying out while the rhyme dictionary keeps them**, **the dispute over the new rhymes**; and answer: **if it were done over again, how many rhymes would you design for regulated verse** (give your scheme, and say who is sacrificed).
::: solution
Points for marking: the five mechanisms (**the rhyme dictionary = a database filed by rhyme (to look a character up is to run a query); used-together and used-alone = an administrative hot-fix; the Pingshui merger of cases = the market cutting the specification; keeping dead sounds = a debt of compatibility; the new rhymes = changing the runtime**); the main line of "freezing and thawing" (**the poet's ordinary day: choosing words inside the statutory rhyme groups = writing while wearing a catalogue; borrowed rhyme, cross-rhyme (*tōngyùn* 通韵, 'rhyming across groups the rules allow') and matching rhyme words in order = licit smuggling, and a display of skill**); the design (**an open question; the mark turns on the honesty of "who is sacrificed": fewer rhyme groups = sacrificing mutual translation with Tang poetry; keeping the old rhymes = sacrificing learners of Putonghua — there is no lossless legislation**); if the conclusion can say "**every system of metre is language policy: the rhyme dictionary was not written by the poem; it was written by the planning bureau of the city which the poem has to obey**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The three cases closed: definition (**filed by the rhyme dictionary: the ear is not on the establishment**), the instruments of punishment (**off-rhyme means being struck off, borrowed rhyme is a special pardon, a repeated rhyme word is banned, matching rhyme words in order is extra weight**), timbre (**-ang is an amplifier, -m is a frozen corpse: half of rhyming is the acoustics of the vowel**), regime (**salon minutes → official rhymes → a cut-down examination edition → three constitutions side by side → the new rhymes issue the licence**). One sentence of method: **on meeting a rhyme, ask first: under which code? In force from which year?** The next chapter assembles the main machine: the four level-and-oblique patterns of the regulated poem, and the engineering drawing of adhesion and opposition (the tonal links between lines).
:::
