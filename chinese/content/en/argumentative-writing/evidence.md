title: "Evidence"
---

A claim has to be paid for; evidence is the method of payment — **the previous chapter set up the client; this chapter begins the audit: the credit of an argumentative essay is written half in the claim, and half in 'the way the author cites and retells facts'**.

## Definition: five material silos {#def-liaocang}

::: definition The nature of the material, and its use {#def-wulei}
**① Facts and data** (**the hardest, and also the easiest to misuse: the four checks on data were already taught in Chapter 8 (denominator, base, scope, legend) — the writing course adds a fifth check: a use put in the wrong place ('taking the percentage from survey A across to prop up conclusion B: the data did not lie; the porter swapped the delivery note')**); **② Authoritative statements** (**two switches, the legitimate domain and the margin of disagreement (a return visit to Chapter 1 of the logic course): what writing requires is 'cite the consensus, and mark the disagreement' ('"experts hold" has to be written "the view of most cardiovascular societies is …, and a minority of researchers reserve their opinion"': the correct use of authority is not the final whistle; it is a way-station that can be checked)**); **③ Statutes and texts** (**in an argumentative essay the 'original wording of a document' is rare hard currency: a citation must carry the article number and the period in which it is in force (statutes are repealed, guidelines are updated: to cite a document of 1998 in dealing with a dispute of 2024 is to fight a new battle with an old map)**); **④ The case and the anecdote** (**for the question of posts, see the next section: a case is a good witness, and a bad chair of the witnesses)**); **⑤ Experience and intuition** (**'I have been a form teacher for ten years; one look and I know' — it is entitled to enter as a 'starting hypothesis', and not entitled to serve as 'the end-point of the conclusion': a long-serving form teacher's intuition is a compressed database; once it is decompressed, a source can be found for most of it, and the part for which no source can be found is called prejudice (the trade version of Lesson 1's 'language sense is a product of statistics')**). **The common discipline** (**the value of evidence = source × relevance × timeliness — if any one of the three is zero, the product is zero ('"true, important, but out of date" and "vivid, relevant, but of unknown source" are one and the same invalid ticket')**).
:::

::: definition Three posts for the case {#def-gehgang}
**Post one: falsifying a universal** (**one counter-example kills one law (the full-cell force of Chapter 1 of the logic course) — '"so long as you work hard, you can get into a key school" is shot through by one particular person: here the case is a sniper rifle'**); **Post two: exemplification / making concrete** (**the regularity has already been established elsewhere; the case is there to let the reader see what it looks like — 'the statistics say that 60% of those who drop out are girls; little D's story gives this figure a face: legitimate, and necessary: an abstract sense of justice does not move people; only a sense of justice that moves people can raise action (a paper should not ask to move people, but the reader of a policy argument does need a face)'**); **Post three: the opening bait** (**use a story to bring the reader into the dispute — but what matters is that it only takes the ticket and not the wage: the opening anecdote must 'retire' in the second paragraph, and the load-bearing of the whole piece is handed to structure and data (the standard way a *shuangwen* ('gratification writing') dies: opening story + data in make-up + an ending that returns to the same story — inside the rhetorical closed loop of an echo between opening and close, one isolated case is enshrined as a god)**). **Employment beyond its authority** (**'my neighbour / my classmate / my grandfather' appointed as the principal evidence = running a pay-in-full business on an anecdote's savings: the test — delete the case; does the argument still hold? If it still holds, the case is ornament; if it collapses, the whole piece was one person holding the building up (for an argument in which one person holds the building up, there is only one standard of acceptance: that person must remain standing through to the final hearing)**).
:::

## Three virtues of quotation {#def-yinwen}

::: definition Do not break the text, do not shift the sense, do not mount it {#def-sande}
**① Do not break the text** (**put the stretches before and after '游戏是……的麻醉' ('games are an anaesthetic for …') together, and it is often a different sentence: a quotation's neighbours are not idle talk; they are its court (in practice: a quotation of more than 15 characters must be checked against one screen of context on each side; a quotation from a translation must be checked against the original and the translator — a quotation that has passed through translation is second-hand smoke)**); **② Do not shift the sense** (**a famous saying changes its sense in travel: the *judou* case of '民可使由之不可使睹' ('The people may be made to follow it; they may not be made to see it.') (Our translation.) (the traditional editions that mark the pauses disagree; move the punctuation once and the stance flips) — a quotation from Classical Chinese carries its own 'risk in sentence division', and this is a minefield particular to the argumentative essay in Chinese (the countermeasure: a disputed quotation is downgraded to 'the gist', and is not enshrined inside quotation marks as evidence)**); **③ Do not mount it** (**using a famous saying as a picture frame in place of reasoning: the three characters '鲁迅说' ('Lu Xun said') are not the evidence; they are the box the evidence is packed in (check the goods inside the packing: in which essay of Lu Xun 鲁迅, said to whom, and what quarrel he was in that year — 'every sentence of a famous person belongs to the battlefield he was on at the time: before you set the ammunition of 1925 into the wall of 2025, first check the rifling')**). **The procedure for checking a source** (**for a famous saying, first check the earliest source, then check whether it is the original work or a quotation at second hand, and last check the context — the model case, of textbook grade, is precisely the popular sentence '事实自己会说话' ('Facts will speak for themselves.') (Our translation.): the evidential enquiry into it often stops at the layer of retelling by newspapermen of the nineteenth century, and the original text is hard to find (this lesson only sets up the procedure, and does not set quotations to be memorised: the procedure for checking a source is worth more than any one quotation — it is the only dowry this course lets you take away)**).
:::

::: example A full audit of a *shuangwen* {#ex-chazhag}
Read this extract from a hit piece, billed as 'solid in its data', and open an **audit report** (at least five lesions, and for each lesion open one rectification slip):
“**权威报告显示，90% 的家长支持推迟上学；某重点中学试行后，一本率上升 30%。校长说：‘我们只是把孩子的睡眠还给了孩子。’数据显示，睡眠不足青少年的比例高达 78%，‘这不是个例，这是时代病！’专家称，推迟上学一小时可提升 GDP。**”
'**An authoritative report shows that 90% of parents support delaying the start of school; after a trial at a certain key secondary school, the first-tier admission rate rose by 30%. The principal said: "We have only given the children's sleep back to the children." The data show that the proportion of adolescents short of sleep is as high as 78%. "This is not an isolated case; this is a disease of the age!" Experts say that delaying the start of school by one hour can raise GDP.**' (Our translation.)
::: solution
An example of the lesions (five, to start with): ① 'Authoritative report' has no issuing body, no year and no sampling frame (rectification: supply 'who, when, which institution, how large N is, and in which square' (the body of parents is itself stratified — the 'support' of parents at key schools and of parents at ordinary schools are two species)); ② 'The first-tier admission rate rose by 30%' lacks three things (the base (from 1% to 1.3% is also 30%), selection bias ('a certain key secondary school' = the effect of a famous school, and self-selection of the intake), and the time window (rectification: supply a comparison school and the scope of the difference (the 'borrowing a field' technique of Chapter 7 comes on duty here))); **③ The quotation exceeds its authority** (**the principal's words are an expression of feeling, not data; legitimate as 'testimony from the person who carried the measure out', illegitimate as evidence that 'the measure is effective': whoever benefits is the one who speaks, and the speech counts as a stance, not as proof**); **④ The logical springboard between '78%' and 'a disease of the age'** (**a prevalence figure + a rhetorical ascent = every intermediate link by which a factual claim is promoted straight into a policy claim has been left out (repair the chain: insufficient sleep → loss in schoolwork → delaying the start of school as the optimal solution — each link must be delivered on its own)**); **⑤ 'Experts say … raises GDP'** (**an uncovered short across scales (sleep — schoolwork — the labour force — GDP: the causal chain is too long to be verified, the optimistic mirror-image of a slippery slope; rectification: either give the model and the assumptions, or delete it — 'the moment a quotation of the "experts say GDP" kind appears in this piece, it may be taken as the circuit-breaker on the author's credit')**); **A bonus item** (**not one figure from the other side, and not one opposing view, appears in the whole piece: the general outbreak of Lesson 1's 'pretending there is no opponent' — the last line of the audit report: recommend that the whole piece be downgraded to a 'survey of wishes'**).
:::
:::

::: intuition Facts will not speak for themselves {#int-shishi}
**'Setting out the facts' is an honest act, so long as you do not pretend that the facts will fall in and answer the roll** (**every fact you choose has passed four gates: it can be found, you rate it highly enough, its source can be checked, and you dare to sign your name — all four gates are your fingerprints (so honesty, in the writing course, is not the low standard of 'not lying'; it is the high standard of 'declaring the selection prejudice': of your own accord to write the one line 'the data in this essay come mostly from studies of urban schools; conclusions about the countryside need caution' is worth more than a whole piece of 'objectively speaking')**). **The mix of hard material and soft** (**an argumentative essay is not a data report: all hard, and it reads like an autopsy; all soft, and it reads like fortune-telling (a reference for the mix: the layer of fact hard, the layer of mechanism clear, the layer of value true — hard material is responsible for truth, soft material is responsible for getting through: the right function of quotations and cases is to let the hard material be understood, not to stand guarantor for the hard material)**). **Handling a conflict among several sources** (**when three accounts, each in the speaker's own words, of the same event contradict one another (an admissions dispute, a clash between teacher and student): the author's professional integrity shows in 'setting them out side by side + assessing the dimensions of credibility (the degree to which an interest is at stake, nearness in time, the degree to which the detail can be checked)', and not in making peace ahead of time on the reader's behalf (Rashomon is not the absence of truth; it is that the truth needs a procedure of arbitration: the author of an argumentative essay is often witness, lawyer and judge in one person — at the least, declare which hat you are wearing today)**).
:::

::: history Two traditions in the view of evidence {#sec-liangtiaotong}
**The Chinese road** (**Confucius: '文献不足故也，足则吾能征之矣' ('It is because the documents are insufficient; were they sufficient, I could cite them in evidence.') (Our translation.) — the character '征' ('cite in evidence') is the pre-Qin inscription of the burden of proof; *The Doctrine of the Mean*: '博学之，审问之，慎思之，明辨之，笃行之' ('Learn it broadly, enquire into it closely, think about it with care, distinguish it clearly, and carry it out in earnest.') (Our translation.) — five operations in the handling of evidence (the textbooks run the standard for the materials of 'argument' all the way to 'enquire into it closely'; Wang Chong's '疾虚妄' ('detesting the empty and the false') together with the house rule of evidential research among the Qing Confucians, '无征不信' ('Without evidence, there is no belief.') (Our translation.) — in Chinese writing the tradition of 'adducing citations' is thicker than the tradition of 'reasoning': this is a heritage and it is also a trap: strong at citation, short at inference. Zhang Xuecheng's '六经皆史' ('The Six Classics are all histories.') (Our translation.) demotes the Classics to historical sources, and by that move lays down a constitution for 'whatever is cited can be checked' (the right of definition in Chapter 2: evidential scholarship is the verifiability movement of the Chinese world, only its object is texts and not the world)**); **The Western road** (**Aristotle: 'the example, paradeigma, and deduction, syllogismos, are the two genes of argument'; in 1605 Bacon's *The Advancement of Learning* laid the foundation for taking evidence from experience; in the nineteenth century the 'criticism of historical sources' was made into a method (the prevailing account: the verification of historical sources, with the Ranke school as its mark, was institutionalised, and history moved out of literature to keep a household of its own — 'in the nineteenth century every discipline set up a customs house for its own evidence'; in the twentieth century evidence-based medicine made the 'levels of evidence' into a pyramid (Chapter 8) — the writing course's view of evidence is in fact the combined customs declaration of the customs houses of the several disciplines)**). **The confluence in the Chinese department** (**after 1917 the 'method of adducing citations' entered the textbooks of Chinese (*guowen*); in the 1980s the 'three elements of evidence (fact, principle, data)' of the argumentative essay took fixed form; after the 2010s 'fact-checking' entered as the occupation of a new genre — honesty about evidence passed from the scholar-official's self-discipline, became a norm for editors, and then became a post that can be contracted out: every time it is contracted out, 'checking for oneself' grows a degree less familiar: so this chapter is to train the feel of an audit into the hand, in the very age when you least need to audit**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in the evidence
**Ornamental citation** (**quotations pile higher and higher, and do less and less: a citation is not a side dish; it is a structural member — every sentence cited must be able to answer 'if it is deleted, does the argument collapse?' (if it does not collapse, delete it: a famous saying is not wall-boarding)**); **The swindle of the average** (**the three brothers of the average (the mean, the median, the mode) used interchangeably: 'an average monthly pay of 20,000' and 'a median of 8,000' are true at the same time, and both are telling the truth — which one the reporter chooses is the stance (the countermeasure in writing: wherever income or house prices are involved, always mark which one was used)**); **The chain of second-hand citation** (**citing Lu Xun from a public account, citing an experiment from the abstract of a paper, reading a book out of a review — every hop is a risk that the sense will shift (the procedure: important evidence is traced to the first-hand source; secondary evidence is marked '转引自' ('quoted from'): to quote at second hand is not the crime; to conceal the second-hand quotation is)**); **Timeliness left unrepaired** (**behind 'according to the statistics' sits a census of 2009: data rot, and the law is updated (before delivery, search the whole piece for the character '年' ('year') and read it through once more: this is the cheapest quality check, and the one most able to save a life)**).
:::

::: exercise Checking a source, in practice {level=1}
Choose three widely circulating 'famous sayings of famous people' (of your own choosing), and run a complete source check: the earliest source → whether it is the original text → the chain of translation or of second-hand quotation → the context (what quarrel he was in that year) → the conclusion (usable as evidence / able to serve only as 'the gist' / abandonment advised). Attach a review of fifty characters: **which hop is the easiest to lose**.
::: solution
Marking: **a complete procedure weighs more than whether the conclusion is right or wrong** (**even if you cannot find it, write 'where you looked, and at which hop you stopped' — a responsible 'the doubt stands' passes; a handsome 'it is said' does not**); what the review is testing (**the most dangerous hop is usually 'from second-hand to first-hand': everyone supposes that someone else has already checked (the personal version of the circular citation in Chapter 8: the three foreign media outlets of the world of quotations)**).
:::
:::

::: exercise The travels of one figure {level=2}
Follow one published figure (for example 'the scale of the internet population' or 'the divorce rate') through its changes from the original release to three retold versions: in a list, mark the scope lost or added at each hop (denominator, age band, urban and rural, year), and write one sentence giving **the strongest claim this figure can hold up in your essay**.
::: solution
Marking: **a comparison table of the three hops** (**common lesions: wiping off the year, wiping off the age group, and telling the '离结比' ('the ratio of divorces to marriages') as a 'divorce rate' — the trilogy in which scope evaporates**); **the strongest claim no wider than the data** ('"for 2023 the ratio of divorces to marriages in our country was X% (the scope of the Ministry of Civil Affairs)" can hold the weight; "half of all marriages are breaking" cannot: the waistline of the data decides the skirt of the sentence').
:::
:::

::: exercise A short essay: honesty about evidence is a style {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing '**honesty about evidence is not a moral slogan; it is a feature of the style: it is written in the parenthetical notes, in the three characters "转引自", and in the paragraph that of its own accord declares the limitations — the reader may not check, but can always feel it: the aura of an essay is the aura of its checkability**'. You must call on at least six of these: **the product of the three**, **the three posts of a case**, **the three virtues of quotation**, **declaring selection prejudice**, **the procedure for the chain of second-hand citation**, **the three brothers of the average**; and give a ruling: **in the age when AI-generated citations (including fabricated references) appear in bulk, is the writer's 'discipline of checkability' a burden or a moat — give your draft of a professional norm (at least three articles, including one aimed at yourself)**.
::: solution
Points for marks: the six mechanisms (**product, posts, three virtues, declaration, chain, scope**); the ruling (**for reference: the view that it is a burden holds in the short term (checking for oneself is slower than machine drafting); over the long term, checkability is a scarce good — when citations flood, 'a sentence with a complete source' carries a premium (the logic of hard currency in an age of inflation); an example of a draft norm: ① key data from two sources; ② a second-hand quotation must be marked; ③ towards oneself — for every claim, write from memory its 'way of dying' (closing the loop with Lesson 2: when the script is marked, look at whether one of the three really is 'harder on oneself' (binding only other people = the symmetrical scepticism of Chapter 8: you are still a fan)**); if the conclusion can be written '**honesty about evidence is, at the last, an aesthetics of time: a sentence that lies takes effect at once, and depreciates year by year; an honest sentence is half a beat slow, and yet it appreciates at compound interest — the person who writes is, in the end, living inside an interest rate**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed on audit day: five silos (**data, authority, statutes, the case, experience — source × relevance × timeliness; one zero and the whole is zero**), three posts (**falsifying, making concrete, bait: do not let it take employment beyond its authority**), three virtues (**do not break the text, do not shift the sense, do not mount it**), the mix (**facts hard, mechanism clear, values true**), the procedure (**trace it to the first-hand source, mark the second-hand quotation, run the check on the years**). The method in one sentence: **every piece of evidence is delivered to the standard that 'the other side will call in an independent audit'**. The next chapter connects the evidence to the circuit of inference: methods of reasoning.
:::
