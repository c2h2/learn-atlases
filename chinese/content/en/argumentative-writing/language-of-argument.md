title: "The language of argument"
---

The first five chapters govern whether it is right; this chapter governs whether it reads well — but first set down a constitution: **language is not the clothing of an argument; it is its skin: change the skin and the body temperature changes (the writing version of the alloy theory of Lesson 15: saw the cross-section open, and content and form, each taken by itself, is not the original work)**.

## Definition: the accuracy layer {#def-zhunque}

::: definition The public accounts of connectives {#def-lianjie}
**“因此／然而／况且／换言之／也就是说” ('therefore / however / moreover / in other words / that is to say') = the customs declaration of a logical connective.** (**Three cases from an audit of misuse**: ① “他每天打卡，可见他敬业” — "He clocks in every day; from this, one can see, he is devoted to the work." (Our translation.) — 可见 ('one can see that') underwrites an induction, but clocking in and devotion to the work are a correlation, not a definition (connectives lie more often than conclusions: when you examine an essay, examine the connectives first — “全文‘因此’共八处、其中五处前件推不出后件：这份审计报告比读后感值钱” — "In the whole text 'therefore' occurs eight times, and in five of them the antecedent does not yield the consequent: this audit report is worth more than a note of impressions after reading." (Our translation.)); ② “成绩下降，由此可见管理松弛；管理没有松弛，可见成绩不会下降” — "Results have fallen; from this one can see that management is slack. Management has not been slack; one can see that results will not fall." (Our translation.) — a false dilemma, borrowing 可见, fires two shots in a row (denying the antecedent, from Chapter 4, returns to life along a side path: the connective issues a permit to an invalid form); ③ after “换言之” the topic has been changed (**rewording and changing the topic, 换言 and 换题, differ by one character: the check — delete “换言之”; can the sentence before and the sentence after still be restored into each other? (If restoration fails, the author is quietly amending the contract.)**).) **A return visit to “之所以……是因为……”** ('the reason why … is that …'). (**An upgraded version of Lesson 1's issuing a second invoice for 的原因 and 因为 ('the reason' and 'because'):** the pattern itself is legitimate; the abuse lies in taking an inverted cause for profundity: “他成功，是因为他坚持” — "He succeeded; that is because he persisted." (Our translation.) — the inversion adds no information, only a pause (a common cosmetic for pseudo-causation: disguising temporal order as a mechanism by means of an inverted sentence).)
:::

::: definition The focal length of qualifiers, and positions of emphasis {#def-jiangjiao}
**For one and the same proposition, whichever room the qualifier lives in, the focus lights that bed.** (**“在双职工家庭中，多数家长支持延时托管”** — "In dual-earner families, most parents support extended after-school care." (Our translation.) (The limitation sits on the main clause = the scope of the whole text.) **vs “多数家长在双职工家庭中支持延时托管”** — "Most parents support extended after-school care in dual-earner families." (Our translation.) (Ambiguous: does the supporting take place inside the family?) **vs “家长总体上支持延时托管，尤其在双职工家庭”** — "Parents on the whole support extended after-school care, especially in dual-earner families." (Our translation.) (A concessive supplement = treating the limitation as insurance rather than as a contract.) — Read it through once after writing: is my limitation a scope, or a patch?) **The law of positions of emphasis.** (**In Chinese, the opening of a sentence and the close of a sentence are the only two strikes of the gavel in the whole text:** move “然而” ('however') to the opening, and the force of the rebuttal rises by half a grade; move “并不必然” ('not necessarily') to the close, and the reader is left a rear corridor in which to turn — but abuse the gavel and it goes dumb: two “正是” ('it is precisely') in one paragraph, four “恰恰” ('just exactly') in three paragraphs, and it reads as if someone were stamping a foot beside your ear the whole time.) **The quota on the character 是 and on inversion.** (**“真正的问题是”** ('the real problem is'), **“值得注意的是”** ('what is worth noticing is') — each time one of these two phrases is used, the reader's trust is overdrawn once: in a piece of a thousand characters, twice each is the ceiling (at the third time, it has itself become the problem that gets noticed).)
:::

## The decorum layer: person and numbers {#def-deti}

::: definition Four regimes of ownership for "we" {#def-women}
**The everybody form** (“**我们都爱孩子**” — "We all love children." (Our translation.) — Check the exchange rate: replace it with “部分人” ('some people'); does the sentence collapse? If it does not collapse, the deposit is false (money is paid into the reader's account, yet the passbook is not his: a reader for whom the payment was made will cash it out by walking away).) **The fellow-practitioner form** (“**我们知道**” — "We know." (Our translation.) — An old fault in academic writing: stuffing “本领域共识” ('the consensus of this field') into a paper by two people (Chapter 8, the grading of evidence: a consensus must be able to produce the documents).) **The in-group form** (“**咱们打工人的苦**” — "The hardship of us who work for a wage." (Our translation.) — A rhetorical embrace, a legitimate drawing-in: to recognise it is not a crime; to let it bypass the argument is the crime (“说‘咱们’的时候，他递来的不是证据，是工牌：先问工牌能不能刷开数据室的门” — "When he says 咱们, what he hands over is not evidence but a staff pass: first ask whether the pass can swipe open the door of the data room." (Our translation.)).) **The hostage-taking form** (“**任何一个有良知的人都会同意**” — "Anyone with a conscience will agree." (Our translation.) — A moral hostage (Chapter 9's jointly signed policy of appeal to the crowd and of fear: to disagree = to own that one has no conscience: there is only one unbinding pattern, and it works — “良知与本题结论没有推导关系，请出示论证” — "Conscience stands in no inferential relation to the conclusion of this question; please produce the argument." (Our translation.)).) **The double ledger of numbers and of vague wording.** (**Where it is precise, do not use rhetoric** (“超标 3.2 倍” ('exceeds the limit by 3.2 times') is to be written as 3.2 times, not as “触目惊心” ('shocking to the eye')). **Where it is rhetorical, do not be precise** (“这种做法令人疲惫” — "This practice is wearying." (Our translation.) — followed by “据统计 76% 的同事皱眉” — "According to the statistics, 76% of colleagues frowned." (Our translation.) — using figures to put make-up on a feeling, so that neither side pays out (the language version of Lesson 3's mismatch of use: numbers govern facts, adjectives govern attitude, and each keeps its own books; muddle the accounts once, and the audit downgrades the whole text).)
:::

## The rhythm layer {#def-jizou}

::: definition Balanced and loose sentences, the four-character frame, and three red lines {#def-jouzou}
**Balanced sentences and loose sentences = a design for breathing.** (**A balanced sentence (parallelism, antithesis) raises the speed and the pressure; a loose sentence slows down and leaves white space:** in a tense stretch of argument, use loose sentences (a causal chain needs every link to be seen clearly); a mobilising stretch and a closing stretch may be balanced (the emotion is settled).) **Three legitimate uses of parallelism, and one abuse.** (**Enumeration, the storing up of force, and the parallel couplet are all legitimate; abuse = standing in for the argument** — “它节约时间，它提高效率，它改变命运” — "It saves time, it raises efficiency, it changes fate." (Our translation.) After three lines of balanced sentences, all the reader remembers is the rhythm: for each extra line of parallelism, one degree of doubt is automatically lost: rhythm is collecting the tax on logic's behalf (the mnemonic for recognising it: if the items of a parallelism can change places with no loss of information, they have no information, only volume).) **The pull of the four-character frame.** (**A four-character idiom (*chengyu*) is a citation of the classics that takes three seconds:** “众所周知、不言而喻、毋庸置疑” ('as everyone knows', 'it goes without saying', 'there is no room for doubt') — three four-character frames can cover three missing arguments (the procedure for repaying the debt: once you have used “毋庸讳言” ('no need to shrink from saying it'), the next sentence must lay open the part that 讳 names, the part left unsaid: the citation a *chengyu* spared you must be remembered, and repaid).) **Three red lines.** (**① Irony is lost in the retelling** (your irony will be screenshotted into a claim taken at face value — the quotation-mark assassin of the internet age: do not dress an important claim in irony's cloak of invisibility (the price of Lesson 16's “曲笔” ('the oblique brush') is played again on today's page).) **② A pun cannot be carried across into the conclusion.** (**“棋如人生，落子无悔”** — "Chess is like life; a piece, once set down, is not regretted." (Our translation.) — The two tracks of a pun cannot run merged on the day of judgement (taking a rhetorical coincidence for an argumentative necessity: Chapter 6's interest-paying clause for analogy applies equally to the *chengyu*).) **③ Humour cannot stand in for a concession.** (**An opponent laughed to the ground has not admitted fault:** the joke has won, and the problem is still where it was — humour is the strong liquor of rhetoric: you may toast the reader with it; you cannot make it the staple food.)
:::

::: example Three-register handling: three faces of one paragraph {#ex-sandang}
The kernel passage: “**学校统一收集手机，课间还是有人玩，说明这办法效果有限。**” — "The school collects phones in one place; during the breaks people still play on them, which shows that the measure has a limited effect." (Our translation.) Rewrite it, separately, as ① the academic register (for a project report), ② the newspaper-commentary register (for an evening-paper page), ③ the examination-hall register (for a *gaokao* script written up from a floor of 60 marks), each in 70–110 characters; then review them against one another: **what each register has sacrificed for the sake of its own reader**.
::: solution
① **The academic register** (“**某校实施手机统一保管制度后，课间时段学生自报使用时长仅下降 18%（n=312，两学期），提示以物理隔离为主的干预对同伴在场情境下的使用行为效力有限**” — "After a certain school put into effect a system of unified custody for mobile phones, the duration of use that students reported of themselves during breaks fell by only 18% (n=312, two semesters), which suggests that an intervention consisting mainly of physical isolation has a limited efficacy upon use-behaviour in situations where peers are present." (Our translation.) — Sacrificed: readability, and the sense of being on the scene (the terminology takes “玩” ('playing') apart into “同伴在场情境下的使用行为” ('use-behaviour in a situation where peers are present'): the price of precision is body temperature).) ② **The newspaper register** (“**铁盒锁得住手机，锁不住走神：课间十分钟被交出来的不只是屏幕，还有本该用来吵架、望天、发呆的时间——制度管物，教育管心，这笔账不能只让校长一个人算**” — "An iron box can lock up a phone; it cannot lock up a mind that wanders: what is handed over in the ten minutes of the break is not only the screen, but also the time that ought to have gone on quarrelling, staring at the sky and staring blankly — a regulation governs things, education governs the mind, and this account cannot be left for the head teacher alone to reckon." (Our translation.) — Sacrificed: falsifiability (“锁不住走神” ('cannot lock up a wandering mind') — how fine it sounds, and how impossible to check: the payout rate of a quotable line is extremely high (Lesson 3's mounting-and-framing clause, moved to the language layer)).) ③ **The examination-hall register** (“**统一收纳手机，收的是道具，收不走动机。若课堂本身缺少让人抬头的理由，任何保管制度都只是把问题寄存（亮桩式判断句＋一个比喻封顶＋限度：本文不反对收纳，反对把收纳当作教育）**” — "Storing the phones in one place stores the props; it does not store away the motive. If the class itself lacks a reason for anyone to look up, any system of custody only leaves the problem in deposit (a stake-showing judgement sentence + one metaphor as a cap + a limit: this essay does not oppose the storing; it opposes treating the storing as education)." (Our translation.) — Sacrificed: evidence (the examination-hall register assumes, by default, that “后文会补” ('the text that follows will supply it'); forget to supply it, and the offence is the same as the newspaper register's; but it has kept two things: an arguable stake, and the bumper of a qualification).) **Points for the mutual review.** (**The three registers are not three consciences; they are three different contracts:** the academic sells what can be checked again, the newspaper sells a sense of direction inside the resonance, the examination hall sells clarity within two minutes — change the register, not the honesty: not one register is permitted to write “有限” ('limited') as “无效” ('of no effect'), or “动机” ('motive') as “品行” ('moral character').)
:::

::: intuition "I can't say it clearly" is a piece of intelligence, not an apology {#int-shuo}
“**我说不清，但我感觉是这样**!” — "I can't say it clearly, but I feel that it is so!" (Our translation.) — **This sentence is often taken for modesty; in fact it is a piece of intelligence: it announces that the claim has not yet taken shape** (the language version of Lesson 1's “写不出 ABC 的只是情绪” ('what cannot be written out as A, B and C is only emotion')). **Three forms of the illness of not being able to say it clearly.** (**① The concept has not been defined** (the “好学校” ('good school') in your mind has three faces); **② the mechanism has not been driven through** (you know the two ends; the middle is a tunnel); **③ the counter-example has not been dealt with** (there is one you are dodging) — the order of the prescription is the order of the course: Lesson 2 plants the stake, Lesson 4 the circuit, Lesson 6 clears the ground: all the grammar of the writing course returns, in the end, to the alphabet of the logic course.) **The illness in the other direction: saying it too clearly.** (**A style of “过度确定” ('excessive certainty')** (every sentence is utterly categorical, and every judgement carries no error bar) does not survive ten minutes in the inspection of the accounts in Lesson 3: a mature style is “强度与证据同轨” ('strength and evidence on the same track') — wherever the evidence has walked to, the tone follows: the honesty of language is, in the end, an art of following.)
:::

::: history Four enactments of the view that wording should convey {#sec-cida}
**Spring and Autumn period.** Confucius, “**辞达而已矣**” ("In wording, to convey is enough." (Our translation.)) (*Analects* — conveying the meaning is the upper limit: ornament that goes beyond conveying the meaning is opposed); and again “**言之不文，行而不远**” ("If the words are not patterned, they do not travel far." (Our translation.)) (*Zuo Tradition* — without literary colour they are not handed on far: **taken together, the two sentences are the first pair of tensions in the Chinese study of literary prose: the tax-saving school and the far-travelling school, and from then on they keep one shared set of accounts**). **Southern Dynasties.** *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, the chapter “情采”: “**文附质也**” ("Patterned writing is attached to substance." (Our translation.)) “**质待文也**” ("Substance waits upon patterned writing." (Our translation.)) (ornament and substance waiting each upon the other: **the parallel-prose (*pianwen*) version of the alloy theory: it is not that a naked thought exists first and is then dressed; only when the clothes are formed is there a shape**). **Tang.** The writers of ancient-style prose (*guwen*): “惟陈言之务去” ("One's task is nothing other than to be rid of stale phrasing." (Our translation.)) (Han Yu 韩愈, *Da Li Yi shu* 答李翊书 ('A Reply to Li Yi') — **to be rid of stale phrasing = attack and defence by counter-example, at the language layer: the *chengyu* and the stock phrase are thought's default options; remove them, and one is forced to begin really thinking (the positive recipe for Chapter 9's “清单不生产作品” ('a list does not produce a work'): clearing out template sentences is the power-on self-test of an argument)**). **Qing.** Gu Yanwu 顾炎武, *Rizhi lu* 日知录 ('Record of Daily Knowledge'): “**辞有滥而意不违，则宁写而不删；不若辞足以达意，而无乎冗字也**” ("If the wording is excessive and yet the meaning is not betrayed, then one would rather write it and not delete it; that is not as good as wording sufficient to convey the meaning, and without a redundant character." (Our translation.)) (a commonly circulated adaptation — **when characters are deleted, empty exaggeration is deleted first: the linguistic discipline of evidential scholarship: every adjective must be able to produce its evidence (this is the earliest "customs house of the adjective" in the Chinese-speaking world)**). **Modern.** (**The programme of the modern vernacular (*baihua*)** (**the common account: “话怎么说，文章就怎么写”** — "However the words are spoken, so the essay is written." (Our translation.) (Hu Shi 胡适 (Hu Shih)) — **the illusion of speech: supposing that to remove parallel pairing is to have removed stale phrasing, when in fact one has only changed to another batch of stale phrasing** (“随着社会的不断发展” ('with the continuous development of society'): the birth certificate of the new eight-legged essay; the rise of a critical study of language (Lesson 16's “名目” ('designations'), a branch shop of semantics: a systematic audit of the stock phrases of official documents))). **Contemporary.** (**The intervention of visual layout: boldface, subheadings and bullet markers have changed the rhythmic unit of argument in Chinese** (Chapter 8, “信息呈现即论证环境” ('the presentation of information just is the environment of the argument'): the second half of the language lesson is on the page and on the screen).)

::: warning Four pits of language
**Inflation of connectives.** (**Before “综上所述” ('to sum up') there is only one sentence; before “由此可见” ('from this one can see') there is only a mood:** a connective's credit stands in inverse proportion to its stock (each time you use “显然” ('obviously'), check for yourself whether you have just proved it).) **The false plural.** (**If nowhere in the whole text can “我们” ('we') be replaced by a concrete group of people** (“本校高二年级 412 名学生” — "412 students in the second year of senior secondary at this school." (Our translation.)) **= a hollow joint signature** (the language version of Chapter 3's declaration of selection bias: whoever you represent, name them first).) **Dependence on quotable lines.** (**Every paragraph wants, at its end, a sentence that can be copied out:** the rate of being excerpted and the rate of argument stand in inverse proportion (the paragraph version of Lesson 1's slogan clause: a quotable line is a compressed biscuit; a whole text cannot consist only of biscuits).) **Rhetoric's disclaimer.** (**“我开玩笑的”** ('I was joking'), **“开个玩笑别当真”** ('just a joke, don't take it seriously'), stuck on after the offence — there is no medicine of regret for language: the price of irony and of the pun is already paid at the moment they are uttered (Chapter 6: to be responsible to the reader = to be responsible from the first sentence).)
:::

::: exercise An audit of connectives {level=1}
Take a paragraph of your own old work (or write an imitation), circle every connective, and make a table: connective / antecedent and consequent / whether it pays out (pays out / exaggerates / is misaligned) — and write a **scheme of replacement, or of deletion and revision** (at least three places).
::: solution
Marking: **connectives of the “因此” ('therefore') kind are each brought in for a hearing** (**whether the antecedent can be written as a legitimate form of “若……则……” ('if …, then …') — if it cannot be written, it is exaggeration**); **the scheme of revision must give the substitute connective, or the change in the sentence's meaning after the deletion** (**delete “可见”, and the sentence is on the contrary more honest: a connective is sometimes not a bridge but a slide**).
:::
:::

::: exercise A check-up of person {level=2}
Replace every “我们／大家／任何人” ('we / everyone / anyone') in a paragraph of an editorial with **a group of people who can be named, and a scale**, and judge place by place: after the replacement, does the sentence still hold? List **the inventory of original sentences that collapse after the replacement**, and diagnose the lesion.
::: solution
Marking: **the collapse list is the real assignment of this piece** (**a collapse = the original was propped up by a false deposit (the same kind of audit as Lesson 2's “真正的 X” ('the real X'))**); if nothing in the whole piece collapses, an explanation is required (**whether the author really did delimit each place, or your replacement was not hard enough — both of the honest possibilities must be written**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the ethics of skin {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**‘语言是透明的工具’是天真的：它更像是皮肤——你透过它看世界，世界也透过它摸你：所以修辞的伦理不是‘少用’，是‘别用它借刀’——借读者的手，砍自己没查证的结论**” — "'Language is a transparent tool' is naive: it is more like skin — you see the world through it, and the world also touches you through it: so the ethics of rhetoric is not 'use less of it', but 'do not use it to borrow a knife' — borrowing the reader's hand to cut down a conclusion you have not verified." (Our translation.) You must call on **the customs house of connectives**, **the four regimes of "we"**, **the double ledger**, **the tax rate of parallelism**, **the three red lines** and **the three-register contract**, at least six items; and give a ruling: **where the line lies between optimising expression in order to persuade and optimising expression in order to manipulate — write three criteria that can be handed to an editorial office to enforce**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**customs house, ownership regime, double ledger, tax rate, red lines, contract**); the ruling on the line (**for reference: manipulation = writing the fallible as the necessary, writing what was paid on another's behalf as a consensus, writing the unproved as the obvious — three specimen criteria: ① a test that strength is aligned (draw five judgement sentences at random, and compare their qualifiers with the table of evidence: a mismatch rate >20% is sent back for revision); ② hostages brought to zero (every pattern of the form “任何……的人” ('anyone who …') must be rewritten as a group that can be named); ③ a quotable line must be restorable (each quotable line must be rewritable as a synonymous judgement with the rhetoric removed, otherwise it is demoted to a title — what the marking looks at: whether the criteria can also be carried out by the author (symmetrical scepticism: the language version of Chapter 8's football-fan clause)**); if the conclusion can be written “**修辞的荣誉在于它愿意被还原：好比喻经得起‘换句话说’，好排比经得起删，好‘我们’经得起点名——皮肤之下，你仍是那个要交税的人**” — "The honour of rhetoric is that it is willing to be restored: a good metaphor can stand 'in other words', a good parallelism can stand being deleted, a good 'we' can stand being named — beneath the skin, you are still the person who has to pay the tax." (Our translation.), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Archived as dermatology: accuracy (**the customs house of connectives, the focal length of qualifiers, the quota on emphasis**), decorum (**the four regimes of ownership for "we", numbers and adjectives in separate books**), rhythm (**the breathing of balanced and loose sentences, the tax rate of parallelism, a debt a *chengyu* borrows must be repaid**), the three red lines (**irony is lost, a pun does not enter the verdict, humour does not serve as a concession**), the three registers (**change the register, not the honesty**). The method in one sentence: **of every piece of rhetoric, ask one question: whom were you working for — for logic, or for evasion**. The next chapter enters the city: current-affairs commentary and the writing of criticism.
:::
