title: "Reviews"

The previous chapter said that criticism of literature and the arts has to clear the customs of argument; this chapter spreads the whole market out — **from an academic book review to an eight-character piece of sales copy, five exchange rates of one and the same sentence: "值得一读" ('worth reading') needs three pages of argument at the highest tier, and at the lowest tier only a patter rhyme: the first step in writing across the tiers is to change currency**.

## Definition: one spectrum {#def-guangpu}

::: definition Five tiers of genre and the exchange rate of evidence {#def-wudang}
**Academic book review** (**a contract with one's peers: length reckoned by the thousand characters, comparison reckoned against three to five works in the same field, judgement reckoned in contribution and shortcoming — its "good" must be open to reconsideration**); **press book note** (**a contract to guide the public reader: translate the jargon, give a chapter by which to enter, answer "why should I spend two hours on this book" — the evidence called for is cut by half, but the honesty is not**); **short review on a platform** (**a market in word of mouth: inside thirty characters, deliver a judgement one can pass on — "what it tests is compression, not omission: compression keeps the skeleton of the argument, omission cuts it away**); **sales recommendation** (**a commercial contract: the audience is a buyer, not a reader; what is sold is "what experience you will get" — enthusiasm is allowed, invention is not ("'read in three days' is a promise, 'of benefit for a lifetime' is fraud: claims of the experience kind are to be audited under the dual rules of the advertising law and of conscience**)); **belly-band and celebrity line** (**an advertisement for the advertisement: when every book is "the most stunning of the year", the purchasing power of "stunning" falls to zero — the inflation of recommendations, and a reform of the home currency (the marketing branch of the inflation of citation in Chapter 3: the history of money is played out again here, and the currency's name changes from "经典" ('classic') to "封神" ('deified')**). **The shared base** (**a court used in common by the five tiers: have you read it (the evidence comes from inside the text), what do you set it against (the comparison set), dare you say for whom it is not worth it (qualification) — the tier changes, the three questions do not ("a spectrum is not a slippery slope: what slides down is not the genre, but honesty**).
:::

## The three pairs of glasses of a book review {#def-sange}

::: definition Description, evaluation, recommendation {#def-mulu}
**① Description** (**"what it tells" — what the author says on the back cover does not count: first prove, in 80 characters, that you have really read it (description is the legitimate form of a mild disrespect towards the author: to paraphrase is to run a quality check; the technique: use the sentence form "本书主张……为此它提出……但它在＿＿处让步" ('This book claims ……; to that end it proposes ……; but at ＿＿ it concedes'. (Our translation.)), one sentence holding three things: the claim, the method, the weak point**)); **② Evaluation** (**"what proportion of the thing it meant to do has it done" — set the author's intention against the achievement (the reading version of the three report sheets in Lesson 15, "intention ≠ achievement ≠ consequence": evaluation does not ask "do you like it?", it asks "what will its intended readers gain, and what will they lose?"; three choices of comparison set: books of the same kind, the author's earlier books, the ambition inside the book ("to judge an introductory book by Tolstoy's standard is laziness, and so is judging 《万历》 by the standard of a *shuǎngwén* 爽文 ('pleasure-read') — choose the wrong comparison set, and the whole review is automatically void**)); **③ Recommendation** (**"for whom" — the ethics of recommending: "suits everyone" = suits no one; sell the book to the right people, not to everyone ("to push a work of popular economics on 'everyone who wants to change their fate' is to sell a scalpel as a protective charm**). **The order of the three must not be shuffled** (**to recommend before describing = to sell the goods; only to describe, and not to evaluate = a porter of the contents page; to have evaluated, and not dare to recommend = academic slipperiness (the professional honour of a book review stands at the third step: your judgement has to make the reader dare to stake two hours on it**.
:::

::: definition The engineering of honesty in a recommendation {#def-gongcheng}
**The three-piece set** (**whom it suits (specific down to a situation: "for a reader of thirty who is hesitating over whether to change profession", not "for everyone"); whom it does not suit ("not suitable: people who want a formula out of success studies (成功学); Chapter 2 of this book will disappoint you" — **"不适合我" ('not for me') is the five characters in a recommendation that are the dearest and the least often seen: at one stroke they complete qualification, a persona and a screening in reverse: the person who writes them draws wages by the readers' refund rate**); one locating of the evidence (**a page number or a scene: "the comparison table on page 47 repays the price of the book" — a recommendation that can be checked back is a sample garment; one that cannot is an atmosphere squad**)). **A case history of belly-band diseases** (**celebrity lines by the wholesale (if every reader were a person who "read it until the tears came", tears would be priced by the *jin*, a catty, whose weight has varied by period); inflated figures (the basis of the count in "10 million copies sold": how many volumes a boxed set is counted as); hitching a ride on authority ("shortlisted for a certain prize" written as "awarded a certain prize" — the branch, in the publishing trade, of the three checks on the basis of a count in Chapter 3**.
:::

::: definition Presence: the ground rent of reviews of film and theatre {#def-changjing}
**The performance is over, and the evidence left is only your memory** (**the review version of the "presence tax" in Play 15: the discipline of evidence in a theatre review is stricter — version, performance, cast and house must be marked (two casts of one and the same title can be two works: a theatre review that gives no cast is reviewing the script, and that is called an after-reading note**)); **Spoiler grades** (**this chapter refines the contract of the previous chapter: a review of the structural kind holds only when the spoiler reaches the vital point ("to review 《东方快车谋杀案》 (*Murder on the Orient Express*) without speaking of the ending is to review a haircut without speaking of the hair" — here the spoiler is a duty); but to spoil at the wrong level is a breach of contract ("one sentence, 'he is the murderer', is murder upon a person who has not seen it, and yet necessary to the argument: graded marks (core trick / key turn / detail) are the reviewer's seat belt**)); **The paradox of the "spoiler-free review"** (**its workable solution: move the claim up to a height at which it still holds without a spoiler ("the moral centre of gravity of this film lies in complicity, not in suspense" — the higher the claim's level of abstraction, the lower its dependence on spoilers: what a spoiler tests is not boldness, but the altitude of the claim**.
:::

::: example Three tiers of text for one and the same book {#ex-sandang}
For a work of economic history on the Ming and Qing transport of tribute grain (*cáoyùn* 漕运), write texts at three tiers and assess them yourself: **① an abstract of an academic book review** (150 characters; it must contain a comparison set and one shortcoming); **② a press book note** (200 characters, with a chapter by which to enter and a "for whom"); **③ a sales recommendation** (80 characters, with a "whom it does not suit").
::: solution
① **The academic tier** ("**This book uses the tribute-grain transport to reconstruct the dual track, of payment in kind and of payment in money, in Ming state finance. Set against earlier treatments in institutional history, its advantage is that it charges transport wastage and inflated levies back onto the account books of the two levels, the centre and the subprefecture-and-county; its thesis that 'administrative deficit is endogenous' can be read in parallel with studies of Qing customary fees (陋规). The shortcoming is that, in the section on southern civilian grain transport, the sample leans towards official compilations, and the boat-household point of view rests on only three stone inscriptions. The calculation of the rates in Chapter 4 can be checked over again, and the data in the appendix are the most suitable to cite.**" — self-assessment: the three items are all present, the comparison set (older works of institutional history, studies of Qing customary fees), the shortcoming (a biased sample), what can be checked back (Chapter 4 and the appendix); the risk: one's peers will go and look up those three stones**); ② **The press tier** ("**If you think the tribute-grain transport was only 'shipping the grain to Beijing', Chapter 2 will correct that sentence of yours: how one river fed the trades of a hundred thousand people, and brought forth a bureaucratic language lying between an expense claim and a division of spoils — the best way into this book is Chapter 2 and the conclusion, the former giving the scene, the latter the question. Suits: readers who read history and want, in passing, to understand a little of the fiscal economy; does not suit: the two hours before sleep, for someone who wants a thrill and the palace rooms of the famous.**" — self-assessment: a chapter by which to enter, plus "for whom" in two clauses, signs the contract of the reading guide in full; the one place, "bureaucratic language", is rhetoric, but it can be checked back, and it barely passes**); ③ **The sales tier** ("**For people who work supply chains, work in finance, or are just now suspicious of 'the language inside the system': the table of rates on page 4 will remind you of your own expense form. Not for those who want chicken soup (鸡汤, comfort-talk) or a pleasure-read — this book guarantees only to make you better at doing the accounts.**" — self-assessment: whom it suits is specific down to a situation, whom it does not suit is named, the evidence is located to a page; it has not caught the belly-band disease (it does not say "必看" ('must-read') or "封神" ('deified'): the moment they are used, the home currency inflates again**).
:::
:::

::: intuition Recommendation is prophecy, a bad review is surgery {#int-yanyu}
**A recommendation is subject to one test: the fulfilment rate** (**"you will like it" is a statement about the reader's future; to write it is to open a position for tomorrow's self: for a person who writes recommendations over a long time, reputation = the payout curve of past sentences — this is why issuing "best of the year" without limit is the trade injuring itself: what it overdraws is the common reserve of every honest recommender (the version, for this genre, of the tragedy of the commons**). **The three duties of a bad review** (**to the matter ("it treats the characters as containers for an agenda", and not "it is rotten" — an adjective is a placeholder in the written judgement; the mechanism is the judgement itself); give a comparison ("what XX, on the same subject, did in three pages, this one misses across three hundred" — a bad review with no comparison set is only a temper**); **keep what is fair (distinguish the work from its mishaps ("the absence of the director of the first part is not the same as incompetence in the principal creators — scold an industrial problem into a moral problem, and the bad review inflates too**"). **The old account of 《知言》** (**Mencius's knowing the person and the times (知人论世) — using one's understanding to meet the poet's intent (以意逆志) is "得之" ('getting it'); not knowing the person and yet discussing the times is the lesion of most bad reviews (the popular version of the intentional fallacy in Lesson 15: mind the measure — using one's understanding to meet the intent is not to summon the author into court (intention is an excellent scaffolding and a very poor court of last instance (the clause of Lesson 15, pasted in as it stands): apart from spoilers, the second taboo of reviewing is that "the author is dead" is misread as "the person who studies the author is guilty"**).
:::

::: history The account book of praise {#sec-haoping}
**Warring States.** Mencius, "**颂其诗，读其书，不知其人，可乎？是以论其世也**" (Chant their poems and read their books, and not know the persons — is that acceptable? Therefore one discusses their times. (Our translation.)) (《万章下》 — **the first methodological clause of reviewing: knowing the person and the times**); **Southern Dynasties.** 《文心雕龙·知音》 (*The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, 'Knowing the Tone') (**the essentials of the received account: the chapter enumerates the blindnesses of prizing the ancient and slighting the present, and of exalting oneself and suppressing others, and sets out a procedure, "缀文者情动而辞发，观文者披文以入情" (The one who composes is stirred in feeling, and the words come forth; the one who reads opens the text so as to enter the feeling. (Our translation.)) — **the meta-question of Chinese criticism: on what grounds do we judge a work we do not fully understand; "无私于轻重，不偏于憎爱" (Impartial as to light and heavy, not swayed by hate or love. (Our translation.)) (a set phrase of that chapter) laid down the law beforehand for honesty in reviewing (the Southern Dynasties version of the iron man of Lesson 1**)); **Song–Qing.** Remarks on poetry (诗话), essays on literature, and critical marking (评点) (**interlinear comments in the manner of Jin Shengtan 金圣叹: the summit of making a recommendation present on the page — one note in the upper margin, of the single word "妙" ('marvellous'), and three lines of evidence (traditional critical marking in fact keeps the discipline of evidence better than most of today's "绝了" ('incredible')**)); **The 1920s–30s.** The birth of the modern book-review column (**newspaper supplements opened columns of book reviews, and publishing and criticism fed each other — "new books need a reputation, criticism needs a position to hold: this exchange produced the earliest group of professional reviewers in China, and also the earliest disputes over soft articles (软文, paid copy dressed as a review) (human nature had no need to wait for the algorithm**)); **The 1980s–90s.** The reading craze and "book talk" (书话) (**the manner of book talk in the line of Tang Tao 唐弢 and Huang Shang 黄裳: the recommendation capped once it has been made literary — the private voice-part of the informal essay stands surety for a public judgement (the classical contract of "I read it for you"**)); **The 2000s to the present.** Platform scores (**star ratings on the model of Douban (豆瓣) became, in fact, the infrastructure of a national word of mouth — and, coming with that, an arms race: fans pushing the charts, five stars from the water army (水军, paid posters), one star from the rival camp ("the seismology of systems of scoring: the median is more honest than the mean, the comment section more honest than the score — to write a recommendation today is to make a signal inside the noise: this is in itself a problem of the method of argument (the entertainment industry's branch shop of the base rate and the sample frame in Chapter 8**).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of recommending
**The atmosphere squad** (**the whole piece is "震撼、封神、必看" ('stunning', 'deified', 'must-read'), and there is not one location that can be checked back: the first audit of a recommendation is "what is left after every adjective is deleted" (what is left as page numbers and scenes passes; what is left as exclamation marks returns to zero**); **The wrong comparison** (**judging an introductory book by the standard of the Nobel Prize, judging an experimental play by the standard of the box office — a comparison set out of place = the whole piece void (choosing the comparison set is the reviewer's constitutional court, and it is greater in power than the verdict**); **A spoiler past its powers** (**writing "I do not like it" as "its ending is bad": a spoiler may serve the argument only, and may not serve the venting of anger (to spoil a work of the core-trick kind is equivalent to harm done to the reader's property**); **Person and book made one** (**the author's house collapses (塌房), and the book is burned; the work is great, and the personal character is pardoned — both ends are the lazy administration of a cancellation regime: "knowing the person and the times" is a method, not a law of collective punishment (连坐) (the intention clauses of Lesson 15: the accounts of the work and of the author are to be kept in two books, and both books are to be kept**).
:::

::: exercise Compression test {level=1}
Take a long review of 300 characters that you have written or have seen, compress it into a short review of 30 characters, and you are required to **keep the skeleton of the argument** (object + judgement + one thread of evidence); then the other way: expand one sentence, "绝了" ('incredible'), into a short review of 80 characters that would pass.
::: solution
Marking: **the compression must not leave only emotion** (**if any one of the three pieces of the skeleton is missing, it is "omission rather than compression"**); **the expansion must bring in evidence that can be checked back** (**any one of a scene, a page number, a performance and a cast — the lesion of "绝了" is zero reference: the surgery of expanding is to rewrite the exclamation mark as a full stop plus the evidence**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Writing the three-piece set {level=2}
For a work you have just finished (any one of a book, a play or an exhibition), write one "honest recommendation": whom it suits (down to a situation), whom it does not suit (name one kind of reader), one sentence of evidence that can be checked back; and draft for it **one way of praising that will not inflate** (forbidden: 震撼, 封神, 必看, 天花板 — 'stunning', 'deified', 'must-read', 'the ceiling').
::: solution
Marking: **the list of forbidden words is a hard bar** (**if a word of inflation appears, rewrite — the aim of the exercise is to restore the value of the currency, not to practise modesty**); **"whom it does not suit" must be a real group of people, and not an imagined enemy** ("not for people with no patience" is an insult; "not for readers who want an operating manual" is a service**); the point tested in "a way of praising that does not inflate" (**specific to the point of being rechecked: 'it dares, in episode 3, to make the protagonist disappear for 40 minutes' beats ten times "炸裂" ('explosive')**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the honourable poverty of the critic {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing "**the critic is the honourably poor department of the cultural economy: he saves time for the reader, lights the blind spots for the author, and maintains the value of the currency for the market — none of the three things produces sales directly, so the whole of his income comes as interest on reputation: this is why a favourable review can be cheap, a bad review must be expensive, and the inflation of recommendations is in the end paid for by the most honest authors**". You must call on at least six items: **the five-tier exchange rate**, **the three pairs of glasses**, **the three-piece set of a recommendation**, **the fulfilment rate and the commons**, **the three duties of a bad review**, **the measure kept in knowing the person and the times**; and give a ruling: **"whether a bad work deserves to exist"** — setting out from the professional ethics of the reviewer (you are not permitted to cite "the market will decide" as the answer: the market's answer is also an argument; take it apart, and then answer**).
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**exchange rate = the level of the contract; the three glasses = the sequence of operations; the three-piece set = the engineering of qualification; the payout curve = the accounting of reputation; the three duties = the norms of the surgery; knowing the person and the times = two books of account**); the ruling (**for reference: the right to exist is separated from the right to a favourable review — a bad work may of course exist (inspection is not the critic's post; the clauses, in Lesson 6, on the right to press a question), but the critic is under no duty to maintain the value of the currency on its behalf: to refuse a recommendation and to give a bad review are both the practice of the profession, whereas "not to evaluate" is the negative liberty most abused (what really ought to be examined is "the algorithmic cause of the silence" — if the silence is a strategy, silence is the gentle version of a false statement: only when "the market will decide" is taken apart into "who is defining visibility" (a return visit to the platform argument of Chapter 8) has the eye of the question been answered**); if the conclusion can be written "**on the balance sheet of the honourably poor department, the only thing that cannot be depreciated is public credibility: a favourable review gets you liked today, public credibility gets you cited tomorrow — this line of work, reviewing, deals in futures on time**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The market, archived: the spectrum (**five exchange rates, one and the same base of honesty: having read, having compared, daring to say for whom it is not worth it**), the three glasses (**description, evaluation, recommendation: the order must not be shuffled**), the three-piece set (**whom it suits, whom it does not suit, one sentence that can be checked back**), presence (**the cast marked, spoilers graded, the altitude of the claim**), the account book (**the fulfilment rate, the surgery of the bad review, the two books of knowing the person and the times**). The method in one sentence: **only the person who draws wages by the readers' refund rate is fit to write a recommendation**. The last chapter: back to the examination hall — deliver this course in 90 minutes.
