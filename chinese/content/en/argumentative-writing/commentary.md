title: "Commentary and editorials"

The first seven chapters are the training ground; this chapter enters the city — **current-affairs commentary is the coming-of-age rite of the argumentative essay: it requires you to speak when the information is incomplete, to produce evidence when positions are clamorous, and, on the premise that your own piece will have expired by tomorrow, to sign your real name**.

## Definition: two contracts {#def-lianghetong}

::: definition The contract of current-affairs commentary: timeliness and the public {#def-shiping}
**The timeliness clause** (**"It must be said now" is the admission ticket of current-affairs commentary, and also its expiry date: the next day it is already a tin** ("Publish yesterday's indignation today, and the flavour is unchanged while the nutrition is entirely lost — what has expired is not the words, but the relevance"); **from this arises one professional ethic: speed is the vocation of current-affairs commentary, and guarding against reversal is the executor of its will** (set out in detail in the next section)); **the public clause** (**a private matter must pass through one translation before it deserves the newspaper: "我的早餐涨价" ('My breakfast has gone up in price.' (Our translation.)) is a diary; "早餐价格沿供应链的传导在哪个环节被截留" ('At which link is the transmission of breakfast prices along the supply chain being intercepted and held?' (Our translation.)) is current-affairs commentary — the first operation of current-affairs commentary is to translate "I" into "we"** (but the audit of Lesson 7 comes first: this "we" must be nameable: the "we" of current-affairs commentary = a list of those affected, not a rhetorical carpool)); **the not-yet-due clause** (**"The stakeholders have not finished speaking" — a sense of procedure in public discussion: what current-affairs commentary adjudicates is often not the facts (facts belong to investigation), but "what handling of this matter counts as an account given": a commentator's power is the power to press questions, not the power to close the case** ("closing the case beyond one's authority" is the most respectable abuse of power in current-affairs commentary: writing "应查明" ('it should be ascertained') as "已查明" ('it has been ascertained'), the piece can be got out, but credibility cannot be got back)).
:::

::: definition Three steps: news peg, issue, claim {#def-youtou}
**The news peg is a hook, not the claim** (**the difference between overnight melon and a good news peg: the news peg is happening now, and the stakeholders have not finished speaking — "the use of the news peg is to invite the reader in; once they are in, you have to start posting the ledger"**); **issue-framing** (**rewrite the event from "who is bad" into "which rule has failed": the same event can be framed as four issues — food safety, the governance of outsourcing, the disclosure of information, and parental supervision — the choice of issue comes before the claim, and the bias of the choice of topic is already complete at the moment of choosing** (the institutional edition of Chapter 6's square: a newspaper's "square" is called the pre-edition meeting)); **settling the form of the claim** (**go through the full set from Lesson 2: debatable, single, qualified, and with a way of dying — the special point of a claim in current-affairs commentary lies only in the occasion: "it must be capable of being accepted and signed off within 1,200 characters: a claim large enough to need a book is dishonest towards a newspaper** ('abolish such-and-such a system', and grand prescriptions of that kind, should most of the time be rewritten as 'such-and-such a clause should be reconsidered according to such-and-such a procedure': the virtue of current-affairs commentary is an executable modesty")).
:::

## A genealogy of forms: five faces {#def-wufu}

::: definition Voice, licence and volume {#def-shengbu}
**News commentary** (**close against the battlefield, one matter and one argument, the smallest quantity of reasoning but the hardest timeliness: "its KPI is speed and accuracy, not depth — setting a quick commentary to do a deep commentary's work is like sending an ambulance on a long-distance run"**); **"this paper's commentator" / the legal person's voice** (**the "we" signed "本报" ('this paper') is a legal person: it has the right to represent the editorial department, and also the duty not to dress itself up as the truth — in a piece signed by the institution, the strength of the claim must be lower than in the same piece under the author's personal name** (the institution apologises for a wrong character, and also apologises for a claim that has gone too far: the qualifier on the legal person's voice is its liability insurance)); **the column** (**a licence for a personal style: readers forgive extremity, and do not forgive muddle — a column's privilege is "I say it as established fact", and its duty is "I am ready at any time to be named and to own the account"** ("a signature is the guarantee: a nameless column is the hot money of the world of current-affairs commentary")); **essayistic commentary / the *zawen* lineage** (**a renewal of the page subscription for the daggers and spears of Lesson 16: arguing by image and irony — but the dermatology red line is enforced to the letter** (irony is lost in a screenshot: the modern tax on current-affairs commentary in the *zawen* manner, paid over to those who browse by screenshot)); **the editorial** (**a consensus-advocacy form: the highest volume paired with the lowest information — so the editorial loses marks the fastest: its technical difficulty is "accommodating disagreement on the premise that one must unite"** (a well-written editorial fits pipes to the dispute; a badly written one brushes green paint over the conclusion)).
:::

::: definition The balancing method and unfinished towers {#def-peiping}
**A balancing reference for a thousand-character piece of current-affairs commentary** (**one stake (a factual core of about 200 characters) + one reason (a mechanism of about 300) + one rebuttal (a concession of about 200) + one remedy (a countermeasure of about 200) — balancing is not the eight-legged essay; failing to balance is** (all stake and no reason = angry-youth prose; all reason and no stake = empty policy prose; all remedy and no rebuttal = advertising copy — the shared lesion of the three illnesses: treating the genre as a safe zone)); **three questions for the remedy paragraph** (**who carries it out (a named institution, or "有关部门" — "有关部门" ('the departments concerned') is the largest unclaimed account in Chinese), who pays the cost (from where the budget and the personnel are allocated), how the side effects are monitored ("on whose account is the cost of the four characters "加强监管" ('strengthen supervision') entered: the unfinished towers of current-affairs commentary are concentrated in the remedy paragraph: treating "应当加强" ('it ought to be strengthened') as completion and handover"**)); **the headline = a prospectus** (**the strength the headline promises = the credit line of the whole piece: the headline raises "astonishing", and the body pays out "ordinary" — that is a false statement** ("the headline is the advance cheque of current-affairs commentary: the day it is honoured shows character — and the day of honour is always earlier than the day of publication, earlier than you imagine: a reversal often comes that same evening")).
:::

::: definition The other column of commentary: literary and arts criticism {#def-yiping}
**The division of labour** (**current-affairs commentary handles "what is to be done"; literary and arts criticism handles "is it worth it, and why" — but the two columns share one and the same skeleton: claim, evidence, concession, limit** ("literature and the arts are not a zone exempt from inspection: 'this film made me cry' is the admission ticket; 'what it uses to make me cry, and whether that counts as good' is the assignment" (the writing edition of Lesson 15's lesson on the expression of feeling: feeling must pass the customs of reasoning))); **the special character of the material of evidence** (**the evidence of literary and arts criticism is textual detail: one shot, one rhyme, one silence — to quote is to give evidence** ("empty talk of "演技炸裂" ('the acting explodes') is not as good as identifying three seconds: Lesson 3's 'digging out the mechanism' is called, in a film review, 'pointing out how it managed it'")); **the ethics of spoilers** (**a spoiler is first of all a problem in the contract of the form: arguing "the failure of the narrative structure" may require spoiling through to the end, in which case declare it first** ("hand the choice back to the reader: this is the smallest test of the spirit of contract between critic and reader")); **a score must be reducible** (**a star rating = a claim, and must be reducible to a list of sentences of judgement** ("if the list of reasons for giving seven points is exactly the same as the list for giving nine, the score is only a graph of the heartbeat")).
:::

::: example A canteen circular: a three-part outline and taking apart one another's news pegs {#ex-shitang}
The education bureau of a certain city issues a circular: canteen contractors at a number of schools have been "食材以次充好" ('passing off inferior ingredients as good'); the list is published, the persons in charge are under investigation, and the schools involved are removed from the excellence appraisal. **Task: ① each of you frame it as one of the four issues, and display the stake; ② evaluate one another with the person at your desk: whose words the other person's choice of topic is saving; ③ write a "concession paragraph" — write the strongest rebuttal on behalf of the association of contracting enterprises, in one sentence, and place it in your main text.**
::: solution
① Four sample issues — **the regulation issue** ("评优挂钩是运动式问责，常态化的飞行检查与家委会抽检权才是要害" ('Linking it to the excellence appraisal is campaign-style accountability; what is decisive is regularised flying inspections and a parents' committee's right to sample-test.' (Our translation.)); way of dying: the pilot schools given the right to sample-test do not improve); **the contract issue** ("校园餐的病根在低价中标：评标规则应设成本红线而非最低价" ('The root illness of school meals is that the lowest price wins the bid: the rules for evaluating bids should set a red line for cost, not the lowest price.' (Our translation.)); way of dying: after the red line for cost is raised, the price of the meals is passed on to the parents); **the information issue** ("通报式公开是终点式公开：应建立家长可实时查询的供应链台账" ('Disclosure by circular is endpoint disclosure: a supply-chain ledger that parents can query in real time should be established.' (Our translation.))); **the responsibility issue** ("学校把经营权外包，不能把监护外包：校长陪餐制应从‘吃’升级为‘查’" ('A school may outsource the right to operate, but it cannot outsource guardianship: the system of the principal sharing the meal should be upgraded from "eating" to "checking".' (Our translation.))); **the point tested in the mutual evaluation** (**every issue is saving words for some person who bears responsibility: the regulation issue saves the enterprise a moral narrative, the contract issue saves the regulators the pursuit of responsibility, and the information issue offends neither side but is the hardest to put into effect — only when this layer, the "map of saved words", has been drawn have you understood the politics of choosing a topic** (the newsroom edition of Chapter 6's law of the square)); ② **a model concession paragraph** ("**承包协会或辩：以次充好是个别企业逐利，不应上升为对集中供餐模式的否定——同一供应链标准下，社会餐饮的合格率并不低。此点在案：模式本身不是病灶。**" ('The contractors' association might argue: passing off the inferior as the good is individual enterprises chasing profit, and should not be raised into a denial of the model of centralised meal supply — under the same supply-chain standard, the pass rate of catering for the public is not low. This point is entered on the record: the model itself is not the lesion.' (Our translation.)) — how to place it: after the factual core and before the mechanism paragraph, and come back with "即便如此，校园场景的不可退出性仍然使……" ('Even so, the impossibility of exiting the school setting still makes…' (Our translation.)) (Chapter 6's placing the concession first: let the opponent's trump card be played through once on your ground, and then you take it into your own ranks)).
:::

::: intuition Speed, reversal and delayed judgement {#int-fanzhuan}
**The number-one occupational risk of current-affairs commentary is reversal** (**and most reversals are not overturned by new evidence; they are overturned by a procedure one has oneself omitted: not waiting for the full text of the circular, not asking the other side, not checking the original document — "three hard disciplines against reversal: ① a key fact must have a second, independent source (the working edition of Chapter 8's circular citation); ② when a specific accusation is involved, leave the party accused one line ("截至发稿未获回应" ('No response had been received by the time of going to press.' (Our translation.)): this line is a disclaimer, and also the decency of the news profession); ③ write the "time lag of emotion" into the schedule: the hottest day is often the day when the evidence is thinnest — a judgement delayed by six hours is, on average, of higher quality than a judgement that jumps the start ("a delay is not dullness; it is leaving the judgement until after the evidence has arrived in the account: a current-affairs commentator may admit error, but should not make a profession of admitting error")**). **"The structural reason for rushing to pass sentence"** (**a platform's reward function bills by the repost, and does not bill by rehabilitation** (the current-affairs ledger of Chapter 8's theory of platforms: the way a writer resists the system is not to pretend that they have no KPI, but to write the discrepancy of the KPI into the piece ("putting "本文基于截至某时的通报" ('This piece is based on the circular as of such-and-such a time.' (Our translation.)) at the beginning is to install a withdrawal button for the reader: this is the cheapest honesty available to a commentator in the digital age"))).
:::

::: history Four generations of voices in newspaper opinion {#sec-sida}
**1900s** The birth of the form of newspaper opinion (**the political essays of Liang Qichao 梁启超 in the period of *Qingyi bao* 《清议报》 ('Journal of Pure Discussion') and *Xinmin congbao* 《新民丛报》 ('Journal of Renewing the People') (the common account: that single form of his, "评论时局" ('commenting on the current situation'), swept the world of scholars; the self-description "笔锋常带情感" ('The tip of the brush often carries feeling.' (Our translation.)) comes precisely from this form's consciousness of itself — the first identity of current-affairs commentary in China is "a platform for enlightenment"** (the site of a coup in literary form, countersigned twice, in Lesson 2 and Lesson 1))); **1918–1920s** Journalism textbooks and the establishment of the *sheping* 社评 ('editorial commentary') form (**the common account: around the New Culture Movement, the first Chinese textbooks of journalism assigned newspaper forms such as the editorial, current-affairs commentary and correspondence each to its post and its fixed establishment, and from then on the writing class had a job description for *sheping* — current-affairs commentary turned from talent into a profession by way of textbooks and the staffing of the pages, not by famous pieces**); **1940s** In the great rear area, current-affairs commentary and *zawen* (polemical essay) flowed together (**an after-echo of Lesson 16's "the square takes its post": to publish = to be present = risk — the "reversal risk" of that era was administered on its behalf by the inspection organs; today's is collected on its behalf by traffic**); **1978** The discussion of the criterion of truth (**the common account: one article by a specially invited commentator set off a discussion nationwide — the peak of energy in the history of Chinese current-affairs commentary: it proves an old matter — the weight of speech does not depend on volume, but on whether it has hit the sentence the system urgently needs** (an ideal-type specimen of current-affairs commentary, and also the gentle trap for everyone whose talent goes unrecognised: the words an age needs are not there every day; most manuscripts only get the ground hoed)); **1980s–2000s** The golden age of current-affairs commentary, and its becoming a profession (**the common account: metropolitan newspapers expanded their pages, and the column system and the profession of "current-affairs commentator" appeared ("once writing had a payslip, it also had the newsroom's stop-loss line: the byline system wrote the risk into the identity card"**)); **2010s–the present** Platformisation (**"commentary" on public accounts runs in parallel with the commentary end of official new media: the cycle of publication shrinks from the day to the hour, and the "overnight tin" becomes the "tin by the hour" — the boundary of the profession is rewritten once more: today's current-affairs commentator is half an author and half their own fact-checker (the working edition of Chapter 8's everyone-an-editor**).
:::

::: warning Four pits of current-affairs commentary
**Taking anger for insight** (**the concentration of emotion and the depth of mechanism are often inversely proportional: the hottest matter is the hardest to write — because every angle has been occupied in the first six hours: a good strategy is "to do the part of the accounts that others have left out"** (others attack character; you examine the contract; others examine the contract; you ask who supervises the supervisors)); **circular commentary** (**"要完善制度" ('the system must be improved'), "要加强监管" ('supervision must be strengthened'), "要落实责任" ('responsibility must be carried out') — the three "musts" are an all-purpose tonic and also zero calories: institutional criticism in current-affairs commentary must be specific as far as "which article, who carries it out, how it is reconsidered", or else it is only scratching an itch for the system** (the editorial edition of Lesson 2's "attach-importance" style)); **a byline beyond its authority** (**using the voice of "本报评论员" ('commentator of this paper') to write one's private satisfaction (the institution's credit has been put through on your card), or using the anonymity of "网友说" ('a netizen says') to write an accusation at the grade of a legal person (you have escaped the responsibility you ought to bear): the voice and the bearing of responsibility must be of the same weight**); **a moral headcount** (**using "哪个中国人都看不下去" ('No Chinese person could bear to go on looking.' (Our translation.)) in place of evidence: a kidnapping "we", once it is in the newspaper, is the statutory form of that** (the front-page edition of Lesson 7's hostage clause: unbinding takes only one step — write out that nameable list of those affected)).
:::

::: exercise Practice in issue-framing {level=1}
For one and the same news item (choose for yourself a hot topic of this week), write sentences of claim for four different issues (one each for regulation / contract / information / responsibility), and give each a line on "whose words this choice of topic saves"; then choose one and write it up as an outline of 200 characters (stake — concession — reason — remedy).
::: solution
Marking: **the four sentences must not be paraphrases of one another** (**changing the label without changing the dimension = one issue wearing four waistcoats**); **the "map of saved words" must name names** (**whose words were saved: the person being attacked, the regulator, or the reader's own action**); the outline must contain all four pieces, and the "remedy" must pass the three questions (who carries it out / the cost / the side effects).
:::
:::

::: exercise Making a film review into an argument {level=2}
Choose a recent film and write a short review of 250 characters. Requirements: ① one debatable point; ② three textual details as evidence (one each of shot / line of dialogue / edit); ③ one sentence of mechanism explaining "what it uses to move me"; ④ a declaration of spoilers.
::: solution
Marking: **the details must be able to be checked back** (**a point in time, or a location of the scene — "有一幕" ('there is a scene') is not the giving of evidence; it is the ribbon-cutting of a memory**); **the mechanism sentence may not use pure words of feeling of the "演技炸裂" ('the acting explodes') class** (**identify how the technique manipulates your attention and your expectation: the screen edition of Lesson 3's "digging out the mechanism"**); **if a star rating is given, it must be reducible to three sentences of judgement** (the scoring edition of the clause that a quotable sentence must be reducible).
:::

::: exercise A short essay: the weight of a byline {level=3}
Write between six hundred and a thousand characters, judging the claim that "**the whole dignity of current-affairs commentary is written in those two characters of the byline: anonymity lets a person dare to speak, and a byline makes a person responsible — and the whole technique of the profession of commentary is to let one person both dare to speak and be responsible: this is a virtue more precise than 'courage'; it is called proceduralised courage**". You must call on at least six items: **the two contracts**, **issue-framing and the map of saved words**, **the legal person's voice**, **the balancing method**, **the three questions for the remedy**, **the three disciplines against reversal**; and you must adjudicate: **on algorithmic platforms everyone has a commentary page — will the profession of "current-affairs commentator" still exist? (You must give the irreplaceable core of that profession, or honestly declare the form of its transformation.)**
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**contract = the qualification to practise; issue-framing = a map of the politics; voice = responsibility accounting; balancing = an engineering code; the three questions = supervision of implementation; the three disciplines = a safety redundancy**); the adjudication of the profession (**for reference: the argument from the core — what is irreplaceable in the profession lies neither in "speed" (a machine is faster) nor in "daring" (anonymity dares more), but in "being willing to pledge one's own history as guarantee for this one piece": the byline is the carrier of the possibility of recourse; the argument from transformation — the profession is merged into "checking plus the curation of argument": from writer to auditor (in marking the paper, see whether anyone admits that "the incentive to dare to speak is falling, and the technique of taking responsibility is rising in value" — an answer that makes the trade hurt a little more is the more credible**); if the conclusion can write "**the weight of a byline is, in its essence, a commitment across time: whether one is willing to let tomorrow, next month, and an opponent's screenshot look at today's version — only a sentence that dares to be re-examined in the future is a sentence that now deserves to be read**", that is an A.

Filed on entering the city: the contract (**timeliness and the public: translate "I" into a "we" that can be named; the power of commentary is to press questions, not to close the case**), the three steps (**news peg — issue-framing — claim: the bias of choosing a topic is complete at the moment of choosing**), the genealogy (**five faces; the voice and the questions borne are of the same weight**), the balancing (**stake, reason, rebuttal, remedy: the three "musts" are a zero-calorie tonic**), the arts column (**feeling passes customs, details can be checked back, a score can be reduced**), the risk (**the three disciplines against reversal: proceduralised courage**). The method in one sentence: **first ask what this contract permits me to say, and then say what others dare not say**.
