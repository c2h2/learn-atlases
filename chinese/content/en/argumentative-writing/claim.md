---
title: "The claim"
---

The previous chapter drove in the pile; this chapter works that pile further — **a claim is not "what I want to say", but "what I dare let people check": its type, its syntax, its strength and its bumper can all pass an acceptance check before you set pen to paper**.

## Definition: four types of claim {#def-sixing}

::: definition What it is / what it counts as / whether it is good / what to do {#def-sitype}
**① A factual claim** ("**after-school services lower the pickup-and-drop-off costs of dual-income families" — checkable; death condition: the scope and definition of the data**); **② a definitional claim** ("**order-brushing (刷单) ought to count as fraud, not as marketing" — what is in dispute is classification and the status of the name; death condition: legislative purpose, and precedents of the same kind (the writing scene of Chapter 2, "the power to define is the power to allocate": the whole work of a definitional claim = explaining why the old pigeonhole cannot hold the new facts**)); **③ a value claim** ("**a rotten film should be eliminated by the market, not rescued by censorship" — what is in dispute is the standard of evaluation; death condition: the ordering of values is swapped ("you talk of efficiency, he talks of fairness: it is not that someone won; the scoring sheet was changed**)); **④ a policy claim** ("**short-video platforms should, for minors, turn off automatic continued play by default" — it contains an agent who carries it out, and a means; death condition: feasibility and side effects ("after 'ought' there must be a person, money and consequences: if any one of the three is missing, the claim is only a wish**). **The test for a mismatched type (the pathology of missing the question)**: **a policy prompt written as an essay of values = answering "what is to be done" with "it is very important" (the name on the case notes of the fourth pit in the previous chapter); a definition prompt written as a report of facts = supposing that once you have checked the facts clear you have won, when the other side has never denied the facts and has all along been seizing the right to name ("in the debate 'is esports a sport?', the competition side never denies that the players train hard — what they want is the household registration of the word 'sport'"); a value prompt written as a list of facts = "I set the data out and won until numb", yet one sentence from the other side, "I grant all the data; I do not grant the standard", zeroes the whole hall — each of the four kinds of claim is matched with its own evidence; match the wrong type, and being entirely right is still labour for nothing**.
:::

## Syntax and strength {#def-jufa}

::: definition The one-sentence principle and the verb as gavel {#def-yijuhua}
**The syntactic acceptance check**: **one sentence, one gavel-verb (ought / must / may / is worth / is inadvisable), and one "because" that can hold the whole essay** ("**if you can write down 'this essay holds that X, because A, B and C', the claim exists; if you cannot write A, B and C, what exists is only emotion (an outline is a cheque you draw on yourself: a bad cheque cannot cheat anyone else; it cheats you, and you alone**)). **Three grades of strength (the writing version of the inverse law from the logic course)** (**a strong proposition ("must be prohibited by legislation") needs a chain of evidence that can bear enforcement over the whole country; a medium proposition ("should be piloted in scenario X") needs only one credible design for a pilot; a weak proposition ("worth discussing") is safe, but close to zero contribution — the discipline of choosing a grade: leave the nerve of a strong proposition to the policy sentence, and write the line of retreat of a weak proposition into the qualifiers; do not reverse them (a common novice's ailment: the claim is "prohibit everything", while the evidence only suffices for "a pilot of one term": an overdraft of strength = an asset bubble of the whole essay**). **Qualifiers = the claim's bumper** ("**under condition X / for group Y / within three years" — a limit is not diffidence; it is fitting the claim with a crumple zone: contrarians specialise in the hero who has set no limits ("'homework ought to be reduced' is a hero; 'for students in 初二 and above whose median time on homework exceeds 90 minutes, written homework should have a cap on the total' is an armoured vehicle fitted with a bumper: to refute it, one must first find the patch that was left unlimited, and you have left no such patch**)).
:::

::: definition Three questions of defensibility {#def-sanwen}
**① Is there an opposing side?** (**you can at once say "who objects, and why they object": if you cannot, send it back to the furnace (the list of objectors is the claim's birth certificate: the qualification review, in Lesson 1, of "reserving a seat"**)); **② Can it be broken down?** (**it can be split into two or three sub-claims, each able to pass an acceptance check on its own — if it will not come apart, that usually means only one word inside is giving off heat ("once 'technology changes education' has been split into three sentences, each of which holds, you will find that not one of them needs arguing: what is hot is the word, not the reason**)); **③ Does it have a death condition?** (**you can write one sentence, "if it is discovered that …, this claim is thereupon void" — a claim for which you cannot write a death condition is not sturdy; it has never taken the field at all (the writing-class version of Popper: a falsifiability clause is the claim's certificate of insurance**). **When all three questions are passed, the claim is fixed in its form; thereafter the sole duty of the whole essay is to arrange the answers to these three questions into an order the reader is willing to walk (Lesson 4: structure is the order of the walking**.
:::

::: example A workshop for processing the prompt {#ex-jiagong}
Five real prompts, or simulated ones, each worked into **a claim that carries a limit and a death condition**, and labelled with the type of claim: ① “垃圾是放错位置的资源” — "Rubbish is a resource put in the wrong place." (Our translation.) (Shanghai, 2019); ② “俗话说：‘车有车道，马有马道’，数字时代，人有人的道” — "As the saying goes: 'A cart has a cart-road, a horse has a horse-road'; in the digital age, people have a people's road." (Our translation.) (Tianjin, 2023); ③ “假如云看不见自己变化的形状／当你如飞鸟一样俯瞰这片土地／你会明白带宽有限，人生无限” — "Suppose a cloud cannot see the shape of its own changing / when you look down on this land as a bird in flight does / you will understand that bandwidth is limited, and life is unlimited." (Our translation.) (Tianjin, 2020); ④ “好故事会发生在身边／好故事就在我们身边” — "A good story will happen close at hand / a good story is right beside us." (Our translation.) (Shanghai, 2023); ⑤ “他好读书，不好文学” — "He is fond of reading, and not fond of literature." (Our translation.) (an imitation: a comment-style prompt reworked from a remark on a certain student who "only grinds hard problems and does no generalising").
::: solution
① **Definitional type turned into a policy type** (**"'resource' is a name for a capacity to process: only when the marginal cost of recycling is lower than extraction does rubbish deserve the name of resource — therefore municipal subsidies for sorting should go first to 'categories that can turn a profit'"; limit: under the condition "cost lower than extraction"; death condition: the comprehensive energy use of the sorting group in the pilot cities is higher than that of mixed incineration ("translate the lyrical sentence into a sentence that divides the accounts: the prompt gives you sentiment; you give the prompt a balance sheet**)); ② **value + policy** (**"the 'road' of the digital age should be allocated by rules of right of way, not by the customary practice of platforms: a platform's default settings ought to be regulated as 'occupying the road'"; death condition: the cost of regulation is higher than the losses from accidents ("the relevant properties of the cart-road analogy are 'exclusion and responsibility', not 'fast and slow' — a metaphor's post inside a claim is load-bearing tester, not chairman of the board**)); ③ **a value claim + a limit** (**"for students already connected to the network, the risk in 'unlimited' is not an overload of knowledge but the bankruptcy of attention: the school-based curriculum should add explicit training in 'the use of bandwidth'"; death condition: after the intervention, the attention measures do not improve ("the prompt says life is unlimited; your claim dares to say 'which kind of unlimited, for whom, and at what cost': poetry is written towards the wide; a claim is nailed towards the narrow**)); ④ **a trap question mixing fact and definition** (**"'happens close at hand' is a place-of-origin bias in the economy of narrative: the standard of a good story should be that it has rewritten the reader's table of causes, not distance — therefore selections for the Chinese course should raise the quota of 'texts from far away'"; death condition: the control class shows no difference on the empathy measure after reading texts from far away ("take care: this question's pull is to treat 'close at hand' as a virtue by default — only when a claim dares to examine the prompt does it count as a negotiation between equals**)); ⑤ **a rewriting in the style of a comment (policy)** (**"'only grinding hard problems' is a mismatch of the feedback cycle: a hard problem carries a large amount of information on the single item, but a low rate of information per unit of time — a 'generalising period' should be set, with a compulsory share of 30%"; death condition: after the generalising period is added, the post-test does not differ from the problem-grinding group ("rewrite the criticism as a mechanism, then rewrite the mechanism as a prescription that can be tried: argument in the style of a comment slips most easily from a qualitative judgement to a prescription; this chapter teaches you to take this road on purpose**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The claim is a client {#int-jiafang}
The claim is the client (甲方, the party that commissions) for the whole essay: material, structure and language are all its contractor — if the client has been specified wrongly, the harder the work, the more awkward it becomes ("a store of materials cannot save a wrong claim: it will only decorate an unsafe building until it looks more like a show flat). Three steps in raising the client (the acceptance sheet before you write) (narrow it (treat a large topic in a small way: "on moral backbone" is not as good as "the exchange rate of moral backbone in a time of peace" — the narrower the edge of the knife, the deeper the cut (the "road" of Question 2 takes only the one sense "right of way": one word, one sense, is the claim's permit to start work**)**); **change places (stand your own claim in the opposing camp and read it through once: a knowing smile, or sitting on a mat of needles? Sitting on a mat of needles is the good news)**; **budget (estimate the cost in evidence of each sub-claim: A needs annual reports looked up, B needs only one experiment, C needs interviews — a claim for which you cannot draw up a table of costs will be overturned, at two in the morning, by you yourself**). **The claim's sense of right and wrong** (**do not use rhetoric to raise the strength: writing "worth a pilot" as "must legislate", so as to swell the noise, is to stake the good faith of the whole class on your own nerve — the argumentative essay's earliest education in character: do not exaggerate your own proposition, just as you do not tamper with someone else's position**.
:::

::: history Several changes of generation in the view of the claim {#sec-huandai}
**Pre-Qin**: establishing one's words and establishing a principle (**the *Yizhuan* (the commentaries on the *Changes*): “修辞立其诚” — "In cultivating one's words, establish one's sincerity." (Our translation.) — the ancestral sentence of the ethics of the claim: sincerity first, wording after; the Mohists' “以说出故” — "By explanation, bring out the ground." (Our translation.) — a claim must have a ground it can rely on (an official visitor from Chapter 10 of the logic course**)); **Han**: "argument" (*lun* 论) becomes a genre (**Wang Chong 王充, in the author's preface to *Lunheng* (*Discourses Weighed in the Balance*), states its guiding case: “疾虚妄” — "Detest the baseless and the false." (Our translation.) — it set the house rule of the trade, that "a claim must be arguable and checkable"; the "words of one school", in the manner of *Guo Qin lun* ('On the Faults of Qin'), began to carry a consciousness of copyright**); **Southern Dynasties**: the "Lunshuo" chapter of *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons* (**“圣哲彝训曰经，述经叙理曰论” — "The constant teachings of sages and the wise are called classics; to set forth the classics and give an ordered account of principles is called *lun*." (Our translation.) — the page of the register on which "argument" (*lun*) is entered in an independent household, out of being a dependant of the classics; “义贵圆通，辞忌枝碎” — "In the sense, what is prized is being rounded and unobstructed; in the wording, what is to be shunned is being twiggy and fragmentary." (Our translation.) — the parallel-prose version of the standard for an acceptance check on a claim**); **Song**: forerunners of the argumentative essay and of a new style (**Wang Anshi 王安石, “务为有适用而作” — "Make it one's business to write what is of practical use." (Our translation.) — the claim comes on with an engineering demand in hand; Su Shi's 苏轼 essays that reverse a settled case made "taking a cramped path" into a method: the age in which a claim = a consciousness of one's angle**); **Qing to the modern period**: statecraft prose and the newspapers (**Wei Yuan's 魏源 *Haiguo tuzhi* (*Illustrated Treatise on the Maritime Kingdoms*) and the tradition of statecraft writing (the common account: the claim begins to answer for "effect" — the argument of 师夷, "learn from the foreigners", first asks "does it work?", and only then "is it respectable?"; Liang Qichao's 梁启超 new style — the claim learns to be serialised in the magazines: the claim begins to answer for "circulation": two pressures that, to this day, hold a meeting in the blood vessels of examination-hall essays**); **after 1917**: the study of genres enters the curriculum (**national-language textbooks (*guowen*) set up the subject "the argumentative essay" (the four-part division, set beside narration, exposition and practical writing, is still in use: this is the largest act of genre legislation in the history of the teaching of writing in China — the four-part division is both a map and a set of rails: generation after generation has driven "the point that can be argued" onto the track of "argument"**); **the present** (**the Toulmin form "claim + qualifier + rebuttal" enters writing textbooks; the "depth" clause in the *gaokao* marking criteria (going through the phenomenon into the essence; the view having a power to enlighten) — the state examination, by its marking rules, issues a yearly version number for "the view of the claim" (Lesson 10 reads this contract closely**).
:::

::: warning Four pits of the claim
**The "attach importance" claim** ("**we ought to attach importance to reading" — "attach importance" has no subject, no budget and no death condition: a sentence with the three absences, and dead, does not deserve to be the client (rewrite: who, spending what, in exchange for what**)); **smuggling by definition** (**"real reading is intensive reading" — using a definition to smuggle a conclusion through (the writing flare-up of the immunisation in Chapter 2: once "the real X" has taken up residence in your claim, please produce that definition's tenancy agreement**)); **one topic, many husbands** (**the first half argues "we must go slowly", the second half argues "we must go fast": two clients rent one building between them, and the construction crew can only build the building into an S (how to find it: check how many sentences the "because" can hold — whichever will not fit are all ex-husbands**)); **vanity of strength** (**adding weight so as to seem deep: "should" is promoted to "must", "must" is promoted to "only" — depth is not in the pitch of the voice, but in cutting open the mechanism (the "depth" in the marking criteria means the layer of mechanism, not the layer of volume: an essay that lowers "must" back to "worth a pilot", with the density of evidence unchanged, often sees its mark rise rather than fall**.
:::

::: exercise The three-question acceptance check {level=1}
Put the **three questions of defensibility** (a list of the opposing side / a breakdown table / a death-condition clause) to five claims you have made up yourself, and rewrite on the spot any that fail: ① "Reading makes a person progress"; ② "The school canteen ought to taste better"; ③ "Online games cause short-sightedness in adolescents"; ④ "The truly strong are all lonely"; ⑤ "A class log is worth trying for three months".
::: solution
Marking: **each of the three questions is given one answer that can be checked** (**"progress" must be brought down onto an indicator; "taste better" must define whose mouth, and whose budget pays; "causes short-sightedness" and "is correlated" must be separated in the accounts, plus confounders (time spent outdoors); "the truly strong" = caught in the act of smuggling by definition; ⑤, the policy type, is the healthiest, and must still have a death condition written ("if after three months there is no change in the transparency of class business, withdraw it"**); the pass line for a rewrite = **an opponent would appear on the list, and would recognise themselves**.
:::
:::

::: exercise Shifting the grade of strength {level=2}
Write the same kernel shifted through three grades (one sentence each: strong / medium / weak), and match each grade with **the minimum evidence that fits it**: the topic **"mobile phones and marks"**. Then judge which grade should be used, respectively, for the school newspaper, the parents' meeting, and a suggestion to the education bureau.
::: solution
Marking: **the three grades are clearly distinct** (strong, "should be banned from being brought in at all" = needs a controlled experiment across the whole school + the cost of enforcement; medium, "recommend that classes collect them for storage in one way" = needs data from one pilot class + the rate of disputes; weak, "worth discussing" = needs only an account of how the controversy now stands**); matching the grade to the occasion** (**the school newspaper may use the medium grade, and leave room for discussion; the parents' meeting, the medium grade + a clause for managing emotion; a letter of suggestion should be medium on the high side, but must attach feasibility and a budget — the hearing-level of the occasion decides the upper limit of the proposition's strength: to speak at the wrong grade is, in the world of argument, a petition that skips the proper level of authority**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the death condition of a claim {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**a claim that has not had its death condition written is a kidnapping of the reader: it outsources 'I might be wrong' for someone else to discover — a good author claims this debt in advance**". You must call on at least six of the following: **the four types of claim and the test for a mismatched type**, **the one-sentence principle**, **the three grades of strength and the matching of grade to occasion**, **the limit as bumper**, **the three-question acceptance check**, **the two pits, "attach importance" and "smuggling by definition"**; and give a ruling: **an examination-hall essay has only 90 minutes — should one give up luxuries of the "death-condition clause" sort? Give your specification of a "minimum runnable claim for the examination hall"**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**matching of type, syntax, shifting the grade, the insurance, the acceptance check, the pits**); the ruling (**for reference: within 90 minutes the death-condition clause is not a luxury but a saving — it cuts away, on your behalf, two sub-claims you cannot finish writing ("a claim that has a death condition brings its own budget of length"); the minimum specification for the examination hall: one sentence + one limit + one opposing side + one line of death condition (about 60 characters), and all the rest is construction (in marking, see whether anyone has argued "giving up the death condition" into being the rational course (for example, "it does not pay to trade length for a confidence level; use a concessive sentence instead": that also counts as holding, but one must admit that this is a downgrade, not a free pass**); if the conclusion can write "**writing a death condition is not a curse that one's own house will collapse; it is a declaration: this house can stand being checked, and can afford to pay — all the credit of a claim comes from that soft spot the author dares to leave**", it is an A.
:::

::: summary
The processing sheet, filed: the four types (**what it is / what it counts as / whether it is good / what to do: match the wrong type, and being entirely right is still labour for nothing**), syntax (**one verb, one "because"; if you cannot write A, B and C, do not set pen to paper**), strength (**the occasion decides the upper limit: do not put nerve in place of evidence**), the bumper (**a limit is a crumple zone, not a cloth to cover shame**), the three questions (**opposing side, breakdown, death condition**), the theory of the client (**set the claim wrong, and the more diligently you decorate, the more awkward it is**). The method in one sentence: **first let the claim lose once, then let it take up its post**. The next chapter checks the books: the kinds of evidence, and honesty.
:::
