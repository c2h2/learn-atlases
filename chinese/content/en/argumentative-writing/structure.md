title: "Structure"
---

The claim is fixed, the evidence is prepared, and the circuit has been chosen; this chapter lays the wiring — **structure is not the study of placement whose aim is to make the words sound fine; it is the road the author paves for the reader: only when the road is level will anyone be willing to walk up to the doorway of your conclusion**.

## Definition: four layouts {#def-banshi}

::: definition Parallel, progressive, contrastive, refutation {#def-sizhong}
**The parallel layout** (**three sub-claims stand in one row, on the same level and in the same dimension — the discipline of the parallel: the sub-claims must answer one and the same question ("'individual, society, state' is a drawer that holds anything, not an analysis: any topic can be stuffed into it, which shows that it has said nothing; then check 'mutual exclusion' and 'exhaustiveness': two sub-claims permeating each other = the cut is not clean; when the three have been said and a fourth level is still left = the cut is not complete"**)); **the progressive layout** (**what it is — why — what to do, or phenomenon — mechanism — countermeasure — the check-up for progression: delete the third paragraph; has the second already finished what there is to say? If it has, the progression is fake (real progression = the later paragraph takes the earlier as its premise and does not repeat that paragraph's accounts: like a staircase, not three copies of the same picture**)); **the contrastive layout** (**two cities, one for and one against: two pictures on the same coordinates ("勤则治，怠则废" — 'Where there is diligence, there is order; where there is idleness, there is ruin.' (Our translation.) — the hidden pit of contrast: if the two cities are not framed in the same dimension (the eastern city talks of results, the western city of health), the reader discovers no comparison, and discovers only that the essay has left its topic**)); **the refutation layout** (**first break, then build: set up the target — dismantle what is load-bearing — clear the site — raise a new building (the full set of procedures is Lesson 6; this chapter remembers one line first: the greatest risk of the refutation layout is "only breaking, not building" — you finish dismantling a dangerous building and hand over a ruin**)). **The method of choosing a layout** (**look at the shape of the claim: a policy claim leans progressive (present state — causes — plan — risks); a dispute over values leans contrastive; a topic on which the opponent is strong leans to refutation; the parallel layout saves the most effort and is also the most mediocre — "using the parallel is not the mistake; writing the parallel as three paraphrases that mean the same thing is the mistake"**).
:::

::: definition Three things at the opening, three prohibitions at the close {#def-touwei}
**Three things the opening does** (**① show the stake (the fourth kind of opening in Lesson 1); ② offer a seat (the opponent takes a seat — "an essay with no opponent in the first paragraph begins to lie in the second"); ③ announce the route ("the whole piece takes two steps: first explain why the ban does not work, then explain what kind of rule does work" — a route map is not a preview of the contents list; it is a budget you commit yourself to: announcing a route and not walking it is a breach of contract**)); **The share of length given to the opening** (**in an essay of a thousand characters, the opening is capped at one hundred and twenty characters: patience in the examination hall is as limited as the reader's bladder ("an essay that sets out from the dictionary mostly dies on the way to the toilet"**)). **Three prohibitions at the close** (**① the slogan returning (shouting the opening sentence louder is not called winding up; it is called tinnitus**)); **② elevation that takes a hostage ("let us join hands and walk into a new era" — the exchange rate between elevation and the claim is zero: you cannot fly from a concrete proposal to humankind unless you change engines three times on the way**); **③ the stories linking up (the same story at head and tail = the ceremonial version of offering a single case to a god (Lesson 3) — the correct use of a story at the close: change to a variation of the story, let the new story and the claim be checked against each other, and the reader, in a familiar mouthfeel, eats a new account**); **What the close ought to do** (**the cinch (fold the sub-claims back into the main claim within one sentence — "the complexity that one sentence can hold is the only complexity you have really mastered") + limits (the ways of dying in Chapter 3 are cashed in here: this essay applies to …, and does not apply to …: an honest close is the most expensive advertisement in the whole essay) + the next step (give the reader an action they can afford: the sign-off of an argument is not an exclamation mark; it is a work order**)).
:::

## The paragraph: the basic engineering unit {#def-duanluo}

::: definition The four-piece set and the signpost piece {#def-sijian}
**One paragraph that advances a claim = point + evidence + analysis + summing-up** (**the point sentence is shown at the head of the paragraph (the route along which an examination script is marked is a vertical sweep: the sentence at the head of the paragraph is your job advertisement**); **evidence comes in according to the nature of the material (Lesson 3); the analysis piece is where the profit of the whole paragraph lies: it states why the evidence supports the point, what alternative explanations it has ruled out, and to what degree ("the three lines after 'the data show that X is rising' — 'this shows …, but it may also be that …, therefore within the present claim …' — are the heart of the essay"**); **the summing-up sentence writes back to the claim (writing back is not repetition; it is settling the account: 'thus the ban has got behaviour under control, and cannot get motive under control' — settle it in one sentence, and only then can the next paragraph open a new account**) — **a paragraph missing the analysis piece is bricks packed into a load-bearing wall: the outside looks the same, and at inspection the light shows through**). **A transition sentence = a signpost and also an amending clause** ("**what was discussed above is efficiency; what follows is fairness: in this question the two are not the same thing**" — two functions: to shut the door on the previous section, and to open the door on this one; of complaints about the structural disease "a leap in the line of thought", eight tenths are caused by a missing signpost (a signpost sentence costs twelve characters; the damages are a whole paragraph of misunderstanding: the king of value for money in the whole essay)). **The discipline of paragraph length** (**for an argumentative paragraph, one hundred to two hundred characters is the norm; above two hundred and fifty, first suspect that you have sewn two paragraphs into one; below fifty, it is usually one example plus one exclamation**).
:::

::: definition Two grades of craft in the outline {#def-tigang}
**Word outline versus sentence outline** (**a word outline is a prompt for memory ("opening: mobile phones; paragraph two: a British study …"); only a sentence outline is a drawing for the logic (the sentence for paragraph two: "a sample of schools that volunteered for the trial makes it impossible to separate 'improved results' from 'the school's willingness'" — on the drawing you can see what is load-bearing; on the prompt slip you can see only the words**)); **Three lines for accepting a sentence outline** (**one complete sentence of judgement to each paragraph; each sentence marked with its function (establish / prove / refute / close); between two neighbouring sentences a connective can be written (do you write "therefore" or "on the contrary"? If you cannot write one = the two paragraphs do not know each other**)); **The order of writing ≠ the order of acceptance** (**you may first write the paragraph that is best stocked (usually the other side's strongest argument, or the mechanism paragraph), but before delivery you must read the whole through once more in the reader's order — "which paragraph you write first is the author's right; how they are arranged is the reader's contract: the price of a breach is that nobody knows your second paragraph is paying the first paragraph's debt"**).
:::

::: example Structural CT: a consultation on five diseases {#ex-ct}
A synopsis of a student exercise, 《劳动教育必修》 ('Labour Education as a Compulsory Course'), runs as follows. Please carry out a **structural CT**: identify the diseased sentence of each of the five diseases, and open one operation sheet for rearrangement for each — “**第一段：劳动是人类文明的源泉（160 字抒情排比）。第二段：高尔基热爱劳动。第三段：鲁班热爱劳动。第四段：李大钊赞劳动。第五段：可是现在很多同学不爱劳动（‘有的’列举四个‘有的’，170 字）。第六段：因此我们要重视劳动教育。第七段：让青春在劳动中闪光！**” ('**Paragraph 1: labour is the source of human civilisation (160 characters of lyrical parallelism). Paragraph 2: Gorky loved labour. Paragraph 3: Lu Ban 鲁班 loved labour. Paragraph 4: Li Dazhao 李大钊 praised labour. Paragraph 5: but now many classmates do not love labour (four instances of 有的, "some", in a list, 170 characters). Paragraph 6: therefore we must attach importance to labour education. Paragraph 7: let youth shine in labour!**' (Our translation.)) The whole text is 720 characters; 重视 ('attach importance') appears five times, and 因为 ('because') zero times.
::: solution
A consultation on the five diseases — **① top-heavy** (**paragraph 1, 160 characters of lyrical parallelism: the opening is doing the job of a literary essay, and has neither shown the stake nor offered a seat: operation sheet — compress it to within 90 characters, keep one sentence of occasion, "why talk about labour at this moment", and supply a stake that can be argued (should labour education be counted in the credits for compulsory courses, or should it only be advocacy?**)); **② the example cabinet (repetition on a flat plane)** (**paragraphs 2, 3 and 4 repeat one and the same function three times (examples in praise of labour): three copies are not three sub-claims: operation sheet — merge them into one paragraph of examples, and spend the characters saved on supplying the analysis piece (what is the mechanism of labour education: skill? character? an attitude towards manual workers? — three schemes, and the policies differ: one sentence making clear which one this essay takes**)); **③ a "listing" with no "attribution of cause"** (**the four 有的 in paragraph 5 = an inventory of phenomena: why they do not love labour (the weight given in assessment? parents doing it instead? the timetable squeezing it out?) — not one of these is answered — the level of "why" is absent as a whole (the progressive layout breaks off at the second step**)); **④ a fake conclusion** (**paragraph 6, "therefore we must 重视": a jump from "the phenomenon exists" to "one ought to 重视", with the causal chain and the detail of policy in between entirely omitted (the same pattern as lesion ④ in the account-audit case of Lesson 3: a claim of the 重视 type returns from the dead: operation sheet — replace it with a concrete prescription + the party who is responsible + the cost (under which item of the comprehensive quality assessment it is entered, who records it, and how falsification is prevented**))); **⑤ a close in which the slogan returns** (**paragraph 7, an elevation whose exchange rate is zero: operation sheet — change it for "limits + the next step" (this essay argues only the design, against cheating, of the 'manner of recording', and does not deal with the dispute over values as to 'whether a labour course ought to be established'; the suggestion is that this school first pilot, for two semesters, a 'dossier of the labour process' — one paragraph that drags the whole text back from a speech to an engineering job**)); **Overall inspection** (**in the whole text, 重视 five times and 因为 zero times: this is a publicity vehicle with no engine — the suggested skeleton after rearrangement: occasion (40 characters) — stake (60 characters, including a concession) — mechanism paragraph (the path by which labour education changes attitudes, and the evidence) — the other side's strongest argument (an added burden; falsification) and the handling of it — prescription paragraph (the design of the manner of recording) — limits and the next step: the number of characters unchanged, the functions doubled**).
:::

::: intuition Structure is the economics of attention {#int-zhuyili}
**The reader forgives only three times** (**the first roadblock: it takes two readings to understand; the second: the reader begins to doubt how many layers you have; the third: the reader decides to look only at the conclusion — the essence of structure is to outsource the rate of misunderstanding to your own labour (lay ten more signpost sentences yourself, and the reader beats the retreat three times fewer**)). **The traditional resources of the Chinese paragraph** (**opening, continuation, turn and close (起承转合) are in fact an "attention scheduler": opening = a promise, continuation = performing the contract, turn = framing anew (a turn is not a change of topic; it is the same account entered under another heading: the mild version of the right of definition in Chapter 2), close = settling the account — the 过接 ('joining transition') and the 束股 ('binding leg') of the eight-legged essay (八股) are the strictest training in transition sentences in all history (the signpost is made a compulsory piece of the genre: seen from today, they are shackles; seen from then, they were newbie protection in the starter village**)). **How to read 文无定法 ('writing has no fixed method')** (**this sentence, which is correct, is often used as a way to avoid learning layouts: a layout is gravity, not shackles — first walk as the gravity goes; only when the walk is familiar do you know where you may jump, and whether the jump will make you fall (the structural version of "a set form = points left unset, and made public" in Lesson 15: the rule comes first, and only stepping outside it is style; stepping outside where there is no rule is called losing the way**)).
:::

::: history The ledger of sectional method {#sec-zhangfa}
**The Southern Dynasties.** *The Literary Mind and the Carving of Dragons*, the chapter ‘Paragraph and Sentence’ (《文心雕龙·章句》; “章句在于情理，而述作不失其统” — ‘Sections and sentences lie in feeling and reason, and composition does not lose its thread.’ (Our translation.) — a commonly received adaptation) + the chapter ‘Fuhui’ (《附会》; “总一统理，首尾一体” — ‘Draw all under one order of principle, so that head and tail are one body.’ (Our translation.) (the general sense)) — for the first time the study of the essay lays down a law for "unity" (**Liu Xie 刘勰's definition of structure: the art of total order — not the arranging of seats, but the building of a railway**); **From the Song and the Yuan to the Ming and the Qing.** The study of sections and sentences (**Zhu Xi 朱熹 taught people, in reading, to seek first 文势 ('the momentum of the writing'); Lü Zuqian 吕祖谦, in Guwen guanjian (《古文关键》, 'The Key to Ancient-Style Prose'), opened a course of 评点 ('pointed commentary') that "looks at where a piece begins, where it turns, and where it ends" — for the first time structure becomes knowledge that can be taught (the way a genius proceeds, written out as navigation instructions**)); **The Ming and the Qing.** The eight-legged essay (**breaking the title — receiving the title — opening the exposition — entering the matter — the opening legs — the middle legs — the later legs — the binding legs — "opening, continuation, turn and close" institutionalised down to the level at which paragraphs are numbered: the efficiency is astonishing (anyone, with half a year's training, can write a finished piece), and the cost is equally astonishing (the point that can be argued gives way to speaking in the sages' stead: "the taming of the claim" in Lesson 2 — a famous case of the craft of structure and freedom of thought as sworn enemies**)); **The 1920s–30s.** The modern study of the essay enters the curriculum (**“言之有物、言之有序” — 'Have substance in what you say, and order in what you say.' (Our translation.) — becomes the slogan of the teaching of writing; the movement "grammar and logic into the classroom" (the sequel to the genre coup of Lesson 16: structure begins to be taught as a problem of logic rather than a problem of qìyùn 气韵 ('breath and resonance')**)); **The 1980s to the present.** The genre of the examination hall (**a tug of war between "the syllogism-style structure for examinations" and the "anti-template" movement; after 2014, task-driven questions hitch the points that score on from "complete" to "completing the specific task" — the conductor's baton of the marking criteria is the real textbook of the study of structure (Lesson 10 reads this contract closely**)).
:::

::: warning Four pits of structure
**The illusion of a contents list** (**taking "this essay falls into three parts" for a route map: a route map must write the claims ("first show that X does not work, then argue that Y does"), not the numbers of a contents list**); **Dependence on subheadings** (**using four subheadings to cover four short paragraphs that will not join — a subheading is the sugar-coating of a contents list: take the subheadings away, and do the paragraphs still know the road? (if, taken away, they do not know it, then what you have written is a PPT, not an essay**)); **Treating the allotment of characters as structure** (**"100 for the opening, 100 for the close, and the middle scraped together until there is enough": structure is a counterweight of functions, not a democracy of character counts (if the mechanism paragraph ought to occupy 300 characters, write 300 — egalitarian paragraphs are a pancake spread flat**)); **Revision that revises only the sentences** (**a common disease: three rounds of work on the draft, all of them spent polishing words and sentences, and the structure not moved once (the study of the order of revision: first inspect the skeleton (is the layout the right one?), then inspect the paragraphs (are all four pieces there?), and only at the end is it the sentences' turn: a person who revises in reverse order will revise until dawn**)).
:::

::: exercise The sentence drawing {level=1}
For the stake you nailed in Lesson 2, draw a **sentence outline**: six paragraphs (including a concession paragraph and a limits paragraph), one complete sentence of judgement to each paragraph + a function label (establish / prove / refute / close) + a connective written between neighbouring sentences (therefore / however / going further / compared with that).
::: solution
Marking: **each sentence can be accepted on its own** (**a sentence of judgement, not a noun phrase**); **the column for connectives is the best at catching bugs** (**a break where, in two places, "therefore" should have been written but "on the contrary" was written, or where "going further" was written when it is really only a change of example — a break on the drawing is the spot of the pit where the reader stumbles once the piece is written out**).
:::
:::

::: exercise One skeleton, three layouts {level=2}
Choose any one issue (for example "tiered management of mobile phones on campus"), and set out an outline in the progressive layout and one in the contrastive layout (six lines each), and state: **if you change to the refutation layout, who is the strongest target you will set up, and which sentence is its load-bearing wall**.
::: solution
Marking: **the mechanism paragraphs of the two outlines must not be interchangeable** (**if they can be swapped, the two versions are the same essay with only the title changed**); marking of the target in the refutation layout: **it must be the genuinely strongest position on that issue (the steel man, 钢铁人, of Lesson 1: the target must be one the opponent in person would nod at and claim as their own**); the load-bearing wall must point to **the one sentence such that, if it is dismantled, the position collapses** ("not 'this view is no good', but 'its effectiveness depends on parents supervising the whole way through, and that assumption breaks in the dual-earner household': when you dismantle a wall you dismantle the mortar, not the doorplate").
:::
:::

::: exercise Short essay: structure is courtesy {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**making an essay hard to read is not depth; it is a misappropriation of other people's time: structure is the author's overall choice of whether to leave the convenience with themselves or with the reader — and it is also the original public morality of the argumentative essay**". You must draw on at least six items: **the method of choosing among the four layouts**, **the three things the opening does**, **the close's "limits + the next step"**, **the paragraph's four-piece set**, **the sentence outline**, **the three forgivenesses**; and adjudicate: **what structure academic writing, official documents and the examination essay each "reward" — why one and the same logic wears different clothes in different genres (give one instance of "changing clothes and meeting with an accident")**.
::: solution
Points for marks: the six mechanisms (**the method of choice = the shape of the claim; offering a seat = publicness; limits = honest pricing; the four-piece set = the paragraph's profit; the drawing = constructability; the three forgivenesses = a budget system**); the adjudication (**for reference: academic writing rewards signposts set densely + qualifiers filling every cell (a defensive kind of writing: "this essay does not claim" at every point); official documents reward the conclusion placed first + the grounds following immediately after (the route on which "to receive the document is already to decide"); the examination hall rewards showing the stake in the first paragraph + a function sentence at the head of each paragraph (the marking route: two minutes for one question) — an instance of changing clothes and meeting with an accident:** **carrying the voice of the research paper into the examination hall (qualifiers fill every cell, and the marker supposes you dare not state the claim outright), and carrying the voice of the examination hall into the research paper (sentences of parallelism and elevation streaking before the literature: the structural version of "using the wrong genre contract" in Chapter 8**)); if the conclusion can be written as "**structure is the public infrastructure of writing: every stretch of signpost you build is saying, on your behalf, one sentence to a stranger — your time deserves respect; and the best way to respect the reader is to let them, one day, also be able to walk the night road you have walked**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The wiring, filed: the four layouts (**for the parallel, check mutual exclusion and exhaustiveness; for the progressive, check the staircase; for the contrastive, check the same frame; for refutation, check that a claim is established**), head and tail (**show the stake, offer a seat, announce the route; cinch, limits, work order**), the paragraph (**the four-piece set + the signpost sentence + the discipline of paragraph length**), the outline (**the sentence drawing + connectives as a lie-detector**), the way of reading (**three forgivenesses; 文无定法, 'writing has no fixed method', is the right to step outside the frame, not a coupon exempting you from study**). The method in one sentence: **draw the drawing first, then carry the bricks**. The next chapter is given over to taking the other side apart: refutation.
:::
