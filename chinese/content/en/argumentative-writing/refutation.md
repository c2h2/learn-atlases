title: "Refutation"
---

The previous chapter said that the greatest risk of the refutational pattern is demolishing without establishing; this chapter strips the procedure of demolition down to the screws — **the whole dignity of refutation lies in the quality of the target: you cannot win a real storm by striking a straw man**.

## Definition: the trilogy of refutation {#def-sanqu}

::: definition Mounting the target, stripping the load, clearing the ground {#def-gongxu}
**① Mounting the target** (**The steel-man restatement (Lesson 1) is upgraded here into a procedure: in the other side's own terms, state the other side's position until they nod and claim it — the 'claiming standard' is the first quality check of refutation: if the other side will not sign for the target, what you have shot through is air (the technique: set the target sentence off as its own paragraph and add quotation marks, so that readers of the whole piece see the original and your restatement at the same time: mounting the target in public is the in-court production of evidence in a refutation)**); **② Stripping what bears the load** (**Which sentence is the other side's position living on? — find the load-bearing sentence: the conclusion, the evidence, or the reasoning (the three points of refutation in the next section); the technical rule: strike the beam, not the window paper ('window paper' = the other side's wording, tone and emoji; the beam = the assumption on which their position depends in order to stand: shooting through the window paper only helps the other side redecorate ('a retort of the "you cannot even read the data" kind is striking the window paper: the other side's next line is "it is you who cannot read it", and the whole room goes back to volume'))**); **③ Clearing the ground** (**When the demolition is finished there must be a handover: what position do you leave (your stronger explanation), or at least what boundary-post ('what is needed here is an explanation better than "neither is easy"') — a victory celebration may not be opened directly on the ruins: demolishing without establishing = handing the readers you have just seized, on the spot, to the next wave of rumours ('the vacuum clause in the refuting of rumours (Chapter 8) becomes a discipline of writing here: the explanation must occupy the vacuum')**).
:::

::: definition The three points one may refute {#def-sanwei}
**Refuting the claim** (**A direct collision: 'By your standard, the conclusion ought to be the opposite' — the bravest, but not often used: the other side's conclusion is often not the output of their argument, but a projection of their position**); **Refuting the evidence** (**Pulling the firewood out from under the cauldron: check the source, check the basis of the figure, check the timeliness, check the sample (Lesson 3's checking of the accounts, invoked as a whole — 'refuting data takes less effort than refuting a view, and is safer: if the data are wrong, the other side's eloquence is automatically refunded'; but one must guard against 'pulling the firewood by nitpicking at words' (a great victory on an insignificant decimal place, while the real layer of mechanism is not harmed by a hair: pull the wrong firewood, and the pot is still boiling))**); **Refuting the reasoning** (**Do not refute the facts; refute only what is drawn out: 'I never deny your data; what I deny is that they lead to your conclusion' — the life-saving sentence-form of the debate hall (the confounding of Chapter 7, the square of Chapter 6, and affirming the consequent in Chapter 4 all open for business inside this one sentence: refuting the reasoning is turning the other side's ammunition into your own exhibit)**). **The study of choosing a point** (**The opponent strong on facts and weak on logic → refute the reasoning; the opponent's logic polished and the data old → refute the evidence; both of the opponent's hands hard → only refuting the claim is left: at that point first ask yourself 'is the evidence I have brought enough to take down one load-bearing wall?' — if it is not, change your line of work and take up qualification ('within the scope he has set, what he says is right, but this scope is worth discussing anew': when you cannot refute, staking out ground is also a tactic (the flexible counter-attack of the right to define in Lesson 2)**).
:::

::: definition The dance-steps of concession and refutation {#def-wubu}
**The three functions of 诚然 ('granted')** (**Drawing a boundary (the part I admit stops here, and I will no longer defend it); showing weakness in order to be believed (a person who delivers the goods of their own accord sounds more like a human being: the dance-step version of Lesson 1's 'a concession is a beam'); shrinking the battlefield (pressing the dispute down from ten items to two: the harder you press, the higher your rate of winning on those two)**); **The duty of 但是 ('but') to catch the ball** (**The turn must deal with the ball that 诚然 has thrown back: the strength of what was conceded, the refutation paragraph may not quietly take back (the breach clause of Lesson 4) — the mark of a breach: the concession paragraph says 'it really is effective in the short term', and the refutation paragraph does not mention the short term at all, and spends the whole piece striking at the long term — the reader thinks you have won two rounds, when in fact you have changed opponents**). **Advanced: putting the concession first** (**Place the strongest objection after the first paragraph (first finish saying the other side's best point for them), and let the whole piece advance on the track of 'even so, still …' ('putting the concession first is the highest courtesy in refutation, and also the inoculation against a "straw-man return stroke": the objector finds that their trump card has already been played by you, and played rather well')**).
:::

::: example Three cases of refutation in public opinion: surgery on the point {#ex-sange}
① A popular-science piece: 'A certain drink's sugar content exceeds the limit by 300%; drinking it over a long period causes obesity.' Comment A: '**Where did these data of yours come from? I have not found them, so they are certainly invented, and the article is a pack of nonsense.**' ② A commentary on current affairs: 'After the traffic restriction was relaxed, speed in the morning peak fell by 5%, from which one can see that the restriction policy had been overrated.' Comment B: '**The fall in speed shows that there are more cars on the road; more people shows that everyone has more money; if they have money they of course support the relaxation — you cannot refute that.**' ③ The affirmative on a motion: 'Minor mode should be on by default.' The negative: '**What right have you to decide for the child? This of yours is paternalism!**' — For each, point out the choice of point to refute and the lesion, and write a 'competent refutation' for each (within 80 characters).
::: solution
① **The direction, refuting the evidence, is right; the technique is in breach** (**'I have not found it' = an appeal to ignorance (Chapter 9: not finding it does not equal non-existence, and does not equal truth either) + 'a pack of nonsense' = pushing over the beam (the data) together with the whole building (all of the claims) (striking too hard in the refutation: one piece comes to nothing, and the whole board is lost — the opponent has only to prove a single figure, and the rest of your judgements, held jointly liable, are buried with it)**); a competent refutation: '**Please mark the basis of the test and the baseline of comparison (is "300% over the limit" against the national standard, or against products of the same kind?); if these cannot be supplied, this article's claim about sugar is downgraded to unverified.**'; ② **Treating the causal chain as a noose (an argument that makes itself)** (**Each link of the four-link chain has offered only 'it is possible': this is an optimistic slippery slope plus begging the question (using 'the relaxation has an effect' to explain 'speed fell', then using 'speed fell' to prove 'it was overrated') — the lesion is in choosing the wrong point to refute: the only thing effective here is to refute the reasoning (supply a competing explanation: the season, roadworks, flexible working — if any one of them holds, the arrow breaks)**); a competent refutation: '**The fall in speed equally coincides with the start of term and with the rainy-day rate of increase; if there is no control group, these data cannot choose between "the restriction was overrated" and "traffic grew of itself".**'; ③ **A failed demonstration of refuting the claim + clearing the ground ad hominem** (**Sticking the label 'paternalism' on the opponent = substituting an accusation of motive for a refutation of the content (the drafted version of the circumstantial ad hominem) + skipping entirely the other side's load-bearing assumption (on by default ≠ impossible to turn off: the beam of their claim is 'the nudge effect of the default value', not the grand phrase 'deciding for the child')**); a competent refutation: '**The beam of this claim is "the default setting markedly affects time of use". If checkable data are supplied showing that having it on by default reduces the average daily duration and that the channel of appeal works, our side accepts; otherwise it is only an outsourcing of risk.**' — **The general verdict on the three cases: what is most common in the arena of public opinion is not that people cannot refute, but that they treat refutation as a performance of identity: the point refuted, the breadth of the refutation, and the courtesy of the refutation all give way to taking a side**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The aim of refutation is not to win {#def-mudi}
**Refutation is pushing both sides onto a stronger position of their own** (**What you take apart is his weak version; what remains is his real position — from then on, what you face (and what the reader remembers) is the upgraded version throughout: this is why refutation must be honest: your kindness to the target is a courtesy to your future opponent (Lesson 1: every weakened version you overturn has failed to help the opponent improve their weapon)**). **The silence clause** (**'The best form of some refutations is silence' holds in conversation, and does not hold in an essay: an essay has no silence, only absence; if you do not deal with the strongest opposing side, you tacitly grant that it wins — a judge who is absent cannot decide the case, but the plaintiff may close it on one side alone (the warning in reverse of Lesson 1's 'each round of a quarrel eliminates evidence': not refuting ≠ not authorising)**). **The study of being refuted** (**The other half of a lesson on refutation is being the target: four virtues when one is refuted — admitting (one sentence, 'on this point I was wrong', beats three pages of patching it up), distinguishing (sign separately for his reasonable refutation and his unreasonable rhetoric), setting a time limit ('allow me to check these data; I will reply in the next piece'), and, most important of all: do not expel the refuting opinion together with the person who made it (a death sentence on an opinion need not be carried out on the person who holds it: this is the minimum military regulation of public discussion, and this lesson's general return to the ad hominem fallacy of Chapter 9)**).
:::

::: history The two transmissions of the art of refutation {#sec-shuangchuan}
**The Chinese line** (**The Mohist method of blasting from within in ‘非攻’ ('Against Offensive War'): 杀一人谓之不义、攻国则誉之为义——“此在知义与不义之别乎”？ 'To kill one person is called unrighteous, yet to attack a state is praised as righteous — "Is this to know the difference between the righteous and the unrighteous?"' (Our translation.) Set off the contradiction inside one and the same set of values: the earliest ‘以子之矛’ ('with your own spear') to be set down in writing (the Chinese lineage of reductio: call in no help from outside, and audit the false accounts with your own ledger alone)**); **Song** Wang Anshi, *Da Sima jianyi shu* 《答司马谏议书》 ('Reply to Remonstrator Sima') (**First condense the list of the other side's charges — 侵官 ('encroaching on official powers'), 生事 ('stirring up affairs'), 征利 ('pursuing profit'), 拒谏 ('rejecting remonstrance') — then take them apart one by one by names and actualities: “名实已明，而天下之理得矣” 'Once names and actualities are clear, the principles of the world are obtained.' (Our translation.) — the model of written refutation in Chinese: the first step is to tidy the opponent's remarks for them (a good-faith summary), the second is to return the labels one by one ('send the labels you stuck on back to the label factory'), the third is to establish a constructive argument on the return stroke (“如曰今日一切当袭而守之，则非所闻” 'If it be said that today everything should be taken over and kept as it is, that is not what has been heard.' (Our translation.)) — the three-part method is to this day the skeleton of an essay in refutation (the royal version of the contrastive form in Lesson 5)**); **Qing** the industrialisation of refutation in the detection of forgeries by evidential scholarship (**Yan Ruoqu's *Shangshu guwen shuzheng* 《尚书古文疏证》 ('Evidential commentary on the ancient-text *Book of Documents*') banishes, with several dozen pieces of internal and external evidence, a classic of a thousand years (the common account: the representative work in the detection of forgery in the ancient-text *Book of Documents*) — the summit of 'firewood-pulling refutation': do not revile the author; prove only the sources: the way a book is refuted is to leave every sentence it quotes unable to go home**); **The Western line** (**Aristotle's 《辩谬篇》 (*Sophistical Refutations*) — the founding text of the handbook for the refuting side (Chapter 9); the scholastic 'three roads of refutation' (contradiction, derivation, and an analogy that does not fit — the standardised examination of medieval debate); Popper's 'refutation first' (**the scientific standing of a theory depends on the manner in which it can be refuted — raising refutation from an art of debate to a production line of knowledge: a doctrine that does not refute is not stronger, it is poorer**); **The modern age** (**Cross-examination in legal procedure and the principle of 'hearing the opposite side' (the institutionalising of 兼听则明 ('listen to both sides and you see clearly'): truth gains in value under the testing of evidence — 'refutation is not the enemy of truth, but the auditor of truth: and the condition for the auditing trade to prosper is that both sides dare to let the audit in the door')**); **Where the Chinese line converges** (**The common account: the two transmissions of the genres of argument converged at the beginning of the twentieth century ('antithesis–thesis' sentence-patterns entered the *zawen* (polemical essay) and the editorial by the translator's pen: the joining section of '匕首与投枪' ('daggers and javelins') in Lesson 16 is precisely the modern costume of the dance-steps of refutation)**).
:::

::: warning Four overturns of refutation
**Stealing the target** (**While restating, 'tidying it up a little' for the other side until it is easier to strike: the scene of the crime in a caricature reductio (Chapter 4) — the mark of the overturn: words the other side never said appear in your refutation: before you hand the piece in, do a 'quotation-mark check': every word quoted in the refutation paragraph must be able to find its original householder in the target paragraph**); **Stopping after a touch** (**'This way of putting it is obviously wrong', 'it goes without saying' — a refutation with no argument is a blank cartridge: there is not even a report: stripping what bears the load must come with a drawing (which evidence, which piece of reasoning, how it breaks)**); **Winning the rhythm and not the reason** (**Snatching the words, interrupting, cutting a person down with a polished sentence: technique in an oral debate, proof of the crime in a written refutation — paper does not interrupt, so every 'now hear me out' on paper leaves a fingerprint**); **Clearing the ground ad hominem** (**'Once one knows what he is like as a person, his view is not worth refuting': writing the circumstantial ad hominem as the climax of a paragraph — the reader will remember your pointed wit, and will also, the next time he is needed to check the data, remember that you never need to: the persona-asset of an essay in refutation cannot survive one labour-saving piece of harshness**).
:::

::: exercise Hunting the load-bearing sentence {level=1}
Take five common positions (made up yourself, or taken from a comments section), and for each make one column of **extracting the load-bearing sentence**: 'Once this position admits ______, all the remaining statements are automatically refunded'; then for each choose a point to refute and write a refutation of 60 characters.
::: solution
Marking: **the load-bearing sentence must be a single sentence, and sincerely accepted by the side that holds the position** (**writing 'they do not really believe this' = you are altering the target on the other side's behalf (attempted theft of the target)**); the point of refutation matches the load-bearing sentence (an evidence-sentence is met by refuting the evidence, a reasoning-sentence by refuting the reasoning); where 'obviously / needless to say / everyone understands' appears in the refutation paragraph, marks are deducted (the blank-cartridge clause).
:::
:::

::: exercise Rewriting with the concession put first {level=2}
Take a stretch of argument you wrote in Lesson 4, **move** its strongest opposing side **to after the first paragraph**, and with 'even so, still …' rewrite the skeleton of the whole piece (an outline of six lines); then test yourself: would the opposing side say 'he has already admitted us'?
::: solution
Marking: **'still' must carry new argument** (**the advance after the admission cannot rely on volume: either add evidence, or change the camera position (a competing explanation), or narrow and then reinforce (a qualifying clause)**); the criterion for guarding against 'being taken over' (**the boundary of the concession must be written as one sentence that can be checked ('I admit that its short-term indicators improved; what I dispute is the long-term attribution') — where the boundary is blurred, the concession becomes the source of the other side's bulletin of victory: this is the only real risk in the dance-steps of concession**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: the code of honour in refutation {level=3}
Write from six hundred to a thousand characters, commenting on '**The code of honour in refutation: together we force the best position out, and then each takes their own back — victory is defined not as the other side falling silent, but as his having changed for the better, and you for the better too; if, after the refutation is over, your disagreement is exactly what it was before the refutation, only louder, then the side that has really lost is the reader**'. You must call on at least these six: **the claiming standard for mounting the target**, **striking the beam and not the window paper**, **the study of choosing among the three points of refutation**, **putting the concession first, and guarding against being taken over**, **the silence clause**, **the four virtues of being refuted**; and give a ruling: **faced with a malicious refutation (a deliberate caricature of you), what is the legitimate limit of a counter-strike — write three rules you would dare to use now**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**claiming = producing evidence; beam and window = the allocation of resources; choosing a point = the study of tactics; putting it first = the inoculation; absence = authorisation; being refuted = the military regulation**); the ruling on a counter-strike against malice (**sample rules: ① correct only the target, and do not go after the person ('this is not my position', once only; from the second time on, downgrade to no reply, and archive the original text — a repeated defence of oneself is fodder for the other side**); ② use their caricature as evidence of their motive (point out, for the middle layer to see, the distance between the other side's exaggerated version and their real supporters: **the true audience of a malicious refutation is the bystander; the object of your counter-strike is always the bystander and not the opponent**); ③ always provide an entrance to 'how to refute me correctly' (**the last article of the code of honour: write the opponent a manual — if you dare to give it, that shows you are not afraid: this is where the whole piece scores highest, because it is confidence that can be carried out**); if the conclusion can be written '**the end of refutation is not conquest, but a mutual audit: you have refuted me into strength, and I have refuted you into solidity — the whole profit of public discussion lies in the margin between this coming in and that going out**', that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The procedure, filed: the trilogy (**claiming, stripping the beam, clearing the ground: the vacuum clause**), the three points of refutation (**claim / evidence / reasoning: the study of choosing a point = matching the ammunition**), the dance-steps (**the three functions of 诚然 + 但是 catching the ball + putting the concession first**), the aim (**pushing both sides onto a stronger position of their own; the four virtues of being refuted**), the overturns (**stealing the target, the blank cartridge, seizing the rhythm, clearing the ground**). The method in one sentence: **first make the target nod, then let the beam break**. The next chapter adds the colour: the language of argument, and the measure of rhetoric.
