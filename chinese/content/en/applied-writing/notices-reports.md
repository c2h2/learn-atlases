title: "Notices, reports and requests"
---

The previous chapter used three columns to choose the document type. This chapter deals with the three that are easiest to mix up — **their real difference is not in the content but in the loop: a request (请示) requires a reply, a report (报告) requires only to be read and noted, and a notice (通知) does not require a reply but does require to be carried out. Whether a procedure has a closed loop can be told at a glance from which document type is in use.**

## Definition: the open loop and the closed loop {#def-bihuan}

::: definition Three loops, three genres {#def-sanhui}
**Closed loop** (**a request → a reply (批复); a letter of consultation (商洽函) → a letter of reply (复函):** when it is sent out, one must assume that the other party is to state a position within the time limit. **That is why the 'hard requirement' below holds: your request must be specific enough to be approved in a single sentence.** ② **Open loop** (**report, notice, bulletin (通报), announcement (公告), public notice (通告), opinion (意见):** when they are sent out, they require only to be received and to be carried out, and do not require a reply. **They therefore carry no risk of being 'rejected', and are by nature easier to write empty.** **This is the sociological observation of the whole chapter:** an open-loop genre is cost-free speech inside an organisation; the extent to which it is abused is inversely proportional to the strength of accountability. ③ **The accident of mixing** (**received account: a report must not smuggle in matters that belong in a request.** Stuff a closed-loop request into an open-loop document, and the receiving side has two ways of handling it, both of them against you: **treat it as a report, to be read and noted** (your matter is handled by no one), or return it for correction (you go through one further procedure). **This is the real consequence of that document in the worked example of the previous chapter:** it is not that it is irregular; it is that it cannot be processed. **This lesson gives one mnemonic:** if you want a reply, use a closed-loop document type; if you want something carried out, use a downward document type; if you only want to leave a trace, use a report — **and admit honestly that the third is often the real purpose.**
:::

::: definition Issuing, approving and transmitting, and forwarding {#def-pizhuan}
**Received account.** A notice applies to issuing, and to conveying, matters that are required to be carried out; to approving and transmitting (批转) official documents of lower organs; and to forwarding (转发) official documents of higher organs and of organs that are not in a relation of subordination. There is, besides, a class of matter-specific notices, used for meetings, inspections, and the submission of materials. **The three acts do not have the same effect.** ① **Issuing, or printing and distributing.** Handing the regulations, plans and measures drawn up by this organ over to lower organs to carry out: the effect comes from this organ, and the notice is the instrument of delivery. ② **Approving and transmitting.** Passing a document that has come up from below on to a wider range of lower organs, for them to carry out. **Its substance is a capital increase:** the document approved and transmitted thereby acquires the effect of the organ that approves and transmits it, and a piece of local experience thereby becomes a general requirement. **This lesson calls this 'the class upgrade of effect'.** ③ **Forwarding.** Sending a document of a higher organ, or of an organ not in a relation of subordination, out into one's own jurisdiction. **It creates no effect. It does two things: it endorses, and it redistributes.** **There then appears an observable pathology.** A document is forwarded through four levels, and each level adds 'in the light of local conditions' (结合本地实际) and 'strengthen organisational leadership' (加强组织领导); by the time it reaches the grass roots it has become five sheets of paper saying one thing. **Received account: in recent years, and more than once, special documents have required that documents be streamlined, and that forwarding level upon level and simple copying be prevented.** **This lesson's explanation:** forwarding is a ritual that gathers the responsibility for knowing upwards and pushes the responsibility for carrying out downwards. **With each forwarding, the one who forwards obtains a posture that can be produced for show** ('I have conveyed it'), and at the same time commits no resources. **This is the mechanism of 'implementing documents by means of documents' (以文件落实文件):** it is not laziness; it is an optimal response when assessment looks only at traces. **To cure it, what is examined must be not the number of documents issued but the rate at which matters are closed.** **The 'two indicators' at the end of Chapter 3 are cashed in here.**
:::

::: definition The meeting notice and the return slip {#def-huizhi}
**Received account.** The usual required elements of a matter-specific notice: time, place, those who are to attend, the content, the materials and the requirements for registering, the contact, and the deadline for the return slip (回执). **The technical points of this lesson.** ① **Those who are to attend must be specified down to the post, or to a number.** 'The responsible persons concerned' (有关负责人) will bring one deputy and three section cadres into the room together, when the purpose of the meeting needs only one person who can decide. ② **Requirements for materials must state the format and the number of characters.** 'One paper for exchange' (交流材料一份) will get you one slide, a speech text of three pages, and a report of twenty thousand characters. Format is not fastidiousness; it is what makes the cost of every submission the same, and so comparable. ③ **The return slip is the smallest realisation of a closed loop.** The one sentence 'Please report the return slip to ×× before 8 October' (请于 10 月 8 日前将回执报××) turns an open-loop notice into a matter that can be assessed. A meeting notice with no return slip is only an informing; only one with a return slip is a convening. **From this the lesson draws a general technique:** any open-loop text that one hopes will be carried out should come with a return slip, a time limit, or a default value. **'If there is no reply, follow scheme 2' (如未回复则按第 2 方案), in the email of the previous chapter, and the return slip here, are two ages of the same technique.**
:::

::: definition The four columns of a report, and the test of the problems column {#def-silian}
**Received account.** A report applies to reporting on work, presenting a situation, answering an enquiry from a higher organ, and submitting materials. The usual structure is the basic situation, the principal measures, the problems that exist, and proposals for what follows. **This lesson's way of writing the four columns.** ① **Basic situation.** Write only quantities that bear on the decision: population, area, organs, the previous year's base, and the total and the structure for the current period. **Any background that does not bear on the decision is deleted, without exception.** ② **Principal measures.** Write 'what was done — what was put in — which measurable indicator was changed'. **A measure that lacks the third item is not an achievement; it is an activity.** ③ **Problems that exist.** They must be checkable, and one of them must be this organ's own. **This lesson's formula for the test:** the number of characters in the problems column, divided by the number of characters in the whole text, is a rough indicator of a report's honesty. **A report whose ratio is zero has publicity for its use; the correct document type for it is not the report.** **This is not a joke about wording. To treat publicity as a report is to have the higher organ decide where the information is short, and institutionally that is a dereliction of duty.** ④ **Proposals for what follows.** Write the things to be done at the next step, and the support that is needed. **Wherever 'we earnestly request approval of ××' (恳请批准××) appears here, a closed-loop request has been stuffed into an open-loop document; page 2 of this chapter has already stated the consequence.** **Received account: a request should be made in a separate document, one matter to one document.**
:::

::: definition The request: a last sentence that can be approved {#def-qingshi}
**Received account.** A request applies to asking for instructions, or for approval. It should be one matter to one document; in general only one principal recipient organ is named, and it must not be copied to lower organs. The higher organ should issue a reply. **Four required elements.** ① **Grounds.** The policy basis, the practical need, and the consequences of not resolving it. **The basis must be written out with the document's title and its reference number** (Chapter 3: only what can be verified is a line of defence). ② **The matter.** What is wanted, quantified down to a sum of money, a number of places, an area of land, or a number of established posts. ③ **Schemes.** It is best to give two or three schemes to choose from, and the price of each. **A request that offers only one scheme hands the whole risk of the choice to the person who replies.** **More than one scheme is a courtesy, and also a form of self-protection.** ④ **The closing.** 'The request above is submitted; please reply' (以上请示，请批复); 'Whether this may be done, please reply' (可否，请批复). **The core technical requirement of this lesson:** your 'matter' must be written as a sentence on which the higher organ can say 同意 ('agreed') directly. **A request so vague that it cannot be approved by a single word will, at the stage of receipt, be returned for correction.** **There is then a simple method of self-checking that can actually be used.** Read the matter you are requesting once through, and try adding the two characters 同意 in front of it. **If, once they are added, the grammar does not work or the scope is unclear, that is the place which is to be returned.** **This move is what this lesson calls the 'agree' test (同意测试). It is the most useful tool in this chapter, and it applies to every request: proposing a topic to a supervisor, quoting a price to a client, applying to a spouse for a budget.**
:::

::: definition Before the event and after it {#def-shiqian}
**Received account.** 'Request instructions before the event, and report after it' (事前请示、事后报告) is a discipline of handling documents. Acting first and obtaining approval afterwards is, in practice, often treated as an inversion of procedure. **This lesson's analysis.** ① **Why some people act first and request afterwards.** Time will not wait. What matters more is that a written request leaves a record of having been refused, whereas communication by speech leaves none. **The intuition experiment in the next section will reckon this account.** ② **The effect of a fait accompli.** To make the request only after a thing has been done is to put the person who must reply into a dilemma: to approve is to endorse it; not to approve is to waste money already spent. **It is effective now and then, and its long-term cost is that procedure is devalued.** Once everyone has learned to act first and speak afterwards, a reply is no longer a decision; it is only a registration. **This is a negative lesson in techniques of the 'irrefutability' kind:** it wins this once, and loses the rule as a whole. **The 'overdrawn intensity' (透支强度) of argumentative writing, in Course 4, has an administrative version here.** ③ **How to be urgent in the right way.** When there truly is an emergency, what complies is not to refrain from requesting, but to write the request shorter and more definite: state the subject, the temporary measures already taken, the subsequent ratification or the authorisation that is needed, and the time limit for completing the formalities afterwards. **One sentence from this lesson:** urgency has never been a reason for omitting procedure; urgency is a reason for requiring procedure to supply a short version.
:::

::: example Turning a briefing on achievements into a report {#ex-chengji}
An excerpt of the original: “活动开展以来，各单位高度重视、精心组织、群众反响热烈，取得明显成效，未发生重大问题，干部思想认识大幅提高。” Since the activity began, every unit has attached great importance to it and organised it with care; the public response has been enthusiastic; clear results have been achieved; no major problem has occurred; and cadres' thinking and understanding have greatly improved. (Our translation.) Rewrite it as a qualified excerpt from a report: **give at least four figures (including the basis and the source)**, one specific problem of 'falling short of what was expected', and one sentence of proposals for what follows; and calculate the share of the problems column before and after the rewrite.

::: solution
**A sample rewrite** — **“一、基本情况：本次行动涉及 12 个街道、87 个点位，2024 年 3 月至 9 月累计投入 41 万元、8 260 工时（财务处台账、人事处工时表）。二、完成情况：整改问题 312 项，其中完成 268 项、正在办理 44 项；验收抽查 30 个点位，合格 24 个（91—9 月督查通报第 7 期）。三、未达预期：城中村 6 个点位未按期完成，原因是第三方施工单位合同到期未续；此项影响整改完成率 7 个百分点。四、下一步：10 月 20 日前完成剩余 44 项并公示验收结果；所需追加经费另行请示。”** I. Basic situation. This operation covers 12 subdistricts and 87 sites; from March to September 2024 the cumulative input was 410,000 yuan and 8,260 working hours (the Finance Office's ledger, and the Personnel Office's table of working hours). II. Completion. Of 312 problems under rectification, 268 have been completed and 44 are in hand; of 30 sites spot-checked at acceptance, 24 passed (supervisory-inspection bulletin No. 7, for 91–September). III. Short of what was expected. Six sites in urban villages were not completed on time, because the third-party construction unit's contract expired and was not renewed; this item affects the completion rate of rectification by 7 percentage points. IV. The next step. Complete the remaining 44 items before 20 October, and publish the results of acceptance; the additional funds required will be requested in a separate document. (Our translation.) **All four figures carry a basis and a source.** '反响热烈' ('the response was enthusiastic') and '大幅提高' ('have greatly improved') are deleted entirely — they are the 无量词 ('wording without a quantity') referred to on page 1 of this chapter. **The share of the problems column.** In the original the problems column has a character-count of zero, so the ratio is zero; after the rewrite it is about a tenth. **The reading this lesson asks for is not 'the more characters the better', but this:** if that ratio stays at zero over a long stretch, the lower levels of that organ have already stopped treating the report as a source of information, and treat it as ritual; ritual can be written short. **Received account: the requirement, in recent years, to shorten the length of documents stands in just this causal relation with the phenomenon.** **Marking:** to be able to write this one causal sentence is an A for this chapter — **'the document is too long' has never been the problem itself; 'the document cannot be trusted' is. And the cheapest remedy for what cannot be trusted is to write it short.**
:::

::: intuition Why they would rather telephone {#def-dianhua}
**An organisational phenomenon.** A matter that ought to be written as a request is often pursued first through ten telephone calls; by the time the written request finally arrives, both sides have long known the result. **Three explanations.** ① **Speech leaves no record.** Words that were refused need not be archived, and do not become a precedent for the future. **One failed telephone call does not change the relation of power between you; a request that has been returned does.** ② **Speech can be revised.** Inside one and the same telephone call the request can be shrunk from 'additional funds' to 'give a little first'. That is negotiation, whereas a request, once it is on paper, becomes an offer. **All three costs of 'the engineering of refusal' (拒绝工程), in Course 7, are present here:** putting it in writing raises clarity, and at the same time raises the visibility of being refused. ③ **The cost of it is the organisation's.** For a matter got done on the telephone, if something goes wrong, no one can prove who agreed at the time. **The 'number of written requests' can therefore be read as an indicator of how strong an organisation's accountability is** — not that red tape is heavy, but that no one is willing to sign a name on paper. **The conclusion this lesson can put to use:** if you want to set a precedent for a matter, require a written request; if you want to protect the handling officer, allow communication by speech but require a confirmation afterwards. **A confirmation is milder than a request, and yet it has the same function of memory.** **The difference among all three genres in this chapter comes down, in the end, to this sentence:** a closed-loop document creates memory; an open-loop document only makes a sound.
:::

::: history From palace memorials to systems for handling documents {#sec-zouzhe}
**Ming and Qing.** Routine memorials (题本), personal memorials (奏本) and palace memorials (奏折) formed several tracks for reaching upward. The vermilion rescript (朱批) was the reply in the emperor's own hand. **Received account:** a thoroughly mature system of open loops and closed loops. The palace memorial went straight for the emperor to read, the vermilion rescript answered it, and it was handed back to be archived, forming a closed loop with a file that can be checked. **Procedures such as 'court flogging' (廷杖) and 'deliberation and reply' (议复), for their part, decided how many levels of reply it needed.** ② **1912.** The Republican scheme of official-document forms abolished old types such as the memorial (奏), the consultation (咨) and the instruction (札), and fixed a new list. **Received account:** one change of regime at once redrew the three tables, 'upward, parallel and downward' — **which bears out that point of the previous chapter: a new government first lays down how things are to be written.** ③ **1951/1981.** The new China repeatedly unified the methods for handling official documents, and the separation of the request from the report gradually became clear. **Received account.** ④ **1993/2000.** The regulations on the handling of official documents of administrative organs remained in use into this century, and made explicit such requirements as 'a request shall be one document, one matter'. **Received account:** that this sentence has been stressed again and again is itself the proof that it is hard to carry out. **A rule that has to be restated once every ten years is one that is pushing against the inertia of organisations.** ⑤ **2012.** The regulations now in force carry this on and consolidate it; the standard of format was updated at the same time. **Received account.** ⑥ **The last ten years and more.** Office automation, and electronic systems for handling documents, have spread; 'streamline documents, control length, and prevent forwarding level upon level' has become a requirement of reducing the burden. **Received account. This lesson's reading:** electronic circulation turns 'return for correction' into an action that can be counted, and so the cost of a wrong document type, and of missing required elements, changes from personal relations into data. **Compliance of format therefore improves rapidly in front of the machine, while emptiness of content does not improve** — **a machine will not read whether the column for 'problems that exist' stands at zero.**
:::

::: warning Four pits in this chapter
**The document type '请示报告' ('request-report'), which does not exist.** It commits both errors at once: it both requires a reply and need not be replied to, and so no one processes it (the accident of mixing, above). **Treating an 'opinion' as a compulsory basis.** **Received account: an opinion applies to putting forward views, and methods of handling, on important questions; it may go upward, downward, or in parallel.** Its binding force depends on whether the issuing organ has made clear 'carry this out accordingly' (照此执行); otherwise 'act according to the opinion' has no way of being violated that one could determine. **This pit is the commonest at the grass roots: a guiding opinion is loaded, level upon level, into a hard assessment.** ③ **Using 'as soon as possible' (尽快) in place of a time limit.** The 'no date' of the previous chapter appears here for the second time. **Every 'as soon as possible' is a blank cheque, and the payee is luck.** ④ **Reporting bad news only at the end of the year.** The time value of information, for the taking of decisions: a single gathering-up at the year's end is to let the decisions of the whole year rest on data that are out of date. **The right rhythm for a report is the month in which the problem appears.**
:::

::: exercise Write a real request {level=1}
For one campus situation (any one of: funds of 10,000 yuan, one further post, or the temporary borrowing of a venue), write a request of no more than two hundred characters. It must contain the four required elements, and it must pass the 'agree' test.

::: solution
Marking: **one principal recipient organ, one matter, a sum or a number of places included, the reference number of the document relied on, and a closing that asks for a reply.** **Of the six, one missing means one deducted.** **If '同意 plus your request' does not read, it is returned to be rewritten** (the 'agree' test, above).
:::
:::

::: exercise Measure a report {level=2}
Find one annual work report that has been made public. In 120 characters, calculate its **share of the problems column**, and point out how many of the 'problems' in it are in fact intentions for the next step.

::: solution
Marking: **it must be possible to bring out one formulation that is 'disguised as a problem'.** **The typical case is 'some areas still need to be further strengthened' (有的领域还需进一步加强):** the mark of a sentence of this kind is that any organ, in any year, can copy it out as it stands (the problems column, above: checkable, and the one item that is one's own).

::: exercise Short essay: the closed-loop organisation {level=3}
Write between six hundred and one thousand characters, assessing this claim: '**The maturity of an organisation can be measured by its ability to handle closed-loop documents: where there is a request there is a reply, where there is a reply there is a time limit, and where there is a time limit there is a record. Put the other way round, if an organisation conducts its substantive business largely by means of open-loop documents — letting a meeting stand in for a decision, minutes stand in for a reply, and speech stand in for a request — it is not that it is inefficient, but that it is systematically producing deniability: so that, every time responsibility is traced after the event, that one sentence of commitment cannot be found.**' You must call on at least six of the following: **the three loops**, **approving and transmitting, and forwarding**, **the technique of the return slip**, **the share of the problems column**, **the 'agree' test**, **before and after**. And you must decide: **is 'deniability' always a bad thing?** (You must give one situation in which it is legitimate, and state an observable mark that distinguishes the legitimate from dereliction of duty.)

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**loop, approving and transmitting, return slip, share, test, sequence in time**); a legitimate situation (**a passing answer must give 'the delay of an irreversible decision'**: a measure that affects other people's major rights and interests, while the dispute is not yet decided, deliberately keeps its written form, so that it will not be treated as a commitment already made and thereby constrain later correction; **or a policy experiment in a period of trial and error, which needs to leave those who carry it out some room to adjust.** **Here deniability is a tool against 'committing too early'**); the distinguishing mark (**it must be observable — three candidates:** ① **whether the reason for not committing was made public.** If the decision that has been shifted into speech, and the reasons for it, are never stated to the outside, then it is the first kind. ② **whether a record is supplied after the event.** Once the experiment has ended, is there a formal report and an evaluation? The one who supplies the record was experimenting; the one who never supplies it was evading. ③ **who bears the risk.** If the uncertainty is left with the people least able to bear it — residents, the handling officer, lower levels — then however good its name, it is dereliction of duty. **Marking looks at whether at least two of the three are given, and at whether it is made plain that 'where the risk lands' is the ultimate criterion.**) If the conclusion can be written as “所以本章不是教人回避书面，而是教人先问：这件事现在应当被固定下来吗；如果应当，就用闭环文种并给出时限；如果不应当，就把它写明为试验并规定何时必须转为书面——真正危险的从来不是模糊，是不肯承认自己在模糊” — 'So this chapter does not teach people to avoid writing things down. It teaches them to ask first: should this matter be fixed now? If it should, use a closed-loop document type and give a time limit; if it should not, write it explicitly as an experiment, and prescribe when it must be turned into writing. What has never been truly dangerous is vagueness. It is being unwilling to admit that one is being vague.' (Our translation.) — that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The loops, filed: the three documents (**a reply wanted, something to be carried out, only a trace to be left**), approving and transmitting (**the class upgrade of effect**), forwarding (**a posture can be photocopied; resources cannot**), the return slip (**the cheapest device for turning an open loop into a closed loop**), the problems column (**a rough indicator of honesty**), the 'agree' test (**only a request that can be approved by a single word is a request**), before and after (**urgency is a reason for requiring a short version of procedure, not a reason for omitting it**). One sentence of method: **when the writing is done, ask first — should this matter be fixed? If it should, use a document type that has a reply.** Next chapter: letters and minutes — the politics of parallel documents.
:::
