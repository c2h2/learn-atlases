---
title: "Official documents and their format"
---

The previous chapter: a private person uses one sheet of paper to stand surety for the worst day; this chapter: the state uses one sheet of paper to stand surety for everything — **the current rules fix the official documents of Party and government organs at fifteen kinds, and another national standard specifies the layout down to the millimetre and the number of lines; this is not fussiness, it is humanity's earliest, and also its most thorough, project of “把文体写进物理参数” ('writing a genre into physical parameters')**.

## Definition: effect and prescribed form {#def-xiaoli}

::: definition The two words in the definition {#def-liangci}
**The prevailing account.** The current regulations describe an official document as a document by which Party and government organs exercise leadership, perform their functions and handle official business, and which has a specific effect and a standardised form. **Two key words.** **① Specific effect.** Once an official document has been issued, it changes certain people's duties, powers or resources. **It is not a statement; it is a disposition.** So that line from the previous chapter, “你在分配义务” ('you are allocating duties'), is here written out to its limit: one character alters a budget; one sentence opens or closes a qualification. **② Standardised form.** Form is not a requirement of good looks; it is a requirement of recognisability. **The recipient must be able, within two seconds, to judge who this is, what they are doing, and whether it has to be dealt with.** **This lesson's definition:** the document type is a code of authority, and the format is the document's identity card. **From this comes the criterion of judgement for the whole chapter:** whenever an official document is under discussion, first ask which kind of administrative act it is performing, and then ask which suit of clothes it is wearing. **A mismatch of the two is the root of every error in this chapter.** **To use the wrong document type is not a problem of prose; it is to declare a position one does not hold.**
:::

::: definition Fifteen kinds and one matrix {#def-juzhen}
**The prevailing account.** The current fifteen kinds are: 决议 (resolution), 决定 (decision), 命令（令） (order), 公报 (communiqué), 公告 (announcement), 通告 (public notice), 意见 (opinion), 通知 (notice), 通报 (circular), 报告 (report), 请示 (request for instructions), 批复 (official reply), 议案 (proposal), 函 (official letter) and 纪要 (minutes). Before this, the rules for administrative organs set thirteen kinds; the current rules fold in the document types of Party organs, add two kinds to the list, and rename one. **This lesson does not memorise the names; it first builds a three-column matrix.** **① The direction of issue.** Upward: 请示, 报告, and 议案 **in some cases**. Downward: 命令, 决定, 通知, 通报, 批复, 公告, 通告, and 意见, **mostly downward**. Parallel: 函, and also 意见 and 通知 between organs that are not in a relation of subordination. Internal, or of the class of meeting minutes: 纪要. **② Whether the addressee is specified.** 公告 and 通告 face unspecified addressees; 请示, 报告 and 函 face a specified organ. **This column decides directly which one you ought to use:** to speak of a matter to society one cannot use a “请示”, and to ask one organ for an official reply one cannot use a “通告”. **③ Whether a reply is required.** A 请示 must receive a 批复 (**a closed loop**); a 报告 asks only to be read and noted (**an open loop**); a 函 that consults must be answered by a letter in reply (**a closed loop**); a 通知 and a 通报 generally do not require a reply (**an open loop**). **How this lesson uses it:** once these three columns have been filled in, the document type is almost determined of itself. **The common shape of choosing the wrong document type is to write an open loop as a closed loop** (carrying a 请示 inside a 报告) **or to write a closed loop as an open loop** (using a 函 to put forward a demand that ought to have been a 请示). **Chapter 4 deals specifically with these two accidents.**
:::

::: definition Header, body and imprint {#def-sanqu}
**The prevailing account.** The current format standard divides the page into three parts, the header (版头), the body (主体) and the imprint (版记). The header contains the copy number (份号), the classification and the period of confidentiality (密级和保密期限), the degree of urgency (紧急程度), the identifier of the issuing organ (发文机关标志), the document number (发文字号), the signer (签发人), and the like. The body contains the title (标题), the principal receiving organ (主送机关), the text (正文), the note of attachments (附件说明), the inscribed name of the issuing organ (发文机关署名), the date of the document (成文日期), the seal (印章), the annotation (附注) and the attachments (附件). The imprint contains the organs copied to (抄送机关), and the organ and the date of printing and distribution (印发机关 and 印发日期); there are also page numbers (页码). **How this lesson reads them:** these three stretches are not zones of typesetting; they are three time-layers of one administrative act. **① The header = the prior piece** (前件). **Who is exercising the power, under what number, at what classification, how urgent it is, and by whom it was signed for issue:** it answers where the standing of this document comes from. **So one rule is extremely hard:** an upward document shall mark the name of the signer, **because a decision by the level above needs a natural person who can be called to account.** **Authorisation shows its face in the header.** **② The body = the act itself.** **Title, addressee, text, attachments: this layer does that thing.** **③ The imprint = the subsequent piece** (后件). **To whom copies are sent, by whom it is printed and distributed, and when it was printed:** it answers where this document arrived — **delivery is part of the effect.** **This lesson's summary:** an official document prints authorisation, the decision and delivery on one and the same sheet of paper. **This is its deepest difference from a literary text: a literary work does not need to prove that it is a work.** **“文学来盖章” ('literature comes to affix a seal'), from the first course, here meets a precise counter-example: an official document needs a whole set of seals, and the seals have their positions.**
:::

::: definition The document number, the date of the document and the seal {#def-yaojian}
**The document number.** **The prevailing account:** it is composed of the issuing organ's code characters (代字), the year and a serial number; the year is enclosed in six-corner brackets, and the character “第” is not written. **This lesson's definition:** it is the document's unique identifier. **Without it, once a document has been filed it cannot be retrieved, and in a dispute it cannot be cited.** **A “文件” ('document') with no number is, inside an administrative process, something close to a photograph of a notice.** **② The date of the document.** **The prevailing account:** the standard is the date on which the responsible person signed it for issue, or the date on which the meeting adopted it; for a document issued jointly, the last to sign prevails; the year, month and day are marked in full in Arabic numerals. **Why the strictness goes so far as “标全年份” ('marking the year in full'):** because every time limit is counted from that date. **A document which does not write the year in full throws the responsibility for the calculation onto the recipient.** **That illness from the previous chapter, “无日期” ('no date'), is here a risk that the document loses its effect.** **③ The seal and the inscribed name.** **The prevailing account:** except in specified cases, an official document shall bear the seal of the issuing organ. **This lesson's analysis:** the seal is not an artistic version of a signature; it is a design of proof “不可由个人单独复制” ('that an individual cannot copy alone'). **It changes the subject of the act from a natural person into an institution, so that the document is still valid after the person who handled it has been transferred away.** **This is the technical basis of administrative memory:** an institution can promise, because it lives longer than a person; the seal is the material form of that fact. **“介绍信把信任装进纸里” ('a letter of introduction packs trust into the paper'), from Chapter 2, here receives the version of it that states the mechanism.**
:::

::: definition Twenty-two lines, twenty-eight characters {#def-hangshu}
**The prevailing account.** The current format standard prescribes that the paper for official documents is A4, and gives the dimensions of the margins and of the type area (版心); the number of lines and the number of characters are also prescribed, and the requirement commonly followed in printing is about twenty-two lines to the page and about twenty-eight characters to the line. **Three consequences.** **① The quantity of information has been made into a thing.** **The same text, printed out in different organs, occupies the same number of pages,** **and so “篇幅” ('length') becomes a quantity that can be compared.** **How many pages an organ has issued in a year is an administrative cost that can be counted.** **This is a statistic that can appear only after a genre has been written into millimetres.** **② Format becomes a budget heading.** **Printing, binding, filing and transport are all reckoned by the page;** **to prescribe the layout is in fact to prescribe the bandwidth and the unit price of one administrative communication.** **This lesson's formula:** whatever cannot be measured will not be cut. The reason “精简文件” ('simplifying documents') can become a requirement of governance is that the number of pages has become comparable. **The prevailing account:** in recent years, special documents have more than once required that the number and the length of documents issued be controlled, so as to ease the burden on the grass roots. **③ It creates a new kind of error.** **A document whose layout does not comply may be sent back when it is received, when it is filed, or when it is inspected;** **and so there appears, at the grass roots, a real outlay of working hours: not writing, but setting out again, into a compliant shape, something that has already been written.** **This chapter does not defend this, nor does it despise it.** **Format is a device for avoiding liability, and it is also a kind of tax; the only claim this chapter makes is this —** **the tax ought to fall on the paper, and ought not to fall on the people who do the work.**
:::

::: example A checklist of essential elements {#ex-yaokan}
A certain bureau submits to the municipal government a request for instructions. The cover reads “×局文件 ×局〔2024〕37 号” ('Document of Bureau ×, Bureau × 〔2024〕 No. 37'); the title is “关于申请增加编制的报告” ('Report on an application to increase the establishment'); the principal addressee is given as “市政府、并抄送局属各处室” ('the municipal government, and copied to each office and division under the bureau'); the text ends “可否，请批示” ('Whether or not it may be granted, please instruct'); and the date of the document is “×局 10 月 20 日” ('Bureau ×, 20 October'). Please draw up a checklist of essential elements: **point out at least six problems, and their consequences**.

::: solution
**① A mismatch of document type.** **The title writes “报告” while the ending asks for “批示” ('an instruction'): a report must not carry items that belong in a request for instructions (the prevailing account),** **whereas a request for instructions must be one document for one matter, and must ask for an official reply.** **The consequence: the receiving department cannot assign it according to the procedure for handling documents, and may treat it only as something to be read and noted, so that the matter stays where it is.** **② The elements of the title are incomplete, or do not agree.** **The prevailing account requires that a title be composed of the issuing organ, the matter and the document type, and that the document type agree with the text.** **“关于申请增加编制的报告” writes the act the wrong way round:** **the document that makes an application is a request for instructions; a report is a report back.** **③ Copying to a subordinate.** **A request for instructions must not be copied to a subordinate organ (the prevailing account);** **to copy it to each office and division under the bureau is to announce, as an accomplished fact, a demand that has not been approved.** **The consequences: first, the procedure is breached; second, once it is rejected, this organ will find it hard to explain the matter downwards.** **④ The date of the document is incomplete.** **The year is not marked in full, and the signer is missing.** **An upward document shall mark the name of the signer.** **The consequences: the point from which the time limit begins to run is uncertain, and in a dispute the person responsible cannot be found.** **⑤ The way the principal receiving organ is written.** **“市政府、并抄送” writes the principal addressee and the copy-to in the same place.** **The prevailing account requires the two to be marked separately: the principal addressee before the text, and the copy-to in the imprint.** **The consequence: the responsibility for handling is dispersed.** **⑥ The form of the document number.** **The year mark and the serial number must be written so as to conform to the standard (six-corner brackets, and the character “第” is not written).** **Also still to be supplied:** the contact person and the telephone number (the annotation), a note of attachments (for example, attaching the document that grounds the establishment), and the printing information and the page numbers. **This lesson's marking standard:** of the six, full marks go to anyone who can write the “后果” ('consequence') as one sentence of administrative language (**such as “会被作阅件处理” ('it will be treated as a document for reading'), “无法归档” ('it cannot be filed') and “驳回后难以向下解释” ('after rejection it will be hard to explain downwards')**); **whoever writes “不规范” ('not in standard form') scores nothing —** **“不规范” is not a form of words one can act on.**
:::
:::

::: intuition Why every official document grows into one face {#def-yizhang}
**An analogy.** **Banknotes, passports, postage stamps and official documents share one and the same design logic:** **trust is printed into physical detail, because physical detail is the hardest thing to forge.** **The prevailing account: forging, altering, and buying or selling the official documents, certificates and seals of state organs is conduct that the criminal laws of countries generally punish.** **Three inferences.** **① Layout is a kind of asset.** **Precisely because a sheet of red-header paper (红头) can get people to do things, it is worth counterfeiting;** **so “公文能被用来诈骗” ('an official document can be used to defraud') is not a failure of format, but precisely the proof that format has succeeded.** **② What a uniform layout lowers is the cost of verification.** **The recipient need not understand the content; a look at the header is enough to judge whether it should enter the procedure.** **This is anti-forgery designed for attention.** **It comes from the same source as “可预期性是制度性利他” ('predictability is institutional altruism') in the previous chapter.** **③ It also has a dark side.** **When layout carries trust, a person who commands the layout can exercise power past the content;** **and so in reality two kinds of accident appear: one is an insider abusing the red header, and the other is an outsider counterfeiting it.** **Neither of the two corresponding lines of defence relies on writing words; both rely on verifiability:** **the number can be looked up, the channel can be called back, and a document must leave a trace in the register of documents received.** **The last line of this lesson:** **do not take layout for mere form, and do not put a superstitious faith in layout.** **Layout is a technique made for efficient trust among strangers; it both protects you and may be used by someone impersonating it —** **the only remedy is to make it checkable.**
:::

::: history Layout is sovereignty {#sec-banshi}
**Warring States to Qin and Han.** **Documents on bamboo and wooden slips already had fixed formulae for the signature, the date, the copying and the postal transmission; excavated materials show that their strictness exceeded that of any literary writing of the same period.** **The prevailing account.** **② Tang to Song.** **The classification of the emperor's documents and of officials' documents was finely drawn; name taboo (避讳), elevating the line (抬格) and the use of the seal made up a visual regime.** **The prevailing account:** elevating the line marks rank on the page, which the previous chapter has already set out; one sentence is added here: **in imperial China a uniform layout always stood alongside “书同文” ('one and the same script'),** because what it solved was the same problem: **how to make a document, a thousand li away, still be taken as the same document** (the li, 里, is a traditional measure of distance, and its length changed from period to period). **③ Ming to Qing.** ***Tiben* 题本 and *zouben* 奏本 distinguished matters of business from matters of ceremony; *piaoni* 票拟 and *pihong* 批红 fixed the procedure of handling.** **The prevailing account:** this is the era in Chinese history closest to a “工作流引擎” ('workflow engine'): **the shape of a document decided which hands it had to pass through.** **④ 1912/1951/1981.** **The Republic abolished a number of Qing document types; after the founding of the People's Republic, the methods for handling official documents were unified several times.** **The prevailing account:** in the first batch of technical documents at every change of regime there is a formula for official documents — **a new government first prescribes how to write, and only then prescribes what to write.** **⑤ 2000/2012.** **The rules for administrative organs set thirteen kinds; the current regulations bring Party and government organs together and fix the number at fifteen, and in the same year the format standard was updated.** **The prevailing account.** **This lesson reads that as one “接口统一” ('unification of interfaces'):** **only after the two systems had been merged did retrieval and filing across the systems become possible.** **This is that matter of enterprise IT at the end of the twentieth century, several decades earlier than it, only struck onto paper.** **⑥ The 2000s to the present.** **Rules for the exchange of electronic official documents, for electronic seals and for the management of electronic archives were established one after another; and at the same time “精简文件、控制篇幅” ('simplifying documents, and controlling their length') became a requirement of easing the burden.** **The prevailing account.** **The close of this chapter:** when an official document turns from paper into a strip of data, the physical constraints of layout disappear, but its three things must be kept: **a unique identifier, a signing-off that can be verified, and a trace of delivery —** **the standards for electronic documents are written entirely for these three things, and are not written so that the documents will look good.**
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this chapter
**Memorising the names and not building the matrix.** **The fifteen kinds do not need to be memorised; what is needed is the three-column judgement: direction, whether the addressee is specified, and whether a reply is required (the matrix above).** **Taking format for typesetting.** **Leaving out the signer, leaving out the date of the document, and copying a request for instructions to a subordinate are all problems of powers and of procedure, not problems of good looks (the worked example above).** **Supposing that the “红头” ('red header') is the effect.** **The effect comes from official authority and from procedure; layout is responsible only for being recognised, and for being counterfeited by others (③ in the intuition above:** **any document that persuades people by layout alone deserves to have its document number checked first).** **Taking the easing of the burden to mean writing less.** **What the prevailing account requires is that number and length be controlled, not that necessary procedures be cancelled;** **failing to issue a document that ought to be issued is to turn the easing of the burden into a dereliction of duty (the clause on the tax, above).**
:::

This engineering has the same source as law: both assume that people will forget, will repudiate, and that someone will impersonate another; and the way the two fail is also the same: when observing the form is rewarded more than realising the content, form begins to eat the thing it was originally there to protect**'). ::: summary
Layout, filed: the definition (**effect + prescribed form: it changes rights and duties, and it must also be recognised within two seconds**), the document type (**a code of authority, chosen by the three columns**), the three sections (**authorisation, the act and delivery, printed on one sheet of paper**), the document number (**a unique identifier, the precondition of retrieval and of citation**), the line count (**turning length into a budget heading**), anti-forgery (**layout is an asset, and so it can be used under false pretences; only verifiability is the line of defence**). The method in one sentence: **do not look at whether it is written so as to look like an official document; first check whether it has those few characters that let you call someone to account**. Next chapter: notices, reports and requests for instructions — the open loop and the closed loop.
:::
