---
title: "Official letters and minutes"
---

The previous chapter was vertical documents: those above approve, and those below report. This chapter is horizontal — **an official letter assumes that the other party owes you nothing, and owes only a reply; minutes are the pen that rewrites the voices in a room into organisational memory, and are therefore the most dangerous of the two genres in this chapter**.

## Definition: the official letter is a parallel document {#def-han}

::: definition Consultation, enquiry and a request for approval {#def-shangqia}
**The usual account** (**an official letter applies between organs that are not subordinate to one another, for consulting on work, for enquiring about questions and replying to them, and for requesting approval and replying to matters submitted for approval** (**three points** (**① "not subordinate to one another" is the key attributive** (**when you ask a department that stands in no relationship of subordination to you, but that has the power of approval, to do one thing, use an official letter and not a request for instructions** (**this is a high-frequency error: to use a request for instructions is to declare yourself a subordinate of your own accord; to use an official letter to seek approval from a true superior is to evade leaving a trace** (**neither error concerns tone; what they concern is the administrative relationship** (② **the outgoing letter and the reply** (**the opening of a reply should cite the title and the document number of the letter that came in** (**“你单位《关于××的函》（×函〔2024〕5 号）收悉”** ('Your unit's *Letter concerning ××* (× Letter 〔2024〕 No. 5) has been received and noted.') (Our translation.) — **this one sentence is at once courtesy and law: the courtesy lies in acknowledging that the other party spoke first, and the law lies in pinning the two documents into one and the same chain that can be retrieved** (**what Course 4, argumentative writing, calls “引证通胀” ('citation inflation') is here used the other way round:** **to cite an accurate number for the other party is the cheapest goodwill** (③ **the strength and weakness of the closing** (**“请予支持” ('kindly grant support') is a request; “请研究函复” ('kindly examine this and reply by letter') asks for a reply; “专此函达” ('this letter is sent for this purpose alone') gives notice; “特此函复” ('this reply is made hereby') closes the matter** (**the four manners of wording must not be mixed:** **write a notice as a request, and you will receive a pile of replies; write a request as a notice, and you will receive nothing** (**this lesson's definition:** **the official letter is the most democratic document type, because it assumes that the other party has the right to say no;** **the whole of its technique grows from here.**
:::

::: definition Courtesy is an interest rate {#def-limao}
**Why parallel documents are especially courteous** (**a vertical document speaks by authority; a horizontal document has no authority it can use;** **what it relies on is a repeated game: today I issue a letter asking for assistance in an investigation, and help you obtain evidence; only tomorrow are your data open to me** (**three mechanisms** (**① reciprocity is an account-book** (**this account-book has no formal record, so it is kept up by reputation;** **the cost of keeping that reputation up is precisely those polite formulas that look superfluous** (**this lesson's conversion:** **“为盼” ('which is what I hope for'), “为荷” ('for which I should be obliged') and “顺致敬意” ('with my respects') are not ornament; they are a little interest paid in advance for the other party's help in future** (② **a threat in a parallel document must be packaged** (**you cannot order a unit of the same rank; you can only make it see the consequences of not acting;** **parallel documents have therefore developed a rhetoric:** **“根据××规定，该项工作由你单位牵头”** ('Under the provisions of ××, your unit takes the lead in this work.') (Our translation.) — **it does not bring a charge; it puts the matter back in its place** (**this is the same technique as Course 4's “罪名不指控做了什么，而是重新归类” ('A charge does not accuse anyone of what was done; it reclassifies.') (Our translation.), used on its positive side** (③ **a time limit is the only supporting structure a parallel document has** (**there is no superior who can chase the matter up, so the deadline must be written into the letter, and paired with a default value:** **“请于 11 月 10 日前函复，逾期我处视为无异议并据此办理”** ('Please reply by letter before 10 November; if that date is passed, this office will treat the matter as raising no objection, and will proceed on that basis.') (Our translation.) — **this sentence has already, on page 4 of this chapter, been criticised for reading silence as consent;** **this lesson still requires you to write it, because in a parallel document to leave the deadline unwritten is to hand the power of decision to the other party's schedule** (**of the two harms, take the one that can be checked.**
:::

::: definition The record, the minutes, the bulletin and the resolution {#def-sizhong}
The usual account (minutes are used for recording the main circumstances of a meeting and the matters decided; one and the same meeting often produces four texts at once (a table of the division of labour (① the meeting record (aims at completeness, and is not itself a basis for implementation; ② the minutes (aim at selection, and, inwardly, may serve as a basis for implementation and for supervision; ③ a meeting bulletin or a press release (aims at image, has a function of publicity on the inside, and has no binding force on the outside; ④ **a resolution or a decision** (**aims at being usable outwardly, must be put to a vote, and must be issued as a formal document type** (**this lesson's criterion, in one sentence:** **the difference among these four texts is four answers to "who is allowed to act on this basis"** (**the commonest accident in practice is to take the second and do with it the work of the fourth:** **both the usual account and scholarly discussion point out that matters which touch the rights and duties of citizens and of legal persons ought to be made outwardly in a formal document type; to set duties outwardly by internal minutes is to produce an effect that is easily called into question, and that the person affected cannot retrieve** (**this is not a problem of wording:** **a document that cannot be retrieved also cannot be appealed against.**
:::

::: definition The four elements of one satisfactory decided item {#def-siyao}
**The four elements** (**the content of the decision, the responsible unit, the time limit for completion, and whether a formal document needs to be issued separately** (**taken one by one** (**① the content of the decision is written as one sentence that can be judged** (**“原则同意方案” ('agreement in principle to the plan') is not a decision; “按方案第 2 稿执行，涉及金额以财政审核数为准”** ('Implement according to the second draft of the plan; where sums of money are involved, the figure verified by finance shall prevail.') (Our translation.) is (② **the responsible unit is written down to a specific unit** (**“各有关部门” ('the departments concerned') is the vague reference criticised on page 4 of this chapter;** **responsibility evaporates inside a vague reference** (③ **the time limit is given to the day** (**the "无日期" ('no date') disease of the previous chapter is at its most deadly in minutes, because minutes are often the only warrant supervision has** (④ **whether a document is to be issued separately** (**wherever the item takes effect upon an external party, one must write “由××另行印发正式文件” ('a formal document shall be printed and issued separately by ××')** (**this column is the boundary clause of the minutes:** **it stops an internal record from quietly becoming an external basis** (**this lesson gives one self-check:** **copy out every decided item in the minutes, and ask, "if, three months later, someone pursues responsibility on this basis, can this item point to one person and one date?";** **the line that cannot point to them is the line that still has to be discussed again that evening —** **note that the correct use of this self-check is to ask it at the meeting, and not to write it after the meeting.**
:::

::: definition Who was present, and whose words entered the minutes {#def-shui}
**The usual account** (**minutes are in general signed and issued by the chair of the meeting, or by a person whom the chair has authorised; who attended, who was absent and who asked for leave are usually stated** (**three observations, from political science, in this lesson** (**① the attendance list is part of the effect** (**it states who made this decision;** **the silence of those absent cannot be recorded as consent, unless the rules of procedure so provide** (**“沉默一般不构成承诺” ('silence does not generally constitute a promise'), from the pragmatics of Course 7, has its institutional version here** (② **whether disagreement is to be written** (**the usual account: minutes may record differing opinions; in practice they are often written as “会议认为” ('the meeting holds'), which smoothes the disagreement flat** (**this lesson's position has two sides:** **to write the disagreement protects the minority (when trouble comes later, he has a record**); not to write the disagreement protects implementation (**minutes that leave three positions on the page cannot be supervised**) (**the correct practice is therefore this: smooth the disagreement out of the decided items, and separately leave a trace with the one sentence “会议听取了××方面意见” ('the meeting heard the views of the ×× side')** (**this is a technique that can be taught** (③ **who the signatory is** (**one and the same set of minutes has a different weight when the chair signs and issues it and when the office prints and issues it under a seal affixed on another's behalf;** **minutes under a seal affixed for another often mean that nobody is willing to put a name to them** (**the previous chapter said “上行文要标签发人，因为授权要露脸”** ('An upward document must label the person who issues it, because authorisation has to show its face.') (Our translation.); this chapter adds one sentence: **the face on the minutes is the last person responsible for this room.**
:::

::: example Turning a chat record into minutes {#ex-jiyao}
“会上大家一致认为这项工作压力很大、情况复杂，要求各相关部门抓紧研究，尽快拿出办法，领导要重视，经费问题另行解决。” ('At the meeting everyone agreed that the pressure of this work is great and the situation complex, and required the departments concerned to look into it at once, to produce a way of dealing with it as soon as possible, that the leadership must take it seriously, and that the question of funds be settled separately.') (Our translation.) Rewrite this as three satisfactory decided items (one undecided item may be included), and state what you deleted, and why.

::: solution
**A sample rewriting** — **“一、原则同意《××整治方案》第 2 稿，由××科牵头按第 2 稿组织实施，涉及资金以财政评审数为准（承办单位：××科；时限：2024 年 11 月 20 日前完成实施方案印发）。二、同意建立周调度机制，自 11 月起每周五 15 时召开视频调度会，由××同志召集，各街道书面报送进度（时限：首次会议 11 月 8 日；本条不需另行发文）。三、会议听取了关于经费保障的两种意见，责成××处于 11 月 15 日前提出保障测算与来源安排，报下一次办公会决定（未决事项；在此之前不得对外承诺资金）。四、涉及对经营主体设定整改义务的条款，由××科另行起草正式文件并按程序对外公布，本纪要不作为对外依据。”** ('1. Agreement in principle to the second draft of the *×× Rectification Plan*; the ×× Section shall take the lead in organising implementation according to the second draft, and where funds are involved the figure appraised by finance shall prevail (responsible unit: the ×× Section; time limit: the printing and issuing of the implementation plan to be completed before 20 November 2024). 2. Agreed to establish a weekly dispatch mechanism: from November, a video dispatch meeting at 15:00 every Friday, convened by Comrade ××, with each subdistrict reporting progress in writing (time limit: the first meeting on 8 November; this item does not need a separate document). 3. The meeting heard two views on the provision of funds, and charges the ×× Division with putting forward, before 15 November, an estimate of the provision and an arrangement of the sources, to be decided at the next office meeting (undecided; until then no funds may be promised externally). 4. Clauses that set a duty of rectification upon business entities shall be drafted separately, by the ×× Section, as a formal document and published externally according to procedure; these minutes shall not serve as an external basis.') (Our translation.) **(** **the four things deleted** — **① “大家一致认为” ('everyone agreed')** (**it bears neither the trace of a vote nor the trace of a disagreement, and is the most expensive empty sentence in minutes: it makes everyone responsible, and therefore there is no one who is responsible** (② **“各相关部门” ('the departments concerned')** (changed to one specific responsible unit; ③ “尽快” ('as soon as possible') (changed to two time limits given to the day; ④ “领导要重视” ('the leadership must take it seriously') (it is a posture, not an act; posture ought to be recorded in the bulletin, and not in the minutes (marking: the fourth item must be got right (moving duties that face outward out of the minutes is the only rule in this chapter that "protects those who are not present" — the readers most easily overlooked in minutes are the people who are not in the room and yet are bound by them.

::: intuition A meeting is not a decision {#int-kaohui}
Two things that are easily confused (holding a meeting apportions the cost to those who are present (everyone has heard it, and afterwards it is hard to go back on it); only the minutes are the step that turns an endorsement into a duty (three inferences (① the meeting first, and the minutes written up afterwards, is the ordinary practice; the minutes first, and the meeting supplied afterwards, disguises a decision as a collective intention (**it is more concealed than “先办后请” ('act first, request afterwards'), because it has not even done the thing; it has only made a record** (② **why some meetings run long** (**because the real distribution takes place in the room, and everyone knows that words written into the minutes have to be answered for;** **a long meeting is thus an advanced form of postponing the signature** (**this lesson's standard for judging how long a meeting lasts is very simple:** **when the meeting breaks up, can one write three decided items that carry a responsible unit and a time limit?** **If one can, the meeting was production; if not, it was rehearsal** (③ **the scarcity of minutes is disappearing** (**sound recording, video recording and online collaborative documents make a "complete record" the default;** **when everyone may be played back, the "power of selection" in the minutes changes from something tacit into something that can be cross-examined** (**for the writing, this makes only one demand:** **you must state in the minutes the standard by which you select —** **the demand sounds like an exaction, but it is in fact the self-protection that saves the most trouble:** **a document which states “本纪要只记载议定事项，不记载讨论过程”** ('These minutes record only the matters decided, and do not record the course of the discussion.') (Our translation.) **wards off, for the author, both of the accusations that will come later.**
:::

::: history The tradition of recording words {#sec-jiyan}
**Pre-Qin to Tang** (**the recording of words and the recording of events had their own offices; the usual account: the court diary (起居注) recorded the emperor's words and acts, and the Tang had a practice of the “时政记” ('record of current government') kind, in which the chief ministers and others recorded the substance of current government** (**this lesson's reading:** **China very early treated "writing the meeting down" as a task of power that needed institutional protection:** **who does the recording, and whether the record may be shown to others, have always been political questions** (② **Song to Qing** (**会要 ('collected institutes'), 日历 ('daily calendars'), and the various registration files of the Grand Council; memorials and vermilion rescripts formed one complete closed loop of question and answer** (**the usual account** (**parallel documents in this period distinguished addressee and tone under such names as “咨”, “札” and “移”;** **an extremely fine set of horizontal genres is a mark that the bureaucratic system has matured** (**one point is added here to the previous chapter's “书同文” ('the same script'):** **sameness of writing means not only a unified script, but also a unified tone** (③ **1876 / the late Qing** (**a handbook of rules of procedure was published in the English-speaking world and sold for a long time; its main points are the motion, the second, the order of speaking, and the rules of voting** (**the usual account:** **the way it works together with minutes is worth pointing out:** **the rules of procedure decide what counts as "decided"; only then can minutes be said to record it** (**at a meeting with no rules of procedure, the minutes can record only how loud the voices were** (④ **1912 / 1949** (**the old-style document types were abolished, and a new-style system of meetings entered the organs** (**the usual account:** **from this century the meeting became one of the main forms of decision in Chinese organs, and documents were layered accordingly** (⑤ **2000 / 2012** (**“会议纪要” ('minutes of a meeting') was entered among the document types of administrative organs; the regulations now in force rename it “纪要” ('minutes') and apply it uniformly to Party and government organs** (**the usual account** (⑥ **the last ten years and more** (**voice recorders, online minutes and collaborative documents; and, at the same time, criticism and a clearing-up directed at too many meetings and at “以纪要对外设定义务” ('setting duties externally by means of minutes')** (**the usual account** (**the close of this chapter:** **once recording becomes cheap, the writing of minutes changes from a technical chore into an object of accountability:** **the sentence you choose to write down will become the only sentence that is acknowledged —** **this is why this chapter sets the two genres side by side: the official letter deals with your relations with other people, and minutes deal with who drafts the history.**
:::

::: warning Four pits in this chapter
**Using a request for instructions to a department that approves** (**where there is no relationship of subordination, use an official letter;** **to lower yourself to the rank of a subordinate brings no benefit at all, and brings only a file that is in error (above, ①**); **a reply that does not cite the letter which came in** (**with no citation, the two documents do not know each other in the archive, and cannot later be made into a chain (above, the reply, ②**); **“大家一致认为” ('everyone agreed')** (**it records neither the vote nor the disagreement, and is the commonest sentence in minutes by which responsibility is shed (above, the example, ①**); **using minutes to set duties externally** (**both the usual account and doctrine question its effect, and the person affected cannot retrieve it (above, the four texts, ④:** **a document that cannot be looked up also ought not to be complied with.**

::: exercise Write an outgoing letter {level=1}
For one campus situation (asking to borrow a venue from some municipally run hall; asking a sister institution about a practice; checking one leak with the water-supply company), write an official letter of no more than one hundred and eighty characters. It must contain a cited basis, the matter, a time limit and a closing.

::: solution
Marking: **the closing must match the real strength of your purpose** (**if what you want is a reply, use “请研究函复” ('kindly examine this and reply by letter'), and do not use “专此函达” ('this letter is sent for this purpose alone');** **and there must be a deadline given to the day, or a default value (above, the time limit, ③**.

::: exercise A short essay: who drafts the history {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**组织记忆不是发生过的事的总和，而是被签发的那部分。因此纪要的写作者处在一种奇怪的位置：他既无决策权，又事实上决定了什么算作决策。任何一次把‘讨论’写成‘同意’、把‘有人反对’写成‘会议强调’，都会在未来某天变成一个不可追问的事实。所以一个成熟组织对纪要写作者的要求不是文笔，而是两种可核对的诚实：写明选择标准，写明缺席与分歧的记载规则**” ('**Organisational memory is not the sum of what happened, but the part that was signed and issued. The writer of minutes therefore stands in a strange position: he has no power of decision, and yet in fact decides what counts as a decision. Every time ‘讨论’ ('discussion') is written as ‘同意’ ('agreed'), and ‘有人反对’ ('someone objected') as ‘会议强调’ ('the meeting stressed'), it becomes, on some future day, a fact that cannot be questioned. What a mature organisation therefore requires of the writer of minutes is not a gift of style, but two kinds of honesty that can be checked: to state the standard of selection, and to state the rules by which absence and disagreement are recorded.**') (Our translation.). You must call on at least six items: **the official letter assumes that the other party may say no**, **courtesy is an interest rate**, **the default value and silence**, **the division of labour among the four texts**, **the four elements of a matter decided**, **the signatory and the list of those present**; and you must decide **whether a minority ought to be given a "right to verify the minutes" (that is, a formal channel for objecting to what has been recorded) — you must explain the way it works together with the rules of procedure, and give one form in which it is most likely to be abused, together with a way of preventing that**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**saying no, the interest rate, silence, the four texts, the four elements, signing and issuing**); the mechanism (**an acceptable answer: the right of verification must be exercised within a fixed time limit after the meeting, by a written objection appended after the minutes rather than by deleting or altering the text, and must not on that account suspend the carrying out of what has already been decided according to rule —** that is, “留痕不改行” ('leave a trace; do not change the action')**; the way it works together with the rules of procedure is this: the rules of procedure decide what counts as decided, and the right of verification decides whether what was decided has been recorded accurately (**of the two, one governs the happening of the fact, and one governs the recording of the fact**); the form of abuse (**it must be specific: using the objection as a means of delaying implementation, or writing the objection up as a second decision, so that supervision has nowhere to set its hand (**an acceptable answer on the means of prevention must contain the two points of a time limit and “不停止执行” ('implementation is not stopped'), and may add “异议须指向记载准确性而非决定当否” ('an objection must be aimed at the accuracy of the record, and not at whether the decision was right')**); if the conclusion can be written “**所以本章的道德结论很窄也很硬：记不写下某句话，本身就是一种决定；因此写纪要的人应当在文首用一行字说明自己依据什么选择——这一行字是全课最小也最有效的免责声明，因为它把一支改写历史的笔，变回了一支记录的笔**” ('**So the moral conclusion of this chapter is narrow, and it is hard: writing a sentence down or not writing it is itself a decision; the person who writes the minutes should therefore use one line at the head of the text to state the basis on which he selects — that one line is the smallest, and the most effective, disclaimer in the whole lesson, because it turns a pen that rewrites history back into a pen that records.**') (Our translation.), that is an A.

::: summary
The horizontal file: the official letter (**the other party owes you nothing, only a reply; so courtesy is an interest rate**), the reply (**cite the number, and the chain is formed**), the time limit (**the only supporting structure of a parallel document**), the four texts (**who is allowed to act on this basis**), the four elements of minutes (**content, unit, time limit, and whether a separate document is issued**), the list and the signing and issuing (**the last person responsible for this room**). The method, in one sentence: **when you write minutes, remember that people who are not in the room, and yet are bound by them, are also your readers**. Next chapter: plans, summaries and investigation reports — carry what you say to the point where it can be checked.
:::
