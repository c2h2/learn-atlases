---
title: "Plans, summaries and investigation reports"
---

The previous chapters deal with what is happening at this moment; this chapter deals with three kinds of time — **a plan is a verifiable commitment concerning the future, a summary is an audit of the past, and an investigation report is the taking of evidence at the scene; they share one discipline: any statement that cannot be checked is not written in**.

## Definition: the plan {#def-jihua}

::: definition Six elements and the mode of acceptance {#def-liuyao}
**Received account.** Documents of the plan type generally set out the target, the tasks, the measures, the steps, the time limit and the division of responsibility. This lesson supplies the sixth item that is often left out. **① Target.** It must be measurable. Received account: of the five adjectives current in management studies (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-limited), only one can really save a plan — measurable. **② Tasks and measures.** The task says what is to be accomplished; the measures say by what means it is to be accomplished. What has tasks and no measures is a wish list. **③ Steps and time limit.** Broken into stages, down to the day. Words such as “力争” ('strive to'), “确保” ('ensure') and “大幅” ('by a large margin') must not appear in the time-limit column (the previous chapter's discipline of numbers). **④ Division of responsibility.** One matter, one unit. A matter assigned to two units is the same as not having been assigned. **⑤ Mode of checking.** Who accepts the work, on the basis of what materials, and what follows if it falls short. This lesson's formula: **a plan with no mode of acceptance is not a plan; it is a declaration of stance.** The correct document type for a declaration of stance is a speech text, not a red-header document. **⑥ Level.** Received account: in practice the three words “规划”, “计划” and “安排” ('programme', 'plan' and 'arrangement') are often distinguished by time limit and by how coarse or how fine they are. The real way to write is by layers: for three years, write the direction; for the year, write the targets; for the quarter, write the deliverables; for this week, write the actions. Cram the three layers into one document, and the result is usually that none of the three is there. **The precision of a plan can extend only as far as the date on which its next step can be checked. This is the first physical law of this chapter.**
:::

::: definition Once an indicator becomes a target {#def-mubiao}
**Received account.** A maxim of management studies that is often quoted has, roughly, this sense: when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure. **Three mechanisms.** **① The inevitable result of self-selected indicators.** If assessment allows the party being assessed to choose the counting rule (口径), the rational course is to lower the base first and then to over-fulfil. There then appears a symptom of the genre: the targets in a plan are always higher than the previous year's actual figure by a percentage that neither hurts nor itches. **② Substitution.** A target that is hard to fulfil is replaced by an approximation that is easy to fulfil: “提高质量” ('raise quality') becomes “发表论文数” ('the number of papers published'), and “改善体验” ('improve the experience') becomes “办结率” ('the rate of cases closed'). This is not fraud; it is the indicator migrating. Every migration leaves a trace in the document: the wording of the target column changes from result-words to action-words. This is the change most worth noticing when one reads a plan. **③ The distribution is averaged away.** A plan whose aggregate meets the standard can conceal the fact that half the units have not met it. Therefore, wherever a plan has an aggregate target, one must at the same time require that the distribution be given, or the three worst units. This point is used three times later in the chapter. **This lesson's countermeasure is only one, and it is technical:** require every plan to write, beside the target, “这个数字如何被核查、由谁核查、以及未达标时会发生什么” ('how this figure is verified, by whom it is verified, and what will happen if it falls short') (Our translation.). Leave any one of these unwritten, and the plan is sent back — **it is cheaper than any call to “改进文风” ('improve the style of writing').**
:::

::: definition The experience column is the only asset {#def-jingyan}
**Received account.** A summary usually contains the basic situation, the main achievements, the existing problems, the experience, and the intentions for what follows. **Judge each column in turn.** **① Basic situation.** Write only the quantities that bear on the conclusion, the base figure included. **② Achievements.** They must carry the counting rule, the time point and the source. “大幅增长” ('growth by a large margin') is not an achievement; it is an adjective. **③ Existing problems.** The previous chapter gave a rough indicator of honesty: the share of the character count taken by the problems column. Here a harder one is added: at least one of the problems must be this organ's own responsibility. To attribute every problem to insufficient support from above, to those below not being of one mind, and to objective conditions, is the least laborious way to write a summary, and the way with the least information in it. **④ Experience.** The received name is “经验体会” ('experience and what was grasped from it'). This lesson's definition: experience is abstracting one way of doing something into a rule that can be used again. It is the only part of a summary that is of use to the future; the rest is only an inventory of liabilities. A piece of experience that qualifies must be capable of being tested once it is detached from the original scene: “推行清单化是因为清单比会议便宜” ('Checklist practice is pushed because a checklist is cheaper than a meeting.') (Our translation.) can be tested; “领导重视是关键” ('That the leaders attach importance to it is the key.') (Our translation.) cannot. **⑤ Intentions for what follows.** This is the seed of the next plan. Wherever a request to close the loop appears here, ask for instructions in a separate document (the previous chapter). **This lesson's way of reading.** Align some unit's plans for five years running with the problems column of the summary for the year before each. If the two repeat each other closely, the organisation is making a sequel to the same thing, and not doing something new. This sentence can be unfolded at the intuition experiment in this chapter.
:::

::: definition Three kinds of distortion {#def-shizhen}
**① A ratio in place of an absolute quantity.** “增长 50%” ('a rise of 50%') may be from 2 to 3. For every percentage, the base figure and the counting rule must be given at the same time (the third course, “没有分母的分子” ('a numerator with no denominator')). **② An average concealing the distribution.** “人均” ('per person') and “平均办理时长” ('average time taken to handle a matter') are the most easily pulled by extreme values. For every average, the median, or the three worst units, must be given at the same time. **③ Taking a point for the whole.** The single best typical case is used to reach a conclusion about the whole domain. This is survivorship bias in the form of an official document. The criterion this lesson gives is very simple: look at how the sample is chosen. The three wordings “随机抽取” ('drawn at random'), “按类别分层” ('stratified by category') and “经基层推荐” ('recommended by the basic level') correspond to three strengths of conclusion, and the third can often prove only that “被推荐的那几个很好” ('the few that were recommended are very good'). Received account: sampling and typical-case investigation are two different methods, each with the conditions under which it applies. **Mixing them is the commonest technical error in this field, and the hardest kind to detect — because, grammatically, it is entirely smooth.**
:::

::: definition Three columns, the counting rule and the ladder of strength {#def-sanlan}
**The three columns must be kept apart.** Fact, inference and recommendation are each given a subheading. The result of writing them mixed together is that a recommendation reads as though it were a fact. This is the chapter's common requirement for all three kinds of document, and it is most fatal in an investigation report. **② The three items of the counting rule.** The statistical counting rule (what counts as one case), the time point (as of which day), the sampling frame (from which list one draws) — if any one of them is missing, the figures cannot be compared. **And figures that cannot be compared are worse than no figures, because they will be cited again and again as a basis.** This is the most important downstream effect of an investigation report. **③ A ladder of the strength of conclusions.** This lesson ranks five grades: re-checkable vouchers and direct observation > agreement among the independent statements of several people > the statement of a single person in the know > the agreed account of the parties > “大家普遍认为” ('everyone generally holds'). The last grade ought not to appear in any document, because it shifts responsibility onto a collective that does not exist. The third course's scale of strength of evidence gains one new grade here: “座谈会上的意见” ('opinions at a discussion meeting') belongs to the fourth grade; it can prove only that the materials lack the fifth grade. **④ The three items of a recommendation.** The body that is to carry it out, the resources required, and the consequences of not carrying it out. If any one of the three is missing, it is a complaint and not a recommendation. **Received account.** In recent years the statutes and the requirements of accountability bearing on the quality of statistical data have been strengthened more than once, and it has been repeatedly stressed that statistical counting rules and the sources of data should conform to the norms. **This lesson's neutral conclusion:** when the person under investigation is the person who gives the marks, the honesty of the report does not depend on the author's courage, but on whether there is a second channel for reporting upwards. **This is the ethical boundary of the investigation report: document studies can lay down the counting rule; it cannot lay down who will dare to write by that rule.**
:::

::: example Auditing three figures {#ex-shuzi}
A summary contains three sentences: “①全年办理事项同比增长 46%；②群众满意度 98.5%；③政策覆盖率达到 100%。” '① Matters handled over the whole year rose 46% on the year before; ② the public's satisfaction was 98.5%; ③ the policy coverage rate reached 100%.' (Our translation.) For each sentence, supply what it leaves out, and judge which conclusions would be overturned.

::: solution
**① The rise of 46%** — **what must be supplied: what the previous year's base figure was, and whether the counting rule has changed** (**if this year brings in matters that were not included before, the rate of growth is a product of the change in the counting rule**), **and the absolute quantity** (**from 29 items to 42 items is also 46%**). **If the base figure is very small, the conclusion “工作显著加强” ('the work has been markedly strengthened') is overturned;** **if the counting rule has changed, then even the two characters “增长” ('growth') do not hold.** **② Satisfaction of 98.5%** — **what must be supplied: the sampling frame (who was drawn), the manner of return** (**a follow-up by telephone, or scanning a code at the window: the latter is a self-assessment by a person who has just had the matter dealt with, and those whose matter was not completed are not in the sample**), **and the definition of “满意” ('satisfied')** (**whether it is “无投诉” ('no complaint') or “愿意再来” ('willing to come again')**). **The typical overturning: when the rate of return is under three tenths and the forms are filled in mainly on the spot at the window, the figure can be read only as “在场且愿意填的人中 98.5% 表示满意” ('Of the people present who were willing to fill it in, 98.5% said they were satisfied.') (Our translation.)** **③ A coverage rate of 100%** — **what must be supplied: where the list for the denominator comes from** (**the population on the register, usual residents, or the number of people who have already applied**); **and whether those not covered may have been left out of the count** (**a person with no fixed dwelling may be contained in none of the lists, and yet be covered in the statistics**). **This item is the one most often overturned, because the denominator is usually compiled by the party being assessed.** **Marking:** **all three places must land on the three words “口径、时点、样本框” ('counting rule, time point, sampling frame');** **any place that writes only “数据不翔实” ('the data are not full and accurate') is a fail —** **“不翔实” ('not full and accurate') is not a verdict one can act on, and with the previous chapter's “不规范” ('not up to standard') it belongs to the same class of words that idle.**
:::
:::

::: intuition Why a plan is the sequel to a summary {#def-xuji}
**An observable coincidence in time.** The summary is written in December, the plan in January. Their author is often the same person, using the same last year's file on the same computer. **Three inferences.** **① The plan therefore grows, by its nature, into “上一年问题的改写版” ('a rewritten version of the previous year's problems').** The received phenomenon: the resemblance between each year's plan and the previous year's problems column is higher than its resemblance to this year's real needs. **The writer is not to blame:** to write a plan that is genuinely new, the investigation has to be done all over again, and the investigation has no budget. **② The best way to read a plan is therefore to read it side by side with the previous year's summary.** See which problems have been carried across to become targets, and which have been carried across to become the same sentence with the wording slightly changed. Those carried across into wording are this organisation's real pattern already in place. This lesson calls this “两栏并读法” ('the method of reading the two columns side by side'); it is the same technique as the third course's “并表法” ('the method of setting tables side by side'). **③ A practical suggestion for the writer.** If you must write a new plan in the place where a sequel goes, the honest practice that saves the most trouble is to add a column, “本年度不再做的事” ('things that will no longer be done this year'). To give something up is a decision that can be recorded; yet the great majority of plan-genres have no such column, and so every failure lives on for ever in the manner of “继续推进” ('continue to press ahead'). **The final blow of this chapter:** whether an organisation will really change direction is seen from whether its document template has that column, “不再做” ('no longer to be done') — **this is more reliable than any strategic slogan.**
:::

::: history From 'no investigation, no right to speak' to the counting rule {#sec-diaocha}
**1930.** A slogan turned field investigation into an epistemological requirement, its rough sense being “没有调查，没有发言权” ('No investigation, no right to speak.') (Our translation.) **Received account: this lesson's two readings.** First, it places the duty of writing a report ahead of the right to draw a conclusion. Second, it at the same time makes the investigation report into a ticket of admission — and so the question “谁定义口径” ('who defines the counting rule'), for the first time, becomes a political question. **② 1936/1961.** A work of economic anthropology taking the Chinese countryside as its object gained a reputation in international scholarship; in the same period, separately, typical-case investigation with “解剖麻雀” ('dissecting a sparrow') as its method was advocated. **Received account: these two traditions have run side by side down to now.** One requires method, and re-checking by peers; one requires the typical, and what can be told. The difficulty of writing an investigation report comes, for the most part, from these two traditions meeting in the same stretch of text. This sentence is the simplified account this chapter gives of the whole dispute over method. **③ From 1953.** The first long-term plan began, and the units came to form a cyclical genre, “规划—计划—总结” ('programme—plan—summary'); a statistical system was established at the same time. **Received account: this lesson points out one consequence for the genre.** Documents of the plan type have been given an extremely high place in the organisational life of China, because such a document is at once a technical document and a document of commitment. Earlier than literature, it prescribed how a unit narrates its own future. **④ The 1980s.** Management by objectives, the responsibility system and “量化考核” ('quantified assessment') entered organs and enterprises; after sociology was restored, methods of field investigation returned to research and to policy research. **Received account.** **The key turn in this chapter:** once indicators are many, the law “指标一旦成为目标就不再是好指标” ('Once an indicator has become a target, it is no longer a good indicator.') (Our translation.) has a great many testing grounds (the indicator clause above). **⑤ 1983/1996/2009 and after.** Laws on statistics were enacted and revised more than once, and the quality of statistical data and the norms for the counting rule were stressed again and again. **Received account.** **⑥ The last ten years and more.** Modes of investigation of the “督帮一体” ('supervision and help as one') and “四不两直” ('four nots and two directs') kind were advocated; at the same time there appeared criticism of excessive trace-keeping and of assessment by materials, and a clearing of these away. **Received account.** **Closing word of this chapter:** when “做了没有” ('was it done or not') can be proved only by the document, the document speaks in place of the thing. **So the whole technique of this chapter's three kinds of document can be reduced to one line: write what is verifiable in the place where it can be verified, and say the rest by some other means.**
:::

::: warning Four pits in this chapter
**Self-chosen targets** (**the received maxim: once an indicator has become a target it is no longer a good indicator; the countermeasure is not to use fewer indicators, but to write the mode of verification and the consequences of falling short into the same column (the indicator clause above)**); **writing a time limit with “拟” ('it is proposed'), “力争” ('strive to') and “尽量” ('as far as possible')** (**these three words make the whole document impossible to accept; they belong to the register of a speech text (item ③ of the six elements above)**); **no problem of one's own in the summary** (**a summary that turns every problem into an external cause has a quantity of information close to zero (item ③ of the four columns above)**); **taking opinions from a discussion meeting as evidence** (**this is strength of the fourth grade, and can prove only that the materials are insufficient (item ③ of the strength ladder above):** **“大家普遍认为” ('everyone generally holds') is the only sentence this chapter forbids.**
:::

::: exercise Adding a column to a plan {level=1}
Take a real annual plan (a school, a society or a unit will all do), and for three of its targets supply in each case **the mode of verification, the person who verifies, and the consequences of falling short**; if some item in the original cannot be supplied, write why.

::: solution
**Marking:** **the item marked “无法补” ('cannot be supplied') must say which register it belongs to** (**in most cases a speech text, or the register of publicity;** **this shows that the item should not appear in a plan (items ① and ⑤ of the six elements above).**
:::

::: exercise Short essay: verifiability and ethics {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing “**现代组织之所以大量生产可核验的说法，是因为它已经无法核验动机；而一旦可核验成为唯一被承认的形态，未被度量的部分就会被系统性地低估，直到无人再记得它曾被做过。因此应用文写作里的‘具体’是一种道德要求而不是一种文体偏好：它要求作者只说那些自己愿意被别人拿去检查的话。这也解释了为什么最熟练的文书能够用完全真实的数字讲述一个完全错误的故事**” 'The reason a modern organisation produces verifiable statements in such quantity is that it can no longer verify motives; and once the verifiable has become the only form that is recognised, the part that has not been measured is systematically undervalued, until no one any longer remembers that it was ever done. The "specific" of practical writing is therefore a moral requirement and not a preference of genre: it requires the author to say only those things which they themselves are willing to have others take away and check. This also explains why the most practised document can tell an entirely wrong story with entirely true figures.' (Our translation.) You must draw on **the six elements and the mode of acceptance**, **after an indicator has become a target**, **the experience column**, **three kinds of distortion**, **separation of the three columns** and **the ladder of strength**, at least these six; and you must decide: **in an age when the party being assessed can also verify, what is the bottom line of the ethics of writing — you must give one rule that can be written into a template, and explain how it would be circumvented**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**acceptance, the alienation of indicators, experience, distortion, the three columns, strength**); the template rule (**a rule that qualifies must be one sentence that can be carried out, for example: “凡出现数字，其后紧跟括注（口径／时点／来源）；凡出现结论，须标注其属于事实、推断或建议三类之一；凡引用他人意见，须给出人数与访谈时间。” 'Whenever a figure appears, a parenthetical note follows immediately (counting rule / time point / source); whenever a conclusion appears, it must be marked as one of the three classes, fact, inference or recommendation; whenever another person's opinion is cited, the number of people and the time of the interview must be given.' (Our translation.) — marking looks at whether the rule only increases the cost of writing and does not increase the risk to the person being verified; otherwise it will certainly be circumvented**); the manner of circumventing it (**it must be specific:** for example, filling in every parenthetical note as the rule requires, yet choosing a different counting rule (**replacing the denominator with the number of people who have already applied**) or swapping the indicator (**replacing “办结时长” ('time taken to close a case') with “首办响应时长” ('time to a response on first handling')**); the correct formulation of the bottom line is therefore not “格式合规” ('compliance as to format'), but **“同一事项连续多年保持口径不变，且口径变更须另行说明理由”** ('For the same matter the counting rule is kept unchanged over successive years, and a change of counting rule must have its reasons stated separately.') (Our translation.) (**this rule can be audited, because it turns the change itself into a thing that has to be explained**); if the conclusion can be written as “**所以本章的终点是一条很窄的职业伦理：不比人会说，要比人肯把自己说过的话交出去检查；文书学能做到的极限，就是让交出检查这件事有固定格式**” 'So the end point of this chapter is a very narrow professional ethic: do not compete at who is able to speak; compete at who is willing to hand what they have said over to be checked. The limit of what document studies can do is to give a fixed format to this business of handing something over to be checked.' (Our translation.), that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Filed by time: the plan (**no mode of acceptance, and it is a declaration of stance**), precision (**it can extend only to the next date that can be checked**), the summary (**the experience column is the only asset; in the problems column, one item must be one's own**), distortion (**ratio, average, taking a point for the whole**), the investigation report (**the three columns of fact, inference and recommendation kept apart**), figures (**counting rule, time point, sampling frame**). The method in one sentence: **write only those things you are willing to have others take away and check**. Next chapter: news writing — turn the inverted pyramid upside down.
