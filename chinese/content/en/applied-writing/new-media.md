---
title: "Writing for the screen"
---

The readers of the previous eight chapters sat in front of you, or were obliged to reply in writing; the reader of this chapter is on a six-inch screen, gives you only three lines, and may delete you at any moment — **the argument of this chapter is: new media has not invented any new fault of writing; it has only raised the reward for every old fault tenfold, and at the same time brought the cost of correction down almost to zero**.

## Definition: three hard constraints {#def-santiao}

::: definition Three lines, the algorithm and instant feedback {#def-yueshu}
**① Length is cut into three pieces** (**the headline, the cover image, and the first few dozen characters visible on the first screen;** everything outside these three has to be won (**the previous chapter said that the first reader of the inverted pyramid is the layout editor;** today the first reader is the distribution system — **it reads the headline and the opening paragraph, and decides, on behalf of a million people, whether to let them see it** (② **the distributor is not present** (**you do not know your editor, and he has no name either;** he is a set of rules ranked by interaction rate (**and so the object of writing changes from ‘the reader’ to ‘the reader’s next action’** (**this lesson’s definition:** this is the first time in the history of writing that the principal evaluator of a text neither reads it nor is responsible for it (**Chapter 1’s ‘目录即审查’ (‘a catalogue is censorship’) has here an automated descendant: censorship no longer needs anyone to turn up in the night** (③ **feedback at the same speed as the text** (**the comment area is part of the text;** the meaning a passage acquires elsewhere, after it has been taken in a screenshot, is also its meaning (**what this chapter later calls ‘截图友好’ (‘screenshot-friendly’) will develop this point** (**the three constraints together give a single criterion:** writing on a screen is a kind of writing completed in other people’s hands — **so its professional ethics is no longer ‘write it clearly’, but ‘still stand after it has been taken out of context’.**
:::

::: definition Four kinds of headline and one criterion of delivery {#def-sizhong}
**The information headline** (**gives a change that can be checked, and two endpoints (the news criterion of the previous chapter**); **the suspense headline** (**hides one key item, and opens a gap;** ③ **the benefit headline** (**‘三句话让你省下××’ (‘three sentences and you save ××’ (Our translation.)): it promises a deliverable gain;** ④ **the emotion headline** (**names a feeling and recruits those who feel the same** (**this lesson has only one criterion, but it is a hard one:** whether the body, within three paragraphs, answers the question the headline has created; **if it has answered, it is technique; if it has not, it is fraud rather than style** (**this one can be quantified:** look at the drop-off position — **the curve of reader loss that most platforms supply can be located, inside the body, on the very sentence that was not made good** (**received account: completion rate, average time spent, and drop-off position are the ordinary indicators of a content platform** (**this lesson neither prettifies these numbers nor scorns them; it only points out how they are used:** they are the only record that can tell you ‘at which sentence you lost a stranger’ (**the author of the previous chapter’s publicity piece cannot get hold of such a thing; the author of this chapter can, and will not look — this is this chapter’s most cutting sentence of judgement on writing for new media, and also its kindest.**
:::

::: definition The sentence that can be screenshotted {#def-jietu}
**A new rhetorical unit** (**the unit of design was once the whole piece, then the paragraph, and is now a sentence that can be taken in a screenshot on its own;** its conditions are that it be short, that it take a position, and that it remain complete once detached from its context (**three consequences** (① **the unit of quotation has replaced the structure of the argument** (**a piece’s real asset is those three sentences of it that may be forwarded;** and so writing begins to be optimised for the sentence rather than for the argument (**and argument is slow, conditional, and full of ‘but’;** the sentence that can be screenshotted is the opposite: it has to be unconditional (**this is what this chapter calls ‘the economics of being taken out of context’:** you know, while you are writing, that you will be taken out of context, and so you cut yourself in advance into a shape that can be cut (**received account: this kind of thing is often summed up as ‘fragmentation’; this lesson refuses that diagnosis:** it is not that attention has grown shorter; it is that the unit of transmission has grown smaller; and the unit of transmission has always been decided by the channel, from the number of characters on a bamboo slip to the width of a column in a newspaper (**what Chapter 1 and Chapter 14 call ‘the format of distribution changes the content’ is here reproduced in full** (② **remaining complete once detached from context is a dangerous requirement of design** (**it means that every sentence you write must withstand two readings: the reading you give it, and the reading someone else gives it** (**this lesson’s technical answer:** write the qualifier into the sentence that will be screenshotted; **do not write ‘这种做法必须反对’ (‘this practice must be opposed’ (Our translation.)); write ‘在××情形下、未经××程序的做法，我反对’ (‘a practice under circumstances ××, and without procedure ××, I oppose’ (Our translation.)) —** the latter is five characters shorter, and yet it makes the screenshot impossible to use as some other sentence (**this is the only form in which the previous chapter’s ‘a qualification is a bumper’ still works in the age of the screen** (③ **the balance sheet of a view** (**a sentence that has been forwarded in great numbers will be brought out, at every remark you make afterwards, for the account to be squared** (**so this chapter’s professional advice is one sentence only:** do not write a sentence you do not intend to answer for across a hundred years; **the sentence sounds exaggerated; its mechanism is plain:** a screen does not rot, whereas most of the lies of the age of paper were pardoned by the rotting of the paper (**Chapter 8’s ‘the luck of the archive’ is here turned over:** today’s problem is that the luck of the archive is too good.
:::

::: definition Correction, deletion and the labelling of advertisements {#def-gengzheng}
**① A correction must quote the original sentence** (**received account: current regulations relating to content and advertising require that paid promotion be prominently marked as an advertisement; professional norms on the side of news and publishing require that a correction be timely, and in a comparable position** (**this lesson’s explanation:** an advertisement is labelled not so that the reader will kindly filter it for you, but so that your two kinds of utterance do not pass for each other: **once a commercial recommendation is mixed with a personal judgement and left unlabelled, what you lose is not the trust of one particular occasion, but the credibility of all those sentences that were not labelled** (② **silently deleting a post is the worst kind of correction** (**it changes history without recording the change (the previous chapter has already said so; one mechanism is added here:** deletion on a screen does not make the text disappear; it only makes the text lose the author’s version control — **the deleted screenshot goes on circulating, and from then on nobody can correct its meaning** (**so the only practice this chapter can accept is: keep the original, correct it prominently, and say what was changed;** if it must be taken down, leave a line even so: ‘本文已于某日撤下，原因如下’ (‘this piece was withdrawn on such-and-such a day; the reason is as follows’ (Our translation.)) (**this is the minimum realisation, on a platform, of the previous chapter’s ‘correct it in one letter, and keep one new version’** (③ **three things in a reprint** (**authorisation, naming the source, and not changing the headline** (**received account: the provisions concerning the right of communication on information networks, and trade custom, turn upon these three points** (**this lesson’s account of the mechanism:** reprinting under a changed headline is a transaction that leaves the gain with the reprinter and the responsibility with the original author; **so to judge whether a platform is reliable, look at one thing only —** whether it has ever borne, for you, something you wrote wrongly (**this sentence applies equally to a platform, to an institution, and to the first piece of content you post yourself.**
:::

::: definition Subscription accounts and service accounts: the open loop and the closed loop {#def-dingyue}
**Received account** (**platforms built around accounts distinguish two kinds: one pushes content to subscribers on a cycle; one is used to transact business, and can reach users who already exist** (**this lesson recognises at once that this is the table in Chapter 4** (① **the push type is an open-loop document** (**it asks only to be received, and does not ask for a receipt;** it therefore slides naturally towards ‘once it has been sent, it counts as done’, and the indicator by which it is assessed is naturally the number of pieces posted (**this is the platform version of the previous chapter’s ‘an open-loop genre is speech given free’, and it is also why content of the push type is always emptier than content of the receipt type** (② **the transaction type is a closed-loop document** (**it requires an action: to confirm, to pay, to book, to reply;** and so it has a time limit, a default value, and a receipt (**this lesson’s reading:** on one and the same technology, the two genres do not live equally long — **the open-loop part is quickly exhausted by the algorithm; the closed-loop part becomes infrastructure** (**this explains a common phenomenon: the function of an account that has the most value over the long term is often those three entrances, in its menu, for getting something done** (③ **a mass send is not a private message** (**to put an announcement into a one-to-one conversation is a confusion of permissions (Chapter 2’s ‘copying in is an illocutionary act’, recurring on the mobile side** (**this lesson’s advice:** before every mass send, first ask whether it is a notice, a request, or publicity — **the three purposes correspond to three headlines, three endings, and three settings of whether a receipt is required.**
:::

::: example Audit a viral piece {#ex-baokuan}
The headline: ‘我劝你千万别去××上班，除非你符合这三点’ (‘I urge you, on no account go to work at ××, unless you meet these three points.’ (Our translation.)); the first fifty characters of the body: ‘昨天收到一条留言，看完我沉默了……’ (‘Yesterday a message came in; when I had finished it, I fell silent…’ (Our translation.)). Please carry out three audits: **① what question the headline creates, and whether the body answers it within three paragraphs; ② which sentence is most likely to be taken in a screenshot, and how the meaning deforms once it has been; ③ a compliant revision that does not lose the power to spread**.

::: solution
**① The delivery check** — **the headline opens three gaps: the reason for not going, the definition of the three points, and the condition constituted by ‘除非’ (‘unless’);** ‘除非’ makes the headline a form that any content at all can fulfil (**because, so long as three items are listed, any reason counts as an answer**) — **this is the steadiest armour of clickbait: what it promises is a quantity, not a content** (**if, six paragraphs in, the body is still circling inside the mood of ‘沉默了’ (‘fell silent’), then this chapter’s criterion is met at once:** **it is fraud rather than style** (② **the sentence most likely to be screenshotted** — **‘我劝你千万别去××上班’ (‘I urge you, on no account go to work at ××’ (Our translation.));** once it is in a screenshot it loses the conditional clause ‘除非’, and the sentence changes from advice with a condition attached into a prohibition, and may be taken as an accusation against one particular organisation (**this is precisely ‘the economics of being taken out of context’:** it is not that someone else has distorted you; it is that, in designing it, you placed the qualifier where it would not be screenshotted (**the revision: write the condition into the sentence that will be screenshotted —** ‘三类人我不建议去××：需要稳定排班的人、抗拒重复劳动的人、以及把这份工作当过渡的人；其余人我认识不少做得很好的’ (‘Three kinds of people I do not advise to go to ××: people who need a stable rota, people who resist repetitive labour, and people who are treating this job as a transition; of the rest, I know quite a few who have done very well.’ (Our translation.)) (**it is equally short, it equally takes a position, but it remains honest when it circulates on its own** (③ **three practical revisions** — **first, change the headline into a question that can be checked (**‘××岗位的三个真实门槛：排班、重复劳动与晋升周期’ (‘Three real thresholds of the ×× post: the rota, repetitive labour, and the promotion cycle.’ (Our translation.))**); second, let the first fifty characters give two endpoints and one source (**‘访谈了 11 名在职与离职员工，其中 4 人任职超过三年’ (‘Eleven employees in post and employees who had left were interviewed, of whom four had held the post for more than three years.’ (Our translation.))**); third, if the piece contains a commercial collaboration, label it where it first appears (**received account: current regulations require paid promotion to be prominently marked ‘广告’ (‘advertisement’)**) (**Marking: whoever can point out that the form ‘除非’ makes the headline unfalsifiable scores an A for this chapter — **this is the refutability criterion of the third course, applied to a single line of headline;** **and it is at the same time this chapter’s blessing to everyone who writes for new media:** **only a sentence that can be refuted will not be misunderstood.**
:::

::: intuition Writing without an archive {#def-dang}
**An observation** (**on new media almost nobody ‘revises’: a text is either left as it was, or deleted;** a platform usually keeps an extremely short editing window, and a notice that it ‘已被修订’ (‘has been revised’) (**three consequences** (① **no version, no history** (**you cannot say when you changed your mind;** and so the author’s voice looks consistent for ever, and consistency is not honesty, it is the absence of an archive (**Chapter 6 says that ‘a history rewritten from one side only will certainly be met with a demand that the versions be produced’; here there is not even a version** (② **it makes both plagiarism and self-plagiarism hard to prove** (**how far a time of publication can be trusted depends on the platform’s record;** this is why ‘截图为证’ (‘the screenshot as proof’), on a screen, is both the principal evidence and the weakest (**③ it shifts responsibility from the author to the channel** (**to delete it is for it not to exist; and ‘not existing’ leaves any discussion without an object** (**this lesson’s conclusion:** in an environment that does not keep versions, the author must take the work of the archive upon himself: **archive the original as it stood when it was published, write the correction as a new item and link it to the old one, and write down his most important sentences together with their dates** (**this is not fastidiousness; it is the thing that the ten courses of this curriculum have handed over again and again:** you cannot control how other people quote you; you can only control whether you have a base text you can produce — **and on new media that base text is one you must copy out yourself, because nobody is under any obligation to keep it for you.**
:::

::: history Five changes of trade {#sec-gaixin}
**The 1990s** (**websites and forums: for the first time, writing had an instant comment area; the relation between editor and author was postponed into a new post, the ‘版主’ (‘moderator’)** (received account (② **around 2005** (**blogs brought the long essay and the personal column back to the centre, and brought with them a first question: who is to check** (received account (③ **2009–2012** (**Weibo wrote a limit on length into the genre** (**this is the second time, since the bamboo slip, that ‘a physical parameter determines the length of the sentence’** (**received account: account-based push platforms then went online, and divided subscription and the handling of business into two kinds of interface** (**the previous chapter’s ‘by what unit it is sold’ becomes here ‘by what interface it is distributed’** (④ **around 2016** (**short video and live streaming: writing degenerates into the script and the subtitle;** the script to be spoken aloud, the subtitle bar and the picture caption become three genres that translate one another (received account (⑤ **the 2020s** (**algorithmic recommendation comes to dominate distribution, and a share of advertising revenue makes ‘time spent’ the unit of pricing** (**this lesson’s decisive turn:** you no longer write for the reader; you write for the duration; **and duration is an indicator that rewards neither truth nor goodness** (**Chapter 3’s ‘once an indicator becomes the target’, applied to the content industry** (⑥ **now** (**generative tools enter the chain by which copy is supplied: writing, accompanying images, summaries and replies to comments can all be automated** (**received account:** **the last institutional judgement this lesson gives the whole course:** when the marginal cost of production and the marginal cost of consumption both tend towards zero, what is scarce changes its kind — **experience that can be verified, work that can be opened, a person who can be questioned, and an author willing to leave the versions** (**this is not a word of comfort; it is the conclusion of this course: from Homer’s singer to you in front of the screen, literature and technology have changed a great deal, and what has been scarce has always been these two things:** **that it can be said clearly, and that it can be produced.**

::: warning Four pitfalls of this chapter
**Treating the hook as a promise** (**a headline of the ‘除非’ (‘unless’) type cannot be falsified, and therefore cannot be trusted either (the example above:** only a sentence that can be refuted will not be misunderstood (**)**); **treating ‘网传’ (‘as circulated online’) as a source** (**the previous chapter’s three conditions for anonymity are not relaxed on the screen;** and the screen makes the number of times a rumour is reprinted look like evidence (received norms require a reprint to be traced to its source (**)**); **putting deletion in place of correction** (**it makes the text lose version control rather than lose its influence (responsibility ② above (**)); leaving the advertorial unlabelled (what it steals is not only the trust of the present occasion, but the credibility of every sentence of yours that was not labelled (labelling ① above: whoever mixes a commercial recommendation with a personal judgement and does not mark it, thereafter has every sentence of praise taken at a discount.

::: exercise Short essay: producibility {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing the claim that ‘**the screen has changed the direction of the two costs of writing: the cost of making an error has fallen, because a text can be deleted at any moment; the cost of being misunderstood has risen, because a text can be cut out at any moment. Any platform that rewards only the speed of publication, and does not require producibility, will push the writer to one and the same place: sentences that are useful at this moment and cannot be claimed later. The professional ethics of writing for new media therefore cannot be built on “conscience”; it can only be built on a procedure that can be produced: the archive, the version, the source and the correction — they do not make a person better; they only make a bad remark harder to write**’. You must draw on at least six items: **the three hard constraints**, **the criterion of delivery**, **the sentence that can be screenshotted**, **deletion and correction**, **the three things in a reprint**, **the open loop and the closed loop**; and you must decide **whether the procedure that ‘makes a bad remark harder to write’ can be carried out at the level of one person (how much force one person’s archive can have — you must give a design at the level of the platform, and say at what point its real tension with ‘freedom of speech’ lies**.

::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**constraints, delivery, the screenshot, deletion, reprinting, the open and closed loops**); the design of the platform (**an answer that passes must be a default setting that can be audited:** for example, keeping by default a history of versions that can be consulted, and opening to the reader an entrance to ‘本文已被修订’ (‘this piece has been revised’); a reprint must carry an identifier of its source and the original headline; the correction and the original text are displayed in the same place; and the author is given an archive tool that exports, in one action, the whole of his own historical text (**marking looks for whether the answer includes the item ‘作者可导出’ (‘the author can export’) —** this is the only procedure that works at the level of the individual, because it moves the cost of archiving from the individual back to the platform**); the tension (**it must be stated in writing that this is not the abstract conflict of ‘freedom vs regulation’, but one concrete risk: a history of versions, and the function of export, make a temporary error permanent, and thereby inhibit trial and error and candid revision — **a person who knows that every sentence of his can be produced may write more conservatively** (**this is precisely the inherent cost of a system of verifiability, and this chapter never pretends that it has none**); ways of easing it that can be used: **limit the range within which the history can be seen, and the period for which it is kept; allow an author to correct of his own accord and under his own name, without remaining permanently searchable; and, inside the indicators, distinguish ‘the number of corrections’ from ‘the number of errors’** (**count the putting-right as performance, and not as a stain**); if the conclusion can say ‘**so what this chapter at last hands the reader is not a technique but a habit: take being able to produce the text as the default setting, and delete, while they are still in your own hands, the sentences that cannot be produced — if a course can leave only one sentence, let it be this: do not write a sentence you would not in future dare to acknowledge, because the screen remembers what you have said better than you do**’, that is an A.

::: summary
A screen archive: constraints (**the first reader is the distribution system**), the headline (**it must deliver within three paragraphs**), the screenshot (**write the qualifier into the sentence that will circulate on its own**), deletion (**changing history without recording the change**), reprinting (**authorisation, source, the original headline**), the open and closed loops (**an entrance for getting something done outlives a push**), the archive (**no version, no history**). One sentence for the whole course: **the morality of practical writing is that the person bound by it can read it, can question it, and knows by whom he is bound — from an IOU to a push, this sentence holds in the same way.**

:::
