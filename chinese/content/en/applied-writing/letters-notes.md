---
title: "Letters and notes"
---

The previous chapter said that practical writing is an institutional act; this chapter starts from the two smallest kinds — **a letter is the absence of presence, and a note is evidence from the time of presence: the whole of the former's format is there to make up for people not being together, and every required element of the latter is there to pre-write the testimony for the worst day the relationship will have**.

## Definition: the format of a letter is a stand-in device {#def-buwei}

::: definition Five-part structure and what it does {#def-wuduan}
**The received account.** Traditional letters fall roughly into five parts: the form of address and the opening honorific, the opening formula, the body, the closing courtesy and the good wishes, and the signature and the date. **What each part does.** ① **The form of address and the opening honorific** (提称语). “尊前” ('before one who is respected'), “崇鉴” ('for your lofty inspection') and “勋鉴” ('for your meritorious inspection') are for elders and for official circles; “台鉴” ('for your inspection'), “惠鉴” ('for your kind inspection') and “大鉴” ('for your honoured inspection') are for equals; “道鉴” ('for your learned inspection') and “讲席” ('at your lecture-seat') are for academic circles; “膝下” ('at your knee') is for one's parents; “如晤” ('as if meeting') is for a younger generation (the received account: in the letter-ritual manuals of the Ming and Qing, *shūyí* 书仪, formulae of this kind were classified in fine detail; today they have been greatly simplified to “您好”, 'hello'). — **This lesson's definition:** the opening honorific is a table of rank that must be filled in on the spot: **it does not express feeling; it declares a relationship** (the claim of Chapter 8 of linguistics, “称呼语是一根裸露的社会语法” ('a form of address is social grammar laid bare') (Our translation.), is made good in this lesson: **write one character wrong, and the other person knows at once what relationship you think the two of you have**). ② **The opening formula.** Phrases of the kind “敬禀者” ('respectfully reporting') and “久疏侯问” ('long since I enquired after you'); **its job is to acknowledge the delay:** first apologise for not being present, then come to the business (this step is peculiar to the letter; in email, what corresponds to it is “抱歉这么晚回复”, 'sorry to reply so late'). ③ **The body.** One matter at a time is best; **if one paragraph holds three matters, the person replying will answer only the last** (this is the chapter's technical explanation of “邮件没有回音”, 'the email brought no reply' (Our translation.)). ④ **The closing courtesy and the good wishes.** “肃此敬颂钧安” ('respectfully ending here, and wishing your honoured peace'), “顺颂时祺” ('and so wishing you the well-being of the season'), “即请台安” ('and herewith wishing you peace'). **Their pragmatic function is to close the topic:** to tell the other person that no reply is needed, or that a reply is hoped for (omit them, and the letter is in an “unclosed” state, and is easily set aside). ⑤ **The signature and the date.** Put one's name to it, add “敬上” ('respectfully submitted'), and write the date; **the date is the only required element of a letter that cannot be left out, because it nails this document to the chain of cause and effect** (the section on notes will press the point harder).
:::

::: definition Taige, name taboo, and rank on the page {#def-raige}
**The received account.** In former times, letters and official documents showed respect by “抬” ('raising'): *píngtái* 平抬 ('flat raising') is to begin a new line flush with the top of the margin; *nuótái* 挪抬 ('shifted raising') is to leave one space blank; characters used for elders and in forms of address were often under name taboo. **Three observations in this lesson.** ① **Rank is written on the layout.** Without reading the words, the blank spaces alone tell you who is superior: **this is the purest specimen of format as a map of power** (the previous chapter's “抬格是格式的极端形态” ('*taige* is the extreme form of format') (Our translation.) here comes down onto a single sheet of letter paper). *Táigé* 抬格 is that raising. ② **It is extremely efficient, and extremely fragile.** The moment the relation of rank changes, the whole layout becomes an offence at once; **so, after the imperial examinations were abolished and the state was changed to a republic, *taige* was among the first courtesies to be abolished** (the received account: reform of layout has always come before political reform, or together with it, because layout is more visible). ③ **What remains today.** We still use some low-cost *taige*: in an email, writing the other person's name on the first line, putting the request in the first paragraph, and writing the sign-off in full. **This lesson's judgement:** politeness has not disappeared; it has only changed its way of encoding; **and every change of code will cause a person who does not know the new code to be misread as rude** (this sentence is the key to the whole chapter. It explains every verdict you have received of the kind “他这人不会说话” ('this person does not know how to speak') (Our translation.)).
:::

::: definition The subject line, the cc and the bcc {#def-biaoti}
**The subject line simply is the matter** (事由). The three elements of an official-document title are the issuing organ, the matter and the kind of document; an email subject can use the same formula: “请审核××合同（12 月 5 日前回复）” ('Please review the ×× contract (reply before 5 December)') (Our translation.) is ten times better than “合同” ('contract'); **the first function of the subject line is to let the other person decide the priority without having to call on you.** ② **Copying someone in is not looking on; it is a performative.** Adding someone to the cc is to declare “此事已有见证” ('this matter now has a witness') (Our translation.); **it changes the nature of the letter on the spot:** the same sentence, with a cc and without one, is two documents (this is why “我抄送一下领导” ('I'll just cc the leadership') (Our translation.) is often experienced as a kind of attack: **it really does change the administrative conditions of the act**). ③ **A blind copy is a forged parallel document.** The bcc makes the same sentence have different pragmatic effects for different recipients; **once it is seen through, the loss is not on this matter, but on the rate at which everything you state afterwards is marked down** (the reverse of Course 3's “幸存者打八折” ('survivors are marked down to eighty per cent') (Our translation.): a person seen through once must have everything they say afterwards marked down to eighty per cent). **This lesson's verdict:** the bcc is a transaction that uses one convenience to buy out long-term credit, and in the great majority of cases the price is bad. ④ **How to write the duty to reply.** “收到请回复” ('please reply on receipt') (Our translation.) is rude because it adds an obligation from one side only and gives no reason; **the better way is to write the default out:** “如 12 月 5 日前未获回复，我处将按第 2 方案执行” ('If no reply has been received by 5 December, this office will proceed under Option 2') (Our translation.) — **this one sentence at once gives a deadline, gives a reminder, and gives the other person a right not to reply** (the “请求的合格条件” ('felicity conditions of a request') of Course 7, pragmatics, is here only a matter of one line).
:::

::: definition Electronic data is not lighter than paper {#def-dianzi}
**The received account.** In the handling of disputes, records of electronic communication are one form of evidence that can be admitted; and their integrity depends on the platform, the time and the original carrier. **Three points of practice.** ① **You think you have deleted it; in fact it has only been copied away.** The other party's screenshot, the platform's backup and a forward by the other party's colleague make three copies or more; **so the tone of an email is not “反正没人看” ('no one will look anyway'), but “会被打印给第三方看” ('it will be printed for a third party to see')** (Our translation.). ② **The traces of a deletion or an alteration cost more than the deletion or the alteration.** The received account: whether electronic evidence is accepted turns on whether it is complete, and on whether it could have been tampered with. **So do not, on a document already sent, make the move of “悄悄改一下” ('quietly changing it a little'):** one letter that corrects it, and a new version kept, is the proper path for a document (this is the same principle as Chapter 6's “总结只写成绩是宣传” ('a summary that writes down only the successes is publicity') (Our translation.): **whoever rewrites history from one side alone will be asked to produce the versions**). ③ **A formal letter should go by a formal channel.** Email is for leaving a trace; instant messaging is for negotiation; **to leave a key agreement only inside a chat box is to write the contract on a napkin.** **This lesson offers one formula to remember:** negotiation may be as one pleases; for the thing to be concluded, it must land on paper; what lands on paper may be short, but it must be retrievable.
:::

::: definition The twelve required elements of a loan note {#def-jietiao}
**The received account.** In a dispute over a private loan, a loan note (借条) with probative force should usually contain the following. **This lesson lists twelve items:** ① the name written as “借条” ('loan note') (**do not write “欠条” ('debt note'), “收据” ('receipt'), or mix “借条” and “欠条”**); ② the borrower's full name, consistent with the identity document (**a nickname, a pet name, or the work unit's name used in place of a person, are all future grounds of defence**); ③ the identity-card number; ④ the lender's name; ⑤ the amount (**in complex numerals and ordinary numerals together, and the two consistent**); ⑥ the currency; ⑦ the means of delivery (**cash or transfer; for a transfer, write the account, and keep the voucher**); ⑧ the interest rate (**the received account: whether interest was agreed directly affects whether interest can be claimed**); ⑨ the deadline for repayment; ⑩ the purpose of the loan (**a loan whose unlawful purpose is known is not protected; the received account**); ⑪ the date of issue and the borrower's signature (**a fingerprint pressed where necessary**); ⑫ the number of copies and who keeps them (**the received account:**) — **four technical notes from this lesson.** First, **“今借到” ('have now received as a loan') is not the same as “今借” ('now borrow'):** the former contains a statement that delivery has already taken place (**this is an admission of the fact of delivery**). Second, **the complex numerals are an anti-forgery technique** (壹贰叁肆伍陆柒捌玖拾佰仟万): **the received account has two theories of their origin, one that it is no later than the Wu Zhou, one that they were a Ming device against malpractice over money and grain; this lesson does not adjudicate, and records only the function — to turn a numeral, to which one stroke can be added, into a sentence that is hard to alter** (the procedural version of Course 8's “押韵就是公证” ('rhyme is notarisation') (Our translation.)). Third, **the date must be handwritten, and complete down to the day:** a loan note whose date has been altered will have its probative force called into question. Fourth, **one loan note cannot have two or more people “handling” it (经手) with no division of roles:** whether the two who sign together are joint borrowers or a guarantor, the liability is completely different (the received account: **this is precisely Chapter 1's line “未写主语与受文者” ('the subject and the addressee were not written') (Our translation.), returning in the private sphere: whoever bears the obligation must be named**).
:::

::: definition The receipt, the request for leave, and the two characters “结清” {#def-shouju}
**The receipt** (收据). State what was received, the quantity and the specification, the nature of the sum, the unit and the person who handled it, and the date; if a separate invoice is needed, or if tax is already included, that should be noted (**this lesson's criterion:** the column for the nature of the sum is the real legal document: “预付款” ('advance payment'), “货款” ('payment for goods'), “结算款” ('settlement sum'), “代管款” ('funds held for another') and “赠与” ('gift') each correspond to a different obligation to return what was received). ② **“结清” ('settled in full') is a disposition of a right.** To write “双方账目已结清” ('the accounts of both parties have been settled in full') (Our translation.) on a receipt is, in one sentence, to extinguish a batch of claims not yet asserted (**it is more effective than any explanation, and harder to revoke than any explanation**). **This lesson's ethical clause:** a right that can be given up in one sentence ought not to be yielded inside a text the other party has drafted — **so this chapter requires you to read the last line first.** ③ **The request for leave** (请假条). Its accurate name is “an application to suspend performance of the contract”; four items are therefore indispensable: **the time of starting and of ending (down to the day, and down to the half-day)**, the reason (it may be brief, but it must be checkable), who takes over the work during the period, and a means of contact (**a request for leave that lacks “who takes over” has as its consequence that the leave is interrupted by the telephone:** this is not luck; it is a hole in the document). ④ **The message note and the notice note** (留言条, 告知条). This is the only genre in practical writing that allows the colloquial, but it still requires three things: **who, what matter, and what is wanted before what time** (**lack one, and it becomes a greeting that needs a call back**).
:::

::: definition The chain of proof: how a document becomes a kind of tax {#def-zhengming}
**The received account.** In the second half of the twentieth century, the letter of introduction long served as a voucher of personal identity and of credit; as the system of identity documents and the manner of population movement changed, it withdrew from everyday use. **This lesson does not write nostalgia; it writes structure.** ① **The essence of a letter of introduction is to move credibility from the relationship onto the document.** A person is believed not because someone stands guarantor, but because there is a sheet of paper bearing a seal (**this is one of the greatest inventions of modern administration, and also its heaviest burden**). ② **When every document needs another document to prove it, the document becomes a tax.** The received account: every so many years there appears a complaint of the kind “证明我妈是我妈” ('prove that my mother is my mother') (Our translation.), and afterwards many places have carried out reforms of “减证便民” ('cut the certificates, and make it easier for the public') and of the notification-and-commitment system (告知承诺制). **This lesson's technical analysis:** the notification-and-commitment system replaces “proof in advance” with “checking afterwards, plus penalties for bad faith”; **it moves the cost off the person doing the business and onto the authority, and therefore requires the authority actually to check** (**so whether a reform that cuts certificates is real is seen by whether it has at the same time increased the budget for checking**). Chapter 1's “谁付转换成本” ('who pays the conversion cost') (Our translation.) has a clean test here. ③ **A ladder of the strength of evidence.** An oral statement < a message note < a loan note with a signature < a loan note plus a voucher of transfer < a voucher of a right that has been notarised or registered. **This lesson's definition of writing:** writing is not recording; it is encryption — **every required element you write in extra today is a stretch of testimony in some future dispute; and it is cheapest while the relationship is still good, which is also when no one is willing to write it** (**this is where the moral value of practical writing lies:** it is an advance payment made for the worst day).
:::

::: example An evidence audit of a loan note {#ex-zhudu}
“今借到张某人民币伍万元整，年底前归还。李某。” 'Received today as a loan from a certain Zhang, renminbi fifty thousand yuan exactly, to be returned before the end of the year. A certain Li.' (Our translation.) Point out seven risks, and rewrite it as an enforceable text (the short style may be kept).

::: solution
**Seven risks.** ① “张某” carries no information from an identity document, so someone of the same name can raise a defence; ② there is no identity-card number; ③ the amount is only in complex numerals and not in ordinary ones, and it is not written whether “伍万元” is the currency; ④ “年底前” ('before the end of the year') is ambiguous across the turn of the year (the lunar year, the fiscal year and the calendar year each have a version); ⑤ the means of delivery is not written, so the lender must prove by other evidence the source of the cash and the delivery; ⑥ interest is not written, and a claim made afterwards is often rejected (the received account); ⑦ the purpose is not written, the date of signing is not written, and the number of copies is not written (**there is a further, hidden risk:** “归还” ('to return') does not say to which account it is to be returned, so it may later be claimed that the debt was already discharged in cash). **The rewrite.** “借条：今借到王小明（身份证号××）人民币 50 000 元（伍万元整），于 2025 年 2 月 1 日前归还至其××银行账户（尾号××）；月利率 0.5%，逾期按 1% 计；款项用于家庭装修。本借条一式两份，借款人已收到上述转账。借款人：李×（签名并按指印） 2024 年 10 月 20 日” 'Loan note: received today as a loan from Wang Xiaoming (identity-card number ××) renminbi 50,000 yuan (fifty thousand yuan exactly), to be returned before 1 February 2025 to that person's account at ×× Bank (ending ××); monthly interest 0.5 per cent, and if overdue reckoned at 1 per cent; the sum is for decorating the family home. This loan note is in two copies. The borrower has received the transfer stated above. Borrower: Li × (signature and fingerprint). 20 October 2024.' (Our translation.) **How it is marked.** First, do not look at the number of characters; look at the number of required elements: **after the rewrite, the required elements must come to ten or more.** Second, point out one place where “I deleted something” — **for example, writing “before the end of the year” fixed as a specific date deletes the borrower's room to explain things over the coming year.** Write that sentence, and only then have you understood this chapter: **every vagueness on a loan note is room that one party leaves for a future self, and the party who does the writing is usually not the party who loses.** This point also applies to the last email, the next contract, and every occasion on which you are embarrassed to write the thing clearly.
:::
:::

::: intuition Politeness is reinvented, not abolished {#int-liwu}
**An observation that can be checked.** The opening courtesies, the enquiries after well-being and the self-deprecation of the traditional letter are compensatory devices designed for the channel condition “the delay is long, and transmission is hard”; after instant messaging cancelled the delay, these devices were not kept in their original form, but new ones grew: **the time taken to reply became etiquette, “在吗” ('are you there?') became a demand, read-and-not-replied became a way of taking a stance, and the length of one voice message became a rank.** **Three inferences.** ① **Politeness is a technique, not a moral quality.** It is reinvented along with the channel; **so to blame “现在的人不会说话” ('people nowadays do not know how to speak') (Our translation.) is to blame water for no longer being able to freeze.** ② **The new devices hurt people just the same, and they are harder to correct.** The faults of the old courtesy are written on the face of the words, and can be pointed out; the faults of the new courtesy hide in durations and in statuses, and cannot be cited (**“你为什么两小时不回我” ('why did you not reply to me for two hours?') (Our translation.) has no corresponding code of rites to consult**). ③ **One thing a writer ought to do.** In the circle you are in, write the important courtesies into the text: for example, at the first contact, state “本群内工作消息请在当日回复，其余可周末处理” ('for work messages in this group, please reply on the same day; the rest may be dealt with at the weekend') (Our translation.) — **turning unwritten politeness into a text is the only practice that can protect outsiders.** **The last sentence of this lesson:** the long letter will not come back, but it can be rewritten as a well-structured email: acknowledge the delay, talk about only one matter, give the default, and leave one sentence of permission not to reply.
:::

::: history From a foot of silk to cutting certificates {#sec-jianzheng}
**Warring States to Han.** Family letters and official writings on bamboo and wooden slips and on silk; the formulae of the letter took shape when writing materials were costly (the received account: the channel condition “字字计价” ('every character has its price') (Our translation.) explains the compressedness of Classical Chinese (Literary Chinese; *wényán*)). This is the private version of Course 1's “材料决定文体” ('the material determines the genre') (Our translation.). ② **Six Dynasties to Tang.** One work of literary theory devotes a chapter to letters and records (书记); upward letter-forms such as the *qi* 启, the *zhuang* 状 and the *biao* 表 came to a strict sense of the proper degree (the received account: in this period the letter was for the first time discussed as a genre with norms of its own). ③ **Song to Qing.** Manuals of letter etiquette (*shuyi*) circulated: reference books teaching people to choose words according to status; anthologies of letters (*chǐdú* 尺牍) became popular reading (the received account: this lesson's reading is that, when a society comes to contain a great many people who need to deal with strangers, a kind of book on “怎么写信” ('how to write a letter') will sell well; **this sentence holds once in the 1990s, once in the 2010s, and once today**). ④ **1912/1920.** The formulae of official documents were replaced, and the vernacular written style (语体文) was promoted; letters in Classical Chinese and in the vernacular (*báihuà*) coexisted for several decades (the received account: those who had the hardest time in the transition were the people writing the letters: both the old set of opening honorifics and the new might be present, and to choose wrong was to fail in courtesy). **This is the small-scale experience of the previous chapter's “制度层惯性大于语言层” ('inertia at the level of institutions is greater than at the level of language')** (Our translation.). ⑤ **1949 to the 1990s.** Letters of introduction and certificates from the work unit made up a system of credit; at the same time, the family letter was still the main written genre of the ordinary household (the received account: an age of documents centred on the work unit put trust almost entirely into paper). ⑥ **The 2000s to the present.** Text messages, instant messaging and email replaced the everyday letter; “减证便民” and the notification-and-commitment system cut down the matters requiring proof (the received account). **This chapter's closing:** the shrinking of the letter is not a decline of culture, but its being split into three shares: the part that is feeling is handed to chat, the part that is business to email, and the part that is evidence to notes and vouchers; **and this chapter's requirement becomes correspondingly simpler:** of every passage, ask once — which share does it belong to?
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this chapter
**Writing a formal letter in the tone of a chat** (**the form of address and the closing formula are not decoration; they are a declaration of one's standing** (above, the five parts, ①)); **supposing that an email will “反正没人看” ('not be looked at anyway')** (**in law it is admissible, and technically it cannot be recalled** (above, electronic data, ①)); **mixing the loan note, the debt note and the receipt note** (借条, 欠条, 收条) (**the facts the three have to prove are different** (the received account); **to mix them in the writing is to volunteer for a game of proof** (above, the twelve required elements)); **treating the cc as a harmless technical move** (**it changes the administrative nature of the document, and it also changes the other party's position in the bargaining** (above, the cc, ②): **whenever you change the conditions under which someone else acts, you should say so beforehand** — this is the only definition of politeness in this chapter).
:::

::: exercise Giving a letter a check-up {level=1}
Take an email you sent recently and, in 80 characters, point out: **whether its subject line is the matter or a title, how many matters are in the body, and whether the default has been written out**.

::: solution
How it is marked: **the question about the default must be answered with a rewritten sentence** (“如×日前未回复，我将……” 'If there is no reply by day ×, I shall……': if you cannot write it, that shows you do not in fact dare to set a deadline (above, the duty to reply)).
:::

::: exercise Writing a real note {level=2}
Write a loan note for a loan of three thousand yuan between classmates, and a receipt for class funds that have passed through someone's hands, each in no more than eighty characters, but with the required elements complete.

::: solution
How it is marked: **the loan note must contain the date, the full name, the amount in complex and in ordinary numerals, the means of delivery and the term for repayment; the receipt must state the nature of the sum** (**one point off for each item missing;** writing the “nature of the sum” as “钱” ('money') scores zero (above, the twelve required elements and the clause on “结清”)).

::: exercise Short essay: the death of the letter and the birth of the document {level=3}
Write six hundred to a thousand characters, assessing “**书信的衰落不是文笔的衰落，而是它被拆解了：情感交给聊天，事务交给邮件，证据交给凭证。可惜这三样东西各自继承了书信的一部分功能，却没有继承它的慎重——我们在最不必留证的地方留下了最多的记录，又在最需要留证的地方保留了最少的文字。因此教当代人写文书，重心不在文采也不在格式，而在一种新的分配判断：什么值得被固定下来**”. 'The decline of the letter is not a decline of skill with the pen, but its having been taken apart: feeling is handed to chat, business to email, and evidence to vouchers. The pity is that these three things have each inherited one part of the letter's function, and have not inherited its circumspection — we have left the most records where there is least need to keep evidence, and kept the least writing where evidence is most needed. So, in teaching people today to write documents, the centre of gravity is neither in literary grace nor in format, but in a new judgement of distribution: what is worth fixing in place.' (Our translation.) You must call on at least six of these: **the five-part stand-in device**, **taige and the encoding of politeness**, **the cc as a performative act**, **the fact that electronic data cannot be recalled**, **the required elements of a loan note, and encryption**, **the chain of proof as a kind of tax**; and you must adjudicate: **whether “什么值得被固定下来” ('what is worth fixing in place') can be given as a rule that can be taught (you must propose a rule, and use it to judge a real dilemma: for example, whether an oral agreement should be confirmed in writing in the group)**.

::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**the stand-in, the encoding, the cc, the impossibility of recall, the encryption, the tax of proof**); the rule (**the form of a rule that passes is conditional, not emotional —** for example: “凡满足以下两条之一即应固定：**①对方日后可能否认；②否认一旦成立会使你损失金钱、时间或名额。**若两条皆否，则不写，因为文书是要成本的，而过度留痕也是一种税（上方证明税②）” 'Fix it in place if either of the following two holds: ① the other party may later deny it; ② if the denial succeeds, you will lose money, time or a quota. If neither holds, do not write, because a document has a cost, and keeping too many traces is also a kind of tax (above, the tax of proof, ②).' (Our translation.)); the judgement of the dilemma (**an action that can be carried out must be given:** send one message in the group, “按刚才电话所说，我将……，如无异议今日 18 时前告知” 'As was said on the telephone just now, I shall ……; if there is no objection, say so before 18:00 today.' (Our translation.) — **its virtue is that it meets, at the same time, the need to fix the thing in place and the need not to give offence:** it gives the other party a chance to deny, and is therefore not a record made by one side alone; **marking looks at whether the answer points out that the sentence “无异议视为同意” ('no objection counts as agreement') (Our translation.) itself still carries a risk, because it reads silence as a promise** (the received account: silence does not generally constitute a promise) — **whoever can add this stroke scores an A**); if the conclusion can be written “**所以本章最后教的不是怎么写，而是何时必须写：一个成年人应当能为自己说过的话，挑出那几句需要留下证据**” 'So what this chapter teaches at the end is not how to write, but when one must write: an adult ought to be able to pick out, from the things they themselves have said, those few sentences for which evidence needs to be left.' (Our translation.), that is an A.
