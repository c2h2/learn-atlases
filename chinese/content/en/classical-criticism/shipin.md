---
title: "Zhong Rong’s *Grades of Poets*"
---

The last book refused to judge, because it was cataloguing a trade; this one does almost nothing else — **it puts the poets of five centuries into three grades and writes one sentence of reason for each man. The argument of the chapter: criticism became a discipline not when it produced definitions but when it adopted this refutable posture**.

## A book of rankings {#def-pauming}

::: definition Three grades and one sentence of reason {#def-sanpin}
**The standard account**: a treatise on five-character poetry divides some hundred-odd poets from Han to Liang into upper, middle and lower grades, attaching a short assessment to each. Note first what the format does. **① It must commit**: no hedging, no "each has its merits"; **a ranking is a promise**. **② It must give reasons**, and reasons can be wrong whereas praise without reason cannot be discussed at all. **③ It moves disagreement off the man and onto the judgement**: you may object that so-and-so belongs in the middle grade without impugning anybody's character (**standard account**). This is this chapter's first sentence: **to write evaluation in a refutable form is the real invention in the history of criticism — more durable than any single aesthetic thesis**. Set it against the last chapter, which supplied six criteria and withheld their weights. **This book supplies the weights — three grades are the weights.** The two works therefore complement one another, and later ages treated them as rivals: one of the standing misreadings of reception history.
:::

::: definition Direct pursuit {#def-zhixun}
**The standard account**: among the book's much-quoted claims is that, examining the fine lines of ancient and modern poets, most of them are not got by borrowing (**补假**) but by "direct pursuit" (直寻); and it criticises the tonal pedants of the day, whose scruples over the four tones and eight maladies make writing cramped and damage its native beauty. Three layers. **① The textual layer**: "direct pursuit" targets two contemporary vices — the packing of book-learning into lines, and the regulation of sound (**standard account**: this was the air of Qi-Liang prosody; it is the target). **② The theoretical layer**: it claims that the origin of the fine line is the poet's direct encounter with the scene, not the working-over of other people's sentences — a thesis about genesis. **③ The testable layer**: the thesis can be worked against, since many of the "pursued" lines the book itself praises have antecedents in earlier texts; and "direct" is in any case a matter of degree. **This lesson's verdict: "direct pursuit" is a true proposition in a false form.** It rightly opposes a kind of writing in which quantity of reading is the merit; it wrongly supposes an intuition that passes no way through language. The poetry-course dictum that allusion is the appendix to a petition meets its antagonist here, **and that two-sided argument has run for two thousand years because it is really an argument about the teaching of writing: how much of what can be taught is indirect?** Those who insist that only the direct counts have usually already secured an unteachable asset. **This lesson does not let that sentence pass; refute it in the essay.**
:::

## The system of descent {#def-yuanchu}

::: definition An ancestor for every man {#def-zushan}
**The standard account**: the book marks most poets with the source of their manner — "his origin is so-and-so" — and runs several lines back to the *Odes* and to a handful of early writers. Call this the genealogical method, and credit it with three functions and two failures. **Functions: ① it converts scattered resemblance into a narratable relation** — "is like" becomes "inherits", and style acquires a history; **② it is a compression of memory** — to hold a poet's source is to hold half his assessment, and before print that is knowledge infrastructure; **③ it installs a standard** — to say a man comes from X is to make X the measure (**standard account**: the list of originators is simultaneously a canon). **Failures: ① misidentification**: stylistic likeness may come from a shared situation, a shared genre, or coincidence, and a system that cannot distinguish "resembles" from "has read" is writing the history of resemblance, not the history of influence. **② The unplaceable poet**: when a writer belongs to no line, the system must either force him in or push him down**. This lesson advertises the most famous case: the book placed a poet of enormous later prestige in the middle grade, with a source of modest name (**standard account**; the line about his origin is transmitted with the entry). Its two-thousand-year afterlife matters not for the grievance but because it exposes the shape of the system — the next section.
:::

::: definition A poet in the middle grade {#def-zhongpin}
**The standard account**: the book set a poet now universally ranked among the greatest in the middle grade and gave him an unimpressive ancestry; from the Song onwards his standing rose until several critics placed him at the top. This lesson treats the episode as a complete record of a grading failure, in four steps. **① Explanation inside the system**: the weights lay on ornamental brilliance and on "wing-strength"; in that calculus this poet's plainness reads as bareness, and bareness scores low. **That is not prejudice, it is the consequence of weights.** **② The lifespan of the system**: a grade is the output of an evaluative scheme at one moment, and **schemes outlive the poets they grade more rarely than one hopes; the status of a work can move because the weights of the scheme that grades it were changed** — this is the chapter's central law. **③ How posterity changed it**: the Song did not re-rank him so much as replace the weights, **re-describing "unworked" from a defect into an achievement** — the last chapter's case of the defect restated as the ideal, with a textbook instance. **④ The requirement on you**: whenever you read a list — a prize shortlist, a ranking, a mark scheme — ask three questions. **What are the weights? Is the list longer-lived than what it ranks? And who got in not by winning but by changing the weights?** Those three are transferable professional skills.
:::

::: definition Flavour {#def-ziwei}
**The standard account**: the book calls five-character poetry the flavourful among writings, and praises its power to point at things and shape them, to exhaust feeling and write the scene closely. Treat the category "flavour" (**味**) separately, because it is the root metaphor of the next thousand years. **① It puts the verdict in the body**: not "true" but "worth chewing", which yields a new argument form — **a good poem is one you re-read**, a factual claim about you that can be checked. **② It cannot be translated**: you cannot render "has flavour" into a definition, which is both its strength and its trouble; the translation drill from the last chapter is exactly the right tool here. **③ It plants the later vocabulary**: flavour, resonance, interest, spirit-resonance (**standard account**: chapters seven and nine are descendants of this line). This lesson's neutral judgement: **a category that keeps being used without ever being defined must be doing something definitions cannot do — it leaves the burden of judgement on the person who experiences, instead of handing it to a rule**. And once a rule is installed, taste loses its seat. **That is the politics of aesthetic categories, and it is the split the next chapter handles.**
:::

::: example Three readings of one phrase {#ex-pingyu}
Take the abstract form of a typical entry: "**A: this poet's source is such-and-such; B: his manner is spare and quiet; C: he excels in such-and-such subjects**". Say what kind of judgement each of the three is, and whether the other two survive if the first is refuted.

::: solution
**Kinds**. A is a relational claim — a genetic statement, falsifiable by bibliographies, self-reports and contemporary records; **it is the only one of the three with a truth value**. B is a style description: a generalisation that can be supported or weakened by the distribution of features in the corpus but never reduced to zero falsity. C is a statistical claim about subject-matter, and checkable. **If A falls** — suppose it is shown that the poet never read the supposed source — **B and C do not become false, but A's argumentative work vanishes: the description loses its explanation.** Readers therefore keep B and C and delete A, **which is how the genealogical system actually runs day to day: once the classification is dismantled, the description lives on comfortably**. From this, a technical rule for reading any history of criticism: **convert every "influence" claim into a "similarity" claim and see what is left.** If what is left is "they handle the same situation in the same way", then you had an explanation and you still have one — only its evidential requirement is much lower. The logic course's hunt for the alternative variable applies exactly. **Marking: whoever writes "swapping in similarity lowers the evidence requirement" takes an A**. That operation is this course's standard procedure in influence study, and the only step that turns a second-century genealogy into a modern tool (**standard account**: what modern scholars do when debating influence versus coincidence is precisely this).
:::
:::

::: intuition Why lists never go away {#int-bang}
**A stubborn phenomenon**: every generation knows that rankings are stupid, and every generation goes on making them. Four reasons. **① Ranking forces commitment, and commitment produces a space for argument; unranked criticism is ranked secretly, in the length of its paragraphs and the temperature of its adjectives. This lesson's formula: any evaluative system that refuses to rank is ranking in the dark, with its weights unpublished.** **② It manufactures controversy, and controversy is its distribution channel**: a book that put a man in the middle grade gets copied more than one that praised everyone. **③ It is memory infrastructure**: a three-column table of a hundred names is portable knowledge. **④ It fixes the critic's position: whoever publishes a ranking must defend it, and the defence generates theory.** The fourth is the point of the section: **theory does not grow out of praise; it grows out of being asked, in front of people, why that man is only middling.** The sentence holds for viva voce examinations, design reviews, and the list you forwarded in the group chat. **The chapter's lesson in one line: if you want a judgement to produce knowledge, put it in an order someone can object to.** That is why *Grading the Poets* matters more than several finer prose works of its century.
:::

::: history The rise and fall of a book of grades {#sec-shengjiang}
**Qi-Liang transition**: the book is written, its purpose no more than to set grades among five-character poets (**standard account**). **Sui and Tang**: it is quarried for material by classbooks; already there are complaints that some grades are unfitting (**standard account**). **This lesson's point: being attacked proves that the order it set out was taken seriously as a claim.** **Song**: printed editions circulate while a new canon forms elsewhere, **and the middle-grade poet rises** (**standard account**). **Ming**: the archaists and the advocates of nature each quote it as ally — the former taking its account of sources, the latter its attack on borrowed phrases (**standard account**). **This lesson calls it the quartering of one book by two factions, the standard fate of systematic works.** **Eighteenth to nineteenth centuries**: critics link "flavour" forward to the theory of spirit-resonance, and read the genealogical method as a method of literary history (**standard account**). **Twentieth century**: it enters textbooks of "the history of criticism" and of "poetry-talk studies", and acquires the label "the earliest surviving专著 on poetry" (**standard account**). The final caution: **labels of the form "the earliest" are products of institution-building; they depend on which bodies of writing are counted as "about poetry", and that test is a twentieth-century one**. This lesson does not deny the book's value; it asks you to name the office that issued the certificate whenever you use the word "earliest".
:::

::: warning Four traps in this chapter
**Treating "direct pursuit" as a fact** (**it is a proposition against a fashion, and it postulates intuition without language**, the three layers above); **reading "comes from" as "has read"** (**resemblance is not influence**, the two failures); **taking a grade as final** (**the scheme is shorter-lived than the work**, the middle-grade poet, item 2); **accepting a label such as "earliest" without asking who awarded it** (history, final section — **this rule applies to every "first" and every "beginning" in this course**).
:::

::: exercise Essay: ranking produces theory {level=3}
In six hundred to a thousand words, discuss the claim that **any evaluative system that does not rank is ranking in the dark. The academy refuses to seat works in order, and completes the ranking through venues, citations and the length of letters of recommendation; the difference is that its weights are unpublished, hence unanswerable. Zhong Rong's contribution was not his taste but his nerve: he was willing to publish his taste as a list that a hundred posterities could object to. Any criticism today that claims to make no value judgement has simply outsourced the value judgement to an algorithm.** Deploy at least six of: **the format of grade plus reason**, **the three layers of direct pursuit**, **the two failures of the genealogy**, **the case of the middle-grade poet**, **the category of flavour**, **the quartering of the book by two factions**. Then adjudicate: **is publishing a ranking or refusing to rank more defensible morally — and you must take account of the position of those placed at the bottom.**

::: solution
Marking: six mechanisms; the adjudication (**you may take either side, but you must handle the constraint "the position of the bottom-ranked"**). **Publishing ranks**: defensible because the weights are visible and therefore appealable — **this is its one hard merit**; the cost, which must be stated, is that published ranking amplifies boundary cases into injuries of identity, and that once cited the ranking becomes self-fulfilling: **resources flow to the top of the list, and the reasons at the top come to define what counts as good**. **Refusing to rank**: defensible because it avoids that distributional self-fulfilment; the cost is that the weights go underground and are exercised by the unattributed — "outsourced to the algorithm". **The shape of answer this course endorses: publish the weights rather than the ranking** — state the criteria, their shares, and which works the criteria sacrifice. **Ranking may stay inside the committee; the standard must be public and appealable.** That substitution replaces the false question "rank or not" with the real one "who signs, and can one appeal" — **highest marks for this distinction**. **A conclusion able to write "so what Zhong Rong really left us is not a list of middle-grade names but a professional ethic: you may speak very firmly, provided the weights, the grounds and the office of appeal are printed on the same page. Writing a paper, grading staff and choosing a school for your child are three copies of the same form**" earns an A.
:::
