title: "Wang Guowei’s *Remarks on Song Lyrics*"
---

The last chapter's categories were all inherited; this one examines a small book whose categories came from elsewhere — **and it is the end of this course, because the two highest terms of Chinese criticism were never refuted, only replaced, and the operation of replacement never changed in two thousand years**.

## What kind of book {#def-shenmeshu}

::: definition Editions and form {#def-banben}
**The standard account**: a treatise on the *ci* lyric was published in instalments in a journal in the first decade of the twentieth century; it runs to some dozens of entries, and the author revised and cut it later, the uncut version containing the more philosophically loaded entries. Do the editorial work first. **① It was never a book**: it was written and revised piecemeal, so **the sentences we quote may come from a part the author himself removed**. ② **It is in entry form**; the last chapter listed four causes of that genre and this adds a fifth — **the author had unsystematic things to say and wanted to publish at once.** ③ **It appeared on a new distribution device**: a journal, in issues, addressed to readers he would never meet. **Criticism appeared as a serial for the first time — and a serial need not be consistent, because it need not be bound.** So the opening paradox: **traditional in form, modern in mode of publication, foreign in its problem-set.** See all three at once, **because any one alone yields a false literary history** — which is also this course's general verdict on all "turning-point of modernity" narratives.
:::

::: definition Realm {#def-jingjie}
**The standard account**: several of its propositions are among the most quoted in modern Chinese — lyrics take the "realm" (境界) as the highest; what can render real scenes and real feelings is said to have a realm; and there are realms made and realms written, realms with a self and realms without, illustrated by the couplet "tears in my eyes, I ask the flowers and they do not answer; the scattered red drifts past the swing" and by "the cold waves rise quietly, the white birds come down slowly". Three analyses. **① "Realm" is not his coinage** — the word has prior use in Buddhist translation and in poetry-talks; **his move was to promote it to the highest standard, not to invent it**, which is the same trick as the last chapter's four schools quarrelling over the content of one sentence: he changed only what was poured into it. **② Sincerity of delivery replaces fidelity to a model** — that is the operative substitution of criteria: **the old standard required a person who had read a great deal; the new one requires only that a reader, while reading, feel that scene and feeling have been handed over.** ③ **Its binary pairs are a new grammar** — ideal and realist, subjective and objective, made and written — each with its own history of argument in nineteenth-century European aesthetics (**standard account**: that the author studied that tradition early and wrote on it is well documented). The verdict of this lesson: **the *Remarks* is a translation written in the dress of a notebook.** Every entry reads as if it were Zhang Xie or Yan Yu, while the taxonomy is imported. **That explains both its quick acceptance (it reads like home) and its long misreading (everyone assumed it was continuing the old conversation)** — a technical conclusion worth carrying out of the course, and one more piece of evidence for the third course's rule that **a borrower often changes the criterion while keeping the wording**.
:::

## Blocked and unblocked {#def-ge}

::: definition An executable criterion {#def-buge}
**The standard account**: the book grades lyrics as "blocked" (隔) or "unblocked" (不隔): if every line is before you as if you were there, it is unblocked; where substitution and decoration oblige the reader through a middleman, it is blocked. **Audit it first for strengths. ① It is operative**: it locates the question at one place — does this line require guessing? **② It is refutable**: if you call a passage blocked, I can show at the same passage that it is not, provided we can state what prior knowledge the reader needs at which word. **③ It is anti-authoritarian**: it does not ask for sources, so it does not reward reading volume — that is what I mean by the democratisation of criteria. **Now audit it for failures. ① It has no scale**: blocked how much, and how much blocked is fatal? The old missing-weight defect of the previous chapter returns. **② It rests on an unexamined premise**: "the reader ought to feel it at once" is itself a taste; the partisans of allusion answer that a moderate middleman prolongs the after-taste (**standard account**: the Ming-Qing quarrel continued). **③ It has no institution**: nobody co-signs a verdict of "blocked" and there is nowhere to appeal — **so a criterion degenerates into a password.** The prescription: **a criterion separated from an institution can no longer be held to account** — the theoretical peak of the chapter and the nineteenth and last instalment of this course's argument for auditability.
:::

::: definition Three entries on quotation {#def-sanze}
**The standard account**: near the end, three lyrics about love are made to stand for the three stages through which "those who achieve great enterprises and great learning" must pass: climbing alone to a high tower; the belt growing loose; and turning to find, in the dim light, the one who stands where the lanterns are fading. **Teach these as the end of the whole course, because they are a single act of citation. ① The three lines were love-poems**; the appropriation has nothing to do with their sense. **② The method is "breaking off the chapter"** — precisely the use that chapter one found in the first theory of poetry: taking one line at a banquet to complete one's own sentence. **③ And it succeeded at the hardest thing in two thousand years: for a mass readership the three lines acquired a new use while the old one did not disappear.** The course summary: **Chinese criticism begins with an act of citation and ends with one; the intervening two thousand years spent themselves finding a justification for the practice.** These entries were written before the author turned to ancient history, and he worked by quite another method for the following decades (**standard account**) — **not gossip: it shows the book to be an unfinished project, and unfinished projects are easier for posterity to rewrite than completed works.** Chapter one's "the programme survived four rewrites"; its last instance is this.
:::

::: example Judge yourself as blocked {#ex-ziwo}
Take a passage you wrote recently (an article, a report, a message). Find one place liable to be judged blocked, and write: ① what the reader must already know in order to get it at once; ② whether that prior knowledge belongs in the text or should be assumed of the reader; ③ if you supply it, what you pay.

::: solution
**One possible shape.** Original: "This phenomenon has to do with the incentive structure of platforms." ① **The prior knowledge**: the reader must know that "incentive structure" names distribution, metering and revenue split; without it the sentence is a stand-in word — **a category term doing the work of an explanation.** ② **Whether to supply it** depends on whether your earlier pages already defined the three; if they did, restating is redundancy; if they did not, **the sentence is blocked.** This is the correct use of the term: **a checkable claim about the state of a text, not a verdict on elegance.** ③ **The price**: length; an air of not trusting the reader; and it spends words needed elsewhere — and where the text will be truncated (a headline, an abstract, the first screen) **a long explanation is no explanation.** The ruling: **blocked is not a defect but a transfer of cost — the cost of explaining moved from the writer to the reader.** That sentence supplies the missing weight, and is this lesson's only revision of the old category. **Marking: an A for "transfer of cost"; nothing for "write more plainly", which is an attitude and not a criterion.**
:::
:::

::: intuition Why this word survived {#int-huoxia}
**A statistical fact**: "realm" and "blocked" are still used today in aesthetics, textbooks and reviews, while "resonance", "texture" and "metre and tone" survive mainly inside textbooks. Three explanations. **① It was converted into a term of art**: it is taught as the equivalent of a foreign concept, so it can be transmitted in a classroom without a lineage — **the old categories needed joint reading of a canon to be activated; a term of art needs only a definition.** ② **Its opposite is clear**: realm / no realm, blocked / unblocked are two-valued judgements teachable in one sentence, **whereas "resonance" has no negation** — and the last chapter established that unverifiable words cannot enter an examination. ③ **It has institutional carriers**: textbooks and test papers. **The life of a category equals the retirement date of the last teacher who can use it fluently, plus one round of textbook revision.** Joke and truth alike: **before learning any aesthetic term, ask which examination requires it.**
:::

::: history One ending and two ratifications {#sec-jieju}
**1904**: an essay uses a Western philosopher's psychology of desire and release to interpret a novel (**standard account**). **Take one point only: it is the first Chinese criticism to use, wholesale, the problem-structure of another tradition** — the "change of track" promised at the end of chapter eight happens here. **1908–1909**: the entries are published in instalments (**standard account**). **The following decade and more**: the author moves to ancient history and epigraphy (**standard account**). **This lesson reads that turn as the chapter's second piece of evidence: what he gave up was not literature but the critical essay as a vehicle — apparently because he no longer believed that entries could deliver judgements** (**standard account**: unproven, so flagged as speculation; and flagging it is exactly the practice this course has taught, **namely that a borrowed claim must declare its layer**). **1927**: his death; the honours and the controversies that followed are outside this chapter. **Twentieth century**: his thesis is collected three times over, by histories of aesthetics, histories of criticism, and school textbooks, with divergent judgements (**standard account**). **Twenty-first century**: "realm" is a stock word in classrooms and art reviews, its philosophical background mostly forgotten (**standard account**). Conclusion: **this is the last chapter, and it closes the course's loop. From "intent" in chapter one to "realm" in chapter ten, the two highest categories of Chinese criticism were not refuted but replaced — and the operation never changed in two thousand years: find a new supreme word, declare that the value of works depends on it, and attach three examples. You can now take that machinery apart.** That is the only ending this course offers: not conclusions but a reusable tool. In the next course we take it to other work.
:::

::: warning Four traps in this chapter
**Quoting the book without noting the author deleted the sentence** (**the edition question**, form section); **calling it a continuation of poetry-talks** (**a translation in notebook dress**); **using "blocked" as a style verdict** (**it is a transfer of cost, and has no scale or institution**, the executable criterion); **treating the three stages as the author's aesthetics** (**they are an act of citation**, the three entries — **and it is the oldest act in the tradition**).
:::

::: exercise Essay: the replacement of categories {level=3}
In six hundred to a thousand words, discuss the claim that **the high terms of a discipline are not disproved but replaced, and the operation has three steps: find a new supreme word, declare that value depends on it, and attach three examples. Wang Guowei executed the operation in full view — the word was borrowed, the criteria were borrowed, the examples were old lyrics nobody had read that way; and it worked, because his readers were tired of arguing about models and liked a criterion that asked only for the honesty of a scene. What a new supreme word gains in operability it loses in depth, and the field discovers this after one generation: the old critics knew the same scene had been rendered in four other ways, while the new disciple, having seen three examples, calls everything else blocked.** Deploy at least six of: **edition and serial form**, **the promotion of a pre-existing word**, **the substitution of criteria**, **blocked as cost transfer**, **the three stages as an act of citation**, **the survival of a term of art**. Then adjudicate: **has your discipline completed this replacement, begun it, or refused it? Give one operational sign.**

::: solution
Marking: six mechanisms; the adjudication (**an operational sign is required**). **Completed**: the new criterion runs the examination and the selection while the old vocabulary survives only in monographs; **sign: the marking scheme contains a sentence about immediate experience or readability**. **Begun**: the criterion circulates but the apparatus is unchanged — **the commonest state; its symptom is that in the same paper the author uses one vocabulary in the methodology and another in his conclusions**. **Refused**: the discipline keeps an authority criterion, and **its sign is that your first reviewer asks what your position belongs to.** **Highest marks** for the institutional deduction: **a category replaced without an institutional translation is replaced only in the textbook** — that is why "realm" still needs a teacher and "blocked" needs no second reader; **and a discipline grows up when it asks not "is this word correct" but "what work do we use it for; if we changed the work, would it become useless"** — the same question this chapter asked of "intent" in chapter one, twenty-eight courses ago.
:::

::: summary
The chapter delivers: edition (**first ask whether the author deleted the sentence**), form (**serialisation does not require consistency**), criteria (**sincerity of delivery replaces fidelity to a model; democratisation costs depth**), blocked (**a transfer of cost, lacking scale and institution**), quotation (**the three stages are an act of citation, and the oldest one**), survival (**a category's life is its institutional carriers**), and the tool of a whole course: **the three-step operation by which a discipline replaces its highest word.**
