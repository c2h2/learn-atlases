title: "Semantics"

The previous chapter cut the sentence into constituents; this chapter asks what the pieces cut out are worth — **the first move of semantics is to overturn an idea that looks very natural: meaning is not in the word. It lies in the relation between the word and the world, in the relation between word and word, and in the community that uses it; and the answers these three places give are often different**.

## Definition: three separate things {#def-sanjian}

::: definition Reference, sense and meaning {#def-zhicheng}
**The received account** (**Frege distinguishes two "modes of presentation" of one and the same object: the morning star and the evening star have the same reference and different senses —** this one step explains three everyday puzzles (**① why a sentence such as “鲁迅写的《阿 Q 正传》的作者” ('the author of *The True Story of Ah Q* written by Lu Xun') has a meaning (**② the place where the principle of truth-preserving substitution fails is exactly where sense lies:** “小明明知 A 是 A” ('Xiao Ming knows full well that A is A') is absurd, “小明明知 A 是 B” ('Xiao Ming knows full well that A is B') is reasonable (**③ the most stubborn part of translation:** words that fail to match are not a word one cannot find; they are two systems cutting different senses out of one and the same object (Chapter 2, "value" and "segmentation is sovereignty").
**Ogden and Richards's triangle of meaning** (**the received account: symbol—thought—referent, and what holds among the three is "referring", not an equals sign —** this lesson takes that diagram as the constitution of this chapter (**it forbids three common errors:** pasting the word directly onto the thing (and so arguing "what is the original meaning of this word?", whereas the original meaning is only a historical use that has been specially authorised; **equating the word directly with a mental image** (and so requiring every abstract word to be given a picture; **treating the circle of interpretation as a defect** (**a dictionary explains words with words; the circle cannot be cancelled, and can only be earthed by "use"** (**which is why this chapter goes all the way on to pragmatics**.
:::

::: definition Compositionality and its counter-examples {#def-zuhe}
**The received account** (**the meaning of the whole is determined by the meanings of the parts and by the way they are combined —** its power, and its boundary (**power:** it explains why a person can understand a sentence never heard before (the counterpart, at the level of meaning, of the productivity of Chapter 2 (**the boundary is of four kinds, and each kind is a research programme:** ① idioms (**the literal meaning of idioms of the “踢桶” ('kick the bucket') kind is not enough to derive the use; **② tone and rhetoric** (**“你真行” ('you really are capable') is, in another context, a remark that means the opposite (**the part semantics cannot explain is precisely the whole theme of the next chapter; **③ the contribution of the pattern** (**the previous chapter: a considerable proportion of the meaning of “送” ('send; give') and “让” ('let; have someone do') comes from the pattern; **④ collocational restriction** (**“打” can 打伞 ('hold an umbrella'), 打人 ('hit a person'), 打官司 ('go to law'), 打毛衣 ('knit a jumper'):** no one componential analysis can calculate what it can take next (**this lesson's marking:** compositionality is the default assumption, not a theorem that has been proved:** meet a counter-example, and first ask whether it has failed, or whether our division of the "parts" is wrong (Chapter 4, the word boundary:** here, likewise, it is a problem of drawing the line**.
:::

## The structure of word meaning {#def-jiegou}

::: definition Componential analysis: a handsome ledger {#def-yesu}
**The received account** (**word meaning is decomposed into distinctive features: 男人 ('man') ＝ ［＋人］［＋男］［＋成年］ ('[+human] [+male] [+adult]') —** the place where it is handsomest, and the place where it collapses first (**handsome:** kinship terms are almost wholly determined by the features (**this is exactly the subject-matter on which Chinese has the advantage:** the seven words 伯 ('father's elder brother'), 叔 ('father's younger brother'), 姑 ('father's sister'), 舅 ('mother's brother'), 姨 ('mother's sister'), 堂 ('paternal cousin'), 表 ('cousin off the patriline') are squeezed into three in English, because the Chinese ledger has three extra headings (patriline and matriline, seniority, sex (**the worked example in this chapter does this very account; **where it collapses:** “光棍是未婚男子” ('a 光棍 is an unmarried man') is concise, but “新娘” ('bride') is not equal to “＋婚＋女” ('+married +female'):** it has a rite, it has the colour of the skirt, it has a whole scene that need not be spoken (**this is the supplement that the study of frames and scripts (of the frame-semantics kind, the received account) makes to componential analysis:** the meaning of some words is a process sheet, not a sheet of attributes (**Chapter 9, "the grammar of narrative", shakes hands with this chapter here**.
:::

::: definition Categorisation: classical features and prototypes {#def-yuanxing}
**The received account** (**experiments of the Rosch kind show that category judgements have a gradient of typicality and family resemblance, and that boundary and centre are not equally clear —** this lesson's three-point treatment (**①** it does not overturn compositionality** (**what it overturns is "a category must have necessary and sufficient conditions":** that is a wish of logic, not a fact of linguistics; **② it pushes semantics towards the social group** (**typicality depends on the frequency of experience, and on culture (**so the centre of one person's “鸟” ('bird') is, in part, his childhood (**③ a concrete verification in Chinese** (**the marginal members of “蔬菜、水果、粮食” ('vegetables, fruit, staple grain') are where disagreement across the age groups is greatest; and “鱼” ('fish'), in everyday Chinese classification, can count in 鲸 ('whale'), 墨鱼 ('cuttlefish') and 娃娃鱼 ('giant salamander') (the received view: folk classification and biological classification do not agree) —** this is a small experiment that can be done on the spot:** list ten nouns, have people judge “是不是鱼” ('is it a fish or not?'), and the place of greatest disagreement is the edge of the category (**Lesson 1, the study of evidence:** introspective data of this kind are cheap, repeatable, and enough to falsify the "feature theory"**).
:::

::: definition Roles, valency and the several identities of 吃 {#def-jiaose}
**The received account** (**semantic roles such as agent, patient, recipient, instrument and location are independent of syntactic position; a verb's requirement on the number of arguments is called valency** (**the demonstration from Chinese (**the ambiguity of “鸡不吃了” in the previous chapter gets a clean description here:** the same surface, different assignments of role (**look again at “吃”** (**吃饭 ('eat a meal', patient), 吃食堂 ('eat at the canteen', location), 吃工资 ('live on a wage', source), 吃官司 ('be caught up in a lawsuit', the one affected), 靠山吃山 ('live off the mountain one lives by', source) —** its valency is not fixed; rather, the patterns each take one corner of it (**this lesson's conclusion:** a verb is like an appliance with several sockets; the pattern decides which hole is plugged (**this sentence is at the same time this chapter's answer to whether "polysemy is only an arbitrary convention":** polysemy is systematic: the senses share one abstract skeleton, and the differences are supplied by the pattern and the setting (**this also explains an old difficulty of lexicography:** how far does one cut the senses? (**cut as far as "it can be tested by a new pattern", and no further —** that is this chapter's reason for opposing subdivision without end (Lesson 1: **classification is a pair of glasses, not a specimen**).
:::

## Metaphor and rhetoric {#def-yinyu}
::: definition Conceptual metaphor and its risk of circularity {#def-yinyu2}
**The received account** (**Lakoff and Johnson maintain that everyday conceptual systems have a metaphorical structure: time is a resource, argument is war, Up is good —** its real contribution (**it moves metaphor out of rhetoric and into grammar:** it explains why no one finds “把这个论点的基础拆掉” ('tear the foundation out from under this argument') strange:** a dead metaphor is still supplying a structure for inference (**this agrees entirely with Lesson 12, "once a metaphor is put on the payroll it has to turn up for work" (**its risk (**the received account: there is also criticism which finds its examples open to a suspicion of circularity —** a conceptual mapping is inferred from the expressions, and the mapping is then used to explain the expressions; and a cross-linguistic test does not always support the same mapping (**this lesson's attitude:** treat conceptual metaphor as a question sheet (**ask "which directionality does this expression depend on?", and do not treat it as a psychological file already archived; **and demand a falsifiable version** (**if "time is a resource" is true, then one should be able to predict that a language which formulates time as a cycle rather than as a resource will show systematic differences in saving and in punctuality —** the empirical results of predictions of this kind are not tidy (**this is the chapter's honest assessment of that old path, "whether linguistics can talk about the mind"**.
:::

::: example The ledger of kinship terms {#ex-qinshu}
Split “堂兄、表兄、舅舅、姑姑、姨、公公、婆婆、小叔子” ('elder patrilineal male cousin, elder male cousin off the patriline, mother's brother, father's sister, mother's sister, husband's father, husband's mother, husband's younger brother') into a table of semantic features, and answer: **① which headings Chinese has set up in addition on this table; ② in which cells English merges; ③ which cell a recent change, one that can be checked, is now deleting**.
::: solution
**① The table of headings** — **［±patrilineal］［±older than the speaker］［±male］［±blood relative／affine］［generation］** (**“堂” and “表” differ only in the patrilineal column; “舅／姑／姨” differ only in the two columns of sex and patriline (**② where English merges** — **the one word brother-in-law swallows four cells (**the husband's brother, the wife's brother, the sister's husband, and male affines in a broad sense (**sibling merges sex, and cousin merges patriline and seniority (**so translating from English into Chinese is not a matter of growing more complicated; one is forced to check the household register (**this is "segmentation is sovereignty" as it appears in the kinship system, and it is why anthropology treats kinship terms as evidence of institutions (**③ the cell now being deleted** — **seniority:** in the colloquial speech of the young, “堂兄／堂弟” are often unified, by way of “表哥／表弟”, into the two grades “哥／弟” ('elder brother / younger brother'), and are even ordered by age rather than by clan; at the same time the only-child generation leaves “舅、姨、叔” without referents, so that only the function of a term of address remains (**evidence that can be checked is corpora of forms of address, and statistics of family structure (**the methodological close of this lesson:** whether a linguistic change can be noticed depends on which column it moves first:** move the margin (seniority) and it is not easily noticed; move the centre (sex) and it at once starts a dispute over the norm (**this point applies equally to grammar, to pragmatics, and to the problem of reference at the very beginning of this chapter (Lesson 9 will deal head-on with "who has the right to name"**).
:::
:::

::: intuition One word, one sense is a policy goal {#int-yiyi}
**The received account** (**terminology and the compiling of dictionaries have long pursued "one word, one sense", while natural language is always polysemous —** this lesson puts the two things each in its place — **①** polysemy is not laziness** (**a finite stock of symbols has to cope with infinite experience, and extending a sense is the cheapest means:** the cost of coining a new word is making people remember it; the cost of adding one sense to an old word is making the context responsible (**and context is usually free (Chapter 5, "grammar is a kind of compulsion": **some of the forms language forces you to hand in are much cheaper than others, so it prefers to use the cheap ones**); **②** monosemy is an institutional achievement** (**technical terms, national standards, legal provisions, names of medicines:** they have to pay an enormous cost of maintenance (vetting, promulgation, penalties, education) before they can keep up "one word, one sense" (**so the question "how many meanings does a word have, after all?" asks, in science, about a distribution, and in law, about an authorisation —** this is also the lesson of word segmentation in the previous chapter: for a definition to land, someone has to sign**).
:::

::: history Four turns in the study of meaning {#sec-yiyi}
**The referential turn** (**Frege, Russell:** meaning is dealt with, first, as something between language and the world (**the legacy is the formal semantics and the truth conditions of today (**the price is that emotion, rhetoric and the poetic are pushed out of the door (**and this is precisely the part the next chapter has to take over**); **the turn to use** (**the later Wittgenstein:** “意义是它在语言中的用法” ('Meaning is its use in the language.') (Our translation.) (**the received account**) — **this sentence assigns part of the business of semantics to sociology and to pragmatics —** it also brings a risk:** "use" is infinite, unless someone stipulates which uses count (Lesson 1: the boundary between description and prescription comes to court again here**); **the structural turn** (**componential analysis, contrastive linguistics:** segmentations are compared inside the language (**its achievement is this chapter's worked example on kinship terms; **its limit is that it treats language as a static chessboard**); **the cognitive and corpus turn** (**prototypes, frames, scripts, constructions, frequency of collocation (**the received account: large-scale corpora show that the distribution of a word's senses is highly skewed, a few collocations accounting for most of the occurrences (**and so the "core sense" is no longer only an intuition, but a quantity whose proportion can be reported —** the honesty column at the end of this chapter:** a corpus can calculate which sense is the most common; it cannot calculate which sense is the more "true"; **whoever says that frequency is the essence is doing something the constitution at the beginning of this chapter forbids**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in this chapter
**"Look it up in the dictionary and you know the meaning"** (**a dictionary is a circular system plus a history of authorisation:** it tells you which uses have been registered; it does not tell you where the boundary is (② above**)); **taking the original meaning to be what is real** (**the original meaning of “淘汰” has to do with selecting and with removing, but today's meaning is not in the etymology (of the same source as the "etymological superstition" of Lesson 12, on the culture of Chinese characters:** etymology is historical evidence, not the semantics of the present (**this is the course's standing reminder about Chinese characters**)); **handling everything with a table of features** (**frames, scripts, prototypes and metaphorical structure are none of them on the list of attributes (the semantic-feature clause above**)); **"Chinese is especially vague / especially precise"** (**every language compels on some cells and leaves others alone (Chapter 5, the shape of compulsion:** the right answer in a comparison is always set out column by column**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: where meaning is {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**Whenever someone says 'this word does not mean that', he is in fact performing an institutional act: he is claiming, for himself, the right to interpret that sign, and implicitly asking an authority to endorse him. The difficulty of semantics is not that meaning cannot be found, but that meaning lives in three places at once — the segmentation of the system, the usage of the community, and the intention of the particular occasion — all three give a correct answer, and when they contradict one another there is, in language, no court of final appeal**". You must call on at least six items: **reference and sense**, **the triangle of meaning and the circle of interpretation**, **the four kinds of counter-example to compositionality**, **semantic features and frames**, **the prototype and disagreement at the edge**, **the circularity risk of metaphor**; and give a ruling: **among the three, "the segmentation of the system / the usage of the community / the intention of the occasion", which cell the compiling of dictionaries, the language of the court, and the teaching of Chinese to foreigners should each put first — three lines of answer, and each line must give one cost of losing**.
::: solution
Points for marking: the six mechanisms (**sense, the triangle, composition, frames, the prototype, metaphor**); three lines of answer (**the dictionary: the community's usage first, the cost being that it always lags behind new usage, and that marginal members are recorded as "colloquialisms"; the court: the intention of the occasion first (the original intention of the legislation), the cost being that word meaning fluctuates with the psychology accessible to the judge, and can therefore be manufactured strategically; teaching: the segmentation of the system first (set against the native speaker's ledger), the cost being that the rules have been taught but a feel for the language has not, so that the student can say it but cannot understand it when it is heard** (**marking looks at whether all three lines have written the cost rather than only the benefit; and at whether it is granted that:** a different ordering of the three cells leads to a different type of error, and that recognising the type of error is the practical use of this analysis**); if the conclusion can say "**so what this chapter teaches is not 'what meaning is', but which court a dispute should be sent to: ask at the wrong court, and even the cleverest semanticist can give only an answer that is grammatically correct**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
Meaning filed: sense (**where substitution fails is where sense lies**), the triangle (**it forbids three equals signs with the wrong labels stuck on**), compositionality (**the default assumption; each of the four kinds of counter-example is a programme**), semantic features (**kinship terms are Chinese's home ground, and the frame is its patch**), the prototype (**disagreement at the edge can be tested on the spot**), metaphor (**a question sheet, not a file**). The method, in one sentence: **first say in which cell you are looking for meaning, and only then talk about what it is**. The next chapter: pragmatics — the words that are said, and the things that are done.
:::
