title: "Language and linguistics"

The twenty courses before this one have all been using the word "language"; this lesson begins to examine it — **the first lesson of linguistics is not learning to say "subject–predicate–object" (主谓宾), but learning to tell the two apart: are you describing how people speak, or prescribing how they ought to speak? These two things share one name in Chinese, and so native speakers of Chinese are especially liable to go a whole lifetime without telling them apart**.

## Definition: the gate and the threshold of a discipline {#def-meng}

::: definition Five levels and one interface {#def-cengji}
**The usual division of labour** (**phonetics–phonology / morphology / syntax / semantics / pragmatics, plus vocabulary and discourse, these two interfaces (**this lesson calls them "the five workshops of one machine" (**why the workshops have to be divided first:** because the great majority of arguments about language are a crossing of workshops (**"‘涉及到’是病句吗" ("Is 涉及到 a faulty sentence?") (Our translation.) is a question of orthography; "‘被自杀’合法吗" ("Is 被自杀 licit?") (Our translation.) is a question of word-formation; whether “他家儿子考上大学了” ("The son of his family has got into university.") (Our translation.) gets through is a question of syntax; “你这是什么意思” ("What do you mean by that?") (Our translation.) is for the most part a question of pragmatics (**ask them all mixed together, and the quarrel never ends (Lesson 3, "Levels", is the foundation of the whole course: **before judging right and wrong, first settle at which level you are judging**.
:::

::: definition Description and prescription {#def-mingming}
**The prescriptive** (**it is a rule: it tells you how to write so that you will not lose marks (**its value lies in standardised examinations, in editing and publishing, and in teaching the language to non-native speakers (**its problem is not that it is strict, but that it passes itself off as science (**it calls "not yet included" "wrong", and calls "the authorities do not use it this way" "it does not get through" (**the descriptive** (**it is an observation: it records every form that is actually used, including the ones you dislike (**its difficulty is not technical, but a matter of stance: **description is not endorsement** (**a linguist who records 被自杀 is not thereby supporting it, just as a doctor who records a lesion is not thereby praising the bacterium (**this lesson's marking line: **if a branch of learning can answer only "right or wrong?", it is nothing more than a handbook; if it can answer "why would someone say it this way, and who would understand it said this way?", only then does it begin to be of use**.
:::

::: definition Three pairs of dichotomies {#def-erfen}
**Langue and parole** (语言 and 言语) (**received account: Saussure distinguished the social system from individual use — **this lesson's reading:** this is the cut by which linguistics acquired its object (**before this there was only grammar, rhetoric and *xiaoxue* (小学); only after this could there be a science: **you must first assume that a thing called "English" exists before you can study it** (**that thing is in fact a statistical approximation (**this is its most fragile step, and also its most necessary (**the history-of-the-discipline version of Lesson 1, "a list is a pair of spectacles"**.
**Competence and performance** (**Chomsky distinguished the rules internalised by the speaker from the sentences actually uttered (**the methodological consequences are very large: **slips of the tongue, breaks and self-corrections in a corpus do not count as counter-examples** (**this immunises the theory against a great mass of dirty data, and also makes it easy to charge the theory with being unfalsifiable (**this lesson's attitude: **being able to tell which counter-examples are fatal and which are noise is itself a methodology** (Lesson 3's scale of refutability: **"a slip of the tongue does not count" is a cheap defence; it is not a cheap one only when it can independently explain where the noise comes from**.
**Synchrony and diachrony** (**the system at one moment, and change across moments — **Saussure's famous analogy is a game of chess** (**the relations among the pieces at this moment, and the history of the moves in this game, are two separate descriptions (**this lesson adds a corollary that every course of this lesson will use: **the great majority of complaints that "language is decaying" are complaints that treat a diachronic phenomenon as a synchronic error** (**change is not decay, but change is indeed an inconvenience to users: **both statements are true, and they belong to two different levels** (**this is why teachers of school Chinese (语文) and linguists often talk past one another, and are both right (the mediation paper of Lesson 2, "the author is alive / the work is valid", is signed again here**.
:::

## Language, this apparatus {#def-zhuangzhi}

::: definition Identifying features {#def-tezheng}
**The usual list** (**arbitrariness, two-level structure, productivity, displacement, cultural transmission, interchangeability (**this lesson takes up only three of them in any detail (**① **arbitrariness** (**between “兄” and “xiōng” there is no physical reason — **note that it is often misused to mean "language is arbitrary, so one may change it however one likes"** (**it says only that there is no motivation between sign and object; it does not say that one may do as one pleases between sign and sign-system: **inside, the system is extremely conservative** (**you cannot change “的” into “额”, just as you cannot change the horse in xiangqi (象棋) so that it flies (**arbitrariness is at the word level; obligatoriness is at the system level (**this sentence is the first true-or-false question of this chapter**); **② two-level structure** (**a few dozen meaningless units (phonemes / strokes) compose an infinity of meaningful units (**this is the most resource-saving invention of human language (Lesson 1, "finite signs, infinite combinations": **the Chinese script is in fact another two-level structure: a few hundred components write the whole of the literature** (**that the two two-level structures coexist is the reason Chinese writing and speech have been shaping each other for a thousand years (the preliminary to Lesson 12, "Chinese characters and Pinyin"**); **③ displacement** (**one can talk of what is not present, of what has not happened, and of what is impossible (**literature, law, religion and science all depend on this one (**this lesson's definition: **"literature" is the professional abuse of displacement — the speaker is not present, the event has not happened, and the hearer cooperates all the same** (the linguistic root of Chapter 2, "the fictional author", and of Chapter 1, "fiction is a promise"**.
:::

::: definition Animal communication and human language {#def-dongwu}
**Received account** (**the dance of bees, the alarm calls of long-tailed monkeys, variation in whale song, and experiments in teaching chimpanzees sign language (of the Washoe and Kanzi sort; their conclusions are still disputed) — **how this lesson handles it** (**① **first distinguish three explanations** (**an innate apparatus, a limit on learning, and an effect of training (**a monkey's call has reference but no combination; an artificial sign language has vocabulary but little syntax: **the real focus of the dispute is "a lack of capacity" or "a lack of motive and of context"** (**② **why this chapter discusses this** (**because it supplies the only kind of cross-species test: **if language is only a general application of intelligence, then clever animals ought to show sporadic language; if it is a specialised apparatus, then there ought to appear a dissociation of the kind "clever at everything else, and only unable to speak"** (**dissociation cases of autism and of specific language impairment, and "talking parrots", all supply partial evidence (**this lesson does not adjudicate; it only requires that any argument to the effect that "language is what makes a human being human" must first pass this test**.
:::

## A sketch of the history of linguistics {#def-lishi}

::: definition Three prehistories {#def-qianshi}
**India** (**Pāṇini's grammar of Sanskrit (received account: already some centuries BC there was a generative description of Sanskrit verbs and roots — **what is astonishing about it lies in the method:** finite rules and a metalanguage generate all the well-formed forms (**its relation to modern formal syntax is a real genealogy (nineteenth-century European scholars came to it by way of Sanskrit (**the first appearance of Chapter 4, "grammar is an apparatus"**.
**China** (*xiaoxue* (小学): the script, historical phonology and exegesis (训诂) (**received account: their aim was reading the Classics and standardising characters, not describing the spoken language — **this explains a long-standing feature of the linguistics of Chinese:** it takes the text as its object, commentary as its method, and the norm as its motive (**"xiaoxue" is not scientific linguistics, but it supplied the only indigenous stock of concepts after 1900 (**every chapter after this one will recover one item of this very heritage (rhyme groups, divisions and mouth-shapes (等呼), voiceless and voiced, the entering tone, level and oblique tones**.
**Europe** (**from the logical grammar of the *Grammar* (《语法》) to historical-comparative linguistics (**received account: the genealogical study of languages in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries treated language as an organism and as a family tree (**this lesson calls it "the first scientisation of linguistics", and also calls it "the first biologisation": **the former made it a discipline; the latter left it carrying a hundred years of dispute over race and over superiority and inferiority** (the source of Chapter 11, "the evolution clause"**.
:::

::: definition The four modern turns {#def-sizhuan}
**The structural turn** (**Saussure, Prague, Copenhagen, American descriptivism (**what they share: **treating language as a system, and postponing meaning** (**② **the behavioural and fieldwork turn** (**American linguistic surveys of the Indigenous languages of the Americas trained a method for "how to work when no native speaker is available" (Chapter 11's clause on field method: **the technical base colour of this course in fact comes from anthropology** (**③ **the generative turn** (**after 1957: explanation takes priority over description, intuition may serve as evidence, and universal grammar may be hypothesised (**the dispute: **the status of the evidence** (**④ **the functional, cognitive and corpus turn** (**after the 1970s: frequency, usage, constructions, prototypes, corpora — **this lesson's position** (**none of the four turns "overthrew" what came before; each changed the object of study: **system — use — competence — probability:** **this is why the first chapter of any textbook is still setting out these five pairs of concepts** (**it also reminds you: **whenever you read that "linguistics has already proved" something, first ask in which turn that claim holds**.
:::

::: example The five workshops of one Weibo post {#ex-weibo}
The sentence: “被就业了三年，现在连‘被增长’都懒得说了。” Been "employed" for three years; now I cannot even be bothered to say "been increased." (Our translation.) For each of the five levels, raise one question that can be tested (you may not ask "right or wrong?"), and point out: **which level's question is most likely to be taken by an ordinary reader for another level's**.
::: solution
**Morphology** — **can “被＋动词” take a duration complement (时量补语, a postverbal complement of how long)? What does “三年” modify in “被就业了三年”?** (**this is a question of constructions and of argument structure, and it can be tested by substitution — **被表扬了三年 ✓／被吃饭了三年 × (**phonology / orthography** — **the writing convention for quotation marks used together with “被”, and where the stress falls in speech** (**readers most easily take this level for grammar (**because a great part of the Chinese "sense of the norm" is training in writing (**semantics** — **被就业 writes as coerced something the subject themselves brought about; this is a semantic reversal:** it is not “被安排就业” ("arranged into employment") (Our translation.), it is “被统计成就业” ("counted in the statistics as employed") (Our translation.) (**this is where the whole of its satirical force lies (**pragmatics** — **it presupposes that the reader knows a certain kind of falsified statistics; it is an identity marker: the people who can use this word belong to some community of public opinion — **Lesson 10, "a cant phrase = a ticket of admission"**; **discourse** — **“连……都懒得说了” writes the second clause as a comment on the first, and forms a self-cancelling tone** (**the level most easily misidentified** — **semantics and pragmatics:** the reader will say "the meaning of this word does not get through", when what he means is "I do not accept its presupposition" (**this is exactly the disease this lesson means to treat: **translating "I do not agree" into "it is ungrammatical"** (Lesson 11, "Is 'not licit' a grammatical judgement or a judgement of identity?": you now have the tool with which to answer it**).
:::
:::

::: intuition The study of evidence in linguistics {#int-zhengju}
**Four kinds of evidence, and their strength** (**① native-speaker acceptability judgements (**fast, cheap, and easily contaminated by training in the norms**); ② corpus frequency (**objective, but able to prove only that something has occurred, not that it cannot be said**); ③ experiments (reaction time, eye movement, offline ratings: **controllable, but low in ecological validity**); ④ language typology and historical material (**able to falsify universal propositions; the cost is natural observation** (**this lesson's methodological conclusion (**for any one conclusion, first ask which kind of evidence it rests on; a conclusion that has only ① is called "I think"; only ① + ② may be called "in Chinese"; only with ③ added may one dare to speak of a mechanism; only with ④ added may one dare to speak of a universal (the linguistics version of Lesson 3, "the chain of evidence", and of Chapter 8, "independent sources": **this is also why this lesson requires that, whenever you later cite a linguistic 'fact', you mark the level of its source — this is a standing old rule of this course, kept for twenty years**).
:::

::: history Four faces of a discipline {#sec-miankong}
**Pre-modern** (**three prehistories running side by side:** reading the Classics, standardising characters, learning Sanskrit (**the study of language is the servant of other fields of learning**); **the nineteenth century** (**the comparative method:** language becomes an object with a pedigree (**its by-product is national narrative (**this is also the record that later proved hardest to wash off**); **1916–1950s** (**structuralism:** system, opposition, distribution; **field method becomes the technical core** (**in this period Chinese scholars did two things at once: they read rhyme dictionaries as phonology, and they surveyed dialects into maps (Lesson 10 and the dialects course**); **1957–1970s** (**generative grammar:** explanation, competence, universals (**its legacy is to have turned "why can a person utter a sentence they have never heard?" into a scientific question — **its debt is to have lowered the status of the "corpus" — the dispute over the usability of data is still not settled; **1980s–the present** (**corpora, experiments, neuroimaging, computational modelling and large-scale models (**received account: **the concept of "linguistic competence" (语言能力) for the first time had a non-human object of comparison** (**and so the two old questions from the opening of this chapter have come back in a new form:** the boundary between description and prescription, and the boundary between competence and performance**) — this is what is most optimistic about linguistics: **every thirty years it has to remake its own first-year students**).
:::

::: warning Four pits in this lesson
**Taking the norm for science** (**a "norm" is a product of administration and of education (Lesson 9's clause on "the standard language": **it has its own department of production and its own budget; it is not a law of nature** (**but it is not without value: **it is social infrastructure** (**this chapter opposes only its epistemological ambition, not its function**); **reading "language is arbitrary" as "one may change it at will"** (**arbitrariness is at the word level; obligatoriness is at the system level (① above**)); **using Indo-European categories as universal grammar** (**any category that comes from a textbook, **every category that comes from a textbook, must on entry hand in a proof of its distribution in Chinese** (the morphology lesson of Chapter 4 will collect the tax item by item (**)); **reading "linguistics is a science" as "linguistics has a single correct answer"** (**it has different ways of setting its object of study: **system, use, competence, probability** (**a change of object does not bring right and wrong with it (the four turns above**.

::: solution
Marking: **each one must land on a single level, and be given a way of testing it** (**for instance corpus frequency, substitutability, distribution by age:** one may not say only "this is a matter of rhetoric" (**the column "what it is really objecting to" is the main point:** what most such complaints object to is "people who are not of my group speaking in public".

::: exercise Build a counter-example {level=2}
Write one sentence which is required to **conform to every rule of grammar** and yet be **not licit in pragmatics** (that is, full marks for grammar, and nobody would say it).
::: solution
Marking: **the failure to be licit must be given a reason** (**is it a clash of presuppositions, a clash of register, or a clash of identity:** choose one of the three and state it — **the aim of this question: to turn the boundary between competence and performance into a felt sense** (the boundary between semantics and pragmatics in Chapter 5 will test it once more**).

::: exercise Short essay: which level has the right to speak {level=3}
Write six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing "**every quarrel about 'correct' inside a language community is in fact a dispute over jurisdiction: the orthography court, the grammar court, the usage court and the prestige court each claim the right of final judgement, and their criteria do not connect with one another — the first rests on documents, the second on acceptability, the third on frequency, and the fourth on who is speaking. Linguistics cannot declare that all four houses are wrong; it can only nail this table of jurisdiction to the door, so that every judgement states in writing which court handed it down**". You must draw on **the five levels**, **description and prescription**, **synchrony and diachrony**, **arbitrariness and obligatoriness**, **the ranking of the four kinds of evidence by strength** and **the four turns**, at least six items; and adjudicate: **whether one of the four courts ought to hold priority in public education — if so, which one, and what the cost is; if not, what an examination in the standard language ought to test**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**level, stance, time, system, evidence, turn**); the adjudication (**both routes may score, but the cost must be paid — **anyone who maintains that "the usage court has priority" must admit that it cannot stop textbooks from lagging behind, nor the inefficiency of teaching the language to non-native speakers; anyone who maintains that "the acceptability court has priority" must admit that it is unfriendly to communities with a low literacy rate, and that it has treated native speakers' prejudice as the right of final judgement** (**marking looks at whether a concrete institutional design appears (for example: the examination tests only the removal of ambiguity and the transmission of information, and does not test "whether it conforms to some edition of the norm"**); if the conclusion can be written "**so what linguistics gives public education is not an answer, but a map of jurisdiction: it teaches students to distinguish 'I would not write it this way', 'nobody writes it this way' and 'it cannot be written this way' — the difference among these three is the distance between literacy and linguistics**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The introductory file: levels (**crossing workshops is the root of the disease in every argument about language**), stance (**description is not endorsement**), the dichotomies (**synchrony and diachrony: the decay thesis is a confusion of levels**), the apparatus (**arbitrariness at the word level, obligatoriness at the system level**), evidence (**① I think ② in Chinese ③ there is a mechanism ④ it is universal**), history (**the four turns changed the object, and did not change right and wrong**). Method, in one sentence: **first say which level you are speaking at, and only then open your mouth**. Next chapter: what language is — signs, arbitrariness and two-level structure.
