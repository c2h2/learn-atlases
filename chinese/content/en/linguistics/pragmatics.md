title: "Pragmatics"
---

The previous chapter said that meaning lives in three places at once; this chapter deals with the most troublesome of them — **what the speaker, at this moment, wants to get someone to do. Pragmatics has only one introductory sentence: to speak is to do; and it has only one introductory difficulty: what we do is often not the thing the mouth has said**.

## Definition: saying it and getting it done {#def-shuofu}

::: definition Speech acts and the three levels {#def-cengci}
**Received account** (Austin distinguished the constative from the performative, and further split one act of speaking into three layers, 'saying it out', 'doing in the saying' and 'bringing about an effect'; Searle revised the classification and dealt with the difficulty of 'speaking in earnest and play-acting' (**received account**); **the three examples that work best in a Chinese classroom** (“我宣布开会”, “我道歉”, “我请你出去” — 'I declare the meeting open', 'I apologise', 'I ask you to leave' — **note what they have in common:** the sentence does not describe an event; it is part of that event (**two consequences follow at once** (**①** **there are conditions of qualification** (only saying “我同意” ('I agree') at a wedding brings the marriage about; a person who is not the chair, saying “我宣布开会”, is merely ridiculous); **②** **there are sincerity conditions** (say “我道歉” with no remorse, and the act has been accomplished while the attitude is false: **pragmatics is therefore the only discipline that must distinguish 'it was brought off' from 'someone was taken in'** (**this point is the foundation of every later discussion of 'corruption of language'** (Lesson 21, practical writing, and Lesson 6, argument, will both take it up again))).
:::

::: definition Indirect speech acts {#def-jianjie}
**Received account** (Searle: the form of a question can indirectly carry out a request — “你能够把盐递给我吗” ('Are you able to pass me the salt?') is answered by nobody as a question about ability (**the Chinese gradient is finer than the English** (“把盐给我”, “你把盐递给我一下”, “你能不能把盐递给我”, “这菜是不是有点咸了”, “您这儿盐放着方便吗” — 'Give me the salt', 'Pass me the salt a moment', 'Can you pass me the salt?', 'Is this dish a bit salty?', 'Is the salt sitting conveniently by you?' — **the five forms have the same illocutionary force, while their politeness values differ enormously** (**this lesson's definition:** **the degree of indirectness in Chinese is a measuring instrument of interpersonal distance**) — what it measures is not the strength of the need, but 'whether I am qualified to give you a direct order' (**this is also why the request sentences in a Chinese textbook are all correct, and yet a learner offends the moment he opens his mouth: the grammar he uses is the one that scores full marks** (Lesson 10, 'Language and teaching', will write this point up as a class hour))).
:::

::: definition Conversational implicature {#def-hanyi}
**Received account** (Grice: conversation is organised by a premise of cooperation, under which are set four maxims, of quantity, quality, relation and manner; if the speaker deliberately flouts (flout) one of the maxims, the hearer infers a meaning beyond the literal (**a Chinese demonstration** (甲: “小周这人怎么样？” 乙: “他呀，工作挺认真的。” — A: 'What is Xiao Zhou like?' B: 'Him? He is quite diligent about his work.' (Our translation.) — **B has violated the maxim of 'quantity'** (the answer is less than was expected), and so the hearer infers 'he does not want to speak well of him' (**the key properties** (**① cancellable** (“他工作挺认真的，而且别的方面也不差” — 'He is quite diligent about his work, and he is not bad in other respects either.' (Our translation.): **cancellability shows that this is not the meaning of a word** (② **non-detachable** (change to any synonymous wording, and the implicature is still there: **which shows that it attaches to this act of speaking, and not to some word** (③ **it does not depend on the speaker's awareness** (the hearer can derive it even if B had no intention of hinting (**these three are precisely the technical merits of Grice's scheme** (**it gives 'what lies beyond the words' a place that can be tested:** it is not psychological guesswork; it is a cost-accounting of inference (**this lesson adds one localising reminder:** the cooperative assumption of the four maxims carries a strong colouring of 'exchange of information'; whether Chinese phatic talk, polite formulae and push-and-pull are all 'the transmitting of information' is open to doubt (**polite empty talk may be observing another set of maxims** ('The social functions of language' in Lesson 10)))))).
:::

## Face: Chinese on its home ground {#def-mianzi}

::: definition Face theory and Chinese usage {#def-lian}
**Received account** (Brown and Levinson: face divides into positive face (the wish to be approved of) and negative face (one's actions not being hindered), and many linguistic strategies exist in order to lower the degree of threat of a face-threatening act — **two peculiarities of Chinese** (**①** **face is common property** (“给面子、不给面子、留面子、伤面子、看在他面子上”: 'to give face, not to give face, to leave some face, to injure face, for the sake of his face' — in Chinese, face can be transferred between third parties (**this makes 'face' an account that can be reckoned, called in, and reneged on** (**a modern version of Chapter 4, 'Rightness is a credit record'; it also explains why “当众” ('in public', 'before everyone') is the heaviest modifier in Chinese** (**②** **“脸” and “面” should perhaps be entered in separate books** (**received account:** some scholars hold that *liǎn* 脸 is close to moral reputation and *miàn* 面 is close to the prestige of status; the distinction is still disputed in scholarship, but it can be told apart in everyday linguistic feel (“不要脸” and “没面子” are plainly not the same offence)); **this lesson's treatment:** no anthropological verdict, and only one linguistic thing — **to count which sentences are used to maintain which** (**this can be done:** “您赏个脸” ('Grant me the favour of your face' — asking the other party to give face), “别让我难做人” ('Don't make it hard for me to show my face' — asking a third party not to damage one's own moral reputation))).
:::

::: definition Refusal engineering in Chinese {#def-jujue}
**Received account** (research in cross-cultural pragmatics shows that native speakers of Chinese, when refusing a request, more often use indirect strategies: delay, diversion, giving a reason beyond their control, asking a third party to endorse it — **this lesson calls the set refusal engineering** — **its standard procedure:** ① first accept the willingness (“我真的很想帮” — 'I really do want to help'); **② then deliver a force majeure** (“实在不巧／家里那位不同意／单位刚说” — 'it really is awkward / the one at home does not agree / the work unit has just said'); **③ finally add an alternative, or a promise for the future** (**note the technical content of the second step:** the responsibility must be shifted onto an object that cannot be rebutted (**Chapter 5, 'Harm with no defendant', shows its use here:** in Chinese, a credible refusal needs a reason that cannot be negotiated (**so “我妈不让” ('Mum won't let me') is not childish; it is precise pragmatic design** (**this is also why the two characters “不行” ('no', 'that won't do') are close to rude in adult Chinese:** they omit the whole procedure, which amounts to declaring that I do not need to maintain your face (**and maintaining face is precisely the voucher for maintaining the relationship**))))).
:::

## Deixis, presupposition and discourse {#def-zhishi}

::: definition Deictics and Chinese “这” and “那” {#def-zhishi2}
**Received account** (deixis divides into person, space, time and social deixis; Chinese has, in addition, discourse deixis and deixis of tone in “这／那” — **three facts that can be taught** (**①** **“这” and “那” can point to time** (“这阵子忙”, “那年我还小”: 'busy this stretch of days', 'that year I was still small' — the speaker stands inside or outside the time pointed to, and this makes a subjective distance in time (**received account: this contrast of near and far is used heavily in Chinese narrative for the contrast of “当年／如今” ('in those days / nowadays')** (Lesson 12, 'Time as the opponent')); ② **“那” has a derogatory pragmatic colouring** (“他那叫什么文章”, “你那是关心我吗” — 'What sort of an essay do you call that of his?', 'Is that of yours concern for me?' (Our translation.) — **one deictic word carries out the whole evaluation** (**this is why changing “那是” to “这是” can turn a sentence from sarcasm into a declaration of where one stands** (**③ social deixis in Chinese is an entire grammar of terms of address** (您／你, 老师／师傅／大哥／老板, and 'surname + post' together with 'given name + 哥／姐': Chinese uses the form of address to carry the social information that other languages carry by morphology (**choosing the wrong address is a pragmatic failure, not a grammatical error, and its consequences are ten times as serious** (**the worked example in this chapter does exactly this**)))))).
:::

::: definition Presupposition: the ground beneath the sentence {#def-yushe}
**Received account** (some content is the part of a sentence that is taken as true by default (“他又迟到了” presupposes that he has been late before: 'He was late again.' (Our translation.)); questions, negation and conditional clauses make up the problem of 'projection' — **two uses in this lesson** (**①** **the complex question** (“你已经不再偷懒了吗”: 'Have you already stopped slacking?' (Our translation.) — answer yes or answer no, and one has admitted to having slacked before (**this is the commonest device in everyday Chinese and in the discourse of interrogation** (**an application of Lesson 3, 'Presupposition and the implied', and of Lesson 6, 'Clearing the ground'**)); ② **apology and admission** (“如果让你不舒服了，我道歉” presupposes 'you may have been made uncomfortable', and yet cancels 'I did that thing': 'If I made you uncomfortable, I apologise.' (Our translation.) (**so this polite sentence is, pragmatically, one refusal to own the account** (**this lesson gives sentences of this kind a name: the disclaimer construction** (**the pragmatic version of Chapter 6, 'Playful writing = a disclaimer', and of Lesson 2, 'The mediation document'** (**and the only tool for recognising it is knowing how to take sentence after sentence apart into the three layers of the literal, the presupposition and the illocutionary force**)))))).
:::

::: definition Cooperation, politeness and the push-and-pull {#def-lache}
**Received account** (Leech sets the politeness principle out as six maxims: tact, generosity, approbation, modesty, agreement and sympathy; the Chinese 'modesty maxim' has long been observed to be stronger than the 'maxim of quality' — **this is the “哪里哪里” phenomenon** (**someone praises you, and you deny it** (“您做的菜真好吃” “哪有，随便弄弄” — 'The food you made is really delicious.' 'Not at all; I just threw it together.' (Our translation.) — **under Grice's framework it is a violation of quality (saying what is false); under Leech's framework it is standard politeness** (**this lesson's conclusion:** the two sets of maxims will conflict, and the way the conflict is resolved is a parameter of the language community (**so a cross-cultural misreading is often not ill will, but a difference of parameters: the guest cannot hear that “随便弄弄” is etiquette, and the host cannot hear that “真的很好吃” ('it really is very good') is sincerity** (**a change that can be checked:** in the younger generation's replies to praise, the proportion of “谢谢” ('thank you') has risen markedly, and that of “哪里哪里” has fallen (**this is one of the few directly observable cases of a pragmatic parameter being rewritten in the course of one generation** (Chapter 4, 'A slot that is disappearing': this slot is the polite formula (**the reason for its disappearance is probably not Westernisation, but that the only-child generation lacks a family rehearsal-ground for 'yielding in public'** (**this is a hypothesis, and it must be tested on intergenerational corpus data**))))))).
:::

::: example Ten ways of doing a single “不” {#ex-bu}
Task: put a “不” into a Chinese dialogue of 120 characters, requiring that **no direct wording of refusal appear anywhere in the piece**, while the reader can be sure that the “不” has already been spoken; then write 60 characters: **which three steps you used, onto whom the responsibility was shifted, and whose face this utterance maintained (positive or negative)**.
::: solution
**Reference text** (“王：这周六您过来坐坐？李：哎哟，周六……我跟您说，我妈那天复查，我这心里一直悬着。再说我这一摊子事，您也不是不知道。等我这边缓过来，我第一时间给您打电话，真的。” Wang: 'Come and sit a while this Saturday?' Li: 'Aiya, Saturday… Let me tell you, my mum has a follow-up that day, and my heart has been hanging the whole time. Besides, this whole pile of things on my side — it's not as if you didn't know. Once I've come through over here, I'll phone you at the first moment, truly.' (Our translation.)); **the three steps** — **accepting the willingness is omitted, and “哎哟” carries it** (**emotion first, and no promise**); force majeure (the mother's follow-up = a reason that cannot be negotiated and cannot be blamed); the alternative (“我给您打电话” = taking the decision back into one's own hands, because the time is mine to set (**the responsibility is shifted onto the mother and onto “一摊子事”:** neither is Li's fault, and neither can be rebutted by Wang (**the face reckoning** — **Wang receives positive face (invited into the intimate circle of “您也不是不知道”, and given a promise for the future); Li keeps negative face (not forced to declare a position on the spot); **the cost is vagueness:** Wang does not know whether he was truly someone they wanted to invite, and Li also knows that next time this debt will still have to be repaid (**this lesson's marking:** leave the 'cost' column unwritten and there is no mark (**an indirect refusal in Chinese is not politeness free of charge; it is a debt that bears interest (**this is precisely where this chapter and Chapter 4, 'Rightness is a credit record', join in method: pragmatic strategy can be described as bookkeeping, because it was bookkeeping to begin with**))))).
:::

::: intuition The three layers of “意思” {#int-sanceng}
**Three questions can be asked of one and the same stretch of talk** (**① what the literal wording said (semantics); ② what one was doing in saying this sentence (illocutionary force: request, refusal, threat, flirtation); ③ what the other party will do once it has been said (perlocutionary effect: whether he will come, whether he will bear a grudge)** (**the three can fail to coincide at all** (“你要不去就算了” is, literally, the giving of a choice; its force is applying pressure; its effect is often really to make the other party not go: 'If you don't want to go, then forget it.' (Our translation.) (**this lesson's use in teaching:** in the analysis of any interpersonal dispute, write these three columns out, and the quarrel is at once cut by half (**this is the course's only contribution to 'communication skills', and its most solid one** (**and linguistics gives it one further advantage: the three columns do not depend on sincerity; they depend on evidence —** **you can quote the original sentence, you can point to the presupposition, you can show whose face was reckoned wrong** (Lesson 1, the study of evidence: **pragmatics is the layer easiest to verify, because the consequences happen on the spot**)))))).
:::

::: history Three rectifications of the name of pragmatics {#sec-zhengming}
**1930s–1950s** (**both the study of grammar and logical positivism set out to clear away 'emotion and use':** **pragmatics was for a time a dirty word**); **around 1955 Austin lectured at Harvard on 'How to Do Things with Words', and the text of the lectures was published in 1962 (received account)** — **an explosion from within:** logicians found that ordinary language is not bad logic, but another set of rules (**1960s–1970s** (**Grice's paper of 1975, Searle's revised classification of speech acts, and the analysis of conversational structure (turn-taking, adjacency pairs, repair mechanisms** (**received account:** pragmatics acquired a methodology of its own: **it relies on recordings, not on introspection** (**this step matters more than the theory** (**1970s–1980s** (**face theory, cross-cultural pragmatics, language transfer** (**received account: one finding cited again and again is that a second-language user's grammatical errors are often forgiven, while a pragmatic failure is often taken for a defect of character** (**and so 'pragmatic competence' entered the syllabus of language teaching** (**this was the first time pragmatics changed the classroom**); **2000s to the present** (**online communication has made a new terrain of the pragmatic layer** (**received account: punctuation and modal particles, emoji, delay in replying, and whether the original wording is quoted, have all become carriers of politeness value** (**reports of the kind “句号显得冷淡” ('a full stop looks cold') are not a joke:** the function of a symbol is pragmatic, because it appears inside the particular expectations of a particular channel (Chapter 1, the format of distribution: **pragmatics always lives on the channel** — **this is the first sentence at the close of this chapter, and the whole of the preview of the next**))))))))).
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls in this chapter
**Equating politeness with sincerity** (**pragmatics does not measure what is in the heart; it only measures function:** “哪里哪里” may come from genuine feeling, and it may come from habit (**to condemn polite formulae as hypocrisy is a moral judgement; to analyse polite formulae is pragmatics** (**do not mix the two** (Lesson 1, description and prescription)))); **taking indirectness for vagueness** (**an indirect refusal in Chinese is extremely clear inside the community:** “再说吧” is not “待定” ('to be decided'); it is a polite form of “不” (**to translate it as 'to be decided' is a misreading across communities, not a defect of the language**)); **reading another community by the parameters of this one** (**to hear “随便弄弄” as modesty, or to hear it as self-belittling, is in either case an assumption about parameters** (the push-and-pull clause above)); **supposing that pragmatics can be taught** (**pragmatic knowledge is transmitted far less efficiently than grammar:** it needs the occasion, the risk, and repetition (**this is also why an adult learner can speak fluently and still 'not seem one of our own'** (Lesson 10: the best instrument for measuring how far a syllabus can teach))).
:::

::: exercise Annotating illocutionary force {level=1}
Take five sentences of everyday Chinese (customer service, a parents' group, or the workplace — any one), and for each mark **the literal, the illocutionary force, and the expected perlocutionary effect**; point out one sentence in which the three contradict one another.
::: solution
Marking: **the illocutionary force must be named with a verb** (**request, press, sound out, show goodwill, dissociate:** one may not write 'say it euphemistically' (**the 'expected perlocutionary effect' must state whose face it depends on, and which kind:** otherwise one has only changed the words, and not analysed (the marking point of the worked example))).
:::

::: exercise Measuring the parameters {level=2}
From two groups you can reach (by age, region or trade — any one of these), collect five replies each to praise or to an invitation, and report in 100 characters: **the difference between the two sides in 'priority among the maxims'**.
::: solution
Marking: **the sample must be countable** (**ten or more:** five can only count as anecdote (Lesson 3: survivors are discounted to eighty per cent)); **one may not conclude that 'some group is more polite'** (**it must be written 'on a given type of speech act, group A more often chooses an indirect form'; otherwise it is pitfall ③ of this chapter**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: pragmatics is a ledger {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing '**The most profound discovery of pragmatics is not that we have meaning beyond the words, but that meaning beyond the words can be reckoned: who is qualified to give a direct order to whom, who owes whom a face, who may, under the gaze of every eye, withdraw which sentence — all of this is entered in an account book with no cover, and the bookkeepers are the hearers, all of them. What an outsider finds hardest to learn is therefore not a thousand words, but the balance of this account; and for the same reason, what is called a light accent and a good attitude is often only that a person happens not to have to pay those accounts**'. You must call on at least six items: **the conditions of qualification on a speech act**, **degree of indirectness as a measurement of distance**, **the cancellability of Grice's maxims, and their conflict**, **face as an account that can be transferred**, **the three steps of refusal engineering**, **the channel's making a terrain of pragmatics**; and give a verdict: **whether pragmatic competence constitutes a kind of inequality (who is required to be more indirect) — you must give one concrete situation and state evidence that can be checked**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**qualification, measurement, conflict, account, procedure, terrain**); the verdict (**a passing answer must point out the direction of the asymmetry, and it must be measurable —** for example, subordinate towards superior, the party who serves towards the customer, women towards men who are strangers (**what can be checked: count the degree of indirectness when the same request is issued, in a Chinese corpus, by different identities (sentence length, question form, the proportion of reason-clauses)** (**marking looks at whether a quantitative gauge of 'whose account is dearer' is given, and it requires that two simplifications be avoided: calling politeness a virtue (it is the lubricant of an inequality**); calling impoliteness honesty (it is often only that one need not pay the account (if the conclusion can be written '**So pragmatics is a discipline of balances: being able to speak straight is a privilege, just as being able to keep silent is a status; and what linguistics can do is to peel these two kinds of luck off moral character and lay them on the account**', that is an A))))).
:::

::: summary
Filed under doing: the act (**the sentence is part of the event, so there is being qualified and not being qualified enough**), indirectness (**the politeness value of Chinese is an instrument for measuring distance**), implicature (**cancellable, non-detachable, not dependent on awareness**), face (**an account that can be transferred between third parties**), refusal (**three steps, and one reason that cannot be rebutted**), the channel (**even a full stop has a politeness value**). The method in one sentence: **write the literal, the illocutionary force and the perlocutionary effect in three columns, and then write down who paid the account**.
