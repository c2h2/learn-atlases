---
title: "The nature of language"
---

The previous chapter settled which level one is speaking at; this chapter asks what material speaking uses — **language is a productive device: finite materials, infinite products; the four pillars of this chapter are arbitrariness, two-level structure, value and displacement, and each must be pressed once through Chinese, because Chinese has left its own scratch on every one of them**.

## Definition: the sign and its three levels of motivation {#def-sanheng}

::: definition Arbitrariness is not the absence of a reason {#def-renyi}
**The received account** (**Saussure: between the signifier and the signified of a sign there is no motivation** ("树" ('tree') and shù stand in no physical relation) — **but this sentence is often read as "language may do whatever it likes", which is to read it exactly backwards** (**this lesson keeps the books on "motivation" under three heads** (**①** **between sound and meaning: arbitrary, and already frozen** (**you cannot persuade anyone to change the reading of "的";** **this is a cost item of the system, and the reason it is conservative** (the previous chapter: "arbitrariness at the word level, obligatoriness at the system level") **); ②** **between morphemes: demonstrable** (**"电脑、火车、内卷、可降解"** ('computer', 'train', 'involution', 'degradable'): **compounding and derivation give new words a local motivation, so the great majority of new words are not made out of nothing, but assembled from old parts** (the entrance to the morphology lesson of Chapter 4) **); ③** **between uses: arbitrary, yet movable** (**extension, metaphor, euphemistic avoidance, parody:** this is the place where change of word meaning, puns and identity-marking all happen (**the conclusion of this chapter, in one sentence:** arbitrariness is a commitment at the level of the system, while the whole of literature and the whole of fashion live on the second and third levels**.
:::

::: definition Peirce's three-part division {#def-pierce}
**Image, index and sign** (**the received account: iconic (resembling its object), indexical (co-occurring with its object), symbolic (resting on convention) —** this lesson uses that division for one concrete task: **to explain why onomatopoeic words are not a counter-example to arbitrariness** (**① onomatopoeia is iconic, but once it enters a language it has to be remodelled by that language's phonology ("喵" ('meow') differs from language to language in both the number of syllables and the tone); ② such words are few, their productivity is low, and most new words do not take this route at all (**so the correct formulation is:** language is symbolic in the main, and the other two kinds are borrowed only within limits; **the poet's very trade is to magnify the latter two** (Lesson 12, "Alliteration, rhyming reduplication and sound-glossing"): **to stuff iconicity back into a symbolic system is an old craft of rhetoric**.
:::

## Two-level structure and productivity {#def-erceng}

::: definition The economics of the two levels {#def-jingji}
**The received account** (**meaningful units (morphemes, words) are made up of meaningless units (phonemes) —** this was a revolution in cost (**remember a few dozen meaningless pieces, and combination will generate endlessly many meaningful ones;** **storage is exchanged for computation** (**the Chinese parallel this lesson offers:** the Chinese character is another two-level structure (a few hundred components, some tens of thousands of characters): **two two-level structures have been stacked in Chinese for more than a thousand years** (the precondition for Lesson 1, "Chinese characters and Pinyin"; it is also what the calligraphy lesson of Lesson 12 means by "the etymological meaning of a component is often still there" — **the arbitrariness of speech and the motivation of writing coexist in the same sentence, and this is one reason readers of Chinese are unusually sensitive to graphic form**.
:::

::: definition The recursion dispute {#def-digui}
**The received account** (**Chomsky holds that recursion (embedding a structure inside a structure of the same kind) is the core mechanism of human language; one study took a language of the Amazon as a counter-example and proposed that recursion is not necessary, and dispute followed —** this lesson's way of handling it (**①** **first take the terms apart** (**"clause embedding" and "coordinate chaining" are two different things:** long sentences in Chinese commonly use chaining ("他说不想去" ('he says he does not want to go'), and multi-level complex sentences), whereas deep embedding is rare in speech (**so the dispute over "whether Chinese prefers recursion" is, for the most part, a dispute about register** (**②** **the levels of productivity** (**phonological rules, word-formation and syntax are all productive, but the degree of productivity differs, and it changes with the times** (**"-ness、可～、～族、微～" suddenly become highly productive in certain years (**productivity is a measurable time series, not a static property of a grammar book (Lesson 1, the study of evidence: **here the evidence of a corpus is better than intuition**); **③** **the price of a counter-example** (**a single counter-example can overturn a universal proposition, and can also overturn a definition:** if "recursion" is revised until even coordination counts as recursion, it is no longer refutable (Lesson 3, refutability: **this is the thing that happens most often in the dispute over "an innate endowment for language" — changing the definition in order to save the theory**.
:::

::: definition Value: what the system confers {#def-jiazhi}
**The received account** (**Saussure: "value" is not the same as "signification"; French mouton and English sheep do not mean the same, because each system cuts differently —** this is the most useful abstraction in the whole course (**because it explains every case of "untranslatability"** (not that no word can be found, but that the two systems cut differently at that point (**the four places where this lesson takes it up again:** the rhyme dictionary (a character's "sound" is fixed by the rhyme category it belongs to), the phoneme (the distinctive value of a sound exists only in opposition), metre (the sound-value of a character is fixed by the position the system of level and oblique tones cuts out on it), and literature in translation (Chapter 8, the borrowed mirror: **what you borrow is not the word, but someone else's cutting**).
:::

## Displacement, fiction and the device {#def-zhiyi}

::: definition Speaking of what is not present {#def-buzai}
**The received account** (**displacement: language can refer to the past, the future, the hypothetical and the impossible —** the literary corollary in this lesson (**①** **literature is the professional abuse of displacement** (**the definition from the previous chapter receives its material here:** getting people to cooperate over what has not happened rests precisely on language already being able to frame propositions about what has not happened; **②** **law and money are the same** (**"the contract provides for three years hence" and "this piece of paper is worth three hundred yuan" are both propositions of displacement, endorsed by an institution** (**③** **a boundary that can be tested** (**animal communication is for the most part limited to food and danger here and now (the received account) —** so if the ability to "talk about what does not exist" is unique to humans, then fiction is not a luxury of culture but a standard output of the device (**this chapter does not adjudicate the question of uniqueness; it only registers the standing of this evidence: it calls for cross-species experiments, and cross-species experiments are extremely hard to control (Lesson 1, the study of evidence: **propositions of this kind can at present rely only on ①＋④, and cannot rely on ③**.
:::

::: definition The innate and the acquired {#def-xiantian}
**Four arguments, and how strong each is** (**①** **poverty of the stimulus** (**the sentences a child hears are finite, and the sentences a child produces are infinite —** forceful, but it depends on the measurement that "what the child hears is in fact not very much", whereas counts over natural corpora show that the amount of input is far greater than the early estimates (**this weakens it**); ② **the critical period, and the universal order of language acquisition** (**stronger:** a universal order is hard to explain by teaching alone; **③** **creolisation** (**when a pidgin becomes a creole, new grammar appears —** often taken as the key evidence for an innate device; the dispute is that the contact situation is itself already complex enough (**④** **the dissociation of specific language impairment and autism** (**there are people with a selective language impairment whose general cognition is normal —** evidence from dissociation has value, but the cases are few and the diagnostic criteria are not uniform (**the methodological conclusion of this lesson (**the "innateness thesis" has not been proved, and it has not been overturned either; what has been proved is this:** these four pieces of evidence come from different workshops, and to line them up as "four supports" is a piece of rhetoric, not an argument (Lesson 1, "the chain of evidence", and Lesson 3, independent sources: **this lesson is itself a textbook**.
:::

::: example The social life of three homophones {#ex-tongyin}
The following three phenomena all rest on the phonological cutting of Chinese. For each, fill in: **which level of motivation it uses, what its main function is (humour? avoidance? identity?), and why it is not easy to reproduce in English**.
① "蓝瘦香菇" ② "555／886" ③ brand names by homophony (of the "骑乐无穷" kind)
::: solution
**① 蓝瘦香菇** — **level: sound and meaning (**it uses the system's licence that "if the sound is close enough, it may stand in for the time being"**); function: **what comes first is not humour but deniability (**a complaint written out as the name of a food is not easy for a platform, or for an acquaintance, to call to account (Chapter 8, "encryption as a protocol of secrecy"; humour is its by-product; **why it is hard to reproduce** (**the fit of syllable to morpheme in English is far less neat than in Chinese:** in Chinese one syllable is very often one morpheme, so a whole sentence can be replaced, character by character, by another string of meaningful syllables (**this is not "Chinese is fun"; the cut is coarse**); **② homophones in digits** — **level: the standard written form, and input (**it is at the same time a product of Pinyin input methods —** the conditions of production once again determine the kind of text (the same kind of thing as the economy of paying calls in Chapter 4**); function: **speed＋recognition inside the circle**; **③ homophones in brand names** — **level: the morpheme (**what is replaced is the morpheme inside a homophonous syllable (**what it offends is the norm of graphic form, not the grammar (so the penalty comes from the administration of writing, not from grammar (**this column is of great diagnostic value: whichever level a society punishes first is the level it treats as order**); **the verdict overall** — **the three phenomena together prove:** inside an arbitrary system there arises a great deal of iconicity that is local and that can be traded; these are not exceptions to language, but by-products of language put to another use — **and every such use needs a motive (avoidance, speed, or advertising):** **this is exactly why "language is arbitrary" and "Chinese likes homophony" can be true at the same time (**the marking point of this chapter:** anyone who sets the two against each other is taking a property of the system level for a prohibition at the vocabulary level**.
:::
:::

::: intuition The cycle of euphemistic avoidance: the three levels of motivation in concert {#int-xunhuan}
**The received account** (**a euphemism, as time passes, takes on the colour of the thing it was meant to veil, and so a new euphemism is required (of the kind "残废—残疾—身心障碍" ('crippled — disabled — physical and mental impairment'); the substitutes for "死" ('death') are continually renewed) —** this cycle is the best specimen of the three levels of motivation working at once (**① sound and meaning are arbitrary:** the substitute may be picked at will, because motivation does not constrain it; **② morphemes are demonstrable:** a new word always borrows old parts ("障碍" ('obstacle') was originally a term for roads**); **③ use is movable:** once the word co-occurs frequently with people, praise and blame stain it (**and so moral progress shows itself, in the vocabulary, as inflation:** the same fund of goodwill has to have its banknote reprinted once every twenty years (**the warning of this lesson:** do not take the inflation for a decline (**and do not take the new word for progress:** what decides a group's situation is its opportunities, not its words (Lesson 9, "Language and society", will deal head-on with the old question of "whether a politically correct vocabulary has changed attitudes"; here we only make one entry in the books**.
:::

::: history Three generations of one concept {#sec-sandai}
**Classical and medieval** (**the received account: the dispute over whether names are "nature" or "convention" (of the kind of the *Cratylus*) —** note: this debate of two thousand years ago has still not changed its question (**arbitrariness versus motivation, and innateness versus use, are precisely its present form (**this is why the present chapter is worth telling as history —** Lesson 11, "the history of ideas": **some questions are not unsolved; it is that the way of solving them expires**); **1898–1950s** (**Chinese is brought into this set of questions:** the dispute over the framework of *Mashi wentong* 《马氏文通》 ('Mr Ma's comprehensive account of the written language'), Gwoyeu Romatzyh and the dialect surveys, and Zhao Yuanren (Yuen Ren Chao) on tone and on the "agent–patient" description (**for Chinese, "arbitrariness" has one further opponent: graphic form (**Chinese characters incline the users of Chinese, by nature, to look for motivation in the graphic form (Lesson 12, the culture of Chinese characters**)); **1950s–1980s** (**structuralism and generative grammar were each institutionalised; at the same time "Chomskyan linguistics" became a word in popular use, meaning any anti-authority style of writing (**once a theory has been borrowed as a pose, its fallibility is forgotten by the public (Lesson 15, retrospective recognition**)); **1980s–the present** (**cognitive linguistics, corpora, experiment and computational models (**the received account: large language models have made "whether linguistic ability needs an internal device of the same structure" a question that can be discussed again (**the closing reminder of this chapter:** any claim that "a model has already proved or overturned the theory of innateness" ought to be downgraded on the table of "four kinds of evidence" — **a model has no native speaker, and so no judgement of acceptability; it has only distribution; and distribution is precisely the weakest kind on that table in Lesson 1, and it was once the newest kind as well**.
:::

::: warning Four pitfalls of this lesson
**"Onomatopoeic words prove that language is not arbitrary"** (**letting the exception stand in for the statistics:** one must at the same time give the proportion of onomatopoeic words and the evidence that they are remodelled in the phonology (the Peirce clause above**)); **"Arbitrary, and therefore open to change"** (**confusing the three levels:** the sound–meaning level is frozen, the morpheme level is demonstrable, the level of use is movable (**all language planning is effective only at the third level (Lesson 9, language policy**)); **"Untranslatability shows that Chinese is special"** (**no language can be translated completely:** this is a universal property of the system (value**)); **using "an innate endowment for language" to fight a philosophical battle** (**treating four pieces of evidence from different workshops as four independent supports,** and assuming that "the acquired thesis" means language is nothing but habit (**yet habit cannot explain this: the correction a child receives is limited, and still the child produces sentences never heard —** this chapter asks only that you tell apart which are observations that can be checked, and which is the metaphysics fitted to them.
:::

::: exercise Coining a word by levels {level=1}
Coin a Chinese word for an experience that has no name (for example, "the state of having read halfway and having no intention of finishing"), and state: **which of the three levels of motivation you have drawn on, and why the word might be accepted**.
::: solution
Marking: **a pure transliteration, or a pure code, may not be used** (**that does not count as drawing on motivation:** compounding, derivation, or a shift in the sense of an old word all count (**"why it might be accepted" must say what the word saves:** a saving in the number of characters, a saving in explanation, or a saving in risk (the three correspond to the three levels of motivation**).
:::
:::

::: exercise Pricing a counter-example {level=2}
Find a popular expression which linguistic purists call "ungrammatical", and write 100 characters: **what part of it is legitimate (distribution), what part is being opposed (level), and what the opponents are really opposing (identity, vagueness, or morality)**.
::: solution
Marking: **the three items must not be merged** (**to merge them is to write an opinion about identity up as a grammatical judgement (Lesson 1, the table of jurisdiction**); **the description of the distribution must be able to bear a substitution test** (**give two contrasting sentences that can be said and two that cannot:** only that is evidence of "whether it conforms or not"**).
:::

::: exercise Short essay: cutting is sovereignty {level=3}
Write from six hundred to one thousand characters, assessing the claim that "**the most profound property of a language is not what it can express, but where it cuts the continuous world: where the cut is coarse, the members can be exchanged for one another, and so homophony, puns and argot arise; where the cut is fine, each member keeps to its own cell, and so precision and untranslatability arise. In comparing two languages, therefore, what carries the most information is not which of them has more words, but how they bring the knife down on the same stretch of experience; and when one language is remodelled by another, what gives way first is never the word, but the edge of the knife**". You must call on at least six items: **the three levels of motivation**, **Peirce's three-part division and the standing of onomatopoeic words**, **two-level structure and the two two-level systems of the Chinese character**, **the problem of terms in the recursion dispute**, **value and untranslatability**, **the cycle of euphemistic avoidance**; and you must rule on this: **a claim of the kind "writing affects thought" — within the framework of this chapter, as a proposition of what strength ought it to be stated? You must give a version that can be tested by experiment, and say what corpus or what experiment it needs**.
::: solution
Marking points: the six mechanisms (**three levels, the three-part division, the two two-level systems, terms, value, the cycle**); the ruling (**an acceptable version must be downgraded to a concrete claim that can be tested —** for example: "a writing system that cuts language with the morpheme–syllable as its unit will make users respond faster on tasks of phonological segmentation, and will show a higher usability in games of homophonic substitution and in the production of argot" (**the test: a reaction-time experiment on phonological segmentation across writing systems (for example three groups, illiterate, alphabetic writing and Chinese characters, or a pre-test and post-test of the same group of children learning Pinyin), together with corpus counts of homophone production (**the marking looks at whether the sources of confounding are pointed out:** level of literacy, the language itself, and differences of reading experience often co-vary with the writing system (**this is why "writing affects thought" is hard to confirm and hard to falsify**); if the conclusion can be written "**so the real conclusion of this chapter is a method in one sentence: do not take the edge of the knife for a joint of the world; that two languages cut the same experience differently is precisely the proof that the experience did not come already cut**", that is an A.
:::

::: summary
The device, filed: arbitrariness (**three levels of motivation: frozen, demonstrable, movable**), two levels (**storage exchanged for computation; the Chinese character is a second two-level structure**), value (**the system sets the price: untranslatability is not a pity, but a cut**), recursion (**clear the terms first, and only then dispute innateness**), innateness (**four pieces of evidence from different workshops**), the cycle (**the inflation of euphemism: goodwill has to be reprinted once every twenty years**). The method, in one sentence: **do not ask whether it can express something; ask where it brings the knife down**. Next chapter: speech sounds and phonology — how sound becomes a system.
:::
