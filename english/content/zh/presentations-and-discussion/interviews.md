Zhou Xin 和 Gabriel Santos 都在参加一家城市博物馆学生讲解员兼职工作的面试。两人被问到同一个问题：*Tell us about a time when you had to deal with a difficult situation in a team.*

**Zhou Xin:** *I think teamwork is very important. In my university, we often do group projects, and sometimes there are problems, for example some members don't do their work. In this situation, I think good communication is the key. We should talk to each other and find a solution together. I always try my best to help the team.*

**Gabriel:** *Last year I was in a group of four doing a survey project for a statistics course. Two weeks before the deadline, one member stopped replying to messages, and his section — the data analysis — wasn't started. I suggested a short call with the others, and we agreed I'd contact him directly rather than complain to the tutor straight away. It turned out he was ill and embarrassed to say so. We split his section between the three of us, he took on the final proof-reading when he recovered, and we handed in on time. We got a good mark, but the main thing I learned was to check in with people early, before a silence becomes a crisis.*

Zhou Xin 的英语很准确，她的看法也很合理。但她回答的是另一个问题——*What do you think about teamwork?*——面试小组对她实际做过什么一无所知。Gabriel 讲了一个具体的故事，有情境、他自己的行动、一个结果，以及他学到的东西。其中每一句话都是证据。面试官会给 Gabriel 的回答打更高的分，不是因为他的经历更了不起，而是因为他给了他们可以打分的东西。

本章讲的是求职面试和学术面试：面试官如何作出判断，如何准备，如何回答行为类、动机类和情境类问题，如何介绍自己的成绩而既不吹嘘也不隐藏，以及如何提出自己的好问题。本章把[[presentations-and-discussion/handling-questions]]中的技能——听完整个问题并直接回答——应用到一场每个回答都在被掂量的对话中。

## 面试官在寻找什么

::: definition 面试和面试问题的种类 {#def-interview}
- 在**结构化面试**（structured interview）中，每位候选人被问到同样的问题，这些问题通常以对工作的分析为基础，回答按商定的标准打分；在**非结构化面试**（unstructured interview）中，面试官问任何看起来有用的问题，判断是整体性的。
- **行为类问题**（behavioural question）问的是你在过去某个情境中**做了**什么：*Tell me about a time when…* / *Give me an example of…*
- **情境类问题**（situational question）问的是你在一个假设情境中**会做**什么：*What would you do if a visitor complained about…?*
- **动机类问题**（motivational question）问的是你为什么想要这个职位，以及你能带来什么：*Why do you want to work here?* / *Why this programme?*
- **胜任力**（competency，也叫 *criterion* 或 *selection criterion*，选拔标准）是这个职位所要求的素质——团队合作、沟通、解决问题、可靠——常常列在招聘广告或**人员规格说明**（person specification）中。
:::

关于人员选拔的研究一再发现，结构化面试比非结构化面试更能预测工作表现。在施密特和亨特（Frank Schmidt and John Hunter）对八十五年研究所作的被广泛引用的综述（1998，*Psychological Bulletin* 124:2）中，结构化面试的估计效度为0.51，非结构化面试为0.38，在这个量表上，1表示完美的预测。萨克特（Paul Sackett）及其同事后来的再分析（2022，*Journal of Applied Psychology* 107:11）调低了其中许多估计值，但仍把结构化面试列为最有力的绩效预测指标之一。关于如何构建面试结构的综述（Campion, Palmer and Campion 1997，*Personnel Psychology* 50:3；Levashina et al. 2014，*Personnel Psychology* 67:1）描述了其主要特点：以工作为基础的问题，对每位候选人问同样的问题，行为类和情境类问题，以及附有好回答和差回答示例的评分量表。

::: theorem 回答被打分的那个问题 {#rule-scored}
在结构化面试中，每个问题都旨在寻找某项特定胜任力的证据。在回答之前，先判断**这个问题考查的是哪项胜任力**，然后给出能为它提供**具体证据**的回答。关于这项胜任力的看法（*Teamwork is very important*）不是证据；你做过什么的叙述才是。
:::

::: proof
当面试官按标准给回答打分时，一个笼统的看法——不管多么合理——里面没有任何与标准相符的东西，所以得分很低。一个具体的例子则包含评分指南所描述的行动和结果。这就是为什么即使面试官喜欢 Zhou Xin，她的回答也会被扣分：面试官没有证据可以填进表格。知道面试常常是结构化的，也能解释候选人注意到的其他特点：面试小组可能照着一张纸念问题，自始至终做笔记，对回答不太有反应。这些都不是冷淡；这是使面试更公平、更有预测力的程序。
:::

## 准备

::: application 为面试作准备
1. **阅读招聘广告和人员规格说明**，列出其中的胜任力。如果是申请学术名额，就阅读项目介绍，以及将会给你授课或指导你的那些人的著作。
2. 从你的学习、工作、志愿服务或其他活动中**准备六到八个故事**，每个故事展示一项或多项胜任力。一个故事可以用于好几个问题。
3. **准备可以预测的问题**：*Tell me about yourself*、*Why this role or programme?*、*What are your strengths?*、*What would you find difficult?*、*What questions do you have for us?*
4. **大声排练**，请一位朋友按与你笔记不同的顺序提问。给你的回答计时：大多数回答应该用一到两分钟。
5. **核实实际细节**：时间、地点或链接，面试小组里有谁，有没有陈述或任务。
:::

## 回答行为类问题

::: definition STAR {#def-star}
**STAR** 是一种被广泛讲授的回答行为类问题的结构：

- **情境**（Situation）：简短的背景——在哪里、什么时候、发生了什么。
- **任务**（Task）：你必须达成什么，或者你面临什么问题。
- **行动**（Action）：**你**做了什么，一步一步地，以及为什么。
- **结果**（Result）：发生了什么，尽可能附上证据——常常还加上第五步：你**学到**了什么，或者你会有什么不同的做法。
:::

::: theorem 讲一个具体的故事，并让它成为你的故事 {#rule-star}
回答行为类问题时：

1. **选择一个具体的场合**，而不是概括你通常怎么做：说 *Last year, in a group of four…*，而不是 *In group projects, I always…*
2. **情境和任务要简短**——各一两句话——把**大部分时间花在行动上**。
3. **你做的事用 *I* 来说**，只有小组共同做的事才用 *we*。面试小组评估的是你。
4. **给出结果**，能用数字或具体成果就用，最后说出你学到了什么。
5. 讲故事用**一般过去时**（*I suggested…*、*we agreed…*），讲你学到的东西用现在时（*I now check in early…*）。
:::

::: proof
具体的故事比笼统的主张更可信，因为它有面试官可以相信或追问的细节；而概括一个人“总是”怎么做，就其本质而言，不是任何具体事情的证据。候选人常常把大部分回答花在情境上，因为要理解这个故事，背景似乎是必要的；但胜任力在行动中，所以行动值得占用大部分时间。*I* 很重要，因为一个完全用 *we* 讲的故事，会让面试官无法分辨候选人贡献了什么；对于习惯把成绩说成是集体成绩的候选人来说，这可能感觉不够谦虚，但在选拔面试中，它只是准确而已。结果使故事完整，而反思——你学到了什么——显示了改进的能力，许多评分指南会明确为此加分。
:::

::: example 重建 Zhou Xin 的回答 {#ex-star}
Zhou Xin 本来有一个真实的故事可以讲：在一个关于博物馆参观人数的小组项目中，两个成员对用在线问卷还是用访谈争执不下，项目停滞了一周。她建议先做少量访谈，再用访谈结果来设计问卷，小组同意了。运用 STAR 和[[#rule-star]]重建她的回答。
::: solution
**情境**：*In my second year, I was in a group of five researching why visitor numbers at a local museum had fallen.*

**任务**：*Two members disagreed strongly about method — one wanted an online survey, the other wanted face-to-face interviews — and after a week we still hadn't started collecting data.*

**行动**：*I suggested that the two of them weren't really in conflict: interviews would tell us what to ask, and the survey would tell us how common each answer was. So I proposed that we do six short interviews first and use them to write the survey questions. I set up a call to discuss it, and I offered to do two of the interviews myself so that it wouldn't add work for the others.*

**结果**：*Both of them agreed, we finished the interviews in four days, and the survey got over a hundred responses. Our tutor said the combination of methods was the strongest part of the project.*

**收获**：*What I took from it was that a disagreement about method is often a disagreement about what question you're answering — and that it helps to say that out loud early.*

**改变了什么。**一个具体的场合；简短的情境和任务；用 *I* 讲述的详细行动；一个有数字和外部评价（导师的）的具体结果；以及用现在时表达的反思。这个回答说出来大约需要一分半钟。
:::
:::

::: warning 什么都用 *we*
✗ *We organised the event, we found the speakers, we did the publicity, and we got two hundred people.*
✓ *We were a team of four. My part was the publicity: I designed the posters and ran the social media accounts, and I also persuaded two of the speakers to come.*

面试小组无法给团队打分。如果每句话都用 *we*，他们就分辨不出是你领导了这个项目，还是你只是参加了会议。用一句话诚实地肯定团队——*It was a team effort*——然后用 *I* 描述你自己的贡献。
:::

::: quiz
回答 *Give me an example of when you had to meet a tight deadline* 时，哪个开头最好？
- [ ] *I'm very good under pressure, and I always meet my deadlines.*
- [ ] *Deadlines are very important in any job, because…*
- [x] *Last spring I had three days to translate a forty-page report for a visiting delegation.*
- [ ] *We usually have lots of deadlines at university.*
::: solution
第三项用一句话交代了情境和任务，开始了一个具体的故事。第一项是没有证据的主张；第二项是关于截止期限的看法；第四项是一个概括。
:::
:::

## 其他常见问题

### *Tell me about yourself*

这常常是第一个问题，但它不是请你讲述人生经历。一个有用的结构是**现在—过去—将来**：你现在在做什么，是什么经历让你走到这里，以及为什么这个职位或项目是自然的下一步。控制在一分钟左右。

::: example *Tell me about yourself* {#ex-yourself}
Gabriel Santos 是历史专业三年级学生，正在申请博物馆讲解员的职位；他周末在一家咖啡馆打了两年工，还在一个儿童读书俱乐部做志愿者。为他写一段一分钟的回答。
::: solution
*I'm in my third year of a history degree here, specialising in the nineteenth-century city — so I've spent quite a lot of time in this museum's archive, actually. Alongside my studies, I've worked weekends in a café for two years, which has taught me a lot about dealing with the public when things are busy, and I volunteer with a children's reading club on Wednesday afternoons, where I've learned that the way you tell a story matters as much as the facts. This role brings those things together: talking to visitors of all ages about history, in a place I already know well. So I'm really keen to find out more about it.*

**结构。**现在（*I'm in my third year*），过去（咖啡馆和读书俱乐部，每项都说明了它教给他什么），将来（*This role brings those things together*）。每个部分都与这份工作相联系；没有任何内容只是因为它属实才出现在那里。
:::
:::

### *Why this role?* / *Why this programme?*

有力的回答表明你**了解**这个机构或项目的某些具体情况，并把它与你自己的兴趣或经历**联系**起来：*I was particularly interested in your outreach work with schools, because…* / *I'd like to work with Professor Adeyemi's group because my dissertation used the same method on a different kind of data.* 笼统的回答——*It is a famous university*——告诉面试小组你没有去了解。

### 长处、短处和困难

谈**长处**时，给出一两个，每个附上简短的证据：*I'm good at explaining things clearly — the reading club children's parents often tell me their kids talk about the stories at home.* 谈**短处**或**困难**时，给出一个真实的、对这个职位不是核心的短处，并说明你正在怎样改进：*I used to find it hard to say no to extra work, and I'd end up overcommitted. I now keep a weekly plan and check it before I agree to anything new.* 一个实际上是伪装成短处的长处（*I'm a perfectionist*），面试官大都认得出来，通常听起来是在回避。

### 情境类问题

情境类问题——*What would you do if…?*——要求用**第二类条件句**和 *would* 来回答：*If a visitor complained that a tour was too long, I would apologise, ask what they'd most like to see, and offer to take them straight there.* 适用的原则与行为类回答相同：要具体，给出你的理由，如果你遇到过类似的情况，就说出来：*Actually, something similar happened at the café…*

## 介绍成绩

::: theorem 准确地而不是谦虚地描述成绩 {#rule-achievements}
在面试中，要**准确、实事求是地**描述你做过的事：你的角色、你的行动和结果，有数字或外部认可的就附上。让**事实**来承担评价；你不需要夸自己（*I am excellent at…*），但也不要贬低或隐藏你的贡献（*I just helped a little*）。只在你确实没有把握的地方使用模糊限制语。
:::

::: proof
在大多数说英语的场合，面试官期待候选人谈论自己的成绩，并且按字面意思理解他们听到的话。一个实际上负责了宣传、却说 *I only helped a bit with the publicity* 的候选人会被信以为真，得分比说 *I ran the publicity* 的候选人低。实事求是的描述化解了谦虚与自我展示之间表面上的冲突：*We had two hundred people, compared with eighty the year before* 并不自夸，却能让面试小组自己得出结论。模糊限制语是准确性的一部分，而不是谦虚的一部分（[[modality/hedging-boosting#rule-what-to-hedge]]）：如果你不知道，*I think it was partly because of the posters* 是诚实的；如果你知道，这样说就错了。
:::

::: example 太谦虚、太自夸、准确 {#ex-achievement}
就同一项成绩——一个学生组织了一场为当地儿童医院募款的慈善跑——写出三个版本：一个太谦虚，一个太自夸，一个准确。
::: solution
- **太谦虚**：*It wasn't really me — lots of people helped, and I just did some small things. It was nothing special.*——面试小组什么也没了解到，而且可能会信以为真。
- **太自夸**：*I single-handedly organised the most successful charity event in the university's history. I'm a natural leader.*——无法核实的主张和自我吹捧；面试小组会打折扣。
- **准确**：*I organised a five-kilometre charity run with three other students. I was responsible for getting permission from the council and the university, and for recruiting runners — I spoke at fourteen society meetings in two weeks. In the end a hundred and sixty people took part, and we raised about four thousand pounds for the children's hospital.*

准确的版本对她自己的行动用 *I*，顺带肯定了团队，并给出了事实——十四次会议、一百六十名跑者、募得的金额——面试小组可以据此自行判断。
:::
:::

## 学术面试

研究生名额、奖学金和研究职位的面试有其自身的侧重点：面试小组想知道你是否了解这个领域，你的兴趣是否与他们能提供的东西相符，以及你能否批判性地讨论自己的工作。

- **研究兴趣。**能用两三句话说出你想研究什么以及为什么：*I'm interested in how small museums use volunteers, because almost all the research is on large national museums, and the small ones work very differently.* 然后准备好进一步展开。
- **你以前的工作。**预料到会有关于你的毕业论文或项目的详细问题——你的方法、你的结果、你会改变什么。像对待讲话后的提问一样处理它们（[[presentations-and-discussion/handling-questions]]）：先回答，承认局限，并直截了当地说出你不知道的东西。
- **契合度。**知道你想在谁的研究基础上继续工作，以及为什么。*I read Dr Okafor's paper on volunteer motivation, and I'd like to apply her framework to small museums* 显示了真正的兴趣；*Your university has a good reputation* 则没有。
- **讨论。**学术面试小组有时会对一个回答提出反驳，看你如何回应。把它当作一次研讨课来对待——它本来就是（[[presentations-and-discussion/seminars-discussion#rule-disagree-seminar]]）：考虑对方的观点，用理由捍卫你的看法，如果对方是对的就让步。

::: example 学术面试小组提出反驳 {#ex-academic}
Lin Mei 正在参加博物馆学硕士名额的面试。她说她想研究小型博物馆如何使用志愿者。面试小组成员 Okafor 博士说：*But surely small museums just use volunteers because they can't afford staff? Is there really anything to research?* 按照上面的建议，写出 Lin Mei 的回答。
::: solution
*That's certainly part of it — cost is the reason most small museums give. But in the museum where I volunteered, the volunteers weren't just filling gaps: several of them had run the local history society for decades and knew more about the collection than anyone. So I think there's a real question about whether small museums depend on volunteers for **knowledge**, not only for labour — and if they do, what happens when those volunteers retire. I haven't seen much research on that, though I'd be glad to be pointed to some if it exists.*

**它是怎样起作用的。**她对属实的部分作出**让步**（*cost is the reason most give*），提供**来自自己经历的证据**，更准确地**重新界定**了问题，最后以对自己知识的**诚实限定**结束，同时也请求对方的帮助。她既没有放弃自己的想法，也没有把质疑当作攻击（[[presentations-and-discussion/handling-questions#rule-challenge]]）。面试小组考查的不是她是否正确，而是她能否思考这个问题。
:::
:::

::: quiz
面试小组问：*Why do you want to join this programme?* 哪个回答最有力？
- [ ] *Because it is a very famous university with a high ranking.*
- [ ] *Because I want to get a good job after I graduate.*
- [x] *Mainly because of the placement year — I'd like to test my interest in policy work in practice, and the links you have with local councils would let me do that.*
- [ ] *My parents recommended it.*
::: solution
第三项显示了对这个项目的具体了解（实习年、与地方政府的联系），并把它与候选人自己的目标联系起来。其他几项几乎可以用来说任何项目，第四项还把决定交给了别人。
:::
:::

## 提出你自己的问题

::: theorem 提出有见地的问题 {#rule-your-questions}
在大多数面试的最后，你会被问到 *Do you have any questions for us?* 准备**两三个问题**，表明你思考过这个职位，并且你真心想知道答案：关于工作本身、培训和支持、团队，或者怎样才算成功。避免问那些答案在网站上就有的问题，关于薪酬和待遇的详细问题留到后面的阶段再问，除非它们完全没有说清楚。
:::

::: proof
候选人的问题是最后一项证据：一个有见地的问题显示了调查研究和真正的兴趣，而 *No, I think you've covered everything* 则可能显示两者都没有。问题也确实有用，因为面试对候选人来说也是一次决定，有些事情——典型的一周是什么样子，新员工如何得到支持——只有问了才能知道。关于薪酬和假期的问题是合理的，但在第一次面试时提出，可能给人一种待遇比工作更重要的印象；这些问题通常在有望得到录用时才讨论。
:::

好的问题：

- *What would a typical week look like for a guide in the first month?*
- *How are new guides trained — is there shadowing before the first tour?*
- *What do the best guides here do differently, in your experience?*
- 学术面试：*How often do students in the group meet their supervisors?* / *Are there opportunities to present work in progress?*

## 线上面试和面试的结尾

线上面试增加了[[listening-and-conversation/calls-online-meetings]]中的那些挑战：延迟，没有视觉提示的话轮转换，以及忍不住照着屏幕念笔记。说话时看着摄像头，笔记只写几个关键词，放在摄像头附近；如果连接中断，马上说出来：*Sorry, you froze for a moment — could you repeat the question?*

最后，简短地感谢面试小组，如果合适，再确认一下你的兴趣：*Thank you very much for your time. I've really enjoyed hearing about the role, and I'm very interested in it.* 事后发一封简短的感谢邮件，在某些行业和国家很常见，在另一些则不常见；只要简短，就没有坏处（[[pragmatics/writing-to-people]]）。

::: note 英汉对比
- **谦虚。**许多中国人从小被教导，谦虚地介绍自己是有礼貌的，“我只是做了一点小事”这样的说法在汉语中是礼貌的。译到英语面试中，它们常常被按字面理解。在英语面试中谦虚也受到重视，但它是通过**准确、实事求是的**描述来体现的，而不是通过轻描淡写（[[#rule-achievements]]）。这是惯例的差异，许多说英语的人也觉得自我展示令人不自在；本章的方法对每个人都有帮助。
- **“我们”和 *we*。**把成绩说成是集体的（我们），是很自然的，而且常常是恰当的。在面试中，要用以 *I* 开头的句子补充你自己的角色（[[#rule-star]]）。
- **你的名字。**面试小组可能不知道你名字中哪个是姓。清楚地介绍自己：*I'm Zhou Xin — Zhou is my family name, and Xin is fine.* 用不用英文名是个人的选择，而不是一种期待。
- **时态。**✗ *I have graduated in 2023.* ✓ *I graduated in 2023.* 已经结束的时间（*in 2023*、*last year*）要用一般过去时；现在完成时用于到现在为止的经历，不带具体时间：*I've worked with children for three years.*（[[tense-and-aspect/present-perfect]]）
- **正式程度**：*Dear interviewers, it is my great honour to be here* 在大多数面试中听起来过于正式。*Thank you for inviting me — I'm very pleased to be here* 既热情又得体。
:::

::: history
通过考试选拔人才有着悠久的历史——中国的科举是从隋唐一直延续到1905年的笔试，英国的《诺斯科特—特里维廉报告》（Northcote–Trevelyan Report，1854）提议通过竞争性考试选拔文官——但对面试的研究是20世纪的课题。早期的综述，例如瓦格纳（Ralph Wagner）1949年的综述，发现典型的非结构化面试并不可靠。弗拉纳根（John Flanagan）的关键事件技术（critical incident technique，1954）——收集那些决定一份工作成败的具体行为的叙述——为询问过去具体事件的问题奠定了基础。莱瑟姆（Gary Latham）及其同事在1980年描述了**情境面试**（situational interview），詹兹（Tom Janz）在1982年描述了**模式化行为描述面试**（patterned behaviour description interview），它们是今天 *What would you do if…?* 和 *Tell me about a time when…* 这类问题的前身。20世纪90年代以来的综述证实，结构化使面试更公平、更有预测力。
:::

## 后续内容

这是本课程的最后一章。它所用的技能来自整个课程：倾听和回答问题（[[presentations-and-discussion/handling-questions]]），清楚的演说表达（[[presentations-and-discussion/delivery]]），以及建设性的讨论（[[presentations-and-discussion/seminars-discussion]]）。书面申请材料——求职信、个人陈述和后续邮件——是语气和读者对象的问题，在[[pragmatics/writing-to-people]]中讨论；对关于自己的主张进行模糊限制和强化，见[[modality/hedging-boosting]]。《语用学》（*Pragmatics*）课程（[[pragmatics/meaning-beyond-words]]）解释了本课程许多选择背后的礼貌和直接程度惯例。

::: summary
- 结构化面试对每位候选人使用同样的、以工作为基础的问题和评分，比非结构化面试更能预测工作表现（Schmidt and Hunter 1998；Sackett et al. 2022）——所以要回答被打分的那个问题（[[#def-interview]]，[[#rule-scored]]）。
- 准备六到八个展示职位描述中各项胜任力的故事，并大声排练。
- 用 **STAR** 形式的**一个具体故事**回答行为类问题，把大部分时间花在**行动**上，你的部分用 *I*，最后讲结果和你学到的东西（[[#def-star]]，[[#rule-star]]）。
- *Tell me about yourself*：现在—过去—将来，与职位相联系。*Why this role?*：显示具体的了解。
- 用 *would* 和第二类条件句回答情境类问题，能联系真实经历时就联系起来。
- **准确地而不是谦虚地**描述成绩：让事实来承担评价（[[#rule-achievements]]）。
- 提出两三个你自己的**有见地的问题**（[[#rule-your-questions]]）。
:::

## 练习

::: exercise 问题的种类 {level=1}
把每个面试问题归类为行为类（B）、情境类（S）或动机类（M）（[[#def-interview]]）。

(a) *What would you do if two colleagues asked you for help at the same time?* (b) *Why do you want to study in this department?* (c) *Tell me about a time you made a mistake at work.* (d) *What attracted you to this role?* (e) *Give me an example of when you persuaded someone to change their mind.* (f) *How would you handle a visitor who was rude to you?*
::: solution
(a) S (b) M (c) B (d) M (e) B (f) S
:::
:::

::: exercise 属于 STAR 的哪一部分？ {level=1}
把每个句子标为 S、T、A、R 或 L（收获）。

(a) *I set up a shared spreadsheet so everyone could see who was doing what.* (b) *We needed to organise a conference for eighty people in six weeks.* (c) *I was the secretary of the geography society last year.* (d) *In the end, the conference ran on time and the feedback forms averaged four out of five.* (e) *Since then, I've used a shared plan for every group project.* (f) *I also phoned each speaker a week before to confirm.*
::: solution
(a) A (b) T (c) S (d) R (e) L (f) A
:::
:::

::: exercise 改正时态 {level=1}
改正时态错误：(a) *I have finished my degree in 2024.* (b) *Last summer I have worked in a hotel.* (c) *I study English for ten years.* (d) *When I was at school, I have won a prize for science.*
::: solution
(a) *I finished my degree in 2024.* (b) *Last summer I worked in a hotel.* (c) *I've studied / I've been studying English for ten years.* (d) *When I was at school, I won a prize for science.*
:::
:::

::: exercise 从 *we* 到 *I* {level=2}
改写这个回答，使候选人自己的贡献清楚可见，同时保留一句肯定团队的话。

*We decided to start a recycling scheme in our halls. We talked to the university, we got bins, we made posters, and we got most students to take part.*
::: solution
参考答案：*Three of us started a recycling scheme in our halls. My part was dealing with the university: I wrote the proposal to the accommodation office, met the facilities manager twice, and got them to provide twenty bins free of charge. The others designed the posters. By the end of the term, the cleaners reported that most rooms were separating their recycling.*（最后一句为结果提供了一个外部来源。）
:::
:::

::: exercise 长处和短处 {level=2}
就关于你自己的每个问题写一个简短的回答（三四句话）：(a) *What would you say is your main strength?* (b) *What do you find most difficult?* 遵循本章的建议。
::: solution
参考答案：(a) *I think my main strength is organisation. For example, as treasurer of the badminton club I set up a simple system for tracking payments, and we ended the year with no unpaid fees for the first time.* (b) *I find speaking in large groups harder than one to one. I've been working on it by volunteering to present in seminars, and I now prepare two or three points in advance so I'm ready to come in.* 答案各异；检查长处是否有证据，困难是否真实、对职位不是核心，并附有你正在采取的改进措施。
:::
:::

::: exercise 你的问题 {level=2}
写出在下列面试结束时你可以提出的三个有见地的问题：(a) 大学实验室研究助理的暑期工作，(b) 环境政策硕士项目的名额。
::: solution
参考答案：(a) *What would the research assistant's main tasks be in the first few weeks?* / *Is there a chance to be involved in writing up results?* / *How does the lab usually support assistants who are new to the equipment?* (b) *How are dissertation supervisors matched with students?* / *Do students on the programme have opportunities for placements with policy organisations?* / *What have recent graduates gone on to do?*
:::
:::

::: exercise 分析并重建一个回答 {level=3}
阅读这个对 *Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly* 的回答，找出至少四个问题，并把它改写成一个约150词的 STAR 式回答。需要时可以编造合理的细节。

*Learning new things is something I really enjoy. I am a fast learner and I am very hard-working. At university we have to learn a lot of things quickly, like new software and new methods. I think the key is to be patient and practise a lot. My teachers always say I learn quickly. So I think I can learn quickly in this job too.*
::: hint
这位候选人实际上在回答哪个问题？有没有一个具体的场合？*她*做了什么？
:::
::: solution
**问题：**没有具体的场合；关于素质的主张（*fast learner*、*hard-working*）没有证据；用一个笼统的看法（*the key is to be patient*）代替了行动；用转述的称赞（*My teachers always say*）代替了结果；没有反思。

**改写（一种可能）**：*In my third year, I joined a project that used a mapping program I'd never used, and I had two weeks before I needed to produce maps for our first report. I worked through the program's free tutorials for an hour every evening, and I picked one real task from our project for each tutorial, so I was learning on our own data. When I got stuck, I asked a PhD student in the department for half an hour of help rather than losing a day. By the deadline, I'd produced the six maps we needed, and the supervisor used two of them in her presentation to the funding body. What I learned was that I learn fastest when I practise on a real problem straight away — and that asking for help early saves time.*
:::
:::

::: exercise 口语：模拟面试 {level=3}
选择一则真实的招聘广告或项目介绍。列出它所要求的三项胜任力，并为每一项准备一个 STAR 故事。请一位朋友按任意顺序用以下问题面试你：*Tell me about yourself*、两个行为类问题、一个情境类问题，以及 *Do you have any questions for us?*。把面试录下来，对照[[#rule-scored]]、[[#rule-star]]和[[#rule-achievements]]检查每个回答。
::: solution
答案各异。对每个回答检查：你是否识别出了胜任力？你是否讲了一个具体的故事？大部分时间是否花在行动上？你的部分是否用了 *I*？你是否给出了具体的结果和反思？长度是否在一到两分钟之间？你是否实事求是地描述了成绩，既不轻描淡写也不自我吹捧？你自己的问题是否显示了调查研究？如果一个回答以看法开头（*I think teamwork is very important*），就练习改为从情境开始。
:::
:::
