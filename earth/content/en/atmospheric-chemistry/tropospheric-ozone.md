Tropospheric ozone is the pollutant nobody emits: it is assembled in the sky from nitrogen oxides and volatile organics by sunlight, and the recipe's products — a lung irritant, a crop-killer, and the third most important greenhouse gas the atmosphere has ever held — arrive on days that may carry no local smokestack at all. The story begins in a London fog. In 1905 the public-health physician **Henry Antoine Des Voeux**, addressing a chemistry congress, needed a word for the "great fog or fog-smoke" of his city and coined **smog**; for half a century the word meant coal smoke and sulphurous fog — Donora in 1948, the Donora killer fog, the London 1952 disaster — until Los Angeles revealed a second, stranger species of smog that burned the eyes, cracked rubber, damaged trees, and peaked not in winter coal-smoke inversions but on bright summer afternoons downwind of traffic. **Arie Haagen-Smit**, the Dutch organic chemist who decoded it in the 1950s, found that the oxidant was ozone and that its factory ran on automobile hydrocarbons and NOx under sunlight — photochemical smog. Seventy years later the problem he named is global, is the target of the most expensive air-quality programme in history, and is winning and losing at the same time: surface ozone is falling over the United States and Europe where the recipe's ingredients are being cut, rising over South and East Asia, and being quietly re-based upward everywhere by methane, by a warmer biogenic world, and by the smoke of an era of megafires.

## The pollutant you did not emit

::: definition Why ozone is a secondary pollutant {#def-secondary-ozone}
Ozone has no significant primary source: engines emit NO and hydrocarbons, not O₃, and the tropospheric burden is assembled in place by the photochemical engine of [[atmospheric-chemistry/photochemistry]] — Leighton's cycle with its NO-to-NO₂ conversion debt, the peroxy radicals that pay it from VOC oxidation, the null-futile cycles that make net ozone depend on the **ratio** of NOx to VOC rather than either alone. The surface mixture is therefore a sum of three ledgers: a **background** of 20–40 ppb (stratospheric intrusions through tropopause folds, roughly 5–10 ppb at mid-latitudes; free-tropospheric ozone produced from methane and transported down; hemispheric pollution plumes), a **regional increment** of 10–40 ppb built downwind of NOx and urban/VOC sources over hours to days, and **local peaks** at traffic sites and fire plumes. Because the engine is nonlinear, the same 1 ppb of NOx makes ozone in a VOC-rich city and destroys it (titration, the NO₃–N₂O₅ night chemistry) in the city core — which is why ozone maps are the inverse of every other pollutant's map at the hottest hour, why the "weekend effect" appears, and why ozone standards are met or missed on **meteorology as much as emissions**: a stagnant 32°C afternoon with light winds is the design condition, and the design value — the 4th-highest 8-hour mean of the year in US practice, 70 ppb under the 2015 standard — is as much a statistic of climate as of chemistry. The conversion to remember: 1 ppb ozone = 2 µg m⁻³, so the standard is 140 µg m⁻³, and the 2021 WHO guideline (100 µg m⁻³, 8-h) is about 50 ppb — a level that pre-2000 Los Angeles would have called clean.
:::

::: example From ppb to micrograms {#ex-ppb-conversion}
Express the US 2015 ozone standard (70 ppb, 8-hour mean) in µg m⁻³, and the WHO 2021 guideline (100 µg m⁻³) in ppb.
::: solution
At 25°C and 1 atm, 1 ppb O₃ = 2 µg m⁻³, so 70 ppb = **140 µg m⁻³**; 100 µg m⁻³ = **50 ppb**. The exercise matters because every health index, every sensor readout and every Indian and Chinese air-quality index is in µg m⁻³ while the atmospheric-chemistry literature is in ppb — and because the WHO guideline of 50 ppb sits **below** the typical background of many polluted regions, meaning that for a third of humanity, meeting the guideline requires not just local clean air but a cleaner hemisphere.
:::
:::

## Lungs, forests, bread

::: definition Damage: stomatal flux, yield, carbon, health {#def-ozone-damage}
Ozone has no emission control that bypasses its chemistry, and no receptor that escapes its oxidation: it is destroyed on every surface it touches, and the damage is the destruction. In lungs it reacts with the lining fluid, provoking inflammation, reduced lung function and asthma attacks — the epidemiology attributes roughly **400,000 premature deaths per year** globally to surface ozone, with strong effects on children's lung development and on outdoor workers. In plants the pathway is the same reaction at the stomate: ozone enters for gas exchange, dies on the cell wall, and the plant pays **carbon to repair** — photosynthate diverted from growth and grain, visible as stippling on tobacco and spinach leaves (the bioindicators that first revealed the damage) and invisible as **5–10 per cent yield losses in wheat and soybean per 10 ppb** of growing-season ozone in the FACE (free-air enrichment) experiments, the gold standard of the field. Forests pay twice: reduced growth and, because ozone damages the carbon sink itself, a **7 per cent or more cut in forest carbon uptake** in high-ozone regions — a feedback that weakens the land sink the climate system is counting on. Agronomists index the dose as **A40** (sum of daytime hours above 40 ppb) and **W126** (a weighted cumulative uptake), the latter preferred because it weights the stomatal flux — the plant's actual dose — rather than the air's concentration. The policy consequence is that ozone is the one pollutant whose control pays **three dividends at once**: health now, crop yields now, and radiative forcing, because ozone is itself a greenhouse gas with a global mean forcing near **0.4 W m⁻²** — one of the few mitigation targets that cools the climate and saves bread and lungs in the same regulation.
:::

::: example The yield price of a hotter sky {#ex-yield}
A wheat region's growing-season ozone rises from 45 to 61 ppb; FACE experiments give roughly 6 per cent yield loss per 10 ppb on the A40 scale. Find the fractional yield loss, and the grain lost from a 100-million-tonne regional crop.
::: solution
The rise is 16 ppb, so the loss is $0.06\times1.6 = 9.6$ per cent — roughly **10 per cent**, or **10 million tonnes**, several times the annual grain export of most nations. The arithmetic is why the ozone–climate negotiations of the 2010s began to treat methane (the background's master variable) as an **agriculture policy**: a 20 per cent methane cut buys several ppb of background everywhere, and several ppb is several per cent of the world's bread.
:::
:::

## The night's other chemistry

::: definition Nighttime ozone loss and the N₂O₅ connection {#def-night-ozone}
At sunset the photochemical factory closes but the chemistry does not: NO₂ reacts with the ozone that remains to make **NO₃** ($k = 1.2\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{cm^{3}\,s^{-1}}$, a lifetime of minutes in polluted air), NO₃ photolyses in daylight, which is why it is the night's oxidant, and NO₃ equilibrates with N₂O₅ ($\mathrm{NO_3 + NO_2 \rightleftharpoons N_2O_5}$), the reservoir whose heterogeneous fate decides the region's nitrogen budget. N₂O₅ hydrolysing on aerosol ($\mathrm{N_2O_5 + H_2O \rightarrow 2\,HNO_3}$) is the **nighttime nitrate-aerosol factory** — the winter-haze nitrate of the North China Plain and the Po Valley, and the mechanism by which NOx leaves the air as particle rather than as acid gas — while the competing uptake on fresh sea-salt releases ClNO₂ (nitryl chloride), which photolyses at dawn into chlorine atoms that detonate the morning oxidation an hour before the OH engine starts. The night also **removes ozone** where NO is high (titration, the reason city cores are ozone-poor at 3 a.m.) and **redistributes** it where NO is low: nocturnal NO₃ oxidation of aromatics and isoprene makes organic nitrates that breathe NOx back in the morning, the "slow burn" that lets a city's daytime ozone depend on emissions from yesterday's afternoon. The lesson for the policy mind: ozone chemistry does not stop when the monitor stops being informative — the night is where the nitrate aerosol, the chlorine and much of the next day's NOx are made, and the reason ozone and PM2.5 must be regulated as one coupled system.
:::

::: example The nocturnal nitrate factory {#ex-nightrate}
A winter haze episode keeps $[\mathrm{O_3}] = 20\ \mathrm{ppb}$, $[\mathrm{NO_2}] = 60\ \mathrm{ppb}$, and aerosol provides an N₂O₅ uptake coefficient $\gamma = 0.02$ at a surface area concentration of $300\ \mathrm{\mu m^{2}\,cm^{-3}}$. Estimate the N₂O₅ lifetime against hydrolysis (air at 270 K, mean molecular speed $2.3\times10^{4}\ \mathrm{cm\,s^{-1}}$) and state what it implies for the 10-hour night.
::: solution
The pseudo-first-order loss is $k = \gamma\,\overline{c}\,A/4 = 0.02\times2.3\times10^{4}\times3\times10^{-6}/4 = 3.5\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}$, a **~50-minute lifetime** — one twelfth of the night, so the aerosol processes the NOx pool many times over and most of the nitrate haze is made between dusk and dawn, exactly as observed. The exercise is the reason China's nitrate haze responded to **neither** SO₂ nor NOx cuts alone: the nitrate factory runs on the night, on aerosol area, and on the N₂O₅ that only exists when ozone and NOx coexist — chemistry with a sense of irony.
:::
:::

## Trends: clean West, dirty East, and fire

::: definition The geography of winning and losing {#def-ozone-trends}
The global surface-ozone record (the GAW/EMEP/CAMS network, the US EPA AQS archive, and a growing low-cost-sensor layer) tells a story of hemispheric divergence. **United States and Europe**: summer design values fell roughly 1–2 per cent per year from 2000–2015 despite flat or rising background — the photochemical increment collapsed as NOx fell 40–50 per cent and reactive VOC controls bit, and the "credit" for clean air is the largest public-health success of the US Clean Air Act lineage. **South and East Asia**: the mirror image — the North China Plain grew at 1–2 ppb per year through the 2010s on NOx doubling, the Ganges Plain now shows the world's fastest growth and some of its highest design values, and East Asian emission cuts after 2013 slowed the rise while **exposing the background problem**: with the regional increment trimmed, the hemispheric baseline (methane-driven, wildfire-punctuated) is what remains, and it is rising slowly everywhere (+0.2–0.5 ppb per decade in the remote records). **Intercontinental transport** makes the divergence a shared ledger: roughly 1 ppb of a region's background per 10 per cent change in another continent's NOx, so US standards now carry a few ppb of Asian and wildfire chemistry, and Europe's carries African and North American — the reason ozone is the first major pollutant whose management plans must be **hemispheric treaties in all but name**. **Wildfire smoke** is the 2020s' violent new term: pyrocumulonimbus injection of NOx and CO into the upper troposphere, plumes that add tens of ppb downwind for days (the 2020 western-North-American and 2019–20 Australian episodes exceeded every sensor record), and a feedback that couples the ozone problem to the fire problem — with smoke ozone now exceeding the **health** standard over millions of people on days whose local emissions were zero. And **methane** is the master variable of the baseline: at roughly 1 ppb of background ozone per 100 ppb of methane, the Global Methane Pledge's 30 per cent cut is, in ozone terms, a 1–2 ppb global discount on the hardest-to-reach part of every future standard — the clearest current example that air-quality, climate and agriculture policy are one subject wearing three hats.
:::

::: example Attainment arithmetic {#ex-attainment}
A city's design value is 82 ppb: background 42, regional increment 40. The standard is 70 ppb. If the regional increment can be cut 25 per cent by local NOx and VOC controls, find the new design value and the residual gap.
::: solution
New increment $= 40\times0.75 = 30$, design value $= 42 + 30 = 72$ ppb — a **2 ppb residual gap** that local controls cannot close, because the background is not local. The exercise is the 2020s air-quality story in two lines: the attainable future for many regions is "background plus the smallest increment chemistry allows," and the only remaining control on the last few ppb is hemispheric — methane and intercontinental agreements, or nothing.
:::

::: warning Ozone is not "good up high, bad down low" as a slogan, and its controls are not free of side-effects
Five errors. **"Stratospheric repair fixes tropospheric ozone"**: the two reservoirs exchange slowly (a year-scale pipe, a few per cent per year), and Montreal's ozone recovery has **reduced** stratospheric chlorine's destruction of... nothing in the troposphere — the coupling is weak and mostly radiative; tropospheric ozone is a photochemistry problem, full stop. **"Cutting NOx always cuts ozone"**: in VOC-limited cores it raises it (the weekend effect, the Beijing winter paradox), and in the night it restores NO₃ oxidation and nitrate aerosol — the particle dividend can even be negative for visibility and PM2.5 while ozone falls; the ozone–PM2.5 trade-off is the hardest optimization in current regulation. **"Sensors prove clean air"**: electrochemical ozone sensors drift, are humidity-biased, and are certified nowhere near reference UV-photometry — the low-cost layer is a beautiful event detector and a terrible compliance instrument. **"Wildfire ozone is temporary, so it doesn't count"**: the episodes are days, the **stomatal dose** of a forest season is cumulative, and a single pyrocumulonimbus event can inject more NOx into the upper troposphere than a continent's month of surface emissions, with a week's ozone production per molecule instead of a day's. **And "the ozone–temperature correlation proves climate change is making ozone"**: the correlation is mostly meteorology (stagnation, heat, biogenic VOCs, and the same low winds that trap emissions); the climate signal is real but smaller — background, methane, isoprene and fire — and separating them requires the trend-at-constant-meteorology methods that the assessment literature has spent twenty years building.
:::

::: history
**Henry Antoine Des Voeux** coined "smog" in 1905 for London's coal fog; the killer coal smogs — **Donora, Pennsylvania (1948)**, **London (1952)** — were sulphur and particulate affairs, and their clean-up (the UK Clean Air Act 1956) was the first modern air-quality law. The photochemical era begins with the Los Angeles episodes of the 1940s — rubber cracked, orchards stippled, eyes burned, and the city sued an industrial landscape it could not name — and was decoded a decade later by **Arie Haagen-Smit**, who identified ozone and PAN and demonstrated that automobile hydrocarbons plus NOx under sunlight were the factory; the chemical core of that factory, the Leighton cycle, came from the rocket chemist **Morris Leighton**, whose 1961 book named the peroxyacetyl nitrates' role as the invisible transport form of NOx. The global era is the **GAW** baseline network (1970s–), the US EPA's AQS archive, the **STAOCP** assessment that made methane–ozone policy explicit, and the 2021 WHO guideline revision that halved the 8-hour limit on the strength of the epidemiology — a guideline now being read, at 50 ppb, against a global background that human chemistry has already lifted from a preindustrial ~10 ppb to 30–40.
:::

::: quiz
A city cuts NOx emissions 40 per cent in its dense core. The most likely first-order effect on **peak** summer ozone there is:
- [ ] a 40 per cent fall, like clockwork.
- [x] little change or an increase, because the core is VOC-limited and titration is removed.
- [ ] no effect, because ozone is stratospheric.
- [ ] an immediate fall to background.
::: solution
In VOC-limited cores, NOx destruction of ozone (titration and the NO₂ sink) partly masks production; cutting NOx can **raise** ozone until VOC chemistry is cut alongside — the weekend effect and the Beijing winter paradox. The far suburbs, NOx-limited, see ozone fall. Nonlinearity is the first lesson of ozone control.
:::
:::

::: summary
Tropospheric ozone is assembled in the sky from NOx and VOCs by sunlight — a secondary pollutant with a background of 20–40 ppb (stratospheric intrusions, methane chemistry, hemispheric transport), a regional increment built downwind of cities, and a design value that is half chemistry, half meteorology. Its damages are one oxidation reaction at many interfaces: ~400,000 premature deaths a year, 5–10 per cent crop loss per 10 ppb, a damaged forest carbon sink, and 0.4 W m⁻² of greenhouse forcing — the rare pollutant whose control cools the climate, saves harvests and lungs together. The trends diverge: falling design values in the clean West, rising baselines and new maxima in the industrializing East, intercontinental transport making every region's air a treaty, and wildfire smoke writing new records on zero-emission days. Methane is the master variable of the baseline, and the Global Methane Pledge is, in ozone's ledger, a harvest policy, a health policy and a climate policy at the price of cutting a gas we already have reasons to cut. Ozone is the pollutant we did not emit — and the proof that we cannot control it alone.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="7"}
A pyrocumulonimbus event injects NOx into the upper troposphere where its lifetime is ~7 days instead of the boundary layer's ~1 day. If ozone production per NOx molecule scales with lifetime, find the factor by which the event's per-molecule ozone production exceeds a surface emission's.
::: solution
The factor is the lifetime ratio, $7/1 = 7$ — the reason fire smoke, injected above the boundary layer's rain and night chemistry, makes ozone so much more efficiently than the same NOx released at the surface, and why megafire seasons show up in remote background records.
