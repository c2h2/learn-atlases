Phosphorus and sulfur are the carbon and nitrogen cycles' older siblings — one without a sky, one haunted by it. **Phosphorus** has no significant gas phase: its cycle begins in rock, passes through life in the phosphate ester that runs every cell's energy economy, and ends in sediment, where it waits tens of millions of years for the next orogeny. That single design flaw — no atmospheric buffer, no fast reservoir — makes the phosphorus cycle the one place on Earth where **an element's geological scarcity and an ecosystem's local abundance can coexist**: we mine in a century the phosphate that weathering would deliver in a millennium, feed it to crops half-wasted, and drown the lakes and coasts in the remainder while the mines' finite ore (two-thirds of it in one country's ground) sets a deadline no other nutrient cycle carries. **Sulfur** has the opposite character: four oxidation states from sulfide to sulfate, a biogenic gas phase (DMS) that seeds cloud and carries the ocean's scent, and a deep-time role as **oxygen's shadow ledger** — the pyrite buried in sediments is the account book of the Great Oxidation, and its isotope fingerprints are how we read the age when the atmosphere first turned oxidising. This lesson is both cycles and their entanglement with the oxygen story told in the course's last lesson.

## Phosphorus: the rock cycle

::: definition No sky, no buffer, no second chance {#def-phosphorus}
Phosphate ($\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}$) is life's **energy currency and information backbone** (ATP, DNA, RNA, phospholipids), and the biosphere holds and cycles it greedily (the Redfield ratio's 1-in-123 atoms, the rapid internal recycling in cells and soils), but the **global supply is set by rocks**: the igneous apatite of continental crust, weathered at the natural rate of ~0.02–0.04 Tg P yr⁻¹ to rivers and sea, buried in marine sediment, and returned by uplift on the orogenic clock. The design consequences are the lesson: **no gaseous buffer** means no global redistribution, no atmospheric self-correction, and **local, irreversible-in-practice scarcity and excess at once**; **no fast abiotic sink** means added phosphorus stays bioavailable for decades (unlike nitrogen, which denitrifies away — the reason freshwater and lake management is a phosphorus problem while the sea's is nitrogen's); and **finite ore** means the cycle's human phase has a supply side: mining now lifts **~220 Mt of phosphate rock a year** (~20–30 Tg P, roughly **an order of magnitude over natural weathering** by most budgets), concentrated terrifyingly — **Morocco and the Western Sahara hold ~70 per cent of the world's reserves** (~50 of ~70 Gt), with China and the United States, the other historical pillars, already in decline — which is why the **"peak phosphorus"** literature — the 2009–2013 debate of **Dana Cordell, Sten Åke Sverdrup and the Cordell-and-White school** argues not that phosphorus runs out but that **geology, geopolitics and quality degrade before scarcity bites** (the phosphate dust, the cadmium and uranium content of poor ores, the 1970s and 2008 fertilizer-price shocks that each triggered a stockpiling panic). The human budget's other half is waste: **crop-use efficiency is ~20–25 per cent at the field and ~15 per cent from mine to plate** (the **Cordell and White** "from the mine to the plate to the sea" chain), so the phosphorus added to farmland accumulates as a **soil legacy** (the decades of surplus built into continental soils, now a reservoir that will continue leaking to streams for decades **even if fertilizer stopped tomorrow** — the "legacy phosphorus" literature's cold comfort about lake recovery), is lost to **erosion** (the tile-drained, eroding corn belts export dissolved and particulate P at rates that make the Mississippi's load the classic case), and arrives at water bodies where the **trophic-state arithmetic** is merciless: total phosphorus above ~35 µg L⁻¹ makes a temperate lake eutrophic (the **Dillon and the Vollenweider** load-concentration models, the N-1000 and OECD-Eutrophication Survey lineage), cyanobacteria bloom (the microcystin and anatoxin seasons of Lake Erie's western basin, whose 2014 "do not drink" advisory left 500,000 Toledo residents without water — the richest agricultural watershed in the Great Lakes system doing to a city what the Baltic's eutrophication does to its coastlines), and anoxia follows the bloom's respiration. The recovery record is the lesson's hope and its warning: the **1972 Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement's** phosphorus caps **worked** (Lake Erie's clarity and fisheries returned within a decade — the largest successful eutrophication reversal on Earth), and the blooms **came back anyway** in the 2000s as the source mix shifted from point-source sewage phosphorus to **dissolved fertilizer and manure phosphorus carried by tile drains and storm runoff** (the dissolved fraction now dominates, is algal-available immediately, and is not treated by any wastewater plant), the soil legacy releasing what the 1970s surplus stored — the case study that makes phosphorus the cycle whose policy lesson is "**point sources are the easy half; the landscape is the hard half**," and whose global future (the 2020s' African and South Asian fertilizer expansion, the phosphorus-scarcity-versus-eutrophication double bind) is the nitrogen lesson's twin, written in rock.
:::

::: example The lake's ledger {#ex-lake-P}
A lake of volume $10^9$ m³ renews completely 5 times a year and receives a total-phosphorus load of 150 tonnes yr⁻¹. Using the steady-state box model $C = L/(Q/V)$ (no internal loading), find the concentration and classify the trophic state; then explain in one sentence why real lakes exceed this number and why cleanup lags.
::: solution
Flush volume $= 5\times10^9$ m³ yr⁻¹, so $C = 150\times10^6\ \mathrm{g}/5\times10^9\ \mathrm{m^3} = 30$ µg L⁻¹ — oligo-to-mesotrophic on paper; real lakes exceed it because of **internal loading** (anoxic summer release of iron-bound phosphorus from the sediment — the internal recycle that keeps shallow lakes locked in the turbid, cyanobacteria state even after external loads fall, the **Moss and the Scheffer/Carpenter regime-shift** framework) and the **soil legacy** (the decades of surplus still draining through the catchment) — which is why the Vollenweider models that set the 1972 caps were right about the target and optimistic about the speed, and why Lake Erie's 2010s relapse is the phosphorus cycle's best-argued parable.
:::
:::

## Sulfur: oxygen's shadow ledger

::: definition Sulfur's four lives: pyrite, sulfate, DMS, and the isotope archive {#def-sulfur}
Sulfur cycles through **four redox states** — sulfide (S²⁻, in pyrite and anoxic water), elemental sulfur, and sulfate ($\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}$, the oxidised ocean's dominant anion at 28 mM) — and its modern cycle is a **two-circuit system**: a **slow geological circuit** (pyrite burial in sediments, the oxidative weathering of those sediments at uplift, the net flux that controls atmospheric O₂ over millions of years because **burying sulfide means burying the oxygen equivalents that would otherwise have been consumed** — the same organic-carbon-burial logic as carbon, with pyrite as the reduced-electron carrier of half the deep-time budget), and a **fast biological-surface circuit** (sulfate-reducing bacteria — the **desulfovibrio lineage, the Postgate school** — respire half the organic matter in marine sediments, making sulfate reduction **the second-most-important respiration pathway on Earth after oxygen**, and handing the ocean its rotten-egg and mud-flat chemistry; the reoxidation of their sulfide, or its burial as pyrite, is the ledger's debit and credit). The **gas phase** is biology's contribution: phytoplankton (the coccolithophores, dinoflagellates, and the **Emiliania huxleyi** blooms visible from space) synthesize **DMSP** — dimethylsulfoniopropionate, arguably Earth's most abundant organosulfur compound, made for osmotic and oxidative stress — and bacterial and viral cleavage converts it to **DMS**, ~15–30 Tg S yr⁻¹ to the air, the compound that gives the sea its smell and whose oxidation products (methanesulfonate, non-sea-salt sulfate) **seed cloud condensation nuclei over the oceans** — the basis of the **CLAW hypothesis** (**Charles Lovelock, Robert Charlson, Meinrat Andreae, Stephen Warren**, 1987), the Gaia-flavoured proposal that DMS closes a climate-stabilising feedback (warmer → more DMS → more cloud → cooler), whose **sign** remains unresolved (the 2010s' ocean warming and acidification experiments show DMSP responses of both signs, and the ocean's DMS flux shows no confirmed climate feedback yet) and whose lasting contribution is the demonstrated fact: **the biosphere regulates a cloud-relevant aerosol precursor at planetary scale**. The anthropogenic sulfur story is the atmosphere's: coal and smelter SO₂ made the **acid rain** decades (the 1970s–80s — the Swedish and Canadian lake kills, the Harz forest dieback, the Adirondacks' chemistry, the 1990 US Clean Air Act **cap-and-trade** that cut SO₂ 90 per cent at a fraction of predicted cost — the acid-deposition lesson's triumph), and its **unintended experiment**: shipping fuel's **IMO 2020 sulphur cap** (3.5 to 0.5 per cent, cutting maritime SOx ~80 per cent, ~4 Tg S yr⁻¹) removed a bright veil over the busy Atlantic and Pacific shipping lanes in January 2020, and the measured **~0.1 W m⁻² of lost cooling** that followed is the sharpest natural experiment yet on aerosol radiative forcing — the reason the sulfur cycle's modern lesson is **the masking bargain**: we have been cooling the planet with our sulfur, and every clean-air victory **unmasks** a fraction of the warming we have banked (the **store of unmasking** quantified in the aerosol-forcing literature: the ~0.4–0.5 W m⁻² of remaining aerosol cooling, the 2022 *Nature* **"peak-soot"** argument of **Richardson and colleagues** — that accelerating shipping and clean-air sulfur cuts have passed the maximum of aerosol cooling, adding a step of additional warming on top of greenhouse forcing). The deep-time ledger closes the circle with the oxygen lesson: the **mass-independent sulfur isotope fractionations (Δ³³S)** preserved in Archean sediments (the mass-independent-sulfur work of **Farquhar, Johnston, Wing and London**, 2000–2007) are the anoxic-atmosphere fingerprint — UV-driven sulfur photochemistry that only operates without an ozone shield — and their disappearance at 2.45 Ga, alongside the pyrite-burial record and the **Canfield Ocean** hypothesis (**Donald Canfield**, 1998: the Proterozoic's sulfate-poor, sulfide-rich mid-depth seas), is the isotope backbone of the Great Oxidation narrative.
:::

::: example Pinatubo's sulfur budget {#ex-pinatubo}
The 1991 Pinatubo eruption injected ~20 Tg of SO₂ (as sulfur) into the stratosphere, converted nearly fully to H₂SO₄ aerosol (molar masses: S 32, H₂SO₄ 98). Find the aerosol mass produced, and — given the observed ~−2 W m⁻² global radiative forcing over the following year — estimate the global temperature response using $\Delta T \approx F/3.2$ (K per W m⁻², the Planck-feedback value), and compare with the observed ~0.5 °C dip.
::: solution
The 20 Tg is SO₂, so sulfur is $20\times32/64 = 10$ Tg and H₂SO₄ $= 10\times98/32 \approx 30$ Tg — the ~30-megatonne stratospheric sulfate cloud that circled the globe in three weeks; the forcing estimate $-2/3.2 \approx -0.6$ °C, within error of the observed ~0.5 °C dip (the extra cooling damped by land and ice geometry) — the exercise is the volcanic-eruption calibration of climate sensitivity, and the reason every geoengineering "solar radiation management" number is a Pinatubo extrapolation, and the reason the **unintentional** Pinatubo's acid fallout, ozone loss and ecosystem effects are the SRM debate's honest control group.
:::
:::

## The sea's phosphorus: the missing-phosphorus problem

::: definition The marine phosphorus cycle and its open accounts {#def-marine-P}
The ocean's phosphorus ledger is the biogeochemistry course's most stubborn open budget. Riverine dissolved phosphorus input (~0.02–0.06 Tg P yr⁻¹, doubled by humans) looks tiny against the sea's phosphate inventory and its ~25,000-year residence time, and the classic puzzle — the **"missing phosphorus"** — is that the **demand** implied by marine nitrogen denitrification (~200–300 Tg N yr⁻¹) at Redfield proportions would require phosphorus supplies **far larger than rivers deliver**, which the sea could only sustain by drawing down its inventory unless (a) the **dissolved organic phosphorus** pool (the "microbial P pump" of the **Dale Karl and colleagues** Hawaii Ocean Time-series — the alkaline-phosphatase machinery that lets microbes mine organic P when phosphate runs short, making the marine P cycle substantially **open and internally recycled** rather than river-fed), (b) **non-Redfield stoichiometry** (regional N:P of 10–30, the **Granger, Deutsch and colleagues** maps), or (c) **hidden sources** — hydrothermal vents, authigenic minerals and boundary sediment fluxes, quantified in the 2010s — supplies the difference) supplies the difference. The modern synthesis — the **Andrey Paytan, Gideon Goldhammer and the Poulton school** of carbonate and boundary-flux geochemistry — holds that rivers deliver **more total phosphorus than the old dissolved figures** (particulate and dust-bound P, much of it bioavailable in the low-nutrient tropics, the Saharan dust-P fertilisation of the Atlantic tracked by 2020s deposition networks), that authigenic carbonate and iron-phosphorus shuttles buffer the deep-sea record, and that the ocean's phosphorus inventory is **younger and more dynamic** than the textbook residence time implies — a conclusion with a deep-time twist: the **Neoproterozoic and Cambrian phosphorite deposits** the great phosphorite deposits of the terminal Proterozoic and early Cambrian — the sedimentary record of a supercontinent's weathering phosphorus flood, read in the tradition of the **Cook and Fölling school**hypothesis, the phosphorus cycle's most consequential sentence, and the reason the element's modern scarcity-versus-excess double bind is, in deep time, the difference between a microbial and an animal ocean.
:::

::: example When the ratio breaks: nitrogen fixation as the ocean's thermostat {#ex-np-thermostat}
The surface Eastern Mediterranean runs N:P ≈ 6:1 (nitrate 0.3 µM, phosphate 0.05 µM) against Redfield's 16:1. Find the nitrogen shortfall per unit phosphorus relative to Redfield demand, and explain in two sentences why this ratio violation is the strongest evidence for the **Deutsch-regulation** view that marine nitrogen fixation responds to close the gap — and what limits that response.
::: solution
Redfield demand for 0.05 µM P is 0.8 µM N — a shortfall of $0.8-0.3 = 0.5$ µM N, the signature of an ocean **nitrogen-starved by its own stoichiometry**; diazotrophs (the *Trichodesmium*, the unicellular cyanobacteria) should bloom on the unused phosphorus and add nitrogen until the ratio returns toward 16 — the negative feedback that keeps the ocean's nitrogen inventory from collapsing — and the response is limited by **iron, phosphorus availability and mixing**, the same co-limitations that make the regulation real but leaky, the ocean's thermostat set loosely rather than tightly.
:::

::: warning Phosphorus scarcity and excess are the same crisis, "peak phosphorus" is not about running out, DMS feedback is not confirmed, unmasking is not a reason to keep polluting, and euxinia is not a distant curiosity
Five errors. **"We are running out of phosphorus" and "phosphorus is everywhere" are both true and both incomplete**: the ore is finite and geologically concentrated (a supply-side risk measured in centuries, with a geopolitics problem in the 2030s), while the **cycle's throughput** is doubled and its waste is eutrophying coasts — the crisis is **distribution and efficiency**, not depletion alone, and the policies that solve each (recycling urine, struvite recovery, manure-to-land ratios, diet) are the same policies, which is why the phosphorus community's "nutrient cascade" framing (the **Smil, Cordell, the Rockström planetary-boundary** accounting) treats scarcity and eutrophication as one problem with a broken middle. **"Peak phosphorus means 2030 famine"**: the peak-P literature's honest claim is **price and quality** (declining ore grades, cadmium, energy and transport costs, export concentration), not exhaustion — phosphate rock lasts centuries at current use; the famine framing was a rhetorical error that set the serious supply-risk literature (the **Cordell and White** efficiency-and-governance agenda) back a decade. **"CLAW is proven / CLAW is dead"**: both overstatements — the DMS-to-cloud-condensation-nuclei link is observed, its **climate feedback sign** is unresolved (warming may increase or decrease community DMS production; the 2010s' ocean acidification suppresses DMSP in some experiments and not others), and the honest statement is that **a biologically-produced aerosol precursor of climate significance exists and its feedback is unmeasured** — one of the largest open questions in cloud physics, and a standing argument against confident Earth-system claims in either direction. **"Since sulfur unmasking warms, clean air is bad for climate"**: the arithmetic is real (the IMO 2020 step is ~0.1 W m⁻² of added forcing, the remaining aerosol store ~0.4–0.5) and the conclusion is monstrous and wrong — the sulfur that cools is the sulfur that kills (the particulate-mortality burden of the remaining aerosol, in the millions per year, dwarfs the unmasked warming's projected harm in the same air), and the correct reading is that **aerosol masking was never a policy option, only a debt**, and the only lever that pays both bills is CO₂ (the 2020s' "peak-soot" argument's actual use: raising the **urgency and the realism** of temperature targets, not defending clean-air law). **And "deep anoxic, sulfidic oceans are a Proterozoic curiosity"**: the Canfield mid-depth euxinia debate aside, **modern coastal euxinia is expanding** (the Black Sea's 1.5 million km² of sulfidic water, the Baltic's new anoxic basins, the dead-zone sediments that switch to sulfate reduction and then to sulfide accumulation as the organic rain intensifies — the **Baltic and Black Sea** systems as the eutrophication-to-euxinia continuum), and the sulfur cycle's deep-time lesson — that anoxia plus sulfate plus sunlight makes **hydrogen sulfide**, the gas that kills coasts and, in the Permian–Triassic, may have helped kill the planet (the green-sulfur-bacteria biomarker and Mo-isotope evidence of the greatest extinction's euxinic shallows) — is a warning written in the only archive that has seen it happen.
:::

::: history
The phosphorus story begins with **Hennig Brand**'s 1669 distillation of phosphorus from thousands of litres of urine (the alchemist's last, and the nutrient cycle's most vivid origin tale); **Liebig**'s 1840 doctrine and the **guano and bone-meal booms** that followed (the 1840s–70s — the first global nutrient-commodity bubble and crash, and the guano wars that prefigured the resource geopolitics of phosphate); the **Nauru and Banaba** island-mining tragedies of the twentieth century, whose rehabilitation disputes ran into the 2000s; the post-1947 industrialization of acid-cured superphosphate (the Tennessee Valley Authority and the Florida hard-rock belt); and the lake-science awakening — the **OECD Eutrophication Survey (1969–76)**, **Robert Vollenweider and Richard Dillon**'s load-concentration models (1968–75, the arithmetic behind the **1972 Great Lakes Water Quality Agreement**, the largest successful nutrient policy in history), its 2000s relapse into the **legacy-phosphorus literature (Sharpley and colleagues)** and the ongoing Mississippi-hypoxia and Baltic action plans. The sulfur science runs from Lavoisier's sulfide chemistry and the nineteenth-century sulfuric-acid industry (the contact process, 1875) through the **acid-rain discovery** — the Scandinavian lake surveys of **Wright and Henriksen**, the Hubbard Brook sulfate findings, the 1976 Swedish Chemistry Board report that coined the era's policy vocabulary — and the **1990 Clean Air Act Title IV cap-and-trade** (the market-design triumph that cut SO₂ ~90 per cent far below predicted cost). The marine-sulfur and DMS thread: **Beijerinck**'s *Spirillum desulfuricans* (1915) and the **Postgate school**'s sulfate-reducer physiology; **Charlson, Lovelock, Andreae and Warren**'s 1987 CLAW paper; the **Andreae-Crutzen** global sulfur budget (1997) and the **Lana and colleagues** global DMS-flux synthesis (2011); and the **IMO 2020** monitoring papers (2020–2023) that turned a shipping regulation into the largest deliberate aerosol experiment in history. The isotope archive: **Farquhar, Johnston, Wing and London**'s mass-independent sulfur discoveries (2000–2007, the anoxic-atmosphere fingerprint), **Donald Canfield**'s 1998 euxinic-Proterozoic hypothesis and the **Lyons, Reinhard and the trace-metal school's** molybdenum and sulfur-isotope tests of it — the deep-time sulfur cycle now a template for reading oxygen on other worlds, the Δ³³S signature a search target for the next generation of exoplanet and Mars geochemistry.
:::

::: quiz
Freshwater eutrophication is usually controlled by phosphorus rather than nitrogen because:
- [x] nitrogen-fixing organisms relieve nitrogen shortage, so added P keeps raising productivity; there is no analogous P relief.
- [ ] lakes have no nitrogen at all.
- [ ] phosphorus evaporates into the air.
- [ ] nitrogen is toxic to algae.
::: solution
Where nitrogen can be fixed (cyanobacteria bloom when P is abundant and N scarce), nitrogen self-supplies and phosphorus remains the binding constraint — the **David Smith and David Schindler** synthesis (the Experimental Lakes Area's whole-lake additions, the 2008 *Science* paper that settled the debate) — which is why lake management caps phosphorus, and why cyanobacteria blooms fix their own nitrogen once phosphorus arrives.
:::

::: summary
Phosphorus cycles from rock to sea with no atmospheric detour, and every consequence follows: supply set by weathering and mining (220 Mt of rock a year, 70 per cent of reserves in one country's ground), scarcity and eutrophication as one distribution problem, a soil legacy that will leak for decades after the fertilizer stops, and a recovery record (Erie's 1970s success, 2000s relapse) that proves point sources are the easy half. Sulfur cycles through four redox states with a biological gas phase: pyrite burial is half the deep-time oxygen ledger (the Δ³³S archive and the Canfield Ocean are how we read it), DMS is the biosphere's cloud-seeding emission (CLAW's sign still unmeasured), and anthropogenic SO₂ was both the acid-rain evil and the aerosol mask — whose ongoing removal (the IMO 2020 cap's ~0.1 W m⁻² step) is the unmasking the climate system is now paying for. Both cycles are the nitrogen lesson's mirror: phosphorus shows what happens with no sky and no exit; sulfur shows what happens when the sky is a dumping ground and then, suddenly, a clean one.
:::
