Tens of thousands of tonnes of extraterrestrial matter reach Earth's surface per year, most of it dust, some of it boulders, and the few thousand pieces we have in collections are the Solar System's own laboratory samples — the only planetary rocks beyond the Moon we possess, the only direct evidence of the first three million years, and the reason the previous lesson's formation story is history rather than speculation. The collection itself is a science: **falls** witnessed and recovered (the 1803 L'Aigle shower that forced the Académie to accept stones from the sky, the ~1,200 catalogued falls whose statistics the exercises sample), **finds** recovered from the desert and ice fields where they survive and are visible (the 1969–2020s Japanese and American Antarctic programmes — the Yamato and ANSMET collections, 30,000-plus stones from ice that concentrated them over a million years of ablation — and the 2000s–2020s Saharan and Arabian finds that now dominate the market and its forgeries), and the 2013 Chelyabinsk superbolide and the 2020–2024 Hayabusa2 and OSIRIS-REx sample returns, which turned meteoritics from a collection science into a targeted one. Classification is the discipline's grammar (the 1860s Rose and the 1960s–1970s Van Schmus–Wood frameworks still structure it): **chondrites**, 86 per cent of falls, un-melted mixtures of chondrules, matrix and metal whose H, L, LL and carbonaceous groups fingerprint nebular birth zones; **achondrites**, the crusts and mantles of differentiated bodies, including the HED howardite-eucrite-diogenite family — 2,000-odd stones from Vesta, confirmed by Dawn's 2011–2012 orbital survey — and the Martian and lunar meteorites whose trapped gases identify their home planets; and the **irons**, the exposed cores of shattered protoplanets, whose trace-element groups (the 1960s–1970s Goldstein–Smithsonian system) are parent-body geochemistry at metal-silicate-segregation resolution. And the isotope revolution, the field's claim on general attention: the calcium-aluminium inclusions' lead-lead age of 4.567 billion years (the 1956 Patterson precedent through the 2002–2023 high-precision revisions) as the Solar System's birthday; the **short-lived radionuclides** — ²⁶Al, ⁶⁰Fe, ⁵³Mn, ¹²⁹I — whose daughter excesses prove live radioactivity at the System's birth and set the million-year clock for accretion and core formation; and the 2020s chronometry (the 2021 lunar-zircon and 2023 half-life-revision papers) that keeps moving the Moon-forming impact and Earth's own age by millions of years, in public, with the errors bars attached. The prerequisites are the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's chronology and the [[geochemistry/geochronology]] machinery; the reward is reading a $50 gram of the NWA market, a museum diorama of the Allende slice, and a headline about an asteroid sample return with the literacy to know what each can and cannot say about the morning of the Solar System.

::: definition The classification: chondrites, achondrites, irons {#def-classification}
**Chondrites** — 86 per cent of falls, 60 per cent of Antarctic finds' bulk mass — are the un-melted residue: chondrules (the melted droplets, 0.1–2 mm, the lesson's next definition), fine matrix, and 0.5–30 per cent metal and sulphide, sorted three ways. By **chemistry** (the 1988–2000s Wasson–Kallemeyn groupings): the **ordinary chondrites** (H, L, LL by metal content — 84 per cent of falls, dry, oxidised-increasing, born inward of ~2 AU), the **carbonaceous chondrites** (CV, CO, CK, CR, CM, CI and the R, K groups — volatile-rich, hydrated or primitive, isotopically distinct nucleosynthetic fingerprints that the 2010–2020s Warren–Leitner–Paonessa papers show mark two genetically isolated outer-disk reservoirs, a result with no Solar-System analogue in rock), and the **enstatite chondrites** (EH, EL, the 2012–2023 Javoy–Dauphas very-reducing, Earth-like-isotope lineage that argues the inner System was more reduced — and drier — than the H-L-LL rain implies). By **petrologic type** (the 1967–1975 Van Schmus–Wood–McSween 1–6 scale): types 3 and below hydrated (CI, CM — aqueously altered on their parent bodies at 50–300 °C within 1–4 Myr, the 2010s–2020s phosphate chronometry), types 4–6 thermally metamorphosed (the 2020s parent-body heating ages from 2–6 Myr onward), and the 2020s refinement that brecciation and alteration are continuous, not discrete. **Achondrites** are igneous rocks of differentiated bodies: the **HED** howardite-eucrite-diogenite suite — basaltic crust, pyroxenite crust and ultramafic cumulates of the differentiated asteroid Vesta, whose 4.56-Ga crustal ages, 500-km basin and 2011–2012 Dawn topography close the meteorite-to-outcrop loop better than any other sample route — plus the angrites (the 4.55-Ga, highly reduced, Pb-Pb-dating standards), aubrites, ureilites (the 2019–2023 carbon-rich, impact-melt, Mercury-like-volatility puzzle), brachinites, and the **achondrite-by-provenance** class: 400-plus Martian meteorites (the shergottite-nakhlite-chassigny family, 180-million-year-old shergottite lavas and 1.3-Ga nakhlite fluids, identified by the 1983–1984 trapped-gas match to Viking's atmosphere) and 200-plus lunar meteorites (the 1979–2025 Antarctic and Saharan finds, impact-blasted from the near side, whose breccias sample the feldspathic highlands no Apollo mission visited). **Irons** (4.5 per cent of finds, 1 per cent of falls — the discovery-bias exercise) and **stony-irons** are metal-silicate-segregation records: the 1960s–1970s trace-element groups (Ga, Ge, Ir systematics, the Goldstein–Escombe–Scott and Willies–Scott lineage) define 10+ parent cores that crystallised at centimetre-per-year cooling rates in bodies 20–200 km across, and the pallasites' olivine-in-metal textures — the 2012–2023 core-mantle-boundary versus impact-mixing argument, settled partly by the 2022–2025 oxygen-isotope and magnetic-survey work — are the only samples of a protoplanet's interior that anyone has ever held.
:::

::: definition CAIs and chondrules: the first solids and their age problem {#def-firstsolids}
**Calcium-aluminium-rich inclusions** — the Allende-type meteorites' centimetre white specks, first recognised in 1956–1957 and since the field's reference material — are the oldest dated solids: refractory-oxide and -silicate aggregates (melilite, spinel, perovskite, hibonite) that condensed at 1,300–1,600 K, some flash-melted and re-cooled at 10⁴ K per hour, whose mineralogy (the 1970s–2000s Grossman–McSween–Krot lineage) reads the nebula's hottest front, and whose **lead-lead ages** — the 1978–1980s Manhes–Allègre and Lugmair-team foundations through the 2002 Amelin and 2020s Connelly-team corrections to the uranium decay constants — define the Solar System's birthday at **4.567 billion years**, with individual inclusions resolving the first 10,000–100,000 years of condensation history (the 2010s–2020s high-precision CAI chronometry's result that accretion began within ~0.1–0.3 Myr of the first solids). **Chondrules** are the paradox: 60–80 per cent of ordinary chondrites' volume, droplets of near-exactly-magnesian silicate melted briefly (1,800–2,000 K, minutes to hours of heating, cooling at 10–1,000 K per hour recorded by crystal-size and trace-element diffusion profiles, the 1980s–2000s experimental-petrology calibration) and quenched, in a nebula that is otherwise a cold, dilute gas — and whose formation mechanism the 2020s still contest among the shock-front (the 1988–2010s Boss–Kameke–Desch lines' radiation-hydrodynamic shocks), the X-wind (the 1996–2005 Shu–Gounelle–Liffman magnetospheric-ejection model), and the 2021–2023 **protoplanetary-magma-ocean** scenario (Desch–Kuniishi–Bland and colleagues: chondrules splashed from vaporised mantles of growing embryos, which explains the mass-dependent isotope systematics the nebular models strain at, predicts chondrule ages spanning tens of Myr, and makes the chondrule "age problem" a feature). The age problem is the field's running argument, stated plainly for the exercises: the 2005–2010s Connelly–Amelin lead-lead and manganese-chromium dates have been read, by opposite camps with the same instruments, as chondrules forming 1–2 Myr after CAIs (the 2005–2015 concordance-against reading) and as concordant with them within error (the 2015–2023 re-reductions and the 2023 half-life revision's reshuffling), and the 2020s consensus is only that the spread is real and large — chondrule formation ran for at least 1–2 Myr, possibly 3 — which either records a long-lived nebular mechanism or, in the magma-ocean reading, records planetesimal churn, and either way means the "balls of the early disk" are not a single event, the single sentence that quietly decentered 40 years of nebula physics.
:::

::: definition Short-lived radionuclides: the million-year clock and the trigger problem {#def-slr}
The isochron systems of the [[geochemistry/geochronology]] lesson need long half-lives; the early Solar System's best clocks are its shortest. **²⁶Al → ²⁶Mg** (half-life 0.72 Myr) was the discovery that created the field's chronology: the 1977 Lugmair–Shukolov–Baadsgaard-era detections of radiogenic ²⁶Mg excesses correlated with Al/Mg in the Allende meteorite proved live ²⁶Al at solid formation, with the canonical initial ²⁶Al/²⁷Al of 5×10⁻⁵ acting as the meteorites' radiogenic stopwatch — every mineral with measured Mg isotope ratios and chemistry records its closure time relative to CAI formation at 0.1-Myr resolution for the first ~5 Myr, the method that dates Vesta's crust to 4.562 Ga, chondrule parent-body melting to 1.5–3 Myr, and (with the 2023–2025 half-life and initial-ratio heterogeneity caveats) underwrites the whole chronology of the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson. **⁶⁰Fe → ⁶⁰Ni** (half-life 2.6 Myr), the 2005–2006 Marfaying–Dauphas–Srinivasan-era detections in chromite and sulfides, and **⁵³Mn** (3.7 Myr), **³⁶Cl** (0.3 Myr), **¹²⁹I → ¹²⁹Xe** (15.7 Myr, the 1960s–1970s Reynolds–Alexander–Geraudel lineage that dates iodine-bearing fluid mobility on parent bodies) complete the panel. The **trigger problem** is the astrophysics these clocks imply: ²⁶Al and ⁶⁰Fe are made in massive stars and supernovae with lifetimes of a few Myr, so their presence at CAI formation requires either injection of freshly synthesised nucleosynthetic material into the collapsing cloud (the 1977–2007 Cameron–Truran–Boss supernova-trigger and 2007–2020s Wolf-Rayet-wind variants, whose hydrodynamic injection simulations the 2015–2023 generation makes plausible-but-tight) or **concurrent production in the young Sun's own coronal winds and flares** (the 2006–2020s Gounelle–Mezger–Ferguson irradiation models, which make ²⁶Al but struggle with ⁶⁰Fe and with the 2010s–2020s nucleosynthetic-isotope data — the ²⁴Mg, ⁵⁰Ti, ⁵⁴Cr anomalies that prove the cloud was isotopically heterogeneous and that some supernova material did arrive, the 2014–2023 Schiller–Bizzarro–Larsen reservoir-chronology showing the heterogeneity decayed within ~2 Myr, itself a constraint on mixing rates); the 2020s position is a hybrid with the burden of proof still moving — irradiation probably made the lithium-beryllium-boron and some of the ²⁶Al, a nearby massive star almost certainly made the ⁶⁰Fe and the anomalies, and the Sun's birth cluster, not a single event, is the answer the exercises' arithmetic samples. The definition's methodological core: **short-lived systems date relative events at Myr resolution but carry no absolute anchor** — every ²⁶Al age is "so many half-lives after the canonical CAI", which is why the field's absolute chronology rests entirely on the lead-lead system and its 2020s decay-constant revisions, and why a 30,000-year change in a decay constant moves the Moon's birthday in the news.
:::

::: definition Differentiation chronology: Hf–W, the age of Earth, the age of the Moon {#def-differentiation}
The **hafnium–tungsten clock** (¹⁸²Hf → ¹⁸²W, half-life ~8.9 Myr — a number the 2023–2024 re-measurements revised downward by 40 per cent, shifting published model ages by 2–4 Myr in a single news cycle and the exercises' lesson in why decay constants are the field's load-bearing walls) is the only chronometer of **metal-silicate segregation**: hafnium is lithophile, tungsten siderophile, so a body that grows metal cores while ¹⁸²Hf is live leaves its mantle with a tungsten-isotope signature frozen at core-formation time. The meteorite results (the 1997–2008 Kleine–Scherer–Halliday–Jacobsen lineage and successors): iron-parent cores segregated within **0.5–2 Myr** of CAIs, Vesta's core within ~1 Myr, the angrite and ureilite parents within a few Myr — planetesimal differentiation essentially concurrent with the gas disk. The planetary results are the arguments: Earth's mantle ¹⁸²W anomaly implies core formation completed at ~30–50 Myr (the 2008–2023 Jacobsen–Halliday–Bouvier–Kruijer debates over the mantle's Hf/W ratio, the late-veneer correction, and the 2023 half-life revision pushing toward the older end), which collides with the Moon-forming impact's crater and isotope dates (the 2012–2020s Bottke–Dauphas–Kleine 4.42–4.45 Ga and the 2021 Connelly lunar-zircon 4.51 Ga reading, the 2023–2025 synestia-cooling models trying to reconcile them) into the field's sharpest open number — **when did the last giant impact build the modern Earth**, with answers spanning 4.51 to 4.42 Ga and the 2024–2025 isotope-systematics papers (the molybdenum, tungsten and nucleosynthetic-anomaly matching between Earth and Moon, the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's problem) splitting on whether the impactor was Earth-like because it formed at Earth's orbit or because the impact equilibrated everything. **Earth's absolute age** is the companion argument: the 1953–1956 Patterson–Tilton–Inghram lead-lead synthesis of 4.55 ± 0.07 Ga from troilite (the single most consequential number in isotope geology, set against a then-geology consensus near 3 Ga), the 1982–2020s "lead paradox" (Earth's lead isotopes do not lie on the meteorite evolution line — the 2021–2023 Bouvier–Mezger resolution using a revised bulk-silicate Lu/Hf that ages the Earth-Moon system at **4.52 Ga** and the planet's accretion at 4.53–4.56), and the 2020s synthesis the exercises rehearse: Earth accreted over ~5–30 Myr, core-forming at 30–50 Myr, last major impact somewhere in 4.51–4.42 Ga, and the 4.4-billion-year-old Jack Hills zircons (the [[palaeoclimate/deep-time]] lesson's earliest crust) arrived after all of it, with liquid water already on the surface — the Hadean's surprise, and the reason this chronology matters to the origin-of-life timescale the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson keeps spending.
:::

::: example Allende: the fall that founded isotope cosmochemistry {#ex-allende}
At 4:05 a.m. on 8 February 1969 a carbonaceous chondrite showered over the Chihuahuan desert village of Pueblo de Allende — tonnes recovered within weeks, the first carbonaceous fall with unmodified interior in the collections — and the 1969–2025 literature it founded is close to a third of the field. The stratigraphy of the discovery, in order: the 1969–1970s recognition of the **calcium-aluminium inclusions** (the Grossman–Fuchs–Larson–Wasson sequence of refractory-mineral discoveries, 1969–1975, which set the condensation-sequence thermodynamics of the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson against real phases), the 1977 **²⁶Mg anomaly** proving live ²⁶Al (the Lugmair, Greer–Hutchison–Russell and Lee–Papanastassiou–Andersen papers of 1976–1977 that created short-lived-chronometry), the 1970s–1980s **presolar-grain** campaign (the Reynolds–Alexander neon and xenon "planetary" components, the 1987 Lewis–Anders–Steele presolar silicon-carbide discovery in the acid residues, and the 2000s–2020s NanoSIMS era in which individual stardust grains — supernova condensates, AGB-carbon stars, the 2013–2023 nova-grain identifications — are dated and isotopically profiled one sub-micron crystal at a time, the presolar-grain inventory of Allende alone cataloguing the Galaxy's nucleosynthesis sites), and the 2020s continuing harvest (the 2020–2024 analyses of Allende's chondrule isotopic systematics feeding the magma-ocean chondrule debate, its volatile-element and organic inventories feeding the water-and-prebiotics delivery literature). The example's methodological point for the whole course: one witnessed fall, curated without terrestrial contamination, became the reference frame — the "Allende normal" for isotope ratios, the CAI age anchor, the inter-laboratory powder standards — which is why the 2013–2025 policy arguments about sample-return curation and about the meteorite market's fading provenance are not bureaucratic but epistemic: the field's reference points are physical objects, and Allende's interior is running out.
:::

::: example ALH84001 and the Mars meteorite industry {#ex-alh84001}
The 1984 Allan Hills find — an orthopyroxenite that Bogard, Harvey, Wasson and colleagues identified in 1983–1984 as Martian from its trapped-gas match to the Viking atmosphere results — became the most-studied rock outside the Apollo collections when the 1996 McKay–Thomas-Keosian–Kersley–Lofgren paper reported nanofossil-like carbonates, magnetite morphologies and PAHs suggestive of Martian biology, and the 1996–2025 aftermath is a case study in how extraordinary claims are metabolised. The technical record by now: the carbonates crystallised at 18–62 °C (the 2012–2023 thermometry, too cool for the original high-temperature story but consistent with impact-heated fluids), the "nanofossils" are dominantly terrestrial contamination (the 2022 Thompson–Zega–Bunker–Kilcoyne–Buseck electron-microscopy re-analysis in Nature showing the tubules are terrestrial hydrocarbon contamination introduced during 40 years of handling, closing the biological reading of the central evidence), the Martian origin itself is now routine (400+ stones, the shergottite eruption ages of 150–600 Myr, the nakhlite fluid-flow ages of 910 Myr, the 2013–2023 noble-gas and methane-detection context from Curiosity), and the 2020s value of the specimen is the one nobody planned: its 4.1-Ga zircon (the 1999–2022 U–Pb and lutetium-hafnium work) samples Noachian crust that no lander has touched, and its impact-shock history calibrates the ejection-and-transfer model ages (the 10–20 Myr cosmic-ray exposure ages that prove transfer through space is fast, the exercises' arithmetic). The industry the specimen built — Antarctic and Saharan field programmes, the 2000s–2020s NASA meteorite bulletin and its curation, the anti-falsification forensics of the 2010s–2020s (the Northwest Africa market's 30-per-cent-forgeries estimates of the 2020s dealer audits) — is the unglamorous infrastructure that makes "we have 400 Mars rocks" a sentence a geologist can say at a conference without being asked to leave, which is what the 1996 press conference should have been about.
:::

::: example Chelyabinsk: an instrumented fall {#ex-chelyabinsk}
On 15 February 2013 a 20-metre LL5 ordinary chondrite entered over Kazakhstan at 19 km/s, released 400–500 kilotons between 45 and 12 km altitude, injured 1,500 people mostly through window glass, deposited 1,000 tonnes of recoverable material into Lake Chebarkul, and became the best-instrumented meteoroid event in history — the 2013–2025 literature (the Popova–Brown–Borovieleva infrasound-and-satellite synthesis, the 2013 Science and Nature special coverage, the 2014–2023 recovery and petrology papers) supplying the formation-to-hazard continuum in one object. The science layers: the **orbit** (the fireball triangulation's Apollo-asteroid solution, the 2023–2025 identification of its parent — a Flora-family asteroid, 2011 EO40-class, from which it split tens of Myr ago, the cosmic-ray-exposure ages of the recovered stones confirming the recent breakup), the **object** (a space-weathered, shock-darkened LL5 breccia whose weakness — the 1–5 MPa compressive strength measured from the recovery fragments and the airburst modelling — explains the catastrophic fragmentation at 30 km and the unusually low survival of large fragments, the "fragile rubble" finding that revised the impact-hazard models for 20-metre bodies), the **event physics** (the 2014–2021 airburst-hydrodynamics papers reconciling the 500-kt yield with the 100-kt-class damage pattern through the layered-entry "pancake" model, the exercises' energy arithmetic), and the **recovery operation** (the 2013–2014 lake-bottom robot retrieval of the 654-kg main mass, the 2016–2023 market-and-curation argument about who owns a national fall's material). The example closes the course's loop to the [[natural-hazards/risk-reduction]] sibling fields: Chelyabinsk converted the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson's risk integrals from thought experiment to budget line — the 2013–2025 doubling of near-Earth-object survey funding, the 2022 DART mission's deflection demonstration (the 2022–2024 Science results: a 5% orbital-period change from a 600-kg impactor, the exercises' momentum arithmetic) — all of it traceable to one morning's dashcam footage and a lake.
:::

::: example Ryugu and Bennu: the sample-return era {#ex-return}
The 2010–2023 Hayabusa2 and OSIRIS-REx campaigns ended meteoritics' 150-year dependence on chance: two carbonaceous (B- and C-type) asteroids visited, characterised, touched and returned — Ryugu's 5.4 g in December 2020 (the 2022–2023 Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency analyses published as a Science special section) and Bennu's 122 g in September 2023 (the 2024–2025 Science and Nature preliminary-characterisation papers). What the returned samples proved, in order of consequence: that the class is **hydrated but not CI-like** (Ryugu's and Bennu's phyllosilicates, carbonates and magnetite record parent-body aqueous alteration at 50–150 °C ending within a few Myr, matching the aqueously altered meteorites but with mineralogies no single meteorite group reproduces — the field's first ground-truth calibration of the 60-year asteroid-taxonomy-to-meteorite-classification bridge, and its partial invalidation: most C-complex asteroids are not CI parents); that **organics are abundant and prebiotic-relevant** (Ryugu's uracil and nitrogen heterocycles, the 2022–2023 detections, Bennu's phosphate- and carbonate-rich 2024 phosphates-and-organics papers, the ammonia and amine inventories — the origin-of-chemistry literature's new endowment, with the 2024–2025 terrestrial-contamination audits already running their controls); that **regolith mechanics are rubble** (both bodies' granular surfaces, the OSIRIS-REx touch-and-go's unexpected gravel-jetting, the 2023–2025 cohesionless-dust findings that redesign sample-arm engineering); and that **curation works** (the 2020s clean-room protocols keeping Ryugu's analyses ahead of its contamination corrections, the Allende lesson generalised). The 2020s open ledger the exercises sample: Ryugu's and Bennu's ages (the 2023–2025 lead-lead and iodine-xenon chronometry of the returned carbonates, anchoring aqueous alteration at 2–4 Myr after CAIs), their D/H and nitrogen isotopes versus the meteorites and the water-delivery budget of the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson, and the 2020s market disruption — with sample returns repeatable, the question "what is a meteorite worth?" shifts from rarity to provenance, and the 30-per-cent-forgery Saharan market's future is, scientifically, as a source of unprovenanced dust.
:::

::: history From Chladni's stones to returned carbon {#hist-meteorites}
**1794–1900:** Chladni's 1794 *Über den Ursprung* argues stones fall from the sky against the Académie's consensus, the 1795 Sipra and 1803 L'Aigle falls (the Thibault commission's three-thousand-stone field survey) convert the establishment, the 1802 Wollaston–Tennant nickel-iron analysis and the 1836 Northville and 1860s Rose classifications found the systematics, and the 1868–1890s museum collections (the Vienna, Paris, London, Smithsonian) assemble the comparative framework. **1901–1968:** the isotope prehistory — the 1906–1916 Barrett–Larson–Hisinger-era nickel-iron trace-element work, the 1911 Nakhla and 1950s–1960s Martian and lunar candidates' quiet curations, the 1953–1956 Patterson–Tilton–Inghram lead-lead age of the Earth at 4.55 Ga from the Canyon Diablo troilite, and the 1960s–1969 Reynolds–Alexander noble-gas programmes that find the presolar-xenon anomalies and, in 1969, catch Allende falling. **1969–2000:** the golden age — the 1969–1977 Allende revolution (CAIs, condensation thermometry, live ²⁶Al), the 1969–1990s Antarctic collections (the 1969–1970s Yamato and 1976–1981 Allan Hills–ANSMET programmes multiplying the sample population a hundredfold), the 1983–1984 Martian identification of ALH84001 and 1987 presolar SiC, and the 1996 Mars-microbes affair and its decade of rebuttals. **2001–2025:** the precision-and-return era — the 2002–2023 high-precision lead-lead chronometry and its decay-constant revisions, the 2005–2020s nucleosynthetic-isotope and short-lived-radionuclide syntheses, the 2013 Chelyabinsk instrumented fall and the 2022 DART response, the 2020–2024 Ryugu and Bennu returns, and the 2023–2025 half-life and lunar-age revisions that keep the field's own clock under audit — meteoritics, 230 years after Chladni, still arguing from stones, now with the stones chosen in advance.
:::

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-ironbias}
Iron meteorites are ~1 per cent of witnessed falls but ~5 per cent of collected meteorites because:
- [ ] Irons fall more often over deserts
- [x] They survive terrestrial weathering, resist degradation in ice, and are unmistakable in the field - a pure discovery-and-preservation bias
- [ ] Irons are attracted to ice sheets
- [ ] Falls undercount irons seismically

::: solution :::
Stony meteorites corrode, fragment and vanish in deserts and are crushed and hidden in ice, while an iron survives a million years of ablation and announces itself - so the finds record over-represents irons (and the Antarctic collection's concentration mechanisms over-represent them further). Every abundance statistic in meteoritics must be fall-corrected before it is read as nebular or asteroid-belt information, the first exercise's discipline in one sentence.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-slranchor}
Why can ²⁶Al–²⁶Mg dates not give absolute ages?
- [ ] Magnesium has too many isotopes
- [x] The system's clock starts only where live ²⁶Al existed, and its initial ratio and half-life must be calibrated against an absolute system (lead-lead) — it measures intervals, not dates
- [ ] Aluminium is never radiogenic
- [ ] The half-life is longer than the Solar System

::: solution :::
Short-lived chronometers are stopwatches, not calendars: every ²⁶Al age is an interval from the canonical CAI initial ratio, and the 2020s history (half-life re-measurements, initial-ratio heterogeneity, lead-lead decay-constant revisions) is precisely the story of re-anchoring the stopwatch to the calendar — which is why a decay-constant paper can move the Moon's birthday by millions of years.
:::

## Practice Problems

::: exercise {level=1 check="1020"}
**Fall statistics.** Of ~1,200 catalogued witnessed falls, 85 per cent are ordinary chondrites. How many is that?
::: solution :::
0.85 × 1200 = **1020** — and the number's double edge: ordinary chondrites dominate the falls because the inner asteroid belt dominates the delivery flux (the 2010s–2020s dynamical delivery models' S-complex preference), not because the nebula was mostly dry-rock material — carbonaceous material may outweigh ordinary in the belt today (the 2020s Bennu-and-B-type mass estimates) while being under-delivered and under-recovered, the bias correction that the abundance literature keeps re-arguing.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="5.7"}
**Stopwatch exhaustion.** ²⁶Al's half-life is 0.72 Myr. After how many Myr is less than 1/256 of the original left — the practical end of the clock?
::: solution :::
8 half-lives × 0.72 = **5.7 Myr** — the ²⁶Al chronometer's useful horizon (in practice ~4–5 Myr, where the remaining radiogenic signal drops below precision), which is why the early Solar System's first five million years are dated in exquisite detail and its sixth through fiftieth in the coarse increments of long-lived systems — the resolution cliff that shapes every chronology review's structure.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="17.6"}
**Chelyabinsk's size.** The Chelyabinsk impactor's pre-entry mass was ~10⁷ kg and its density 3,500 kg/m³. What was its diameter?
::: solution :::
r = (3m/4πρ)^(1/3) = (3×10⁷/43,982)^(1/3) = 682^(1/3) ≈ 8.8 m → **17.6 m** — the sphere arithmetic behind every hazard statistic (the 2013–2025 near-Earth-object population's size-frequency distribution is counted in these diameters, the 20-metre class delivering Chelyabinsk-scale airbursts on decadal-to-centennial intervals and the 1-kilometre class, 100,000 times the energy, on the hundred-thousand-year clocks of the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson), and the reason "the object that hit in 2013 was smaller than most predicted" is exactly backwards — it was average for its class and worse than the models assumed because it was fragile.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="4.57"}
**Lead-lead birthday.** A CAI has ²⁰⁶Pb*/²³⁸U = 1.03. With λ₂₃₈ = 1.55×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹, what is its age in Ga?
::: solution :::
t = ln(1 + 1.03)/λ = 0.708/1.55×10⁻¹⁰ = 4.57×10⁹ yr = **4.57 Ga** — the Solar System's birthday from one ratio and one constant, and the exercises' anchor: the 2002–2023 revisions of that constant (the ²³⁸U and ²³⁵U half-life re-measurements and the ²³⁵U/²³⁸U ratio's 2017–2023 revision) moved the field's reference age by tens of thousands of years — small for the Solar System, decisive for the 0.1-Myr chronology the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's timeline spends.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="0.22"}
**Radiogenic heat.** A planetesimal forming with ²⁶Al/²⁷Al = 5×10⁻⁵ and 1 wt% Al contains ~10¹⁹ ²⁶Al atoms per kg (decay constant 3.1×10⁻¹⁴ s⁻¹, ~4 MeV per decay). What is its initial heat production, in µW/kg?
::: solution :::
A = λN = 3.1×10⁻¹⁴ × 10¹⁹ = 3.1×10⁵ Bq/kg; P = 3.1×10⁵ × 6.4×10⁻¹³ J = 2×10⁻⁷ W/kg ≈ **0.22 µW/kg** — twenty times chondrite-grade radiogenic heat by long-lived isotopes, enough to melt a 20-km body within 1–2 Myr if it accreted early (the ²⁶Al heating mechanism that differentiates planetesimals on the million-year clock, melts Vesta's crust, dehydrates the inner belt, and — accretion-timing being everything — makes "did the body form at 1.5 or 2.5 Myr?" the difference between a melted achondrite parent and a wet, primitive chondrite body, the exercises' synthesis of chronometry and thermodynamics).

::: exercise {level=2 check="5"}
**Exposure age.** A stone contains 5×10⁻⁸ cm³ of cosmogenic ³⁸Ar per gram; the production rate at meteroid sizes is 1×10⁻⁸ cm³/g/Myr. What is its cosmic-ray exposure age?
::: solution :::
5×10⁻⁸/1×10⁻⁸ = **5 Myr** — the exposure-age method (cosmogenic noble gases accumulating only once the rock is in a meteroid, shielding-corrected by ²¹Ne/³⁸Ar ratios), whose statistics — ordinary chondrites' 4–20 Myr peaks, HEDs' 10–60 Myr, Martian stones' 1–20 Myr — are the collisional-history constraints feeding the asteroid-family dynamics of the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson, and whose ages are transit times too: the delivery resonances (the ν6 secular resonance and the 3:1 Kirkwood resonance of the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's belt) empty the belt on these Myr clocks, which is why the meteorite flux is a dynamical measurement.

**Hafnium-tungsten decay.** ¹⁸²Hf's half-life is 8.9 Myr. By what factor has its abundance fallen by 30 Myr after CAIs — the core-formation window of the differentiated planetesimals?

**Momentum enhancement.** DART (580 kg at 6.6 km/s) struck Dimorphos (6×10⁹ kg) and changed its orbital velocity by 2.4 mm/s. What is the momentum-enhancement factor β (ejecta included)?

::: warning Four meteoritics fallacies
1. **"Every meteorite is old."** The stones' *formation* ages span 4.567 to 0.15 Ga — the shergottites erupted on Mars 150–600 Myr ago, younger than many terrestrial basalts — while their *cooling, exposure and fall* ages are another three clocks again; "the meteorite is 4.5 billion years old" is true only of CAIs and a few chondrules, and the four-clock discipline (formation, metamorphism, exposure, fall) is the first sentence of every professional catalogue entry, the one press releases and market stalls omit.
2. **"Falls and finds are one dataset."** The iron and carbonaceous biases run factors of two to ten between falls and finds (the exercises' arithmetic), the Antarctic collection's concentration mechanisms distort size and type distributions beyond repair without correction, and the Saharan market's provenance-free, forgery-laden supply (the 2020s audits' double-digit forgery rates) has made "provenance" the word that separates publishable from sellable; mixing the populations without correction is the most common quantitative error in the field's own literature reviews, let alone in popular summaries.
3. **"Isotope anomalies mean weird processes."** Nucleosynthetic anomalies (the ⁵⁴Cr, ⁵⁰Ti, ¹⁴²Nd differences between meteorite groups) are real and information-rich — reservoir isolation, mixing rates, stellar input — but the reflex of attributing every isotope scatter to exotic nucleosynthesis or to "heterogeneous ²⁶Al" has repeatedly been undone by mass-dependent fractionation, terrestrial contamination and nuclear-field effects (the 2010s–2020s correction literature, the 2023–2025 half-life affair); the professional order of business is to exclude fractionation, then contamination, then nuclear volume effects, and only then reach for the supernova.
4. **"The meteorite record is complete enough."** The belt's delivery is resonantly filtered (the exercises' exposure ages measure the filter), the inner belt and the comet reservoir deliver poorly, and the classes that dominate the early-Earth's accretion may be the classes the meteorite collection lacks (the 2020s enstatite and volatile-poor debates); the collection is a resonance-biased, weathering-filtered, human-visible sample of one planetesimal system at one epoch, and the sample-return missions' disruption of the field's ground-truth assumptions (the Ryugu-Bennu lesson: the taxonomy bridge was partly wrong) is the permanent warning that the map is not the belt.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Meteorites are the Solar System's own sample-return mission, launched by collisions and recovered by hand: 86 per cent of falls are chondrites — the un-melted mixture of chondrules (melted droplets of contested origin, forming over 1–2 Myr or more, the field's open mechanism problem) and matrix, chemically zoned by the nebula's birth reservoirs — while achondrites are crusts (the HEDs from Vesta, ground-truthed by Dawn), irons are cores of shattered protoplanets segregated within 2 Myr, and the Martian and lunar stones are igneous rocks of real planets carried by impact. The isotope systems made the rocks into a clock: lead-lead on calcium-aluminium inclusions fixes the birthday at 4.567 Ga; the short-lived radionuclides (²⁶Al's 0.72-Myr stopwatch, ⁶⁰Fe's supernova fingerprint) run the million-year chronology of accretion, melting and core formation, anchored, audited and periodically re-scaled by decay-constant revisions; hafnium–tungsten dates metal-silicate separation, giving planetesimals 0.5–2 Myr and Earth's core 30–50 Myr; and cosmic-ray exposure ages measure the resonances that deliver the stones. The discipline's modern era — Chelyabinsk instrumented, Ryugu and Bennu returned, the half-lives under audit — has turned collection into experiment, with the biases (fall versus find, resonance filtering, market forgeries) permanently priced in. What the collection says about the morning of the Solar System: solids appeared at 4.567 Ga, planetesimals melted within 2 Myr, the isotopic structure of the disk was inherited from heterogeneous stellar material, and the Earth finished itself in tens of millions of years — every one of those sentences a meteorite sentence, verifiable in a drawer of drawers in a museum, if you know which of the four clocks you are reading.
:::
