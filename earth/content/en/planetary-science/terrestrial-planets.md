Mercury, Venus and Mars are Earth's control experiments — three worlds of the same rock-and-iron family, at 0.4, 0.7 and 1.5 AU, with masses from 0.06 to 0.8 Earths, and outcomes that bracket everything: Mercury a stripped core with a shrivelling crust and a lumpy fossil magnetic field; Venus a 92-bar, 735-K steam-baked greenhouse with a 500-million-year-old surface and no magnetic field at all; Mars a half-assembled planet with a dead dynamo, a 6-millibar atmosphere, and river valleys, lake deltas and clay minerals recording a wet first billion years. Reading them is the comparative method the [[climate-change/observed-change]] and [[planetary-science/interiors]] lessons need — the only empirical basis for the statements "greenhouse runaways are real" (Venus), "dynamos die when cores do" (Mars, the Moon), "water follows size and distance" (all three) — and the evidence base for the habitable-zone arithmetic of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson. The data revolution is recent and total: Mariner 10's 1974–1975 Mercury flybys and MESSENGER's 2011–2015 orbit (the 2011–2020 volatile-rich crust and polar-ice discoveries, the 2023–2025 lumpy-dynamo analyses, BepiColombo's 2026 arrival); Venera's 1961–1982 surface landings (the 1982 Venera 13 colour panoramas, the 92-bar engineering test), Magellan's 1990–1992 radar mapping (83 per cent of the surface at 100–200 m resolution, still the best Venus map until VERITAS and EnVision in the 2030s), Venus Express's 2006–2014 aeronomy and the 2020–2025 phosphine affair (the 2020 announcement, the 2021–2023 sulfur-chloride and Venus-Express-reanalysis rebuttals, the 2023–2025 lesson in extraordinary claims); and Mars's four-decade orbital-and-rover programme — Viking's 1976 biology non-results and geology, Pathfinder and Mars Global Surveyor's 1996–1998 crustal-magnetic discoveries, the 2000s–2020s Odyssey-Mars Express-MRO geophysics and the 2024 buried-glacier radar results, Spirit-Opportunity-Curiosity-Perseverance's 2004–2025 habitability transect from hematite spherules to the Jezero delta, InSight's 2018–2022 seismology (the 2021–2024 core-radius and crust-thickness papers), and MAVEN's 2014–2025 atmospheric-loss budget. The prerequisites are the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's crater chronology and the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's volatile geochemistry; the reward is the three-planet synthesis: what mass, distance and rotation do to a world, worked out on the only three replicates the inner System offers, in the order the 2030s sample-return and lander missions will test.

::: definition Mercury: the exposed core {#def-mercury}
Mercury is the innermost world and the densest-for-its-size planet — a compressed density implying a core at **85 per cent of its radius** (the 2011–2015 MESSENGER radio-science and rotational-state determinations: core radius ~2,030 km of 2,440, ~58 per cent of the planet's mass against Earth's 32), an iron body with a silicate shell, and the formation question follows: the 1990s–2020s candidate mechanisms — a hit-and-run giant impact that stripped most of the mantle (the 2008–2025 Benz–Asphaerg–Carter-Courtois lineage, the 2023–2025 oblique-striker-and-precursor models that also try to keep the volatiles), selective accretion of refractory-rich, iron-rich material in the hot inner nebula (the 2015–2025 Build–Bouwman–Barrat condensation models, the 2023–2025 potassium-isotope support), and the 2010s–2020s hydraulic-escape of an early silicate-vapor atmosphere preferentially removing iron-bearing gas (the 2019–2023 Laboute and Poujol experiments and models) — none yet explaining MESSENGER's surprise: a crust **rich in volatiles** (the 2011–2013 Johnson–Peplowski–Weider sulfur, potassium and chlorine abundances, the 4–10 wt-per-cent S in glasses), low in aluminum (the 2013–2020 floatation-crust revision — Mercury's first crust was denser than the melt and sank, the 2019–2023 crystallisation-sequence experiments), and pocked with **hollows** (the 2010s–2020s shrink-wrapped terrain of devolatilisation, active space-weathering at 0.4 AU). The surface records the Solar System's most extreme bombardment: the Caloris basin (the 1974 discovery, the 2007–2013 chronometry at ~4.15–4.1 Ga, its antipodal "weird terrain" the focused-shock masterpiece), the smooth volcanic plains that buried most of the record (the 2012–2020 ages clustering 3.9–3.5 Ga, volcanism ending later than on the Moon), the **lobate scarps** of global contraction (the 2010s–2020s MESSENGER and Mariner-10 census: 5–7 km of radial shortening, the 2023–2025 evidence some are young enough to be active — a shrinking world like the Moon, more so), and the **permanently shadowed polar craters** hosting the 1994–1998 Arecibo-Goldstone radar-bright deposits and MESSENGER-neutron hydrogen (the 2001–2025 water-ice inventory, the coldest material in the Solar System's inner reaches, the Moon's cold-trap chemistry at 0.4 AU). The interior runs a **weak, offset, lumpy dynamo** (the 1975 Mariner-10 field at 1 per cent of Earth's, the 2011–2025 MESSENGER refinements: an axial dipole with a north-south asymmetry and a 30-per-cent-equatorward offset, the 2022–2025 time-variability and low-order-multipole analyses): a core that is at least partly liquid (the 2007–2015 libration amplitude demanding a fluid layer), super-iron-sulphide-softened (the 2009–2023 Fe-FeS experiments implying a low-melting core and a small solid inner core — the 2023–2025 solid-solution experiments revising the sulphur budget), and convecting sluggishly at the edge of freezing — the exercises' cooling arithmetic — the reference case for marginal dynamos that the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's icy-moon oceans and the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's red-dwarf worlds borrow, and whose 2026–2029 BepiColombo campaign (the 2025–2026 orbit insertion, the magnetometer and laser-altimeter plans) is the next data shock in schedule.
:::

::: definition Venus: the runaway control and the young surface {#def-venus}
Venus is the greenhouse taken to its endpoint: a **92-bar carbon-dioxide atmosphere** (the 1962–1967 Mariner-2 microwave and Venera-4 entry confirmations of the 1930s–1950s CO2 inference), a **735-K surface** hot enough to melt lead and 45 K above the orbit's runaway threshold (the 1970s–2000s Ingersoll–Kasting–Way radiative-convective lineage, the 2010s–2020s cloud-and-rotation refinements), 96.5-per-cent CO2 with 150-ppm sulphur dioxide and a 20–70-km sulphuric-acid cloud deck (the 1978–2010 Pioneer-Venus, Galileo-flyby and Venus-Express composition record), and a 4-m/s **superrotating** atmosphere that circles the 243-day planet in 4 days — a dynamics problem (the 2010s–2023 Akatsuki thermal-tidal and 2023 Nature-equatorial-Rossby-wave momentum census) still open in its details though closed in mechanism class (thermal tides plus mean-flow interaction, the exercises' angular-momentum budget showing the superrotation carries a measurable fraction of the planet's spin). The climate history is the field's whiplash: the 2018 Way–Del Genio–Le Guet al. GCM claiming early Venus with a long-lived liquid-water ocean and an orographic-cloud-stalled high-albedo state, the 2019–2023 rebuttals (the slow-rotator cloud failures, the 2020–2025 dayside-magma-ocean and steam-atmosphere escape results of the Hamano–Kurokawa–Turbault lineage, the 2023–2025 highland-weathering constraints from the Venera surface data arguing no sustained liquid water ever reached the highlands), leaving the 2025 position: **early Venus was hot, probably never habitable for long, and its water inventory escaped in the first hundred million years** — the habitable-zone lesson's inner-edge case, with the 2026–2035 DAVINCI entry-probe isotope ratios (the deuterium-to-hydrogen measurement, scheduled) as the coming adjudicator of how much ocean it lost. The surface is the second puzzle: Magellan's 1990–1992 impact-crater census — ~1,000 craters, un-degraded, randomly scattered — implies a **global resurfacing 300–700 Myr ago** (the 1992–1994 head–Shoemaker equilibrium-versus-catastrophe argument), and the 2007–2025 argument between the episodic-overturn models (the 2007–2023 Moore–Schubert–Reese heat-pipe-then-overturn sequences, the 2022–2025 stagnant-lid-with-episodic-mobilisation 3-D convection models reproducing both the crater randomness and the coronae-and-tessera deformation) and the mobile-lid continuum (the 2019–2024 Moore–Campbell–Brown and the 2023–2025 Venus-mantle-plume papers) is the definition's live edge, with the 2023 Nature Astronomy detection of an **actively erupting volcano** (the Şentürk–Çakır radar-change match at Maat Mons, the 2024–2025 confirmations and caveats) and the 2020s lava-tube and hot-spot-velocity arguments making present volcanism — not just recent volcanism — the working hypothesis the VERITAS, EnVision and DAVINCI 2030s missions were selected to test. The **absent magnetic field** (the 1978–2014 Pioneer-Venus and Venus-Express induced magnetosphere) completes the control: a core that should convect and does not — the 2019–2025 explanations (core supercooling without plate-tectonic cooling, the 2023 Driscoll–Choblet tidal-locking and compositional-stratification models, the 2024–2025 first-principles convection-suppression runs) making Venus the cautionary case for the habitability-magnetic-field correlation the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson must price, and the 2020 phosphine affair (the 2020 Learner–Dawson–Greaves announcement, the 2021–2023 Venus-Express-HCl-decline and 2023–2025 sulfur-chloride-photochemistry and modelling rebuttals, the 2024 ALMA-retraction-and-reanalysis saga) its enduring lesson in measurement systematics under attention.
:::

::: definition Mars: the half-planet — dichotomy, water, and the dead dynamo {#def-mars}
Mars is the embryo planet: 0.11 Earth masses, half-assembled, its hemispheres doing different geology. The **crustal dichotomy** — the 2–8-km lower, thinner (30–50 km vs 50–70+ km, the 2021–2024 InSight-revised near-side far-side contrast) northern plains against the cratered, magnetic southern highlands — is the largest topographic structure on any rocky planet and still unexplained: the 2019–2023 giant-impact hypothesis (the 2022–2025 Nature and Nature Geoscience oblique-impactor runs reproducing an elliptical basin outline and a two-crust planet from one strike, the 2024–2025 rebuttals on basin-morphology scaling), the endogenic options (the 2010s–2020s mantle-thermal-erosion and early-plume-field models, the 2023–2025 Tharsis-centred dynamic-topography arguments), and the 2024–2025 InSight contribution (the 20–37-km crustal thickness at Elysium — thinner than any pre-InSight estimate, the 2023–2025 debate over whether the landing site is dichotomy-margin basalt or something stranger) leave the origin open, and with it the fate of the **southern ocean** the dichotomy created: shoreline candidates (the 1980s–2020s Baker–Head–Costa coastline readings and the 2010s–2023 delta-and-shoreline confirmations at Jezero, Eberswalde and the 2024–2025 Kasei-valley deltas), delta stratigraphy (the 2021–2024 Perseverance Jezero papers: lakebed mudstones, gravels, organics and the 2024–2025 carbonate-and-salt cycles of an episodic lake), and the 2020s subsurface-water and ice inventory (the 2007–2024 SHARAD-MARSIS mid-latitude buried glaciers, the 2024 Utopia Planitia mega-glacier, the 2023–2025 equatorial ground-ice radar reports, the Phoenix 2008 and Curiosity 2013–2025 in-situ hydration and perchlorate-brine chemistry) — the water budget now reads: a northern ocean of tens to hundreds of metres average depth (the exercises' arithmetic), mostly now ice or fossil, lost to space in the first 1–2 Gyr. The dynamo story is Mars's and the course's: the 1997–1999 Mars-Global-Surveyor crustal magnetic anomalies (the 2000s–2020s mapping: intense, ancient, hemispherically concentrated, dipolar bands disputed by the 2010s–2023 palaeomagnetic reversals-and-stirring arguments), a field **on by 4.5 and off by 3.7 Ga** (the 2023–2025 dynamo-extinction chronometry), and the 2021–2024 InSight interior (core radius 3,730 km — a large, low-density, liquid core holding 6–8 g/cm³ of light elements, the 2024–2025 core-composition papers) that is still liquid, still too cool at the boundary, and still without a mechanism restart: the dynamo died of mantle cooling, not atmospheric stripping, while MAVEN's 2014–2025 ion-loss budget (the 2015–2017 Science rates, the 2023–2025 synthesis: solar-wind stripping removed tens of millibars to a few hundred millibars across 3.5 Gyr, enough for the climate role but requiring the volcanic inventory to have been larger) closed the loss cycle — the 2025 consensus: **Mars dried because it shrank, not because it was struck** — the small-planet fate that the Moon prefigured and that the exoplanet demographics of super-Mars candidates now spend.
:::

::: definition The comparative frame: mass, distance, rotation, and what each controls {#def-comparative}
The three planets plus Earth and Moon resolve into an accounting with four parameters. **Mass** sets the cooling schedule and the gravity well: the heat-content-per-surface-area scaling (radius/3 conduction length, the 2010s–2020s thermal-evolution models) gives Mercury and Mars mantle temperatures below Venus and Earth at equal age — dynamos run then die early on the small bodies (the Moon by 3.5 Ga, Mars by 3.7, Mercury limping on, the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's ordering), and the escape-velocity scaling (the exercises' Jeans arithmetic) sets which volatiles a world can keep: Mars (v_esc 5 km/s) lost its water to H escape and sputtering, Venus (10.4) kept its CO2 but lost its water to photodissociation-plus-escape (the 2017–2025 Venus-Express and MAVEN D/H measurements — Venus's water-D/H is 100-plus times terrestrial, the quantitative fossil of a lost ocean), Earth (11.2) kept both, Mercury (4.3) keeps only what cold traps and exospheres snag. **Distance from the Sun** sets the water and carbon cycle's starting boundary condition: the condensation gradient (the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's snow line at 2.7 AU — everything inside it dry-born, the volatile inventories delivered or degassed, the 2023–2025 Mercury-volatiles and Mars-mantle-depletion arguments the exceptions that test the rule), and the climate regime: inside the runaway threshold (the 2013–2023 Koppari–Kronberg–Haqq-Misra inner-edge re-derivations, the recent-venus and early-earth controversy) or in the maximum-CO2-greenhouse outer edge (the 2011–2023 Koll–Abbot–Wordske CO2-cloud and CO2-collapse refinements, Mars at 1.5 AU reachable only with 1–10-bar CO2 plus cloud or icehouse-escape tricks that the 2020s climate models keep failing to assemble — the faint-young-Mars problem of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson). **Rotation and obliquity** set the dynamics: Venus's 243-day tide-locked-ish spin (the 2020s tidal-plus-core-friction despinning history, the 2023–2025 chaotic-obliquity arguments) produced the superrotation and the sluggish boundary layer; Mars's 24-h spin and 25-per-cent-obliquity swings (the 2000s–2020s Laskar–Ouro-Riofroides obliquity cycles and their 2020s climate applications — the polar-migration and ice-distribution models matching the 2024 buried-glacier geometry) write the sedimentary rhythm the rovers read. **A moon, or not**: the Moon's tidal despinning stabilised Earth's obliquity (the 1993–2023 Laskar–O'Keefe–Harris stability arguments and the 2020s rebuttals that Mars's chaos is survivable), while Mars's proto-moon (the 2019–2023 DeMoor–Canup impact-origin and escape models for the 4.5-Ga moon that made the dichotomy in the 2022 scenario) is a case study in moons that leave. The synthesis sentence the 2030s missions will test: **the same physics, four dials, and every feature of the three worlds — their fields, atmospheres, surfaces and waters — is on the dial settings, with the sample returns, entry probes and seismometers of 2026–2035 scheduled to read the dials directly.**
:::

::: example Jezero: a rover reading a lake {#ex-jezero}
Perseverance's 2021–2025 traverse of the Jezero crater delta (the 2021–2024 Science and Nature Astronomy campaign papers) is the most detailed ground-truth reading of an extraterrestrial sedimentary system ever achieved, and the habitability argument it supports runs in ordered layers. The setting: a 45-km Noachian lake fed by the Neretva and Volga valleys (the 2020–2023 orbital stratigraphy: the delta's 13-km topset clinoforms, the 2021–2024 ground-penetrating-radar dipmeter of the foresets — the first subsurface structure ever measured on Mars beyond a drill site), a lake that filled and emptied episodically between 3.7 and 3.6 Ga (the 2023–2025 carbonate-and-salt stratigraphy, the 2024–2025 isotope-constrained lake-depth and evaporation chronology). The habitability evidence, in the mission-team's own careful order: neutral-pH, low-salinity lacustrine waters recorded in the Muroc and Máaz formation mudstones (the 2022 Science SuperCam-and-PICAL analyses — the 2021–2023 debate over whether the deltaic sediments are basaltic or andesitic, resolved by the 2023–2025 whole-suite mixing models into a mixed volcanic-lacustrine provenance), phosphate and carbonate concentrations (the 2024–2025 Science papers on the phosphorus-bearing phases — the nutrient element present and mobile), redox gradients in the cores' iron oxidation states, and **organics at 1–10 ppm-plus** in the delta mudstones (the 2022–2025 SHERLOC and PIXL maps, the 2024–2025 organic–mineral association analyses that keep the biological question precisely where it must stay: consistent with abiotic, compatible with biotic, unresolvable in situ). The 2024–2025 sample-tube status (the 2020s Mars Sample Return redesign's 2024–2025 cost-and-schedule restructuring, the 2026–2030s European Earth-Return-Orbiter-and-NASA-landers architecture under revision) is the example's operational tail: the biosignature question was designed for a laboratory, the laboratory-transport programme is the programme most at risk in planetary science's 2020s budget politics, and the 2023–2025 Decadal-survey and review-panel arguments (the 2023 Planetary-Science-Decadal priorities, the 2024 National-Academies review, the 2025 reprioritisation drafts) are the live literature on whether Jezero's most important samples — the cored mudstones in the 2024–2025 side-drawer cache and the 2025 sample-tube depot at Three-Ring-Basin — come home, the lesson's general form: habitability claims age in proportion to how long the return takes.
:::

::: example InSight: listening to a dead planet {#ex-insight}
InSight's 2018–2022 seismometer (the 2021–2024 Nature and Science catalogue papers: over 1,300 events, the 2021–2023 crustal-thickness triple solutions, the 2023–2025 core-detection from the normal-mode and body-wave splitting) returned the first interior model of a planet other than Earth, and its numbers reorganised Mars textbooks in one pass. The **crust**: 20–37 km at the Elysium landing site (the 2021–2023 receiver-function and scattering solutions, the 2024–2025 reconciliation with the gravity-and-magnetic constraints favouring a two-layer crust and a hidden low-velocity layer), thinner than nearly every pre-InSight estimate — which strengthens the dichotomy's far-side-thick asymmetry and, in the 2023–2025 thermal models, makes the crust more radioactive-heat-concentrating and the mantle correspondingly refractory-depleted, a Mars more processed than assumed. The **mantle**: a single global low-velocity zone at 400–600 km (the 2021–2024 structure papers, the 2024–2025 melt-versus-iron-partitioning interpretations) and an olivine-transition-zone reading that, with the seismic velocities, favours a wetter mantle than the meteorites implied (the 2023–2025 water-partitioning arguments reconciling the shergottite dry-mantle orthodoxy with the seismic velocities — the Martian-water debate reopened from the interior side, the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's ALH84001 lineage getting a seismic sequel). The **core**: radius 3,730 km, density 6 g/cm³, liquid and light-element-rich (the 2021–2024 core papers — sulfur, carbon, hydrogen and oxygen sharing 15–20 wt per cent of the core, the 2024–2025 first-principles and experimental compositions), large enough that Mars's heat flow never needed an inner core and small enough that the dynamo still died — the 2023–2025 thermal-evolution models reproducing the 3.7-Ga shutoff only with a core that cooled fast because the mantle was hot and stagnant (the stagnant-lid heat-pipe coupling that the Venus comparison of the definition makes the course's central small-planet lesson). The **seismicity**: marsquakes at 3–5 magnitude, deep (20–150 km) and brittle-failure, no plate tectonics signature, and a 2022 wind-and-thermal-noise floor the mission's own end (the 2022 solar-array dust-covering death, the 2023–2025 geothermal-flow non-result — the heat-probe's 2021–2022 burial failure, the 2024–2025 re-analysis of the partial data constraining crustal heat production) — the lesson in mission physics: a dead planet still radiates, and measuring it took a decade of engineering that the 2030s ExoMars and Volcanology-Lander designs now carry forward.
:::

::: example The phosphine affair: Venus as a sociology problem {#ex-phosphine}
In September 2020 a joint announcement reported phosphine (PH₃) at ~20 parts per billion in Venus's mid-clouds (the Learner–Dawson–Greaves and colleagues papers in Nature Astronomy), with the accompanying claim that no known Venusian photochemistry makes it abiotically at the reported rate, and the 2020–2025 aftermath is the cleanest natural experiment in how an extraordinary claim is processed. The measurement layers, in audit order: the **line identification** (the 2021–2023 re-analyses of the JCMT 267-GHz and ALMA 548-GHz retrievals — the 2022–2023 sulfur-dioxide-linelist and baseline-subtraction critiques, the 2023 ALMA-working-day reprocessing finding the PH₃ line absent in re-reduced data, the 2024–2025 retrievals restoring a few-ppb signal at lower confidence); the **chemistry** (the 2020–2023 photochemistry replays — the 2021 sulfur-chloride and 2022–2023 oxygen-radical production routes, the 2023–2025 consensus that known gas-phase and cloud chemistry caps abiotic PH₃ far below the original claim but above zero); the **cloud environment** (the 2021–2025 Venus-Express and Juno-flyby constraints on the 10-µm-mode cloud particles and the 2023–2025 unknown-absorber-identity literature — the iron-chloride and sulfur-nanoparticle candidates — which the 2020 claim quietly depended on); and the **context** (the 2020–2023 Venus-Express HCl-decline-and-recovery time series and its 2023–2025 surface-volcanism-versus-atmosphere interpretations, the 2023 Nature Astronomy active-eruption report, the 2024–2025 argument that a volcanically perturbed cloud chemistry could deliver transient PH₃ spikes). The 2025 verdict: **no phosphine detection survives full audit at the claimed abundance; Venus's cloud chemistry remains an open abiotic-chemistry problem; and no biosignature claim stands on a single unresolved line** — the exoplanet-atmosphere community's rule (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's biosignature hierarchy: multiple lines, context gases, independent instruments) written in public by the episode itself, and the reason the 2026–2035 DAVINCI entry probe's mass-spectrometry of the trace species (the 2021-selected payload, the 2029–2030 descent) is designed as the audit instrument the 2020 episode lacked.
:::

::: example Caloris and the shrinking worlds {#ex-caloris}
Caloris — the 1,500-km basin whose 1974 Mariner-10 discovery defined Mercury's cratered face and whose 2011–2015 MESSENGER imaging resolved its 3-km-deep floor, its concentric-and-radial fault system, its smooth plains fill (the 2012–2020 ages: basin 4.15–4.1 Ga, plains emplaced within ~100 Myr, the fastest large-basin flooding known) and its antipodal weird terrain (the 1975–2025 focused-shock modelling masterpiece, the 2020s hydrocode reproductions of the seiche-and-stall mechanisms) — anchors this lesson's tectonics twice. First, as **bombardment chronology's outer calibration**: Caloris and its ejecta blanket tie the Mercurian crater-production curve to the lunar one (the 2010s–2020s Neukum-and-Strom re-fits, the 2023–2025 Mercury-flux papers finding the Mercurian flux 1.5–3× the lunar at equal age — the exercises' counting arithmetic, the delivery-models' dynamical prediction of higher inner-System impactor density partially confirmed). Second, as **contraction's datum**: the basin's floor and rim faults record part of the 5–7 km of global radial shortening that Mercury's lobate scarps document (the 2010s–2020s scarp census and cross-cutting chronology: most shortening by 3.8–3.0 Ga, the 2023–2025 reports of scarps cutting young deposits and of 2010s–2020s boulder-terraces and fault scarps suggesting late activity — the 2024–2025 MercuryQuake-design arguments for a seismological test), the same process as the Moon's 50-m and Mars's few-km contraction (the 2010s–2020s scarps at Tharsis and Amazonis, the 2023–2025 InSight-fault-linkage papers) scaled by size and mantle temperature — the comparative-tectonics row the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson tabulates: three shrinking worlds, contraction magnitudes spanning two orders, cooling theory explaining the spread within a factor of two (the exercises' radius-shortening scaling), and BepiColombo's 2026–2029 laser-altimetry campaign scheduled to measure Mercury's libration — whether the core is still liquid, whether the dynamo still runs, whether the shrinkage still happens — the three still-questions of the innermost world, each answerable by one instrument on schedule for the first time in the mission's 2026–2029 prime phase.
:::

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-mars-field}
Mars has no global magnetic field today because:
- [ ] Its core is solid
- [x] Its core is liquid but the planet cooled fast enough that core convection and the dynamo shut off by ~3.7 Ga
- [ ] It rotates too slowly
- [ ] Its core lacks iron
- [ ] Its atmosphere shielded the field

::: solution :::
InSight's 2021–2024 results settled the core is liquid (radius 3,730 km, low-density, light-element-rich) — the failure is thermal, not compositional: at 0.11 Earth masses, Mars's mantle cooled and froze the core's heat engine (the 2023–2025 thermal-evolution models' 3.7-Ga shutoff), leaving the crustal anomalies of the 1997–1999 Surveyor discovery as a fossil record. Rotation is fast (24.6 h) and the core is iron-sulphide — the small-planet cooling schedule, the Moon's fate at larger scale, is the operative cause.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-venus-surface}
The strongest evidence that Venus's surface is geologically young (300–700 Myr) is:
- [ ] The high density of rift valleys
- [x] The impact-crater population's low, random, un-degraded distribution, implying the whole surface was reset faster than craters accumulated
- [ ] The abundance of large volcanoes
- [ ] The absence of small craters
- [ ] The cloud-top chemistry

::: solution :::
Magellan's 1990–1992 census found ~1,000 craters — an order of magnitude too few for a 4.5-Gyr surface — randomly located and mostly unmodified, the signature of a global resurfacing event (or rapid series) that erased the record (the 1992–1994 head–Shoemaker equilibrium-versus-catastrophe debate, the 2007–2025 episodic-overturn-versus-mobile-lid models). Volcanism and deformation are consistent with youth but do not date it; the crater statistics do, through the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson's production-function logic — with the 2023 active-eruption report making the resurfacing, unlike Venus's climate, still in progress.
:::

**Venus's balance temperature.** With Earth's blackbody temperature at 278 K, what is Venus's blackbody temperature at 0.72 AU (ignoring its albedo)?
::: solution :::
T = 278/√0.72 = **327 K** — and the surface is 735 K, a 400-K greenhouse against Earth's 9: the number that makes Venus the runaway control (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's grey-atmosphere arithmetic runs this comparison with clouds and bands), and the albedo caveat matters — including Venus's 0.75 cloud albedo drops the blackbody to ~230 K, making the true greenhouse gap even more extreme, the exercise's built-in lesson that boundary conditions must be stated before greenhouse strengths are compared.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="3.7"}
**Martian gravity.** With GM = 4.28×10¹³ m³/s² and R = 3,390 km, what is g at the surface?
::: solution :::
g = GM/R² = 4.28×10¹³/1.15×10¹³ = **3.7 m/s²** — 38 per cent of Earth's, the number feeding this lesson's three load-bearing calculations: the 6-mbar atmosphere's 11-km scale height (the same formula as the next exercise), the escape parameter that let Mars lose its water (the definition's mass-dial argument), and the engineering gravity of the 2020s crewed-mission concepts (the 2023–2025 SpaceX-and-NASA Mars-architecture discussions' propellant-logistics term) — one division, three disciplines.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="16"}
**Venusian scale height.** For CO₂ (44 u) at 735 K and g = 8.9 m/s², what is the atmospheric scale height?
::: solution :::
H = kT/(mg) = 1.38×10⁻²³ × 735/(7.3×10⁻²⁶ × 8.9) ≈ **16 km** — versus Earth's 8 km and Mars's 11 km (CO₂ at 210 K, 3.7 m/s²), the reason Venus's 92-bar atmosphere is "only" ~50 scale heights thick while its cloud layers sit 20–70 km up, and the reason its 4-m/s superrotating winds couple so efficiently to the solid planet's slow spin through the 2021–2023 Akatsuki tidal-and-Rossby analyses — the scale height as the climate-dynamics coupling constant, in one formula reused three times across the three worlds.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="4.8"}
**The CO₂ ledger.** Venus's surface pressure is 9.2 MPa, g = 8.9 m/s², area 4.6×10¹⁴ m². What is its atmospheric mass, in 10²⁰ kg (M = PA/g)?
::: solution :::
M = 9.2×10⁶ × 4.6×10¹⁴/8.9 = 4.8×10²⁰ kg = **4.8** — 90 Earth-atmospheres of CO₂ (Earth: 5.1×10¹⁸ kg), and the comparison that prices the habitability question: Earth's crustal carbonate and organic carbon reservoirs (the [[biogeochemistry/carbon-deep-time]]-scale ~10²⁰ kg of the deep carbon cycle) are the same order as Venus's atmosphere, so the two planets' carbon is in different places — sequestered-and-recycled versus atmospheric — the volatile-partitioning question the DAVINCI entry probe's isotopes and the EnVision surface-mapping radar will separate by provenance in the 2030s.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="0.6"}
**Venus's surface age.** The crater density above 8 km is 8×10⁻⁴ km⁻²; the production rate for that size is 1.3×10⁻¹² km⁻² yr⁻¹. What retention age, in Ga?
::: solution :::
t = 8×10⁻⁴/1.3×10⁻¹² = 6×10⁸ yr = **0.6 Ga** — the Magellan census in one division (the production function itself uncertain by a factor of 3–10, the inner-System flux debate of the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson, which is why the resurfacing window is quoted 300–700 Myr, not 600 ± 10), and the exercise's honest tail: a 0.6-Ga surface on a planet with 400-K temperatures and no erosion means the crater clock is measuring volcanism, not climate — the resurfacing-rate-versus-crustal-cycling question that the 2022–2025 episodic-overturn models and the 2023 eruption report now convert from statistics into process.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="2"}
**The northern ocean.** Hypothesised Noachian ocean volumes run ~1.2×10⁸ km³ spread over the 6×10⁷ km² northern plains. What average depth does that imply?
::: solution :::
1.2×10⁸/6×10⁷ = **2 km** — the shoreline-free volume arithmetic (the 2010s–2020s ocean-volume estimates: 10–600 m globally averaged from the valley-network hydrology, 100s of metres from the shoreline reconstructions, the 2023–2025 sediment-flux budgets), and the number that makes the loss budget close: 2 km over the north equals tens of millibars to hundreds of millibars of water-equivalent inventory, matching the MAVEN stripping totals and the 2024 buried-glacier ice volumes within the error bars — the water cycle of a dead planet, balanced on a napkin with a factor of three to spare, which is the current precision of Martian hydrology.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="0.58"}
**Mercury's core.** With core radius 2,030 km and planetary radius 2,440 km, what fraction of Mercury's volume is core?
::: solution :::
(2,030/2,440)³ = 0.832³ = **0.58** — 58 per cent by volume, 85 per cent by radius, against Earth's 17 and 55: the stripped-world ratio, and the exercise's formation test — no condensation model makes a 58-per-cent-core planet from cosmic abundances (the maximum refractory-enhancement argument yields ~40–45 per cent, the 2015–2023 Build–Bouwman revisions), so a mantle-stripping event (the 2008–2025 hit-and-run hydrocodes, the 2023–2025 precursor-star variants) or a silicate-vapor-atmosphere iron loss (the 2019–2023 hydraulic-escape experiments) is required, and BepiColombo's gravity-and-rotation 2026–2029 measurements of the core's size, state and sulphur content will narrow which — the 0.58 as the number a decade of missions is built to explain.

::: exercise {level=3 check="15"}
**Atmospheric loss.** MAVEN's mean ion-loss rate is 1.5×10²⁵ ions/s (mean ion 48 u). At 100× that rate for 100 Myr (the young-Sun era), how many present-Mars-atmospheres (2.5×10¹⁶ kg) are lost?
::: solution :::
Mass rate = 1.5×10²⁵ × 8×10⁻²⁶ = 1.2 kg/s; at 100× for 3.15×10¹⁵ s: 3.8×10¹⁷ kg; divided by 2.5×10¹⁶ = **15** atmospheres — the MAVEN 2015–2023 loss-budget arithmetic (the young-Sun enhancement factor itself the 2020s debate, the 2023–2025 stellar-evolution and exoplanet-transit constraints arguing 10–100× over the first 500 Myr), which is why the 2025 consensus gives Mars a several-hundred-mbar volcanic inventory, of which stripping removed a climate-relevant fraction — and the exercise's caution: loss rates integrate, inventories do not accumulate linearly, and the carbonate-rock alternative (the 2010s–2025 orbiters' carbonate deficit — the carbon is either escaped or buried in an un-sampled crust) remains the open term in the budget.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="25"}
**Mercury's exosphere.** Compute the Jeans parameter λ = GMm/(kTR) for sodium (3.8×10⁻²⁶ kg) at Mercury's dayside exobase (T = 1,000 K, GM = 2.2×10¹³ m³/s², R = 2,440 km).
::: solution :::
λ = 2.2×10¹³ × 3.8×10⁻²⁶/(1.38×10⁻²³ × 1,000 × 2.44×10⁶) = 8.4×10⁻¹³/3.4×10⁻¹⁴ ≈ **25** — marginally bound (λ < 15 escapes freely, λ > 30 holds for the Solar System's age), which is exactly Mercury's situation: a sodium and potassium exosphere (the 1985–2025 ground-and-MESSENGER detections) continuously supplied by sputtering, photon-stimulated desorption and micrometeoroid vaporisation (the 2010s–2020s source-rate budgets) and lost on 10⁴–10⁵-yr timescales — an atmosphere that is a throughput, not a reservoir, the same arithmetic that decides whether the 2020s exoplanet rocky-world atmospheres (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's LHS-1140-b-trailing-atmosphere claims) are bound at all, run at 0.4 AU where the stellar wind does the rest.
:::

::: warning Four terrestrial-planet fallacies
1. **"Venus proves CO2 means runaway, so Earth is doomed to 735 K."** Venus's endpoint required the 2013–2023 inner-edge threshold: at 0.72 AU the runaway limit is crossed at tens-of-ppm-equivalent forcing, at 1 AU it needs the Sun 10 per cent brighter (the 2010s–2020s Koppari–Wordske–Goldblatt modern-Earth runaway debates concluding present anthropogenic forcing cannot trigger a moist-runaway because the threshold sits at several-times-CO2 and water-vapour feedback saturates — the [[climate-change/observed-change]] lesson's measured feedback strengths are the empirical anchor). Venus is the boundary condition, not the prediction; the confusion is a boundary-condition error, and the 2020s exoplanet-habitable-zone literature's careful inner-edge definitions exist precisely because the Venus-fear version mis-states the physics.
2. **"Mars's ancient rivers mean life, or will mean life."** The 2021–2025 Jezero organics are consistent with abiotic sources (the 2023–2025 meteoritic-and-hydrothermal organic-delivery budgets matching the observed abundances), and habitability is not biology; conversely the 1976 Viking biology non-result is not a life-absence result (the 2010s–2020s perchlorate-radiation-oxidant chemistry explaining the 1976 signals abiotically — the 2023–2025 re-analysis literature). The honest 2025 statement: **Mars was habitable where and when we have sampled; whether it was inhabited requires the sample return or a surface biosignature survey that no current instrument provides** — the position the 2024–2025 Mars-Sample-Return restructuring must defend on exactly these grounds.
3. **"Mercury is a dead, airless rock."** It has a global magnetic field (weak, offset, lumpy, possibly time-variable — the 2022–2025 MESSENGER analyses), a partly molten interior with a sulphur-softened core, a shrinking crust with possibly active scarps, a sodium exosphere in active throughput, and permanently shadowed polar ice (the 1994–2025 radar-neutron-thermal triangulation) — the 2011–2025 MESSENGER result set that rewrote the textbook's first page, and the 2026–2029 BepiColombo programme designed to rewrite the second. "Airless" means 10⁻¹⁵ bar, not zero; "dead" means no plate tectonics, not no activity; the lesson generalises to every body we have orbited only once.
4. **"More missions means more knowledge, linearly."** The 2020s counter-examples are this lesson's: the phosphine episode (one instrument, one line, a retracted abundance — the 2021–2025 audit), the Mars-Sample-Return cost growth that by 2025 had deferred the one measurement that settles the biosignature question (the 2024 National-Academies review's architecture critique), and InSight's heat-probe failure (the 2021–2022 mole non-deployment costing the crustal heat-flow datum the mission was partly built for). The knowledge-per-dollar function has steps, not slopes: sample returns, entry probes and seismometers deliver step changes, single-instrument detections deliver retractions, and the 2025–2035 portfolio decisions (the Decadal-survey priorities, the Artemis-versus-Mars balance) are bets on which steps the field needs next — a resources-and-reserves problem in the [[earth-resources/resources-reserves]] course's precise sense.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Mercury, Venus and Mars are Earth's three replicates, and the comparative dial-reading works: **mass** set the cooling schedule (Mercury's 85-per-cent-radius core limping on a marginal lumpy dynamo; Mars's liquid 3,730-km core dynamo dead by 3.7 Ga; Venus's core convecting or not against the 2019–2025 suppression models), **distance** set the water and carbon partitioning (Venus's 4.8×10²⁰-kg CO₂ atmosphere and 100× terrestrial D/H — a lost ocean's fossil; Mars's 2-km northern ocean now ice, radar and stripped; Mercury's ice only in 40-K traps), **rotation** set the dynamics (Venus's 4-day superrotation on a 243-day planet, Mars's chaotic obliquity writing the sedimentary rhythm), and **a moon or not** set the climate's stability. The surfaces record the same physics at different settings: Caloris's 4.15-Ga floor and 5–7 km of global shrinkage on Mercury; Magellan's 1,000 craters dating a 300–700-Myr resurfacing on Venus, with the 2023 eruption report making it present-tense; Jezero's delta mudstones, phosphates and 1–10-ppm organics recording a datable habitable lake on Mars, cached in tubes for a return that the 2024–2025 programme restructuring must justify. The methodological lessons are the course's: boundary conditions before greenhouse comparisons (Venus is not Earth's future, it is Earth's boundary), habitability is not biology (Jezero's organics are compatible with both), single lines do not make biosignatures (the phosphine audit), and the loss budgets close only when stripping, cold traps and burial are all counted (Mars's water, Venus's ocean). With BepiColombo arriving at Mercury in 2026, VERITAS-EnVision-DAVINCI sailing for Venus in the 2030s, and the Mars sample cache awaiting a return architecture, the three control experiments are entering their instrumented, sampled, adjudicated phase — the comparative method, which this course has built on the Moon, meteorites, impacts and this lesson's three worlds, about to meet its second data revolution.
:::
