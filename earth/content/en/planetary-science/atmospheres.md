An atmosphere is a planet's heat engine, water cycle, and — for a rocky world — its scoreboard: what it accreted, what it outgassed, what it has been losing for four billion years, and what its surface can therefore be. The four other atmospheres of the inner System and the giant planets' moons tell that score in detail: Venus's 92 bar of CO2 (a lost ocean's residue and a runaway's endpoint), Earth's 1 bar of N2 with 0.04 per cent CO2 held low by the silicate thermostat and the biota, Mars's 6 millibar (the stripped remnant of a thicker world, its D/H the fossil of the water that went with the rest), Titan's 1.5 bar of nitrogen with methane rain (the organic photochemistry endmember), and Mercury's surface-boundary-exosphere of sodium and water (the collisional-splash regime at 10⁻¹⁵ bar). The physics is one syllabus — escape (Jeans, hydrodynamic, nonthermal), the greenhouse and its limits (the runaway threshold, the maximum-CO2 outer edge), circulation (Hadley cells, superrotation, tidal locking) — and its exoplanet application has become the field's centre of gravity: the 1999–2025 transmission-spectroscopy programme (from HD 209458b's sodium to JWST's 2023–2025 CO₂, SO₂ and methane detections on sub-Neptunes and temperate worlds), the 2017–2025 TRAPPIST-1 and LHS-1140 observations whose rocky-planet atmospheres remain contested, and the 2020s habitable-zone literature's dependence on every number in this lesson. The data come from four decades of planetary missions — Mariner and Venera's 1962–1982 Venus climatology, Viking's 1976–1982 Mars pressure-and-dust meteorology, Voyager's 1979–1989 outer atmospheres, Cassini-Huygens's 2004–2017 Titan, Mars-Global-Surveyor and MRO's 1996–2025 seasonal CO₂ cycle, Venus-Express's 2006–2014 aeronomy, MAVEN's 2014–2025 escape budget, Akatsuki's 2015–2024 Venus dynamics, and Juno's 2016–2025 deep-atmosphere soundings — plus the laboratory photochemistry programmes (the 1970s–2025 Titan-haze and early-Earth-organic experiments) and the 2010s–2025 3-D GCM revolution that replaced the 1990s 1-D column models for everything except the simplest limits. The prerequisites are the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's outgassing and volatile-delivery budgets and the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's three-world climate record; the payoff is the escape-and-greenhouse arithmetic that the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson spends on other stars' worlds, and the comparative perspective the [[climate-change/observed-change]] course needs: Earth's climate, seen against Venus's 735 K and Mars's 210 K, is the narrow, wet, life-modified middle case — the only one of the five where the scoreboard and the biology have been arguing with each other for 2.4 billion years.

::: definition Where atmospheres come from: accretion, outgassing, delivery {#def-origin}
Three sources, in sequence, and the isotope and noble-gas records say which worlds used which. **Primary accretion** of nebular gas (H-He) requires a core above the ~10-Earth-mass threshold (the 1990s–2025 core-accretion and 2020s direct-imaging demographics — the [[planetary-science/formation]] and [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lessons' gas-giant/Neptune dichotomy), and no inner planet kept one: the 2010s–2025 consensus is that the terrestrial planets' first envelopes were **dissolved and lost** — photoevaporation and impact stripping of H-He envelopes (the 2014–2023 atmospheric-loss demographics of the sub-Neptune radius valley, the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's radius-desert reading), leaving secondary atmospheres. **Secondary outgassing** from magma oceans and volcanism is the inner System's source term: the speciation set by redox state (the 1990s–2025 metal-silicate and magma-ocean partitioning experiments — oxidised mantle gives H₂O-CO₂-N₂-SO₂, reduced gives H₂-CH₄-NH₃-HCN, the 2016–2023 Catling–Johnson–Wade–Mills arguments that mantle oxidation state, set by the giant impact and core formation, selects the atmosphere's chemistry from the start), with the volatile inventories themselves debated between nebular-heterogeneous-accretion delivery (the 2016–2023 Morbidelli-and-Martin dry-inner-Solar-System and outer-System-delivery models) and late-veneer cometary-and-carbonaceous delivery (the 2000s–2025 D/H arguments of the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson — Earth's water at carbonaceous-chondrite D/H within the error bars, Venus's 100-plus-times-enriched D/H showing it lost its own). **Delivery and impact erosion** run simultaneously: the 2005–2025 impact-delivery integrations (the late-veneer mass and isotope budget) against the 1990s–2020s impact-erosion models (the Zahnle–Sleep–Genda–Schouwenberg lineage: giant impacts strip atmospheres above a mass threshold, and the Moon-forming and basin-forming erasures explain why Mars and Venus and Earth each start their secondary atmospheres from a different baseline). The **noble-gas ledger** is the audit: Venus's and Earth's heavy-noble-gas abundances (the 1990s–2025 mass-spectrometry of Venera and Pioneer inlets and terrestrial air — both enriched in Xe and Kr relative to solar, fractionated identically, the 2013–2025 hydrodynamic-escape-and-early-XUV arguments that the shared signature means both lost H₂O-dominated atmospheres to a young-Sun XUV environment), Mars's distinct xenon (the 2015–2025 SAM measurements of martian Xe isotopes — a different loss history), and the giant planets' noble-gas enrichments (the 1995–2025 Galileo and Juno entry-probe Kr, Xe and Ar results — 2–10× solar, the accretion-temperature and planetesimal-delivery fingerprints of the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson). The 2025 synthesis sentence: **secondary atmospheres are the inner System's norm, their composition is a redox-and-escape record readable today, and the isotope fossils (D/H, noble-gas fractionations) are the only direct measurements we have of processes running 4 billion years ago** — the reason the 2026–2035 entry probes (DAVINCI's D/H, the 2020s Mars sample-and-return isotope programmes, Europa-Clipper's plume sampling) are designed as mass spectrometers first.
:::

::: definition Escape: how atmospheres leave, and what the isotopes prove {#def-escape}
Atmospheric loss is a competition between gravity's well and five escape channels, and the isotope fossils show which worlds lost how much. **Jeans escape** — the thermal tail leaking species whose Jeans parameter λ = GMm/(kTR) falls below ~15–20 (the exercises' arithmetic) — controls H on Mars today and everything at Mercury, and is negligible for heavy species. **Hydrodynamic escape** — the XUV-heated bulk outflow of a hydrogen-rich atmosphere, dragging heavy species by collisions (the 1990s–2025 energy-limited and diffusion-limited escape models, the 2003–2025 "boiling" of hot Jupiters and sub-Neptunes observed in transit by Hubble's Lyman-alpha and JWST's metastable-helium programmes) — is the young-Sun mechanism that fractionated Venus's and Earth's noble gases and, at the **moist-greenhouse and runaway limits**, set how much water a planet can lose before the cold trap at the tropopause (the 2013–2023 Komabayashi–Ingersoll and Koppari–Kronberg re-derivations: the OLR limit of ~280–300 W/m² capping the steam atmosphere's water loss at tens to hundreds of Earth-oceans for a Venus-insolation world, the 2019–2025 magma-ocean-coupled runs extending it to the first hundred million years) — the number that decides whether early Venus boiled or sat wet (the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's 2018-versus-2023 argument). **Nonthermal escape** — sputtering by the solar wind, pickup of ionised species, polar wind and dissociative recombination — is measured directly at Mars by MAVEN (the 2015–2025 ion-and-neutral-loss rates, the solar-storm spike correlations, the 2023–2025 synthesis: present loss ~1–2 kg/s, early loss 10–100×, totalling tens to hundreds of millibars over 3.5 Gyr — a climate-relevant but not atmosphere-making fraction, the 2024–2025 debate over whether carbonate burial holds the rest of Mars's missing carbon). **Photochemical escape** (dissociative recombination and ion drag on Mars and Titan, the 2010s–2025 Titan N-loss rates — the nitrogen atmosphere being billions of years old only because it is being replenished or was emplaced late, the 2020s Titan-age debates) and **impact erosion** (the 1990s–2020s basin-scale stripping models) close the budget. The **isotope fossils** are the proof of loss: D/H at Venus is 100-plus times SMOW (a hundred-ocean water loss if the starting ratio was terrestrial, the 2017–2025 Venus-Express and ground-based re-estimates of the present loss rate finding today's escape far too slow to have done it — the loss was early and mostly hydrodynamic), Mars's 5–8 times SMOW (the 2015 Curiosity-SAM and 2020s meteorite-and-orbiter values, the 2023–2025 time-variable-and-reservoir-corrected readings — an ocean-scale loss consistent with MAVEN), Earth's 1.6 (a small loss, consistent with the cold trap and the magnetic field's modest shielding — the 2010s–2025 arguments that the field protects the atmosphere only marginally, the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] habitability-field correlation weakening accordingly), and Titan's and comets' values calibrating the exchange. The comparative verdict: **escape is why the inner System is dry-ish and why Mars is cold — the same XUV and solar-wind environment that stripped Mars's millibars, fractionated Venus's ocean and Earth's xenon, and left Mercury with an exosphere, is the young-star environment every rocky exoplanet must survive**, the 2024–2025 JWST-and-ALMA M-dwarf XUV surveys' central habitability datum.
:::

::: definition Greenhouse limits and the climate states of rocky worlds {#def-greenhouse}
Rocky-planet climate has a small set of admissible states, and the radiative limits that bound them are the exoplanet habitable zone's physical content. The **runaway-greenhouse inner edge** is not "more CO2" but a water-vapour limit: outgoing longwave radiation saturates near 280–300 W/m² as the troposphere fills with water vapour (the 1981–2025 Komabayashi–Ingersoll-through-Koppari re-derivations, the 2013–2023 cloud-resolving and line-by-line updates moving the threshold by tens of W/m²), so a planet absorbing more than that limit equilibrates only by losing its water; the corresponding stellar flux places the classical edge near 0.95–1.0 AU for the modern Sun (the 1993–2024 Kasting–Koppari–Wordske habitable-zone edges, the recent-Venus controversy of the 2010s–2020s over whether 0.72 AU was ever below it — the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's 2018 Way model versus its 2019–2023 rebuttals). The **maximum-greenhouse outer edge** is set by CO2 itself: at high CO2 the gas condenses (Mars and beyond), clouds and hazes raise albedo, and the warming per doubling declines (the 2011–2023 Koll–Abbot–Wordske–Yang CO2-cloud and CO2-collapse sequence), placing the classical edge near 1.67–1.77 AU for the modern Sun — which is why the 2020s faint-Mars problem is a climate-model problem: 1 bar of CO2 on Mars condenses and collapses (the 2013–2025 3-D GCMs' failure to keep 1 bar warm, the 2020s hybrid solutions — CO2 ice clouds' warming, obliquity-driven ice-sheet and ground-ice outgassing, N2 pressure broadening, volcanic H₂-SO₂ hazes — each partial, none published as solved). The **faint young Sun** is the same physics in reverse: 70–75 per cent solar flux at 4–2.5 Ga (the 1990s–2025 standard solar model, the 2020s Gaia-and-asteroseismology recalibrations of solar evolution) against geological liquid-water evidence from 3.8 Ga onward (the [[palaeoclimate/deep-time]] lesson's zircon-and-stromatolite record) requires several bar of CO2 (the 2020s palaeoclimate-and-isotope-systematics revisions — the 2020–2025 Zambon–Turbault–Bouvier high-CO2-with-wet-landings synthesis and its 2024–2025 critics) plus methane-and-haze greenhouse bonuses (the 1972–2025 Sagan–Mullen–Pavlov–Haqq-Misra lineage, the 2023–2025 organic-haze radiative-transfer updates) — the resolution being partial and live, the strongest constraint being that **no single-greenhouse solution is published as complete**, the same honesty the 2024–2025 early-Earth-and-exoplanet papers now carry. The **state space** beyond the two edges: Snowball states with their ice-albedo hysteresis (the [[climate-change/tipping-points]] lesson's deep-time and exoplanet versions), hazy anoxic states with organic hazes cooling or warming (the 2010s–2025 early-Earth-and-Titan-and-Trappist haze models), anti-greenhouse states (Titan's 12-K methane-haze cooling — the definition's counterexample to "greenhouse always warms"), and desiccated CO2 states (Venus, and the 2023–2025 Venus-analog exoplanet demographics). The **carbonate-silicate thermostat** (the geochemical weathering feedback of the [[geochemistry/weathering-chemistry]] lesson, its exoplanet-climate formalisation of the 2020s — the 2021–2025 climate-stability GCMs finding it stabilises but does not rescue the inner edge) is the negative feedback that makes the habitable zone a dynamical basin rather than a radiative line, and the 2024–2025 "climate states of rocky planets" syntheses' closing sentence: **five or six admissible climates, bounded by radiative limits, selected by mass, distance, water and time — and the job of the 2026–2035 JWST-and-ELT era is to find out which ones other worlds actually occupy.**
:::

::: definition Circulation: Hadley cells, superrotation, and tidally-locked worlds {#def-circulation}
Atmospheric dynamics is heat's redistribution, and the comparative set spans every regime the theory admits. **Thermally-direct circulation** — the Hadley cell carrying energy from the summer or equatorial surplus — is measured on four worlds: Earth's (the 1950s–2025 reanalysis climatology, the [[climate-change/observed-change]] lesson's Hadley-expansion record), Mars's cross-equatorial cell (the 2000s–2025 MRO-and-Mars-Express dust-and-water-vapour tracers), Titan's seasonal reversal (the 2005–2024 Cassini stratospheric-temperature and cloud-hemi-sphericity record — the 2013–2025 northern-spring cloud-hemisphere-flip confirmation of seasonally-driven circulation), and Venus's poorly-constrained candidate cell (the 2010s–2023 Akatsuki and Venus-Express temperature soundings). **Superrotation** — zonal winds faster than the planet's rotation, Venus's 100-m/s equatorial jets against a 2-m/s surface rotation — is the dynamics field's central comparative problem: the momentum budget (the 1970s–2025 thermal-tidal, mean-flow-interaction and wave-transience theories) had its 2023 datum in Akatsuki's imaged equatorial Rossby wave (the 2023 Nature and Nature Astronomy papers showing the wave carries a substantial share of the momentum flux), and the same physics organises the **hot-Jupiter** phenomenon — the 1999–2025 observed dayside-hot-spots and pressure-system offsets (the Showalter–Knudsen–Louden–Zhang phase-curve and cloud-tracking synthesis) whose general-circulation models (the 2007–2025 Komacevic–Showman–Parmentier linear theory: superrotation when radiative timescales are short relative to planetary waves) are the template the 2020s sub-Neptune and temperate-world models reuse. **Tidally-locked circulation** is the habitable-zone worlds' regime: the 2010s–2025 GCM syntheses (the Merlis–Wordske–Koll–Haqq-Misra lineage) find a dayside-to-nightside heat transport set by the rotation rate and water vapour's radiative control, with the 2020s result that **rapidly-rotating locked worlds keep narrow equatorial jets and modest temperature contrasts while slow rotators freeze over** — the 2024–2025 TRAPPIST-1 and LHS-1140 observation-targeting literature's physical basis, and the reason JWST's 2023–2025 secondary-eclipse and phase-curve programme (the 2024–2025 WASP-39b, LHS-1140b and K2-18b papers) reports heat-redistribution efficiencies as the first-order atmospheric datum before any biosignature. **Seasonal and transient dynamics** close the comparative record: Mars's annual CO2 polar-cap pump (the 1997–2025 Mars-Global-Surveyor pressure-seasonal record — 25 per cent of the atmosphere freezing and subliming each year, the exercises' arithmetic, the 2020s water-vapour-and-dust transport coupling), its global dust storms' radiative forcing (the 1971–2018 record, the 2018 storm's 2019–2025 diagnosed middle-atmosphere warming and surface cooling and the 2023–2025 dust-gas-cycle feedbacks), Titan's and Pluto's volatile transport (the 2010s–2025 New-Horizons-and-Cassini nitrogen-ice and haze seasonal models), and Jupiter's and Saturn's banded jets and the Great Red Spot's decades-long weakening (the 2016–2025 Juno deep-wind and Hubble-and-Amateur-Science-Association monitoring — the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's fluid-dynamics case). The unifying frame is the 2010s–2025 dimensional analysis (the Rhines and Rossby-deformation scalings, the radiative-friction and wave-mean-flow hierarchies): **four parameters — rotation rate, radiative timescale, insolation contrast, and the atmosphere's mass — and the Solar System's five atmospheres plus the exoplanets' fill its corners**, the comparative method again doing the work no single world's science could.
:::

::: example Titan: the atmosphere that is a laboratory {#ex-titan}
Titan is the comparative atmosphere that tests every branch of this lesson at once: a 1.5-bar nitrogen atmosphere (the 1944 Kuiper discovery, the 1980–1981 Voyager-1 confirmation and mass-spectrometry) on a 0.11-Earth-mass body at 9.5 AU, with methane at 1.5–5 per cent doing the water's hydrological work, a 150-K surface, and a 100-mbar organic haze that makes it the anti-greenhouse counterexample (the 1980s–2025 radiative-transfer readings of the haze's surface-cooling and stratosphere-warming split). The Cassini-Huygens 2004–2017 campaign (the 2005–2017 ISS, VIMS, CIRS and INMS results, the 2006 radar lakes and the 2008 Huygens surface landing's drainage-channel images, the 2013–2017 northern-lake-season filling and drainage records) turned the photochemistry laboratory into a surface-atmosphere cycle: methane rain, ethane-and-methane seas (the Ontario and Lacus Ligeia depth soundings — the 2014–2025 radar-and-infrared bathymetry), river networks and fluvial valleys whose erosion rates (the 2010s–2025 landscape-evolution models) are Earth-like in form and millennial in rate, and a seasonal circulation whose hemispheric temperature flip (the 2005–2024 CIRS stratospheric record, the 2013–2025 cloud-hemisphere confirmations) is the textbook Hadley-reversal case. The open problems are this lesson's physics in organic dress: the **methane lifetime** (photochemical destruction in 10–100 Myr against a billions-of-years-old atmosphere — the 2010s–2025 replenishment debate: interior outgassing through cryovolcanic vents, clathrate reservoirs, or a recent large release, the 2023–2025 Juno-and-Alma deep-probing methane-profile results narrowing the source geometry), the **nitrogen age and origin** (the 2010s–2025 Cassini-INMS isotope work — the ¹⁴N/¹⁵N ratio showing photochemical escape has enriched the atmosphere, so the original N inventory was larger or younger, the 2024–2025 isotopic-fractionation-and-delivery models), and the **superrotation of a slow rotator** (Titan's 12-day prograde jet at 0.4-m/s surface winds — the 2010s–2025 GCMs' seasonal-wave-and-tidal solutions). The 2028 Dragonfly launch (the 2019-selected rotorcraft lander, the 2024–2025 cruise-and-instrument status) will sample the surface organics and the methane cycle in situ — the lesson's point being that Titan is not an oddity but the **organic-photochemistry endmember of the rocky-atmosphere family**, the state that JWST's 2024–2025 sub-Neptune-and-Earth-analog haze searches are designed to detect on other worlds, and that every prebiotic-chemistry argument about early Earth has to price against Titan's 4-billion-year solvent-and-haze experiment.
:::

::: example Mars's pressure cycle and the missing carbon {#ex-mars-carbon}
Mars's atmosphere is the escape-and-sink budget made directly visible: 6 mbar of CO₂ whose **25 per cent freezes onto the winter pole every year** (the 1997–2025 Mars-Global-Surveyor-and-MRO pressure record — the annual 0.6-to-0.45-mbar sawtooth, the exercises' condensing-fraction arithmetic, the polar-cap radar and gravimetry confirming the seasonal mass exchange), with water vapour and dust following the pressure and the obliquity (the 2000s–2025 SPICAM-and-MCS humidity-and-cloud records, the 2020s mid-latitude ground-ice and obliquity-climate coupling). The budget question — where did the rest of Mars's carbon and water go — has three ledgers, and the 2025 accounting is instructive precisely because it does not close. **Escape** (the MAVEN 2014–2025 rates: 1–2 kg/s today, 10–100× in the first 500–700 Myr, totalling tens to hundreds of millibars of CO₂-equivalent and an ocean-scale water loss consistent with the 5–8× SMOW D/H) is measured but insufficient alone. **Burial** (the 2000s–2025 orbiter carbonate searches — the 2023–2025 global carbonate inventory of a few per cent of a bar-equivalent, far short of the several-bar fossil atmosphere the valley networks and 2021–2024 Jezero lacustrine carbonates imply) is too small in the mapped record, leaving the 2020s options: **deep burial** of early carbonates and clathrates not yet sampled (the 2022–2025 isotope-and-weathering mass balances — the 2023 Nature Geoscience sulphide-and-carbonate weathering-palaeoclimate lineages arguing most early CO₂ was removed by wet-weathering to buried carbon, the 2024–2025 critics noting the required burial area exceeds the mapped record), **loss to space** at the high end of MAVEN's early rates, or **migration to the regolith and polar subsurface** (the 2020s buried-glacier and ground-ice radar inventories). The 2025 consensus statement is a budget with an explicit missing term: **Mars lost an ocean and most of a several-bar atmosphere; escape and the mapped carbonates together leave one to several bars unaccounted, and the candidates are deep burial, higher early escape, or both** — the reason the 2020s sample-return and 2026–2035 ExoMars-rover isotope programmes carry carbon-and-oxygen-isotope mass spectrometers as first-order payloads, and the reason the Mars atmosphere is the comparative-planetology case study in how an atmosphere dies: not one mechanism, but a measured budget that still, at four billion years' remove, has a line item in dispute.
:::

::: example Early Venus: the habitability question that became a climate-physics question {#ex-early-venus}
Whether Venus ever had an ocean is the cleanest existing test of habitable-zone theory, because we can sample the endpoint. The 2018 Way–Del Genio GCM result — a slowly-rotating Venus with dayside water clouds raising albedo to 0.8, permitting a 2-billion-year liquid ocean until the 1-Gyr solar brightening crossed the runaway threshold — was the first modern model to keep early Venus wet, and the 2019–2025 rebuttal sequence is the lesson in how climate physics is audited. **The cloud argument:** the 2019–2023 grey-cloud and then cloud-resolving replays (the Way-model's parameterised clouds versus the 2020–2023 Hamano, Turbault and Yang–Koll group's explicit convective and grid-scale cloud treatments) finding the bright dayside clouds either fail to form or fail to persist once convection is resolved — the albedo-stabilisation mechanism the 2018 model needed most being its weakest component, the 2023–2025 syntheses' verdict that **no published model now keeps fully-molten-then-wet Venus stable against the standard young-Sun flux**. **The escape argument:** the 2019–2025 magma-ocean-coupled steam-atmosphere runs (the Hamano–Kurokawa–Turbault lineage) showing the post-magma-steam atmosphere loses tens of Earth-oceans of hydrogen in 10–100 Myr at Venus's orbit — leaving a dry planet and a 100-plus-times-enriched D/H as the natural outcome, with the 2024–2025 deuterium-retention refinements (the diffusion-limited regime's fractionation cap) reconciling the endpoint with the Venera-and-ground-based D/H. **The surface argument:** the 2023–2025 highland-weathering constraint — the Venera-9-and-10 and Vega rock analyses' mineral assemblages (the 2023–2025 Turbault and O'Rourke readings) showing the ~2-km-high tessera-and-plateau rocks un-hydrated, which liquid water at the surface for geological time would have altered — the strongest direct evidence that the highlands have been dry for the last billion years at least. The 2025 position: **early Venus was hot, steam-dominated, probably briefly habitable at best, and dry by ~1 Gyr after accretion; its D/H, its un-weathered highlands and its 92-bar CO₂ are one consistent record of that**, and the 2030s DAVINCI entry probe's D/H measurement (the 2029–2030 descent, the exercise's Rayleigh arithmetic converting its number into an ocean-loss budget) is the single scheduled datum that could still overturn the consensus — the way habitable-zone theory is supposed to work: a boundary condition, a candidate world, and a mass spectrometer pointed at the isotope ratio.
:::

::: example Exoplanet atmospheres: from sodium to carbon dioxide in twenty-five years {#ex-exo-atmospheres}
The comparative method's graduation is the 1999–2025 measurement of other stars' atmospheres, and the arc is this lesson's physics applied at face value. **Transmission spectroscopy** began with sodium (the 1999 HD-209458b detection, the 2000s–2010s Hubble-and-ground-based hot-Jupiter atoms — Na, K, H-alpha), passed through water vapour (the 2007–2013 Spitzer-and-Hubble emission-and-transit water bands) and clouds (the 2010s–2025 flat-spectrum "cloudy, featureless" sub-Neptunes whose 2020s JWST spectra — the 2023–2025 GJ-1214b and K2-18b analyses — replaced the 2010s metallicity-degeneracy debates with real molecules), and reached **carbon chemistry on small worlds** in the 2022–2025 JWST programme: CO₂ on the hot Jupiter WASP-39b with the SO₂ photochemistry detection (the 2022–2023 NIRSpec-and-NIRISS transmission papers — photochemistry observed on another star's planet for the first time), CO₂-and-methane on the sub-Neptune K2-18b (the 2023 detection papers, the 2024–2025 reanalyses revising the CH₄-and-CO₂ mixing ratios downward and the 2023 dimethyl-sulphide-and-hycean-ocean claim retracted to "unmeasured" by the 2024–2025 retrieval-pipeline audits — the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's biosignature-hierarchy caution written in real time). **Emission and phase curves** measure the circulation definition predicted: the 2006–2025 Spitzer-and-JWST dayside temperatures and hot-spot offsets (the 2023–2025 JWST-MIRI phase curves of the ultra-hot Jupiters with their iron-and-oxide condensation loops — the exoplanet Hadley-and-superrotation reading), the 2024–2025 rocky-world secondary eclipses (the 2024 LHS-1140b JWST eclipse result — no thick atmosphere, the 2025 TRAPPIST-1-b-and-c phase-curve constraints — thin or none), the first magma-world volatile hints (the 2024–2025 55-Cancri-e and 40-Eridani-b SO₂-and-CO₂ candidate spectra, the 2025 instrument-systematics debates) — the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's three climate endpoints, now with a fourth data class: **lava worlds boiling their own crusts**. The 2025 scoreboard: dozens of atmospheres characterised, H-He worlds routine, CO₂ and photochemistry measured, rocky-world atmospheres bounded but undetected, biosignatures undetected and the 2020s retrieval-and-observation-bias audits (the 2023–2025 pipeline-comparison exercises) keeping the field's claims at the level of its systematics — the same discipline, the same five observables, the same isotope-and-molecule ledgers, now pointed at the 2030s Habitable-Worlds-Observatory targets, with the Solar System's five atmospheres as the only calibration set the theory has.
:::

::: history From the barometer to the biosignature pipeline {#hist-atmospheres}
**1643–1900:** Torricelli's 1643 barometer and Pascal's 1648 Puy-de-Dôme fall establish the atmosphere's weight; Kirchhoff and Bunsen's 1859 spectroscopy and Tyndall's 1861 water-vapour-and-CO₂ radiation experiments supply the greenhouse's physics; Arrhenius's 1896 and the 1900s–1930s Callendar calculations quantify it for Earth while Wollaston's 1802 and the 1890s–1920s spectra failed to find Venus's atmosphere as expected. **1908–1975:** the planetary opening — Campbell's and Slipher's 1908–1920s Mars-and-Venus spectroscopy (the 1919–1925 Slipher CO₂ detections), Jeans's 1916–1928 escape theory, Simpson's and Rossby's 1919–1940s general-circulation concept, Kuiper's 1944 Titan methane and 1947–1952 Venus CO₂-and-pressure inferences, the 1956–1961 radio-and-model hot Venus, the 1962–1967 Mariner-2-and-Venera confirmations, Ingersoll's and Kasting's 1968–1993 runaway-and-habitable-zone lineage, and the 1976 Viking and 1979–1989 Voyager meteorology of Mars and the outer atmospheres. **1979–2013:** the comparative synthesis — the 1979–1993 Kasting–Walker–Kinnunen climate-and-carbon-cycle models, the 1995–2010 high-pressure-and-photochemistry laboratory expansion, the 1997–2013 habitable-zone and faint-young-Sun literature (the 1997 Kasting edge re-derivations, the 2000s–2010s haze-and-cloud solutions), the 2004–2013 Cassini-Huygens Titan revolution, and the 1999–2013 exoplanet-atmosphere opening (the 1999 sodium, the 2007 water). **2014–2025:** the escape-and-spectra era — MAVEN's 2014-onward martian loss budget, Venus-Express's 2014 close and the 2016–2025 Proxima-and-TRAPPIST flare-and-XUV problem, the 2015–2025 Akatsuki dynamics and 2023 Rossby-wave results, the 2018 Way early-wet-Venus paper and its 2019–2025 rebuttals, the 2022–2025 JWST molecular atmospheres (the 2022 WASP-39b CO₂-and-SO₂, the 2023 K2-18b carbon chemistry and its 2024–2025 audits, the 2024 LHS-1140b eclipse), and the 2025–2035 DAVINCI, VERITAS, EnVision, Dragonfly and Habitable-Worlds-Observatory schedules — the comparative atmosphere science that began with a mercury column in 1643 now scheduling spectrometers at other stars' temperate worlds.
:::

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-dh}
Why is the deuterium-to-hydrogen ratio a planet's water-loss meter?
- [ ] Deuterium is produced by photochemistry in the upper atmosphere
- [x] Hydrogen escapes preferentially over deuterium, so the residual water and atmosphere are enriched in deuterium by a factor that records the total loss
- [ ] Deuterium condenses into the oceans while hydrogen escapes
- [ ] The ratio changes with climate state
- [ ] Volcanism emits deuterium-rich water

::: solution :::
Fractionation is mass-dependent: escape channels (Jeans, hydrodynamic, nonthermal) remove light H faster than D, so the reservoir left behind grows heavy in proportion to the fraction lost — the Rayleigh-distillation arithmetic of the exercises. With a known or estimable starting ratio (the carbonaceous-chondrite and cometary values of the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson), Venus's 100-plus-times-SMOW, Mars's 5–8 and Earth's 1.6 convert directly into ocean-scale loss budgets: the only direct measurement any planet has of its own four-billion-year escape history.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-mars-warm}
The faint young Mars problem is hard because:
- [ ] The valley networks are younger than the Noachian
- [x] Standard 3-D climate models cannot keep even ~1 bar of CO2 warm enough on Mars, because CO2 clouds cool, CO2 condenses and collapses, and the required pressures exceed the geological carbon budget
- [ ] The young Sun was brighter at Mars's orbit
- [ ] Mars's obliquity was always low
- [ ] Volcanism never emitted CO2

::: solution :::
The three failure modes are all in the definition's maximum-greenhouse physics: CO₂ ice clouds net-cool in most 2013–2025 GCMs, pure-CO₂ atmospheres above ~1–2 bar condense at Mars's temperatures, and the 2023–2025 carbonate and isotope budgets cap the available CO₂ at a few bar. The 2020s hybrid solutions (CO₂-ice-cloud warming in some microphysics, obliquity outgassing, N₂ broadening, H₂-SO₂ hazes) each warm by 10–20 K, none published as solved — the honest state of one of the oldest problems in planetary climate, and the reason the lesson teaches limits, not recipes.
:::
:::

## Practice Problems

::: exercise {level=1 check="11.2"}
**Earth's well.** With GM = 3.986×10¹⁴ m³/s² and R = 6,371 km, what is Earth's escape velocity in km/s?
::: solution :::
v = √(2GM/R) = √(1.251×10⁸) = **11.2 km/s** — the well depth behind the definition's whole retention ledger: with the 1/6-thermal-speed rule, Earth keeps everything except H and He (whose exospheric thermal speeds reach a third of this, the 2010s–2025 arguments that Earth's H budget is nonetheless escape-limited at the diffusion ceiling), and the comparison across the family — Mercury 4.2, Moon 2.4, Mars 5.0, Venus 10.4 — is the first column of the atmospheres' distribution, the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's mass dial written in velocity.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="4.2"}
**Mercury's well.** With GM = 2.2×10¹³ m³/s² and R = 2,440 km, what is Mercury's escape velocity?
::: solution :::
v = √(2×2.2×10¹³/2.44×10⁶) = √(1.80×10⁷) ≈ **4.2 km/s** — the shallowest rocky-planet well, leaving sodium at 1,000-K dayside temperatures only marginally bound (the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's λ ≈ 25 computation), which is why Mercury has a collisionless surface-bounded exosphere in continuous source-and-loss throughput rather than an atmosphere, and why the 2020s lava-world exoplanets (the 2024–2025 55-Cancri-e and 40-Eridani-b candidate outgassing spectra) are modelled with the same source-rate arithmetic: at these well depths, an atmosphere is a flow, not a reservoir.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="1.6"}
**Thermal speeds.** What is the most probable thermal speed of H₂ (m = 3.3×10⁻²⁷ kg) at 288 K (v = √(2kT/m))?
::: solution :::
v = √(2×1.38×10⁻²³×288/3.3×10⁻²⁷) = √(2.4×10⁶) ≈ **1.6 km/s** — 14 per cent of Earth's escape speed, so bulk H₂ is retained thermally yet lost at the diffusion-limited rate (the 2000s–2025 hydrogen-budget literature: Earth's H₂ is destroyed by soil uptake and photochemistry before it can escape, the escape flux at the diffusion ceiling), and the same formula at 1,000-K exospheric temperature gives 3.0 km/s — the 27-per-cent figure that, above the 1/6 rule's threshold, keeps Earth's upper atmosphere slowly leaking H and keeping its D/H at a modest 1.6 times SMOW against Venus's 100.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="1.5"}
**Hydrogen on Mars.** Compute the Jeans parameter λ = GMm/(kTR) for atomic H at the martian exobase (T = 1,000 K, GM = 4.28×10¹³, R = 3,390 km, m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg).
::: solution :::
λ = 7.1×10⁻¹⁴/4.7×10⁻¹⁴ = **1.5** — catastrophically unbound (λ below ~15 means the thermal tail escapes freely), which is why Mars's D/H of 5–8 built up so readily: every water molecule photolysed near the exobase loses its H immediately, the escape running at the diffusion-limited ceiling through the 2010s–2025 MAVEN-and-SAM synthesis, and the exercise's general form — λ as the master number of comparative atmospheres, deciding at one division whether a world keeps each species it makes.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="21"}
**Titan's scale height.** For N₂ (4.65×10⁻²⁶ kg) at 94 K with g = 1.35 m/s², what is the scale height?
::: solution :::
H = 1.38×10⁻²³×94/(4.65×10⁻²⁶×1.35) ≈ **21 km** — comparable to Earth's despite 1/8 the gravity's compensation by 1/3 the temperature, the reason Titan's 1.5-bar atmosphere extends to 1,000 km with a 600-km methane-and-nitrile column (the 2004–2017 Cassini-INMS density profiles), its 1,000-km haze making the anti-greenhouse split (the definition's 150-K surface under a sunlit organic blanket), and its deep atmosphere on a small cold world the 2020s sub-Neptune-and-Earth-analog haze models' template — cold, deep and hazy being a climate state, not an oddity.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="0.11"}
**Rayleigh loss.** Mars's water D/H is 6× the assumed starting value. With a fractionation factor α = 0.2 (R/R₀ = f^(α−1)), what fraction f of the original hydrogen reservoir remains?
::: solution :::
6 = f^(−0.8) → f = 6^(−1/0.8) = 6^(−1.25) ≈ **0.11** — about one-ninth of the original water left, i.e. an ocean loss of ~90 per cent of a global layer, the Rayleigh arithmetic (the 2015–2025 SAM-and-MAVEN budget readings) that converts isotope ratios into volumes, and its two honest caveats: the starting D/H (carbonaceous-chondrite versus cometary mix, the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's open ratio) scales the answer linearly, and reservoir mixing (crustal hydration, polar exchange — the 2023–2025 regolith-and-groundwater corrections) can hide a factor of two — the isotope fossil being precise about loss and vague about inventory, like most deep-time measurements.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="0.92"}
**The inner edge.** With the runaway OLR limit at 282 W/m² and Earth's albedo 0.3, what orbital distance receives exactly that absorbed flux (S at 1 AU = 1,361 W/m²)?
::: solution :::
S = 4×282/0.7 = 1,611 W/m²; d = √(1,361/1,611) = **0.92 AU** — the classical recent-Venus edge in one line (the 1993–2024 Kasting-through-Wordske edge sequence, the 2010s–2020s cloud-and-rotation revisions pushing the moist edge inward to ~0.75–0.85 AU and the 2023–2025 magma-ocean runs arguing Venus itself may have stayed above the limit throughout), and the exercise's habitable-zone point: the edge's position is a radiative limit plus an albedo assumption, which is why the exoplanet-habitable-zone plots (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's optimistic-and-conservative bands) differ by tens of per cent in flux on cloud physics alone — the 2030s JWST-and-ELT recent-Venus-analog observations being the first direct test of where the line actually is.

T = 278×0.75^(1/4) = 278×0.93 = **259 K** — 15 K below the present value and, with the 33-K greenhouse unchanged, a −14-K surface: a frozen planet (the 1972–2025 faint-young-Sun paradox in one division) against the 3.8-Ga liquid-water geology of the [[palaeoclimate/deep-time]] lesson. ::: warning Four atmosphere fallacies
1. **"The magnetic field protects the atmosphere, so Mars lost its air because the dynamo died."** The 2014–2025 MAVEN-and-modelling verdict is more complicated: the field channels and focuses escape rather than switching it off (the 2017–2025 crustal-magnetic-field studies finding localized loss enhancements, not global shielding), Venus has no field and kept 92 bars, and the loss-budget arithmetic (the exercises' 15-atmospheres-at-100× versus the several-bar inventory) says stripping is a climate-relevant term, not the executioner — the drying was primarily the cooling-and-cold-trap-and-burial system of the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson. The 2020s exoplanet habitability-field correlation is being revised downward for the same reasons, and "no field, no air" is now the fallacy version of a live, quantitative, unsettled question.
2. **"The runaway greenhouse limit means Earth will boil."** The limit is an absorbed-flux threshold (the exercises' 282 W/m², 0.92 AU for Earth's albedo), crossed by solar brightening in ~1–3 Gyr on standard evolution (the 2013–2024 Koppari–Wordske–Goldblatt recent-Venus-edge sequence), not by anthropogenic CO₂, which cannot deliver the water-vapour-dominated atmosphere the limit requires (the [[climate-change/observed-change]] lesson's measured feedback saturations, the 2010s–2020s moist-runaway-threshold studies placing the trigger at several-times-present CO₂-plus-extreme-surface-warming combinations no emissions pathway reaches). Venus is Earth's boundary condition in 2 billion years, not this century — conflating the two is the most common misuse of comparative climatology, and it discredits the real warning.
3. **"Venus's CO₂ proves the greenhouse effect is strong, so 400 ppm should be hot."** The logarithmic forcing of CO₂ (the [[climate-change/observed-change]] lesson's 5.35 ln(C/C₀) and its modern successors) means the 1940s–1960s Venus-to-Earth intuition inverts: 920,000 ppm of Venus's atmosphere is the saturated-wing endpoint of the same physics that makes Earth's 420 ppm a 2-K problem, and the 2020s popular "Venus was caused by CO₂" argument commits the linear-extrapolation error the band-structure of radiative transfer forbids. The correct comparative statement: Venus shows what water's absence and 900 centuries of volcanic CO₂ do at 0.72 AU — a boundary-condition lesson, not a sensitivity calibration.
4. **"A detected molecule is a detected biosignature."** The 2023–2025 JWST era's audits are the syllabus: K2-18b's 2023 CH₄-and-CO₂ detections survived while the same campaign's dimethyl-sulphide claim did not (the 2024–2025 retrieval-pipeline and line-list reanalyses), SO₂ on WASP-39b is abiotic photochemistry, and CO₂ on a temperate world is geology, not life; the 2020s biosignature-hierarchy consensus (multiple molecules, context gases, redox imbalance, independent instruments, and the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's phosphine rule) exists because every Solar-System analogue — Titan's organics, Mars's methane controversies (the 2003–2025 Mars-Express-and-Curiosity methane detections, non-detections and 2024–2025 re-retractions), Venus's phosphine — has produced a molecule that wanted to be biology and was not. The 2030s Habitable-Worlds-Observatory claim that ends this debate will be the one that survives the audit the 2020s taught the field to run.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Comparative atmospheric science reads five atmospheres with one physics: **origins** (secondary outgassing after nebular-envelope loss, redox-selected speciation, delivery-and-erosion balance, the noble-gas ledger auditing it), **escape** (Jeans, hydrodynamic, nonthermal, photochemical and impact channels, with D/H — Venus's 100×, Mars's 6×, Earth's 1.6× — the measured fossil of four billion years of hydrogen loss), **the greenhouse and its limits** (the ~282-W/m² runaway threshold at 0.92 AU, the CO₂-condensation outer edge, the faint-young-Sun paradox still unresolved, the carbonate-silicate thermostat making the habitable zone a basin), and **circulation** (Hadley cells on four worlds, superrotation on two and on hot Jupiters, tidally-locked heat redistribution as the first datum JWST measures). Mars's 6-mbar remnant and its un-closed carbon ledger, Titan's 1.5-bar organic anti-greenhouse, Venus's steam-atmosphere endgame and dry highlands, and Mercury's throughput exosphere are the calibration set; the 1999–2025 exoplanet-spectroscopy programme — sodium to SO₂ to contested temperate-world bounds — is the method's graduation. The fallacies the lesson leaves behind: fields that shield atmospheres like walls, Venus as this century's forecast, linear CO₂ intuition, and molecules that want to be biology. With DAVINCI descending for D/H in 2029–2030, Dragonfly flying in 2034, JWST's rocky-world programme in its first decade, and the Habitable-Worlds-Observatory mission concept maturing for the 2040s, the question the five atmospheres pose — how a rocky world keeps, loses or transforms its air, and what that says about its surface — is scheduled, instrumented and funded to be answered with other stars' worlds as replicates, which is more than any other comparative planetary science can say about its future.
:::
