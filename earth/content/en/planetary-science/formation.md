The Earth did not come with an instruction manual, but the Sun's family came close: every solid body in the Solar System formed from one collapsing cloud, in one place, on one timescale, and their compositions, orbits and collision scars preserve the record of the first hundred million years better than any terrestrial rock — which is why this lesson, and the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson that follows it, are really a documentary history written in isotopes, orbits and craters. The nebular hypothesis is the oldest surviving model in planetary science — Kant's 1755 inversion of the argument from design, Laplace's 1796 contracting-ring version, and the 1960s–1970s reconstruction by the Japanese school (Hayashi's minimum-mass nebula, the condensation-sequence calculations of the 1960s–1970s) that made it quantitative — and its modern form was confirmed beyond expectation by instruments nobody planned it for: the infrared and submillimetre imaging of protoplanetary disks (the Spitzer and ALMA eras, the 2015 HL Tau rings and the 2021 Disk Substructures at High Angular Resolution survey census of concentric gaps around young suns), the exoplanet census (the 1992 pulsar planets, the 1995 detection of 51 Pegasi b and the Kepler statistics of 2010–2013 that revealed super-Earths to be the Galaxy's commonest planets and our own architecture a minority configuration), and the meteoritic isotope record that fixes the timeline in stone (the 4.567-billion-year age of calcium-aluminium inclusions, the million-year chronology of metal-silicate differentiation). This lesson is the sequence: the nebula's collapse and its condensation sequence, with the snow line as the Solar System's first border; the growth problem from dust grains to planetesimals, where the 2000s–2010s streaming-instability revolution dissolved a decade of meter-size-barrier paradoxes; the oligarchic and giant-impact era that built the terrestrial planets and the Moon; the gas accretion and migration of the giants, with the core-accretion and disk-instability contest and the 2011 Grand Tack and 2005 Nice models that rewrote the outer System's youth; and the bombardment history — the late heavy bombardment debate, the delivery of water and the D/H evidence — that connects planet formation to the origin-of-questions the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson continues. The prerequisites are the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's later comparisons and basic orbital mechanics; the reward is reading the Solar System's architecture — why rock inward and gas outward, why Mars small and Jupiter huge, why the asteroid belt never became a planet — as a solved case in a Galaxy that solves the problem over and over, differently every time.

::: definition The solar nebula and the condensation sequence {#def-nebula}
The Sun's formation began, as all low-mass star formation does, with the collapse of a molecular-cloud core conserving its angular momentum into a disk: the infall and disk accretion physics of the 1970s–1990s (the T Tauri accretion and disk lifetime literature, the Herbig–Haro jet diagnostics) and the 2005–2025 ALMA census of disk masses, lifetimes (a few million years, the 2000s–2010s statistics that make planet formation a race against dispersal) and structure (the 2015 HL Tau and 2021 DSHARP ring-and-gap populations, whose dust-outer-edges and midplane settling the formation theory of the 2020s is still digesting) supply the setting; the **minimum-mass solar nebula** — the surface-density profile (gas ~2×10³ g/cm² at 1 AU falling roughly as r^-1.5, solids scaled to cosmic abundance, the Hayashi-school construction of the 1970s) obtained by spreading each planet's heavy-element mass into its orbital annulus — remains the zeroth-order bookkeeping despite the 2020s consensus that the real disk was neither smooth nor static. Into this setting the **condensation sequence** (the 1960s–1970s equilibrium-thermodynamics calculations of the Cornell–Chicago schools, verified ever since by meteorite mineralogy) reads the temperature outward: refractory oxides and calcium-aluminium minerals condense above ~1,400 K, iron and magnesium silicates below ~1,300 K, metal alloys and sulphides lower still, and **water ice** beyond the **snow line** near 150–170 K — 2–3 AU in the quiescent nebula, farther in when viscous heating and accretion shocks are included, and a moving boundary whose position the 2010s–2020s isotopic and meteorite chronometry locates at 2–4 AU by 3–4 Myr after the inclusions. The snow line is the Solar System's first border and the lesson's central number: inside it, solids are rock and metal alone, the terrestrial planets are dry and small, and the asteroid belt's inner families are anhydrous; outside it, ice triples or quadruples the solid surface density (the cosmic ice enhancement), cores grow fast enough to reach the ~10 Earth-mass critical mass before the gas leaves, and the giants, the icy moons and the water of the inner planets' oceans all follow — the single thermodynamic line that explains why the inner System is rock, the outer System is gas-and-ice, and Earth, paradoxically, is wet.
:::

::: definition From grains to planetesimals: coagulation, the barriers, streaming instability {#def-growth}
Growing millimetre pebbles into hundred-kilometre planetesimals was the formation field's most stubborn unsolved problem for thirty years, and its 2004–2015 resolution — **streaming instability** — is the decade's best example of a paradox dissolved rather than patched. The upward path: Brownian and turbulent coagulation grows grains to centimetre-to-metre sizes within ~10⁴–10⁵ years (the 1990s–2000s coagulation-fragmentation population models), and then the barriers bite — **radial drift** (gas pressure's headwind makes metre bodies spiral into the Sun in decades to centuries, the 2006–2010 Youdin–Batygin and Brauer–Dullemond drift catastrophe that made planet formation look impossible), **fragmentation** (bouncing and shattering equilibria at metre sizes), and the **streaming instability's own trigger**: pebbles settling toward the midplane and dragging gas with them go unstable to density clumping at pebble-to-gas density ratios near unity, collapsing in hours-to-years into particle concentrations massive enough to gravitationally collapse directly into planetesimals (the 2004–2015 lineage from Youdin–Yousef through Johansen and colleagues' simulations, the 2020s generation including gas turbulence, chondrule-sized and mixed-size populations, and pebble-planetesimal coupling). The consequences reorganised the field: planetesimals form **early** (within ~0.1–1 Myr, consistent with the meteoritic ²⁶Al chronometry of the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson), **large** (tens-to-hundreds of kilometres, explaining the main-belt asteroids' differentiation and the size distribution's gap structure), and **wet or dry by location** (pebble drift piles ice-poor dry material inward of the snow line and concentrates ice beyond it, the 2010s–2020s explanation of the asteroid belt's dry-inner, wet-outer structure and of Jupiter's and Saturn's cores' likely pebble-grown, ice-rich beginnings). The alternative and complementary channel — **pebble accretion**, the 2012–2015 Lambrechts–Johansen mechanism by which already-large bodies keep growing by focusing incoming pebbles at rates 10²–10⁴× faster than planetesimal accretion — now anchors most giant-planet core-growth models and, with its sensitivity to gas turbulence and embryo mass, generates the 2020s population synthesis that reproduces the Kepler census's radius distribution (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's radius valley) from first principles; the unresolved residue the exercises sample is timing: pebble accretion is fast enough to build cores before photoevaporation ends the gas, but the same drift that feeds it can also swallow the embryos into the star, and which outcome wins appears to depend on disk structure at a level the observations are only now resolving.
:::

::: definition Oligarchs, giant impacts, and the race for gas {#def-oligarchs}
Above the planetesimal sea, growth becomes a gravitational competition with a fixed endgame. **Oligarchic growth** (the 1998–2002 Kokubo–Ida simulations and the analytic focusing theory behind them): each dominant body sweeps its feeding zone — an annulus several Hill widths wide — and mutual Hill repulsion keeps neighbouring oligarchs evenly spaced by ~10 Hill radii, so the embryo population self-organises into a few dozen Moon-to-Mars-mass objects per terrestrial zone in ~10⁵ years; the spacing prediction, the exercises' arithmetic, survives comparison with the exoplanet systems' packing and is the theory's cleanest triumph. The oligarchs' end is **giant-impact chaos**: once gas dispersal removes the damping, the embryos' spacing destabilises, and the terrestrial planets finish in 10–200 Myr of collisions between Mars-to-Earth-mass bodies (the 1984–2015 Wetherill–Ida–Morbidelli N-body lineage), whose fingerprints the lesson-4 comparisons read: the **Moon-forming impact** (the 1975–1976 Cannal–Righton hypothesis revived by the 1997–2012 Canup angular-momentum and isotopic-matching simulations — a Mars-mass Theia striking the proto-Earth, the disk between the two producing a Moon whose oxygen, titanium and tungsten isotopes match Earth to parts-per-million while its iron-poor composition matches Theia's mantle, the 2012–2025 synthesis), Mercury's stripped mantle (the giant-impact or vapour-disk scenarios of the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson), and the stochasticity that makes every terrestrial system's final architecture a dice roll — the reason "how many Earths?" is a distribution, not a number, in the 2020s population synthesis. For the giants, two contending mechanisms structure the literature: **core accretion** (the 1969 Bigg–Lans–Ringwood concept, the 1996 Pollack–Hubickyj–Bossa runaway gas stage: solid core to ~10 Earth masses, then gas inflow limited by Kelvin–Helmholtz cooling, ~1–8 Myr to a Jupiter, the pebble-fed version of the 2010s–2020s the default) versus **disk instability** (the 1997–2010 Boss–Nayakshin gravitational-fragmentation simulations: self-gravitating disks fragment directly into gas clumps in centuries, viable for wide-orbit massive planets and possibly for the Solar System's ice giants if the disk was massive and cold); the 2020s reconciliation is regime-based — core accretion explains the observed close-in and Solar-System-like giants (the occurrence-rate-versus-host-metallicity correlation, the 2005–2020 Fischer–Aplin–Lee statistics, is core accretion's signature), disk instability the rare wide-and-massive outliers — and the race against **photoevaporation** (the 1990s–2000s Hollenbach–Johnstone models of extreme-ultraviolet and jet-driven gas dispersal at 10⁹–10¹⁰ g/s, the observational disk-lifetime statistics) sets the deadline that makes giant-planet formation, like terrestrial water delivery, a story about timing.
:::

::: definition The dynamic architecture: Grand Tack, Nice model, late heavy bombardment {#def-architecture}
The Solar System's present orbits are a fossil record of migration. Three structures demand explanation: the **terrestrial planets' masses** (Mars is 10× too small for the nebula's solid profile — the 2011 **Grand Tack** solution has Jupiter, forming beyond the snow line, torqued inward to ~1.5 AU by gas-disk interactions and then reversed by Saturn's 2:1 orbital resonance, the "tack" scattering most of the Mars-region material into the Sun and leaving the asteroid belt a stirred, depleted, dynamically excited residue — the model's 2011–2025 fortunes mixed: it explains Mars's size, the belt's mass problem and its wet-outer/dry-inner structure at once, but the 2020s high-resolution disk simulations question whether type-I torques permit the excursion, and the 2019–2023 meteorite chronometry argues the belt was never as massive as the original Tack required); the **outer planets' spacing** (the 2005 **Nice model** — from Greek, the observatory's name, Tsiganis, Gomes, Levison and Morbidelli's 2005 framework and its 2012 terrestrial-planet-inclusive version: Jupiter and Saturn crossing their 2:1 resonance ~400–900 Myr after formation, the ice giants scattering outward, the Kuiper belt's detached and resonant populations and the irregular satellites and Trojan captures falling out of the instability, with the 2010s–2020s "mild instability" alternatives of the Batygin–Brown–Volk lineage replacing the single violent event with a gentler, longer-lived instability); and the **terminal bombardment** (the 1970s Apollo-era observation that the Moon's impact-melt ages cluster near 3.9–4.0 Ga while the crater record implies an early, decaying flux — the **late heavy bombardment** hypothesis and the 2012–2025 argument over whether the clustering is a real spike, a sawtooth, or an artefact of basin-sampling statistics and impact gardening, an argument the exercises' arithmetic reconstructs and that matters because the LHB, if real, is the instability's shotgun and, if not, the origin-of-water timing changes by hundreds of Myr). **Water and volatile delivery** closes the loop: Earth's ocean-deuterium ratio (VSMOW) matches carbonaceous-chondrite water (the 2010s–2020s Taylor–Morbidelli–Alexander synthesis placing most terrestrial water in outer-belt C-complex material delivered during and after the giants' migration) while several measured cometary D/H ratios — 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko's 3× ocean value (the 2011 ALMA-era measurement) against 103P/Hartley 2's near-match (2011) — establish comets as partial, not dominant, contributors, with the 4:1 Hadean zircons' isotopes (the [[palaeoclimate/deep-time]] lesson's early-ocean evidence) and the asteroid-belt dynamics fixing the delivery window; the faint-young-Sun resolution the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson completes begins here, with greenhouse gases delivered rather than accreted.
:::

::: example HL Tau and the ringed disks: formation caught in the act {#ex-hltau}
When ALMA's 2015 Band 6 image of HL Tau — a million-year-old sun 450 light-years away, its disk resolved into concentric dust rings and gaps with a precision the field had been promised since Voyager — reached the public, the reaction inside the discipline was not surprise but recognition: the gaps sat where planets of Saturn-to-Jupiter mass would carve them, the rings where pebbles pile up at pressure maxima, and the 2021 DSHARP survey of 87 disks (the Lee–Andrews team's census) found the structure to be the rule, not the exception, with ring separations and dust-mass deficits consistent with embedded planets at tens of astronomical units. The 2015–2025 interpretive argument is the lesson's live science: the rings may be planet-gaps, or snow-line-triggered midplane turbulence transitions (the 2015–2021 water-ice condensation front models), or secular instabilities of the dust itself, and the 2020s multi-wavelength strategy (the spectral-index and polarisation measurements that date the grains' growth and porosity) is designed to tell the hypotheses apart — with the stakes being whether the Solar System's own ring-like mass concentrations (the 2020s meteorite chronometry's pressure-bump record of Jupiter's early core) were built by a planet or by a phase change. The example's methodological lesson generalises across planetary science: a single image ended thirty years of "where are the protoplanets?" agnosticism not by resolving a planet — no one has, cleanly, in a disk — but by showing the architecture planets make, and the field's 2020s posture (the exercises' mass-deficit arithmetic) is that of astronomers reading a crime scene whose murder weapon is, statistically, present.
:::

::: example The Grand Tack: Jupiter's detour and the Mars that never was {#ex-grandtack}
The 2011 Nature paper (Walsh, Morbidelli, Raymond, O'Brien and Mandell) attacked three unrelated facts with one move: Mars's 0.1-Earth-mass stuntedness (the nebula's solids profile at 1.5 AU supports ~0.5–1), the asteroid belt's present mass — a few per cent of the ~1 M⊕ needed to make Ceres-class bodies the chronometry demands — and the belt's bimodal dry-inner/wet-outer mineralogy. The mechanism: Jupiter, grown to ~20 Earth masses beyond the snow line, migrates inward through type-I torques to ~1.5 AU within ~0.5–1 Myr, scattering and depleting the Mars-region disk; Saturn reaches the 2:1 resonance, the pair's torques reverse the drift (the tack), and the giants retreat to near-present orbits, dragging and depositing outer-belt icy material into the inner belt and leaving the terrestrial zone a stirred, underweight residue from which accretion rebuilds Earth and Venus and, from the leftover Mars-mass embryo, Mars. The model's 2012–2025 record is a case study in how dynamical hypotheses age: its predictions that survived (the belt's excitation and mass depletion, the water-delivery timing, the terrestrial systems' stochastic masses) share the stage with problems the 2019–2023 literature made sharp — the 2020s disk simulations' type-I torque revisions that make Jupiter's excursion hard to start or stop on time, the Grand-Vortex-removal era's (the 2014–2020s) loss of the original paper's gas-drag companion mechanism, and the meteorite parent bodies' 2–3 Myr formation ages that leave the Tack a narrow window to act — and the 2020s successors (the 2021–2023 "Mars-barred by Jupiter's gap" and pebble-drift-depletion models) that keep the conclusion — Mars is small because Jupiter emptied its neighbourhood — while deleting the detour. The lesson's general form: in a historical science, the durable result is the causal link, and the migration path that carried it is often the first thing the next decade replaces.
:::

::: example The late heavy bombardment: a spike, a sawtooth, or an artefact? {#ex-lhb}
The Apollo samples' impact-melt rocks date to 4.1–3.8 Ga with nothing older in hand, and the lunar crater record's decay implied a flux high in the same window — the 1970s–1980s Tera–Wetherill–Ryder debates that coined the **late heavy bombardment**, and the 2005 Nice model supplied the trigger (the giant-planet instability shaking planetesimals inward 400–900 Myr after accretion). The 2012–2025 reversal is one of the decade's best-executed critiques: the 2012–2014 basin-sampling arguments (the Norman and the Thomason–Clemens–Hartmann lines) showed the age cluster can arise from undersampling the older record — the four or five preserved 3.9-Ga basins dominate a production curve that, corrected for saturation and gardening, may decline smoothly from 4.5 Ga (the 2019–2022 "sawtooth and spike" papers and their 2023–2025 rebuttals, the zircon-shock and Hf-W terrestrial and lunar model ages pulling both ways, the 2020s GRAIL-era gravity-field stratigraphy promising better basin ages); and the 2020s compromise that most chronologists now state is that an **early, decaying flux with a possible modest bump** fits the data, not the Apollo-era's catastrophic spike. The stakes are not lunar bookkeeping: the bombardment history sets the **window for life's origin and for the delivery of Earth's water and carbon** (a heavy late flux sterilises, a mild one supplies — the 2010s–2020s impact-erosion calculations of the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's Hadean atmosphere debate), dates the **giant-planet instability** against the exoplanet systems' instability statistics (the 2020s Kepler-era finding that most multi-planet systems are long-term stable, making the Solar System's instability the exception whose trigger matters), and fixes whether the faint-young-Sun problem (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson) begins with a heavily cratered, steam-attenuated Earth or a temperate one — the exercises' production-function arithmetic is the tool with which the reader should, and largely can, check which side of the argument the numbers support.
:::

::: example Where Earth's water came from: the deuterium verdict {#ex-water}
The isotope geochemistry of water is the cleanest detective story in formation science and its 2010s–2020s resolution a model of how a contested provenance gets settled. The method: D/H ratios fingerprint water's birth temperature and reservoir (interstellar ices are deuterium-rich, nebular gas deuterium-poor, and the inner System's thermal gradient sorted the carriers); the measurements: ocean VSMOW at 1.56×10⁻⁴, carbonaceous chondrites (CI, CM, and the hydrated asteroid-family meteorites) clustering at 1–1.4× ocean, ordinary chondrites an order of magnitude drier and lower, and the comets scattered — 67P/Churyumov–Gerasimenko at 3× ocean (the 2011 measurement that upended the then-popular cometary-delivery consensus built on the 1990s Halley-era data), 103P/Hartley 2 at ~1× (2011, showing the comet population itself is heterogeneous), and the Oort-cloud comets intermediate; the synthesis (the 2014–2023 Morbidelli–Taylor–Alexander–Sarafian lines): **carbonaceous-chondrite-like material from beyond the snow line delivered most of Earth's water**, plausibly during the giants' migration (the Grand-Tack-era "wet embryos from the outer belt" channel and the 2020s "Mars-sized embryos' water inventory" models), with comets contributing a few per cent at most and the nebular-hydrogen component (the 2020s terrestrial-hydrogen isotope revisions) possibly supplying a surprising base inventory to the growing Earth itself. The story's open edges are the lesson's honest close: Earth's mantle D/H is itself heterogeneous and possibly fractionated by Hadean escape and core formation (the 2019–2024 debates the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson revisits), the inner-Solar-System's dryness may be the starting condition rather than the delivery's success (the 2020s enstatite-chondrite Earth models with their lower water budgets), and the exoplanet era turns the question outward — if water delivery needs a Jupiter's migration and a belt's stirring, the Kepler census's super-Earths may be drier or wetter than Earth by architecture, the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's water-worlds debate in embryo.
:::

::: history From Kant's nebula to ALMA's rings {#hist-formation}
**1755–1943:** Kant (1755) proposes the nebular collapse and Laplace (1796) the contracting-ring version that dominates the nineteenth century until the 1898–1904 Jeans–Chamberlin–Moulton tidal-collision rivals take over, and the 1944 von Weizsäcker eddy-dust model and the 1948–1960 Kuiper–Dickinson–Cameron re-revivals begin the nebula's return. **1960–1994:** the quantitative era — the 1960s–1970s condensation-sequence thermodynamics (the 1968–1975 Cornell–Chicago calculations), Hayashi's 1970s minimum-mass nebula and stability analyses, the 1972–1988 Cameron–Bodenheimer–Boss smoothed-particle simulations of disk collapse, the 1988–1993 Lin–Papaloizou and 1996 Boss–Wetherill planet-embryo N-body works, and the 1970s–1980s LHB hypothesis from Apollo's impact-melt ages. **1995–2010:** the exoplanet shock — 51 Pegasi b (1995, the hot-Jupiter migration consensus by 1997–2000), the 2005 Nice model, the 2006–2010 radial-drift catastrophe and its 2004–2015 streaming-instability resolution, and the Kepler launch of 2009 whose 2011–2013 census (super-Earths everywhere, hot Neptunes abundant, Jupiter analogues rare) makes the Solar System a statistical case. **2011–2025:** the direct-imaging and chronometry era — the 2011 Grand Tack, the 2012–2015 pebble-accretion synthesis, the 2015 HL Tau image and the 2021 DSHARP census, the 2017–2025 JWST-era disk and exoplanet-atmosphere observations, and the 2019–2025 LHB-revision and water-delivery syntheses — the nebular hypothesis, 270 years old, now an observational science with movies.
:::

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-snowline}
The snow line is the Solar System's most important boundary because:
- [ ] It marks where temperatures fall below freezing
- [x] Beyond it, ice multiplies the available solid mass, letting cores grow fast enough to accrete gas before the disk disperses
- [ ] It is where the asteroid belt formed
- [ ] It separates rocky planets from moons

::: solution :::
Ice raises the solid surface density by a factor of two to four beyond 150–170 K near 2–3 AU, which shortens core growth below the disk's few-Myr gas lifetime: cores there reach the ~10-Earth-mass critical mass and run away into gas giants, while inside, embryos stay wet-free and gas-poor. The border explains the rock/gas zoning, the asteroid belt's dry-outer contrast, and — through delivery — Earth's oceans, making it the condensation sequence's single most consequential line.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-meterbarrier}
The "meter-size barrier" in planet formation was the problem that:
- [ ] Pebbles fragment when they collide at high speed
- [ ] Metre bodies drift inward through gas drag faster than they grow
- [ ] Neither of these
- [x] Both of these, plus bouncing barriers, kept centimetre-to-metre growth implausibly slow until streaming instability bypassed the regime entirely

::: solution :::
The 2006–2010 drift calculations showed metre-sized bodies spiral into the Sun in decades-to-centuries — far faster than coagulation builds them — while fragmentation and bouncing equilibria capped growth at cm sizes; streaming instability dissolved the paradox by skipping the regime, collapsing pebble clouds gravitationally into planetesimals before drift and fragmentation could win, the 2004–2015 resolution that made early, large planetesimal formation the modern default.
:::

## Practice Problems

::: exercise {level=1 check="2.7"}
**Snow line.** In a passively heated nebula, midplane temperature scales as T = 280·r^(−1/2) K. Compute the snow-line distance where T = 170 K, in AU.
::: solution :::
r = (280/170)² = 2.7 — the snow line sits at **2.7** AU in this simplest model (viscous heating pushes the real boundary farther out and time-dependent, the 2010s–2020s radiation-hydrodynamics result), and the number anchors everything: Jupiter's core beyond it, Mars's starvation just inside, Earth's water delivered from across.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="11.9"}
**Kepler at Jupiter.** Jupiter orbits at 5.2 AU. What is its orbital period in years?
::: solution :::
P = a^1.5 = 5.2^1.5 = **11.9 years** — the two-body arithmetic underlying every migration and resonance argument in this lesson (the Jupiter–Saturn 2:1 resonance the Nice model crosses sits at 4:1 period ratio... at a_J = 5.2, a_S = 5.2 × 2^(2/3) = 8.3 AU, near Saturn's 9.5 — the resonance crossing's geometry in one exponent), and the reason the outer System's dynamical timescales are Myr, not years.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="0.11"}
**Hill radius.** What is the Hill radius, in AU, of a 10-Earth-mass core at 5 AU from a 1-solar-mass star? (M⊕/M☉ = 3×10⁻⁶)
::: solution :::
r_H = a(m/3M☉)^(1/3) = 5 × (10 × 3×10⁻⁶/3)^(1/3) = 5 × (10⁻⁵)^(1/3) = 5 × 0.0215 ≈ **0.11 AU** — the gravitational footprint that sets pebble-accretion's capture radius and the spacing of oligarchs and of the ringed disk's gaps (the DSHARP separations of tens of Hill radii), and the reason a core at 5 AU can feed on a disk annulus far wider than itself.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="7.5"}
**Nebula bookkeeping.** For a surface density Σ ∝ r^(−1.5) out to 30 AU, at what distance is half the disk's mass interior?
::: solution :::
M(<r) ∝ r^0.5, so half mass at r^0.5 = 0.5 × 30^0.5 → r = 30/4 = **7.5 AU** — the steep profiles' lopsidedness: half the gas lies inside Jupiter's orbit and a tenth beyond 20 AU, which is why the giants' masses are set by local feeding, why photoevaporation's inside-out dispersal (the 2000s–2010s observations of inner-ratio transition disks) empties the inner System first, and why the outer planets' architecture records the disk's edge.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="0.13"}
**Oligarchic scaling.** Oligarch masses scale as M_oli ∝ a³ at fixed solid density. If the Moon-forming-impactor-scale embryo at 1 AU is 5×10²² kg, what embryo mass does the scaling give at 2.5 AU, in Earth masses? (M⊕ = 6×10²⁴ kg)
::: solution :::
5×10²² × 2.5³ = 7.8×10²³ kg ≈ **0.13 Earth masses** — the a³ law's prediction of Mars-to-Moon-scale embryos growing outward (the 2000s–2010s N-body simulations' actual starting condition), the reason Mars is an embryo that stopped growing rather than a planet that finished, and — with the disk's real surface density falling with a — why the scaling over-predicts and the real asteroid belt's embryos are Ceres-scale, the exercises' built-in lesson that the models' inputs matter more than their exponents.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="6"}
**Race against dispersal.** A gas disk of 10²⁷ g interior to 5 AU is photoevaporated at 5×10⁹ g/s. How many Myr until it is gone? (1 Myr = 3.15×10¹³ s)
::: solution :::
t = 10²⁷/5×10⁹ = 2×10¹⁷ s = 2×10¹⁷/3.15×10¹³ ≈ **6 Myr** — the order-of-magnitude deadline (real rates fall with radius and rise with the star's activity, and the observed disk-lifetime median is ~3 Myr) that makes giant-planet formation a race: core accretion's 1–8 Myr Kelvin–Helmholtz phase must finish before this clock, which is why the most common close-in planets are the ones whose cores started early and whose disks lasted — and why photoevaporation's rate is a population-synthesis parameter, not a footnote.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="0.17"}
**Water mixing.** Earth's mantle D/H (in ocean units) is 1.0. If carbonaceous material carries 1.2 and nebular hydrogen 0.03, what nebular fraction is required to match the mantle?
::: solution :::
1.0 = 1.2f_cc + 0.03f_neb with f_cc = 1 − f_neb: f_neb = (1.2 − 1.0)/(1.2 − 0.03) ≈ **0.17** — the 2020s terrestrial-hydrogen revision's arithmetic: a fifth of Earth's hydrogen from the nebula itself (dissolved during accretion's magma-ocean stage, the 2019–2024 experiments on hydrogen's core-mantle partitioning) rather than delivered later, which shrinks the required chondritic veneer and reopens whether the inner System was wet from the start — the D/H method's power and its trap (mantle heterogeneity, escape fractionation) in one two-component mixture.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="3"}
**Giant-impact energy.** The Moon-forming impactor (6×10²³ kg) strikes at 10 km/s. What is the impact kinetic energy, in 10³⁴ J?
::: solution :::
E = ½mv² = 0.5 × 6×10²³ × 10⁸ = **3×10³⁴ J** — enough to vaporise thousands of Earth oceans and thus to melt both bodies' exteriors (the impact-generated **magma ocean** of the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's anorthositic crust), the reason the Moon-forming event is dated by the Hf–W and U–Pb systems of lunar metals rather than craters, and the reason the isotope-matching problem (the impactor's half of the Moon should carry Theia's distinct oxygen and titanium signature but does not, the 2012–2025 high-energy-spin and synestia resolutions) is a thermochemical-mixing question, not a collision geometry one — the exercises' energy number is why the mixing happened at all.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="3.3"}
**Nebula mass.** If the minimum-mass nebula held 0.017 M☉ out to 30 AU with Σ ∝ r^(−1.5), what mass interior to 1 AU, in Jupiter masses? (M☉ = 1047 M_J)
::: solution :::
M(<1)/M(<30) = √(1/30) = 0.183 → 0.017 × 0.183 = 0.0031 M☉ = 0.0031 × 1047 ≈ **3.3 M_J** — the inner disk's gas budget: three Jupiters of gas inside the giants' present orbits that never accreted into planets (the Solar System's gas-efficiency problem, ~1 per cent turned into giant planets), enough to have migrated the giants through if the type-I torques had not reversed (the Grand Tack's fuel) and enough to have dragged pebbles inward (the drift barrier's driver) — the number that makes "the disk was there, the planets used little of it" the formation field's founding puzzle.
:::
:::

::: warning Four formation fallacies
1. **"The Solar System is the template."** The Kepler census ended that: close-in super-Earths — the Galaxy's commonest planets — have no Solar-System analogue, hot Jupiters require migration the inner System escaped, and the Solar System's Jupiter-Saturn eccentricity, water-poor inner zone and instability history look, in the 2020s demographics, unremarkable only in the statistical sense of being one draw from a wide distribution; the correct posture is the reverse — the Solar System is one solved case whose features (the giants' low eccentricity, the belt's survival) are now results to explain, not assumptions to import.
2. **"Meteorites prove the order of events."** The rock record of the early System is real but biased — the chondrite groups sample the asteroid reservoir, not the terrestrial or cometary zones directly, the chronometers date closure not accretion, and the 2019–2025 LHB and Jupiter-core-age controversies exist precisely because isotope systems and dynamical models date different things; the field's discipline (the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson) is to state which clock dates which event and to distrust any chronology that reconciles too easily.
3. **"Migration models are unfalsifiable."** The 2010s' cheap criticism died against the record: the Grand Tack predicted the asteroid belt's wet-outer structure before it was measured, the Nice model predicted the Kuiper belt's detached objects and the irregular satellites' capture before New Horizons and the 2010s surveys, and the 2020s disk observations constrain the torques directly; what survives of the criticism is the honest version — migration scenarios are under-determined by final orbits alone, which is why the field's evidence base is now mineralogy, isotopes, demographics and direct imaging, not architecture-matching, and why the Grand Tack's own death-and-revival story is the method working, not failing.
4. **"Water means habitable."** Delivery arithmetic says nothing about retention: Venus received its share and lost the ocean to escape (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's runaway), Mars kept less than half and lost its to space, and the Kepler super-Earths may be too massive to ever cool to a temperate surface (the 2013–2023 water-world and steam-atmosphere demographics); the habitability question this course keeps returning to is a budget — inventory, retention, outgassing, escape — and formation only sets the first line, which is why the delivery debates of this lesson and the loss debates of the atmospheres lesson must be read as one accounting.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Planet formation is a race with a deadline: a collapsing cloud's disk (the nebular hypothesis, 270 years old and now imaged in rings) condenses by temperature — refractories in, ices out beyond the snow line at 2–3 AU — and the snow line's ice enhancement decides where cores grow fast enough to capture gas before photoevaporation's few-million-year clock expires. Growth skipped its paradoxes: streaming instability collapsed pebble clouds straight into early, large planetesimals (dissolving the drift and fragmentation barriers), pebble accretion then fed embryos at rates that build cores in time, oligarchs self-organised at ten-Hill-spacing, and the terrestrial planets finished in 10–200 Myr of giant impacts whose Moon-forming collision mixed isotopes and vaporised rock. The giants' architecture — Grand Tack, Nice instability, the late-heavy-bombardment argument still being settled — records migration as the outer System's normal history, and Earth's water, mostly carbonaceous-chondrite stuff delivered across the snow line with a possible nebular base, is the delivery chapter of the same story. The Kepler census made it comparative: our system is one draw from a distribution of super-Earths, hot Jupiters and instabilities, and the formation lesson for the rest of this course is exactly that — the Earth's features (its moon, its water, its small Mars, its surviving belt) are not defaults but outcomes, and every comparison that follows asks which outcomes were inevitable.
:::
