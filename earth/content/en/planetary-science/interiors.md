We cannot drill deeper than a few kilometres — a tenth of a per cent of the way to Earth's centre — yet the interiors of the other planets are among the best-measured structures in science, because physics reaches where machines cannot: seismic waves map layer boundaries by arrival time, gravity and libration weigh and stiffen the deep interior, the moment of inertia counts how mass is distributed, and magnetic fields and their fossils report who is convecting where. This lesson is the comparative interior science of the Solar System's rocky worlds, built on the geophysical method chain (seismology, gravimetry, rotation, heat flow, palaeomagnetism) and applied across the size ladder: Earth's fully resolved structure (the 1906–1975 Oldham-Gutenberg-Birch-PREM sequence), the Moon's small core and shattered crust (the 1970s–2025 Apollo-network-to-GRAIL synthesis), Mars's InSight-determined 20–37-km crust, global low-velocity mantle zone and 3,730-km liquid core (the 2021–2024 papers), Mercury's 85-per-cent-radius core and lumpy dynamo (the 2011–2025 MESSENGER results), and the icy moons' subsurface oceans and odd dynamos (the 1996–2025 Galileo and Juno induction and libration evidence, the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's domain, previewed here for the convection argument). The physics is one course: heat sources (accretion, core formation, short-lived and long-lived radionuclides — the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's ²⁶Al and ⁴⁰K legacies), heat transport (conduction in lids, convection in mantles and cores, the parameterised-convection scaling that ties cooling rate to radius), phase changes (the inner cores freezing, the 410-and-660-km mantle transitions, the oceans under ice), and the two great dividers of interior fate: **size** (the cooling schedule that killed the Moon's and Mars's dynamos and spares Earth's for billions of years) and **surface mobility** (stagnant lid versus plate tectonics, the heat-escape switch that the [[climate-change/observed-change]] carbon cycle and the habitability literature both depend on). The prerequisites are the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's accretion energetics and the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's metal-silicon differentiation chemistry; the payoff is the synthesis this course has been circling: why the Moon, Mars and Mercury are what they are, why Earth is the exception that needs explaining, and what the 2026–2035 missions (BepiColombo's libration, EnVision's gravity, the lunar-geophysical and Mars-network seismometer deployments) will measure next.

::: definition Reading interiors from outside: the method chain {#def-methods}
Five physical observables, combined, determine an interior no probe will touch. **Seismology** is the only direct imager: waves reflect and refract at impedance jumps (the 1906 Oldham core shadow, the 1909 Mohorovičić discontinuity, the 1926 Jeffreys core-mantle boundary and 1936 Lehmann inner core), arrive late or early by the velocities along their paths (the 1975 Dziewonski–Anderson PREM radial model and the 1980s–2025 seismic-tomography imaging of subducted slabs to the core-mantle boundary and of the large low-shear-velocity provinces beneath the Pacific and Africa — the 2020s debates over whether those provinces are thermal, compositional or relict crust), and convert modes at hidden layers (the Ps and ScS receiver functions that InSight turned on Mars in the 2021–2023 crustal-thickness papers). **Gravity** in two forms: the hydrostatic figure and the gravity field's spherical harmonics give the density profile's shape (the 1970s–2025 Jupiter-and-satellite-and-Mars field analyses), while free-air and Bouguer anomalies map lateral mass contrasts (the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's mascons, the 2011–2012 GRAIL crustal porosity). **Rotation** contributes the moment of inertia factor (C/MR² — 0.3308 for Earth against a uniform sphere's 0.4, the oldest quantitative proof of central concentration, measured from satellite-orbit precession and, for Mercury, the 2007–2015 MESSENGER gravity field's J₂ and the libration amplitude that proved the fluid layer under the crust), plus the tidal Love numbers and the libration's phase lag, which measure rigidity and dissipation at depth (the 2010s–2025 icy-moon libration and Love-number arguments for subsurface oceans). **Surface heat flow** — measured directly only on Earth (the 1.01 W/m² oceanic mean, the 0.05–0.08 W/m² continental range, the 2010s–2025 revised radiogenic-heat budget resolving the 2000s surface-heat-flow paradox) — is the cooling rate itself, and on other worlds is inferred from tectonic and volcanic style or, for InSight, attempted and half-lost (the 2021–2022 mole failure, the 2024–2025 partial-data constraints). **Magnetism** reports convection's history: present fields from orbiters (the 1975 Mariner-10, 1979 Voyager, 1995–2003 Galileo and 2011–2025 Juno and MESSENGER campaigns), ancient fields from crustal anomalies (the 1999 Mars Global Surveyor discovery) and from remanent magnetisation in returned rocks (the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's lunar palaeointensity saga). The combination rule — each observable is degenerate alone (a gravity field fits many profiles, a moment of inertia fits many two-layer recipes) and the joint inversion plus mineral physics (the 1960s–2025 experimental and first-principles equations of state that say what pressure and temperature do to olivine, perovskite and iron) breaks the degeneracies — is the discipline's quiet triumph: **Earth's interior is the most precisely known deep structure of any planet, and the other rocky worlds are now read at 10–30-per-cent precision, one mission at a time.**
:::

::: definition Core formation: metal finding metal {#def-core-formation}
Differentiation is gravity sorting materials by density in a mechanical and chemical sense at once, and its central event is **metal-silicate segregation**: molten iron, with its eutectic iron-sulphide lowering the solidus by hundreds of kelvin (the 1990s–2025 Fe-FeS experimental studies), draining through silicate melt or crystal mush to form a core within the first ~30–50 Myr of Solar System history (the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's hafnium-tungsten chronometry — the 2002–2025 ¹⁸²W anomalies in chondrites and achondrites dating metal segregation to 10–50 Myr for the differentiated planetesimals and 30-plus Myr for Earth and Mars, the 2021–2024 lunar and martian W-isotope revisions). The physics has three regimes, distinguished by the dihedral angle (the 1990s–2020s high-pressure experiments: wetting at low pressure and shallow melt fractions, dry at deep mantle conditions) and by permeability: **percolation** of melt through interconnected pores at dihedral angles below 60° (the 2000s–2025 iron-rich-melt transport experiments), **disequilibrium descent** as diapirs, droplets and slurries (the 2010s–2025 metal-rain and slurry-layer models), and **magma-ocean settling** — the 1970s–2025 canonical picture in which the accretional and ²⁶Al heat (the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's energy budget) melts the whole body and metal sinks in hours-to-years droplet showers, equilibrating chemically at depth. That equilibration is the lesson's great quantitative payoff: core-formation pressure and temperature are recorded in the mantle's siderophile-element abundances (the 1970s–1990s Ringwood, 1990s–2025 metal-silicate partitioning experiments — Ni, Co, W, P — whose simultaneous fit demands equilibration near 25–60 GPa and 2,500–3,500 K for Earth, i.e. at the base of a deep magma ocean, the 2010s–2025 matching of all five elements' abundances plus the ¹⁸²W isotope constraint in single models), and the same arithmetic applied to Mars (the 2010s–2025 shergottite trace-element fits favouring a shallower, cooler, more reducing equilibration — a smaller planet's core forming at lower pressure) and to Vesta and the Moon (the 2010s–2025 lunar-metal-stability arguments for a small, sulphur-bearing core formed late, the 2021–2024 Apollo-sample W-and-S-isotope support). The **light-element budget** is core formation's open ledger: Earth's core is 5–10 wt per cent lighter than pure iron-nickel (the 6 g/cm³ density deficit at core-mantle-boundary pressure and temperature, the 1990s–2025 high-pressure experiments and first-principles calculations), and the candidate light elements (Si, O, S, C, H — the 2010s–2025 partitioning experiments' best compromises: perhaps a few per cent each of Si, O and S, the 2023–2025 debates over hydrogen's solubility in the deep mantle and over carbon's fate) are a petrological thermometer and oxygen-fugacity record of the accretion endgame, while on Mars (the 2021–2024 InSight core density of ~6 g/cm³ at 3,730-km radius — 15–20 wt per cent light elements, far more than Earth's, the 2023–2025 Fe-S-C-H experimental reconciliations) and Mercury (the 2009–2023 FeS-solidus arguments for a sulphur-rich, low-melting, partly molten core) the light-element inventories are the reason those cores are still liquid and those dynamos behave as they do — core formation, in the 2025 reading, is not a completed event but an ongoing interior condition.
:::

::: definition Mantle convection, lids and the two tectonic regimes {#def-convection}
Between the core and the surface, rocky planets run one heat engine with two possible designs. The mantle is a very viscous fluid on geological time (the 1970s–2025 post-glacial-rebound and geoid constraints giving upper-mantle viscosities of 10²⁰–10²¹ Pa s, the 2010s–2025 transition-zone and lower-mantle estimates rising to 10²²–10²³), and it convects when the Rayleigh number exceeds criticality (Ra = ρgαΔTd³/κη ~ 10⁶–10⁷ at onset; Earth's mantle sits at 10⁷–10⁸ in the 2010s–2025 definitions, the exercises' arithmetic giving 10¹⁰ on nominal values — the exact number depends on which viscosity the definition adopts, which is the convection literature's honest uncertainty). Convection's surface expression is the course's great divide: **stagnant-lid** planets (the Moon, Mars, Mercury, Venus, the 2025 consensus) carry their heat through a conductive lid, recycle no crust, lock their volatiles out of the deep cycle, and cool on the radius/3 conduction schedule; **mobile-lid** Earth recovers its crust at subduction zones, cools efficiently, hydrates its mantle, and runs the silicate-weathering carbon cycle of the [[climate-change/observed-change]] and [[geochemistry/weathering-chemistry]] lessons — the 2000s–2025 question "why is Earth alone mobile-lid?" (the 2007–2025 mobile-lid-versus-stagnant-lid parameter studies finding a narrow habitable corridor in viscosity, water content and yield strength; the 2019–2025 hypotheses that plate tectonics needs continental crust, ocean water, or both, and may have started as late as 3.2–2.5 Ga, the [[earth-resources]]-relevant onset debate of the 2020s) still unresolved and central to the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] habitability demographics. Between the endmembers the 2010s–2025 vocabulary has multiplied: Venus's episodic-overturn and plume-lid regimes (the 2022–2025 3-D models reproducing coronae and tesserae without subduction), early-Earth's sluggish-lid and sagduction regimes (the 2010s–2025 Archaean-tectonics debates, the [[structural-geology]]-adjacent literature), and Mars's single-plate-with-internal-strain reading of the Tharsis stresses (the 2010s–2020s flexural-and-gravity models). Convection's pattern is set by the internal heating fraction (the 2000s–2025 numerical experiments: bottom-heated flows organise into sheets and plumes, internally heated flows into broad upwellings and small downwellings — the difference between Earth's slab-dominated and Venus-and-Mars's plume-and-mantle-structure patterns), by the phase transitions (the 410-and-660-km olivine boundaries whose Clapeyron slopes — positive at 410, negative at 660 — partly penetrate and partly block downwellings, the 1990s–2025 tomography and mineral-physics joint reading), and by the core-mantle-boundary heat flux, which couples mantle and core evolution in both directions: the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's dynamo-collapse mechanism (mantle cooling thickening the thermal boundary layer and strangling core convection) is this coupling, and the 2020s–2025 coupled mantle-core thermal evolutions (the Roberts–Davies–Cobles lineage reproducing Earth's field-strength stability and Mars's early shutoff from the same physics) are its current synthesis. The parameterised-convection scaling (dT/dt ∝ −T^(1+b), b ~ 0.2–0.3 for stagnant lids, the 1980s–2025 Frank-Kamenetskii and boundary-layer derivations) closes the engineering: **a body twice the radius cools half as fast, which single line explains why the Moon died at 3.5 Ga, Mars at 3.7, Mercury limps, and Earth will run for another two billion years.**
:::

::: definition Dynamos: engines, energy budgets, and death {#def-dynamo}
A planetary magnetic field is generated where an electrically conducting fluid convects in a rotating frame — the mean-field dynamo theory of the 1950s–1970s (the Parker–Steenbeck–Roberts αΩ lineage) made quantitative by the 1995–2025 geodynamo simulations (the Glatzmaier–Roberts 1995 self-consistent run, the 2000s–2025 Earth-like parameter explorations and the 2020s machine-learning-assisted and data-assimilating models) and read against the fossil record by palaeomagnetism. The energy budget is the interior-science part: a dynamo runs on the core's entropy production — **secular cooling** (the core at 4,000–5,000 K cooling a few tens of kelvin per billion years, the 2013–2025 ab-initio thermal conductivities of core materials having raised κ by 2–3× and thereby forced the 2014–2025 reanalysis: pure thermal cooling cannot power a 3-billion-year field without an implausibly hot early core or a cold early core that has since frozen), **inner-core growth** (the latent heat and light-element expulsion of the 2013–2025 "faint young Sun" resolution — a young inner core driving compositional convection, the 2020s debates over the core's melting curve and the 2024–2025 seismic and first-principles challenges to the inner core's existence-as-we-know-it), **compositional stratification** (the 2010s–2025 stable layers from early light-element segregation that can mute a dynamo entirely — the Venus-suppression and Mercury-quiet-field options), and **mechanical powering** (precession and tides, weak and steady — the Moon's and Mercury's candidate extras). The scaling laws (the 2000s–2025 Christensen–Chandranathan–Stevenson energy-balance scalings linking field strength to convective power and core radius) turn palaeointensity into thermodynamics: the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's 20–100-μT lunar field required more core power than any plausible lunar mechanism supplies, which is why the 2011–2025 literature preferred transient mechanisms (the 2011–2019 basal-magma-ocean and 2020s core-sulphide-layer dynamos) over steady ones. Field **topology** carries interior information too: dipolar fields need rapid rotation (the Rossby-number transition of the 2000s–2025 simulations — the reason Mercury's slow spin and small core give a multipolar, lumpy, offset field, the 2022–2025 MESSENGER analyses finding time-variability that favours a weakly-convecting, compositionally-stratified core), while crustal-remnance patterns date the dynamos of dead worlds (the 1999–2025 Mars-crustal-magnetic analyses: strong, ancient, hemispherically-concentrated magnetisation acquired 4.5–3.7 Ga, the 2020s dynamo-extinction chronometries, and the 2010s–2025 disputed Mars-reversal and stirring arguments). The **induction** method extends the census to the icy moons: time-varying external fields (Jupiter's magnetosphere at the moons' orbits) induce secondary fields whose amplitude and phase measure a subsurface ocean's depth and conductivity (the 1996–2003 Galileo-Europa-and-Ganymede and 2015–2025 Juno-and-Hubble results — the 2021–2024 reanalyses revising Europa's induction amplitude, the Ganymede intrinsic-field-and-ocean geometry debates), the 2024–2025 Europa-Clipper-and-JUICE campaigns scheduled to finish the job. The comparative lesson the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] habitability debate must absorb: **dynamos are common, sustained dipolar dynamos are the expensive special case** — requiring a large-enough body (to keep the core hot), a cold-enough mantle (to let the core dump heat), no fatal stratification, and fast enough rotation — and the Solar System's tally (Earth yes for 3.5 Gyr, Ganymede yes for some billion years, Mercury maybe, Moon and Mars had them and lost them, Venus perhaps never) is the demographic the exoplanet-field-requirement arguments have to average over.
:::

::: example How Earth's core was found, and un-found {#ex-earths-core}
The core's discovery is the method chain's founding case and its cautionary tail in one sequence. **1906:** Oldham identifies the core shadow — P waves absent in the 103–142° angular range — proving a central discontinuity; **1914:** Gutenberg locates it at 2,900 km (the 1990s–2025 re-readings of the original travel-time curves confirming the depth to a few tens of kilometres, a century before InSight would measure Mars's at 3,730); **1926:** Jeffreys shows the outer core transmits no S waves — liquid; **1936:** Lehmann finds the inner core from P-wave focusing anomalies; **1952:** Birch turns the revolution quantitative — the density jump at the core-mantle boundary (8.2 to 10 g/cm³, requiring compression-consistent iron-nickel at 136 GPa, the exercises' pressure arithmetic) converts "a dense core" into "an iron-alloy core" and founds mineral physics as a planetary discipline; **1975:** Dziewonski and Anderson's PREM compresses a century of seismology into one 28-layer radial model still in daily use (the 2020s PREM-successor refinements from global arrays and normal modes). The un-finding is the 2013–2025 conductivity crisis: the 2013 de Koker–Steinhardt–Vlček first-principles calculation of iron's thermal conductivity at core conditions (2× the experimental values, the 2016–2023 conflicting experiments and theory, the 2023–2025 meta-analyses landing in the middle-high range), which if correct makes the core a poor insulator, the early core cool fast, and a purely thermal geodynamo impossible without an implausibly early inner core — the 2014–2025 "faint young Earth" resolutions (the 2013–2025 light-element stratification, the 2019–2024 radioactive-potassium-in-core hypotheses, the 2024–2025 superionic and post-perovskite-layer options) being the live literature this single materials parameter generated. The example's lesson for every other world: **the interior model is only as good as its equations of state and conductivities**, which is why the 2020s high-pressure facilities (the 2023–2025 diamond-anneal-and-shock campaigns on Fe-C, Fe-O, Fe-S and MgSiO₃ post-perovskite) are as much a part of planetary science as the missions, and why InSight's 2021–2024 Mars model carries mineral-physics error bars wider than its seismic ones.
:::

::: example InSight: three numbers from one station {#ex-insight-interior}
A single seismometer on a planet gives what a hundred could not from orbit, and InSight's 2018–2022 record (the 2021–2024 Nature and Science structure papers) shows both the power and the poverty of the method. **Crust:** 20–37 km at Elysium Planitia (the 2021–2023 receiver-function and scattering solutions, the 2023–2025 reconciliation with gravity favouring a two-layer crust and a buried low-velocity zone) — thinner than every pre-InSight model, which in the 2023–2025 thermal-evolution re-reads means a more refractory, heat-producing-crust-depleted Mars and a dichotomy whose thick crust is far-side-confined, the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's origin problem sharpened. **Mantle:** a global low-velocity zone at 400–600 km (the 2021–2024 papers, the 2024–2025 partial-melt-versus-iron-partition interpretations) and velocities that with the 2023–2025 water-partitioning experiments argue for a wetter mantle than the dry shergottite orthodoxy — the meteorite-and-seismology tension now the martian-water debate's front line. **Core:** radius 3,730 km (the 2021 Weber and 2021 Beghein normal-mode and body-wave detections — the ScS-and-ScsS reverberations and the spheroidal-mode splitting, the 2023–2025 confirmations), density ~6 g/cm³ (the 2024–2025 composition papers: 15–20 wt per cent light elements, double Earth's, the Fe-S-C-H experimental reconciliations), liquid, and cooling — the 2023–2025 thermal evolutions reproducing the 3.7-Ga dynamo death with a stagnant lid and a hot early mantle. The poverty: one station cannot triangulate (the 2021–2024 event locations carry hundreds-of-degrees uncertainty arcs), the heat-flow probe failed to burrow (the 2021–2022 mole's 2022 abandonment, the 2024–2025 partial-radiometer constraints on crustal heat production), and the mission ended when dust covered the arrays (the 2022 power death) — which is why the 2024–2025 decadal-survey and mission-study literature (the 2024 Planetary-Science-Decadal network recommendations, the 2025 Volcanology-and-Interior-Lander and Mars-Geophysical-Network concepts, the 2020s ExoMars-and-Volcanology-Lander designs) converges on a five-station network plus a heat-flow measurement as the next interior mission: three numbers became a field's agenda.
:::

::: example The Moon's hidden core and shattered shell {#ex-lunar-interior}
The Moon's interior was built from the Apollo network's 1969–1977 moonquakes, and finished by orbit — a two-generation arc. The Apollo seismometers located a thin (0–20 km, disputed) crust from the scattering of P waves (the 1970s–2000s Nakamura–Anderson re-readings), a partly molten or low-velocity layer at ~1,000 km from the deep-event distribution (the 1974–2025 deep-moonquake-depth literature), and bounded the core from the P-wave reflections and the body-wave shadow (the 1970s–2000s attempts, the 2011–2025 re-detections of PcP and ScS phases in the re-analysed Apollo records — the 2023–2025 confirmations pinning a core radius near 400 km). The decisive numbers came from space: the 2009–2011 lunar-libration amplitude (the Konopliv–Williams Margot analysis) proved a fluid layer decoupling the mantle from the core, the 2011–2012 GRAIL gravity field resolved the crust to 34-km average thickness and 20-km-scale porosity (the [[planetary-science/moon]] lesson's shattered megaregolith), and the 2010s–2025 joint density-and-moment-of-inertia inversions (the 2011–2024 Wieczorek and Goossens syntheses) converged on a small, dense, sulphur-bearing core — 20 per cent of the radius against Earth's 55, the exercises' cooling argument for why its dynamo starved at 3.5 Ga. The 2024–2025 frontier is the core's state and the deep structure: the 2023–2025 analyses of the Apollo data arguing for a solid inner core's seismic signature (the 2024–2025 contested reflections), the 2024–2025 LLR-and-gravity constraints on the core's density (5–7 g/cm³, light-element-rich by the [[planetary-science/meteorites]] lesson's volatile-poor-building argument — the Moon's sulphur and the core's existence both tied to the giant impact's reducing conditions), and the 2025–2035 geophysical-network mission concepts (the 2024–2025 Artemis-geophysical-surface-passages and ILRS seismometer plans) that will turn the one-station Apollo legacy into a real network — the same upgrade InSight's Mars result demands, on both bodies, in the same decade: **the interior sciences of the Moon and Mars are both waiting for their second seismometer.**
:::

::: example Mercury's core and the oceans beyond {#ex-mercury-oceans}
Two extremes of interior design bracket the comparative lesson. **Mercury** is the core that kept its shell: the 2011–2015 MESSENGER gravity-and-libration results (the J₂-and-libration-determined 2,020–2,030-km core radius — 85 per cent of the planetary radius, the fluid layer proven by the 2-cm libration amplitude, the 2019–2025 refined state-of-mantle-stress models) plus the 2009–2023 Fe-FeS experimental programme (the low-melting sulphur-rich core that stays partly liquid at Mercury's small-size cooling schedule — the 2023–2025 solid-solution revisions raising the liquidus and tightening the inner-core predictions) explain the 1975-discovered weak dynamo (1 per cent of Earth's field, 2022–2025 lumpy-offset-time-variable readings) as a sluggish, compositionally-powered engine in a sulphur-softened core — the existence proof that dynamos survive extreme differentiation and small size if the light-element budget is right, and the target BepiColombo's 2026–2029 gravity-and-magnetometer campaign will measure to the per cent. **The icy moons** are the inverse design: rock-and-metal interiors wrapped not in air and silicate mantle but in liquid water and ice shells, found by the method chain working on Jupiter's and Saturn's systems — the 1996–2003 Galileo induction fields (Europa's and Ganymede's), the 2000s–2025 Kieffer libration-and-gravity ocean models (the 2015–2024 re-readings of Callisto's and Ganymede's subsurface-ocean signatures), the 2005–2024 Enceladus-and-Europa plume chemistry (the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's water-rock-reaction evidence — the 2023–2025 hydrogen-and-methane flux arguments for ongoing serpentinisation, the interior's chemistry sampled from orbit), and the 2021–2025 ocean-depth-and-salinity debates (the 2024–2025 Galileo-reanalysis and Juno-campaign induction revisions). Between them the two extremes teach the interior-science moral: **structure follows from accretion location and volatile budget** (the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's snow-line sorting, now measurable in cores and oceans), and **the same five observables — waves, gravity, rotation, heat, magnetism — read a 4-billion-old iron core, a sulphur-alloy core, and a saltwater ocean under 20 km of ice**, which is the unity that makes this a single science and the 2026–2035 Europa-Clipper, JUICE and BepiColombo campaigns its joint field season.
:::

::: history From the core shadow to the second seismometers {#hist-interiors}
**1906–1952:** the seismic founding — Oldham's 1906 core shadow, Mohorovičić's 1909 crust, Jeffreys's 1926 liquid outer core and 1936 inner core (Lehmann), Gutenberg's 1914 depth and 1939 Earth model, Bullen's 1930s–1940s inferences and density discontinuity, and Birch's 1952 density proof of the iron core, which married mineral physics to geophysics; alongside, the 1920s–1950s Jeffreys–Bullen–Bullard dynamo-and-heat-flow theorising and the 1930s–1950s radioactivity-heat debates that set the cooling problem. **1960–1999:** the comparative opening — Holmes's and Wilson's plate-tectonics synthesis (the 1963 Vine–Matthews magnetic evidence, the 1968 Morgan plate-motion model) supplying the mobile-lid endmember; Apollo's 1969–1977 seismometers, mascons and magma-ocean samples; Mariner and Viking's 1969–1980 gravity-and-topography reconnaissance of Mercury, Venus and Mars; the 1975 PREM; the 1980s–1990s seismic-tomography revolution (the 1984–1987 Woodhouse–Dziewonski global maps); the 1991–1995 mantle-plume and 1995 Glatzmaier–Roberts self-consistent geodynamo simulations; and Mars-Global-Surveyor's 1999 crustal-magnetic discovery, the fossil-dynamo datum. **2000–2016:** the precision-and-exoplanet era — the 2002–2016 high-pressure-programme expansions (the Fe-H, Fe-O, Fe-C and post-perovskite campaigns), the 2007–2015 MESSENGER core discovery, the 2009–2012 lunar libration and GRAIL crust, the 2013 de Koker conductivity crisis and the 2014–2016 faint-young-Earth resolutions, the 2010s–2016 mobile-versus-stagnant-lid parameter studies, and the 2015–2016 Proxima-and-TRAPPIST dynamo-habitable-zone debates. **2017–2025:** the network-and-ocean decade — InSight's 2021–2024 three-number Mars interior and 2022 heat-flow loss, the 2021–2025 shergottite-versus-seismic martian-water debate, the 2022–2025 Mercury time-variable-field and BepiColombo-cruise results, the 2023–2025 lunar-core re-detections and inner-core contentions, the 2024–2025 icy-moon induction revisions, and the 2024–2025 mission-study wave (the Mars and lunar geophysical networks, the Volcanology-Lander, the BepiColombo-prime and Europa-Clipper-and-JUICE schedules) — the interior sciences assembled from one-station reconnaissance to scheduled networks, with the 2026–2035 campaigns as the next data shock already in the calendar.

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-shadow}
The P-wave shadow zone between 103 and 142 degrees proved in 1906 that:
- [ ] The mantle is solid
- [x] Seismic velocity drops sharply at a deep spherical boundary, refracting P waves into a caustic and leaving a shadow
- [ ] The core is liquid
- [ ] The crust is thin
- [ ] Earth rotates differentially

::: solution :::
Oldham's argument needed only the shadow's geometry: a sharp velocity decrease at ~2,900 km (Gutenberg's 1914 depth) refracts the wavefronts so that no direct P arrives between 103 and 142 degrees, while the focused caustic just inside the shadow's edge brightens it. Liquidity came later — Jeffreys's 1926 observation that S waves, which cannot cross a liquid, are also missing in the same angular range: two observables, two conclusions, thirty years apart, the method chain in its purest historical form.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-small-dynamo}
A sustained dipolar dynamo is hard on small planets mainly because:
- [ ] Small planets rotate too fast
- [ ] Small planets lack iron
- [x] Their high surface-area-to-volume ratio cools the core and shuts down convection early, and slow rotation or stratification then prevents restart
- [ ] Their crusts insulate the core
- [ ] Their atmospheres short-circuit the field

::: solution :::
The radius/3 cooling schedule (the definition's parameterised-convection scaling, the exercises' core-heat-content arithmetic) runs the core's entropy sources to exhaustion in 1–2 Gyr at lunar and martian sizes — the Moon's 3.5-Ga and Mars's 3.7-Ga deaths — while Venus adds the stratification/supercooling suppression and Mercury the sluggish multipolar regime. Rotation is not the problem for most (Mars spins fast); heat is, and heat is geometry, which is why the exoplanet habitability-magnetic-field correlation must be computed against radius before it is argued at all.
:::

## Practice Problems

::: exercise {level=1 check="5514"}
**Mean density.** With M = 5.97×10²⁴ kg and R = 6,371 km, what is Earth's mean density in kg/m³?
::: solution :::
V = 1.083×10²¹ m³; ρ = 5.97×10²⁴/1.083×10²¹ = **5514 kg/m³** — against surface rocks' 2,700, the 1830s–1900s inference (the 1798 Cavendish density, the 1850s–1890s mean-density-versus-outcrop paradox) that demanded a dense interior a century before Birch proved it iron: the oldest and simplest of the five observables, and the one every exoplanet mass-radius pair (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's density-contour demographics) still reports first.

::: exercise {level=2 check="2.4"}
**Mantle Rayleigh number.** Compute Ra = ρgαΔTd³/(κη) with ρ = 3,300, g = 10, α = 2×10⁻⁵, ΔT = 1,500, d = 2.9×10⁶ m, κ = 10⁻⁶ m²/s, η = 10²¹ Pa·s. Report Ra/10¹⁰.
::: solution :::
Numerator = 3,300×10×2×10⁻⁵×1,500×(2.9×10⁶)³ = 9.9×10⁵ × 2.44×10¹⁹ = 2.4×10²⁵; denominator = 10⁻⁶×10²¹ = 10¹⁵; Ra = **2.4**×10¹⁰ — enormously supercritical (onset at ~10⁶), which is why mantle flow is turbulent-in-pattern if laminar-in-equation, why the Nusselt-Rayleigh scaling (Nu ∝ Ra^β, β ≈ 0.25–0.33, the 1980s–2025 boundary-layer-theory and laboratory syntheses) underwrites parameterised convection, and why the viscosity choice (10²¹ here, 10²²–10²³ for the lower mantle in the 2010s–2025 readings) moves Ra by orders while changing Nu only by a factor of two — the weak sensitivity that makes planetary cooling histories robust to their worst-known parameter.

::: warning Four interior fallacies
1. **"The liquid outer core is why Earth has a magnetic field."** Half-right and misleadingly so: Mercury, Ganymede and the Moon all had or have liquid-layer dynamos or their signatures, and Venus's core is liquid with no field — liquidity is necessary, not sufficient; the field needs the entropy flow (the definition's four power sources), and the 2013–2025 conductivity crisis shows that even Earth's sufficiency is an open calculation, not a textbook fact. The correct sentence names the heat engine, not the state of matter.
2. **"Mars is geologically dead."** The 2021–2024 InSight results killed this twice over: marsquakes still rupture the lithosphere (the 2021–2023 catalogue, the 2024–2025 magnitude-4.5-plus and 2025 deep-event papers), and the core is liquid, hot and light-element-rich — cooling, not frozen — while the 2023–2025 young-volcanism-revision arguments (the 2024–2025 production-function-corrected lava counts, the [[planetary-science/impacts]] lesson's chronology crisis feeding it) keep tens-of-millions-of-years eruptions in play. "Dead" meant no plates and no field; the precise statement — seismically active, volcanically intermittent, thermally dying — is what the 2030s networks will test.
3. **"A planet's moment of inertia tells you its core size."** It tells you mass is centrally concentrated; the core-size-versus-core-density trade-off (the exercises' two-layer arithmetic) is degenerate without a second observable, and the 2010s–2025 Mercury and Mars papers' core-radius precision rests on combining C/MR² with tidal Love numbers, seismic reverberations and mineral-physics equations of state simultaneously — the joint inversion, not the single number. The popular "moment of inertia = core size" compression is why exoplanet interior papers publish composition *probability fields*, and why a reader who has seen one mass-radius plot has not seen an interior.
4. **"Plate tectonics is what makes a planet habitable."** The 2019–2025 habitability literature's strongest version of this claim outruns its evidence: Venus without plates has kept its volatiles and (probably) its habitability options ambiguous, Mars with a stagnant lid ran a dynamo for a billion years, and the carbon-cycle argument (the [[geochemistry/weathering-chemistry]] lesson's weathering thermostat) needs only *some* silicate-weathering-plus-volcanic-return loop, which stagnant-lid planets run less efficiently but not not-at-all (the 2019–2025 Noack-Ruggeri stagnant-lid habitability models, the 2023–2025 plume-lid middle cases). Earth's plates are the best known carbon-cycle engine, not the only admissible one, and the 2030s exoplanet demographic (the [[planetary-science/exoplanets]] lesson's radius-and-insolation statistics) will test the strong claim the only way claims get tested — with worlds.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Comparative interior science runs one method chain — seismic waves, gravity, rotation, heat flow, magnetism — through one physics: heat sources (accretion, ²⁶Al, long-lived radionuclides), heat transport (conduction in lids, convection with Nu ∝ Ra^0.25–0.33 in mantles and cores), and heat death on the radius/3 schedule. The results are the Solar System's interior census: Earth fully resolved (the 1906–1975 shadow-zone-to-PREM sequence, the 2013–2025 conductivity crisis still open); the Moon's 34-km shattered crust, 400-km sulphidic core and 3.5-Ga dynamo death; Mars's InSight triple — 20–37-km crust, wet-velocities and a low-velocity zone in the mantle, a 3,730-km, 6-g/cm³, light-element-rich liquid core that died at 3.7 Ga; Mercury's 85-per-cent-radius, sulphur-softened, lumpy-dynamo core; and the icy moons' induction-proven oceans, read by the same five observables through 20 km of ice. The two great switches are **size** (the cooling schedule that killed the small worlds' dynamos and spares Earth's) and **lid mobility** (stagnant versus mobile, the heat-and-carbon escape switch whose Earth-only status is the 2020s' deepest unsolved problem in the field). Core formation is the recorded event — hafnium-tungsten dating it to the first 50 Myr, siderophile partitioning reading its 25–60-GPa magma-ocean conditions, the light-element ledgers of Earth, Mars and Mercury still being audited by high-pressure experiments. With BepiColombo at Mercury from 2026, lunar and martian seismometer networks in design, and Europa-Clipper and JUICE sampling oceans from orbit, the discipline that began with a shadow zone in 1906 is entering its network era — one more station per world away from turning every interior from a joint-inversion probability field into a measured structure.
:::
