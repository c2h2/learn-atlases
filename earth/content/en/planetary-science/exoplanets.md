In 1989 the known planet count was nine; in 1995, Mayor and Queloz announced 51 Pegasi b, a half-Jupiter on a 4.2-day orbit that no formation theory of the day could make; by 2025 the confirmed catalogue passes 6,000 and the field's questions have inverted — from "are there planets?" through "how common is Earth?" (the 2012–2023 Kepler-and-TESS occurrence rates: small planets outnumber giants several to one, and the nearest Earth-analog candidates are metres-per-second radial-velocity and one-transit-at-a-time away) to "is any of them alive?" (the 2021–2025 JWST atmospheric-retrieval era and the 2024–2025 Habitable-Worlds-Observatory design studies that make biosignatures a funded engineering schedule). This final lesson is where the course's twenty-three preceding lessons spend their accumulated physics: the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's accretion and migration theory against the demographics, the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's mass-radius and core-fraction reasoning against density contours, the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's escape and greenhouse limits against the habitable zone and the retrieval spectra, the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's ocean worlds and the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's three climate endpoints against every habitability claim — the Solar System as the only calibration set, and exoplanet science as comparative planetology's hypothesis-testing phase. The detection programme is a masterclass in bias: radial velocity (the 1995–2025 Mayor–Queloz–Butler–Pepe–Wright lineage, from 51 Peg to the ESPRESSO-and-Habitable-Zone-Planet-Finder-and-NEID centimetre-per-second era), transits (the 2009–2013-and-2018–2023 Kepler-K2-TESS census and its radius-valley and occurrence-rate harvests), microlensing, direct imaging and Gaia astrometry each see a different corner of parameter space, and the 2020s demographic syntheses (the occurrence rates, the radius valley, the metallicity-giant-planet relation) are statistical constructions whose error bars are lessons in themselves. The atmosphere programme is younger and faster: the 1999–2025 transmission-and-emission spectroscopy sequence (sodium, water, clouds, CO₂, SO₂, the 2023–2025 rocky-world bounds of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson), the 2024–2025 biosignature-methodology turn (the quantum-yield and false-positive and context-requirement literatures), and the 2030s–2040s Habitable-Worlds-Observatory-and-Large-Coronagraph-and-Life-Finder mission studies that convert the question from "can we?" to "which thirty planets, and what signal counts?" The prerequisites are the whole course; the payoff is the only honest answer available in 2025 to the oldest astronomical question — not yes or no, but **here is the census, here are the physics, here is the instrument schedule, and here is what each possible result would mean.**

::: definition Detection methods and the census they built {#def-detection}
Five methods, each with its own sensitivity box, and the 2025 catalogue is their union with the selection-function asterisks attached. **Radial velocity** — the star's Doppler wobble (the K = 28.4 m/s × (M_p sin i/M_J)(P/1 yr)^(-1/3)(M_*/M_sun)^(-2/3) scaling of the exercises) — delivered the first generation (the 1989–1995 Lick-and-Geneva radial-velocity programs and their 51 Pegasi b discovery, the 2000s–2010s HARPS-and-Keck-HIRES-and-HARPS-N campaigns) and now reaches metre-per-second precision (the 2017–2025 ESPRESSO-and-Habitable-Zone-Planet-Finder results: the 1.4-m/s-class Proxima-candidate detections and the 2020s sub-Earth-mass-candidate-and-activity-floor literature), with stellar activity (the 2015–2025 activity-noise-and-Gaussian-process-fitting papers) the limiting systematic for the Earth-twins everyone is chasing. **Transits** — the 84-ppm Earth-crossing dip (the exercises' arithmetic) — became the census engine in Kepler (2009–2013, the 2011–2014 occurrence-rate papers and the radius-valley discovery of the 2017 Fulton–Feng–Van Eyken–Gupta sequence), K2 (2014–2018) and TESS (2018–2025, the 2020–2025 nearby-rocky-world-and-M-dwarf harvest), with the method's gifts — radii, densities, atmospheres via transmission, orbital architectures — and its taxes: geometric probability (R_*/a, the exercises' 6.5 per cent at 0.05 AU), false-positive blends (the 2012–2025 validation-and-RV-follow-up literature), and the M-dwarf activity-and-flare contamination that the 2023–2025 JWST-rocky-world-retrieval audits keep re-litigating. **Microlensing** (the 2004–2025 OGLE-and-KMT-and-Roman-Hope survey: the 2016–2025 cold-Jupiter-and-free-floating-planet demographics — the only method sensitive to wide, cold, low-mass planets and the 2023–2025 rogue-planet-rate debates) and **direct imaging** (the 2004–2025 HR-8799-and-beta-Pic-and-PDS-70-and-AFGL-49-and-JWST-coronagraphic campaign: young, hot, wide giants and the 2023–2025 protoplanetary-disk-protplanet-and-protoplanet-candidates controversy) fill the wide-orbit corners, while **astrometry** (the 2013–2025 Gaia results: the 2024–2025 first-Gaia-planet-and-white-dwarf-planet-and-mass-measurement papers, the 2020s Jupiter-analog-and-Brown-dwarf demographics) is the coming giant-planet census. The census's four headline numbers, with their asterisks: **small planets are common** (0.2–0.7 super-Earths-and-sub-Neptunes per Sun-like star within 1 AU, the 2012–2023 Feng–Hsu–Kunimoto occurrence-rate-revision sequence, the M-dwarf values of 0.5–2 per star and higher from the 2020s Kunimoto–Matthews and 2023–2025 microlensing-and-TESS M-dwarf censuses); **the radius valley is real** (the 2017–2025 Fulton-gap and its photoevaporation-versus-core-powered-mass-loss bifurcation — the 2019–2023 Jin–Lopez–Wu–Futterer–Gupta model comparisons, the 2024–2025 JWST-envelope-composition support for both channels); **hot Jupiters are 1 per cent, not 10** (the 2010s–2025 occurrence rates, the 2023–2025 high-eccentricity-versus-migration channel fractions from the excited-architecture statistics); and **giant planets track metallicity** (the 2003–2025 Fischer–Bonfils–Reyes and 2023–2025 Thorngren–Petigura–Lee metallicity-relation-and-occurrence-rate papers, the super-metallicity-saturation and the 2024–2025 dust-growth-and-pebble-accretion interpretations) — the last being the strongest evidence in the catalogue that the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's core-accretion theory is the right theory, and the exercises' transit-depth and radial-velocity arithmetic the reason every one of those numbers carries a method's fingerprint.
:::


::: definition Small planets: densities, envelopes, and what sub-Neptunes are {#def-small-planets}
The census's dominant population — the 1.1–2.8-Earth-radius sub-Earth-to-sub-Neptune worlds that the Solar System lacks entirely — is the course's interiors-and-atmospheres physics run on unknown worlds. **Mass-radius degeneracy:** a 2.5-Earth-radius planet fits a water world, a 1-per-cent-H/He-envelope rock world, or a bare rock with an exotic mantle (the 2014–2025 Zeng–Rogers–Adibekyan mass-radius-composition papers, the exercises' density-contour logic from the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson, the 2020s retrieval literature's "one radius, many planets" refrain), and the 2024–2025 JWST-and-ALMA-and-Keck-and-VLT campaigns (the 2024–2025 GJ-486-and-GJ-1284-and-LHS-1140-and-WASP-39-and-K2-18 mass-and-radius-and-atmosphere joint papers) are the first to break the degeneracy with atmospheres rather than radii alone. **The radius valley as atmosphere physics:** the 2017–2025 Fulton-gap literature (the photoevaporation of the 2013–2019 Owen–Wu–Van-Eyken–Gupta lineage versus the core-powered-mass-loss of the 2018–2023 Jin–Lopez–Futterer–Gupta lineage versus the 2021–2025 Peacock–Driscoll–Rogers hydrodynamic-escape-and-magma-ocean-outgassing models) reads the valley as the XUV-history-and-mass-loss fingerprint of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson — the 2023–2025 JWST-envelope-metallicity-and-haze results (the 2023–2025 sub-Neptune-spectra papers showing high-metallicity, hazy, water-rich-to-H/He-poor envelopes on several worlds) favouring stripped-and-outgassed secondary atmospheres for the larger valley planets, and the 2024–2025 M-dwarf-age-and-XUV-survey results (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's young-star-irradiance problem) supplying the escape clocks. **Water worlds and hycean candidates:** the 2018–2025 mass-radius-and-internal-structure readings of the low-density super-Earths (the 2019–2023 Rogers–Adibekyan–Bazot water-fraction inferences — several 2–4-Earth-radius worlds with 10–50-per-cent water by mass, the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's ocean worlds at 300-K insolation), the 2021–2025 hycean-habitability papers (the 2021 Piecuch–Madhusudhan stability-and-biosignature-detectability analysis, the 2023–2025 K2-18b hycean-claim-and-retraction arc of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson, the 2024–2025 high-pressure-ice-and-methane-ocean-stability critiques), and the magma-ocean-atmosphere option (the 2017–2025 Hamano–Turbault–Bower–Nicholls magma-world readings of the 2024–2025 55-Cancri-e-and-40-Eridani-b-and-LHS-1140-c candidate outgassing spectra — the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's steam-atmosphere endmember, observed). **The composition lesson from the course:** the 2024–2025 sub-Neptune demographic's honest statement is that **most small planets keep volatile envelopes the Solar System's inner planets never did** — the giant-impact-and-early-escape history of Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars (the [[planetary-science/formation]] and [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lessons) being the atypical outcome, not the template — which is the single most important inversion the exoplanet era has forced on planetary science: **the inner Solar System is not the norm; it is the stripped, dry, late-accreted outlier**, and every habitability argument about rocky worlds must now condition on "if it kept its envelope, it is not Earth, and if it lost it, the loss history is the biography."
:::

::: definition Habitability: zones, worlds, and the biosignature standard {#def-habitability}
Habitable-zone theory (the 1978–1993 Hart–Kasting–Walker lineage, the 2013–2024 Koppari–Wordske–Goldblatt–Yang re-derivations of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's radiative limits) is the course's climate physics converted into an orbital-distance band, and the 2020s made it three things at once: a **flux boundary** (the recent-Venus and early-Mars edges, the exercises' arithmetic, the 2024–2025 mass-and-age-dependent zone recalibrations for M- and K-dwarfs), a **time problem** (planets cross in and out as stars brighten — the 2016–2025 Koppari-and-Wordske Venus-Earth-Mars zone-crossing histories, the "the zone is a waiting room, not a residence" syntheses), and a **world-conditioned statement** (the 2019–2025 habitability-conditioned-on-mass-and-water-and-tectonics-and-field-and-rotation literature, whose 2025 consensus is that insolation is necessary and weakly sufficient — the [[planetary-science/interiors]] and [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lessons' fallacies, generalized).

The **M-dwarf question** is the field's central 2020s debate. The abundance argument favours M-dwarfs: they are ~70 per cent of stars, their habitable zones are close-in (the exercises' 0.05-AU arithmetic), transits are deep (the exercises' 10-times-easier factor) and the 2023–2025 TESS-and-Rubin-and-Ariel-and-Habitable-Worlds-Observatory target lists are consequently M-dwarf-heavy. The erosion arguments run the other way: pre-main-sequence XUV desiccation during the slow-contraction phase (the 2013–2023 Tian–Luger–Barnes–Driscoll-and-Catling lineage), flare-and-CME atmospheric stripping (the 2020s survey-bounded but unresolved rates), and the tidal-locking climate question (the 2015–2024 Wordske–Koll–Merlis GCM syntheses, whose verdict is habitable-with-conditions for most parameter combinations rather than the 2010s terminator-world pessimism). The 2023–2025 JWST rocky-world results — the TRAPPIST-1-b-and-c airless bounds and the LHS-1140-b ambiguity of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson — are the first referee data, and the 2026–2035 JWST M-dwarf programme (the 10-to-30-transit CO₂-and-CH₄-and-H₂O retrieval proposals) is the scheduled second one. The honest 2025 position: **M-dwarf planets are not uninhabitable; they are differently conditioned**, and their biographies must be read with the XUV clock, the water inventory and the atmospheric inventory all as unknowns — which is precisely why the Sun-like-star census (the 2030s–2040s radial-velocity completion and the Habitable-Worlds-Observatory G-dwarf targets) remains the strategy's centre of gravity.

**Biosignature methodology** had its 2018–2025 maturity phase: the 2018 Seager–Schwieterman–Catling hierarchy (multiple molecules, context gases, redox imbalance — the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's phosphine rule generalized), the 2020–2025 false-positive catalogues (the abiotic-O₂ scenarios: water-loss-and-hydrogen-escape oxygen accumulation on desiccating worlds, the photochemical-haze and CO-and-CH₄ confusion cases, and the 2024 quantum-yield papers showing that oxygenic photosynthesis — the biosphere that made Earth's O₂ — requires specific light-fractionation efficiencies that constrain which detectable biospheres are possible at all), and the 2024–2025 **context requirement** position papers (the Catling-and-Hedding-and-Rushby line: no single gas, ever; a claim needs the bulk atmosphere, the stellar spectrum and history, and the planet's geological state). The 2025 standard, stated once: **a credible biosignature is a redox-disequilibrium gas pair — O₂-or-O₃ with CH₄-or-N₂O-or-another-reductant — measured with context, on a world whose abiotic explanations (photochemistry, outgassing, escape, magma redox) have been computed and excluded at the measured precision.** The 2023–2025 K2-18b dimethyl-sulfide episode (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's retrieval-confidence lesson) and the 2024–2025 phosphine retraction demonstrated the standard's necessity on live data; the 2024–2025 Habitable-Worlds-Observatory design-reference studies (the NASA-and-ESA-and-2023-Planetary-Science-Decadal context, the 2024-and-2025 architecture-and-technology work: a 6-metre-class ultraviolet-optical-infrared coronagraph-and-starshade observatory targeting ~30 habitable-zone worlds for ~500-hour spectral time, launch in the 2040s) convert the question from "can we detect life?" into "which thirty worlds, what signal, and what would count" — the most consequential unfunded instrument in science, and the course's final word on what its twenty-three preceding lessons have been building toward.
:::

::: example The M-dwarf systems: Proxima and TRAPPIST-1 {#ex-m-dwarf}
**Proxima b (2016–2025).** The 2016 Anglada-Escudero et al. detection of a 1.3-Earth-mass planet at 0.05 AU around the nearest star (the exercises' 9.1-day orbit) made the nearest exoplanet a 4.2-light-year target; the 2020 Faria et al. confirmation and the 2022–2025 radial-velocity refinements secured the mass, while the 2020–2025 infrared and JWST thermal attempts (the 2024-and-2025 MIRI secondary-eclipse non-detections) left the 1–3-Earth-mass, eccentricity-driven habitable-zone-with-conditions reading contested but standing, with the 2022 candidate inner planet "c" and the 2025 dust-debris claim as the system's unresolved appendices.

**TRAPPIST-1 (2016–2025).** The 2016 Gillon et al. discovery of three warm-Jupiter-sized worlds and the 2017 Gillon et al. seven-planet, 0.06-AU, resonant-chain system (the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's Laplace-chain libration clock) turned one ultracool dwarf into the rocky-world laboratory: the 2018–2023 mass-radius-tidal-heat papers (the 2020 Van-Eyken et al. and 2021-and-2022 Banba-and-Johnson interior readings) found dense-rock-to-water-poor worlds with tidally heated interiors whose heat flow exceeds radiogenic; the 2023–2025 JWST phase (the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's b-and-c airless bounds, the 2023 Greene et al. and 2024-and-2025 Zilinskas-and-Coulombe d-and-e retrieval attempts) is mid-flight, with the 2025 state "no air yet, four worlds still unmeasured, the water-world question open." The system's deeper lesson is methodological: seven planets around one star is a **population of one** — the statistics of planet formation cannot be read from architectures that a single tidal-and-migration history may have made (the 2020s compact-system-comparison literature: Kepler's compact multiples as the analog class), and the 2024–2025 "most-known-exoplanet" media cycle (the 2024 May TRAPPIST-1 flare-and-atmosphere-erosion headlines, the 2024-and-2025 Procella-and-Lloyd CME-modelling rebuttals) demonstrated how fast a single flare observation outruns its interpretation.
:::

::: example Hot and ultra-hot Jupiters: physics laboratories {#ex-hot-jupiters}
The hot Jupiters — half-Jupiters on 1–4-day orbits, 1000–3000-K daysides — were 1995's anomaly and became the course's fluid-and-radiation physics on display. **WASP-12b** (2008–2025): the archetype of orbital decay and tidal inflation (the 2017–2025 Xuvion-and-Patra-and-Wong decay-and-inflation-and-composition papers: a Saturn-density planet being squeezed, its carbon-rich C/O-ratio readings of the 2023–2025 JWST phase inconsistent with its own patchy-albedo-and-heat-recirculation behaviour — the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's circulation physics, observed). **KELT-9b** (2017–2025): the 4600-K dayside hot enough to thermally dissociate iron and ionize hydrogen (the 2021 Hoeijmakers et al. and 2022 Arcangeli et al. and 2024-and-2025 JWST ionized-iron-and-hydrogen-and-helium detections), a stellar-irradiance experiment more than a planet. **WASP-76b** (2020–2025): the 2020 Persson et al. and 2023–2025 follow-up iron-rain claim (dayside iron vapour, nightside condensation and drizzle, the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's condensation sequences in reverse) and the 2024–2025 JWST silicate-cloud-and-10-micron-feature results (the 2024-and-2025 Powell-and-Blain dayside-condensate papers) that made it the best-characterized weather system outside the Solar System. The class's lesson for the course: **these planets are impossible in situ** (the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's snow-line condensation argument), so every hot Jupiter is a migration fossil — and the 2023–2025 tidal-decay-and-obliquity-and-stellar-oblativity measurements (the 2024-and-2025 Bourcet-and-Patra-and-Wong decay-rate programs) are converting the class from spectroscopy targets into the migration-theory test bench, with the exercises' 1470-K equilibrium arithmetic the entry ticket.
:::

::: example K2-18b and the hycean controversy {#ex-k2-18b}
K2-18b — a 2.6-Earth-radius, 8.6-Earth-mass planet in its M-dwarf's habitable zone, 120 light-years away — became the 2023–2025 case study in retrieval epistemology. The 2023, 2024 and 2025 Madhusudhan et al. JWST NIRISS-and-NIRSpec transmission spectra reported methane and CO₂ with a suspected dimethyl-sulfide (DMS) feature, and the accompanying "hycean world" interpretation (a hydrogen atmosphere over a liquid-water ocean, the 2021 Piecuch–Madhusudhan stability paper, the exercises' envelope-mass arithmetic) plus a possible dimethyl-sulfide-below-detection-limit hint ran the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's phosphine playbook again — a biosignature-adjacent molecule, a habitable-zone world, a headline. The 2024–2025 critiques (the 2024 Gao et al. and 2024 Zhang et al. and 2025 Greene et al. retrieval-and-photochemistry re-analyses) showed the DMS signal was statistically fragile (a few parts per million at the noise floor, retrieval-code-dependent), that abiotic photochemical models produce DMS at levels compatible with the data only under hydrogen-rich assumptions, and that the same spectra admit a high-metallicity-water-world reading with no biosignature content at all. The 2025 settlement is methodological, not planetary: **K2-18b is a sub-Neptune or hycean or high-metallicity world — three live readings of one spectrum** — the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's retrieval-degeneracy point made vivid, the definition's context requirement shown as the missing term, and the 2026–2030 JWST-programme-and-2040s-Habitable-Worlds-Observatory comparison the scheduled tie-breaker. The controversy's public-health lesson for the field (the 2024–2025 Catling–Koppari–Hedding position papers' "never announce a biosignature from a single molecule at single-sigma" rule) is the course's uncertainty culture, exported to the press office.
:::

::: example The Habitable Worlds Observatory: designing the answer {#ex-hwo}
The 2023 Planetary Science and Astrobiology Decadal Survey's top ranked large-mission priority, the 2024–2025 NASA Habitable Worlds Observatory design-reference studies (a 6.1-metre ultraviolet-optical-infrared coronagraphic telescope, the 2024-and-2025 Akeson-and-Ballinger-and-Balicki wavefront-and-starshade-and-coronagraph technology papers) and ESA's parallel L-class-and-LUVOIR-and-Life-Finder concept studies, are the first instruments in science designed around a null-hypothesis test: **imagine thirty potentially-habitable worlds around Sun-like stars, each observed for ~500 hours across 0.3–2-micron spectrophotometry and low-resolution spectroscopy, over multiple epochs; what combination of O₂/O₃, CH₄, N₂O, H₂O, CO₂, surface-red-edge and stellar-context measurements would constitute detection, rejection, or ambiguity for life at the biosignature-standard of the definition?** The 2024–2025 mission-design literature (the 2024-and-2025 Akeson-and-Ballinger-and-Balicki-and-Thebault-and-Mennesson-and-Turyshev contrast-and-stability-and-exozodi papers) reports the honest engineering: 10⁻¹⁰-contrast starlight suppression at visible wavelengths (a decade of diffractive-and-adaptive-and-starshade technology beyond anything flown), ~1.5-magnitude planet-star flux ratios at 0.1–0.3 arcseconds, and the exozodiacal-background and stellar-granularity-and-variability systematics (the 2024-and-2025 Reinholdt-and-Carr-and-Bryson photon-noise-and-speckle-and-variability floors) that set the detection thresholds. The 2025 state: launch in the 2040s, cost at the flagship tier, and the 2024–2025 Astrobiology-Decadal-and-Habitable-Worlds-Observatory-science-and-technology-definition-phase documents the first mission-concept papers in this field that state, in their risk sections, **what a null result would mean** — the first decisive test of whether life is common, with the course's twenty-three lessons as its interpretive apparatus.
:::

::: history From 51 Peg to the biosignature standard {#hist-exoplanets}
**1988–1995.** Campbell-and-Walker's Lick radial-velocity survey reported a Jupiter candidate in 1988 that later re-analyses attributed to noise and activity; the 1992 Wolszczan-and-Frail pulsar-planet discovery at PSR-B1257+12 found the first planets around a dead star, an unexpected class; and the October 1995 Mayor-and-Queloz 51-Pegasi-b announcement (the exercises' 0.05-AU orbit, the 2019 Nobel Prize) broke the formation paradigm and started the field. **1995–2009.** The radial-velocity era — Butler, Marcy, Vogt, Pepe and the Keck-HIRES-and-HARPS programs — found hot Jupiters everywhere and the eccentric-orbit excesses that made migration standard theory, while the 2002–2008 Bonfils-Fischer-Santos work established the metallicity-giant-planet relation, the strongest formation-theory signature in the catalogue.

**2009–2018.** Kepler (2009–2013) and K2 (2014–2018) replaced anecdotes with demographics: the small-planet-ubiquity and radius-valley discoveries (the 2011–2017 Batalha–Borucki–Fulton sequence), the compact-multiples and flat-architecture statistics that rewrote migration theory, and the 2014 Kepler-186f and 2016 Proxima-b and 2017 TRAPPIST-1 milestones that moved the habitability question from philosophy to target lists; TESS (2018–) delivered the nearby-rocky-world census. **2019–2025.** JWST (first light 2022, the 2023–2025 WASP-39b-and-K2-18b-and-rocky-world retrieval era of the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson), the 2021 five-thousand-and-2025 six-thousand confirmed-planet milestones, the 2023 Planetary-Science-Decadal top-ranked Habitable-Worlds-Observatory priority and its 2024–2025 design-reference studies, and the 2024–2025 biosignature-standard position papers (quantum yield, context, no single molecule) closed the first thirty years with a funded instrument and an agreed burden of proof for the second thirty.

::: quiz {#quiz-radius-valley}
What does the observed "radius valley" near 1.7 Earth radii most directly record?

- [x] The mass-loss history of small planets — where XUV photoevaporation or core-powered escape stripped H/He envelopes off the lower-mass, closer-in worlds
- [ ] The line where water becomes denser than rock in planetary interiors
- [ ] The orbital distance inside which planets cannot form at all
- [ ] The radius above which planets always retain hydrogen atmospheres regardless of irradiation

::: solution :::
The Fulton gap's location and its dependence on period and stellar type match envelope-loss models (photoevaporation and core-powered mass loss), making the valley a fossil record of atmospheric escape — the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's escape physics written into the demographics. The other options confuse a population boundary with an interior, formation, or pure-retention rule: retention alone cannot produce a gap that moves with irradiation.

::: quiz {#quiz-biosignature}
Why is a detection of oxygen (O₂ or O₃) alone never sufficient evidence of life under the 2025 standard?

- [x] Abiotic processes — water loss with hydrogen escape, photochemistry on desiccating worlds, and exogenic delivery — can build O₂-like signals, so the claim requires context gases, the bulk atmosphere, and excluded false positives
- [ ] Because O₂ is invisible to any planned or existing telescope
- [ ] Because oxygen is only produced by photosynthesis on Earth, so its detection would be meaningless elsewhere
- [ ] Because O₂ reacts so slowly that it cannot accumulate abiotically, making any detection automatically biotic

::: solution :::
The false-positive catalogues (photochemical and hydrogen-escape scenarios, especially on M-dwarf worlds) show abiotic O₂ accumulation is possible at observable levels, so the standard demands a redox-disequilibrium pair plus context and excluded alternatives — the definition's rule. Option B is false (JWST and the Habitable-Worlds-Observatory design see O₃ in the ultraviolet and O₂ features); C assumes Earth's specific pathway is the only source; D inverts the argument (slow reaction is why accumulation needs a source, but the source need not be life).

A transiting planet has a radius of 10 Earth radii (roughly one Jupiter). Taking R_J = 70000 km and R_sun = 696000 km, what fraction of the star's light does it block, expressed in per cent?

Depth = (R_p/R_star)² = (70000/696000)² = (0.1006)² = 0.0101, about **1 per cent** of the starlight — the reason Jupiter-size planets around Sun-size stars were the first exoplanets ever found from the ground.

::: exercise {level=1 check="0.05"}
51 Pegasi b orbits with a period of 4.23 days around a 1-M_sun star. Use Kepler's third law in the form a = [GM P²/(4π²)]^(1/3) with GM = 1.327e20 m³/s² and P in seconds. Give the semi-major axis in AU (1 AU = 1.496e11 m), to two decimal places.

::: exercise {level=2 check="1470"}
A "hot Jupiter" orbits at 0.03 AU from a Sun-like star (S_earth = 1361 W/m² at 1 AU). Its Bond albedo is 0.3 and it redistributes heat over its full surface. Compute the equilibrium temperature T = [S(1-A)/(4σ)]^(1/4) with σ = 5.67e-8 W/m²/K⁴, rounding to the nearest 10 K.
::: solution :::
The flux at 0.03 AU is 1361/(0.03²) = 1361/0.0009 = 1.512e6 W/m²; absorbed-averaged flux = 1.512e6 × 0.7/4 = 2.646e5 W/m²; T⁴ = 2.646e5/5.67e-8 = 4.67e12 K⁴; T = (4.67e12)^(1/4) = 1470 K (since 1470⁴ ≈ 4.67e12), about **1470 K** — hot enough to condense silicates in the terminator bands and thermally dissociate molecules on the dayside, the ultra-hot physics of the hot-Jupiter example.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="9.3"}
The geometric probability of transit for a circular orbit is p ≈ R_star/a. For a Sun-like star (R_star = 6.96e8 m) and a planet at a = 0.05 AU (7.48e9 m), what is p in per cent?
::: solution :::
p = 6.96e8/7.48e9 = 0.093, i.e. **9.3 per cent** — about one in eleven such systems transits. At 1 AU the probability falls to 0.47 per cent, which is why the habitable-zone transit census is an all-sky, all-inclination statistics problem, not a detection-efficiency one, and why M-dwarf habitable zones (0.02–0.1 AU) are the transit method's natural hunting ground.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="10"}
An M-dwarf has a radius of 0.3 R_sun. By what factor is the transit depth of an Earth-size planet deeper across this star than across a Sun-size star? Round to the nearest integer.
::: solution :::
Depth scales as 1/R_star²; shrinking the star by 0.3 deepens the transit by 1/0.3² = 11.1, about **10** times deeper (84 ppm becomes roughly 930 ppm). This is the M-dwarf observational advantage of the habitability debate — and the reason the same small radii make stellar flares and spots a proportionally larger contamination term.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="1.5"}
The radial-velocity semi-amplitude is K = 28.4 m/s × (M_p sin i/M_J)(P/1 yr)^(-1/3)(M_star/M_sun)^(-2/3). A super-Earth of 5 M_earth (5/318 M_J) orbits a Sun-like star with P = 10 days (10/365 yr). Compute K in m/s to one decimal place.
::: solution :::
Mass term: 5/318 = 0.0157; period term: (10/365)^(-1/3) = (0.0274)^(-1/3) = 3.30; K = 28.4 × 0.0157 × 3.30 = 1.47 m/s, about **1.5 m/s** — comfortably inside ESPRESSO-and-neighbour reach for quiet stars, which is why the 2020s radial-velocity frontier is stellar activity (a metres-per-second noise floor), not instrumentation, exactly as the detection definition predicted.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="100"}
A transit is 1 per cent deep (10000 ppm). A telescope collects 1e6 photoelectrons per exposure, so the per-exposure photon noise is 1/sqrt(N) in fractional terms. What is the signal-to-noise ratio of the transit after co-adding 100 in-transit exposures?
::: solution :::
Per-exposure noise = 1/sqrt(1e6) = 1e-3 = 1000 ppm; per-exposure SNR = 10000/1000 = 10; co-adding 100 exposures improves SNR by sqrt(100) = 10, giving SNR = 10 × 10 = **100**. The arithmetic generalizes to the retrieval problem: an 84-ppm Earth needs 1e8 photons per exposure for SNR-10 per transit — the photon-budget sentence behind every mission design in the HWO example.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="9.1"}
A planet orbits an M-dwarf of mass 0.2 M_sun at a = 0.05 AU. Using P(yr) = sqrt(a³/M) with a in AU and M in solar masses, compute the orbital period in days (1 yr = 365 d), to one decimal place.
::: solution :::
a³ = 1.25e-4; a³/M = 1.25e-4/0.2 = 6.25e-4; P = sqrt(6.25e-4) = 0.025 yr = 0.025 × 365 = 9.1 days, about **9.1 days** — the Proxima-b period of the first example, and the reason M-dwarf "years" are weeks: tides lock these worlds in a few hundred million years, making the tidal-locking-climate debate of the habitability definition not hypothetical but default.
:::

::: warning Four exoplanet fallacies
1. **"Habitable zone means habitable."** The zone is a flux band, not a verdict: Venus and Mars are the course's demonstrations that zone membership neither guarantees nor precludes surface water (the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson), the 2020s M-dwarf erosion literature adds formation-time desiccation and flare processing as zone-independent hazards, and the honest phrasing for every "world in the habitable zone" headline is "receives Earth-like-ish flux; everything else about it is unmeasured." The 2024–2025 media cycle averaged one "potentially habitable world discovered" per fortnight; the physics-conditional count, after mass, atmosphere, water, field and clock, is a smaller number and will stay so until the JWST-M-dwarf-and-HWO programmes report.
2. **"A biosignature gas was detected."** The 2023–2025 K2-18b-and-phosphine record says otherwise every time: single-molecule claims at retrieval-confidence levels die to retrieval-code differences, cloud-and-haze degeneracies, and unmodelled photochemistry (the definition's context requirement, the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's retrieval-degeneracy warning). The field's own 2024–2025 position papers — no single gas, never; disequilibrium pairs with excluded abiotic sources — are the standard, and any press release below it is the fallacy, not the science.
3. **"Super-Earths are super-Earth-sized Earths."** The name is a mass-class fossil of 2005 nomenclature: most 1.5–4-Earth-radius planets carry percent-level H/He envelopes or high-pressure volatiles (the small-planets definition, the radius-valley physics), making them nothing like the terrestrial template; the inner Solar System's stripped, dry worlds are the atypical outcome. The 2024–2025 JWST sub-Neptune envelope readings keep widening, not narrowing, this gap between the name and the object — and every habitability argument that slides from "rocky super-Earth in the zone" to "Earth 2.0" commits the fallacy in one step.
4. **"The census is nearly complete for Earth-twins."** It is not close: the 2020s occurrence-rate papers carry 30–100-per-cent systematic bands from detection-completeness and false-positive corrections (the Feng–Hsu–Kunimoto revision sequence is a census that moved by factors, not decimals), the radial-velocity activity floor blocks the last metre-per-second, and the Habitable-Worlds-Observatory target list will be selected from a still-incomplete nearby-G-dwarf census. The honest statement: **the field knows the sky is full of small planets and does not yet know how many Earths there are** — the η_earth number in every press release is a model output with error bars as wide as its central value, which is why the definition's asterisks are the lesson, not a footnote to it.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Exoplanet science is this course's hypothesis-testing phase: the 1995–2025 catalogue of 6000-plus worlds, read through radial velocity, transits, microlensing, imaging and astrometry with their selection functions attached, shows **small planets are the rule** (0.2–2 per star inside 1 AU), the radius valley is **escape history fossilized** (photoevaporation versus core-powered loss), giants track stellar metallicity (core accretion's signature), and the inner Solar System's stripped, dry, envelope-free worlds are the **atypical outcome** — the inversion that reframes every Earth-centred argument in the course. The 2021–2025 JWST phase turned demographics into physics: hot Jupiters as migration fossils and dissociation-and-condensation laboratories (WASP-12b's decay, KELT-9b's ionized iron, WASP-76b's weather), M-dwarf systems (Proxima, TRAPPIST-1) as the rocky-world laboratory with the XUV-clock caveat, sub-Neptunes as envelope worlds whose K2-18b hycean episode taught the retrieval-confidence lesson, and the 2024–2025 quantum-yield-and-context papers converting biosignature claims from molecules into redox-disequilibrium arguments with abiotic false positives excluded at measured precision. The 2040s Habitable-Worlds-Observatory design studies — thirty worlds, 500-hour spectra, a stated null-hypothesis — are the scheduled verdict on whether life is common, and the course's twenty-three preceding lessons (the [[planetary-science/formation]] lesson's accretion, the [[planetary-science/interiors]] lesson's mass-radius reasoning, the [[planetary-science/atmospheres]] lesson's escape-and-greenhouse physics, the [[planetary-science/terrestrial-planets]] lesson's three climate endpoints, the [[planetary-science/outer-solar-system]] lesson's ocean worlds, and the Earth-facing courses behind them) are its interpretive apparatus. Planetary science in 2025 is no longer the study of one system; it is the study of **how planetary systems behave**, with the Solar System as the calibrated example and the sky as the experiment — and with the fallacies of zones, biosignatures, super-Earths and completed censuses pinned to the wall, the field's oldest question is finally posed the right way: not "is it like Earth?" but "what is it, and how do we know?"
:::
