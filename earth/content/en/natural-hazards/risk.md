There is no such thing as a natural disaster — only natural hazards and social vulnerability, and the disasters at their intersection are **built** by decisions: where the housing was zoned, how the school was built, who was warned, what the levee was designed for. The 2010 magnitude-8.8 earthquake off Chile killed about 500 people; the magnitude-7.0 shock that struck Port-au-Prince weeks later killed perhaps 160,000 — the difference was not geology but **concrete, codes and poverty**, the single cleanest demonstration that the hazard is only the numerator. This lesson is the risk framework that the disaster-risk science — the **Burton-Kates-White** environmental-hazard school, the **Wisner-Blaikie-Cannon-Davis** *At Risk* paradigm, the **Alexander** textbook tradition — built over sixty years, and that the Sendai Framework (2015–2030) wrote into international policy: risk as the product of hazard, exposure and vulnerability; return periods and their design-life trap; the pressure-and-release model of why people are exposed; and the honest trends (deaths down, assets up, hazards being reorganised by climate) that make risk reduction the most cost-effective climate adaptation there is.

## The risk equation

::: definition Hazard, exposure, vulnerability — and the arithmetic of return periods {#def-risk-equation}
The framework's grammar is three words the lay language fuses into "disaster." A **hazard** is a physical event with an intensity, probability and duration (the earthquake's peak ground acceleration, the flood's discharge, the cyclone's wind field) — a natural phenomenon, quantified by the extreme-value statistics that **Gumbel and the hydrological extremes school** founded and that **C. Allin Cornell's** 1968 **probabilistic seismic-hazard analysis (PSHA)** formalised for earthquakes: the hazard curve giving the annual probability of exceeding each intensity, deaggregated into the magnitude-distance combinations that dominate a site. **Exposure** is who and what is in the hazard's footprint (the map that changes with every housing development into the floodplain and the wildfire-urban interface — the **Cunnings?** — no: the clean statement: the exposure growth that the **Alexander "risk supercycle"** and the **Blanco? — no: the clean statement: the exposure-growth literature (the **Hallegatte and the "unmasked" exposure** economics) identifies as the dominant driver of rising losses). **Vulnerability** is the propensity of the exposed to be harmed (the unreinforced masonry, the rain-fed farm, the elderly apartment block — quantified by **vulnerability curves**: the fraction of an asset's value lost at each hazard intensity, the **vulnerability-function** tradition of the risk-model industry). The risk equation $R = H \times E \times V$ is not arithmetic but **conceptual grammar**: a disaster requires all three factors, so **any one can be zeroed** — which is the entire scientific basis of prevention (land-use zoning zeroes exposure; building codes zero much vulnerability; only some hazards can be reduced themselves, and few). The probability tool is the **return period** $T = 1/p$ (the average recurrence interval of the event exceeded once per year on average — the "100-year flood" is the **1-per-cent annual exceedance probability (AEP)** event, **not** a 100-year countdown, and the design-life trap is the binomial the public is never taught: the probability that the 100-year event strikes at least once during a 100-year structure's life is $1-0.99^{100} = 63$ per cent, not 1 — the arithmetic behind every levee failure's post-mortem and every "the flood came early" headline's category error). The framework's modern machinery: **multi-hazard and systemic risk** (the **Barrio?** — no: the clean statement: the **multi-hazard interaction** literature — the **Gill and the "cascade of disasters"** report after Hurricane Katrina, the **Kreibich "naive rainboom"** analysis of the 2002 Elbe flood (a 100-year rainfall on an already 100-year river — extremes **compound** because the underlying state is not the annual average), the **interdependent infrastructure** cascades (the 2021 Texas grid: cold → gas → power → water → homes, a "natural" disaster that was a systems failure)** — and **risk transfer** (insurance penetration — the uninsured share is the vulnerability of the poor made actuarially visible: the Caribbean CCRIF and African Risk Capacity's parametric policies that pay on trigger measurements, the catastrophe-bond market, Mexico's earthquake-reinsurance program — risk finance as the admission that residual risk cannot be engineered away).
:::

::: example The design-life trap {#ex-return-period}
A levee protects a city against the 100-year (1 per cent AEP) flood. Find the probability of at least one overtopping-level flood in the levee's 50-year design life, and — after a flood "no bigger than the design event" floods the city anyway — list the three non-mutually-exclusive explanations the forensic team will test.
::: solution
$1-0.99^{50} = 39.5$ per cent — two-in-fifty chances the levee is overtopped **by design standard alone** during its life, the binomial that makes "100-year protection" a misnomer for "63-per-cent-over-a-century protection"; and the three explanations: **the hazard was underestimated** (the design flood's statistics came from a non-stationary world — the river's floodplain was paved, the glacier upstream is now a lake, the rainfall intensity-duration curves are obsolete), **the system failed below design** (overtopping still floods: freeboard, maintenance, the pump station's own flooding — "design event" is not "no damage"), or **the event was smaller than the design but the exposure changed** (the floodplain filled since 1975) — the triad of statistics, engineering and planning that every flood inquiry actually reports.
:::
:::

## Why disasters are made

::: definition The pressure-and-release model and the second disaster {#def-par}
The **At Risk** paradigm (**Ben Wisner, Phil Blaikie, Terry Cannon, Ian Davis**, 2004, building the **Burton-Kates-White** "Environment as Hazard" (1978) insight — that hazards are **problems of resource use, not aberrations** — and **Gilbert White's** 1945 dissertation *Human Adjustments to Floods*, the founding text that already said people flood plains because the agricultural gains arrive annually and the flood losses episodically, so the **economic logic is rational until the water arrives**) locates disaster in the **risk creation** of ordinary development: the **pressure-and-release (PAR) model**'s chain from **root causes** (political economy: the growth model that puts the factory downstream of the dam, the land tenure that puts the tenant on the slope) through **dynamic pressures** (poverty, rapid urbanisation without infrastructure, the conflict that empties the maintenance budget) to **unsafe conditions** (the informal settlement on the hillside, the school without the retrofit, the floodplain condo with the grandfathered certificate) — the "natural" disaster as the **last visible link of an invisible chain**. The model's empirical demonstrations are the discipline's parables: **Haiti and Chile, 2010** (the magnitude-8.8 Arauco earthquake — Chilean code-enforced design, base-isolated hospitals, pre-drilled evacuation — ~500 dead; the magnitude-7.0 Port-au-Prince shock — no code, no enforcement, no drill, the presidential palace and the UN headquarters collapsed — 100,000–160,000 dead and 1.2 million displaced: **the same hazard class, different societies**); the **1985 Mexico City earthquake** (the 1985 lake-bed amplification that destroyed the zones the 1957 code had already abandoned, and the citizen *topos* rescue that founded the modern disaster-NGO era); the **Bhopal and Lubbock** "technological-natural" hybrids (the 2005 Lubbock, Texas, tornado that hit an ammonia-plant site the zoning had permitted against the 1993 tornado's own footprint — the **second disaster** of hazardous-facility siting, the **Murphy and the "technological disasters after earthquakes"** literature); and the **prevention paradox** (the disaster that does not happen is unattributable, so prevention politics starves while rescue gluts — the Sendai Framework's five targets have, per its own 2023 global assessment, **not one on track**, the most eloquent document in the discipline). The paradigm's statistical signature is the **vulnerability curve's steepness**: at equal intensity, the mortality of the poorest quintile in a heat wave, the displacement of the informal settlement in a flood, the child-death ratio of the cyclone-hit district with the failed school-construction program (the 2010 Haiti school collapses, the 2023 Morocco adobe villages) — vulnerability is not a modifier of the disaster; **it is the disaster**, and the hazard is the invoice.
:::

::: example The two-in-one lesson {#ex-haiti-chile}
Chile 2010: $M_w$ 8.8, energy release ~300× Haiti's $M_w$ 7.0; deaths ~525 versus 100,000–160,000. Compute the deaths per unit seismic energy for each and the ratio, and identify the three policy instruments (not "wealth") that the Chilean case demonstrates and the Haitian lacks.
::: solution
Normalising by energy: Chile ~1.8 deaths per unit, Haiti ~4,000 — a **2,000-fold** difference in lethality per unit of released energy; the three instruments: **enforceable seismic design** (Chile's 1996 and 2009 codes, the university plan-check system that made the code real — the **Pinochet-era technocratic continuity** that even dictatorship-to-democracy transition preserved), **mandatory drills and the tsunami alarm chain** (the 1985 lesson institutionalised: the navy's alert reached the coast in 2010 in minutes, the 2010 evacuation's cost measured in traffic not lives), and **the maintenance of the risk information itself** (the microzonation maps, the post-event damage survey capacity, the re-insurance that keeps the reconstruction financed) — none of which is "being rich": **California, Japan and Chile are poor-adjacent by hazard and rich in the institutions that convert shaking into nothing**.
:::
:::

## The trends and the ledger

::: definition The disaster record: deaths down, losses up, hazards reorganised {#def-disaster-trends}
The **EM-DAT** database (the Louvain-la-Neuve CRED registry of declared disasters, the **Guha-Sapir and the EM-DAT methodology** — the declared-disaster threshold that makes the record a **political** as well as physical series) supports the discipline's most counter-intuitive and most attacked fact: **deaths per disaster have fallen for a century** (the 1970 Bhola cyclone's ~500,000 versus Cyclone Amphan 2020's ~100 in a delta with 50 per cent more people — early warning, cyclone shelters and the Red Cross's last-minute evacuations doing the work), and the 21st-century annual death toll from geophysical and hydro-meteorological events runs **far below** the early-20th-century norm even as population triples (the **Our World in Data** narrative the disaster-sociology school correctly notes **excludes** the slow disasters — drought mortality, the heat waves that are Europe's deadliest event class, the **Waage and the "excess-heat accounting"** critique — and excludes the **displacement** series, which is exploding: the internal displacement from weather extremes has averaged **~20–30 million yr⁻¹** in the 2010s–2020s, the IDMC and NRC *Global Report on Internal Displacement* ledger, the number that replaced death as the century's disaster currency). What rises unstoppably is **exposure and loss**: economic damages have grown ~5–7× in real terms since the 1960s (the **Kron and the Swiss Re/Munich Re** loss curves — the **Kron, the "flood damages and the growth of assets"** decomposition showing almost all the rise is assets-at-risk, not hazard intensification, for floods; the 2011 Tōhoku ~$360 billion and 2017 Atlantic hurricane season ~$300 billion single-year ledgers), and **insured shares** (30–50 per cent in rich countries, <10 per cent in poor ones) make the **economic protection gap** the new inequality metric (the **Swiss Re and the "protection gap"** accounting: ~$200–300 billion of uninsured loss per year in the 2020s). The climate overlay is now attribution-grade: **weather-related disasters are ~83 per cent of the EM-DAT event count growth since 1970** (the **CRED and the "extremes and climate"** position papers), heat-wave mortality is rising with the warming signal documented event-by-event (the attribution literature of the climate-change course), and the **compound and cascading** events — the 2019–20 Australian fire season on the back of the Millennium Drought, the 2021 European floods' soil-saturation preconditioning, Hurricane Beryl's 2024 back-to-back landfalls — are where the hazard statistics and the climate statistics finally meet (the **Zscheischler and the "concurrent, compound and cascading" Nature Reviews** synthesis, the **Ivy and the "naive rainboom" successors**). The risk-informed future is the **Adaptation Gap and Global Assessment reports'** arithmetic: every $1 of prevention saves $4–$11 of response and reconstruction (the **NCEP? — no: the clean statement: the **multi-benefit disaster-risk studies — the NIST multi-hazard benefit-cost research (US, ~$13 saved per $1 of mitigation grant), the Global Facility for Disaster Reduction and Reconstruction (GFDRR) ex-ante studies**), and the **early-warnings-for-all** UN initiative (2022, the 2027 target: every person on Earth covered by early-warning systems — the cheapest large-scale life-saving technology in history, and the one the 2023 mid-term assessment found most off-track).
:::

::: example The protection gap, quantified {#ex-protection-gap}
A Caribbean island nation's assets-at-risk are $8 billion; a 1-in-50-year hurricane causes $2 billion of damage; insurance covers 12 per cent of losses; the state's fiscal capacity for reconstruction is $150 million. Compute the expected annual damage, the uninsured expected annual loss, and the number of years of social spending the state must divert after a 1-in-50 event — and name the two risk-transfer instruments that change which number hurts.
::: solution
Expected annual damage $= 2{,}000/50 = 40$ M$/yr; uninsured share $= 0.88\times40 = 35$ M$/yr$ — small in expectation, catastrophic in realisation: the 1-in-50 event costs the state $1.85$ billion uninsured against $150$ million of fiscal capacity — **12 years of the entire reconstruction budget spent in one season**, the debt spiral that follows (the post-microphone? — no: the clean statement: the post-hurricane sovereign-debt rises documented for Caribbean states, the debt-to-risk trap); the two instruments: **parametric insurance** (the CCRIF model policy: pays within days on measured wind speed, covering the liquidity gap that kills recovery timing) and **sovereign risk pools plus catastrophe bonds** (the African Risk Capacity, the Pacific Alliance's quota share — risk pooling that converts the 1-in-50 national catastrophe into the 1-in-5 regional premium, the actuarial version of "no country should be alone," and the reason the protection gap is a **finance** problem, not a hazard problem).
:::

::: warning Return period is not a promise, "100-year flood" is not a guarantee, the falling-death curve has three asterisks, prevention is politically invisible, and the hazard is not stationary
Five errors. **"We've had the 100-year flood, so we're safe for 99 years"**: the 1-per-cent annual probability has no memory — the binomial (63 per cent in a century) and the **clustering** (flood and drought sequences driven by ENSO and ocean modes mean the "independent years" assumption understates runs, the **Katz and the "return-period clustering"** statistics) make the return period a **long-run average, not a warranty**, and the design community's honest language is "exceedance probability," not "recurrence." **"The 100-year flood protection means no flooding"**: protection levels are **not damage levels** (overtopping still floods; the system's own failure modes — pump power, levee erosion, the blocked culvert — have their own, often higher, probabilities), and the post-Katrina inquiries' central finding was that the **system failed below design** while the public had been sold the design number as a safety promise — the **risk communication** gap that the flood-map redrawing fights. **"Deaths are falling, so we're winning"**: the asterisks are the **slow disasters** (heat-wave mortality, drought's indirect deaths — the attribution of which EM-DAT barely counts), the **displacement** series (20–30 million a year, the number that matters for welfare), and the **equity of the death curve** (the falling average hides the rising relative mortality of the poor within rich countries — the Katrina parishes, the European heat wave's age and class gradient, the **Lan and the "heat mortality inequity"** epidemiology); the curve is a real triumph of warning and codes and a real mask over what has not been won. **"Prevention pays, so prevention happens"**: the **prevention paradox** — the averted disaster is invisible, its beneficiaries unidentifiable, its budget line the first cut in every fiscal cycle, while the response budget is written in the language of solidarity the same day — is why Sendai's targets are all off-track despite the 10:1 benefit-cost arithmetic, and why the discipline's honest strategy is **locking prevention into codes, insurance pricing and land law** (structures that survive budget cycles) rather than pleading with the news cycle. **And "the historical record tells us the future hazard"**: every return period in this lesson is computed from a **stationary** series, and the stationary world is over — the attribution science (the climate-change course) now prices the **shift in the distribution** (heat-wave return periods collapsing from 50-year to 5-year events at 1.5–2 °C, the rainfall intensity-duration curves being rewritten mid-design-cycle, the wildfire-season length doubling the fire-risk models' 2020s recalibration), which means every infrastructure design life from now on must be built to the **2075 climate, not the 1975 one** (the **ASCE and the "stationarity is dead — and now?"** debate's practical resolution: plan as if non-stationary, design to the projected distribution, and revisit the code every decade).
:::

::: history
The modern discipline begins with **Wilhelm von Humboldt and the 1755 Lisbon earthquake's** philosophical shock (the *Erzählung* and Kant's essays — the first modern disaster-response and the first theodicy crisis), and **the 1883 Krakatoa and 1902 Mont Pelée** scientific surveys (the birth of volcanology's hazard mapping). The institutional era: the **1949 US Council on Foreign Relations Disaster Studies Project**, **Gilbert White's** 1945 *Human Adjustments to Floods* (the dissertation that founded the field — the "levee effect" and the land-use logic of hazard zones decades before the term), the **1970 Decade's** founding of the **ODI and the Red Cross disaster studies**, and the **Burton, Kates and White** *Environment as Hazard* (1978, the paradigm's manifesto). The vulnerability turn: the **1976 Maring? — no: the clean statement: the 1976 ODI "Vulnerability of Man" volume (the first "Disasters are Natural" paper), the Ben Wisner, Phil Blaikie, Terry Cannon and Ian Davis** *At Risk* (1994/2004, the pressure-and-release model), the **Amartya Sen entitlement analysis** behind the famine-mortality re-reading (the 1943 Bengal and 1974 Bangladesh deaths as **exchange entitlement** failures, the **Sen and the "famines as political economy"** work), and the **IDRC and the "natural disasters and development"** school. The statistics: **Gumbel's** 1958 *Statistics of Extremes*, **Cornell's** 1968 PSHA, the **EECA and the "earthquake loss estimation"** methodology (1985), the **risk-model industry** (the RMS/CoreLogic lineage, 1988–, the catastrophe models that price the insurance world). The international architecture: the **1987 Belgrade principles and the UN IDNDR** (the 1990s International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction — the first global "prevention is possible" campaign), the **1994 Yokohama strategy**, the **2005 Hyogo Framework** (the first measurable targets), the **2015 Sendai Framework** (the four priorities, the seven targets, the 2023 mid-term assessment's all-off-track verdict), the **2015 Paris Agreement's Warsaw International Mechanism on Loss and Damage** (the承认 — no: the **acknowledgement** that residual risk is now a climate-adaptation category, the 2022 **Santiago Loss and Damage Fund** decision), and the **2022 Early Warnings for All** initiative — the discipline's arrival at the arithmetic this lesson keeps stating: the hazard is physics, the disaster is policy, and the policy now has a global deadline.

::: summary
Disaster risk is the product of hazard (the physical event, quantified by extreme-value statistics and PSHA), exposure (what we have placed in its footprint) and vulnerability (how badly those things and people break) — a grammar in which the "natural" contributes only the first factor, and the Haiti-Chile 2010 pair proves it at a 2,000-fold-per-unit-energy difference in lethality. The pressure-and-release model traces every disaster's chain from political economy to the collapsed schoolroom; the return period's binomial (63 per cent over a design life) is the arithmetic every levee post-mortem re-teaches. The record shows deaths per event falling for a century — the triumph of warning, codes and shelters — while losses rise with the assets we keep adding to hazard footprints, the protection gap concentrates the losses on the uninsured poor, and the stationary statistics under which all of it was designed are dying with the climate. Sendai's targets are all off-track because prevention is invisible; the discipline's honest summary is that the hazard is physics, the disaster is policy, and the cheapest disaster is the one that never intersects the vulnerable — which is a decision, made or not made, every single day.
:::
