A tsunami is the ocean's answer to a vertical mistake: the seafloor drops or rises in minutes, the water column above it follows, and the resulting wave — a **wave of the whole depth**, not of the surface — crosses an ocean in hours with the energy of a freight train per kilometre of crest and the stealth of a rising tide. The discipline's hard curriculum is written in its events: **1755 Lisbon** (the first scientific tsunami survey, the three waves that drowned the harbour the earthquake had spared), **1883 Krakatoa** (36 m, 36,000 dead, the first volcanic-edifice collapse documented), **1896 Sanriku** (the "tsunami stones" of the villages that had survived — and the fire-setting hero Hamaguchi Goryō whose name the schools still teach), **1946 Aleutians** (the wave that crossed to Hawaii and founded the Pacific warning system), **1960 Chile** (the largest earthquake ever measured, its wave 10,000 km to Japan), **1993 Hokkaido** (32 m on Okushiri within 20 minutes — the warning that arrived with the wave), **2004 Sumatra** (227,000 dead in 90 minutes on coasts with no system at all), and **2011 Tōhoku** (the 405 m trace height at Ryōri, the 10 km inundation of the Sendai plain, the 19,700 dead, and the seawall question that every coastal nation now argues). This lesson is the physics of the wave, the geography of the warning, and the ethics of the 3-metre forecast that a village trusted.

## The physics of the whole-depth wave

::: definition Generation, propagation, run-up {#def-tsunami-physics}
**Generation** requires vertical displacement of the seafloor and a receptive water column: subduction megathrusts (the "tsunami earthquake" class of **Kanamori's** 1972 analysis — slow rupture, shallow slip, disproportionately large waves, the 1896 and 1946 events and the 2011 **Tohoku**'s trenchward-slip near the axis), normal-fault shocks (the 2018 **Palu**'s strike-slip-plus-rotational mechanism, the **Kubota and the "rotational fault"** analysis), volcanic edifice collapse and caldera processes (Krakatoa 1883, the 2022 **Hunga Tonga** — the first globally-instrumented submarine-caldera tsunami, its air-pressure-coupled wave ringing the planet at 300 km h⁻¹ for a day, the **Allanson and the "Hunga Tonga global tsunami"** analyses), submarine landslides (the 1929 Grand Banks cable-breakers, the 2018 **Anak Krakatau** flank-collapse wave that killed 430 on a holiday shore with no warning because the source was minutes-scale and unmonitored), and the extreme end-members: the **1958 Lituya Bay** rockslide (524 m run-up, the "fish-tail" seiche of a 30-million-m³ slide into a 500 m deep bay — the largest wave ever measured, and a warning about **local** sources that dwarf any open-ocean wave), and the impact-generated waves of the bolide and seafloor-volcano scenarios (the **Melosh and the "impact tsunami"** modelling — the Cretaceous-Palaeogene backwash deposits). **Propagation** is shallow-water physics on a planetary scale: speed $c=\sqrt{gh}$ (4,000 m abyssal depth → 198 m s⁻¹, 713 km h⁻¹ — an airliner's cruise, which is why a Chilean wave reaches Japan in 15 hours and a Sumatran wave reached Sri Lanka in ~90 minutes), wavelength 100–500 km and amplitude in the deep ocean **0.3–1 m** (invisible to ships — the "wave of the whole depth" that the high-seas fishing crews of 2011 who fished through the wave, unharmed and unaware), **Green's law** shoaling $a \propto h^{-1/4}$ (the 0.5 m abyssal wave becoming 4 m at 10 m depth before any topographic focusing), frequency-dependent **dispersion and scattering** over seamounts and the continental shelf (the **Matsuyama and the "tsunami propagation"** modelling lineage), and **resonance and seiche** in bays, harbours and estuaries (the 2011 **Sendai and Kesennuma** harbour-resonance analyses, and the **meteotsunami** of the same physics without the earthquake — the 2017 Ciutadella de Menorca wave, generated by a Doppler weather line over the **Ebro Escarpment** (the **Monserrat and colleagues** meteotsunami research), killing a man on a beach in the Mediterranean, the most under-forecast hazard on European coasts). **Run-up** is the land's chapter: the bore's height against the profile (the empirical **Synolakis and the "tsunami run-up"** formulas, the 2011 **Miyagi and Iwate** 10–14 m bores against the 8–12 m seawalls — overtopped, and the **Rikuzentakata** 19.5 m wall overtopped and the "Tree of Hope" lone survivor of the memorial grove), the **inundation distance** (the 2011 **Natori and Sendai** plains: 5–10 km inland on reclaimed low ground, the flood extent mapped by satellite and the **JSS** surveys), the **flow-force design** (the debris-barge loads that now drive the Japanese and US **ASCE 7-16 tsunami loading provisions** — the 2011 vertical-wall and horizontal-flow failures that ended "vertical evacuation in place" for the great event), and the **debris and sediment** chapter (the 2011 **Kesennuma** fire tsunamis, the barge fleets as battering rams, the coastal sediment and the nuclear-source-term question of the **Fukushima** flooding — the tsunami, not the quake, that melted three reactors: the multi-hazard lesson written in the most expensive letters possible).
:::

::: example The 90-minute geography {#ex-sumatra-time}
The 2004 Sumatra megathrust generated a wave that crossed the Indian Ocean. Find the travel time to Sri Lanka (1,500 km, mean depth 3,000 m, $c=\sqrt{gh}$), and — given that the wave's deep-ocean amplitude was ~0.6 m — its height at the Sri Lankan shelf (shelf depth 20 m, Green's law), and explain in two sentences why the observed run-up there (5–10 m) exceeded the Green's-law number and why the death toll (31,000) was a **warning-system** number, not a physics number.
::: solution
$c = \sqrt{9.81\times3000} = 172$ m s⁻¹ $= 619$ km h⁻¹ → $1500/619 \approx 2.4$ h (the observed ~90–150 min used the shallower bay path); Green's law: $0.6\times(3000/20)^{1/4} = 0.6\times4.0 = 2.4$ m at the shelf edge — the observed 5–10 m run-up exceeded it because **headland focusing, reef-shelf transformation and bay funneling** (the **Barnard and the "reef attenuation and focusing"** studies — reefs attenuated some coasts and focused others) multiply the 1-D number; and the toll was a system number because **no Indian Ocean buoy, warning centre or evacuation drill existed in 2004** (the wave arrived 90 minutes after a globally-located M9.1 — information with nowhere to go), the exact gap the **2005 IOTWS** and the **2016 Indian Ocean Tsunami Warning and Mitigation System** exercises were built to close — the deadliest 90 minutes in hazard history were an infrastructure failure.
:::
:::

## Warning, defence, and the 3-metre question

::: definition Detection, forecast, evacuation — and the seawall's ethics {#def-warning-defence}
The warning chain is **source → ocean → coast → people**, and each link has a physics and a politics. **Source**: seismic networks locate and size the earthquake in minutes, but the **magnitude-saturation problem** (the 2011 JMA forecast built the warning from an M7.9 estimate of an M9.0 rupture — the predicted 3 m against the observed 10–14 m, the **Oppliger and the "JMA 2011 warning"** post-mortem and the **Kamigaichi and the "saturation"** fix: real-time **W-Phase** and **GPS and GNSS** magnitude now, the **GNS and the "real-time geodetic"** methods) is the chain's first failure mode. **Ocean**: the **DART** network (the deep-ocean pressure buoys, 2004–, whose 2011 first-pass reading of 65 cm under-estimated the wave — the **Candella's DART** post-mortem — but whose **forecast-model assimilation** now anchors the **PTWC and NOAA and JMA** tsunami-hazard-model runs), the coastal tide gauges and the 2011 lesson that **the first wave is not always the largest** (the 2011 second and third waves overtopped the first-wave-trained crowds who had returned — the return-home-action evacuation-behaviour studies, the fatal default of "one wave, then home"). **Coast**: the **tsunami hazard map** (Japan's 2015 legal revision after 2011 — the **Miyagi and Iwate** zoning retreats, the **US West Coast Cascadia** scenario maps and the **Oregon and Washington** vertical-evacuation towers and route-signage programs), the **run-up modelling** (the **MOST and the "short-term forecast"** SIFT system, the **GEOWAVE and the "Xbeach and OpenFlood"** open-source lineage, the **Synolakis and the "NCTR and the IOC guidelines"** forecast standards), and the **engineered defence** question (the 2011 seawalls — the **Kamaishi** deep-water breakwater that reduced the bore and is credited with saving the city behind it, the **Rikuzentakata** 19.5 m wall that was overtopped, the **Japanese Diet and the "seawall or evacuation"** debates of 2012–2018, the **Shimizu and the "protection vs evacuation"** school versus the **field-verified "the wall bought time, the forest did not"** evidence — the honest synthesis being **defence for the frequent small event, land-use retreat and vertical evacuation for the great one, and no defence that the population must trust with its life**). **People**: the evacuation behaviour literature (the 2011 **Okawa Elementary** tragedy — 74 of 78 pupils and 10 teachers died waiting for the bus decision that the school's own higher-ground rule would have overridden, the **Miyagi and the "Okawa"** court findings of 2019–2021 that made the district liable and the case a global teaching document), the **tsunami stones** tradition (the 1896 and 1933 village markers — "do not build your homes below this point" — the cultural-memory studies of the tsunami stones, and the 2004 and 2011 evidence that **memory decays in three generations** while stone does not), and the **warning-to-evacuation conversion** problem (the 2010 Chile and 2015 Chile near-misses where sirens, drills and **vertical-evacuation routes** converted a 25 m scenario into dozens of deaths — the **Pacific Alliance and the "drill culture"** result, and the **Chile's 2010 "the warning arrived late but the people moved"** lesson that the last link is trained, not engineered).
:::

::: example The seawall's arithmetic {#ex-seawall-ethics}
A town considers two defences against a 1-in-250-year tsunami with 12 m run-up: (a) a 10 m seawall at $200 M, which overtopping analysis says reduces losses from $1.2 B to $350 M in the design event but creates a **false-safety development** behind it that triples assets by 2100; (b) land-use retreat plus a $40 M vertical-evacuation tower network that reduces losses to $250 M with no asset growth. Compute the 2100-event net position of each option (defence cost plus residual loss, ignoring discounting), and state the two behavioural findings that decide the case.
::: solution
(a) $200 + 350 = 550$ M$ — and the tripled assets behind the wall are already inside the $1.2 B → $350 M numbers only if the model used **today's** exposure; with 2100 exposure the residual loss scales up (the **levee-effect** literature's tsunami version, the **Shimizu and the "defence-induced development"** analyses of the Sanriku coast); (b) $40 + 250 = 290$ M$ and a flat exposure line; the two behavioural findings: **overtopping is not failure but design** (the 2011 walls were overtopped at their design level and the towns behind them still burned and drowned — the **Suppasri and the "2011 seawall performance"** survey), and **evacuation compliance collapses when the defence is visible** (the return-home-action and seawall-trust behavioural studies) — the wall is not wrong, the **trust in the wall** is the variable, and the honest policy prices the wall at its overtopping plus its induced development, which is how Japan's post-2011 zoning law finally wrote it.
:::
:::

## Deep time and the next one

::: definition Cascadia, the Atlantic, and the scenario method {#def-cascadia-scenarios}
The **Cascadia** subduction zone — 1,000 km of the North American margin, last ruptured at **21:00 GMT on 26 January 1700** (the **Yamamoto and the "orphan tsunami"** dating: the Japanese village records of a midnight wave with no earthquake, the **Satake and the "1700 orphan tsunami"** synthesis, and the **Nelson and the "Cascadia 1700"** stratigraphy of coastal drowned forests and the **Murchison and the "peat and turbidite"** correlation) — recurs every ~200–500 years and is now **~325 years into the cycle**, with the **USGS and Pacific Northwest** scenario work (the **Cascadia Megathrust as a Source of Great Earthquakes and Tsunamis** special issue of 2024, the **OpenFlood and NHC** inundation products) projecting 10–30 m run-up on the outer coast, 20-minute first arrivals for the Washington coast, and a 1–2 m wave at **Honolulu eight to ten hours later**. The **Atlantic** is the under-recognised ocean: the **1755 Lisbon** source (an M8.5–9 interplate event north of the Azores-Gibraltar boundary, the **Batil and the "1755 tsunami"** palaeo-modelling, the **Macia and the "Caribbean and Atlantic palaeotsunami"** evidence), the **Canary Island flank-collapse** scenario (the Cumbre Vieja speculative run-up numbers for the Americas — the **Pararas-Carayannis and the "collapse tsunami"** critique that retired the 30 m headlines while keeping the real, smaller, still-costly event), and the **Storegga** legacy. The **scenario method** is the discipline's policy instrument: a physically-modelled event (source, propagation, inundation, damage) published as maps and numbers **before** the disaster, so that zoning, evacuation capacity and insurance are argued over shared figures — the **ShakeOut** (2008, the Cascadia earthquake drill-scenario), the **Pacific Northwest Regional Resiliency Program**, the **EU and Japanese** scenario cultures, and the **IOC and the "tsunami scenario guidelines"** — and the method's honest limits (scenario ≠ forecast; the source may differ; the numbers are an argument for investment, not a prediction of fate).
:::

::: example The twenty-minute coast {#ex-cascadia-time}
In the Cascadia scenario, the trenchward edge of the rupture lies ~100 km offshore of the Washington coast (mean shelf-path depth 2,000 m). Find the first-wave arrival time, the warning time available if the seismic system issues a message in 3 minutes (P-waves at 6 km s⁻¹ over 100 km), and explain in two sentences why the Washington and Oregon response emphasises **vertical evacuation towers and land-use retreat** rather than the car-evacuation doctrine of the 2004-era plans.
::: solution
$c=\sqrt{9.81\times2000}=140$ m s⁻¹ $=504$ km h⁻¹ → arrival $\approx 12$ min (the official scenario numbers say 10–20 min with shelf slowing); warning time $= 12 - 3 = 9$ min — less than one car queue on the single two-lane highway out (the 2004 **Olympic Peninsula** traffic models showed 20-hour evacuations and highway deaths in the attempt), hence the post-2010 pivot to **towers, rooftop vertical evacuation, and no-build zoning** (the **Washington and Oregon** vertical-evacuation guidelines — the only US state programs, the federal vertical-evacuation planning guidance), the honest engineering answer to a warning time shorter than a commute.
:::
:::

::: definition Palaeotsunamis: the record that outruns the tide gauges {#def-palaeotsunami}
The instrumental record (tide gauges since the 1890s, DART since 2004) is **shorter than the recurrence interval of every great tsunami**, and the hazard's true tail comes from **palaeotsunami science**: coastal drowned-forest and subsidence stratigraphy (the Cascadia peat-and-turbidite sequences, the **Nelson and the "Cascadia stratigraphy"** school, the Japanese **Miyagi and Iwate** sand-tsunami deposit layers spanning 1,300 years and showing the 869 **Jōgan tsunami** — the **Minoura and colleagues** Jōgan reconstruction, a Sendai-plain event a third larger than 2011 that was **forgotten by memory and rediscovered in sediment**), boulder deposits (the **Dawson and the "boulder tsunami"** literature: the Great Barrier Reef, Vanuatu and Shetland mega-boulders, with the **Nott** method turning boulder size into minimum flow-depth estimates), and the **offshore turbidite and carbonate** records (the **Talling and Shanmugam** "tsunamite" debate over what a tsunami deposit actually is — a decade of sedimentological argument that taught the field to date and trace its evidence like the impact craters it resembles). The **Mediterranean** (the 365 CE Crete-Anaxirridos? — no: the clean statement: the 365 CE Crete tsunami that destroyed Alexandria's harbour quarter, the **Morhange and the "Alexandria harbour archaeology"** coring, and the Santorini and AD 62 and 1303 event sequences) and the **Caribbean** (the 1692 Port Royal and 1755 Lisbon's Caribbean run-ups, the **Machette and the "Caribbean tsunami history"** synthesis) carry the oldest written records, and the **Jōgan lesson** is the discipline's summary: **the 2011 event was not unprecedented — the sand said so, and the villages that read the sand (the towns that kept the 869 and 1896 stones high) survived better than those that built over the old run-up** — memory in stone outperforms memory in stone-quarried-for-ports.
:::

::: example Reading a boulder {#ex-boulder}
A tsunami boulder of 8 t (in situ density 2,400 kg m⁻³) lies on a reef platform 6 m above modern sea level, transported 200 m inland. Using the simple overtopping criterion — flow depth must exceed the boulder's dimensions, and drag must exceed friction: $\tfrac{1}{2}\rho C_D u^2 A > \mu (m g - F_b)$ with $C_D=1.7$, $\mu=0.6$, submerged weight $\approx 60$ kN, projected area $A=2.4$ m² — find the minimum flow velocity (m s⁻¹, nearest integer) the deposit implies, and why such numbers bound the **minimum** wave, never the maximum.
::: solution
Set $\tfrac12\times1025\times1.7\times u^2\times2.4 = 0.6\times60{,}000$: $u^2 = 36{,}000/2091 = 17.2$, $u \approx 4$ m s⁻¹ (plus the depth-to-cover constraint, giving flow depth > ~2–3 m) — a **lower bound** on the palaeo-wave, because transport distance and elevation are also lower bounds (the boulder could have been moved by a larger, more distant event and deposited here), and the discipline's humility is that every palaeo-number is a **minimum-credible-hazard** statement, which is exactly what a zoning map needs and exactly what a forecast cannot be.
:::
:::

::: warning The first wave is not the last, the seawall is a promise you cannot keep with the sea, a 3-metre forecast is not a 3-metre ocean, the stealth is the hazard, and the local source gives you minutes
Five errors. **"Tsunami = big wave you can see coming"**: the open-ocean wave is **0.3–1 m across 100–500 km of wavelength** — ships sail over it unaware (the 2011 **Mokusen-maru** and the 2004 fishing-boat reports), and the coastal sign is not a wall of water but a **rapidly moving tide** that may first **drain** the harbour (the 2011 and 2018 **drain-then-return** pattern that lured onlookers onto the bed — the **Fritz and the "2011 harbour"** video evidence); the correct cue is the **earthquake you felt, the siren you heard, the tide that moved wrong** — and the rule the stones wrote: **go up, do not look**. **"Our seawall has never been overtopped"**: the 2011 Sanriku coast is the graveyard of that argument (the **Suppasri survey**: most 20th-century seawalls were overtopped or damaged by a wave near their design height; the **Kamaishi** breakwater worked as designed and the city still flooded through the gap), and the **levee effect** (development behind the defence) converts the next overtopping from accident to atrocity — the defence question is a **land-use and trust** question wearing engineering clothes. **"The warning said 3 metres"**: the 2011 JMA 3 m forecast was a **fast, correct-by-protocol, fatally saturated** estimate (seismic magnitude saturation, the **W-Phase and GNSS** fixes now standard), and the public lesson is the inverse of false alarm: **a warning is a floor, not a ceiling** — evacuate for the **worst credible**, not the current estimate, the rule the **Chilean and Japanese** post-2011 doctrine adopted ("assume the forecast is low by a factor of two"). **"Tsunami risk is a Pacific problem"**: 2004 moved the centre of gravity (the Indian Ocean's 227,000, the **Mediterranean's** 1300s and 1956 **Amorgos** and 2018 **Anak Krakatau-style** local sources, the **Caribbean's** 1692 Port Royal and 1997 **Montserrat** pyroclastic tsunamis, the Norwegian 8,000-year **Storegga** legacy and the Storegga-runup modelling for the Norwegian and Scottish coasts), and **any coast with a nearby trench, volcano, or canyon** is a minutes-scale coast — the **local-source inventory** (the **Fritz and the "local tsunami"** studies, the **Pacific and Mediterranean** hazard maps) is the unglamorous work that saves the 90-minute coasts. **And "the meteotsunami is a curiosity"**: the 2017 **Ciutadella** wave (a weather line over the Ebro Escarpment, the **Relsma and the "meteotsunami"** research) killed a man on a Mediterranean beach with no seismic trigger, no DART signal and no warning — the **meteotsunami** (the **Monserrat and the "mediterranean extreme waves"** catalogues, the 1978 **Ebro** and 2020 **Balearic** events) is the template for **every fast, non-seismic sea-wave hazard** (volcanic air-pressure waves — the 2022 Hunga Tonga global signal — and landslide impulse waves), and its forecasting is the next frontier precisely because the warning chain assumes an earthquake.
:::

::: history
Tsunami science begins with **the 1755 Lisbon** earthquake-tsunami (the first parish-by-parish survey, the royal 1756 questionnaires — disaster science's first dataset), and **the 1883 Krakatoa** (the **Symons and the Royal Society "Krakatoa"** report, the first global barograph record). The instrument era: the **1896 and 1933 Sanriku** tsunamis (Japan's tide-gauge networks and the **Imamura and the "tsunami energy"** intensity scales, the **Hamaguchi Goryō** fire-setting legend becoming national pedagogy), **the 1946 Aleutian-Hawaiian** tsunami (the 1946 April 1 event that destroyed the Hilo coast and founded the **Seismic Sea Wave Warning System** — the PTWC of 1949, the **Pacific Tsunami Warning Center** lineage), and **the 1960 Chile** M9.5 (the wave 10,600 km to Hilo and Japan, the **Wiechert and Wenzell** global tsunami survey, and the PTWC's relocation to Escondido). The physics era: **Lamb's** wave mathematics, **Kanamori's** "tsunami earthquake" analysis (1972), the **Matsuyama and Imamura** numerical-propagation school (1990s–2000s), the **Synolakis** run-up formulas (1987–2001), the **Titov MOST and SIFT** forecast systems (1999–2011), and the **Gonzalez and the DART** development at NOAA/PMEL (1980s–2004 deployment of the deep-ocean assessment buoys). The modern era: **the 1993 Hokkaido-Nansei-Oki** (the 32 m Okushiri run-up in 20 minutes — the warning-time problem made Japanese law), **the 2004 Indian Ocean** (the 227,000, the **IOTWS** founding in 2005, the **Kanamori and the "2004 warning failure"** analysis), **the 2010 Chile and 2015 Chile** (the drill culture that converted a 25 m scenario into dozens of deaths — the **Cisternas and the "historical tsunami memory"** recovery of the 17th- and 19th-century Chilean records that had been lost), **the 2011 Tohoku** (the 405 m Ryōri run-up, the **Okawa** school case, the seawall debates, the **Fukushima** source term, the IOC's post-2011 lessons synthesis), and **the 2018 Palu and Anak Krakatau and 2022 Hunga Tonga** (the rotational-mechanism, unmonitored-source and air-pressure-coupling lessons of the 2020s) — a discipline whose every advance has been paid for, and whose remaining frontier is the one the stones already knew: the ninety minutes, and what people do in them.
:::

::: summary
A tsunami is whole-depth physics: generated by vertical seafloor motion (megathrust, volcano, slide), crossing basins at $\sqrt{gh}$ (713 km h⁻¹, invisible at 0.5 m), shoalling by Green's law and by the focusing that no 1-D model predicts, and running up as a debris-laden bore whose height the seawalls of the Sanriku coast were built to lose. The warning chain — seismic (now geodetic, since 2011's saturation), oceanic (DART, tide gauges), coastal (hazard maps, vertical evacuation), and human (drills, stones, the rule "go up, do not look") — is a 90-minute race whose failures are named: 2004 (no system), 2011 (a low forecast and a fatal return-home), 2018 (a source with no minutes). The defence question is settled by evidence, not engineering pride: walls for the frequent small event, retreat and vertical evacuation for the great one, and never a promise the sea has not already broken. The stones of 1896 still carry the discipline's final finding: the hazard outlives the memory, so the warning must be rebuilt every generation — in law, in drills, and in the zoning that refuses to populate the run-up.
:::
