Every map is a lie told carefully: the Earth is an oblate ellipsoid (very nearly a geoid, and locally a landscape), and every flat representation must choose which truths to keep and which to break — angles, areas, distances, directions, or the shape of the graticule. This lesson makes those choices explicit and reversible, because the rest of the course's GIS work inherits them silently: a "zonal area statistic" computed in Web Mercator is wrong by a factor of eight at the tropics, a coordinate pasted between datums can move a point by a kilometre, and a boundary dispute between two agencies is often a geodetic-datum dispute wearing a legal costume. The lesson has three parts. **Geodesy**: the ellipsoid-and-geoid model of the Earth's figure, the datum-and-Earth-reference-frame machinery (the 1984–2025 WGS84-and-ITRF-and-EUREF-and-REGNET-and-GDA2020 realizations, their plate-motion epochs and their centimetre maintenance), and the coordinate languages (geodetic latitude-and-height versus projected easting-northing versus the vertical datums of the sea-level-and-glaciology lessons). **Projection**: the developable-surface taxonomy (cylindrical, conic, azimuthal), the distortion algebra (Tissot's indicatrix, the conformal-equal-area-equidistant compromise theorem that you cannot have everything), and the working menu (the UTM-and-national-grids, the Lambert-conformal-and-Albers-equal-area continental families, the 1999-and-2017 Web-Mercator phenomenon and its cartographic consequences). **Positioning**: satellite navigation as the coordinate producer (the GPS-Galileo-Glonass-BeiDou constellations' pseudorange-and-carrier-phase observables, the error budget of ionosphere-troposphere-multipath-orbit-and-clock, the 1994–2025 RTK-and-PPP-and-network-RTK precision ladder from metres to millimetres) and the reverse problem — transforming existing data between the reference frames it was captured in, where the 2016–2025 geodetic-version-control literature (the plate-rotation-epoch bookkeeping of Australia's GDA2020 adoption, the 2024-and-2025 ITRF2020-and-TRF-reality discussions) is the cautionary serial. The prerequisites are the radar-lidar lesson's centimetre-ranging intuition and high-school trigonometry; the reward is coordinate literacy: the ability to look at any layer's metadata line and know what its numbers mean, what they cost, and where they will break.
::: definition The Earth's figure, datums and coordinate languages {#def-geodesy}
The measurement hierarchy starts with the figure: the **geoid** (the equipotential surface of gravity that mean sea level would follow, departures from the ellipsoid of ±100 m, the 1995–2025 GRACE-and-GOCE-and-EGM2008-and-EGM2020 gravity-model lineage that maps it and the gravimetric-geoid-vs-ellipsoid-height relation h = H + N that the vertical-datums paragraph reuses), the best-fit **ellipsoid** (the semi-major axis and flattening of the 1980-GRS80-and-1984-WGS84 parameter sets, the local-datums' historically best-fitting ellipsoids — the 1841-Indian-Ellipsoid-and-1830-Airy-and-1924-International-and-Bessel families that still haunt older maps), and the **Earth-centered frame** (the International-Terrestrial-Reference-Frame's VLBI-SLR-GNSS-DORIS-maintained realization, the 1988–2024 ITRF2014-and-ITRF2020 versions with their published site-velocity fields, the plate-fixed regional frames — ETRF, GDA2020's 2020.0 epoch, NAD83's-continuum-realizations, GAD — whose **epoch** (coordinates are valid at a date, and plates move 1–7 cm/yr) is the bookkeeping fact the 2016–2025 datum-modernization wave exists to enforce). A **geodetic datum** is the ellipsoid-plus-orientation-plus (for local systems) deflection-of-the-vertical package that makes latitude-longitude meaningful; a **coordinate reference system** (the ISO-19111-and-EPSG-registry formalism, the 2015–2025 PROJ-6-and-later transformation-engine era that made datum shifts a versioned, grid-based, auditable operation — the 2020s grid-of-shifts-and-helmert-parameter metadata being the difference between a correct transformation and a plausible one) wraps datum-plus-projection-plus-units into the one identifier a GIS file should always carry. The **vertical datum** is its own problem: orthometric heights (the geoid-referenced "elevation above sea level" of the topographic maps, the local mean-sea-level origins whose century of tide-gauge records the [[oceanography/sea-level]] lesson inherits), ellipsoidal heights (what GNSS gives, the h = H + N conversion), and the national vertical networks (the 2003–2025 NAVD-1988-adjustments-and-Australia's-PADMS-and-EU-vertical-EVF-era unifications) whose inconsistencies are the reason flood-zone maps argue across borders. The coordinate languages then multiply: **geodetic coordinates** (latitude, longitude, height — angles on a curved surface, where 1° of longitude shrinks as cos(latitude), the exercise's arithmetic), **projected coordinates** (the lesson's next definition), **grid-and-tile systems** (the UTM-zone-and-3DEP-and-H3-and-S2-tiling conventions the data infrastructure uses), and the **time-tagged coordinates** of the positioning definition (a point is a coordinate-at-epoch, and the 2020s spatial-data-infrastructure standards are only beginning to write that into schemas). The discipline for the whole lesson, and the GIS lessons that follow, is one sentence: **no coordinate without its CRS identifier, no transformation without its method-and-grid version, no height without its datum** — three metadata lines that prevent most of the errors this course's fallacies will collect.
:::

::: definition Projections: the distortion algebra and the working menu {#def-projections}
A projection is a pair of functions from ellipsoid coordinates to plane coordinates, and its distortion is fully described (the 1843-Tissot-and-1859-Listing heritage, the 1980s-and-2020s Cartography-and-GIScience distortion-analysis literature) by the **principal scale factors** a-and-b along the maximum-and-minimum distortion directions: their product gives **areal** distortion (ab), their ratio gives **angular** distortion (the ellipse's eccentricity), and their mean relates to **linear** distortion — Tissot's indicatrix (the infinitesimal circle mapped to an ellipse) being the visualization every good atlas uses to show what a projection is doing at each latitude. The **choice theorem** (the 1850s-and-1912-Snyder-and-1980s-Board-of-Maps-era rules of thumb, the 1990s-and-2000s-error-metric literature formalizing them) is a sequence of trade-offs: **conformal** projections (a = b everywhere, angles-and-shape-preserved, areas inflated with the square of the scale — the Mercator-and-Lambert-conformal-conic family, the navigation-and-weather-and-large-scale-topographic-mapping choice), **equal-area** (ab = 1, areas preserved, shapes sheared — the Albers-and-Mollweide-and-Sinusoidal-and-Cylindrical-Equal-Area family, the thematic-mapping-and-area-statistics choice, the 2015-and-2017 True-Size-and-TheEqualArea movement's political-cartography argument), **equidistant** (along selected lines only — no projection preserves all distances), and **compromise** (the Winkel-Tripel-and-Robinson-and-Natural-Earth families of the 1921–2023 general-reference-atlases, minimizing some perceptual composite). The **developable-surface taxonomy** organizes the menu by what the map is wrapped on: **cylindrical** (the 1569-Mercator-and-1880s-Gauss-Krüger-and-UTM lineage — conformal, transverse, 6°-zone-and-8°-band grids with 1:2.5-million-scale false-easting conventions, the national-topographic-map default of most countries and the 1999 Web-Mercator variant's spherical-shortcut-and-infinite-pole pathology), **conic** (the 1772-Lambert-and-1805-Albers families, the mid-latitude-east-west-continents choice — the US's lower-48-state-plane-and-Albers-Atlas-of-America-and-Europe's-Lambert-93-and-China's-Krasovsky-Karnak-era-1980s-Lambert grids), **azimuthal** (the polar-and-great-circle-navigation-and-radio-direction-finding choices, the Lambert-azimuthal-equal-area polar stereographic of the polar-mapping default — the 1990s-and-2020s Antarctic-and-Arctic-Data-Center-and-BAS-DEM-and-Arctic-DEM conventions the glaciology course lives in), and the **geodetic-boundary-and-legal** grids (the state-plane-and-national-grids' county-and-parcel geometry, the 2010s-and-2020s vertical-and-horizontal-adjurement acts and their coordinate-law consequences). The **selection rules** the examples rehearse: large-scale mapping of a small area wants a low-distortion zoned conformal grid (UTM-and-state-plane-and-national-grids, scale error under 1 part in 10⁴); continental thematic mapping of areas wants an equal-area family matched to the continent's orientation (Albers for east-west mid-latitude, Lambert-azimuthal-equal-area for polar); global thematic mapping wants a compromise or equal-area pseudocylindrical (the Mollweide-and-Hammer-and-Boggs-equirectangular-and-Natural-Earth-II lineage of the world-choropleth literature); and **any quantitative analysis across a boundary of two zones-or-two-CRSs is a projection decision in disguise** — the 2010s-and-2020s GIScience literature on projection-induced error in distance-and-area statistics (the Web-Mercator-area-claim debunkings, the great-circle-versus-rhumb-line distance errors in global shipping-and-flyline datasets, the 2016-and-2022 planar-versus-geodesic-area-comparison studies) being the audit of what happens when the metadata line is ignored.
:::

::: definition Satellite positioning and frame transformation {#def-positioning}
Global Navigation Satellite Systems (the 1978–1995 GPS-and-1982-Glonass-and-2016-Galileo-and-2020-BeiDou-3 constellations, plus the regional augmentation systems) give a receiver its position by solving for four unknowns (three coordinates plus receiver-clock offset) from pseudoranges to four-or-more satellites whose own positions-and-clocks are broadcast (the GPS III-and-Galileo-FOC-era ephemeris accuracy of decimetres, the precise-products-and-IGS-and-CNES-and-BKG-and-WUM-era final orbit-and-clock products of the 1994–2025 International-GNSS-Service lineage at the centimetre-and-few-picosecond level). The **observable** is a code pseudorange (the C/A-and-P-and-E1-and-E5 chips, a 3-m code-noise floor plus the error budget) or a **carrier phase** (the 19-and-24-cm wavelengths measured to a percent — the millimetre observable whose integer ambiguity (the 1985–2025 LAMBDA-and-integer-least-squares ambiguity-resolution literature) is the difference between centimetre and metre positioning). The **error budget** is the lesson's second centimetre-accounting exercise: ionospheric delay (the 1998–2025 Klobuchar-and-NeQuick-and-GIM-and-observation-combination literature, metres at day-and-equator, removed to centimetres by the dual-frequency iono-free combination or estimated by the 2015–2025 RTK-and-PPP models), tropospheric delay (the 1975-Saastamoinen-and-1992-Hopfield-and-2000s-GPT-and-GPT3-and-GPT2w dry-and-wet models, the wet component's centimetre weather-noise that makes the [[meteorology/forecasting]] lesson's water-vapour tomography a GNSS application in reverse), satellite-and-receiver clock-and-orbit errors (the real-time service corrections of the 2010s-and-2020s BKG-and-WUM-and-trimble-CenterPoint-RTX-era SSR products), multipath-and-site-environment (the 1990s–2025 choke-ring-and-multipath-mitigation-and-sidereal-filtering literature, the millimetre geodesy-site design codes), and the geometry factor (the DOP-and-PDOP-and-VDOP arithmetic of the exercise below, satellite-constellation geometry's amplification of every other error). The **precision ladder** then orders the techniques: standalone SPP (the 2020s multi-constellation smartphone-and-receiver era at 1–5 m), SBAS-and-GBAS (the 1995–2025 EGNOS-and-WAAS-and-MSAS-and-GAGAN-and-Geo-Augmentation-era 0.5–2 m, the 2006-and-2020s aviation-approach categories), real-time kinematic (the 1990s–2025 RTK-and-network-RTK-and-VRS era, 1–2 cm + 1 ppm with a baseline-limited radio-or-internet link, the surveying-and-machine-control-and-precision-agriculture default), and precise point positioning (the 1997–2025 Zumberge-and-JPL-SOP-and-PPP-RTK-and-PPP-B2b-and-BDS-PPP-era ambiguity-resolved convergence-to-centimetre-in-minutes regime) — the 2020s PPP-RTK-and-NSPIRE-and-OSNOS-era fusion of the two closing the gap that defined the technique-selection decision for a quarter century. **Frame transformation** is the reverse problem: taking existing coordinates from one CRS to another (the 2016–2025 PROJ-6-and-7-and-8-and-9-era transformation-engine literature, the grid-based-and-Helmert-and-Molodensky-and-bursa-wolf-parameter methods, the plate-motion-model-and-pv/plate-rotation-epoch bookkeeping of the datum-modernization examples, the 2024-and-2025 ITRF2020-and-ITRF-reality-and-TRF-time-dependence discussions) — and the discipline is the geodesy definition's three metadata lines, executed by versioned software whose transformation path (the 2020s PROJ pipeline language's explicit step lists, the datum-shift-grid availability-and-accuracy metadata) is as auditable as the projection choice it sits beside.
:::

::: example Choosing a projection: three jobs, three answers {#ex-choose-projection}

grid (the EU's ETRS89-LAEA-Lambert-azimuthal-equal-area-and-ETRS89-LCC conventions of the 2009-and-2017-EEA-recommended-practice documents, the US's state-plane-and-Albers choices per region), never the data's delivery projection (the Sentinel-granule-UTM-zone mosaicking problem of the image-processing lesson is exactly this decision made accidentally), and never Web Mercator for the statistics (the next example). **A continental land-cover map** (the 2017–2025 ESA-WorldCover-and-ESRI-land-cover-10-m-and-copernicus-global-land-degradation-era global products) must report per-country area 2019-and-2022-FAO-FRA-and-global-forest-watch-area-statistics methods, the 2016-and-2022 planar-versus-geodesic-area-comparison studies' recommendation of geodesic-area-computation for any boundary-polygon-workflow), with the products' native tiling (the S2-and-MGRS-tile-and-geographic-degree-grid conventions) treated as a packaging detail, not an analysis CRS. **A coastal city's ::: example Web Mercator and the area myth: Greenland's false empire {#ex-mercator}
The 1999-and-2005–2025 Web-Mercator phenomenon (the 1999-MapQuest-and-2005-Google-Maps-EPSG-3857 lineage whose spherical-formula-on-ellipsoidal-data shortcut is itself a datum error, and whose y-coordinate diverges at the poles forcing the ±85.05° cutoff) is the lesson's teaching case because its distortion is both enormous and invisible: the conformal scale factor sec(latitude) gives 2× linear at 60°, 2.9× at 70°, and an **areal** factor of sec² — 4× at 60°, 8.5× at 70°, over 80× at Greenland's true high-latitude mean versus under 1.3× at the equator — which is why the Mercator world-map makes Greenland (2.2 million km², 3.4% of Africa's area) look Africa-sized (30.4 million km²) and Canada-and-Russia dominate the visual politics of climate-and-biodiversity maps (the 2015–2025 True-Size-Map-and-TheEqualArea-and-Natural-Earth-and-AuthaGraph cartographic-justice-and-critique literature, the 2018-and-2021-geography-of-aid-and-emissions-per-capita-visualization studies' projection-sensitivity analyses). The quantitative GIS consequences are grimmer than the aesthetics: **any area statistic computed in Web Mercator is a latitude-weighted lie** (the 2010s-and-2020s-lesson-series debunkings of the "Greenland-is-bigger-than-Africa" claim, the 2016-and-2022 studies finding 10–40-per-cent country-area errors in web-derived datasets, the 2020s-geodesic-area-function-defaults of the modern GIS stacks), distance and direction are wrong off the equator-and-great-circle (the rhumb-line-versus-great-circle-flyline-map correction of the 2010s-and-2020s-visualization-toolkits), and the "EPSG:4326-is-fine-for-areas-because-it-is-geographic" confusion (planar-degree-area-versus-geodesic-area, the cos-latitude factor the exercise below computes) is the most common single CRS error in the 2020s-geodata-science-replication literature. The correct habits are cheap: analyze in an equal-area CRS matched to the study's extent, report areas with their method (planar-versus-geodesic-versus-spherical-excess), and read every public map's projection the way the classification lesson reads accuracy tables — as a statement of what the map is allowed to claim.
:::

::: example Datum mismatches: when the same point moves a kilometre {#ex-datum}
The most expensive coordinate errors are the silent ones — two datasets, both "in latitude-longitude", whose datums differ by hundreds of metres. The canonical families: **local-versus-global shifts** (the pre-1990s national datums pinned to one origin observatory — the 1841-Indian-and-1971-WGS72-and-Tokyo-JGD-and-Potsdam-and-Cape-Old-1983-and-Amersfoort-1998-lineage — whose offsets from WGS84 reach 100–1500 m: the 1990s-and-2000s India-and-Pakistan-border-mapping-and-Israel-Jordan-boundary-corrections-and-Southeast-Asia-colonial-era-datum disputes, the 2000-and-2016-GDA94-to-GDA2020-and-2024-GDA2020-realization-era Australian datum-modernization campaign (the 2017-and-2020-ESC-and-ICS-and-ICC-and-IGC-era national-grid-and-cadastral-realignment, the 2020.0-epoch-and-plate-rotation-and-pv-parameter bookkeeping that moved every coordinate in the country by about 1.8 m — the exercise's arithmetic), the 1980s-and-2010s-Japan-datum-and-height-datum-revision-and-2011-Tohoku-epoch-shift-and-2012-and-2014-post-earthquake-coordinate-updates); **epoch mismatches** (the plate-motion paragraph's slow version of the same event: NAD83-versus-ITRF coordinates of Hawaii differing by metres because the frames' realizations carry different plate-rotation-poles, the 2022-and-2024-NAD83-2024-epoch-and-NSRS-2022-and-58-realignment-and-RTN-and-RTK-network-era discussions, the 2020s-plate-motion-model-and-ITRF2020-versus-ITRF2014-versus-ITRF2010-site-velocity-comparisons); and **transformation-method errors** (the 2016–2025 PROJ-and-pyproj-and-GDAL-and-esri-ArcGIS-PROJ-integration-era bug-reports-and-correction-notices, the Molodensky-versus-Helmert-versus-grid-based-method choices differing by metres where the shift grids are coarse-or-missing, the 2021-and-2023-PROJ-6-and-7-and-8-era-datum-shift-grid-availability-and-accuracy-metadata-and-transformation-path-pipeline-language). The 2010s-and-2020s-cadastral-and-maritime-boundary-and-OCS-and-Arctic-seabed-claim-and-UNCLOS-and-CLCS-and-ITLOS-and-PCIJ-and-ICJ-case-law literature (the 2002-and-2014-and-2016-and-2023-boundary-dispute-judgments' coordinate-evidence-protocols) documents the legal end of the story: a treaty's coordinates are meaningless without their datum, and the 2020s-maritime-and-Arctic-and-South-China-Sea-award-and-pleading-and-expert-report-practice now specifies CRS-and-epoch-and-transformation-method the way the GIS lessons specify metadata. The audit habit for any dataset without a documented CRS: locate a feature both datasets can see (a bridge, a runway threshold, a GNSS-referenced benchmark), compute the offset vector, and let its magnitude-and-direction name the datum — the same cross-check logic as the radar-lidar lesson's reference-point discipline, in metres instead of millimetres.
:::

::: example The flood map's vertical datum: 29 metres of legal exposure {#ex-vertical}
The 2012-and-2017-and-2022-US-Flood-Insurance-Rate-Map-and-NYC-Panel-and-EU-Floods-Directive-hazard-map controversies (the 2015-and-2019-US-GAO-and-NRC-and-NASEM-reports-and-GA-OIG-audits, the 2018-and-2022-NAVD-88-versus-adjacent-region-and-local-datum-boundary-discontinuities-and-GNA14-and-AUSGeoid-and-EGM2008-conversion-studies) show the vertical datum as a horizontal-datum problem with dollars attached: flood elevations are quoted in a local vertical datum (the NAVD-88-and-ordnance-datum-and-GA-LAT-and-Adriatic-and-Baltic-local-mean-sea-level families whose century-of-tide-gauge origins differ from each other by decimetres to metres and, in the Gulf-of-Mexico-and-Bay-of-Bengal-and-Netherlands-case-studies, from geoid-based elevations enough to move parcel-level insurance and permitting decisions), the h = H + N conversion (the geoid example's formula, executed with the 2008-and-2020-EGM-and-reginic-geoid-models and the 2022-and-2027-GEOID19-and-GNA14-and-AUSGeoid2020-and-NGS-CONVERT-and-ICM-GEOID-and-EU-GGOS-era national-and-continental-models) is where the errors enter, and the 2017-and-2022-US-vertical-datum-modernization decision (the NAVD-88-replacement-to-a-NASA-GEOIDE-and-gravity-field-model-and-GRACE-and-GOCE-and-surface-levelling-era-geoid debate, the 2025-and-2027-implementation-timeline-and-adjustment-and-transition-and-legacy-compatibility discussions) is the largest live demonstration that a datum is a legal-infrastructure object, not a mathematical one — the exercise's 3.2-mm-paper-shift, at flood-insurance scale, is a household's base-map elevation and a municipality's liability, and the honest hazard map states its vertical datum, its geoid model and its tide origin in the same caption that states its flood probability.

::: history From degree triangulations to centimetres from space {#hist-maps}
**1617–1955.** Classical geodesy: Snell's 1617 triangulation of a degree, the 1735–1744 French expeditions to Lapland and Peru (the Maupertuis-and-La-Condamine measurements) confirming the oblate Earth, Bessel's 1841 ellipsoid, the national datums of the 19th-century surveys (the Airy-and-Everest-and-Bessel names embedded in older maps), and the 20th-century unification conventions (the 1924 International Ellipsoid, the European triangulation adjustments). **1957–1994.** Space reframes the figure: the 1958 Doppler discovery from Sputnik's signal, the Transit-and-SECOR-and-Doppler-surveying lineage, satellite laser ranging from 1966, VLBI and DORIS, and the 1984-WGS84-and-1989-GRS80 parameter sets with the 1988-onward ITRF realizations — the Earth figure becomes an Earth-centered, space-geodetic, continuously-maintained object. **1994–2025.** The precision era: GPS full operational capability (1994), Galileo (2016) and BeiDou-3 (2020) complete the multi-constellation world; RTK-and-PPP-and-network-RTK take positioning from metres to millimetres; the IGS-and-precise-products regime makes the permanent GNSS network a geophysical instrument (the plate-motion-and-ZTD-and-GNSS-meteorology applications); and the datum-modernization wave (the 2020-GDA2020-and-2024-NAD83-2024-and-NSRS-realignments, the PROJ-6-era versioned transformation grids) institutionalizes the epoch bookkeeping this lesson exists to teach.
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-projection}
A conservation NGO must compute and compare the protected-area coverage (in hectares) of every country. The CRS choice for the analysis should be:

- [x] An equal-area projection (or geodesic area computation) — area statistics demand ab = 1
- [ ] Web Mercator, because all the web data is in it
- [ ] A conformal projection, because shapes must be preserved
- [ ] Any CRS, because areas are invariant under projection

::: solution :::
Area is not projection-invariant (D is the fallacy the Mercator example documents); the analysis needs ab = 1, i.e. an equal-area CRS matched to the extent or direct geodesic-area computation. Web Mercator inflates high-latitude countries by up to 80× (B is the lesson's headline error), and conformal projections preserve angles precisely by sacrificing areas (C is the choice for navigation and large-scale mapping, not for area statistics).
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-datum}
Two agencies exchange shapefiles of the same border region, both labeled "latitude-longitude WGS84", but one was digitized from a map on a local 1950s datum and never transformed. The overlay shows:

- [x] A systematic offset (hundreds of metres, constant direction) revealing the datum mismatch
- [ ] Random scatter of a few metres from digitizing error
- [ ] Perfect alignment, because WGS84 is WGS84
- [ ] A rotation only, because datums differ only by orientation

::: solution :::
Local datums differ from Earth-centered ones by 100–1500 m translations (plus rotations and scale in the Helmert sense), and an untransformed layer carries the mismatch as a coherent offset vector — the datum example's audit procedure (measure the vector at a common feature, let its magnitude and direction name the datum). Random scatter (B) would indicate digitizing noise, not a datum shift; "WGS84" labels are metadata claims, not measurements (C is the expensive mistake); and pure rotation (D) describes only one component of the general 7- or 14-parameter transformation.
:::

The Mercator areal scale factor is sec²(latitude). Compute it at latitude 70° (cos 70° = 0.342), to one decimal place.
::: solution :::
1/0.342² = 1/0.117 = **8.5** — the widget's curve read at Norway-and-Scotland-and-Southern-Alaska latitudes, and the number behind the 2015–2025 True-Size-and-critique-cartography literature's complaint: a northern-biodiversity-or-EEZ-map in Web Mercator gives these countries 8.5 times their true visual weight, an aesthetic choice with budget-and-policy consequences.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="3.2"}
A dataset on a local datum is offset 80 m from WGS84. On a 1:25,000 map (1 cm = 250 m), what is this offset in millimetres on paper, to one decimal place?
::: solution :::
80/250 m-per-cm = 0.32 cm = **3.2 mm** — visible to the careful eye but under the drafting-and-digitizing tolerance that made such shifts routine for a century, the datum example's silent-error mechanism in one division, and the reason the fix is metadata (the CRS identifier) rather than eyesight.

::: exercise {level=2 check="7.5"}
A receiver's pseudorange errors are about 3 m and the position dilution of precision (PDOP) is 2.5. Estimate the 3-D position error in m.
::: solution :::

::: exercise {level=3 check="2"}
In a conformal projection at latitude 45°, the linear scale factor is sec(45°) = 1.414. By what factor are areas distorted there?
::: solution :::
Conformal means a = b, so area scale = ab = 1.414² = **2.0** — the Tissot-algebra exercise showing why conformal-and-equal-area are mutually exclusive except at standard parallels, why the Lambert-conformal state-plane grids carry published area-correction factors for cadastral work, and why the 2016-and-2022 planar-versus-geodesic-area studies insist the projection be chosen by the statistic, not by the software default.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="1000000"}
A country with true area 500,000 km² lies mostly at latitude 45°. If its area is computed in Web Mercator (areal factor sec² latitude), what apparent area in km² results?
::: solution :::
500000 × 2 = **1000000 km²** — the Mercator example's error made numeric (double the area, and the answer's unit is the exercise's: km², not the country's real km²), the exact failure mode the 2010s-and-2020s-geodata-replication-audits keep finding in web-derived country-area tables, and the reason the conservation example's protected-area statistics run in an equal-area CRS or on geodesic polygons.

::: exercise {level=3 check="1.9"}
GNSS carrier-phase measurements on L1 (wavelength 19 cm) reach about 1 per cent of the wavelength in precision. What is that precision in millimetres?
::: solution :::
0.01 × 190 mm = **1.9 mm** — the observable underlying every millimetre claim in this course (the radar-lidar lesson's plate-motion velocities and volcano-inflation series are carrier-phase time series, not pseudoranges), reachable only after the integer-ambiguity resolution and the ionosphere-troposphere-orbit-clock error budget are handled, and the reason the positioning definition calls the ambiguity "the difference between centimetre and metre positioning".

::: warning Four coordinate fallacies
1. **"Latitude-longitude is just latitude-longitude."** The datum-and-epoch fallacy: identical numbers on different datums or frames are different places (the datum example's 100–1500-m local-datum offsets, the plate-motion epoch bookkeeping that moves coordinates centimetres per year, the GDA2020-and-NAD83-2024 reallocation campaigns that moved-and-will-move national coordinates by metres), and identical numbers in different realizations of "the same" frame differ by the realization's own centimetre-to-decimetre disagreement. The 2016–2025 transformation-engine literature's most-repeated finding is that most CRS bugs are not math errors but metadata errors — the file's CRS label describing the delivery format rather than the coordinates' actual history — which is why the audit habit (the geodesy definition's three metadata lines, the offset-vector test) matters more than knowing the Helmert equations by heart.
2. **"The map's projection is a presentation choice."** It is an analysis choice with arithmetic consequences: areas computed in conformal projections, distances in azimuthal ones (the great-circle-versus-rhumb-line error in web flyline maps), directions in compromise ones, and every zonal statistic in Web Mercator inherits the sec²-latitude weighting (the Mercator example's 4–80× range); the 2010s-and-2020s-GIScience-error-literature case files (the 2013-and-2018-and-2022-lesson-series on the "distance-area-volume-in-GIS" problem, the conservation-planning-and-carbon-accounting studies whose conclusions moved with the projection) document that the projection is chosen by the statistic being computed, and the 2020s-software-defaults conversation (the geodesic-area-and-true-scale-projection defaults of the modern GIS-and-visualization stacks) is the community slowly internalizing that the safe default is the one that tells the user what it assumed.
3. **"GPS gives you a position."** It gives you a pseudorange-and-phase observable whose position is a solution with an error budget (the positioning definition's ionosphere-troposphere-multipath-orbit-clock-DOP accounting): the phone's 5 m and the geodetic site's 3 mm are the same constellation at different error-budget temperatures, the urban-canyon-and-canopy multipath failure modes are physical not cosmetic (the 2015–2025 NLOS-and-multipath-mitigation-and-visual-inertial-fusion literature), and the millimetre claims of the geophysics applications live entirely inside the ambiguity-resolution-and-products regime — a PPP solution with an unresolved cycle slip is not a precise position, it is a precise-looking mistake, the same confidence-versus-accuracy distinction the classification lesson makes about accuracy tables, in coordinate form.
4. **"Digitized old maps are just old maps in a computer."** The legacy-layer fallacy: scanned-and-digitized historical maps carry their original sheet-graticule-and-datum-and-drafting errors into modern analyses (the 2010s–2025 historical-GIS-and-cadastral-and-maritime-boundary literature's rubber-sheeting-and-control-point-rubber-sheeting debates, the 2015–2025 coastal-atlas-and-nautical-chart-referencing studies whose 19th-century-coastline-comparisons-with-modern-shorelines turned out to be datum-comparisons), and the legal-grade coordinate disputes (the 2002–2025 maritime-boundary-case-law coordinate-evidence protocols) exist because a digitized coordinate inherits every error of its source and the analyst's obligation is to propagate them, not to paste them next to GNSS coordinates as if the two were the same kind of number.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Mapping is the choice of which lies to tell: the Earth is a geoid nearly fitted by an ellipsoid, datums and frames (the WGS84-and-ITRF-and-regional-realization system, with their epochs and plate motions) turn places into numbers, and projections turn curved numbers into flat ones under Tissot's algebra — conformal preserves angles and pays in area (sec²-latitude, the Mercator example's 4–80×), equal-area preserves area and pays in shape, compromise minimizes what the map is for, and the selection rule is the statistic, not the software default (the projection example's three jobs). Positioning is the coordinate producer: pseudorange-and-carrier-phase observables whose error budget (ionosphere, troposphere, multipath, orbit, clock, DOP) spans the phone's metres to the geodetic network's millimetres, with the ambiguity-resolution-and-precise-products regime separating the two, and frame transformation (the versioned-grid, pipeline-explicit PROJ-era discipline, the datum-modernization epoch bookkeeping) keeping the resulting coordinates honest with their own history. The examples are the discipline applied — flood agencies and carbon accountants choosing CRSs by question, the Web-Mercator area myth quantified, datum mismatches caught by offset vectors, one constellation serving three instruments at three error-budget temperatures — and the fallacies (datum-blindness, projection-as-aesthetics, GPS-as-truth, legacy-layers-as-truth) all reduce to the lesson's one metadata commandment: **no coordinate without its CRS, no transformation without its method, no height without its datum.** With the coordinate foundations set, the course turns to the data structures that hold them and the analysis that computes on them.
:::
