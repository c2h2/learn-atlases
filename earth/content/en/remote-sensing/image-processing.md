An image as delivered by a ground station is not a measurement of the surface; it is a measurement of the sensor, by the atmosphere, of the surface, wrapped in file-format conventions — and digital image processing is the sequence of operations that strips the wrappers without damaging the signal. The chain has two independent halves. The **correction** half makes pixels physical and locatable: radiometric calibration from digital numbers to radiance and then to surface reflectance, atmospheric correction that removes path radiance and divides by transmittance, geometric correction that maps the oblique, rotating, terrain-displaced viewing geometry onto a map grid, and co-registration that stacks multi-date-and-multi-sensor scenes on the same ground to well under a pixel. The **enhancement-and-analysis** half makes patterns visible and computable: stretches and band ratios that expose contrasts the eye cannot see, indices such as NDVI whose simplicity hides a calibration requirement, convolutions and filters that trade resolution for noise, Fourier-and-wavelet views that turn periodicity-and-edges into measurable quantities, and fusion operations that combine a fine panchromatic image with a rich multispectral one. Every operation in both halves has a dual: it improves something and it corrupts something else, and the discipline of this lesson is to keep the ledger — smoothing that breaks registration, sharpening that manufactures texture, fusion that invents spectra, indices computed across unharmonized sensors. The prerequisites are the radiometry and sensor geometry of the two preceding lessons; the payoff is that the classification lesson can then begin from surface reflectance, which is the only honest starting material for maps.
::: definition From digital numbers to surface reflectance {#def-correction}
The correction chain is five steps, each with a named error term. **Radiometric calibration** converts digital numbers to at-sensor spectral radiance with per-band gain-and-offset (the Landsat collection-2 and Sentinel-2 L1C metadata's rescaling factors, traceable to prelaunch integrating-sphere characterization and on-orbit vicarious calibration against desert PICS sites to a few per cent). **Atmospheric correction** then inverts L_sensor = L_path + T·L_surface/(d² terms) band by band: the line-transfer radiative-transfer inversions (the 6S-and-MODTRAN-and-LibRadTrans models behind LaSRC-and-Sen2Cor-and-FLAASH), the dark-object subtraction fallbacks, and the aerosol retrieval (the blue-band-and-SWIR dark-target logic of the previous lessons) that the correction needs as input; the residual after a good run is one-to-three per cent of surface reflectance in the visible-near-infrared, more in the aerosol-sensitive blue and water-vapour-sensitive SWIR, and the 2016–2024 harmonization literature (Landsat-to-Sentinel-2 cross-sensor reflectance intercomparisons) is the audit of how well the chain holds across instruments. **BRDF normalization** (for systematic products: the MODIS kernel-model approach) collapses the directional sampling of the BRDF definition onto a common geometry before time series begin. **Bidirectional-and-glint handling** over water (sun glint, adjacency, sky polarization) and **topographic normalization** over mountains (the Lambertian-cosine and C-correction family, and their known overcorrection in steep shaded terrain) close the radiometric half. The **geometric half** starts from the sensor model — rigorous sensor models (the RPC-and-rational-polynomial coefficients of every modern product, or the physical collinearity equations of the whiskbroom era) — and corrects Earth-rotation and attitude effects, then ties to ground: absolute geolocation (Sentinel-2's ~few-metre-without-ground-control, Landsat collection-2's 12-m RMSE claim, the post-2021 Landsat-9-and-Sentinel-2 co-registration to under half a pixel) and relative co-registration for change pairs (the sub-pixel cross-correlation-and-phase-correlation machinery of every serious change pipeline, with residual registration error reported in tenths of a pixel because, as the change-detection lesson will show, unregistered differencing manufactures change at every edge). Terrain displacement (parallax of bright targets up to a pixel per hundred metres of relief at 20° off-nadir) is corrected only in ortho-rectified products with a DEM, and the resampling that goes with every geometric step (nearest-neighbour preserving values, bilinear and cubic preserving smoothness at the cost of mixing neighbours) is a deliberate choice with a documented footprint — the first place a careful reviewer looks.
:::

::: definition Enhancement: stretches, ratios, indices, transforms {#def-enhancement}
**Contrast stretching** maps the histogram's informative range onto the display's 256 levels: linear (percentile clipping), piecewise, or equalization (flattening the histogram to spread levels, at the cost of amplifying noise in rare classes and destroying the linear relationship between brightness and physical cause — a warning that matters because equalized images leak into publications as if they were data). **Band combinations** exploit the signatures lesson: false-colour composites (near-infrared-red-green for vegetation vigour, the SWIR-heavy combinations that make burned scars magenta and active fires cyan), and the **band ratio** in its pure form — dividing two bands cancels shared illumination-and-topography-and-atmosphere terms, which is why the ratio idea underlies both the simplest clay mapping (SWIR over red) and the simplest vegetation metrics. **Indices** are ratios and normalized differences tuned to a target: NDVI (near-infrared-minus-red, bounded, saturating in dense canopy, soil-brightness sensitive — hence SAVI-and-its-T-family and the EVI's aerosol-resistant reformulation), the NDWI-and-NDMI water-and-built indices, the NBR-and-dNBR burned-severity index of the fire literature, the NDII moisture index, and the 2010s-and-2020s hyperspectral successors (the car-and-vegetation indices of the 2021–2024 EMIT era); every index inherits the calibration chain's error budget — an index computed across unharmonized sensors or uncorrected scenes is an index of the atmosphere, not the surface, the fallacy the warnings section returns to. **Principal component analysis** rotates the band space onto its covariance eigenvectors (the first two-or-three PCs holding most variance, the later ones holding noise — the noise-whitening and minimum-noise-fraction transforms of the fusion literature being its practical children), and **Tasseled Cap** rotates multispectral space onto fixed soil-brightness, greenness-and-wetness axes whose decades of use (the 1976-transaction-era transform, the 2014-and-2020 Landsat-and-Sentinel-2 coefficient refits) make it the oldest still-working feature extractor in the field. **Fourier-and-wavelet views** complete the analytic set: the 2-D Fourier transform turns periodic texture (crop rows, dune fields, runways, scan-line noise) into measurable peaks whose position-and-orientation are quantitative descriptors, while wavelets (the 1990s-into-2020s compression-and-fusion literature: the Laplacian pyramid as its practical form) localize frequency in space, which is what fusion-and-change need because edges and periodics are both local. The transform discipline throughout: every operation is invertible in principle or it is not — stretches-and-rotations are invertible, equalization is invertible only if you keep the map, smoothing-and-fusion are not — and irreversible operations on the only copy of a measurement is a curatorial sin the data-policy literature is only beginning to name.
:::

::: definition Filtering, resampling and fusion {#def-filtering}
A **convolution filter** replaces each pixel with a weighted sum of its neighbours; the kernel chooses the trade. Mean filters reduce noise by sqrt(n) over n pixels and blur edges identically; **median filters** (rank statistics, the 1975 classic) kill salt-and-pepper-and-radar-speckle outliers while preserving edges far better, which is why they are the default first step for radar and the default compositing rule (per-pixel median over a time window) for cloudy optical archives; **gradient-and-edge kernels** (Sobel, Prewitt, Canny's later statistical formulation) measure the first-and-second derivatives that the terrain-analysis and road-extraction literatures consume; **unsharp masking** (subtract a blurred copy, add the residual to the original) sharpens the mid-scale contrast the eye likes, at the cost of amplifying noise by exactly the amount it claims to remove — fine for display, forbidden before measurement. The **Gaussian-versus-Laplacian pair** organizes multiscale work: Gaussian blurring builds the pyramid, Laplacian (band-pass) levels store the detail that, summed back, reconstructs the image exactly — the Laplacian pyramid is both a compression scheme and, because its levels are spatially localized frequency bands, the natural arena for **image fusion**. **Fusion** merges a coarse multispectral cube with a fine panchromatic (or finer multispectral) image: component-substitution (swap the coarse intensity for the fine one — fast, colour-distorting), the intensity-HIS-and-Intensity transforms of the 1980s–1990s, the **pansharpening** generation (the 2005-and-2008 NNDiffuse-and-GS-and-HPF methods and their ERGAS-and-QNR spectral-and-spatial distortion metrics, typically holding spectral distortion under a few per cent at 2-m output), and the 2017–2025 deep-learning fusion (GAN-and-cascade-refiner networks that beat classical methods on sharpness and must be audited, via the same ERGAS-style metrics and via endmember-preservation tests, for the textures they can invent). **Resampling** — nearest, bilinear, cubic convolution, Lanczos — accompanies every reprojection-and-scale, each with its documented frequency response: nearest preserves each original value (the only acceptable choice for classified-and-radar-intensity data), cubic-and-Lanczos preserve edge sharpness while mixing neighbours (acceptable for reflectance, never for classes), and the 2-to-10-per-cent area-mixing at edges that any resampling introduces is the floor under every "change at boundaries" claim. The ledger rule for the whole definition: filtering trades resolution for precision, fusion trades spectral fidelity for spatial detail, resampling trades exactness for geometry — and a methods section that names the trades, with the kernel parameters and the metric values, is the difference between a processed image and a processed measurement.
:::

::: example NDVI time series: from 47 scenes to one defensible greenness curve {#ex-ndvi-ts}
Plotting greenness for one field across a Landsat-and-Sentinel-2 growing season looks trivial — extract, plot, done — and is in fact the field's best composite exam. The scenes must be surface-reflectance products (Landsat collection-2 L2SP, Sentinel-2 L2A with its scene-dependent quality caveats); harmonized (the 2016–2024 intercomparison residuals of one-to-three per cent, concentrated in the red-and-blue bands that NDVI divides, meaning the harmonization error is NDVI's error); BRDF-effects considered for the off-nadir Sentinel-2 swaths (a few per cent of NDVI at canopy closure, enough to bend a phenology curve); cloud-and-shadow-and-snow masked (the QA-and-Fmask-and-SCL layers, and the 2018–2024 studies showing unmasked thin cloud is the dominant NDVI outlier source in monsoon regions); gap-filled for Landsat-7's stripes or excluded; composited (the 8-or-16-day maximum-or-median NDVI convention that the MODIS era standardized precisely because per-pixel cloud masking leaves holes); and finally the curve itself read with the ambiguity of the ratio in mind — the red-edge quiz's "which term moved" question, answered by bringing in the SWIR moisture bands and the red-edge positions as co-witnesses. Done this way the 2000–2025 MODIS-and-Landsat phenology literature (the spring-greening-and-drought-browning analyses of the climate-change course) is reproducible to a few NDVI units; done any other way it reproduces the sensor's problems instead of the field's, and the 2017–2022 replication attempts that found "phenology trends" that vanished under harmonized reprocessing are the published cost of skipping steps.
:::

::: example Pansharpening Sentinel-2: 10 metres from 20, honestly {#ex-pansharpen}
Sentinel-2's red, green, blue and near-infrared bands arrive at 10 m, its red-edge-and-SWIR bands at 20 m, and every land-application user wants the whole cube at 10. The honest pipeline (the 2016–2024 pansharpening-benchmark literature's own recommendation) is: resample nothing by naive interpolation; run a component-substitution or GPAPS-style method and a no-reference-quality metric (QNR, D_s and ERGAS computed by degrading the 10-m bands to 20 m, sharpening, and comparing — the space-to-scale consistency protocol that makes the quality numbers mean something); check the spectral distortion band by band (well-behaved methods hold under one-to-two per cent reflectance distortion on average, but the SWIR bands and dark targets do worse); and archive both the sharpened product and the metric values, because every downstream classification of the 20-m-to-10-m sharpened cube carries the fusion's spectral error as a class-boundary bias — the 2019–2023 urban-mapping comparisons show a few per cent of class-area difference between fusion methods, more than the classifier's own variance. The example's generalization: fusion is a legitimate measurement only when its distortion is quantified by degradation experiments and reported; "it looks sharper" is an aesthetic, and the deep-learning fusion literature's 2020s reckoning (the invents-texture problem, the reference-free-metric debates) is what happens when a strong prior meets a weak audit.
:::

::: example Fourier diagnostics: finding the sensor, not the landscape {#ex-fourier}
Take a Sentinel-2 swath over homogeneous cropland, take its 2-D Fourier transform along the across-track direction, and read the spectrum: a well-behaved band shows smooth power decay; a band with detector-column anomalies (the 2023 Sentinel-2A incident, whose dead-and-drifting columns were visible as a raised broadband line-power floor weeks before the anomaly was operationally confirmed), with scan-line-and-mirror artifacts (the VIIRS banding of 2012–2018, a pure along-scan sinusoid in the transform), or with periodic geolocation jitter (the micro-oscillation peaks of the 2020s smallsat literature) shows narrow peaks at the artifact's spatial frequency — the transform as a stethoscope on the instrument. The same machinery does landscape science when the periodicity is real: dune-field wavelength-and-migration statistics (the dominant spectral peak as the transport diagnostic of the geomorphology course), crop-row orientation-and-spacing from historical scenes (the peak's azimuth as a tillage map), terracing-and-plantation censuses, and the 2010s-and-2020s urban-pattern metrics (the Fourier-based landscape-structure indices that quantify sprawl's characteristic scale). The method's caution is the sampling theorem: 10-m pixels Nyquist-limit real features to 20-m wavelengths and more, aliasing folds shorter-period texture (a ploughed field, a corrugated roof district) into false long-wave power, and any spectral peak near the Nyquist corner is a negotiation with the resampling kernel, which is why the filtering definition's "name the resampling" rule is a Fourier statement before it is a processing one.
:::

::: example Registration: the half-pixel that manufactures a flood map {#ex-registration}
Flood mapping by radar-or-optical differencing is, geometrically, a threshold on a difference image — and the difference image's dominant error is not radiometry but misregistration. Simulate it: two perfectly co-located 30-m reflectance scenes of a river town differ nowhere; shift one by half a pixel (15 m, well inside the absolute-geolocation accuracy of every commercial sensor) and the difference lights up at every roofline, field boundary and bridge — a "change map" whose accuracy is a pure function of edge density, exactly the artifact the 2015–2024 change-detection-quality literature catalogs (the "registration-induced change" simulations that now appear as a required ablation in serious flood-and-deforestation papers). The defense is the correction definition's relative-geolocation machinery: phase-correlation-or-cross-correlation tie-point estimation between the pair (sub-pixel by construction), a polynomial-or-thin-plate-spline warp, residual RMS reported in tenths of a pixel, and — for the remaining edge artifacts — morphological filtering of the change layer or, better, edge-aware post-classification comparison (the classification lesson's answer: classify each scene, then compare labels, which converts sub-pixel geometric noise into boundary slivers instead of area change). The quantitative floor is worth memorizing: at 0.3-pixel residual registration, a scene with 5 per cent edge-pixel fraction yields roughly half a per cent of its pixels in false change at conventional thresholds — which is why "the flood was 12 per cent larger than the map says" is usually a statement about the registration report, and why the Copernicus EMS-and-flood-precision literature of 2018–2025 quotes registration alongside inundation area.
:::

::: history Lunar pixels to analysis-ready cubes {#hist-processing}
**1964–1976.** The first large-scale digital image processing happens on the Lunar Orbiter photographs in 1964–1966 (the JPL-and-University-of-Minnesota restorations, scanned and enhanced before Landsat existed); Landsat-1's 1972 digital return makes pixel processing the default within months; the vegetation-index lineage formalizes fast (the 1973 Rouse–Haas–Schell–Deering normalized-difference report at the Goddard vegetation-lineage meetings, its roots in the plant-physiology spectrophotometry of the 1960s); and the transform toolkit arrives in applied form — Kauth-and-Thomas's 1976 Tasseled Cap, the 1970s–1980s median-and-gradient filter literature from the image-processing community, and the Fourier-texture analyses of the 1970s that made "spectral response pattern" a quantitative phrase. **1980s–2000s.** The theory deepens: wavelets (Alexandre Grossmann-and-Jean Morlet's 1984 geophysical source analysis, Stéphane Mallat's 1988 multiresolution framework), the PCA-and-MNF noise-adjusted transforms of the 1980s–1990s hyperspectral era, the 1980s–2000s pansharpening-and-fusion lineage, and the rigorous geometric-model era (the 1980s–2000s RPC-and-collinearity-and-orthorectification literature) that made sub-pixel registration routine. **2008–2024.** Policy and scale collide productively: Landsat's 2008 free release converts the archive into an analysis raw material (the 2010–2020 continuous-multipurpose-sensing-and-Google-Earth-Engine era, billions of pixels per study), the 2015-and-onward Sentinel data make the harmonization-and-fusion problems daily practice, the 2016–2024 collection-2-and-Sen2Cor-and-L2A quality campaigns put per-pixel uncertainty in the metadata, and the 2017–2025 deep-learning processing wave (change-detection-and-fusion-and-super-resolution networks, and the 2020s foundation-model era that inherits this lesson's entire correction chain as its preprocessing) closes the loop in which image processing became, for a generation of Earth scientists, not a preprocessing chore but the discipline that decides what their maps are worth.
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-median}
Why is a median filter preferred to a mean filter as the first step on radar or cloud-contaminated imagery?

- [ ] It is computationally faster for large windows
- [x] Rank statistics ignore outlier magnitudes, so salt-and-pepper spikes and thin-cloud pixels are removed without smearing the edges around them
- [ ] Because it sharpens edges, adding detail the sensor did not measure
- [ ] Because it preserves radiometric calibration exactly

::: solution :::
The median is a rank statistic: a single extreme value cannot move it much, so speckle-and-cloud outliers are replaced by typical neighbourhood values while step edges survive nearly intact (a mean filter would average the outlier into the result and blur the edge identically). Speed (A) is false for large windows; (C) inverts the truth (all smoothing blurs, median less); (D) is false — filtering changes pixel values by construction, which is the filtering definition's ledger.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-index}
A research group computes NDVI trends by mixing unharmonized Landsat-8 and Sentinel-2 surface-reflectance scenes without cross-sensor adjustment. The dominant risk is:

- [x] A sensor-transition step in the trend that is a red-band spectral-response difference, not a vegetation change
- [ ] Increased spatial resolution that oversamples the vegetation signal
- [ ] Atmospheric path radiance inflating the near-infrared band
- [ ] NDVI saturation in sparse vegetation masking greening

::: solution :::
The two sensors' red-and-near-infrared spectral response functions differ enough that the same surface yields NDVI offsets of a few units (the harmonization literature's one-to-three-per-cent reflectance residuals, concentrated exactly in the dividing bands), so a 2015-or-2016-or-2020 sensor switch can inject a step the size of a decade of greening — the NDVI-time-series example's core exam point. Saturation (D) is real but affects dense, not sparse, canopy; (B) is a non-issue for trends; (C) is handled by the reflectance products they did use.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="85"}
A linear stretch maps digital numbers 40–190 onto 0–255. What output value does an input of 90 receive, rounded to the nearest integer?
::: solution :::
Output = (90−40)/(190−40) × 255 = 50/150 × 255 = 85, i.e. **85** — the percentile-clip arithmetic of the stretch definition, and a good reminder that the stretch is a pure reordering: nothing about the surface enters or leaves, only the display changes.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="256"}
How many grey levels does an 8-bit image store?
::: solution :::
2⁸ = **256** — the display budget every stretch must live inside (which is why 12-and-16-bit analysis data are stretched before viewing), and the reason histogram-equalization's promise of "using all the levels" is a statement about the display, not the measurement.
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="9"}
A 30-m pixel is resampled to a 10-m grid. How many output pixels does one input pixel contribute to?
::: solution :::
(30/10)² = **9** — every up-sampling mixes neighbours nine times over with bilinear-or-cubic kernels (the nearest-neighbour exception preserves the single value and the blockiness), and this area-mixing is the floor under boundary-change claims that the registration example quantifies for the change case.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="11"}
A 5-pixel window contains the values 12, 9, 10, 11 and 100 (a spike). What does a median filter output for the centre pixel?
::: solution :::
Sorted: 9, 10, 11, 12, 100 — the median is **11**, the spike ignored entirely; a mean filter would have output 28.4, spreading the spike's influence over the neighbourhood, which is the median-quiz's rank-statistics argument in five numbers.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="25"}
Pansharpening produces a 2-m multispectral cube from 10-m bands. How many output pixels correspond to one original 10-m pixel?
::: solution :::
(10/2)² = **25** — each output pixel is an inference about a quarter-of-a-10-m-pixel's area, constrained (not determined) by the panchromatic detail, which is why the fusion definition demands degradation-tested spectral-distortion metrics: 25 invented sub-pixels per measured pixel is a strong prior with a measurement's clothing on.
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="23"}
How many 16-day composite periods fit in one 365-day year (round to the nearest integer)?
::: solution :::
365/16 = 22.8, about **23** — the MODIS 16-day compositing cadence's calendar, chosen to tile the year with cloud-minimizing windows (the maximum-NDVI convention keeping the clearest-or-greenest pixel), and the reason MODIS phenology products report 23 time steps per year, a granularity the daily VIIRS successors inherit as an option rather than a necessity.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="16"}
A time series is sampled every 8 days. By the Nyquist criterion, what is the shortest oscillation period (in days) it can resolve without aliasing?
::: solution :::
Twice the sampling interval: 2 × 8 = **16 days** — the sampling theorem of the Fourier example, which is why 16-day MODIS composites cannot see sub fortnightly phenology or irrigation cycles (the 2010s-and-2020s daily-dense Sentinel-and-constellation phenology literature exists to get under it), and why 8-day Landsat-and-Sentinel fusion products claim 8-day dynamics only at the cost of the fusion's own error budget.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="0.026"}
NDVI = (N−R)/(N+R) with N = 0.45 and R = 0.15, each with independent uncertainty 0.01. Using the linear (delta-method) propagation with partial derivatives ∂NDVI/∂N = 2R/(N+R)² and ∂NDVI/∂R = −2N/(N+R)², give the NDVI uncertainty to three decimal places.
::: solution :::
Sum = 0.60, sum² = 0.36: ∂NDVI/∂N = 0.30/0.36 = 0.833; ∂NDVI/∂R = −0.90/0.36 = −2.50; σ = sqrt((0.833×0.01)² + (2.50×0.01)²) = sqrt(0.0000694 + 0.000625) = sqrt(0.000694) = 0.0263, about **0.026** — note the red band's derivative is three times the near-infrared's: NDVI's error is dominated by its denominator's red term, so the harmonization literature's red-band residuals are, precisely and quantitatively, NDVI's cross-sensor error.
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="25000"}
A 1000 × 1000-pixel scene has 5 per cent edge pixels. A misregistered image pair flags half of the edge pixels as false change at the working threshold. How many false-change pixels are produced?
::: solution :::
1000 × 1000 × 0.05 × 0.5 = **25000 pixels** (2.5 per cent of the scene) — the registration example's arithmetic made explicit, and the reason change maps are filtered, edge-masked or produced by post-classification comparison: false change is not random noise, it is a deterministic function of the landscape's edge density and the registration report's residual, and any change study that has not measured both has measured the geometry.

::: warning Four image-processing fallacies
1. **"I corrected it, so it is corrected."** Each correction in the chain is a model with inputs, and the inputs carry errors that the correction does not report: atmospheric correction with a wrong aerosol model leaves reflectance errors of several per cent structured like the aerosol field (dust plumes becoming soil boundaries, smoke becoming burn scars — the em-radiation lesson's fourth fallacy in processing form); topographic normalization overcorrects steep shaded slopes in a documented, terrain-dependent way; BRDF normalization mis-models row crops and snow; and the 2016–2024 quality-flag literature (the Sen2Cor scene-classification-and-SCL overconfidence studies, the collection-2 dark-target anomaly analyses) keeps finding that the flags are optimistic exactly where the science is hardest — dark surfaces, aerosol events, snow edges. The corrected pixel is a model output with an unquantified error term unless the user propagates it, and "surface reflectance, therefore trustworthy" is the chain's oldest category error.
2. **"The index is the property."** NDVI is not greenness, NBR is not burn severity, NDWI is not water: each is one functional of a reflectance vector, tuned to one mechanism, confounded by the others — the saturation, soil-background, sensor-response and which-term-moved problems of the index quiz and the NDVI example, compounded by the 2010s-and-2020s replication record in which index-based trends (Amazon browning, Sahel greening, boreal shrubbing) repeatedly required index-independent witnesses (the red edge, the microwave, the field plots) before they were settled. The rule costs nothing and saves careers: every index claim gets a second, mechanistically independent band-pair or sensor, and every index paper states, in its limitations section and not its reviewers' letters, what would falsify it.
3. **"Sharpened is better."** The fusion example's audit applies to every enhancement that adds apparent detail: unsharp-masked images convince geologists of lineaments that are noise spectra (the 1970s–1990s visual-enhancement literature's own controlled trials found this), deep-learning super-resolution passes every visual test while hallucinating textures that its own papers' degradation tests catch only when sought, and the pansharpening metrics (ERGAS, QNR) exist because spectral distortion is invisible to the eye that commissioned the sharpening. The processing rule is absolute: enhancement for display, never before measurement; if a classification-or-change result changes when the enhancement is removed, the enhancement was the result.
4. **"The archive is a dataset."** It is a library with duplicate copies, missing pages, and different editions: Landsat collection-1-versus-collection-2 radiometric-and-geometric differences (the 2019–2022 studies showing trend estimates moving with the collection), processing-baseline steps inside single products (the MODIS collection-6-to-6.1 analyses), sensor degradations that reprocessing does not fully absorb (the Landsat-5 TM band-4-and-5 aging, the ETM+ band-5 warming, the Sentinel-2A 2023 columns), and resampling-history differences between USGS-and-Copernicus distributions of nominally identical scenes. The 2020s analysis-ready-and-versioned-data movement (the STAC-and-DAGSTOR-and-collection-versioning machinery) is the community's admission that processing did not end at launch — and the user's obligation follows: cite the collection, the baseline, the date of processing, and re-run, not merely re-plot, when the archive reissues.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Digital image processing is an error ledger kept in two halves. The **correction half** turns sensor counts into surface measurements and surface measurements into map coordinates: radiometric calibration (digital numbers to radiance, traceable to spheres-and-deserts), atmospheric correction (path subtracted, transmittance divided, one-to-three per cent residuals when the aerosol model cooperates), BRDF-and-topography normalization (geometry removed, model error left), rigorous geometric correction and sub-pixel co-registration (the half-pixel that manufactures flood maps being the cost of skipping it) — and every corrected pixel is a model output whose uncertainty the user must carry, because the quality flags are optimistic exactly where the science is hard. The **enhancement-and-analysis half** makes the corrected cube legible and computable: stretches that reorder without informing, band ratios that cancel shared terms, indices whose cross-sensor steps are red-band physics wearing a vegetation costume, transforms (PCA, Tasseled Cap, Fourier, wavelets) that rotate-and-localize the data into feature spaces, and filters-and-fusion whose trades — resolution for precision, spectral fidelity for spatial detail, exactness for geometry — are quantified by degradation tests or not at all. The examples are one exam in four parts: the NDVI time series (every correction step graded at once), pansharpening (25 inferred sub-pixels per measured pixel, audited or not), Fourier diagnostics (the transform as a stethoscope on the instrument and as a tillage-and-dune map), and registration (false change as a deterministic function of edge density). The fallacies — corrected-for-correct, index-for-property, sharpened-for-better, archive-for-dataset — are all the ledger unkept, and the lesson's whole discipline fits in its inverse: **name every operation, its kernel, its metric and its version, and let no enhancement touch a number that a conclusion will rest on.** With surface reflectance honestly in hand, the next lesson can finally ask what the pixels are.
:::
