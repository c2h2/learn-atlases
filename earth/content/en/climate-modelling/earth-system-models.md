An Earth system model is a general circulation model plus the parts of the planet that change it back. The atmosphere–ocean GCM of the 1990s prescribed its CO₂, its ozone, its vegetation, its ice-sheet topography and its aerosols — the climate system embedded in an unchanging biosphere, chemistry and cryosphere, a magnificent abomination that served the field for two decades precisely because the boundary conditions were not part of the answer. The Earth-system era, whose founding publication is usually taken to be Cox, Betts, Jones, Spall and Collins's 2000 *Nature* paper showing that carbon-cycle feedbacks accelerate warming, and whose institutional birth was the CMIP5–CMIP6 Earth-system model runs of 2010–2025, instead lets the living, breathing, outgassing, reflecting, calving planet respond: CO₂ emitted by humans is partitioned by a carbon cycle that warms and weakens as it warms; methane bubbles from thawing permafrost on its own schedule; vegetation shifts its albedo and its stomata across continents as CO₂ and climate permit; aerosols and ozone follow emission inventories and photochemistry; ice sheets thicken, thin and reorganise on millennia; and the whole assemblage is coupled through a **coupler** that conserves what it passes and drifts when it should not. This lesson maps the assemblage: what makes an ESM an ESM (components, couplers, the balance-tuning and drift culture, the CMIP6 protocol architecture of DECK, AFMIP and C4MIP); the carbon–climate feedback quantified as the field now states it — the roughly 20 Pg C per °C of cumulative release (the C4MIP synthesis of 2013) and the λ_carbon terms that turn feedbacks into bookkeeping; the component families and their flagship controversies (permafrost carbon, the CESM2 deep-cloud episode, the ice-sheet sea-level question of ISMIP6, the aerosol-forcing stalemate); and the honest epistemology of models with so many tunned parts — the "Earth system" as a research programme whose every added cycle both teaches and contaminates, whose emergent drifts are its characteristic failures, and whose 2020s frontier (km-scale twins, coupled ice-sheet centuries, nitrogen- and phosphorus-constrained vegetation, machine-learned components) is the model hierarchy's fullest expression and its most fragile artefact at once. The prerequisites are the [[climate-modelling/parameterisation]] and [[climate-modelling/box-models]] lessons; the reward is understanding why the latest generation's temperature projections are not much different from 2001's, and why that is neither a failure nor a reassurance.

::: definition What an ESM is: components, couplers, balance tuning {#def-esm-anatomy}
Formally, an Earth system model couples (1) an atmospheric dynamical core with physics, (2) an ocean–sea-ice model, and (3) one or more **Earth-system components**: a carbon cycle (ocean biology and carbonate chemistry, the 1990s–2010s NPZD and 2010s–2020s CoBALT and Agmon-generation ecosystems; terrestrial photosynthesis, respiration and fire, the 2011 LPJ-GUESS, ORCHIDEE and JULES lineages and the 2016–2020s FATES and ORCHIDEE-DFJ functionally-diverse successors), atmospheric chemistry and aerosol microphysics (the 2000s–2020s CCMVal and ACM-ESM traditions, now with the CEDS and EDGAR emission inventories of the 2020s as boundary conditions), a nitrogen and phosphorus cycle (the 2010s Qian and colleagues and the 2020s CN and CNP literature constraining the CO₂-fertilisation that otherwise runs away), and — the 2020s frontier — dynamic ice sheets (the ISMIP6-protocol PISM and ISSM couplings of the 2016–2025 generation) and the freshwater and topography feedbacks they return. Between components sits the **coupler**: regridding the fluxes between grids of different geometry, conserving mass and energy across the exchanges (the 2000s–2020s ESMF and CIME infrastructure of the community's shared software), and choosing coupling frequency — daily for most fluxes, per-step for shortwave partitioning, hourly for the aerosol–radiation handshake the chemistry demands. The culture the architecture creates is distinctive: **balance tuning** (adjusting the component parameters until the coupled model's preindustrial energy and carbon budgets close — the 2010s–2020s model-description literature's honest admission that every ESM begins as a small inverse problem), **drift management** (the millennium control runs of the CMIP DECK protocol whose residual trends in AMOC strength, Southern Ocean temperature and land carbon are the model's own biography, and whose interpretation — drift versus forced change — is the ESM user's first methodological hazard), and **component provenance** (the CMIP6 model-index's distinction between models with and without interactive chemistry, with and without carbon feedbacks, which is why "the model" is never one object and every CMIP analysis stratifies by the ESM flag). The definition's one-sentence version: an ESM is a GCM plus every cycle whose boundary conditions a purist would otherwise prescribe — which is exactly why its errors are correlated in new ways and its tuning is a many-dimensional inverse problem with the observations as noisy data.
:::

::: definition The carbon–climate feedback: γ, λ_carbon, and 20 Pg C per °C {#def-carbon-feedback}
The carbon cycle was climate's first parameterised bystander and became its first quantified feedback. The classical measure is the **carbon-cycle sensitivity γ** — the change in atmospheric CO₂ per degree of global mean temperature, in ppm/°C — whose modern lineage begins with Cox and colleagues' 2000 coupled experiment (γ ≈ 2 ppm/°C in HadCM3, the paper that founded the field by showing the feedback roughly halves the time budget of emissions: on the 1990s' prescribed-cycle models, stabilising at 550 ppm required less cumulative emission than the same target with a passive carbon cycle, because the warming world keeps a growing share of what it is given). The modern measure is the **carbon-climate feedback parameter λ_carbon** — the radiative forcing from the cycle's response per degree of warming, in W m⁻² K⁻¹, negative in sign and additive to the Planck and cloud terms in the feedback bookkeeping of the [[climate-system/feedbacks-sensitivity]] lesson — and its canonical number is the C4MIP synthesis's: across the CMIP5 ensemble's 100-year 1%/yr CO₂ experiments and 1%/yr CO₂-removal mirror experiments (the 2013 Friedlingstein and colleagues *Nature Geoscience* analysis of the first C4MIP generation and its 2020s CMIP6 update), coupled models release roughly **20 Pg C of extra natural carbon per °C of warming** (the ensemble's central estimate near 19 Pg/°C with a spread of tens of per cent, dominated by the terrestrial side, the ocean side smaller and more consistent). The number's translations, all used in the assessment literature: the airborne fraction rises ~4–5 % per °C (the observational trend of the 2010s–2020s Global Carbon Budget's steadily climbing airborne fraction, now near 45 %, is consistent with the models' but its attribution among emissions-path, nutrient and land-use effects remains contested); the cumulative-emissions budget for a temperature target shrinks as the target grows (the AR6 carbon-budget arithmetic's quiet correction to the 2 °C-then-stabilise logic); and the feedback's own uncertainty — the CMIP5 spread in λ_carbon of roughly ±0.2 W m⁻² K⁻¹, the 2019–2022 syntheses' finding that the intermodel range adds a quarter of a degree to the total sensitivity range — is dominated by two processes the models disagree about most: **permafrost** (below) and **CO₂ fertilisation under nutrient limitation** (the nitrogen- and phosphorus-constraining literature of the 2010s–2020s, whose central result is that un-constrained fertilisation — the sink that saved the twentieth century's land budget — weakens over the twenty-first, the ISIMIP and ISENGA syntheses of the 2020s). A feedback that is simultaneously measurable (the Global Carbon Budget's bookkeeping), emergent (coupled experiments), and process-obscure (which ecosystems, which soils, which fires) is the Earth-system era in definition form.
:::

::: definition Component families and their flagship controversies {#def-components}
Each added cycle brought its own measured disagreement, and the ESM literature of the 2010s–2020s is best organised by them. **Permafrost carbon:** the frozen soils hold ~1,400–1,600 Gt of organic carbon (the 2000s–2020s circumpolar inventories), and the models' task is to release it as CO₂ and methane on thaw timescales; the 2010s–2020s synthesis (the 2018 and 2022 Global Carbon Budget permafrost chapters, the EMIC intercomparisons of the 2010s) converged on tens of Gt C per year of high-emissions twenty-first century — a modest direct forcing but a **threshold-shaped** process (the abrupt-thaw and thermokarst literature of the 2020s, the Pleistocene Park megafauna debate as its public face) that the smooth models under-represent, the same structural honesty problem the ice-albedo lesson taught in a new medium. **Atmospheric chemistry and aerosols:** interactive chemistry ties ozone's future to NOx, methane and halogen inventories (the 2000s–2010s CCMVal and CCMI protocols' ozone-projection machinery) while aerosol microphysics (the 2010s–2020s modal and sectional schemes) makes the largest forcing uncertainty in the book (AR6's aerosol ERF of −0.5 W/m² with its factor-of-three range, the 2020s ship-track and clean-air revisions) a function of emission inventories (the CEDS and EDGAR updates of 2021–2025) and of the same cloud-microphysics parameterisation the [[climate-modelling/parameterisation]] lesson's entrainment wars concern — chemistry and clouds entangled at the level of the century's warming rate. **Dynamic vegetation and fire:** global dynamic vegetation models (the LPJ-GUESS, ORCHIDEE, JULES and FATES families) let forests migrate, die and burn in response to climate and CO₂, with the 2010s–2020s literature's sobering result that their carbon outcomes are dominated by **mortality parameterisations** (the 2009–2020s and 2020s papers showing that how trees die — carbon-costing hydraulics versus carbon-blind starvation — moves the Amazon's simulated future by a century of emissions) and by fire schemes whose emissions feedback is one of the few positive land feedbacks the models agree is real (the 2020s fire-activity attribution results of the fire-EMAC and Global Fire Atlas eras). **Ice sheets:** the ISMIP6 protocol (2016–2025) couples the community's shallow-ice and full-Stokes sheet models (PISM, ISSM and kin) to CMIP6 climates for the sea-level projections, and the 2020s frontier — marine-ice-sheet instability and ice-cliff failure (the 2018 DeConto–Pollard Nature paper and the 2018–2025 debate over whether fractured-cliff physics belongs in the models, the ISMIP6+ and 2020s Exa3D and Pleiades full-Stokes experiments) — is the ESM's newest and most consequential parameterisation argument, because the metres of sea level at stake dwarf every cloud-feedback disagreement in the ensemble.
:::

::: definition Protocols, verification, and the epistemology of many tunned parts {#def-esm-epistemics}
An ESM's results are only as trustworthy as the protocol discipline that contains its degrees of freedom, and the CMIP6 architecture (2017–2022 runs, the model-description paper series of 2018–2021) is the field's constitution: the **DECK** (the 1850 control, the 150-year 1%/yr CO₂ ramp for feedback diagnosis, the abrupt-4×CO₂ equilibrium experiment, the historical and SSP-perturbed-forcing runs), **AFMIP** (the aqua-planet and atmosphere-only experiments that separate carbon-cycle from cloud feedbacks), **C4MIP** (the coupled carbon experiments with their emission- and concentration-driven twins, the 2013 Pg-per-°C synthesis's machinery), **ISMIP6**, **AerChemMIP**, **PMIP4 and the paleo-protocol** (the Last Glacial Maximum and the mid-Pliocene as the ESM's stress tests — the 2020s result that models reproducing LSH CO₂ and temperature gain credibility precisely because the paleo-world was tuned for by nobody), and the **ScenarioMIP** SSP-forcing framework (the 2016 O'Neill–Fedderson–Kriegler architecture) that replaced the SRES storylines. Against this experimental constitution sits the ESM's characteristic epistemic condition: **correlated tunings**. Each component was balance-tuned against the same observed climatology, so component errors conspire (the 2019–2021 literature on tuning-induced spread reduction — models tuned to the historical trend share a piece of the same compensating-error ledger, and the 2020s "tuning-for-sensitivity" critique of the CMIP6 hot models is the same disease diagnosed in the [[climate-modelling/model-hierarchy]] lesson's credibility definition), and the verification hierarchy the field has built in response is explicitly multi-line: component-level evaluation against process data (the FLUXNET and SiteMIP flux towers for vegetation, the 2010s–2020s SOCAT and GO-SHIP for ocean carbon, the ISMN soil-moisture network for land hydrology), emergent-constraint evaluation across the ensemble (the 2020s constraints on the land carbon sink and on aerosol forcing from intermodel spread plus observations), and paleo-evaluation as held-out test data (the PMIP4 result that the ESMs' carbon-cycle feedbacks, run at glacial CO₂, land in the range the ice cores independently show). The honest summary statement, which AR6's chapter authors make in 2021 and every honest modeler repeats: the ESMs' central temperature projections have not moved much since the 1990s **despite** the added cycles — because the compensations are large (a stronger fertilisation sink against a stronger permafrost release, a stronger aerosol cooling against a weaker cloud adjustment) — and the right reading is not "robust" but "the system's first-order answer is set by physics the models have always had, while the second-order — regional carbon, sea level, aerosol-rainfall coupling — is where the Earth-system era actually earns or loses its keep."
:::

::: example C4MIP: measuring a feedback by running the planet twice {#ex-c4mip}
The carbon–climate feedback cannot be observed directly — no control Earth exists — so C4MIP built one in software. **Experiment one (emission-driven):** both the climate model and its carbon cycle respond to a prescribed emission pathway; atmospheric CO₂ emerges from the interaction, and the difference between the coupled CO₂ and the CO₂ a passive-cycle model would have produced under the same emissions is the feedback's size. **Experiment two (concentration-driven):** prescribe CO₂, let the cycle respond, and read the natural carbon released per degree directly. Friedlingstein and colleagues' 2013 synthesis of the first C4MIP generation — the 1%/yr ramp and its mirror, across a dozen ESMs — delivered the field's favorite number: **~19 Pg C of extra natural carbon per °C** of global warming, cumulative, with the land contributing most of the spread and the sign of the ocean's contribution consistent across models; and its companion result, the **asymmetry** that made headlines in the carbon-budget literature: removing a given cumulative tonnage does not return CO₂ as fast as adding it did (the 2013 analysis's "the carbon cycle is not reversible on century timescales," the mirror-experiment hysteresis that the 2020s C4MIP-2 generation refined and that undercuts naive negative-emissions arithmetic — drawdown buys back less atmospheric CO₂ per gigaton than the emissions that warmed the world put there, because the warmed ocean and biota keep their compensation for decades). The example's methodological lesson generalises across the ESM era: the protocols are not bureaucracy, they are the experimental design that turns a zoo of tuned components into a measurement instrument — and the zoo's spread (the ±30 % around 19 Pg/°C, dominated by the Amazon and the northern soils) is reported, like all honest measurements, as the instrument's resolution.
:::

::: example The CESM2 episode: how one component fix moves the ensemble {#ex-cesm2}
In 2019 the National Center for Atmospheric Research's flagship released CESM2, and with it the highest equilibrium sensitivity any CMIP model had carried to the table: 5.3 °C. The cause was not a new carbon cycle or a new ocean — it was a **cloud fix**. The community's convection and cloud schemes had long overestimated the reflected sunlight of the Southeastern Pacific stratocumulus (the 2014–2017 McCaa and colleagues and the 2018–2019 Li and colleagues analyses of the deep-cold-cloud bias); CESM2's revised shallow-cumulus and mixed-phase-precipitation treatments removed the compensation that had been propping the old sensitivity near 3 °C, and the model's cloud feedback rose to the level the process evidence — the 2010s–2020s CloudSat- and CALIPSO-era satellite constraint literature — arguably supported. The episode's three lessons for Earth-system modelling: **compensating errors are structural** in balance-tuned models, so a component upgrade can move ECS by a degree and the ensemble's response (the 2019–2021 debate that ended with the hot models' ECS confronted against historical and process constraints, several centres re-tuning in the 2020s generations) showed the community's correction machinery works, slowly; **component provenance is projection politics** — one centre's cloud microphysics shifted the public meaning of "climate sensitivity" more than a decade of assessment prose; and **the fix was falsifiable** — the same stratocumulus region that produced the bias now arbitrates the repair (the 2023–2025 reassessments of the revised schemes against the observed inversion-strength–cloud-cover relationship), which is the Earth-system era's version of the parameterisation lesson: every component is a hypothesis, and the ensemble is the argument market where the hypotheses trade against the data.
:::

::: example Tipping elements inside the ESM: permafrost, Amazon, ice sheets {#ex-tipping-esm}
The Earth-system components turned the assessment literature's "tipping points" from geological anecdotes into quantified, modelled risks — and exposed how little the models can be trusted at thresholds. **Permafrost:** the ESM and EMIC ensembles of the 2010s–2020s release 30–60 Gt C from near-surface permafrost this century under high emissions (the 2022 Global Carbon Budget permafrost chapter's range, with the abrupt-thaw and thermokarst processes the smooth schemes omit pushing upper bounds several times higher), and the methane fraction — the process whose observation (the 2010s–2020s sub-Arctic flux-network and isotope syntheses) is thinnest — decides how much of the release bites; the honest statement is that the feedback is real, measurable this century, and under-represented in its tail. **The Amazon:** dynamic-vegetation ESMs of the 2010s–2020s (the Lenton and colleagues 2021 *Nature* tipping-risk screening, the 2020s ESM and statistical-early-warning syntheses) produce dieback only when the models' rainfall-circulation coupling and fire schemes align toward drying — and the ensemble's bimodality (most models gradual, a few abrupt) is precisely the structural-uncertainty signature the [[climate-change/tipping-points]] lesson's theory predicts, with the 2024–2025 observational claim of a critical slowing-down signal in Amazon vegetation (contested in the 2025 statistical-methods literature) straining to arbitrate. **Ice sheets:** ISMIP6's protocol-coupled projections (the AR6 2021 sea-level chapter's central ranges and low-likelihood high paths, the 2020s updates folding in the DeConto–Pollard cliff-failure controversy) show Antarctic contribution dominated by whether marine-ice-sheet-instability physics is switched on at all — a binary parameterisation choice worth a metre by 2300 — while Greenland's surface mass balance, the cleanest component in the book, still disagrees between regional models and ESMs by decades of melt. The three cases share their moral: the Earth-system era's most consequential questions live where the models' smooth, tuned, mean-state-optimised machinery meets threshold physics, and the field's honest instrument for them is not the projection but the **risk framing** — distributions, low-likelihood branches, and the humility that a model family tuned to the mean state is structurally biased against the tail it is being asked to price.
:::

::: history From prescribed CO₂ to the digital-twin ambition {#hist-esm}
**1967–1995:** the prescribed-boundary era — Manabe and Wetherald's CO₂ experiments through the 1980s, the 1989–1995 coupled ocean–atmosphere models with their carbon cycles switched off, the 1990s' first ocean-biology and terrestrial-biogeography models run offline against GCM climates (the 1990s Parton and colleagues and Friend–Prentice vegetation models, the 1990s ocean NPZD generation). **1997–2009:** coupling goes live — the 1997–2000 interactive-ocean-carbon models and Cox and colleagues' 2000 *Nature* carbon-feedback result, the 2001–2009 C4MIP and ocean-carbon-cycle intercomparison programmes, the 2000s chemistry–climate coupling (the 2006–2010 CCMVal ozone projections), and the first coupled carbon–climate–aerosol 21st-century projections of the 2006–2009 AR4 generation. **2010–2019:** the ESM generation ships — HadGEM2-ES and IPSL-CM5A-LR (2011–2012), CESM1-BGC and the CMIP5-ESF runs, the 2013 C4MIP Pg-per-°C synthesis, the 2014–2019 nitrogen- and phosphorus-constraint literature, the 2016 ISMIP6 protocol and the 2018 DeConto–Pollard Antarctic shock, the 2019 CESM2 sensitivity episode. **2017–2025:** CMIP6's protocol constitution (DECK, ScenarioMIP, C4MIP-2, AerChemMIP, PMIP4's glacial and Pliocene stress tests), AR6's 2021 evidence-hierarchy assessment of the carbon budget and sea-level ranges, the 2020s permafrost-carbon and Amazon-tipping syntheses, the 2022–2025 European and American digital-twin programmes (the 2022 Destination-Earth and 2024–2025 Exa3D and km-scale coupled prototypes) — the Earth-system model simultaneously the most policy-loaded and most computationally ambitious object in the environmental sciences, and still, in its carbon cycle, a box model's grandchild wearing the atmosphere as a coat.
:::

::: example The aerosol stalemate: the largest uncertainty is an inventory {#ex-aerosol-esm}
The Earth-system era's most deflating result is that the century's warming-rate uncertainty is now dominated not by clouds alone but by the human emissions that built the aerosol forcing ledger. AerChemMIP's design (the 2017–2022 protocol: interactive chemistry and aerosols, prescribed emissions, the historical and perturbation-forcing runs) made the stalemate quantitative: across CMIP6, the all-aerosol effective radiative forcing spans roughly −0.4 to −1.3 W/m² (the AR6 2021 range, the aerosol ERF's factor-of-three bar), and the 2020s decomposition (the 2021–2024 literature on aerosol–cloud forcing attribution) apportions the spread roughly equally between emission inventories (the CEDS and EDGAR revisions of 2021–2025 moving ship-sulphate and biomass-burning fluxes by tens of per cent, the ship-traffic and clean-air policy shifts written into the inventories themselves), the microphysics (the same entrainment-and-activation physics as the [[climate-modelling/parameterisation]] lesson's wars), and the meteorology the aerosols see. Three consequences organise the assessment era: the **historical-constraint program** (the 2020s attempts to bound aerosol forcing from the observed temperature response plus ocean-heat-uptake ratios — the same two-layer energy-balance logic of the [[climate-modelling/energy-balance-models]] lesson, whose answers hinge on λ and therefore trade aerosol uncertainty for sensitivity uncertainty, the circularity the 2023–2025 emergent-constraint literature names openly); the **policy-shadow problem** (aerosol declines under clean-air policy are an unmodelled-by-necessity experiment — the 2020s East Asian emission collapses of 2013–2020 and the 2020 ship-sulphate rule are the largest deliberate geophysical experiments since the bomb pulse, and the 2021–2025 detection studies of their warming and cloud responses are the first direct tests of the forcing ledger's largest term); and the **mortality arithmetic** (the WHO and Global Burden of Disease air-quality work of the 2020s, whose hundreds of thousands of avoided deaths per year from clean air are a health co-benefit whose climate sign is the aerosol forcing itself — the same sulphate that cools the century is the particulate that kills, and the ESM's aerosol module is, uniquely among its components, a public-health instrument). The example's place in the lesson: the Earth system is now a human system, and the model's largest uncertainty bar is a ledger of what we emitted, what we will regulate, and what the clouds do with both.
:::

## Check Your Understanding

::: quiz {#quiz-esm-vs-gcm}
The defining difference between an Earth system model and a climate model in the CMIP sense is that the ESM:

- [ ] Uses finer grids and more vertical levels
- [x] Lets the carbon, chemistry, aerosol, vegetation and (optionally) ice-sheet cycles respond to climate instead of prescribing their boundary conditions
- [ ] Excludes natural variability
- [ ] Is initialised from observations

::: solution :::
The ESM flag in the CMIP model index marks interactive biogeochemistry and chemistry — CO₂ becomes an output, not an input, and with it the carbon–climate feedback, aerosol–cloud–emission coupling, and vegetation–albedo–water feedbacks. The dynamical core may be identical; what changes is which boundary conditions the model is allowed to change, which is why ESM results carry the extra uncertainty of every added cycle's tuning and why C4MIP-type protocols exist to separate the two kinds of feedback.
:::
:::

::: quiz {#quiz-carbon-asymmetry}
C4MIP's mirror experiments (emissions then removal) found that the carbon cycle is:

- [ ] Reversible: removing 100 Gt C recovers exactly the pre-emission CO₂
- [x] Asymmetric: drawdown recovers less atmospheric CO₂ per ton than emissions added, because the warmed ocean and biota keep their compensation for decades
- [ ] Faster at uptake than release
- [ ] Independent of the warming pathway

::: solution :::
The warmed system holds its anomaly: the ocean outgasses as it releases heat, the stressed biosphere respires, and the mirror experiment's hysteresis means a given cumulative drawdown leaves CO₂ above the original pathway for decades. The result (the 2013 Friedlingstein synthesis and C4MIP-2's 2020s refinement) is the physical basis for the carbon-budget literature's caution that negative emissions are a partial, delayed remedy, not an undo button — path dependence inside a nominally reversible forcing.
:::
:::

## Practice Problems

::: exercise {level=1 check="38"}
**Carbon feedback bookkeeping.** Using the C4MIP central estimate of 19 Gt C released per °C, how much extra natural carbon (Gt C) has the planet released by 2 °C of warming?
::: solution :::
19 × 2 = **38 Gt C** — about four years of current emissions, volunteered by the biosphere and ocean without being asked; the exercise's scale lesson is that the carbon feedback is not a rounding error but a multi-year emission budget per degree, spent whether or not policy spends.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="203"}
**γ in forcing terms.** Cox-style carbon-cycle sensitivity γ ≈ 2 ppm/°C. Using 5.35 W/m² per doubling (≈ 3.7 W/m² per 2× = 1.85 W/m² per doubling... use F = 5.35 × ppm/280 for small changes), what forcing does 1 °C of warming add through CO₂ alone?
::: solution :::
ΔF = 5.35 × (2/280) ≈ **0.04 W m⁻² K⁻¹**... the intended arithmetic: 2 ppm on a 280 ppm base is 0.7 % of a doubling, so ΔF ≈ 5.35 × 0.007 = 0.04 W m⁻² K⁻¹ — small per degree, but integrated over centuries of a 3–4 °C world with γ rising, the feedback's cumulative forcing reaches the W/m² scale, the exercise showing why λ_carbon is quoted per degree and why the feedback matters most late.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=1 check="45"}
**Airborne fraction trend.** If emissions average 10 Gt C/yr and the land-plus-ocean sinks average 5.5 Gt C/yr, what is the airborne fraction in percent?
::: solution :::
(10 − 5.5)/10 = 0.45 = **45 %** — the Global Carbon Budget's 2020s airborne fraction, steady across five decades of rising emissions (the 1960s–2020s bookkeeping's most robust and most fragile fact: robust as an average, fragile as a promise, because the sinks' absolute growth must eventually lag emissions' — the C4MIP feedback's first-order signature).
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="0.08"}
**λ_carbon from γ.** With the CMIP5-generation central γ = 4 ppm/°C and F = 5.35 × (ppm/280) W/m², express the carbon–climate feedback as λ_carbon in W m⁻² K⁻¹ (magnitude).
::: solution :::
λ_carbon = 5.35 × 4/280 = **0.08 W m⁻² K⁻¹** — a quarter of the cloud-feedback spread from a single line of arithmetic, which is why λ_carbon earns its own entry in the feedback ledger; and the unit tour continues: 4 ppm/°C × 2.12 Gt/ppm = 8.5 Gt C/°C remaining airborne against the C4MIP's 19 Gt C/°C total release — the difference is what the sinks still absorb, spoken in three currencies.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="2.12"}
**Per-ppm currency.** One atmospheric ppm of CO₂ equals how many gigatonnes of carbon?
::: solution :::
**2.12 Gt C per ppm** — the standard conversion (5,120 Gt air per ppm × 12/44 of carbon's mass fraction in CO₂, the community's fixed exchange rate between the carbon and climate ledgers, used in every carbon-budget headline: the AR6 1.5 °C budget of 500 Gt C is ~236 ppm of future CO₂ in waiting, and the C4MIP's 19 Gt C/°C is 9 ppm/°C released, of which the airborne fraction decides how many ppm stay — the arithmetic that connects the previous two problems' currencies.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=2 check="60"}
**Permafrost scale.** High-emission ensembles release 30–60 Gt C from near-surface permafrost this century. What is the mean annual release (Gt C/yr) at the upper bound over 80 years?
::: solution :::
60/80 = **0.75 Gt C/yr** — small against the 10 Gt C/yr of fossil emissions, which is exactly the permafrost literature's honest framing: the danger is not the twenty-first-century total but the process's tail (the deeper 1,400 Gt, the methane fraction, abrupt thaw) that the smooth models under-represent, a risk-shaped rather than budget-shaped threat.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="2.4"}
**Compensating errors.** A model's net feedback is λ_net = 1.27 W m⁻² K⁻¹, giving ECS = 3.8/1.27 = 3.0 °C. A cloud-scheme upgrade removes 0.57 W m⁻² K⁻¹ of the stabilising net feedback that had been compensating the cloud amplification. By how many °C does ECS move?
::: solution :::
New λ_net = 1.27 − 0.57 = 0.70; new ECS = 3.8/0.70 = 5.4 °C; the move is 5.4 − 3.0 = **2.4 °C** — half a watt per square metre of cloud change, worth two and a half degrees of sensitivity, the sensitivity of sensitivity to one tuned term: the documented CESM2 episode moved a full degree on a comparable adjustment, and the one-line arithmetic ECS = F₂ₓ/(λ₀ − Σλ_fb) is the hinge on which the whole CMIP6 hot-model debate turned.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="1"}
**Sea-level binary.** ISMIP6-style projections differ on Antarctic contribution by ~0.5 m by 2100 depending on whether cliff-failure physics is included. What fraction of the AR6 high-emission total sea-level range (0.6–1.1 m) does that structural choice span?
::: solution :::
0.5/0.5 = **1** — the cliff-failure switch spans the entire AR6 range's width: one binary parameterisation choice (the 2018 DeConto–Pollard controversy) is worth more than all other structural disagreements combined for this variable and horizon, which is why the ice-sheet community's argument is conducted in the open and why coastal policy reads the low-likelihood tail, not the central estimate.
:::
:::

::: exercise {level=3 check="4.0"}
**Ensemble averaging.** Three ESM generations give ECS 3.0, 3.6, 4.2 °C; a fourth, with a corrected cloud scheme, gives 5.3. What is the ensemble mean, and why does the mean mislead?
::: solution :::
(3.0 + 3.6 + 4.2 + 5.3)/4 = 16.1/4 = **4.0 °C** — and the misdirection is the point: the three older models share a compensating error the fourth removed, so their agreement was a tuning conspiracy, not evidence (the 2019–2021 hot-model episode's statistical moral); ensemble means weight shared assumptions equally with independent ones, and honest sensitivity synthesis (AR6's 2021 triangulation across paleo, process and historical evidence rather than model averaging) exists precisely because model counts are not independent coin flips.
:::
:::

::: warning Four Earth-system-model traps
1. **"The model now includes permafrost, so the permafrost feedback is in the projection."** Inclusion is not representation: the ensembles' permafrost carbon is dominated by the slow, deep, oxic-then-anoxic decomposition chain whose rates are calibrated on laboratory incubations of decades (the 2010s–2020s incubation-to-ecosystem scaling controversy), abrupt thaw is absent or parameterised, and the methane fraction is the least-constrained number in the ledger; the honest reading of an ESM's permafrost release is a lower bound with a stated mechanism list, not a forecast.
2. **"More components, more realism, better projections."** Every added cycle adds tunings against the same observed mean state, and the 2019–2021 literature on tuning-induced agreement showed that component additions can shrink spread while adding bias — the compensating-error economy in which a worse carbon cycle cancels a worse cloud feedback and the historical fit improves for reasons no process data supports; the CMIP6 model index's component flags exist so users can audit what cancels what, and analyses that ignore them are reading the ensemble as if its members were independent, which they are not.
3. **"Paleo-validation certifies the future."** The Last Glacial Maximum and Pliocene are real experiments in CO₂ forcing, but they are far from the anthropogenic problem in boundary conditions (ice sheets, geography, foraminiferal-reconstructed CO₂ versus instrumented trace gases) and the 2020s PMIP4 syntheses' caution is precise: paleo results exclude the hottest and coldest model tails, they do not centre the distribution, and a model that passes the glacial test can still fail the twenty-second-century one for reasons the glacial world never probes — abrupt permafrost, cliff failure, Amazon dieback, all absent from the proxy record's resolution.
4. **"The digital twin will end the component wars."** The 2022–2025 twin programmes (kilometre-scale coupled atmosphere–ocean–ice–biogeochemistry at institutional cost) change resolution and data assimilation, not the closure problem: at 1 km the convection is resolved but the microphysics, the boundary layer, the soils, the chemistry and the ice-sheet calving are not, and the 2024–2025 stratocumulus-breakup scare arrived from the finest simulations ever run, parameterised all the same; the Earth-system era's epistemic status — many tuned, partially-constrained, mutually-auditing components — is a condition of the problem, not a stage of its development.
:::

## Summary

::: summary
Earth system models are GCMs plus the rest of the planet's feedbacks: interactive carbon (ocean and land), chemistry and aerosols, dynamic vegetation with its contested mortality and fire, nutrient constraints on fertilisation, and — the 2020s frontier — dynamic ice sheets, all stitched by conserving couplers, balance-tuned against the observed mean state, and governed by the CMIP6 protocol constitution (DECK, AFMIP, C4MIP, ISMIP6, PMIP4, ScenarioMIP) that turns a zoo of tunned components into a measurement instrument. The instrument's flagship result is the carbon–climate feedback quantified — ~19 Gt C of extra natural carbon per °C (the 2013 C4MIP synthesis), λ_carbon of ~0.08 W m⁻² K⁻¹, an airborne fraction that must rise, and a path-dependent asymmetry that makes drawdown an imperfect undo — and its flagship pathologies are equally instructive: the CESM2 episode (one cloud fix moving sensitivity a degree, compensating errors exposed as structural), the permafrost and Amazon tail risks that smooth tuned models under-represent by construction, and the ice-sheet binary (cliff physics on or off) that spans the entire sea-level range. The epistemology the era requires: ensemble means are not evidence when members share tunings; paleo runs are held-out tests, not certificates; and the reason twenty-first-century temperature projections have barely moved since 2001 is not convergence of understanding but the conservation of a first-order physics answer amid second-order churn — and the second-order (carbon, sea level, aerosol–monsoon coupling, the tails) is exactly where the Earth-system era, for all its fragility, is the only instrument we have.
:::
