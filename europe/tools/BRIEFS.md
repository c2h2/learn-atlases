## sources/naming-europe
Question: What does the name Europe pick out, and whom does a single outline leave out?
Must:
- Europe has been a classical geographical name, a map of Latin Christendom, a balance-of-power idea and, late, a treaty organisation. These are not the same object.
- Byzantium, al-Andalus, the Ottoman empire, Jewish communities and Roma are inside the geography and have often been written as outside the story. Say so with the dates 711, 800, 1453 and 1957 as markers of different maps, not as a single ascent.
- The European Economic Community of 25 March 1957 is a membership list. It is not a definition of the continent.

Must not:
- Do not draw the European Union and walk backwards until the past agrees.
- Do not call Europe a civilisation with one set of values.

## sources/evidence
Question: What can a source support, and what is a silence?
Must:
- Separate a source (a charter, a chronicle, a coin, a parish register, an inquisition file) from the inference a historian draws.
- Use Domesday in 1086 as a survey made for a conqueror, the plague reports of 1347 as witnesses of a disaster rather than a census, and Bloch's own fate as a reminder that the craft has a history. The book is 1949.
- An invented sentence in an example must be labelled invented. Show what it would take to promote it into evidence.
- A silence (no record of a peasant's opinion, no women's votes in a roll) is not evidence that the person had no mind. It is evidence about who was asked.

Must not:
- Do not treat a chronicle's moral as a fact it witnessed.
- Do not invent a quotation from a chronicler.

## sources/time-and-names
Question: Why is a date not a neutral label?
Must:
- There is no year 0. From 1 BCE to 1 CE is one year. The elapsed count from year A BCE to year B CE is A + B minus 1. Work this in an example.
- Gregorian reform: Rome, 4 October 1582 followed by 15 October 1582. Britain, 2 September 1752 followed by 14 September 1752. Russia, 31 January 1918 followed by 14 February 1918.
- The October Revolution is 25 October 1917 Old Style and 7 November 1917 New Style. Both dates name one event.
- The site timeline prints BC. The lesson says BCE for the same numbers.
- A regnal year and a nickname (the Terror, the Middle Ages) are claims. Say who benefits from the name.

Must not:
- Do not call a date before a calendar reform an error.
- Do not insert a year 0 to make the subtraction easier.

## sources/argument
Question: What is the difference between a claim, a cause and a label?
Must:
- A claim says what happened and how we know. A label (Renaissance, absolutism, Westphalia as the birth of sovereignty) bundles a claim and often hides it.
- A cause has to be the sort of thing that could have been otherwise. Use the July crisis only as a forward pointer: a timetable can constrain a government and still leave choices. The full narrative is in [[nations/toward-1914]].
- Counter-evidence is part of the claim, not a later apology. Model this with 476: Odoacer deposes Romulus Augustulus, and the eastern empire continues, and Julius Nepos dies in 480.
- The numbered Claim in this lesson should itself be an example of a checkable sentence.

Must not:
- Do not write a single-cause history of any later event.
- Do not use the word inevitable.

## sources/scale
Question: When does a true story at one scale fail at another?
Must:
- A life (Menocchio is the example in the reading, but do not invent details of his testimony beyond 'an inquisition record of one Friulian miller'), a manor, a kingdom and a trade do not illustrate one another for free.
- Domesday describes a kingdom's survey. It does not describe Europe.
- The Slave Voyages total is a count of embarkations across a trade. It is not a biography, and it is not a world total of all slavery. Point forward to [[states/atlantic]].
- Work an example in which a local fact is true and the national sentence people draw from it is false, using an invented village labelled as invented.

Must not:
- Do not say that microhistory disproves all large-scale history.
- Do not invent the miller's words.

## greece/poleis
Question: What was a polis, if it was not a Greek nation?
Must:
- A polis is a community of citizens plus a territory and, often, a countryside of people who were not citizens. Colonies are new poleis, not counties.
- 776 BCE is the traditional date of the first Olympic games and is a later tradition. 594 BCE is the traditional date of Solon. Use both as traditions.
- There was no Greek state. Shared language, cult and games are not a nation-state.
- Persia is already the great power next door. Do not save it for a contrast in the next chapter only.

Must not:
- Do not call the Greeks a nation.
- Do not narrate a rise from Homer to democracy as a single plot.

## greece/persian-wars
Question: What happened in 490 and 480 BCE, once Persia is treated as an empire?
Must:
- Marathon 490. Thermopylae and Salamis 480, the same campaign season. Plataea 479. The Delian League's usual start is 478 (winter 478/477).
- Darius and Xerxes rule a large empire with its own politics. Greek victory is real and it is not the rescue of Europe.
- The league becomes an Athenian power. That turn prepares the next war. Say so without writing the Peloponnesian War in full.

Must not:
- Do not describe Persians as the East, slaves, or a lesson in freedom.
- Do not give army sizes.

## greece/athenian-democracy
Question: Who was a citizen in democratic Athens, and who was not?
Must:
- Cleisthenes' reforms, often dated 508/507. Use 508.
- Pericles' citizenship law, 451/450. Use 451. After it, citizenship in the form the law states requires two citizen parents. Adult citizen men participate. Women, enslaved people and metics do not.
- The institutions — assembly, council, courts, lot — are real. They are not a prototype of a modern parliament. Do not describe a written constitution.
- Work the exclusions as the centre of the lesson, not as a footnote apologising for a glory.

Must not:
- Do not call Athens the birth of Western freedom.
- Do not give a population figure for Attica.

## greece/peloponnesian-war
Question: What kind of war was the Peloponnesian War?
Must:
- It begins in 431 and Athens surrenders in 404. It is a war among Greek states, with an Athenian empire on one side.
- Socrates is tried and dies in 399. His death is an Athenian event. It is not the meaning of the war and not the Greek view of life.
- Name the Sicilian disaster as part of the war without inventing troop numbers. If you cannot date the expedition from the chronology, describe it without a year. The chronology does not list 415. Do not add 415.
- Defeat does not end the polis or Greek political life.

Must not:
- Do not make the war a tragedy about the excess of democracy unless you state that as Thucydides' argument and not as the fact.
- Do not invent a quotation from Thucydides or from the Apology.

## rome/slavery-and-conquest
Question: How did conquest and slavery belong to one history?
Must:
- First Punic War 264–241. Second 218–201, with Cannae 216 and Zama 202. Third 149–146. Carthage and Corinth destroyed in 146.
- Enslaved people are a structural part of Roman society after these wars, not a private moral failing. Use Bradley's framing: slavery is an institution.
- Provinces are how conquest is governed. A governor is not a modern colonial office, but the extraction is real.
- Tiberius Gracchus in 133 is a crisis inside the city about land and the consequences of empire. Do not make him a liberal reformer.

Must not:
- Do not give a number of people enslaved.
- Do not describe the mechanics of punishment.

## rome/later-empire
Question: Why is 476 a convention?
Must:
- 235 to 284 is the bundle called the third-century crisis. The label is a historian's. Diocletian is 284.
- Milvian Bridge 28 October 312. The agreement of 313, later called the Edict of Milan, is toleration, and the name is later. Nicaea is 325. Constantinople's traditional dedication is 11 May 330.
- The Edict of Thessalonica is 380. Measures against public pagan cult include 391. These belong to Christianisation and are only started here; [[after-rome/christianisation]] continues them.
- Alaric sacks Rome on 24 August 410. Odoacer deposes Romulus Augustulus in 476. Julius Nepos dies in 480. The eastern empire continues. Peter Heather's book is one account, not an oracle.

Must not:
- Do not say Roman civilisation ended in 476.
- Do not give a cause in one sentence (lead, Christianity, or decadence).

## after-rome/christianisation
Question: What changed when Christianity became the official religion, and what did not?
Must:
- 313 is toleration, not establishment. 325 is a council that defines orthodoxy and therefore heresy. 380 is the Edict of Thessalonica. 391 is among the measures against public pagan cult.
- Official religion is a public and legal fact. It does not describe what every inhabitant believed.
- Roman law, landowning and tax do not stop. Christianisation is not the arrival of a European spirit.
- Chalcedon in 451 is a doctrinal split as well as a council. Mention it as a division, and do not narrate every later church.

Must not:
- Do not tell a story of darkness falling on pagan light, or the reverse.
- Do not invent a conversion statistic.

## after-rome/successor-kingdoms
Question: Why is 'barbarian invasions' a bad summary?
Must:
- Goths and Franks build kingdoms in Roman provinces, with Roman land, tax and often Roman administrators. 'Barbarian' is a Roman insult that became a period name.
- The baptism of Clovis is disputed: about 496 and about 508 have both been argued. Teach the dispute. Do not pick silently.
- 476 did not empty the west. These kingdoms are the political history of the next generations.
- The Lombard entry into Italy is usually dated 568. Use it as a later case, not as the same event as the fifth-century settlements.

Must not:
- Do not describe migrations as a racial replacement.
- Do not use the word dark ages as your own claim.

## christendom/popes-and-kings
Question: What were popes and kings fighting over?
Must:

- Magna Carta is June 1215, a peace between a king and his barons, reissued in 1225. It is not a modern constitution. Simon de Montfort's parliament is 1265, in a civil war. Edward I's later-called Model Parliament is 1295.
- Unam sanctam is 1302. The papal residence at Avignon begins in 1309.
- The fight is about office, appointment and jurisdiction.

Must not:
- Do not tell Canossa as the pope's victory over the state.
- Do not call Magna Carta the birth of liberty or of democracy.

## christendom/exclusion
Question: How did Latin Christian law mark people as outside?
Must:
- November 1215: the Fourth Lateran Council. Canon 68 requires distinctive dress for Jews and Muslims. A decree is not a description of every town.
- 1231: Gregory IX and the papal Inquisition. This is a procedure against heresy. It is not the Spanish Inquisition, which is authorised in 1478 and belongs to [[crises/year-1492]].
- Heresy is a category authorities make usable. Moore's argument is that the category is built, not simply found. Present it as an argument.
- Women's legal position varies. Do not summarise 'the medieval woman'. Say that a law about dress and a law about heresy are laws about categories, and that most lives are not those laws.

Must not:
- Do not describe interrogation techniques.
- Do not claim that every Jewish community was marked in practice because a canon said so.

## crises/plague
Question: How should a death toll be stated when the evidence will not support one number?
Must:
- The Great Famine is 1315–1317. Use 1315 as the start.
- Plague is reported at Messina in October 1347, and is in much of western Europe in 1348 and 1349. Further waves follow.
- Do not state a European total. Say that in many studied regions estimates fall between about a third and about a half, and that some places differ.
- Consequences: labour, rents, religious responses. Do not claim the plague caused the Reformation or the Renaissance.
- Work an arithmetic example: if a town of a stated invented size loses a third and then a half of the remainder, the results differ. Label the town invented.

Must not:
- Do not write 'half of Europe died' as a fact.
- Do not describe symptoms in clinical detail.

- February Revolution: 8–16 March 1917 New Style. Abdication of Nicholas II: 15 March 1917 New Style, which is 2 March Old Style.
- October Revolution: 7 November 1917 New Style, 25 October Old Style. The civil calendar changes the next year: 31 January 1918 is followed by 14 February 1918.
- 3 March 1918: Brest-Litovsk.
- 11 November 1918 is the armistice. The German revolution and the January 1919 Spartacist rising can be one paragraph. Do not add years that are not locked beyond 1919 for Versailles, which is the next chapter.
- The new states of the settlement are the next chapter's treaties. Here, the point is the double date and the exit of Russia from the war.

Must not:
- Do not tell October as the destiny of the Russian people.
- Do not invent a slogan.

## wars/second-world-war
Question: How did the European war widen, and when did Germany surrender?
Must:
- 1 September 1939: Poland. 3 September: Britain and France declare war.
- 10 May 1940: the western offensive. 22 June 1940: the French armistice.
- 22 June 1941: the invasion of the Soviet Union. 7 December 1941 Pearl Harbor, then 11 December Germany declares war on the United States.
- 31 January and 2 February 1943: surrender at Stalingrad. 6 June 1944 Normandy. 20 July 1944 the plot fails. 1 August to 2 October 1944 the Warsaw uprising.
- Surrender: Reims 7 May 1945, Karlshorst 8 May 1945, marked in the Soviet Union on 9 May because of the time difference. Teach both 8 May and 9 May.
- Occupation, collaboration and resistance are messy and can coexist in one society. Do not sort a nation into one moral type.
- The genocide is the next chapter. Do not anticipate it with a procedure. You may say that mass murder was part of the war in the east and that the next lesson is where the estimate and the evidence are taught.

Must not:
- Do not describe battles as technique.
- Do not give a casualty total.

## wars/genocide
Question: What is the evidence for the murder of the Jews, and what must a lesson not do with it?
Must:
- About six million Jews were murdered. The figure is the estimate used by historians and by Yad Vashem and the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. It is not a list the perpetrators completed.
- Mass shooting in the occupied Soviet Union begins in the summer of 1941. Chełmno begins in December 1941. Wannsee on 20 January 1942 coordinates a murder already under way. It is not the start.
- Other victims: Roma, disabled people murdered in the so-called euthanasia programme, many non-Jewish Poles, Soviet prisoners of war. Do not invent a second total.
- Lemkin's Axis Rule in Occupied Europe, 1944, uses the word genocide. The UN Convention is adopted on 9 December 1948.
- Evidence is documentary, testimonial and material. Name killing centres as places. Do not describe the method.
- Collaboration and rescue both happened. Neither is the character of a nation.

Must not:
- Do not describe killing procedures, weapons, or how a camp worked.
- Do not invent a quotation from a victim or a perpetrator.
- Do not argue about a present-day state.

## postwar/welfare-and-treaties
Question: Whose welfare, and whose treaty?
Must:
- The Beveridge Report is December 1942, a wartime document that becomes a postwar politics. The NHS begins on 5 July 1948.
- Britain, Sweden, West Germany and France are different settlements. Esping-Andersen's typology is 1990. It is a later classification, not a fact of 1950.
- 18 April 1951: the Treaty of Paris, the ECSC, in force in 1952. 25 March 1957: the Treaties of Rome, EEC and Euratom. Membership is small.
- 5 March 1953: Stalin dies. 17 June 1953: the East German rising. February 1956: the secret speech. October–November 1956: Hungary, and Suez.
- States outside the treaties, including those under the other military system, are still Europe.

- Do not describe one welfare model as Europe's.

## postwar/decolonisation-and-1968
Question: Why do the end of empires and the year 1968 belong in one European course?
Must:
- 14 August 1947 Pakistan. 15 August 1947 India. Partition violence is very large. Do not state a death total.
- 7 May 1954: Dien Bien Phu. Algerian war: 1 November 1954 to the Évian accords of 18 March 1962 and independence on 5 July 1962.
- 1960 is a label, the year of Africa, for many independences.
- 25 April 1974: the Carnation Revolution. 1975: Angola and Mozambique become independent as the Portuguese empire ends.
- May 1968 in Paris. 20–21 August 1968: the invasion ends the Prague Spring. Two 1968s.
- 13 August 1961: the Berlin Wall, as the symbol between the treaties and 1968.
- The empires were European. Their endings are European history and also the history of other places. Say both.

Must not:
- Do not treat decolonisation as a gift.
- Do not give a death total for partition or for Algeria.

## postwar/union
Question: What did the Union become, and what is still not a closed past?
Must:
- Maastricht is signed on 7 February 1992 and enters into force on 1 November 1993.
- 1 January 1995: Austria, Finland and Sweden. 1 January 1999: the euro as book money. 1 January 2002: notes and coins. 24 March to 10 June 1999: the NATO campaign over Kosovo.
- 1 May 2004: the ten states — Cyprus, the Czech Republic, Estonia, Hungary, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, Poland, Slovakia, Slovenia. 2007: Bulgaria and Romania. 1 July 2013: Croatia.
- 15 September 2008: Lehman. Greek loan programmes begin in 2010. The debt crisis is not the same day as the bankruptcy.
- 23 June 2016: the referendum. 31 January 2020: the UK leaves. 31 December 2020: the transition ends.
- 2014: Russia occupies and annexes Crimea. Most states do not recognise it. 24 February 2022: the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. As of 2026 it has not ended. Do not state a casualty total. Do not euphemise the invasion. Do not recommend a policy.
- 1 August 1975 Helsinki and 1979 direct elections and the 1986 Single European Act can be one paragraph of the road to Maastricht if you need them. They are in the chronology.

Must not:
- Do not treat the Union as the happy ending of the atlas.
- Do not urge a side in a live dispute. State the dated facts and the existence of the argument.
