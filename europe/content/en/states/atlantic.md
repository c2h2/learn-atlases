What do we know about the scale of the Atlantic trade, and what must we not invent? The scale this atlas will print is one estimate: on the Slave Voyages embarkation-region table covering 1501–1866, 12,521,354 people were embarked. That figure is not a headcount taken at the time. It is not a count of people who stepped ashore. It is not a share for any one empire. It is not a death total, and it is not a percentage. People died on the voyage, so fewer people disembarked than embarked. The difference will not be given a number. A number that is not locked would only look like knowledge.

The trade these sentences describe is the Atlantic trade in enslaved people. It is not the only slave trade in world history. Other seas, and societies on land, had their own trades and their own enslavements. This lesson will not add them into the total, and it will not invent a second total. Treating the figure as “slavery” in general changes the subject.

What we must not invent matters as much as what we may say. No disembarkation total, no national share, no mortality percentage, and no description of a ship, a hold, or a procedure. The ban is not a claim that the crossing was mild. It is a claim about evidence. [[sources/scale]] warns that a true sentence at one scale becomes false when it is stretched over an ocean without new sources. The sources here are the published estimate, the legal dates, and the revolt those dates do not own.

Abolition will not be told as the moral of a civilisation. Laws changed, and some were reversed. Enslaved people in Saint-Domingue did not wait for a Paris statute. Iberian empires are the political frame, not a prelude that “proper” European history can skip. J. H. Elliott’s comparison of the Spanish and British Atlantic empires, including their labour systems, is the comparison this course uses. It is not a ranking and not a quotation.

## One total, and the sentence it allows

:::definition Embarked estimate {#def-embarked}
The **embarked estimate** is the figure 12,521,354, the sum of people the Slave Voyages embarkation-region table gives as embarked for its coverage of 1501–1866. It is an estimate from that published table, not a contemporary headcount. It is not a count of people who disembarked, not a share belonging to one state, and not a total of every slave trade in world history.
:::

The number may state scale: an estimated 12,521,354 people embarked, on this table, across the coverage. It may state method: a later estimate, not a roll called on a beach. It may not be followed by a death total, and it may not be split into a percentage for Portugal, Spain, England, France or anyone else. Several states carried people. The split is still not ours to invent.

Fewer people disembarked than embarked, because people died. That sentence is complete. It is not the opening of a calculation. A rate, a fraction or a second integer would leave the evidence. The chronology has already closed the teaching question by printing one total and a list of additions to refuse.

1501 and 1866 are the ends of the cited table, not a first ship and a polite last ship, and not a claim that no other slave trade existed outside the span. From 1501 to 1866 the year numerals differ by 365. One integer can summarise scale across that distance. It cannot summarise the path from year to year.

The figure has two points: zero at 1501, and 12,521,354 at 1866, joined by a straight line. The zero is a start for that line, not a census of 1501. Nothing here says the count grew at a constant pace. Peaks, lulls and legal bans are what the line does not know. Sketching them by eye would invent the path the widget withholds. The real trade was not a straight line.

:::theorem The embarked total is not a licence to invent the crossing {#thm-not-invent}
The estimate 12,521,354 authorises a statement of scale for the Slave Voyages embarkation-region table covering 1501–1866. It does not authorise a disembarkation total, a national share, a death total, or a mortality percentage. The claim stops there. It does not say the voyage was survivable in comfort, and it does not say the dead are historically unimportant. It says their number, on this crossing, is not a figure the lesson may print.
:::
:::proof
The chronology prints the embarked total and the ban on a disembarkation total, a national share and a death total, and it says that fewer disembarked than embarked because people died. That is the quantitative evidence. What would count against [[#thm-not-invent]] is a second locked quantity for arrivals, deaths or one empire’s share. There is none. A paragraph about a hold is not that quantity, and the crossing is not taught as a procedure. The widget’s straight line would invent a path in the same way a forged percentage invents a share. The line shows the refusal. It is not a graph of the trade.
:::

:::example The length of the coverage {#ex-coverage}

:::solution
1866 minus 1501 is 365. The span is 365 years. It is the distance between the ends of a published coverage. It is not the length of a voyage, not a count of ships, and not a claim that the trade was the same size in every year of the span. Adding one, to be inclusive, would give 366 and would break the atlas rule.

:::example An invented share {#ex-invented-share}

Cut “a third” and any death total: people died, so fewer disembarked than embarked, and the size of that difference is not a figure we may print. “Half” and “a third” are percentages in disguise. [[#thm-not-invent]] is the ban.

## What the ban is for

The ban on a procedure is a rule about teaching, not a claim of mildness. People were taken, carried and sold, and many died. Naming that is required. Describing a hold or a procedure is not. [[#def-embarked]] already carries the scale. Spectacle would add no locked fact. The subject is who had the power to embark people, what the legal categories were, and what the estimate cannot show.

:::warning Not the moral of a civilisation
A common story uses the embarked estimate as a darkness and abolition as a dawn, in which Europe reveals its character by stopping. The dates fail that story. French abolition on 4 February 1794 is followed by the restoration of colonial slavery in 1802 and by a second abolition only in 1848. The British Act of 1807 strikes at the trade, not at slavery. Coverage runs to 1866, and the widget bends at no statute. Laws are real. A sunrise is an invention.
:::

A second misuse treats 12,521,354 as the enslaved population of the Americas, or of the world, in one year. It is people embarked across a long coverage, not a stock of the living. No population is locked. A third misuse makes this the only slave trade that counts. It was not. Other totals are not in the chronology, and a competition of sufferings would need them. The estimate is not a census of human enslavement. Calling it the whole of the crime turns a limit into a boast.

## Iberian empires, then a French code

The political frame is Iberian. The Spanish monarchy whose titles were divided in the abdications of 1556 ruled American lands in the name of those crowns. [[states/composite-monarchy]] has already refused to treat that labour as a metaphor. This chapter is where the metaphor stops. The labour includes the Atlantic trade and coerced work in the colonies. Elliott’s comparison stops either the Spanish or the British empire from being cast as the modern nation arriving early. Both are imperial. Both organise labour. The comparison is not a score, and 12,521,354 will not be split to invent one. Portugal sits in the same frame. No national share will be given to Portugal, Britain or France. A ranking from another book cannot be used as an explanation here.

In 1685 the Code Noir is issued for French colonial slavery. [[states/fiscal-military]] placed that code in the same year as the revocation of the Edict of Nantes, on 18 October at Fontainebleau, and used the year as capacity: one monarchy can police religion and legislate for colonies. Here the code is the law, as far as we may take it. It regulates slavery in the French colonies. It is not a portrait of a ship, and punishments will not be listed. A code means the trade was not a lawless underside the state failed to notice. The articles themselves are not reproduced. The same year does not make every European state a copy of France, and it does not make the Code Noir the pattern of Iberian labour. The previous lesson’s point still stands: Louis XIV is not the model of all Europe.

:::definition Colonial code {#def-code}
A **colonial code**, as the Code Noir of 1685 is used here, is a body of law by which a European state regulates slavery in its colonies. It is evidence that colonial slavery was a legal status, not an accident outside politics. It is not a description of a voyage, not a census of the people it bound, and not a moral judgement that the code’s authors had stepped outside “Europe”. The metropole and the colony are one legal world in the year the code is issued.
:::

## Statutes are not a sunrise

Later laws that restrict or abolish are instruments, not an arrow of moral progress. Each does a stated thing in a stated jurisdiction. None of them replaces the embarked estimate with innocence.

On 4 February 1794 the French revolutionary regime abolishes slavery in the colonies. The revolt in Saint-Domingue has already begun, in August 1791. The year numerals differ by 3, and August comes before that later February. To tell 1794 as the gift that creates the revolt is to reverse the dates.

In 1802 Napoleon restores colonial slavery. 1802 minus 1794 is 8. A statute can be real and still be undone. In the same year Toussaint Louverture is captured. He dies in 1803. The capture and the restoration share a year and are not one sentence. The expedition will not be narrated, and the manner of his death will not be invented. The chronology gives the capture and the year of death. That is the personal record this lesson will use.

On 1 January 1804 Haiti declares independence. [[revolutions/napoleon]] teaches the declaration again, inside the revolutionary age, not as an appendix to a European moral. It belongs here because the trade and colonial slavery are the world it comes out of. From 1791 to 1804 the year numerals differ by 13. Abolition in 1794 and restoration in 1802 both sit inside that span. A restored colonial slavery is not the same fact as a state that has left the empire.

:::theorem Abolition is not the moral of a civilisation {#thm-not-moral}
The statutes of 4 February 1794, 1802, 1807, 1833 and 1848 change specified laws in specified empires. They do not reveal a European conscience of which the Atlantic trade was the opposite, later corrected. The claim stops there. It does not deny that people argued for abolition, and it does not deny that a statute can free people in law. It denies the sunrise: one character, first guilty, then redeemed, with the embarked estimate as the darkness in between.
:::
:::proof
The revolt begins in August 1791, before French abolition on 4 February 1794, and that abolition is reversed in 1802. Haiti’s independence on 1 January 1804 is not a Paris decree. The British Act of 1807 forbids the trade, not slavery. The 1833 Act abolishes slavery in most of the British empire and leaves apprenticeship until 1838. The second French abolition is 1848. Coverage of the table runs to 1866, and the widget shows no statute as a bend, because the path is not given. What would count against [[#thm-not-moral]] is one statute that ended the trade and slavery in every empire on one day, with the series stopping that day. No such day is locked. A speech about European virtue is not that statute, and no speech will be invented.
:::

In 1807 the British Act strikes at the slave trade, not at slavery itself. People already enslaved are not freed by this statute. Remembering 1807 as “Britain ended slavery” merges it with a later Act, which is the sunrise in miniature.

The 1833 Act abolishes slavery in most of the British empire. “Most” excludes a catalogue of exceptions this lesson will not invent. Apprenticeship continues to 1838. 1838 minus 1833 is 5. A coerced status remains after the Act popularly remembered as the end. Apprenticeship is named as a legal remainder, not described as a procedure of labour.

The second French abolition, in 1848, is a third French fact: abolition, restoration, abolition again. 1848 minus 1802 is 46. That distance is between two legal acts, not a count of people still enslaved. A civilisation walking in one direction does not need the middle term. The middle term is why the story is law and politics.

## Saint-Domingue is not a European gift

The revolt that begins in August 1791 does not fit a history told only from parliaments. Saint-Domingue is a French colony built on slavery, and enslaved people take the initiative. The fighting, the weapons and any casualty list stay out of this lesson. The chronology locks the beginning. That beginning reverses the usual order: the colony rises before Paris abolishes, Paris later tries to restore colonial slavery, and on 1 January 1804 the state that declares independence is Haiti.

Toussaint Louverture is captured in 1802 and dies in 1803. Capture is not a verdict on the revolt, and the death is not the end of the declaration that follows. 1804 minus 1803 is 1. A biography is not a substitute for that date. [[revolutions/napoleon]] teaches the declaration again. It must not turn the Atlantic story into a mere preface. The estimate 12,521,354 is the scale of the trade in which the revolt sits, not a number Haiti was founded to redeem.

The limit faces our sympathy as well as the sunrise. Refusing a European moral does not make the revolt a simple good. There is no constitution of the revolt to paste, and no count of who fought. The order is August 1791, 4 February 1794, 1802, 1803, 1 January 1804. It will not support “Europe gave Haiti freedom”, and it will not excuse the trade because a later state was founded. The dead are acknowledged and not numbered.

:::history
In 1685 the Code Noir is issued for French colonial slavery, the same year Louis XIV revokes the Edict of Nantes. The code is a metropolitan law for colonial slavery, not a description of a ship. French abolition on 4 February 1794, the restoration of colonial slavery in 1802, and the second abolition in 1848 show that the law could be made, unmade and made again. None of those acts is the Slave Voyages total.
:::

## What would count as knowing more

Caution can look like a way of protecting the carriers. It is not. Printing 12,521,354 is not a softening. The refusals stop that largeness being recruited into a death rate, a national league table, or a hold described for effect. [[sources/evidence]] asks what a source can carry. This table can carry a sum of embarkations under a stated coverage. It cannot carry the league table.

What would let the lesson say more is a further locked quantity, with a warning of the same kind. Until then the honest sentence stays the one already given. An estimated 12,521,354 people were embarked. Fewer disembarked, because people died. The frame is Iberian as well as northern. The Code Noir is 1685. The revolt begins in August 1791 and is not a gift of 4 February 1794. The statutes are real, reversible and limited. Anything sharper would be invented.

:::example Restoration is not independence {#ex-1802-1804}
Napoleon restores colonial slavery in 1802. Haiti declares independence on 1 January 1804. How many years lie between those year numerals, and why may you not treat the two facts as one French decision? (Enter the number of years.)
:::solution
1804 minus 1802 is 2. The restoration is a French legal act putting colonial slavery back. The declaration is the independence of Haiti, taught again in [[revolutions/napoleon]]. Two years apart, they are not one decision. Toussaint Louverture is captured in 1802 and dies in 1803, which sits between the restoration and the declaration and cancels neither. The number 2 is only the distance.
:::
:::

## Where this leads

The empires that carried this trade, and the states that partitioned a large European commonwealth while the trade continued, are [[states/east]]. Poland-Lithuania was not a blank, and the Ottoman empire was not outside Europe. Those are the next refusals. The fiscal instruments that paid for wars and companies are [[states/fiscal-military]]. The composite crowns under which American labour began, in this atlas’s dates, are [[states/composite-monarchy]]. Haiti’s declaration on 1 January 1804 returns in [[revolutions/napoleon]]. How far a single number can be asked to stretch is [[sources/scale]].

:::summary
- The Slave Voyages embarkation-region table for 1501–1866 sums to an estimated 12,521,354 people embarked. It is not a contemporary headcount.
- Fewer people disembarked than embarked, because people died. No disembarkation total, national share, death total, or mortality percentage may be invented.
- This is not the only slave trade in world history. The figure is not a world census of enslavement, and it is not a population of the Americas.
- The straight line from 1501 to 1866 is a refusal to invent the path. The trade was not that line. The zero at the start is not a census.
- Iberian empires are the political frame. The Code Noir of 1685 is French colonial law, not a description of a ship, and not the model of every empire.
- French abolition on 4 February 1794 is undone in 1802, when colonial slavery is restored. The second French abolition is 1848. The British Act of 1807 strikes at the trade, not at slavery. The 1833 Act abolishes slavery in most of the British empire, with apprenticeship to 1838.
- The revolt in Saint-Domingue begins in August 1791, before the French abolition. Toussaint Louverture is captured in 1802 and dies in 1803. Haiti’s independence, 1 January 1804, is not a European gift, and it is taught again in [[revolutions/napoleon]].
