What was an Enlightenment public, and who was outside it? The question refuses a mind. Nothing in these dates is a person called the Enlightenment waking up and deciding to become modern. What there is, instead, is print, salons, academies and censors, and a scatter of books with their own years. In 1687 Newton’s Principia is published. In 1751 the first volume of the Encyclopédie appears. In 1762 Rousseau’s Social Contract and Emile appear. In 1764 Beccaria’s On Crimes and Punishments appears. Those are not one programme. A scientific treatise from 1687 is not a salon, and a salon is not an academy that has already let everyone in.

Who is outside is part of the definition. Women hosted and wrote, and were shut out of most academies. Many writers accepted empire. The Atlantic trade is concurrent with the books: the Slave Voyages table covering 1501–1866 estimates 12,521,354 people embarked. This lesson will not invent a path, a disembarkation total, a national share, a death total, or a mortality percentage for that figure. Fewer people disembarked than embarked, because people died. The Seven Years’ War, 1756–1763, sits in the middle of the dates, fought in Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa and India. On 18 October 1685 the Edict of Nantes is revoked at Fontainebleau, and in the same year the Code Noir is issued. The revocation and the code sit on the same line as the books.

None of this causes the French Revolution. The next course opens from a fiscal crisis, in [[revolutions/old-regime]], not from a book. The Estates-General opens on 5 May 1789. That date is the next course’s opening. It is not this chapter’s plot. No deficit will be invented. In 1776 one sentence will do for the Wealth of Nations, and the American Declaration of Independence is context only.

## Publics, not a mind

:::definition Enlightenment public {#def-public}
An **Enlightenment public** is a set of practices by which arguments circulate and are constrained: print, salons, academies and censors. It is not one programme, not one mind, and not a membership that includes everyone. Writers disagree. Many of them accept empire. Women can host and write and still be shut out of most academies. A censor is part of the public, not an interruption of it from outside history.
:::

Take the definition to a date before you trust the capital letter. A book has a year. A salon is speaking in company, often in a house a woman hosts, and this lesson will not invent the conversation. An academy has members, and most academies shut women out. “Most” is not a percentage. A censor permits, delays or stops print, so the public is not a free field. The state that can revoke a religious toleration can also police a book. That capacity was [[states/fiscal-military]]. Here it is the edge of a public. The mind of the age is a personification: several practices given one will, which then causes whatever comes next. [[#def-public]] blocks that will. Practices do not wake up.

:::theorem The Principia and the Encyclopédie are not one public {#thm-two-publics}
Newton’s Principia, published in 1687, belongs to a scientific public earlier than the salons and the Encyclopédie. The first volume of the Encyclopédie, in 1751, belongs to a later public of collective print. They are not one event and not one programme. The claim stops there. It does not exile natural philosophy from the later books, and it does not say that nobody in 1751 had read Newton. It says the dates must not be melted into a single awakening.
:::
:::proof
The years are the evidence. 1751 minus 1687 is 64. A scientific book and the first volume of a collective encyclopaedia, sixty-four years apart, are not one publication. The widget places both on one line and still requires you to refuse the identity. What would count against [[#thm-two-publics]] is a showing that the Principia was produced in the same public, the same year, as that first volume, or that science and the salon are names for one institution with one membership. They are not. A reader in 1751 who knows the Principia shows transmission. Transmission is not identity. Nothing here is a history of physics, and the gap is not a licence to call both books the moment Europe began to think.
:::

On 18 October 1685, at Fontainebleau, Louis XIV revokes the Edict of Nantes, a limited toleration for Huguenots and not equality. The same year the Code Noir is issued for French colonial slavery. [[states/fiscal-military]] used the pair as capacity to fight and to police religion. [[states/atlantic]] used the code as law, not as a ship, a hold, or a procedure. 1687 minus 1685 is 2. A mathematical book and a slave code are neighbours, not the same text, and the later Encyclopédie does not inherit a moral blank from them.

There is no dot for a salon, because a salon is not a year for an invented conversation. The Wealth of Nations and the Estates-General are taught in prose. The Seven Years’ War sits between the Encyclopédie and the Social Contract. A window of six years will not make 1685 and 1764 into one afternoon. The line is a set of dates, not a mood of awakening.

## Books that do not agree

In 1762 two books appear: the Social Contract and Emile. A political treatise and a book about education are not one programme even under one writer’s name. This lesson will not quote them or collapse them into a slogan. From the Encyclopédie’s first volume in 1751, the year numerals differ by 11. Shared readers can still quarrel. Quarrel is what “not one programme” means.

In 1764 Beccaria’s On Crimes and Punishments is a further book, about penal law. 1764 minus 1762 is 2. Close is not identical. A printed argument about crimes is not the policy of every state, and it does not abolish the censor. No statute will be invented to show that a government copied the book. A book shows that an argument was printed. It does not show that the argument governed.

:::definition Not one programme {#def-programme}
**Not one programme** means that the books and institutions gathered under a single later name did not share one set of aims. The Encyclopédie in 1751, the Social Contract and Emile in 1762, and On Crimes and Punishments in 1764 may be read in the same decade and still contradict one another. The phrase does not mean the books were trivial. It means a reader may not pick the sentence she likes, call it the Enlightenment, and treat the other sentences as betrayal of a mind.
:::

A public of print that mattered was a public someone tried to police. [[#def-public]] includes censors because circulation without constraint is a later fantasy. There is no register of banned titles to copy, and none will be written here.

:::example Two titles, one year {#ex-1762}
In 1762 the Social Contract and Emile are both published. Why is it a mistake to treat them as one programme, and what are you forbidden to add in order to make them speak?
:::solution
They are two books in one year: a political treatise and a book about education, under one writer’s name. [[#def-programme]] says shared dates are not shared aims. You are forbidden to invent a quotation, a salon conversation, or a slogan that collapses the titles. The Encyclopédie’s first volume is 1751, eleven years earlier by subtraction, and Beccaria’s book is 1764. Neither is this pair. The year supports publication. It does not support a single will.
:::
:::

## Who is outside, and who is inside on terms

Women hosted salons and women wrote, so they are inside the practices of [[#def-public]]. They were also shut out of most academies. Hosting is not a seat. Both halves are required, and neither is a scene. No conversation will be scripted to prove cleverness or rudeness. “Most academies” is the evidence we have. Particular doors, with particular years, are not locked here.

Many writers accepted empire, so the public cannot be defined as the opposite of empire. The embarked estimate is 12,521,354 people, on the Slave Voyages table for 1501–1866. 1866 minus 1501 is 365, the span of that coverage, not a census of any one year. [[states/atlantic]] forbids a disembarkation total, a national share, a death total and a mortality percentage. This lesson inherits the ban. People were embarked while the Encyclopédie was begun and while Beccaria printed an argument about punishments. Fewer disembarked than embarked, because people died. A writer who accepts empire can still be inside the public. Who is outside includes people bound in the law of the Code Noir, women barred from most academies, and anyone a censor cut off from print. This is not the only slave trade in world history. No other trade’s total will be invented in order to rank sufferings.

The partitions are the same kind of concurrence. [[states/east]] dates the erasure of Poland-Lithuania, a commonwealth with a parliament and not a blank, to 1772, 1793 and 1795. From Beccaria in 1764 to the first partition is 8 years. A book about punishments does not cause that taking, and the parliament is not the cause of it either. Prussia, Austria and Russia remove a state while books about law circulate. Concurrence is the claim. It is not a moral in which the books are a conscience and the partitions are a lesser Europe.

:::warning A mind that awoke
The usual myth gives these practices one face and one morning. The Enlightenment wakes, writes the Encyclopédie, softens punishment, frees thought, and then, being awake, causes the French Revolution. The myth cannot survive the line. The revocation and the Code Noir are on it, in 1685. The Principia, in 1687, is not the Encyclopédie, in 1751. The Seven Years’ War is in the middle. Women host and are shut out. The trade’s estimate runs through the years. A mind does not fit. If you need the myth, you will have to delete the dots that embarrass it. The dots are the lesson.
:::

:::example An invented awakening {#ex-awoke}

:::solution
Keep 1751 as the year of the first volume of the Encyclopédie. Cut “the Enlightenment awoke”: that is a personification, not a date. Cut “rejected empire”: many writers accepted empire, the Code Noir is already 1685, and the embarked estimate for 1501–1866 is 12,521,354, concurrent with the book. Cut “set the French Revolution in motion”. The Estates-General opens on 5 May 1789, and [[revolutions/old-regime]] begins from a fiscal crisis, not from this volume. [[#thm-two-publics]] also keeps 1751 distinct from the Principia of 1687.
:::

## A war in the middle

The Seven Years’ War, 1756–1763, is the global war in the middle of these books, fought in Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa and India. This chapter does not retell the battles and does not add a casualty total or an army size. 1756 minus 1751 is 5, and 1763 minus 1762 is 1, so the Social Contract and Emile sit at the end of the fighting. A public of print is not a public at peace. The war is how “accepted empire” looks when it is dated.

Frederick II invaded Silesia in 1740. That invasion is taught in [[states/east]] and is not refought here. It only marks that the war’s European side includes the kingdom which later shares in the partitions. William Pitt the Elder is the British minister the course’s historical note attaches to 1756. Neither man is a salon, and neither is a campaign to be narrated twice.

## Not the cause of a revolution

In 1776 Adam Smith’s Wealth of Nations is published, a book about wealth and the power of states in a world of empire, not a salon programme and not a cause of revolution. That one sentence is the whole of the book here. The American Declaration of Independence is the same year, and it is context only: a political break in the Atlantic world, not this plot and not the French crisis. No campaign will be narrated.

A single cause would need a path from one text to a crisis of a state, with the other causes shown to be unnecessary. We do not have that path. [[revolutions/old-regime]] begins from a fiscal crisis and asks why a crisis is not yet a revolution. No deficit will be invented.

:::theorem These publics do not cause the French Revolution {#thm-not-cause}
The Encyclopédie, the books of 1762 and 1764, and the Wealth of Nations in 1776 do not cause the French Revolution as a single cause. The claim stops there. It does not say books are politically idle, and it does not say nobody in 1789 had read them. It says a public of print is not a sufficient cause, and this chapter will not tell the Revolution’s plot.
:::
:::proof
The books have their years. The Estates-General opens on 5 May 1789, named here as the next course’s opening. Between 1776 and 1789 the year numerals differ by 13, and between 1751 and 1789 by 38. The gap is not filled by “the books produced the meeting”. What would count against [[#thm-not-cause]] is a locked causal chain from one of these titles to that opening, with the fiscal crisis shown to be unnecessary. There is no such chain. The Code Noir of 1685 and the embarked estimate are further reasons not to tell the books as a mind leaving violence behind. Readers may have opposed the trade. Opposition is not a single will, and many writers accepted empire.
:::

On 5 May 1789 the Estates-General opens. Remember the day, and then stop. The National Assembly, the oath, the Bastille and the August decrees are not borrowed to give this lesson a climax. [[#def-public]] has practices and neighbours: a war, a code, a trade, a partition, a censor. The door to the Revolution is [[revolutions/old-regime]]. Using it does not make the books idle. It refuses to let them make every other fact decorative.

## What a counter-argument would need

A serious counter-argument says that refusing causation makes ideas decorative. Books can matter without being the single cause of a revolution. They can form readers and quarrel with censors, and still leave a fiscal crisis as the door [[revolutions/old-regime]] opens. What would enlarge their role is evidence, locked as these years are locked, that a stated book produced a stated political act. We do not have it. [[sources/argument]] separates a claim from a label. The claim has to say which practice, which boundary, and which year.

:::example A scripted salon {#ex-salon}
Why must you refuse an invented dialogue, set in 1762, in which a hostess and a censor discuss the Social Contract, even if every sentence sounds plausible?
:::solution
The dialogue is an invented conversation, and this lesson does not invent salon talk. Plausibility is not a source. 1762 supports the publication of the Social Contract and of Emile, not a scene. A censor belongs in [[#def-public]] as an institution. A hostess belongs as the fact that women hosted and wrote and were shut out of most academies. The script would also risk turning two books into one programme, which [[#def-programme]] forbids. Keep the year and the boundaries.
:::
:::
