What kind of polity was removed by the partitions? A map that is mostly blank between the Baltic and the Black Sea has already answered, and it has answered wrongly. Poland-Lithuania was a large European commonwealth. It had a king and a parliament. It was not a gap between real states, waiting to be coloured in by neighbours who knew how to govern. In 1772, in 1793 and in 1795 those neighbours took it apart. By the third partition the commonwealth is erased as a state. The kind of polity removed is the question. The answer is not “a mistake”.

The mistake in the usual story is the parliament. A commonwealth with an assembly is treated as a constitutional failure beside monarchies that look, from later maps, more solid. The failure is then asked to explain the erasure, as if the neighbours had been correcting an error. This lesson refuses that moral. Prussia, Austria and Russia take territory. A parliament is not a cause that can replace those takers. Nor will a population be supplied to make the loss feel measured. No population of the commonwealth is locked. “Large” is the word the chronology allows. A forged census would only pretend that size was a number we had earned.

Two other refusals keep the map honest. The Ottoman empire is a European power in the Balkans and on the Black Sea, not an outside. The relief of Vienna on 12 September 1683 is a different frontier, not the same event as a partition. Russia is a European empire by these partitions. It does not have to be saved for a later revolution before it counts as European history. The Prussian crown of 1701 and Frederick II’s invasion of Silesia in 1740 are the rising power on the other side of the same story. The Seven Years’ War, from 1756 to 1763, gets one paragraph, because [[states/enlightenment]] carries it. One paragraph is a discipline, not a slight.

## A commonwealth, not a blank

:::definition Commonwealth {#def-commonwealth}
**Poland-Lithuania**, as this lesson uses the name, was a large European commonwealth: a polity with a parliament and a king, not a blank on the map between states that counted. “Commonwealth” names that political fact. It does not mean the parliament was wise, equal or effective. It does not mean the state was a nation-state in the later sense of [[states/composite-monarchy]]. It means there was a state to remove.
:::

The definition is a test you can take to a map. If the only Poland you can see is the one that appears after the partitions, as a memory or a grievance, you have started from the erasure and called it geography. The commonwealth was there before 1772. Its parliament is part of what was there. So is the crown. Stanisław August Poniatowski is king at the time of the first partition. Naming him is not a biography and not a quotation. It stops the picture of a headless confusion that proper kingdoms had to tidy. A king and a parliament together are an ordinary early-modern arrangement. The Dutch Republic had assemblies and no king. Britain had both a crown and a parliament. Neither fact made those states unreal. It does not make this one unreal either.

“Not a nation-state” is not “not a state”. [[states/composite-monarchy]] has already refused the stronger claim. The partitions remove a state. They do not fill a blank, and they do not need a population to be believed. None is locked.

:::theorem A parliament is not the cause the erasure requires {#thm-not-parliament}
Poland-Lithuania had a parliament. That fact does not make the partitions the commonwealth’s own fault, and it does not explain the erasure as the correction of a constitutional error. The claim stops there. It does not praise the assembly, and it does not deny that historians argue about how it worked. It denies a moral in which neighbours with armies are the remedy for an assembly.
:::
:::proof
In 1772 Prussia, Austria and Russia take territory. Further partitions in 1793 and 1795 erase the commonwealth. Those are the acts. What would count against [[#thm-not-parliament]] is the parliament dissolving the state with no external takers, or a pattern in which every parliamentary state was partitioned and every state without a parliament was spared. Britain’s parliament is not partitioned. A harsh judgement on procedure would not replace the neighbours as the agents, and this lesson will not invent the procedure in order to blame it. The parliament was real. The blame is the invention.
:::

:::example An invented blank {#ex-invented-blank}
The following sentence is invented for the exercise. It is not a source. “In 1772 Poland, a blank made weak by its parliament, was at last filled in by real states.” Which words can stand, and which must be cut?
:::solution
The year 1772 can stand, as the first partition, in which Prussia, Austria and Russia take territory. Cut “a blank”: [[#def-commonwealth]] is the opposite. Cut “made weak by its parliament” and “at last”: both turn the takers into a remedy and the assembly into a fault, which [[#thm-not-parliament]] forbids. Cut “filled in” and “real states”, which assume the commonwealth was not already a state. The supported sentence is only that territory was taken in 1772. It is not a judgement on a constitution, and it is not the end of the story. Further partitions follow.
:::
:::

## Three dates, one erasure

:::definition Partition {#def-partition}
A **partition**, in this chapter, is an act by which neighbouring powers take territory from Poland-Lithuania. The three dates are 1772, 1793 and 1795. Together they erase the commonwealth as a state. A partition is not a verdict on the victim’s parliament, not a census, and not the same event as the relief of Vienna in 1683.
:::

Read the three years as a sequence of acts, not as a single sigh. In 1772 the taking begins: Prussia, Austria and Russia. The state continues, diminished. In 1793 a second partition follows. In 1795 a third, and the commonwealth is gone as a state. From 1772 to 1795 the year numerals differ by 23. Twenty-three years is long enough to forbid the sentence “it vanished one afternoon”, and short enough to forbid the sentence “it faded for centuries until the map noticed”. The fading is a story told by the blank. The dates are a story told by powers that could take land and hold it. We will not give the area taken in any of the three, and we will not give the number of people who changed rulers. Both would be quantities. Neither is locked.

For 1772 the takers are named: Prussia, Austria and Russia. For 1793 and 1795 the record is further partitions and, at the end, the erasure. A separate roster of who signed each later act is not printed here, and inventing one would be like inventing a population. After 1795 there is no commonwealth to put on the map as a state. The polity removed is [[#def-commonwealth]]: a king, a parliament, a large state. Not a blank.

:::example From the first partition to the erasure {#ex-span-partitions}
The first partition is 1772. The third is 1795, and with it the commonwealth is erased as a state. How many years lie between those year numerals? What does the distance show, and what must it not be used as? (Enter the number of years.)
:::solution
1795 minus 1772 is 23. The span is 23 years, by the atlas rule, not an inclusive count of 24. It shows that the erasure is a sequence, not one afternoon and not a slow fade without agents. It must not be used as a population, an area, or a proof that the parliament caused the loss. [[#def-partition]] is a taking by neighbours. The number 23 is only the distance between the first taking and the erasure.
:::
:::

The last three dots are the erasure. They are not Vienna under another name, and they are not Silesia simply repeated. A window of five years will not make 1683 and 1795 neighbours. If the figure seems to, the window is too narrow and the story you are telling is too smooth.

## A different frontier

On 12 September 1683 the siege of Vienna is lifted at Kahlenberg. [[states/fiscal-military]] has already refused to call that relief “Europe versus Asia”. The refusal stands. This lesson adds a different use of the same date. The Ottoman empire does not leave European politics because a siege fails. It remains a power in the Balkans and on the Black Sea. Those are European places. A power that makes war and holds territory there is a European power. The phrase is not a compliment and not a certificate of shared religion. It is a political description. Treating the empire as an outside is how a map pushes the Balkans off the edge of the course and then acts surprised that they return.

:::definition European power {#def-european}
A **European power**, here, is a state that makes war and territory inside Europe, including the Balkans and the Black Sea shore, or that joins other such states in disposing of a European commonwealth. The Ottoman empire meets the first test. Russia, with Prussia and Austria in the partitions, meets the second. The phrase does not mean “civilised”, and it does not mean “the opposite of Asia”.
:::

Vienna is the marker already taught, and it is the wrong marker if it is asked to stand for every later relationship with the Ottoman empire. A siege lifted in 1683 is not a partition in 1772. The powers at Kahlenberg are not the powers erasing Poland-Lithuania, even where a dynasty overlaps. The Habsburg monarchy can be at Vienna and also, later, among the partitioning courts. Overlap of a name is not identity of an event. [[#def-partition]] keeps the dates apart. So does the figure, if you let the window show the gap instead of hiding it.

The limit is the same one that kept the fiscal lesson from inventing casualties. The Ottoman empire’s European standing is not proved by a body count at Vienna, and it is not disproved by the relief. No army size is locked for the siege or for any later Ottoman war this chapter will not narrate. We will not add treaty years that the chronology, for this lesson, does not hand us. The Balkans and the Black Sea are the claim. They do not require a second battle to be true, and a second battle with an invented date would make them look weaker, not stronger.

## Prussia, Silesia, and one global war

In 1701 the elector of Brandenburg is crowned king in Prussia. [[states/fiscal-military]] has already separated that coronation from the English Act of Settlement in the same year. The title is a new legal sentence for a rising power. It is not yet an invasion, and it is not yet a partition. From 1701 to 1740 the year numerals differ by 39. In 1740 Frederick II invades Silesia. A province is taken by armed force. No army size will be given, and no revenue of the province. The fiscal lesson stopped at that invasion so that 1701 would not be remembered only as an English statute. This lesson picks the thread up because the same kingdom is later among the powers that partition Poland-Lithuania. A rising power and a partitioning power can be the same dynasty without the partition being the “point” of Silesia. Silesia is one taking. The commonwealth is another state, removed later, by more than one taker.

The temptation is to make Prussia the adult state and the commonwealth the child. That is the parliament-blame in a Prussian coat. [[#thm-not-parliament]] does not allow it. An invasion shows that a new kingdom uses war. It does not show that the loser deserved it, or that a parliament is a defect beside a successful taking.

The Seven Years’ War is dated 1756–1763. It is fought in Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa and India. It is not only a cabinet war. 1756 minus 1740 is 16, so the war is not the invasion of Silesia, though Frederick II is in both. This paragraph is the whole narrative. No battle, no fleet, no casualty total. [[states/enlightenment]] carries the war. Here it is a dot between Silesia and the first partition. 1772 minus 1756 is 16. The partition is not an episode of the war that ends in 1763.

:::example Silesia is not the world war {#ex-silesia-seven}
Frederick II invades Silesia in 1740. The Seven Years’ War begins in 1756 and runs to 1763. How many years lie between the invasion and the outbreak? What must you still not collapse? (Enter the number of years.)
:::solution
1756 minus 1740 is 16. The invasion and the outbreak are 16 years apart. You must not collapse them into one war, and you must not collapse the Seven Years’ War into a cabinet quarrel: it is fought in Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa and India. You must not give an army a size. The first partition, in 1772, is later still, and it is not an episode of the war that ends in 1763.
:::
:::

:::warning The blank, and the useful parliament
The usual myth has two strokes. First it leaves Poland-Lithuania blank, so that the partitions look like the arrival of political order. Then it discovers the parliament and blames it, so that the blank was deserved. Both strokes protect the takers from being the subject of the sentence. A large commonwealth with a parliament was removed by Prussia, Austria and Russia across 1772, 1793 and 1795. The parliament is not the eraser. The blank is not what was there before the eraser arrived. Do not repair the myth with a population. None is locked.
:::

## Russia is already in the story

Russia enters this atlas as a partitioning power, not as a distant prologue to a later revolution. In 1772 it takes territory with Prussia and Austria. It is still in the story in 1793 and in 1795, when the commonwealth is erased. A state that can do that, in company with two other European monarchies, is a European empire. [[#def-european]] was written to include that test. Saving Russia for a revolution this course has not reached would mean teaching the partitions as a Prussian and Austrian act with a blank on the eastern side. The blank would be false. The eastern taker is part of the erasure.

:::theorem Russia is a European empire by the partitions {#thm-russia}
In the partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795 Russia acts with other European powers to take, and finally to erase, a European commonwealth. Russia is therefore already a European empire in this period. The claim stops there. It does not describe Russian society, serfdom or the church, and it does not require the reader to admire the taking. It only refuses to postpone Russia until some later crisis.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is participation. The first partition names Russia with Prussia and Austria. The later partitions complete an erasure Russia helped begin. A European empire, on [[#def-european]], includes a state that joins in disposing of a European commonwealth. What would count against [[#thm-russia]] is a showing that Russia was absent from the partitions, or that the commonwealth was not a European state, so that taking it was an act outside Europe. [[#def-commonwealth]] blocks the second escape. The first is false on the chronology. A preference for telling Russian history only from a revolution would not be a showing of absence. It would be a choice to leave a participating empire out of the sentence. The limit: nothing here is a history of Russia’s internal government. The limit is not a reason to delete the empire from 1772.
:::

One outsider-story pushes the Ottomans off the map after Vienna. Another pushes Russia off the map until a later revolution. Both keep “Europe” as a few western courts. The partitions and the Black Sea do not fit. [[states/atlantic]] refused a European moral for the trade. This lesson refuses one for which empires were really there.

## What the counter-argument needs

A parliament that could not defend a state may belong in a fuller explanation. Refusing to say so here is not politeness. It is the difference between a description and a blame that replaces the agents. Procedures of the assembly are not locked, so no mechanism will be invented and then called the cause. What is locked is the taking. A cause that deletes Prussia, Austria and Russia has failed even if the parliament was a poor instrument. What would count against [[#thm-not-parliament]] is the assembly voting the state out of existence with the neighbours absent. That record is not in front of us, and neither is a population. [[sources/argument]] separates a label from a claim. “Blank” is a label. The claim we can check is the erasure of a commonwealth, by neighbours, on three dates.

:::example Distance from Vienna to the first partition {#ex-vienna-gap}
The relief of Vienna is 1683. The first partition is 1772. Compute the years between those year numerals, and say why the figure’s window must not be used to hide the gap. (Enter the number of years.)
:::solution
1772 minus 1683 is 89. The gap is 89 years. A window of five years can make distant dots look related if you forget to ask what sits between them: the Prussian crown in 1701, Silesia in 1740, the Seven Years’ War from 1756 to 1763. The gap is why Vienna is a different frontier, not a partition. The number is not a body count from Kahlenberg.
:::

## Where this leads

The publics of print, salons and censors, and the global war that sits in the middle of them, are [[states/enlightenment]]. That chapter inherits the Seven Years’ War, 1756–1763, and must not be told as a mind waking up outside the empires this lesson has just refused to push off the map. The trade those empires also organised is [[states/atlantic]]. The fiscal instruments of the Prussian crown and of the Bank of England are [[states/fiscal-military]]. How a label such as “blank” differs from a claim is [[sources/argument]].

:::summary
- Poland-Lithuania was a large commonwealth with a parliament and a king, not a blank on the map and not a nation-state merely by having a name.
- The partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795 erase it as a state. In 1772 the takers are Prussia, Austria and Russia. The parliament is not the cause that replaces those takers, and no population is stated.
- The Ottoman empire is a European power in the Balkans and on the Black Sea. The relief of Vienna on 12 September 1683 is a different frontier, not a partition and not “Europe versus Asia”.
- Russia is a European empire by the partitions. It is not postponed to a later revolution.
- The elector of Brandenburg is crowned king in Prussia in 1701. Frederick II invades Silesia in 1740. No army size is given.
- The Seven Years’ War, 1756–1763, is fought in Europe, North America, the Caribbean, West Africa and India. It is one paragraph here. [[states/enlightenment]] carries it.
- From 1683 to 1772 is 89 years, and from 1772 to 1795 is 23. The distances keep a siege, an invasion, a global war and an erasure from being one event.
:::
