How did war, tax and debt make a state? A coloured map answers too quickly. It wants one fill for France, one for England and one for a republic, then a caption in which a strong king is the adult form of all three. The dates below are not steps on that ladder. They are instruments: a charter, a truce, an execution, a navigation statute, a restoration, a landing, a bill, a bank, a succession act, a royal title, a union, a peace, an invasion. War is what they serve. Tax is how subjects are made to pay. Debt is how a state spends revenue it does not yet have. None of the three is a moral mood, and none of them forces every European polity into one constitution.

“Make a state”, here, does not mean found a nation. [[states/composite-monarchy]] has already refused that reading of 1556, of 1579, of 1581 and of 1 May 1707. Here a state is a polity that can fight again because it can tax and borrow. The capacity can sit in a republic, in a monarchy that advertises itself, or in a kingdom that kills a king, restores one and later founds a bank. This atlas will not print an army size, an interest rate, a subscription, a share of trade or a total of refugees. “Many” is allowed. A forged figure is not.

The Dutch Republic stops the ladder at the first step. It is rich, it is armed, and it is not a monarchy. That sentence is the objection to calling the period absolutism, and the objection to treating Louis XIV as the model of all Europe. He is one solution, loudly advertised, not the pattern for the republic, the English sequence and the Prussian title. The relief of Vienna in 1683 is not that pattern either. A siege lifted above a city is a military date, not a permanent fact called Europe versus Asia.

## A republic that taxes and fights

The republic is already a state before these fiscal dates. The Union of Utrecht in 1579 allies provinces fighting their king. The Act of Abjuration in 1581 removes him and installs no lasting replacement. Spain recognises the republic at Münster in January 1648. Westphalia, Münster and Osnabrück together, is 24 October 1648. [[reformations/religious-war]] teaches that peace, and the warning that a finished theory of sovereignty is a later reading. Recognition did not create the capacity to tax and to fight.

The charter of the Dutch East India Company, the VOC, is 1602. The republic authorises a company. The company is not the republic, and the republic is not a king in disguise. No capital, no fleet and no profit is locked, so none will be invented. What the year supports is enough: a non-monarchy can delegate armed trade while the war with Spain is unfinished. From 1602 to 1648 the year numerals differ by 46. The charter is older than the recognition. A story in which the republic becomes real only when Spain signs has to explain that gap.

:::definition Fiscal-military state {#def-fiscal}
A **fiscal-military state** is a polity that keeps the capacity to fight by taking tax and by organising debt, so that armed force is not limited to the coin already in a chest or to a single campaign. The phrase names a pressure and a set of instruments. It does not name one constitution. It does not require a monarch. It does not mean that the Dutch Republic, France and England had the same offices under different flags.
:::

Use the definition as a test. An act belongs here if it pays for armed force, decides who may trade, or stops a war, whether or not there is a king. The invasion of Silesia in 1740 belongs as a grab for a taxable province, not as a story about cleverness. The test is the kind of act, not a headcount.

The Twelve Years’ Truce begins in 1609 and runs to 1621. From year A to year B the span is B minus A. Adding one would break that rule. 1621 minus 1609 is 12. A truce is a pause, not a retirement, and not proof that the VOC had carried the republic out of war. In 1621 it ends. No campaign and no fleet will be supplied.

:::theorem A fiscal-military state need not be a monarchy {#thm-not-monarchy}
The Dutch Republic, chartering the VOC in 1602 and entering the Twelve Years’ Truce in 1609, is a rich armed state and not a monarchy. Fiscal-military pressure therefore does not select a single constitutional type. The claim stops there. It does not say the republic was democratic, mild, or free of oligarchy, and it does not say a charter is the same instrument as a tax voted in a provincial assembly.
:::
:::proof
After 1581 the provinces do not settle under a lasting king, and they still fight. They charter a company in 1602 and make a truce in 1609 that lasts until 1621. Spain recognises that republic in January 1648. What would count against [[#thm-not-monarchy]] is a showing that the charter and the truce were a king’s acts, or that the republic could not tax and could not fight. A taste for kings is not that showing, and neither is the label absolutism. A rich armed republic is part of the period, not an exception filed to save the label. The limit: nothing here makes the republic democratic or mild.
:::

:::solution

## One court, loudly advertised

Louis XIV answers the same pressure. He is not the mould of every other answer. One reign is still asked to stand for the continent because the court advertised itself so well. These dates show a monarchy that can police religion and, in the same year, legislate for colonial slavery. They do not make the Dutch Republic, the Bank of England or the siege of Vienna into copies of that court.

The Edict of Nantes had been a limited toleration for Huguenots, not equality. On 18 October 1685, at Fontainebleau, Louis XIV revokes it. The act is not a tax, and it still belongs here. The crown can decide who may worship and can punish the decision. Uniformity is a use of state power. People leave. No number of refugees is locked, and a number of people will not be invented.

In the same year the Code Noir is issued for French colonial slavery. [[states/atlantic]] takes up the code, the trade, and the refusal to invent a death total. Here the point is capacity, not the hold of a ship. One monarchy in 1685 can revoke a limited toleration and issue a colonial slave code. The two acts are not one subject, and they are not two centuries. Filing religion under Europe and the code under “empire, later” breaks a year the chronology keeps whole.

:::theorem One advertised solution is not the model of the period {#thm-not-model}
Louis XIV’s monarchy, revoking the Edict of Nantes on 18 October 1685 and issuing the Code Noir in the same year, is one way of joining royal power to religious policing and to colonial law. It is not the model of all Europe. The claim stops there. It does not deny the capacity, and it does not rank the revocation against another state’s instruments on a scale we have not got.
:::
:::proof
The Dutch Republic is rich and armed without being this monarchy. The English acts below do not copy Fontainebleau. The Prussian crown of 1701 is a new title, not a French constitution sent north. The relief of Vienna in 1683 is an Ottoman war, not a court ceremony. What would count against [[#thm-not-model]] is a showing that those polities shared Louis’s offices, his religious settlement and his colonial code. A shared wish to win wars is not that showing. Calling the period absolutism hides the difference, which [[states/composite-monarchy]] has already refused as a period-name.
:::

:::warning Absolutism, and the model of one king
The myth calls the age absolutism, as if every serious state were a king without brakes, and then offers Louis XIV as the finished example. A republic, a parliament and an Ottoman empire become deviations. The myth cannot survive the VOC charter of 1602, or a revocation and a slave code kept in one year. Capacity is real. A single model is not. Do not repair it with an army size. None is locked.
:::

## A siege is not a civilisation

On 12 September 1683 the siege of Vienna is lifted at Kahlenberg. An Ottoman army is before the city. A relieving force breaks the siege. That is states at war: a Habsburg city, the Ottoman empire, and the princes on the hill. It is not two continents in person. “Europe versus Asia” turns one relief into an essence and then swallows every other quarrel. The chronology forbids that.

We do not have the bill, and casualties will not be invented. Both sides could put an army there. The Ottoman empire is a European power in the Balkans, not weather from outside history. [[states/east]] returns to that, with the Black Sea and the partitions. From 1683 to 1685 the year numerals differ by 2. The revocation is a neighbour, not the same act, and not one crisis of Christendom.

:::quiz
What is the relief of Vienna on 12 September 1683 evidence of?
- [ ] A permanent conflict of Europe against Asia, after which the Ottoman empire stands outside European politics.
- [ ] The moment Louis XIV becomes the model of every European state.
- [x] A siege lifted at Kahlenberg: states at war, not a civilisational essence.
- [ ] The founding of the Bank of England, which paid for the relief.
:::solution
The relief is 12 September 1683, and it is not “Europe versus Asia” as a permanent fact. The Bank of England is 1694, eleven years later by subtraction, and it did not pay for Kahlenberg. Fontainebleau is 18 October 1685. [[#thm-not-model]] keeps the siege from being filed as Louis’s court.
:::

## Instruments, not a moral progress

The English dates are a sequence, not an arrow. An execution does not cause a bank, a bank does not redeem an execution, and a restoration does not cancel a statute. A story of liberty growing up is not in the dates.

On 30 January 1649 Charles I is executed. The state continues. Regicide is not a tax and not a democratic revolution. The war stays untold, and no army is given a size. [[states/composite-monarchy]] has already refused to make that break, or the restoration, into a nation-state. An execution settles who may rule. It does not found public credit.

In 1651 the Navigation Act attaches shipping and colonial trade to English power. No share is locked, so no percentage will be invented. 1651 minus 1649 is 2. Carriage and the killing of a king are concurrent instruments, not a moral sequence. [[states/atlantic]] will not turn the ocean into a metaphor.

In 1660 the Restoration returns a king. From 1649 to 1660 the year numerals differ by 11. The return breaks any arrow from regicide toward a future without kings. Eleven years without an English monarchy embarrass a period-name that can imagine only kings. They are not a democracy, and they are not the Dutch Republic.

:::definition Partisan name {#def-partisan}
A **partisan name** is a later label that praises one side of a settlement and smuggles in a result the settlement did not have. “Glorious Revolution” is a partisan name for the landing of William of Orange in 1688 and for the Bill of Rights in 1689, which restates parliament’s place. The label says glory. The dates say that a Catholic king is replaced, and that parliament’s place is restated. They do not say that a democracy has begun.
:::

In 1688 William of Orange lands. In 1689 the Bill of Rights restates parliament’s place. A Catholic king is replaced, and the crown continues with parliament no longer a guest. Supply that must pass an assembly is a different instrument from a king who advertises the right to take. It is not a democracy: no electorate will be invented, and the Bill will not be quoted. The nickname in [[#def-partisan]] adds glory and the hint of a new age. 1689 minus 1688 is 1. The bank is still ahead, and [[revolutions/old-regime]] is not this landing told later.

:::definition Public credit {#def-credit}
**Public credit** is the capacity of a state to borrow against future revenue through an institution lenders can recognise, rather than only against treasure already collected. The Bank of England in 1694 is the dated English case. The definition does not fix an interest rate, a sum, or a judgement that debt is wise. Debt can sustain a war. Debt can also ruin a government. Which it does is not settled by the founding of one bank.
:::

In 1694 the bank makes debt an office. Lenders face an institution, not only a prince’s promise, and the state can borrow for another war. No sum is locked. 1694 minus 1689 is 5. The bank did not write the Bill. Do not invent a French deficit from the founding: none is locked, and [[revolutions/old-regime]] is where a fiscal crisis is separated from a revolution. Set 1694 beside the charter of 1602 and the revocation of 1685. Three instruments, three polities. That contrast is [[#thm-not-model]].

In 1701 the Act of Settlement fixes a Protestant succession for the English crown, in the Hanoverian line, and attaches conditions to it. The same year the elector of Brandenburg is crowned king in Prussia. One year, two institutions. [[states/east]] follows the Prussian title into Silesia and the partitions. A new crown is not the bank, and 1701 is not a private English anniversary.

On 1 May 1707 the Acts of Union join England and Scotland under Queen Anne. The previous lesson has the constitutional point: a statute, not a natural merging of peoples, Ireland outside. Here, trade and the succession are joined, and no sum in the bargain is locked. The union is a tool on the figure, not one people ripening since 1649.

In 1713 the Treaty of Utrecht ends a war. 1713 minus 1694 is 19, so the bank is already old. The peace does not repeal the Navigation Act and does not make 1688 a democratic revolution. Articles absent from the chronology stay unwritten. Utrecht is the peace among these instruments.

The figure hides the Restoration of 1660, which breaks the arrow, and the charter of 1602, which falls before the line opens. Vienna is there so that a war which is not English stays visible. The bank sits between the Bill and the union. Utrecht closes the line, and the invasion of Silesia falls outside it. Absence from a figure is not absence from the argument.

In 1740 Frederick II invades Silesia. The title of 1701 has a military sequel: 1740 minus 1701 is 39. A coronation is not an invasion. What joins them is a new kingdom using armed force to take a province that can be taxed. No army size, no provincial revenue, and no battle narrative of the War of the Austrian Succession will be added. [[states/east]] carries the thread. The two acts show one monarchy’s capacity to police religion and to legislate for colonies. They do not make that monarchy the model of the Dutch Republic, of England’s bank, or of the relief of Vienna.

## What the counter-argument needs

A serious counter-argument does not repeat that wars are expensive. It says the instruments are costumes on one modern state arriving everywhere at once. The pressure is similar: states that fight look for money. Similarity of pressure is not identity of office. [[#thm-not-monarchy]] and [[#thm-not-model]] are the two failures of that identity. A non-monarchy is in the set, and the loudest monarchy is not the pattern of the others.

[[sources/argument]] separates a claim from a label. Absolutism, “Europe versus Asia” and “Glorious Revolution” are labels. They would be rescued only by a real match of offices: the VOC as a royal company, the Bill as a democratic constitution, Utrecht as a copy of Fontainebleau, Vienna as a war of continents. Praise shows an advertisement. [[#def-fiscal]] is itself only safe if each act earns the name, by tax, debt, armed force or a decision to stop a war, and if the missing number stays missing.

## Where this leads

The Code Noir, same year as the revocation, opens [[states/atlantic]]: what the scale of the Atlantic trade allows us to say, and what we must not invent. The crown of 1701 and the invasion of 1740 return in [[states/east]], with the Ottoman empire as a European power and with Poland-Lithuania removed by partition, not blamed for having a parliament. Labels and claims are [[sources/argument]]. One bank is not a continent: [[sources/scale]]. The peace of 24 October 1648 remains [[reformations/religious-war]].

:::summary
- A fiscal-military state sustains armed force by tax and by organised debt. The phrase does not name one constitution and does not require a king.
- The Dutch Republic is rich, armed and not a monarchy. The VOC is chartered in 1602. The Twelve Years’ Truce begins in 1609 and runs to 1621. Spain’s recognition in January 1648 does not create that capacity.
- Louis XIV is one solution, not the model of all Europe. On 18 October 1685 he revokes the Edict of Nantes at Fontainebleau. The Code Noir is the same year, and [[states/atlantic]] takes it up.
- On 12 September 1683 the siege of Vienna is lifted at Kahlenberg. That is not “Europe versus Asia” as a permanent fact.
- The English dates are instruments, not moral progress: the execution of Charles I on 30 January 1649, the Navigation Act of 1651, the Restoration of 1660, William’s landing in 1688, the Bill of Rights of 1689, the Bank of England in 1694.
- “Glorious Revolution” is a partisan name. A Catholic king is replaced and parliament’s place is restated. It is not a democratic revolution.
- In 1701 the Act of Settlement and the Prussian crown are two events. The union of 1 May 1707 and the Treaty of Utrecht in 1713 are further instruments. In 1740 Frederick II invades Silesia. No army size is stated.
