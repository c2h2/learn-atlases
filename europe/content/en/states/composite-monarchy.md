What is a state that is several kingdoms? A coloured map answers too quickly. It wants one fill for Spain, one fill for Britain, one fill for a republic, as if a border and a capital were the same fact as a law. In the dates this lesson can actually use, rule is often a stack. One person wears several titles. Each title arrives with its own estates, its own courts and its own way of saying what the ruler may demand.

That stack is the thing to define, and then to test. In 1556 Charles V abdicates, and a famous bundle of crowns comes apart without becoming a nation on the way out. In 1579 provinces inside the Habsburg part of that world ally with one another; in 1581 they renounce their king. What lasts is a republic of provinces, not a new national crown. On 1 May 1707 the kingdoms of England and Scotland are joined by statute, under Queen Anne, into one parliament. The statute is a decision. It is not a medical record of two peoples growing into one body. Between the sharing of the Stuart crowns and that statute, this atlas will not insert a year. The chronology does not lock one. A borrowed year would pretend that a dynasty is a date we have settled.

The contrast, held to the end, is with a nation-state. Shared rule is not shared nationhood. A harsh prince is not proof that the parts had one law. A republic that leaves a composite monarchy is still evidence about composites. We are not naming the whole period absolutism, and we are not inventing a constitution for Spain that would make the stack vanish on paper.

## One ruler, several laws

:::definition Composite monarchy {#def-composite}
A **composite monarchy** is a polity in which one ruler holds more than one kingdom, principality or province, and the parts keep distinct laws, estates or privileges. The union sits in the person or the dynasty. It is not a nation-state, and it is not a single constitution that has already replaced the laws of the parts.
:::

Read the definition as a set of questions you can take to a date. Is there one ruler and more than one law? Does the act in front of you merge the laws, or does it move the titles? Who is entitled to consent, and in which assembly? If you cannot answer, the date is not yet a story about a nation. It may still be a violent story. Composite does not mean mild. It means the legal parts are plural even when the prince is singular, and even when the prince spends his reign trying to be obeyed.

The opposite error is to treat every surviving privilege as a brake that worked. Estates could be ignored, bought, bullied or left unsummoned. A composite monarchy can collect taxes, quarter soldiers and punish rebels in several realms at once. The claim is not that local law always won. The claim is that winning, where it happened, still happened inside a stack of jurisdictions rather than inside one national code. What would show the stronger thesis, that the stack had already ended, is a constitution in force that replaced the laws of the parts. We do not have that document for the Spanish monarchy, and we will not write a substitute.

J. H. Elliott’s comparison of the Spanish and British Atlantic empires uses this ordinary fact against a cartoon in which one of those monarchies is the modern nation arriving early and the other is a nation arriving late. The comparison belongs, in its full form, to the labour systems of [[states/atlantic]]. Here it is only a guard-rail. Do not sort the dates below into a race toward a later map.

:::definition Nation-state {#def-nation}
A **nation-state**, as this lesson refuses to find in these dates, is a polity that presents one people, one law and one territory as a single membership. A shared monarch, a shared language or a statute that joins two parliaments is not yet that presentation. The word is a claim about membership. Used of 1556 or of 1707, it has to be shown from the act, not assumed because a modern country has a similar name.
:::

## The abdications of 1556

Charles V is the teaching case because the titles are many and the exit is dated. He held the Spanish crowns, with the American lands ruled in their name, the Burgundian inheritance in the Netherlands and the Franche-Comté, Italian possessions attached to Aragon, and the imperial title. Those are not counties of one kingdom. They are political worlds with their own estates and their own memories of contract. Castile is not Aragon. The Netherlands are not Naples. The empire is not a Spanish province. One man can be the ruler of all of them and still not be the ruler of one state in the sense of [[#def-nation]].

In 1556 he abdicates. The year is the atlas’s date for the abdications, plural, and we will not split it into a second year to make a procession of ceremonies. The plural is the point. He lays the titles down, and they do not travel forward as a single constitution. The Spanish crowns and the Burgundian lands pass to his son Philip. The imperial dignity passes to his brother Ferdinand, who had already been the emperor’s partner in the German lands. The bundle can be untied because it was tied in a person. A nation, had one existed in the strong sense, would not have been divided between a son and a brother as an inheritance.

Philip’s monarchy remains a stack. Distinct councils speak for distinct groups of lands. The Cortes of Castile are not the Corts of Aragon, and neither is the assembly of a Netherlandish province. Viceroys govern kingdoms the king does not live in. American viceroyalties extend the same habit across the ocean: royal law claimed at a distance, local institutions under that claim, and labour systems that [[states/atlantic]] will not reduce to a metaphor. None of this is a constitution of “Spain” in the later sense. To invent one, with articles we do not have, would hide the very plurality the abdication makes visible.

The hardness of Philip’s government is not a counter-example. A prince who presses his Netherlandish subjects, or who expects American revenue, is using the reach of a composite crown. He is not proving that the crowns have melted. The revolt that follows is the proof from the other side. Parts of a composite can refuse the prince and carry their own laws into the refusal.

:::theorem A dated act on titles is not a birth of a nation {#thm-not-nation}
The abdications of 1556, the Union of Utrecht in 1579, the Act of Abjuration in 1581 and the Acts of Union of 1 May 1707 all change who rules. None of them is evidence that a nation-state had replaced a composite of laws. The claim stops there. It does not say the parts were equal, and it does not say a local privilege always survived contact with the prince.
:::
:::proof
The shape of each act is the evidence. In 1556 one man resigns several dignities and they are divided between his son and his brother. No document in this lesson’s record replaces Castilian, Aragonese, Netherlandish and imperial law with one code on the way out. In 1579 provinces make a treaty with one another, and in 1581 they abjure Philip, and the durable result is a republic of provinces which Spain recognises at Münster only in January 1648. On 1 May 1707 two parliaments create the parliament of Great Britain by statute. A statute is an instrument precisely because the union was not already a natural fact. What would count against [[#thm-not-nation]] is a law, in force at one of these dates, that abolished the separate laws of the parts and installed a single national membership, together with evidence that this law is what the date did. The 1707 settlement leaves Scots private law and the Church of Scotland in place. We have no such unifying constitution for the monarchy Philip inherited. A courtier’s boast about one king would not be that law even if we had the sentence. We do not, and we will not invent one.
:::

## Leaving the stack

The lands Philip inherited in the Netherlands had been Habsburg because they had been Burgundian. They were rich, urban and jealous of provincial privilege. The conflict that breaks them away from his rule was already violent before the dates we lock. We will not supply a missing start year, and we will not narrate every campaign. The dates that settle the constitutional result are 1579 and 1581.

The Union of Utrecht in 1579 is an alliance of provinces that are fighting the king, not the manifesto of a Dutch nation already formed. Provinces agree to remain together in the war and to leave a great deal of religion and ordinary government in provincial hands. The agreement is itself composite. It stacks authorities. It does not melt them. Nor does it describe the whole Netherlands. The southern provinces largely remain, or are brought back, under Habsburg rule. One composite monarchy loses a part of itself and keeps a part. That sentence is awkward for any map that wants “the Netherlands” to change colour all at once. The awkwardness is the fact.

The Act of Abjuration in 1581 is the renunciation. The States-General of the rebelling provinces withdraw allegiance from Philip. Paraphrasing the act, not quoting it: a prince who destroys the liberties he was bound to uphold may be forsaken. You do not need to accept the theory in order to see the institutional result. A king is removed in the name of the parts, and the parts do not then disappear into a new national crown that holds. The provinces look for outside princes and do not settle into a lasting monarchy. What lasts is a republic.

:::theorem Absolutism is not the name of the period {#thm-not-absolutism}
Absolutism is a label for a style of royal government, advertised more loudly at some courts than at others. It is not the name of the period these dates belong to. A composite monarchy can be severe without being a single absolute system, and the republic produced by the abjuration of 1581 is a rich armed state that is not a monarchy at all.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the coexistence of unlike polities under the same sky. After 1581 the United Provinces fight, tax and trade as a republic of provinces. The Habsburg crowns continue as a composite monarchy. The Stuart kingdoms, treated below, are a multiple monarchy that will later be joined in part by statute. These are not one constitutional type wearing different hats. The next lesson adds a chartered company, a bank and a French revocation, which differ again. What would count against [[#thm-not-absolutism]] is a showing that every major state at these dates had the same institutions under the same kind of ruler, so that one label predicted the structure. It would also count against the claim if “absolute” were being used only as a moral word for “unpleasant”, with no institutional content. We are refusing that use. Severity is common. The institutions are not the same. A label that hides the republic, or that hides surviving provincial law, fails the dates.
:::

Spain’s recognition of that republic is a later treaty fact, not a second abjuration. In January 1648, at Münster, Spain recognises the United Provinces. The Peace of Westphalia, Münster and Osnabrück together, is 24 October 1648. [[reformations/religious-war]] is where that peace is taught, including the warning that a finished theory of modern sovereignty is a later reading. We use the January recognition here for a narrower reason. From the abjuration to the recognition is a long life for a state that began as a composite rebellion. The republic did not need to wait for a philosopher of sovereignty before it taxed and fought. The fiscal instruments of that life, including the charter of the Dutch East India Company in 1602 and the Twelve Years’ Truce that begins in 1609, are [[states/fiscal-military]]. This lesson stops at the kind of state the rebellion became.

## Crowns held together, then a statute

The Stuart kingdoms are the second composite, and they come with a prohibition. England, Scotland and Ireland came to be held by one dynasty. A king of Scotland held the English crown and the Irish crown as well. The chronology does not lock the year in which that personal union begins. We will not add it. The sentence we are allowed is already enough: the crowns were held together before the parliamentary union. Three kingdoms, three parliaments, more than one church, more than one law. That is [[#def-composite]] in the British Isles. Calling the arrangement Britain, as if 1707 had already happened, is how the later state colonises the earlier one in a paragraph.

Ireland is the part most easily dropped by a story that wants to hurry to Great Britain. It was a kingdom under the same crown, with its own parliament, and with a politics of conquest and plantation that this lesson will not turn into a population. The Acts of Union of 1707 do not include it. After 1 May 1707 a single parliament serves England and Scotland, and Ireland remains a further kingdom under the crown. A later union of Great Britain and Ireland is not given a year in this atlas, so none will be invented here. The omission is a rule of evidence, not a claim that Ireland was unimportant. A multiple monarchy can survive beside a partial parliamentary union. That survival is itself the definition at work.

The three kingdoms also went to war with one another and with their king. The next lesson owns the dated English sequence: the execution of Charles I on 30 January 1649, the Navigation Act of 1651, the Restoration of 1660. Here those events have one use. A multiple monarchy can break apart and be restored without becoming a nation-state on either occasion. The execution did not found the United Kingdom. The Restoration did not either. We will not tell the campaigns, and we will not give an army a size. A civil war is not a missing constitution.

On 1 May 1707 the Acts of Union join the kingdoms of England and Scotland. Queen Anne is the monarch under whom the statute takes effect. The parliaments of the two kingdoms pass the union, and the Parliament of Great Britain replaces them for the purposes the treaty states. Scots private law remains. The Presbyterian government of the Church of Scotland remains. Trade and the succession are joined. Peoples are not declared to have been one flesh. The course’s own historical note is the right length: the union is a statute, not a natural merging of peoples.

The politics that made the statute urgent can be told without a year we do not have. In 1701 the Act of Settlement fixes a Protestant succession for the English crown, in the Hanoverian line, and attaches conditions to that crown. Scotland’s parliament could still threaten a different successor. The union closes that gap by making one succession for the new kingdom of Great Britain. There was also a bargain about trade, and a financial settlement attached to the treaty. No sum is locked in the chronology, so no sum will be stated. Opposition was real: petitions, riots, and argument in print. Opposition is not a referendum, and we will not invent the tally of a modern electorate. Nor is a parliamentary majority a census of what “the Scottish people” wanted. As [[sources/evidence]] puts the method, a silence in the record of ordinary opinion is evidence about who was asked to decide. The decision of 1707 was parliamentary.

The same year 1701 does a second, unrelated piece of title-work. The elector of Brandenburg is crowned king in Prussia. Two composites acquire a new legal sentence in one year: an English succession statute, and a royal title in northern Europe. They are not one event. [[states/fiscal-military]] and [[states/east]] follow the Prussian title into Silesia and the partitions. It appears here only so that 1701 is not remembered as a British private anniversary.

The abdications of Charles V are dated 1556. The Acts of Union take effect on 1 May 1707. How many years lie between the two years? Use the atlas rule that from year A CE to year B CE the elapsed count is B minus A, the same rule by which from 800 to 843 is 43 years. (Enter the number of years.)
:::solution
Subtract. 1707 minus 1556 is 151. The day in May does not change a count that is made in whole years. Adding one, to be “inclusive”, would give 152 and would break the rule used for 800 and 843. The result is 151 years, and the number is not a claim that nothing happened in between.
:::

:::example An invented sentence about 1556 {#ex-invented-1556}
The following sentence is invented for the exercise. It is not a source. “In 1556 the emperor laid down his crowns and the Spanish nation was born at last.” What does the date support, and what has the sentence added?
:::solution
The date supports the abdications of a ruler who held many titles, and it supports the division of that bundle between his son Philip and his brother Ferdinand. The invented sentence adds three things the date does not contain: a single Spanish nation, a birth, and the word “at last”, which smuggles in a story of delay. Castile, Aragon, the Burgundian lands and the empire are not turned into one membership by a resignation. [[#thm-not-nation]] is the check. A sentence may keep 1556 and still fail.
:::
:::

:::example Two unions {#ex-two-unions}
Set the Union of Utrecht in 1579 beside the Acts of Union on 1 May 1707. Both are called unions. Say, in institutional language, what each one joins and what it refuses to be.
:::solution
Utrecht joins rebelling provinces in a treaty for a war, and leaves a large provincial say in place. It does not include the southern Netherlands, which remain Habsburg, and it is not a national constitution. The Acts of 1707 join the English and Scottish parliaments and the succession, under Queen Anne, and they do not abolish Scots private law or the Church of Scotland. They do not include Ireland. One union is a composite rebellion. The other is a parliamentary statute of two kingdoms. The shared noun is not a shared kind of state. Neither is the birth of a nation-state in the sense of [[#def-nation]].
:::
:::

:::example Recognition is not the abjuration {#ex-recognition}
The Act of Abjuration is 1581. Spain recognises the United Provinces at Münster in January 1648. Compute the years between those dates, and say what the length does, and does not, prove about sovereignty.
:::solution
1648 minus 1581 is 67. For sixty-seven years the republic exists, fights and trades before the former king’s monarchy recognises it. The length proves that the state did not begin on the day of recognition. It does not prove that Westphalia invented a finished theory of modern sovereignty. That reading is later, and it is refused in [[reformations/religious-war]]. A treaty can recognise a republic that composite rebellion had already made.
:::
:::

What does 1 May 1707 do?
- [ ] It merges the peoples of England, Scotland and Ireland into one nation.
- [x] It joins the kingdoms of England and Scotland by statute, without being a natural merging of peoples.
- [ ] It is the year Charles V abdicates.
- [ ] It is the Act of Abjuration, and it founds the Dutch Republic.

The Acts of Union take effect on 1 May 1707, under Queen Anne. They create a parliamentary union of England and Scotland and leave Ireland as a separate kingdom under the crown. They are a statute, which is why they cannot be described as a fact of nature about peoples. The abdications are 1556. The abjuration is 1581.

## What the counter-argument needs

A serious counter-argument does not say “maps are simple”, which we already know. It says that law is a technicality and that identity did the real work. On this view, subjects of Charles V, or subjects of Queen Anne, already felt themselves to be one people, so the stack of titles is a lawyer’s delay. Some elites in 1707 did argue in the language of Britain. Netherlandish rebels did sometimes speak as the defenders of an ancient common liberty. Those languages are evidence. They are evidence of arguments made by particular people in particular assemblies and pamphlets. They are not a survey of feeling, and this atlas will not invent the survey.

Identity-language also cuts against the counter-argument as often as it supports it. Provincial privilege, the name of a kingdom, the rights of the Cortes, the Church of Scotland: these are identities too, and they are identities of the parts. A speaker who says “Britain” in a debate about a treaty is making a claim in order to win a statute. The statute’s text, preserving other laws and another church, shows that the claim did not fully describe the result. Feeling, where we can see it at all, is mixed. The legal stack is what the acts record.

What would rescue the counter-argument is a different kind of source, not a louder adjective. It would be a record, at one of our dates, of one membership replacing the several memberships, with the old laws gone. A poem, a sermon or a parliamentary speech could show that some people wanted that result. Wanting is not the same as the act. [[sources/argument]] separates a claim, a cause and a label for this reason. “Nation” is the label. The claim has to say what changed, and how we know.

:::warning The ladder, and the word absolutism
The usual myth puts these polities on a ladder. Composite monarchies are immature nation-states, absolutism is the adult form, and a statute or a strong king is maturity. The ladder makes later borders into the purpose of earlier law. It also prepares a bad moral for Poland-Lithuania, which [[states/east]] will refuse: that a parliament is a mistake beside “real” states. Absolutism is not the name of this period. It is a style some courts advertised. The Dutch Republic after 1581 is enough, on its own, to break a period-name that assumes a king.
:::

## Where this leads

The republic’s taxes, the company charter of 1602 and the Bank of England in 1694 are the next question, in [[states/fiscal-military]], which also refuses to treat Louis XIV as the model of all Europe. The American parts of the Spanish composite, and the trade in enslaved people that several of these states organised, are [[states/atlantic]]. How to tell a label from a claim is [[sources/argument]], and how a true sentence at the scale of one kingdom fails as a sentence about a continent is [[sources/scale]]. The peace that recognised the republic is [[reformations/religious-war]].

:::summary
- A composite monarchy is one ruler and several laws, joined in a person or a dynasty, not a nation-state.
- The abdications of 1556 divide Charles V’s titles. They do not found a Spanish nation, and this lesson invents no Spanish constitution.
- The Union of Utrecht in 1579 and the Act of Abjuration in 1581 turn a composite rebellion into a republic of provinces.
- Spain’s recognition of that republic in January 1648 is a treaty fact, not the invention of modern sovereignty and not the birth of the republic.
- The Stuart crowns were held together before 1707. This atlas does not add a year for the start of that sharing.
- On 1 May 1707 the Acts of Union join England and Scotland by statute under Queen Anne. Ireland is not in the union. Peoples are not merged by nature.
- Absolutism is a style some rulers advertised. It is not the name of the period.
:::

:::exercise January 1648 beside October 1648 {level=2}
Spain recognises the United Provinces at Münster in January 1648. The Peace of Westphalia, Münster and Osnabrück, is dated 24 October 1648. Why is it a mistake to treat these as one fact called “the birth of sovereignty”?
:::solution
They are close in time and they are not the same instrument. The January act is Spain’s recognition of a republic that had abjured its king in 1581 and had been at war for decades. The October treaties adjust the religious peace of the empire and end a war that was not only a Dutch war; Calvinists enter that imperial peace. [[reformations/religious-war]] refuses the later reading in which Westphalia invents a finished theory of modern sovereignty. Collapsing the two dates into one birth uses a label to hide two claims, which is the error [[sources/argument]] is written to stop.

:::exercise What would make 1707 a merger of peoples? {level=3}
Suppose someone claims that the Acts of Union document a nation already formed, and that the statute only wrote down nature. What evidence would count for that claim, and what evidence that we actually have counts against it?
:::hint
Look at what the treaty bothers to preserve, and at who signed. A statute that protects a separate law and a separate church is doing work that “nature” would have made unnecessary.

:::solution
Evidence for the claim would be a showing that English and Scottish law, church and parliament were already one membership before 1 May 1707, so that the statute changed nothing institutional, together with a record of the peoples’ consent that is not just the vote of two parliaments. What we have is the opposite on the institutions: the parliaments pass an act, Scots private law remains, the Church of Scotland remains, and Ireland is outside the union entirely. Pamphlets that say “Britain” show a political argument, not a completed merger. [[#thm-not-nation]] therefore still holds for 1707. The claim of a natural nation would also have to explain the opposition without turning riots into a fake census.

:::exercise The republic as a nuisance to the ladder {level=3}
A narrative says that composite monarchies mature into absolute monarchies and then into nation-states, and it files the Dutch Republic under “exception”. Using only the dates 1579, 1581 and January 1648, explain why “exception” is doing dishonest work, and state the limit of your own point.
:::hint
An exception that is rich, armed and long-lived is part of the period, not a footnote. Say what would count against you: for example a demonstration that the republic really was a monarchy under another name.
:::
:::solution
Utrecht and the abjuration produce a republic of provinces, and that republic is still there to be recognised in January 1648, sixty-seven years after 1581. A ladder whose normal steps are “absolute king” and then “nation” has to call this an exception in order to keep the ladder. The exception is a major European state. Calling it one saves the label and loses the evidence, which is what [[#thm-not-absolutism]] forbids. The limit: nothing in these three dates says the republic was egalitarian, or that provincial institutions protected everyone. A republic can be composite, oligarchical and armed. The point is only that it is not a stage on a monarchical ladder, and not a nation-state in the sense of [[#def-nation]].
:::
