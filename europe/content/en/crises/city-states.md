What did humanists do, if the Renaissance is a label?

They studied and imitated classical Latin, they taught that Latin to boys who would need it, they wrote the letters of republics and princes, and they pursued a name for the writing. That is a practice. It is not a personality called modernity, and it is not a light switched on against a medieval dark. Italian city-states in this century are republics and principalities with factions, exile and mercenaries. They hire the practice because government needs prose. The fall of Constantinople on 29 May 1453 brings Greek books and Greek scholars west, which is a real connection and not the cause. Humanism is already under way. The year often attached to a laurel crown for Petrarch is not locked in this atlas, so it is not used. His name can be used without it.

The same year holds another ending. On 17 July 1453 Castillon closes the conventional brackets of the Hundred Years' War. [[crises/war-and-schism]] teaches that battle, and teaches Joan of Arc, burned at Rouen on 30 May 1431, as a person in the war rather than a founder of a nation. She is on this chapter's figure so that a century of Italian letters is not allowed to become the whole fifteenth century. From 1431 to 1453 is 22 years. From the fall of the city to the surrender of Granada in 1492 is 39 years. Those gaps are the chapter's first defence against a single rebirth.

## Republics, princes, factions

:::definition City-state {#def-city}
An Italian **city-state** in this chapter is a republic or a principality governing a city and a territory, with factions, exile and, often, mercenaries. A **faction** is a group that fights for office inside the city. **Exile** is a political sentence, not a metaphor. A **mercenary** is a hired soldier. The city-state is not a nation-state, it is not an ancient polis, and this lesson gives it no foundation year.
:::

Florence, Venice and Milan can be named, and so can the papal court and Naples, as places where this politics was lived. Naming them is not dating their birth. If a book offers a foundation year for Venice, or a communal birthday for Florence, this lesson still does not use it. The chronology has not locked those festivals. A city-state is known by how it is governed, not by a founding story. Venice is spoken of as a republic. Milan in this period is spoken of as a principality. Florence is a republic that was also a factional regime, changing hands among its own citizens more than once. The years of those changes are not locked here, so they are not listed. A republic is not a proof of peace. A prince is not a proof that "the state", in a later sense, has arrived.

The ancient comparison is a temptation the humanists themselves enjoyed. A **polis**, as [[greece/poleis]] defines it, is a community of citizens and a territory, not a nation-state, and there was no single Greek state. An Italian city is not that polis. It has Christian institutions, a bishop, a commerce in cloth and credit, and a habit of hiring soldiers. Shared classical reading does not cancel the difference. Using Cicero's Latin to praise a republic's liberty can be sincere, and it can be the brief a secretary was given. Sincerity and a brief are compatible. Neither one founds modern liberty.

Exile is how a faction removes its rivals without necessarily killing them. It is also how a city loses the people who then write against it from somewhere else. The literature of exile is part of the practice, and it is evidence of conflict, not of a golden consensus. Mercenaries are how a city fights when it will not rely only on its own people. Hiring a captain puts armed force on a contract. The contract can be broken, and the captain can become a political problem of his own. None of this is a cartoon of tyrants against free men. Republics hired captains. Princes wrote Latin. The distribution of those facts is local, and this lesson will not invent a table of them.

## What the practice was

:::definition Humanism {#def-humanism}
**Humanism**, in this lesson, is a practice: the study and imitation of classical Latin, and for some writers of Greek; an education built on that study; work in chanceries; and a pursuit of fame through writing. It is not a personality type called modernity, and it is not a rejection of Christian belief.
:::

A chancery is the office that writes a government's letters. Diplomatic prose, laws put into decent Latin, a speech for an embassy: these are jobs. A humanist Latin, closer to ancient prose than to the technical Latin of the schools, was a credential for secretaries. Not everyone in a chancery was a humanist, and not every humanist lived on a salary. Fame is the other half. Letters, histories and invectives circulate among people who can judge them, and a reputation is a kind of capital. Patronage and fame operate together. A writer can want to be known and still be paid by a prince. The modern habit is to treat those motives as opposites, the free individual against the courtier. The practice does not require the opposition.

Education is where the credential is made. Tutors and schoolmasters train pupils, mostly boys, to imitate classical prose and to speak in public. Women were not the usual pupils. A few wrote anyway. This lesson will not invent a quotation from them, and it will not invent a count of them. The exclusion is part of the practice's social shape. It is not a reason to call the practice a liberation of "man" in the abstract. [[sources/scale]] is the right caution: a brilliant secretary in one city is not the mind of Europe.

Petrarch is the name most often put near the start of the Latin revival. Use the name, and stop. A year for his laurels is not locked. Adding it would pretend this atlas has settled a date it has deliberately left out. You do not need the missing year to refuse a worse date. The fall of the city in 1453 is not the beginning of a practice that already has him in it. Philology could also be turned on a document. Lorenzo Valla is the usual name for an argument that a text used to support papal temporal power was not what it claimed to be. The year of that argument is not locked, so it is not given. The point is the kind of work: comparison of Latin, not a new doctrine of sovereignty. [[christendom/popes-and-kings]] is where the papal claim itself is taught. Valla does not replace that chapter.

:::theorem The Renaissance is a label, and 1453 is not its cause {#thm-label}
"The Renaissance" names a bundle of practices in letters, art and statecraft. Used as a label for the arrival of the modern person, it hides the claim it ought to state. Humanism, as [[#def-humanism]] defines it, is already a practice before 29 May 1453. The fall of Constantinople, and the movement of Greek books and scholars that follows, is a real connection. It is not the origin of the practice.
:::
:::proof
The evidence for the connection is the date and the traffic. On 29 May 1453 the Ottoman sultan Mehmed II takes Constantinople. The eastern Roman state ends. Books and educated Greeks move west in the years around that conquest, and western readers who wanted Greek had a fuller supply. That sentence is allowed. The evidence against making it a cause is the shape of the practice itself. Chancery Latin, schooling in imitation, and the pursuit of literary fame do not begin on the morning the city falls. Petrarch's name, carried without a year, already sits in the practice. What would count against [[#thm-label]] is evidence that this Latin practice is absent before 29 May 1453 and starts because of the refugees, or evidence that "Renaissance" in the sources of 1453 already means the birth of a modern personality. The second is a later use of a label. The first is what the chapter denies. Art and building are part of why the label is popular. This chapter's claim is about the practice of letters and office. A picture dated in some other book is not a foundation year this atlas has locked, so it is not imported to decorate the claim.
:::

Greek is an addition, and additions matter. A reader who loves Greek will say the real change is the language, so the cause is the fall after all. The reply is to split the practice. Latin humanism does not wait for 1453. Greek humanism is strengthened by the movement of books and people after the fall, and that strengthening is the connection the chronology allows. Strengthening is not beginning. Ottoman rule in the city, and in the Balkans, is not the end of history there. It is a change of sovereignty. The people who left, and the people who stayed, are not a single exodus you may turn into Europe's rebirth. This lesson gives no count of either group.

## Against the dark, and a worked case

:::warning A dark age and a sudden light
The usual myth sets a dark middle age against a renaissance light. It fails because Latin Christendom already had universities, law and a long argument with Aristotle. Thomas Aquinas dies in 1274 inside a practice of argument, which [[christendom/towns-and-learning]] teaches, not as the medieval mind but as a life. Humanists criticised the Latin of that practice. A criticism is not a census of ignorance. The myth is useful to the later label, because a rebirth needs a death to precede it. [[#thm-label]] keeps the practices and drops the death.
:::

The counter-argument should be stated fairly. The humanists said the centuries between themselves and the ancients had been clumsy. They said it in the Latin they admired, and they said it in order to be hired and admired. Their insult is a source for their taste. It is not a source for the absence of cathedrals, commentaries and communes. Taking a partisan history of style as a history of a whole society is the scale error again. What would count against this refusal is a demonstration that, before the humanists, Latin Europe had no schools and no trained argument. Aquinas's death in 1274 is enough to block that demonstration. It does not make Aquinas a humanist. It makes the dark a fiction.

On 29 May and on 17 July the same year ends two different polities' stories, the eastern Roman state and the conventional Anglo-French war. From 29 May to 17 July is 49 days. Count it in the open: from 29 May to 29 June is 31 days, and from 29 June to 17 July is 18, and 31 plus 18 is 49. The interval is not a military secret and not a cause. It is a way of seeing that the label "1453" hides two dates. Neither date is the invention of the printing press. The figure's next dot is about 1454, the start of the range in which Gutenberg's Bible is usually placed, and [[crises/print]] explains why a range is not a day and why a press is not a cause of the Reformation. The city and the press are close. Close is not one cause. Granada in 1492 is further off, 39 years from 1453, and it is not the cultural payoff of a Florentine chancery.

:::example Two dates in one year {#ex-forty-nine}
Constantinople falls on 29 May 1453. Castillon is fought on 17 July 1453. How many days lie between those dates, on the count from 29 May to 29 June as 31 days and from 29 June to 17 July as 18? What may you not conclude?
:::solution
31 plus 18 is 49. The number of days is 49. You may conclude that one year-name covers two endings, an eastern Roman state and a western dynastic war. You may not conclude that the endings caused each other, that they caused humanism, or that 49 is a count of refugees or of the dead. No such count is locked.
:::
:::

:::example An invented chancery order {#ex-chancery}
This order is invented. A city's government tells its secretary to write to another city in classical Latin, to praise the liberty of the republic, and to ask for a captain and his company. What practices does the order illustrate, and what does the label "invented" forbid you to do with it?
:::solution
It illustrates chancery humanism, republican language used as a political brief, and the hire of mercenaries. It does not illustrate a personality called modernity, and it does not show that the city was free of factions. Exile is absent from this particular fiction, which is a reminder that one invented order is not a constitution. You may not promote it into evidence about Florence, Venice or Milan. Promoting it would take a real letter, a real archive and a real date. The order was labelled invented so that you would not search for them. No foundation year is implied, because none was stated.
:::
:::

:::example A sentence that starts the rebirth too late {#ex-fled}
This sentence is invented. "On 29 May 1453 the Renaissance began, because Greek scholars fled Constantinople and taught Europe to think." Which clause is a real date, and which clauses does [[#thm-label]] strike out?
:::solution
The date 29 May 1453, and the fact that Mehmed II took the city, can stand. Greek books and scholars do move, and that connection can stand if it is not called a beginning. Strike out "the Renaissance began" as a label doing the work of a cause. Strike out "taught Europe to think", which is the dark-to-light myth and a scale error: Europe is not a pupil in one classroom. Humanism as Latin, schooling, chanceries and fame is already a practice. The flight does not found it. The sentence uses a true day as bait for a false origin.
:::
:::

:::example From the city to Granada {#ex-granada-gap}
The figure runs from the fall of Constantinople in 1453 to Granada in 1492. What is the elapsed count of years, and why is Granada not the result of humanist prose?
:::solution
1492 minus 1453 is 39. The elapsed count is 39 years. Granada's surrender, taught in [[crises/year-1492]], is a conquest in Iberia on 2 January 1492. A chancery in an Italian city can admire classical Latin for a generation and have no part in that surrender. Sharing a century, and sharing a figure, is not a cause. The Bible's dot at about 1454 is closer to 1453 than Granada is, and it is still not the same cause. The next chapter refuses that identification on its own evidence.
:::
:::

Move the year to 1453 and then to 1454. The dots sit next to each other, and the caption forbids you to make them one cause. The Bible's mark is "about 1454", the start of a range, not a day, which is why the next chapter exists. Joan's dot in 1431 is earlier, a death in a different war. Granada's dot in 1492 is a conquest, not a poem. A window of ten years will not pull 1431 and 1492 into one view as neighbours: the elapsed count from 1431 to 1492 is 61 years. The figure's job is to keep those distances visible while the prose keeps the practices distinct. Blank years are not empty of politics. They are years whose events this atlas has not locked, including the changes of faction inside the cities. Leaving them blank is the alternative to inventing a birthday.

:::history
On 29 May 1453 Mehmed II takes Constantinople, and the eastern Roman state ends. Ottoman rule is not the end of history in the Balkans. On 17 July of the same year Castillon ends the conventional Hundred Years' War. Greek books and scholars move west. Humanism, a practice of Latin, schooling and chanceries, is already under way, and the movement is not its cause.
:::

The worked order and the invented origin-sentence are the same test run in two directions. An invented document can teach you what a chancery was for, if you keep the label. A true date can teach you a false origin, if you let the date authenticate the slogan glued to it. [[sources/argument]] calls that slogan a label tied to a claim. Here the label is Renaissance, and the claim inside it, once unpacked, is that a new kind of person appeared and that 1453 explains the appearance. [[#thm-label]] unpacks it and keeps only what the dates will bear: a practice already going, a conquest in the east, a traffic in books and people, a battle in France in the same summer.

## Where this leads

[[crises/print]] asks what the press changed once the Bible of about 1454–1455 multiplied texts the humanists cared about and many texts they did not. [[crises/year-1492]] takes Granada off this figure and refuses to let it be the birth of the modern world. [[crises/war-and-schism]] is the war that is ending at Castillon while the city falls. [[christendom/towns-and-learning]] is the universities and the death of Aquinas in 1274, the evidence against a dark age. [[greece/poleis]] is the ancient city, which this one is not. [[reformations/luther]] is still a long way off, and a chancery style does not cause it.

:::summary
- An Italian city-state here is a republic or a principality with factions, exile and mercenaries. It has no foundation year in this atlas, and it is not a nation-state or a polis.
- Humanism is a practice of classical Latin, education, chancery writing and fame, with Greek for some. It is not a modern personality and not a loss of Christian belief.
- Petrarch may be named. The year of his laurels is not locked and is not used. Valla's philological argument is likewise undated here.
- On 29 May 1453 Mehmed II takes Constantinople. Greek books and scholars move. That is a real connection and not the cause of humanism, which is already a practice.
- Castillon, on 17 July 1453, is 49 days later and is a different ending. The press, about 1454, is close to the fall and is not the same cause.
- The dark-age myth fails against universities and against Aquinas, who dies in 1274. A humanist's insult is a taste, not a census.
- Granada in 1492 is 39 years after the fall. Sharing the century does not make a conquest into a consequence of prose.
:::

:::exercise Republic, liberty, captain {level=3}
