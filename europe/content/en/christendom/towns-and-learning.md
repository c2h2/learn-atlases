What is a university, if it is not the birth of the middle class? The compliment hides the institution. In the century that runs from the Fourth Lateran Council in November 1215 to the fall of Acre in 1291, with the death of Thomas Aquinas in 1274 between them, some towns had sworn governments, some merchants lived by trade across kingdoms, and some masters and students formed corporations with legal privileges. Those are three facts. They are not one rising class, and they are not the moment capitalism begins.

A commune is a sworn urban government with privileges. A university is a corporation of masters, or of students, with a legal privilege to organise study. Neither privilege is a modern middle class. This atlas has not locked a foundation year for Bologna, Paris or Oxford. The missing birthday is not a blank to fill with a round number. It is a refusal. Teaching the institution without a fake start is the point of the chapter.

## An oath is not a class

A town in this period is a dense place, often walled, with a market and a lord somewhere in its past or its present. Density is not yet a government. A commune is what happens when a body of townsmen swears an association and obtains, or seizes, the right to judge, tax and regulate inside the walls. The lord might be a bishop, a count or a king. The privilege might be granted or wrung out. The oath defines who is in the body. It does not enfranchise every adult in the streets. The unsworn poor, the servants and many of the women who trade or work are not thereby the commune. Italian towns sometimes later fought over whether a wider popolo should share the offices. That fight is real urban politics. It is not the birth of a class with one interest and one future.

The words for the officers vary, and the variation is a warning against one story. Consuls in one city, a podestà brought in from outside in another, a royal charter in a third: these are different ways of holding a privilege. A fair, a wall and a court can sit inside any of them. None of those objects is a sociological type called the middle class, a phrase that bundles occupation, culture and politics as if they had been born together. Merchants could be rich and still divided. Artisans could share a street and not a programme. A lord could live in a town and still be a lord in the sense of [[christendom/lords-and-peasants]], drawing rents from the countryside the town consumed.

:::definition Commune {#def-commune}
A **commune** is a sworn urban government holding privileges: typically a court, a market, a share of taxation, and the right to act as a body. It is not a middle class, not a modern municipality with a universal electorate, and not a claim that the town's poor governed themselves.
:::

:::definition University {#def-university}
A **university** is a corporation of masters, or of students, recognised as having a legal privilege to organise study, to grant a licence to teach, and to act in law as a body. It is not a building, not a birthday, and not the medieval mind. The word for a corporation was not reserved for schools. A university is one use of that legal form.
:::

Bologna can be named for law and for a corporation in which students had unusual weight. Paris can be named for theology and for a corporation of masters. Oxford can be named as the English kingdom's great school of the same kind. None of those sentences is a foundation date. If a book offers you a year for the "birth" of one of them, and the year is not in this atlas's chronology, the year is not available here. Do not invent a substitute. The institution is visible in privileges, faculties and quarrels, not in a starting gun.

Faculties are part of the form and not one curriculum. Arts, theology, law and medicine appear in different combinations. A faculty of theology is not a faculty of law with religious vocabulary. The practice inside them is argument: a question, objections, distinctions, a reply. That practice is why a master's death does not close the corporation. Other masters continue. Students continue. Disagreement is the institution working.

:::theorem Privileges are not a middle class {#thm-privilege}
A commune and a university are privileges held by a defined body. They do not, by themselves, create a middle class or a capitalist economy. The claim does not deny commercial wealth. It denies the extra sentences the wealth is often made to carry.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the form of the institutions. An oath, a charter, a licence to teach, a faculty: each names a body and a right. None of them names a class called the middle class, and none of them states a new economic system. Trade in wool, cloth, grain, wine and goods from further east is real in these towns. Credit and partnership are real. This atlas locks no volume, no price series and no percentage, so none is invented. A privilege to hold a market is not a theory of wage labour and accumulation. Capitalism, as a later word, bundles those further things. Using it here would hide the privilege under a label.

What would count against the claim is a privilege which said that it was founding such a class, or a measure, locked in this chronology, of a new economy beginning at a university's birthday. There is no such measure, and there is no such birthday to attach it to. Town and gown could quarrel. A commune and a university in the same city were not one social actor. That quarrel is everyday evidence against the bundle. What would not count against the claim is the existence of rich merchants. Wealth is granted. The class and the economic system are the steps that wealth does not take by itself.
:::

There is a fair objection. Some families really did live by commerce rather than by lordship, and the streets of the larger towns really did thicken. If we refuse "middle class", are we quarrelling about a word while a new social fact stands in front of us? We grant the fact of commercial wealth and of urban density. We refuse the label's extra claims: that this wealth is one class with one politics, that it begins at a school's foundation, and that it is capitalism. A narrower sentence is the honest one. Towns had privileges, markets and corporations. Comparison can ask who was inside the oath and who was not. The cartoon skips that question because the cartoon already knows the answer.

Which sentence matches [[#thm-privilege]]?
- [ ] A commune is the birth of modern democracy because it was sworn.
- [ ] A university's missing foundation year should be filled with a round number so the story can start.
- [x] A sworn town government and a corporation of scholars are privileges, not a middle class and not capitalism.
- [ ] Commercial wealth did not exist in these towns at all.
:::solution
The claim refuses two promotions: from a privilege to a class, and from a market to capitalism. It does not deny trade. A missing foundation year is not an invitation to invent one. An oath defines a body. It does not enfranchise every inhabitant, so it is not modern democracy either.

## Three dots, and a missing one

:::widget timeline
start: 1200
end: 1300
window: 10
events: 1215,Lateran IV; 1274,Death of Aquinas; 1291,Acre falls
caption: Move the year. A council, a theologian's death and the fall of Acre share a century. They are not one process. The university has no dot, because this atlas has not locked a foundation year.
:::

The figure is an argument about what not to connect. November 1215 is the Fourth Lateran Council, under Innocent III, the same year as Magna Carta and not the same act. [[christendom/popes-and-kings]] keeps the June peace a baronial charter. The council legislates for a society it treats as Christian: pastoral rules, and also the dress rules that [[christendom/exclusion]] reads as law about categories. A council is not a university, even though schools lived under the Church's legal world.

The death of Thomas Aquinas in 1274 is a life ending, not a process completing. Acre in 1291 is the end of the crusader principalities on the mainland, already told in [[christendom/crusades]]. From the council to the death is 59 years. 1274 minus 1215 is 59. From the death to Acre is 17 years. 1291 minus 1274 is 17. Neither gap is a cause. A theologian does not die because a council met, and a port does not fall because a theologian died. Sharing a century, which is all the window shows, is not sharing a mechanism.

The university has no dot. That absence is deliberate. Bologna, Paris and Oxford are institutions in this chapter and not events on the line. A reader who treats the edge of the window as a foundation has mistaken a scale for a fact. The edge is not an event at all. Do not promote it into a year of origin, and do not import a year from outside this chronology to cure the blank.

:::example Council to death {#ex-fifty-nine}
How many years lie between the Fourth Lateran Council in 1215 and the death of Thomas Aquinas in 1274? Why is that span not one process?
:::solution
1274 minus 1215 is 59. The council legislates for Latin Christian society, including rules the next chapter treats as exclusion. The death is one Dominican's life ending inside a practice of argument. Fifty-nine years do not make the death a consequence of the council, and they do not found a university. The figure places them on one line so that the distance, not a fusion, is what you see.
:::
:::

:::example Death to a port {#ex-seventeen}
How many years lie between 1274 and the fall of Acre in 1291? What would be wrong with calling the fall a result of the death?
:::solution
1291 minus 1274 is 17. Acre falls, and the mainland crusader principalities end. Nothing in that military and political ending is explained by a master's death. Seventeen years of calendar do not carry a cause. Trade with the eastern Mediterranean does not stop as a concept because a port falls, and a corporation of masters does not dissolve because Acre falls. The two dots are neighbours on a century-scale figure and strangers as explanations.
:::
:::

:::example An invented grant {#ex-grant}
The following text is invented. "The count grants the sworn men of the invented town of Millcross the right to hold a Saturday market and to judge disputes of that market in their own court. In this year the middle class is born."

What may a historian infer from the first sentence if it were a real charter, and why must the second sentence be refused even then?
:::solution
If the first sentence were a real charter, it would support a privilege: a defined body, a market, a court. It would not support a population, the opinions of the unsworn, or the prices in the market. The second sentence is not a privilege. It is a later label, and in this example it is invented along with the town. [[#thm-privilege]] says a market right is not the birth of a class. Promoting the second sentence would require the charter to use that category and a chronology that dated the birth. We have neither. Millcross remains invented even if the logic is clear.
:::
:::

## A life inside an argument

Thomas Aquinas dies in 1274. He was a Dominican, one of the friars whose work sat in towns and in schools rather than only on the landed estates of an older monasticism. The friars, Franciscan and Dominican, preached, lived by alms, and argued. This atlas has not locked a foundation year for either order, so none is stated. The locked fact is the death. Use it as a life inside a practice, not as the medieval mind. There was no such single mind. Other masters argued before him and after him. Some of his conclusions were disputed in the schools. Dispute is not a scandal that ends the university. It is the ordinary work of a corporation of arguers. This chapter will not date those disputes beyond 1274, because the further years are not locked.

The method is ordinary and worth naming, because "the medieval mind" makes it invisible. A question is posed. Objections are set out. Distinctions are made. A reply is given. Aristotle is among the authorities commented on. The commentary is not a copy. It is an argument about how the authority fits a Christian theological question, and the fit can fail. A reader who wants Aquinas to be the summary of his century has to ignore the objections, including the ones his colleagues kept raising after the death. The date is a death. The practice does not die with him.

:::theorem A death is not a foundation {#thm-death}
The death of Thomas Aquinas in 1274 dates a life inside a practice of argument. It does not date the birth of a European way of thinking, and it does not date the foundation of a university.
:::
:::proof
The chronology locks the death and refuses a foundation year for Bologna, Paris or Oxford. That asymmetry is the evidence. A life can be placed. An institution's birthday, in this atlas, cannot. What the schools show, without a birthday, is a corporation: licence, faculty, disputation. Argument continues after a master dies, which is what [[#def-university]] describes. Calling 1274 the close of medieval thought would need a source in which disputation stops. It does not stop.

What would count against the claim is a foundation charter, locked in this chronology, for Paris or Oxford in 1274, or a record in which his death is treated by the schools as the end of questioning. There is no such charter here. What would not count against the claim is his importance. A major theologian can die in 1274 without becoming a symbol of a whole civilisation. Importance is not identity. The friars' presence in towns shows a religious life that is urban and argumentative. It does not show that Europe acquired a mind that year.
:::

The same century contains a campaign that is neither a commune nor a school. In 1241 Mongol armies reach Hungary and Legnica. They are not the end of Europe, and the Mongol polity is not a footnote to Latin towns. A Saturday market in an invented or a real commune does not describe Legnica. Legnica does not explain the licence to teach. [[sources/scale]] is the general form of this warning. Here the scales are a town privilege, a master's death, a kingdom's war, and a wider irruption. True sentences at one scale are not true sentences at the next. From 1215 to 1241 is 26 years, and from 1241 to 1274 is 33. The arithmetic only shows that the campaign sits between the council and the death. It does not make them a sequence of causes.

## Routes, without a statistical claim

Trade is why these towns matter to the next disaster, and the connection has to be stated narrowly. People and goods moved. Towns were dense. Routes linked inland fairs, river ports and the sea. When [[crises/plague]] tells how plague is reported at Messina in October 1347 and is in much of western Europe in 1348 and 1349, density and routes are part of the background. This chapter does not claim that trade caused the plague. It claims that a history of only manors cannot see the paths. No percentage of townsmen, no size of a fair and no total of ships is locked here, so none is printed.

Women in towns are not a single legal person. Some women traded, especially as widows continuing a workshop. Some lived as religious women in urban communities that were not the old enclosed monastery and not the university. Their position varied by status, marital property and local custom. That variation is the fact. [[christendom/exclusion]] refuses the summary "the medieval woman" for laws about dress and heresy. The refusal starts here for markets and schools. A university privilege that speaks of masters and students is mostly, in its own voice, a privilege of men. Silence about women in the licence is evidence about who was incorporated, not evidence that women in the town had no economic or religious life.

The friars make a further link that should not be prettied. The same religious movement that staffs lecture rooms can staff inquiries into heresy. Dominicans, Aquinas's order, are later among the men commissioned for the papal procedure of 1231, which [[christendom/exclusion]] describes without describing questioning. 1231 is before 1274. 1274 minus 1231 is 43. A procedure and a death are not one event. They do show that "learning" in this century is not a holiday from power. Argument and jurisdiction can sit in one order of friars. That is a fact about institutions, not a stain added afterwards and not a reason to collapse the friar into the inquisitor.

:::example A campaign between two dots {#ex-mongol}
1241 sits between 1215 and 1274 on the year-numbers. How many years from the council to the Mongol campaigns, and how many from those campaigns to the death of Aquinas? What must you not conclude?
:::solution
1241 minus 1215 is 26. 1274 minus 1241 is 33. The campaigns reach Hungary and Legnica. They are not the end of Europe. You must not conclude that the council caused the campaigns, or that the campaigns caused a theologian's death, or that a commune's market privilege describes either. The sums only place a third kind of event between two dots on the figure. Scale stays uncollapsed. No army size is part of the answer.
:::
:::

:::warning The birthday and the class
Two myths repair each other. One gives Paris, Bologna or Oxford a foundation year this chronology has not locked, so that learning can have a start. The other calls that start the birth of the middle class, or of capitalism, or of a modern mind. A corporation needs no such birthday to be described, and a privilege is not a class. Acre's fall and a theologian's death share the figure's century without sharing a cause. Adding an invented year for a university would make the figure look more complete and the history less true.
:::

## Where this leads

[[christendom/exclusion]] reads the council of November 1215 as law that marks Jews and Muslims and that builds a procedure against heresy, not as the birth of a school. [[crises/plague]] is where density and routes meet a disaster this chapter has not numbered. [[crises/year-1492]] is a later set of endings and expulsions, not the meaning of a commune. [[christendom/crusades]] has already said what 1291 ends, and what it does not end. [[sources/scale]] is the discipline that keeps Legnica, a market and a faculty in different sentences. A label such as "the rise of the towns" works as [[sources/argument]] says labels work: it bundles a claim about privileges with a claim about a class, and then hides the join.

:::summary
- A commune is a sworn urban government with privileges, not a middle class and not a universal electorate.
- A university is a corporation of masters or of students, with a licence to teach. Bologna, Paris and Oxford are named without foundation years.
- The figure has no university dot because this atlas has not locked a birthday. A window's edge is not an event.
- November 1215, the death of Aquinas in 1274, and Acre in 1291 share a century and not a cause. The gaps are 59 years and then 17.
- Aquinas's death dates a life inside argument. It does not date the medieval mind. Disputation continues.
- The friars belong in towns and schools. No foundation year for the orders is locked. A procedure of 1231 is not the death of 1274.
- Trade and density matter later for plague, without a statistical claim and without the word capitalism.
- In 1241 Mongol campaigns reach Hungary and Legnica. They are not the end of Europe and not a town's charter.
:::
