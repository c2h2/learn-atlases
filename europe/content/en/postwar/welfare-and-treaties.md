Whose welfare, and whose treaty?

The years after the division were not one European settlement. A British report of December 1942 becomes a politics, and a health service begins on 5 July 1948. Sweden, West Germany and France build different guarantees in the same decades. A book of 1990 will later sort some of those guarantees into three types. The book is not a description of 1950, and it does not describe the German Democratic Republic at all. Alongside the welfare arguments, six states sign a coal and steel treaty on 18 April 1951, in force in 1952, and on 25 March 1957 two further treaties create a common market and an atomic community. The membership is small. Britain is outside it. So is Sweden. So is the GDR. In the same stretch of years Stalin dies on 5 March 1953, a rising in East Germany is suppressed on 17 June 1953, and October and November 1956 contain both a Hungarian uprising and the Suez crisis. Those facts are Europe as well. Calling the Economic Community "Europe", or calling the National Health Service "Europe's welfare", fails the list.

[[postwar/division]] has already dated the two German states and the North Atlantic Treaty. This lesson does not retell that sequence. It asks who is covered when a textbook says "the welfare state" or "the European treaty", and which dated acts are being hidden by the singular.

## A report is not a service

:::definition Welfare settlement {#def-welfare}
A **welfare settlement** is the set of rights, contributions and services by which a particular state insures its people against illness, old age, unemployment or family poverty. It is not a single European model, and a report recommending a settlement is not the same act as the service that later opens.
:::

The Beveridge Report is December 1942. The war is still being fought. The German surrenders of May 1945 have not happened. The report is a wartime document that proposes insurance against want, as a plan for a British politics. We do not quote it. We do not pretend that a plan is a hospital. The British National Health Service begins on 5 July 1948. From the year 1942 to the year 1948 the labels differ by 6. That 6 is not a claim that Parliament passed one bill in December and opened the doors in the same week. December has no settled day in this chronology, so we do not invent one. What we can say is that a wartime report became a postwar politics, and that the service has its own day.

Whose welfare is that? People using a British service. It is not a service for the French insured, a Swedish citizen, or a resident of the GDR. The government that opened it gives a British answer, not a European constitution. Calling the NHS the European welfare state takes one health service, dated 5 July 1948, and gives it a continent it does not cover. Even inside Britain the service is one piece. Unemployment insurance, pensions and housing were not copies of it. [[#def-welfare]] stops the slide from one service to a civilisation.

## Four countries, and a book of 1990

Britain, Sweden, West Germany and France built different settlements. The differences can be described without a percentage, which is necessary, because this chronology gives none.

The British settlement, of which the NHS is the clearest dated piece, mixed a universal health service with benefits that were often flatter and more residual than a continental insurance wage. The Swedish settlement leaned on universal programmes and on a bargain between unions and employers, in a democracy where the same political family held office for long periods. We do not date those Swedish statutes here. The chronology does not list them, and a borrowed year would be a fake precision. What we can say is that the design was not the NHS under another flag. West Germany, the Federal Republic dated by the Basic Law of 23 May 1949, rebuilt social insurance in which occupation and previous earnings shaped the benefit, and it tied labour to industry through co-determination. That is a different "who": the insured worker inside a status, not only the patient at a public surgery. France built a strong contributory system with an unusual weight on family policy, inside a state that also planned parts of the economy. Again we give no founding day beyond what the chronology settles. The French settlement is not an anecdote about the British one.

None of these is the provision of the GDR, or of Poland, or of the Soviet Union. State-socialist societies had guarantees of employment and subsidised goods, and they also had shortages, political control of labour, and, until the dates in later lessons, no independent union on the model of Solidarity. A typology that never looks east of the Elbe cannot be "Europe".

:::theorem A book of 1990 is not a fact of 1950 {#thm-typology}
Esping-Andersen's *Three Worlds of Welfare Capitalism*, published in 1990, is a later classification of welfare regimes. It is not a description of how Britain, Sweden, West Germany or France stood in 1950, and it is not a description of Europe.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the gap between the book's year and the institutions' years, and the gap between its cases and the map. The NHS begins on 5 July 1948, which is before 1950 and long before 1990. The year labels 1990 and 1950 differ by 40. A classification published forty years after a date cannot be the date. The book's usual sorting puts Britain towards a liberal type, Sweden towards a social-democratic type, and West Germany and France towards a conservative or corporatist type. Those are arguments about how far social rights let a person live independently of the market. They are useful to argue with. They are not minutes of a meeting held in 1950.

What would count against the claim is a showing that the 1990 book was in fact a file compiled in 1950 and only printed later, describing every European society including the GDR. It is not. What would also count against it is a showing that Britain, Sweden, West Germany and France had the same rules in 1948. The NHS alone, a universal health service beside status-based continental insurance, is enough to block that sameness. What would not defeat the claim is the discovery that the three types illuminate something real about later decades. Illumination is what a later classification is for. It is not a fact of 1950.
:::

The counter-argument deserves a direct answer. By 1990 the systems had matured, so a typology may describe them better than any single statute of 1948. Grant that. Maturity is a reason to read the book as a book of 1990, not a reason to slide it back onto 5 July 1948. A second objection says the three types are too crude: the NHS looks universal inside a box often called liberal, and later critics thought the book said too little about unpaid care. Grant that too. The awkwardness shows an argument, not a photograph. A third objection says leaving out the GDR is fair because the book is about welfare capitalism. Fair for the book's question. Fatal if the result is then called Europe. The GDR, founded on 7 October 1949, is still there in 1950 and still there in 1990, until German unification on 3 October 1990.

:::example From a wartime report to a service {#ex-six}
The Beveridge Report is December 1942. The NHS begins on 5 July 1948. What does the difference of the year labels measure, and what does it not measure?
:::solution
1948 minus 1942 is 6. That is a difference of year labels. It measures the gap between the year of a wartime report and the year a health service begins. It does not measure days, because December 1942 has no settled day in this chronology. It does not make the report and the service one act. A plan written while the war continues is not a surgery opening on 5 July 1948. The 6 stops you treating them as the same morning.

:::example An invented sentence about welfare {#ex-invented-welfare}
The following sentence is invented. "After the war Europe adopted Beveridge's welfare state." What does the sentence erase, and what would you need before any part of it could count as evidence?
:::solution
It erases the British limit of the report and of the NHS, the different designs in Sweden, West Germany and France, the GDR, and the gap from December 1942 to 5 July 1948. "Europe" and "adopted" have no membership list and no day. Before any part could count as evidence you would need the report, the instrument that opened the NHS, and comparable instruments for the other states, each with its own date. Even then the sentence would still be false if those instruments differed. An invented sentence cannot be promoted by sounding like a summary.
:::

## Two treaties and a short list

:::definition Membership list {#def-list}
A **membership list** is the set of states that signed a particular treaty and are bound by it. The list is not a definition of the continent. States off the list are still in Europe, including states under the other military system.
:::

On 18 April 1951 six states sign the Treaty of Paris: France, the Federal Republic of Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. It creates the European Coal and Steel Community. It enters into force in 1952. Coal and steel were the industries of war as well as of reconstruction. Putting them under a common authority is a political bet that those six can make some decisions together. It is not a bet that the six are Europe. Britain does not sign. The GDR cannot sign and does not. The treaty is in force the year after it is signed, so even for the six the signature and the institution are two dates. We do not collapse 1951 into 1952.

On 25 March 1957 the same six sign the Treaties of Rome. There are two treaties, not one. One establishes the European Economic Community, a project for a common market. The other establishes Euratom, a community for atomic energy. Dropping Euratom because it is less famous is a way of pretending that one signature was one subject. Paul-Henri Spaak is the figure this course ties to that day. We do not invent a speech for him. The treaties are the evidence.

The six are a small membership. On 25 March 1957 the United Kingdom is outside, and so are Ireland, the Scandinavian states, Spain, Portugal, Switzerland and Austria, and the states aligned with Moscow, including the GDR, Poland, Czechoslovakia and Hungary. Spain and Portugal are dictatorships. Their absence is political, not a proof that the Iberian peninsula has left the continent. The United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark join the Economic Community later, on 1 January 1973. That later date is useful exactly because it is later. The 1957 list is not permanent, and the 1973 list is still not the continent. Sweden remains outside in 1973. The GDR remains outside until the state itself ends, which is not this chapter's ending.

The other military system, the alliance organised from Moscow and usually called the Warsaw Pact, is part of the same map. This chronology does not settle a founding day for it, so we do not invent one. Absence of a settled day is a limit on the lesson. It is not a limit on the continent. People living under that alliance are in Europe while the six are signing Rome. [[sources/naming-europe]] makes the same point from the other direction: the Economic Community of 25 March 1957 is a membership list, not a definition of Europe. We are cashing that methodological claim with the names.

:::theorem The Economic Community is not Europe {#thm-eec}
The European Economic Community established on 25 March 1957 is a treaty among a small membership. It is not Europe, and states outside it, including states under the other military system, remain European.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the list and the exclusions. Six states sign. Britain does not. Sweden does not. The GDR does not. Spain does not. A name that fitted Europe would fit those states or would openly be a club. The treaty is openly a club, and textbooks that drop the word "community" and say "Europe" have changed the subject. The same day also produces Euratom, which is a second treaty, so even inside the club there is not one instrument.

What would count against the claim is a signature list of 25 March 1957 that included the states just named, or a legal text in which the six declared their treaty to be the definition of the continent and the others accepted that definition. Neither is the record. What would not count against it is the later accession of the United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark on 1 January 1973. A longer list is still a list. Growth of a club does not turn the club into a continent. What would also not count against it is the real political hope, among the six, that the treaties were a project for peace. A hope is not a map.
:::

The counter-argument is that everyone knew "Europe" was being used as a project-word, and that policing the word is pedantry. The reply is the Hungarian uprising and Suez, which sit in the next section in the months before Rome. If the project-word is allowed to eat the continent, those crises become non-European events that happen to occur in Europe. That is how a membership list disguises itself as a civilisation. [[#def-list]] is the tool that keeps the disguise off. A second counter-argument notes that the coal and steel community and the common market really did bind the Federal Republic to France, and that this binding is part of the history of the division. Grant it. The Federal Republic is inside the treaties. The GDR is not. The treaties are one more institution of division, not an escape from the map [[postwar/division]] drew. They do not unify Germany. German unification is 3 October 1990.

:::example From Paris to Rome {#ex-paris-rome}
Find the date difference from 18 April 1951 to 25 March 1957. Count leap days you are allowed to name.
:::solution
From 18 April 1951 to 18 April 1957 is six years. Two leap days fall inside that span, 29 February 1952 and 29 February 1956, and both years are settled in this chronology for other events, so they may be named. Six times 365 is 2190, plus 2 is 2192, the difference from 18 April to 18 April six years later. From 25 March back to 18 April is 24 days: March has 31 days, so 25 March to 25 April is 31, and 18 April is 7 days earlier, so 31 minus 7 is 24. Then 2192 minus 24 is 2168. The treaties are 2168 days apart on this count. They are not one signing, and the coal and steel treaty has already entered into force in 1952, inside the gap.
:::
:::

## 1953 and 1956 are not outside the story

Stalin dies on 5 March 1953. On 17 June 1953 there is a rising in East Germany, suppressed. From 5 March to 17 June is 104 days. March has 31 days, so 5 March to 5 June is 31 plus 30 plus 31, that is 92 days, and 5 June to 17 June is 12, so 92 plus 12 is 104. The GDR had been founded on 7 October 1949. Less than four years on the year labels, and 104 days after Stalin's death, workers protest and the protest becomes a challenge to the government. We do not invent a death total, and we do not quote a banner. The date and the suppression are enough to block a story in which 7 October 1949 produced consent. A founding is not a settlement. What would count against that reading is evidence that 17 June was a trivial shop-floor quarrel with no political reach, quickly forgotten by a willing public. The fact that the state suppressed it, and that the day is remembered as a political date, tells the other way. It still did not overthrow the state. Failure to overthrow is not the same as consent.

In February 1956 Nikita Khrushchev delivers a secret speech to a party congress. We paraphrase, and we say so: he attacked the cult of Stalin and the terror of Stalin's rule. The speech is not a constitution for Eastern Europe, and it is not a quotation you may reconstruct. It is a crack inside the governing party of the Soviet Union. What people do with a crack is not dictated by the speech. October and November 1956 show two answers that must not be blended.

In those same months the Hungarian uprising is suppressed by Soviet force. Hungary is a European country. It is not a signatory of the Treaty of Paris or of the Treaties of Rome. The suppression is European history. We give no death total, because the chronology gives none. In those same months the Suez crisis runs its course. Britain, France and Israel use force in a dispute over the Suez Canal, and the expedition stops under pressure, including American pressure. We give no sum of money and no death total. France is already inside the coal and steel community. Britain is not, and will not be inside the Economic Community until 1 January 1973. Two Western European states are using imperial force in the same season in which Soviet force is used in Budapest. The Treaties of Rome are still in the future. They are signed on 25 March 1957.

:::example One season, two crises {#ex-1956}
Set October–November 1956 beside 25 March 1957. What can you say they share, and what collapses if you call 1956 "the Europe of the treaties"?
:::solution
They share a neighbourhood of the calendar: the crises come first, the treaties a few months later, in the next year. They do not share a politics. Hungary is an uprising suppressed inside the Soviet military system. Suez is an imperial use of force by Britain, France and Israel. The Treaties of Rome, on 25 March 1957, create an economic community and Euratom among six states. Hungary does not sign. Britain does not sign. Calling 1956 "the Europe of the treaties" uses a membership list that does not yet exist, and it hides a suppression and an imperial expedition. [[#thm-eec]] is about 25 March 1957. It is not a description of the previous autumn.
:::
:::

This is the worked case. Place 5 July 1948, 17 June 1953, October 1956 and 25 March 1957 against the slogans "the welfare state", "the European treaty" and "the thaw". The health service is British and early. The rising shows a state founded in 1949 without consent. The autumn of 1956 is two crises, not one temperature. The treaties are a small list after those crises, not a name for them. We have not measured generosity, and we will not invent an expenditure share. The designs, the dates and the people covered already answer "whose welfare". [[#thm-typology]] and [[#thm-eec]] are the claims that replace the slogans.

:::quiz
Which pairing is right?
- [ ] The Treaties of Rome are signed in October 1956, during the Hungarian uprising, and they include Britain.
- [ ] The secret speech of February 1956 is the text of the Treaty of Paris.
- [x] Hungary and Suez occupy October and November 1956, and the Treaties of Rome are signed later, on 25 March 1957, by a small membership.
- [ ] The East German rising of 17 June 1953 overthrows the GDR and brings it into the Coal and Steel Community.
:::solution
The rising is suppressed. It does not end the GDR or put it on the treaty list. The Treaty of Paris is 18 April 1951, not the secret speech. Rome is 25 March 1957, after the double crisis of autumn 1956, and Britain is outside until 1 January 1973. [[#thm-eec]] is the claim that keeps the list small.
:::

:::warning One model, one name
Two slogans do the damage this chapter exists to stop. The first says Europe built a welfare state, meaning the reader's favourite one, usually the NHS or the Swedish universal system. The slogan fails because Britain, Sweden, West Germany and France differ, because a book of 1990 is not 1950, and because the GDR is not in that book. The second slogan says the Treaty of Rome is the moment Europe was founded. The slogan fails because six states sign two treaties, because a coal and steel treaty already exists, and because the states off the list do not leave the continent. A club can matter without being a civilisation.
:::

:::history
On 25 March 1957 the Treaties of Rome establish the European Economic Community and Euratom. Paul-Henri Spaak is the figure this course ties to that signature. Membership is small, and several European states are outside it. The coal and steel treaty of 18 April 1951 is an earlier and narrower club, not this day.
:::

## Where this leads

The empires that do not fit inside a treaty of six are the subject of [[postwar/decolonisation-and-1968]]. Suez is already an imperial crisis. India and Pakistan, Algeria and the Portuguese empire are the longer account, and 1968 splits into Paris and Prague rather than one youth. The division these treaties sit on is [[postwar/division]]. The reason a membership list must not be renamed a continent is [[sources/naming-europe]]. The reason a label such as "welfare state" has to expose its claim is [[sources/argument]].

:::summary
- The Beveridge Report is December 1942, a wartime document. The NHS begins on 5 July 1948. The year labels differ by 6, and the acts differ in kind.
- Britain, Sweden, West Germany and France build different settlements. None of them is the GDR's provision.
- Esping-Andersen's three worlds are a book of 1990, not a fact of 1950, and not a description of Europe.
- The Treaty of Paris, 18 April 1951, creates the Coal and Steel Community among six states and enters into force in 1952.
- On 25 March 1957 the Treaties of Rome create the Economic Community and Euratom. The list is small. States off it remain Europe.
- The United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark join on 1 January 1973. A longer list is still a list.
- Stalin dies on 5 March 1953. The East German rising of 17 June 1953 is suppressed, 104 days later.
- February 1956 is the secret speech. October and November hold Hungary and Suez. Rome comes after, and does not absorb them.
:::
