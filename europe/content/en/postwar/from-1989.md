What happened in 1989, and what did not end with the Wall?

A popular picture is one night in Berlin, after which history is finished and Europe is whole. The chronology will not support the picture. In 1989 there is a sequence: the Polish Round Table, with no single day settled here; in May, Hungary begins to open its border; on 9 November the Berlin Wall is opened; in November the Velvet Revolution in Czechoslovakia; in December Nicolae Ceaușescu falls; on 25 December 1989 he is executed. German unification is a later day, 3 October 1990. The Soviet Union is formally dissolved on 26 December 1991, the day after Gorbachev resigns, and not on the night the Wall is opened. In the same year as that dissolution, war begins in Yugoslavia. The Bosnian war runs from April 1992 to the Dayton agreement, initialled in November 1995 and signed on 14 December 1995. Srebrenica is July 1995. We state no death total for that massacre or for the Bosnian war. 1989 is not the end of history. The wars are the proof inside the decade.

The run-up is not the year. In August 1980 the Gdańsk agreements recognise Solidarity. In March 1985 Mikhail Gorbachev becomes general secretary. Those facts explain how a crack became possible. They are not 9 November, and they are not 26 December 1991.

## The run-up is not the year

:::definition Run-up {#def-runup}
The **run-up** to 1989, in this lesson, is the pair of dated facts that come before the year and are not the year: the Gdańsk agreements of August 1980, and Gorbachev's arrival as general secretary in March 1985. A run-up can make a later sequence intelligible. It cannot be substituted for the sequence.
:::

Solidarity is a workers' movement in Poland that wins, at Gdańsk in August 1980, a recognition the state will try to take back. The chronology does not date that later suppression, so we do not invent a year for it. What we may say is that the movement is forced underground before Gorbachev, and that it remains the run-up the record names. Poland in 1980 is not yet the Round Table. The distance on the year labels, from 1980 to 1989, is 9. Nine is not a story of steady progress. It is a gap in which a union is recognised and then pushed out of public life, and in which a new general secretary, from March 1985, begins a reform from the top of the Soviet party.

The reform is the second half of the run-up. We paraphrase the policy, and we say so: Gorbachev tried to repair the union, not to publish a timetable for its funeral. Perestroika and glasnost are the usual names of that attempt. They are names of a policy, not quotations, and not a constitution. What would count against the paraphrase is a programme of March 1985 that scheduled the dissolution of the union for December 1991. There is no such programme in this record. The dissolution has its own days, and they are late. A cause has to be the sort of thing that could have been otherwise. The reforms could have been refused, and the union could have broken without them. We do not claim that March 1985 is a sufficient cause of 9 November 1989. [[sources/argument]] is the distinction. Here we only refuse to let the run-up eat the year.

:::theorem 1989 is a sequence, not a night {#thm-sequence}
The events of 1989 are a sequence of different political acts in different countries. The opening of the Berlin Wall on 9 November is one date inside that sequence. It is not the year, and it is not the end of the Soviet Union.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the order the chronology actually gives, including the limits on precision. The Polish Round Table is dated to 1989 and not to a day. Hungary begins to open its border in May, before November. The Wall is opened on 9 November. The Velvet Revolution in Czechoslovakia is also November, a different country and a different form, a civic revolt that displaces a party leadership. Ceaușescu falls in December, and he is executed on 25 December 1989. A single night cannot contain a spring border opening, a November revolt in Prague, and a December execution in Romania. People could already leave the GDR through Hungary before 9 November. The opening is not the first crack.

What would count against the claim is a showing that Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia and Romania had no events of their own, or that those events were the same act as the opening of the Wall. They are not. What would also count against it is a document that dissolved the Soviet Union on 9 November 1989. The formal dissolution is 26 December 1991. What would not count against the claim is the truth that the opening was astonishing, or that television made one city look like the whole of the year. A picture can be true of a night and false of a year.
:::

A counter-argument says the Wall is what memory is for, and that the other dates are specialist detail. Memory is not the chronology. A reader who can say only 9 November cannot say why Hungary's May matters, or why Romania's December stops the myth of a bloodless year. We do not linger on the execution or describe a method. We record that a dictator fell and was executed, so that 1989 cannot be told as one gentle type. The popular verb is "fell". The chronology's verb is "opened". "Fell" hides a sequence already started, and a union not yet ended.

Solidarity's settled date is August 1980, the run-up, not the night the Wall is opened. The Soviet dissolution is 26 December 1991. The execution and the resignation must not be allowed to share a sentence just because both fall on a 25 December.

## What the opening did not do

:::definition Formal dissolution {#def-dissolve}
**Formal dissolution** of the Soviet Union, in this lesson, is the act of 26 December 1991, when the Supreme Soviet of the USSR dissolves the union. It is not Gorbachev's resignation on 25 December 1991, not the Belavezha accords of 8 December 1991, not the failed coup of 19–21 August 1991, and not the opening of the Wall on 9 November 1989.
:::

German unification is 3 October 1990. From 9 November 1989 to 3 October 1990 is 328 days, a count we will do in a moment. The GDR ends as a separate state. The Federal Republic continues. That is the institutional fact. Some people in the former GDR experienced the unification as an annexation, a loss of a state rather than a joining of equals. That experience is a real argument about the politics of 1990. It is not a second date. We do not tell you which description to prefer as a party. We do say that the Basic Law's republic, dated 23 May 1949 in [[postwar/division]], is the state that continues, and that 3 October 1990 is not 9 November 1989. The opening of a wall is not the end of a state. Eleven months, on the rough scale of the 328 days, sit between them.

The union takes longer, and it takes a different path. On 19–21 August 1991 a coup in Moscow tries to seize the government and fails. On 8 December 1991 the Belavezha accords are agreed among republics. We paraphrase, and we say so: the accords treat the union as ended, ahead of the formal act. Gorbachev resigns on 25 December 1991. On 26 December 1991 the Supreme Soviet formally dissolves the union. Resignation is not dissolution. The two acts are one day apart, and they are two years, on the calendar, after a different 25 December. Ceaușescu is executed on 25 December 1989. From that day to Gorbachev's resignation on 25 December 1991 is 730 days, exactly two years, with no 29 February inside the span. The coincidence of the calendar day is a trap. One day is an execution in Romania. The other is a resignation in Moscow. Neither is 26 December 1991.

:::theorem The union outlives the Wall {#thm-union}
The Soviet Union does not end when the Wall is opened. As a formal union it ends on 26 December 1991, after a failed coup, the Belavezha accords and a resignation. 1989 is not the end of history: the Yugoslav wars are inside the same decade.
:::
:::proof
The evidence for the first sentence is the pair of December days and the absence of any dissolution dated 1989. The coup fails on 19–21 August 1991. The accords are 8 December. The resignation is 25 December. The dissolution is 26 December. From 9 November 1989 to 26 December 1991 the union still has a formal existence the chronology can point at. The evidence for the second sentence is Yugoslavia. Slovenia's Ten-Day War is June 1991. The Croatian war begins in 1991. The Bosnian war is dated from April 1992 to Dayton, initialled in November 1995 and signed on 14 December 1995. Srebrenica is July 1995, inside that war, not after a happy ending. A year that contained the end of history would not be followed, inside the decade, by these wars.

What would count against the formal claim is a dissolution decree dated 1989, or a showing that 25 December and 26 December 1991 were the same act. They are consecutive and different. What would count against the refusal of "the end of history" is a peaceful decade after 9 November with no war dated in the former Yugoslavia. The chronology dates the wars. What would not count against either half is the true remark that by late 1991 power had already drained out of the union, or that 9 November felt like an ending to people who were there. Feeling is not the Supreme Soviet's act. Drain is not a permission to move 26 December back to 1989.
:::

The counter-argument about power is the one a serious reader will make, so it should be answered at full strength. By the time of the coup, republics were already acting as if the centre could not command them. The accords of 8 December, in the paraphrase we are using, treat the union as already finished. If that paraphrase is fair, then 26 December looks like a clerk's last signature. Grant the politics. The claim in [[#thm-union]] is about the formal end the chronology dates, not about a feeling that the union was already a ghost in 1989. A ghost story is how the end-of-history sentence gets written: if the real end was the night in Berlin, everything after is epilogue, and a war that starts in June 1991 is an epilogue too. Epilogue is the wrong file. June 1991 is the same year as the dissolution, and it is not the same event. The widget puts "USSR and Yugoslavia" on one dot for 1991. The lesson splits the dot, as the lesson on 1968 split its own.

From 19 August 1991 to 8 December 1991 is 111 days. August has 31 days, so 19 August to 19 December is 31 plus 30 plus 31 plus 30, that is 122 days, and 8 December is 11 days earlier, so 122 minus 11 is 111. The coup and the accords are a season apart. From 8 December to 25 December is 17 days. From 25 December to 26 December is 1 day. Anyone who says "the union ended in August" or "the union ended at Christmas" has a month and has lost the distinctions. [[#def-dissolve]] exists so that those phrases have to name an act.

:::example Unification is not the opening {#ex-unify}
Find the date difference from 9 November 1989 to 3 October 1990. Say what the count forbids you to collapse.
:::solution
From 9 November 1989 to 9 November 1990 is 365 days. The year 1990 is not a leap year, so there is no 29 February inside that span. From 3 October up to 9 November is 37 days: October has 31 days, so 3 October to 3 November is 31, and 3 November to 9 November is 6. Then 365 minus 37 is 328. The Wall is opened on 9 November 1989. German unification is 3 October 1990, 328 days later. The opening does not unify Germany. The GDR's end as a separate state is the later date, and the Federal Republic is the state that continues. A feeling of annexation, which some people in the former GDR held, is an argument about that politics. It is not a rival day.
:::
:::

:::example Two Christmas days {#ex-christmas}
25 December 1989 and 25 December 1991 are easy to confuse, and 26 December 1991 is easy to drop. Separate the three dates, and give the date difference from the first to the second.
:::solution
On 25 December 1989 Ceaușescu is executed. That is the end of a dictator in Romania, inside the sequence of [[#thm-sequence]]. It is not the end of the Soviet Union. On 25 December 1991 Gorbachev resigns. On 26 December 1991 the Supreme Soviet formally dissolves the union. Resignation and dissolution are one day apart and are not the same act, which is [[#def-dissolve]]. From 25 December 1989 to 25 December 1991 is two years. No 29 February falls inside the span, so the difference is 2 times 365, that is 730 days. The shared calendar day is a trap for memory. The acts are an execution, a resignation and a dissolution.
:::
:::

## The decade's other war

Yugoslavia is not a delayed copy of the Soviet dissolution, and the wars were not a fate fixed in advance. Slovenia's Ten-Day War in June 1991 is a short war at the edge of the old federation, in the same month-window as the last summer of the Soviet Union, not the same politics. The Croatian war begins in 1991 and is not the Ten-Day War under another name. The Bosnian war is dated from April 1992. We do not invent a day in April. From April 1992 to April 1995 is three years, and the Dayton agreement is signed on 14 December 1995, later than that third April, so the dated war is longer than three years. It is shorter than four years from April 1992, because the signature is still inside 1995. That is as precise as an undated April allows. Initialling in November 1995 is not the signature on 14 December. Same season, different acts. A peace that is only initialled is not yet the peace the chronology treats as signed.

Srebrenica, in July 1995, is a massacre of Bosnian Muslims by Bosnian Serb forces, inside the Bosnian war and before the Dayton signature. International courts later found that the massacre was genocide. We do not date the judgments, because those days are not in this chronology, and we do not state a death total. We do not describe a method. Naming the crime, the month, and the legal finding without a figure is the limit the record sets. The same limit binds the Bosnian war as a whole. No death total. The absence is not a hint that the war was small, and it is not a permission to borrow a number from a newspaper. It is a rule of this atlas.

This is the worked case against the end of history. Take an invented dispatch dated the morning after the opening. It can know 9 November 1989. It cannot yet know 3 October 1990, it cannot yet know a resignation on 25 December 1991, and it cannot yet know a massacre in July 1995 or a signature on 14 December 1995. If a philosophy tells you that the dispatch already contained the ending of ideological conflict, the dispatch is being asked to see through a decade. [[#thm-union]] is the refusal. A famous argument of 1989, which we paraphrase and do not quote, treated liberal democracy as the end of ideological struggle. The paraphrase may be fair to the argument. The argument is not fair to the decade. The Yugoslav wars are the counter-evidence sitting inside it, not a later apology pinned on after the claim was finished.

The Helsinki Final Act of 1 August 1975 is sometimes recruited as the cause of 1989, because dissidents used its language about rights. The Act is real, and it is not a sufficient cause of 9 November 1989. A constraint is not a night. The same caution applies to Gorbachev: include the run-up, and do not let it become the year. The Single European Act of 1986 and the direct elections of 1979 belong to the road towards Maastricht, taught in [[postwar/union]]. They are not the meaning of the Wall.

:::example An invented dispatch {#ex-dispatch}
The following sentence is invented, and it is dated, inside the invention, to 10 November 1989. "Last night history ended, the German question closed, and the Soviet Union left the map." What can the date of the invention see, and what is it forbidden to know?
:::solution
On 10 November 1989 the Wall has been opened for a day. The Round Table, Hungary's May and the Velvet Revolution are already in the year or in its November. Ceaușescu has not yet fallen. That is December, and the execution is 25 December 1989. German unification is 3 October 1990, 328 days after 9 November, so "the German question closed" is early by the count in [[#ex-unify]]. The union is formally dissolved on 26 December 1991, after the resignation of 25 December 1991. Slovenia's Ten-Day War is June 1991, the Croatian war begins in 1991, and the Bosnian war runs to a signature on 14 December 1995, with Srebrenica in July 1995 and no death total stated. The invented dispatch knows one opening. It is forbidden, by the mere date stamped on it, to know the rest. That is what it would take to promote it: later sources. An invented sentence has none.
:::
:::

:::warning The end of history
The myth this chapter exists to stop is that 1989 ended history, usually by way of one night in Berlin. The night is real: the Wall is opened on 9 November. The ending is not. Poland, Hungary, Czechoslovakia and Romania have their own places in the sequence. Germany is unified on 3 October 1990, not on the night. The union is dissolved on 26 December 1991, the day after a resignation, two years after an execution that shares a calendar day and nothing else. Yugoslavia's wars run through the same decade, to a signature on 14 December 1995. A second myth, quieter and just as costly, treats 25 December 1989 and 25 December 1991 as one Christmas. They are 730 days apart, and they are different acts.
:::

:::history
On 9 November 1989 the Berlin Wall is opened. It is one event in a year that also includes the Polish Round Table and the fall of Ceaușescu, who is executed on 25 December 1989. The opening is not the formal end of the Soviet Union, which comes on 26 December 1991, the day after Gorbachev resigns.
:::

## Where this leads

The treaty signed on 7 February 1992, weeks after the dissolution, is the start of [[postwar/union]]: Maastricht, the euro, the enlargements, a departure, and a full-scale invasion that as of 2026 has not ended. Nothing in 1989 closes that page. The closure of the Wall on 13 August 1961, which this chapter only opens, is dated in [[postwar/decolonisation-and-1968]]. How a label such as "the end of history" hides a claim, and how a run-up fails as a sufficient cause, is [[sources/argument]]. The two German states that unification brings to one institutional end were founded in [[postwar/division]].

:::summary
- August 1980, Solidarity and the Gdańsk agreements, and March 1985, Gorbachev, are the run-up. They are not the year 1989.
- 1989 is a sequence: the Round Table, Hungary's border in May, the Wall opened on 9 November, the Velvet Revolution in November, Ceaușescu's fall in December, his execution on 25 December.
- The opening is not German unification. That is 3 October 1990, 328 days later. The Federal Republic continues.
- A coup fails on 19–21 August 1991. The Belavezha accords are 8 December. Gorbachev resigns on 25 December 1991. The union is formally dissolved on 26 December 1991.
- 25 December 1989 and 25 December 1991 are 730 days apart. They are an execution and a resignation, not one Christmas.
- Slovenia's Ten-Day War is June 1991. The Croatian war begins in 1991. Bosnia runs from April 1992 to a Dayton signature on 14 December 1995.
- Srebrenica is July 1995, a massacre later found by courts to be genocide. No death total is stated for it or for the Bosnian war.
- 1989 is not the end of history. The wars are the proof inside the decade.
:::
