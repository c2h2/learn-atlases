Why do the end of empires and the year 1968 belong in one European course?

Because the empires were European, and because 1968 is not one event. A survey that stops at the English Channel in August 1947 has cut the British state in half. A survey that treats Algiers as only a Parisian drama has cut Algerians out of a war. The same discipline splits the year remembered as a single youth. May 1968 in Paris is a revolt that does not found a new constitution. On 20 and 21 August 1968 an invasion ends the Prague Spring. The widget will put them on one dot. The lesson will not.

The dates that have to stay distinct are these. Pakistan becomes independent on 14 August 1947, India on 15 August 1947, and partition is accompanied by very large violence, for which we state no death total. On 7 May 1954 a French army is defeated at Dien Bien Phu. On 1 November 1954 the Algerian war begins. It runs to the Évian accords of 18 March 1962 and to independence on 5 July 1962. The label "the year of Africa" is attached to 1960 and is a label, not a cause. The Berlin Wall closes the sector boundary on 13 August 1961, between the Treaties of Rome and the crises of 1968. On 25 April 1974 the Carnation Revolution brings down the dictatorship in Lisbon. In 1975 Angola and Mozambique become independent as the Portuguese empire ends. None of these is a gift. None of them is the others.

## Not a gift, and not only Europe

:::definition Decolonisation {#def-decol}
**Decolonisation** here means the end of European imperial rule over particular societies, by war, by negotiation under pressure, or by a break in the metropole. It is European history because the empires were ruled from European capitals. It is also the history of those societies. Neither sentence cancels the other, and neither sentence means that independence was given away.
:::

The British empire in South Asia does not end as a favour. Nationalist movements are older than the two independence days, and this lesson does not invent earlier years for them. What the chronology settles is the pair of days and the violence that comes with partition. Pakistan's independence is 14 August 1947. India's is 15 August 1947. The ceremonies are one day apart. The political partition is not identical with either ceremony, and the violence is not a one-day epilogue you may skip if you are in a hurry to reach a treaty. Hindus, Muslims and Sikhs are among the people caught in very large killings and forced movement. Historians disagree about how to weigh the politics, the fears and the collapse of order. This atlas will not pretend that one sentence is the cause, and it will not state a death total. "Very large" is the settled description. A round number would be a fiction.

Both facts have to be said in one breath. The sovereign that leaves is British, so the departure belongs in a European course, beside the welfare politics and the treaties of the same era, not in a footnote called "overseas". The people who are killed, who flee, and who build two states are not illustrations of Britain. A sentence that only praises a British exit, or a sentence that only narrates Delhi and Lahore as if London had never ruled, each fails [[#def-decol]]. [[nations/empires]] is where the earlier imperial structure is taught. This chapter starts from the ending, and it refuses to call the ending a gift.

:::theorem Endings belong in two histories at once {#thm-two}
The end of British rule in India and Pakistan, the French defeat at Dien Bien Phu, the Algerian war and the end of the Portuguese empire are European history and also the history of other places. A European course that omits them is incomplete. A course that treats them as gifts, or as only European, is false.
:::
:::proof
The inclusion test is institutional. These empires were ruled from London, Paris and Lisbon. Britain's NHS begins on 5 July 1948, inside the year after partition, and the coincidence of the calendar is not an identity of subject, but it does show one state doing domestic welfare and imperial exit in neighbouring years. France signs the coal and steel treaty on 18 April 1951 and is still at war in Algeria on 1 November 1954. Portugal's dictatorship falls on 25 April 1974 in a revolution shaped in part by colonial wars, and Angola and Mozambique become independent in 1975. The metropole's politics and the colony's politics are one archive.

What would count against the claim is evidence that these territories were not ruled from European capitals, or that independence arrived as a unilateral present with no war, no nationalist movement and no crisis in the metropole. The record will not support that. Partition's violence, a military defeat on 7 May 1954, a war from 1954 to 1962, and a revolution in Lisbon are the wrong shape for a gift. What would also count against the claim, on the other side, is a habit of writing only the European capital: Algiers without Algerians, Dhaka and Lahore without their own politics, Luanda as a Lisbon footnote. [[#def-decol]] blocks that habit too. What would not count against the claim is the true observation that Indian history, Algerian history and Angolan history are larger than their place in a European survey. The survey admits the limit. Admission is not omission.
:::

A counter-argument says a European textbook should leave decolonisation to a world-history course. The reply is the inclusion test, not a claim that one plot explains Delhi and Prague. Scales differ, and a local fact is not automatically a continental story, which is the warning of [[sources/scale]]. Here the state that signs, fights and leaves is the European state. Leaving it out leaves a hole. A second counter-argument says the departures were planned transfers, so "gift" is only a harsh word for negotiation. Negotiation after a war, or after a revolution, is not a present. The word hides who had already made the empire impossible to keep.

## Defeat, war, and a label

On 7 May 1954 French forces are defeated at Dien Bien Phu by the Viet Minh. We give no troop numbers and we do not describe the fighting as a technique. The date is a defeat of a European empire in Asia. It is not Paris deciding, in a calm season, to confer independence. This chronology does not add a conference day beside the battle, so we do not invent one. The settled fact is the defeat.

The Algerian war begins on 1 November 1954, the same year. From 7 May to 1 November is 178 days. France does not absorb Dien Bien Phu as a moral and then withdraw from empire. It fights a longer war in Algeria, a territory the French state had treated as part of France, with a large population of settlers. The movement that fights the French state is Algerian. The army and the governments that fight back are French. Torture was used by French forces. It is documented. We name the crime and do not describe the method. The war also breaks the politics of the republic. Charles de Gaulle returns to power during the war. The chronology does not settle a day for that return, so we do not invent one. What it settles are the endpoints: the Évian accords on 18 March 1962, and independence on 5 July 1962.

Those two dates are not interchangeable. An accord is not an independence day. From 18 March to 5 July is 109 days. Many settlers leave around the end of the war. We give no count of them, and we give no death total for the war. Algerian independence is Algerian history and French history in the same sentence. It is not a clause of the Treaties of Rome, which six states had signed on 25 March 1957 while this war was already under way. Integration and imperial war share a decade and not a project.

:::example One day, and what it is not {#ex-day}
Pakistan's independence is 14 August 1947. India's is 15 August 1947. What is the date difference, and what does that one day not measure?
:::solution
The difference is 1 day. It measures the gap between two ceremonies. It does not measure partition, which is not a single morning, and it does not measure the very large violence, which has no death total in this chronology. It does not make the two states one event. [[#thm-two]] needs both the British exit and the South Asian history. A one-day gap is a check on the calendar, not a summary of either.
:::
:::

:::example An invented gift {#ex-gift}
The following sentence is invented. "In 1960 Europe kindly freed Africa, having learned kindness from India." Read it against 1947, 1954, 1962 and 1975.
:::solution
The sentence is a gift story, which [[#def-decol]] forbids, and it uses 1960 as a cause rather than as a label. India's and Pakistan's independence days are 14 and 15 August 1947, with very large violence and no death total stated here. Dien Bien Phu is a defeat on 7 May 1954, and the Algerian war runs to 5 July 1962, not to a kindness of 1960. Angola and Mozambique become independent in 1975, after the Carnation Revolution of 25 April 1974, so a European empire is still being fought over long after the label "1960". Before any fragment could count as evidence you would need instruments of transfer, not an invented mood. Even then "kindly" and "Europe" would remain words without a list of agents.
:::
:::

The year 1960 is a label, "the year of Africa", for many independences, mostly from British and French empires, and the Belgian empire is part of the same cluster of endings. The chronology gives the label and does not give a roster of days, so we do not invent Ghana's day or the Congo's day by gluing them to a nearby settled year. A label bundles events. It does not cause them. The Portuguese empire is the refutation you can check without a roster. If 1960 had been the end of European empire in Africa, Angola and Mozambique would not be waiting until 1975. They do wait. The label is useful as a warning about labels, which is also the method of [[sources/argument]]. It is useless as a birthday of virtue.

Suez, in October and November 1956, is the bridge from [[postwar/welfare-and-treaties]]. Britain and France, with Israel, use force and then stop. The crisis sits after Dien Bien Phu and before Algerian independence. We do not retell it. The order is enough: defeat in 1954, war from 1954, Suez in 1956, Rome in 1957, independence in 1962. The treaties do not cancel the wars.

## A wall between the treaties and 1968

:::definition The wall as a dated symbol {#def-wall}
The **Berlin Wall** in this lesson is the closure of the sector boundary in Berlin on 13 August 1961, and the symbol people later make of that closure. It is not the beginning of division, which [[postwar/division]] dates to 1948 and 1949. It is not the opening of 9 November 1989.
:::

On 13 August 1961 the GDR closes the boundary between the Soviet sector and the western sectors, so that people cannot freely leave. Division already had two states, an alliance and a blockade with its own endpoints. The wall is a later police act, and it is also the symbol that sits between the Treaties of Rome, 25 March 1957, and the double crisis of 1968. People are killed trying to cross. The chronology gives no total, so we do not invent one. Naming the killings without a figure is the honest limit. From 13 August 1961 to 13 August 1989 is 28 years. The opening on 9 November 1989 falls 88 days after 13 August 1989. Seven 29 February dates fall inside the twenty-eight years. This lesson does not list those Februaries, because most of them are not settled events in the chronology. The date difference is 28 times 365, plus 7, plus 88, which is 10220 plus 7 plus 88, that is 10315 days. A symbol that lasts that long is still not the foundation of the division. The foundation has earlier dates. The opening has a later one, taught in [[postwar/from-1989]].

The United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark join the Economic Community on 1 January 1973. That accession sits between Algerian independence and the Carnation Revolution. It is a treaty fact, taught as a list in [[postwar/welfare-and-treaties]], and it is not a substitute for decolonisation. One state can join a market and still be living through the end of an empire. Britain has already left South Asia in 1947. Portugal is about to lose an empire by a different route. The same decades hold both stories. They are not one story.

## Two 1968s

:::theorem One year, two politics {#thm-1968}
May 1968 in Paris and the invasion of Czechoslovakia on 20 and 21 August 1968 share a year and do not share a politics. Neither is the meaning of decolonisation, and neither is the other.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the difference of agents and of results. In Paris, students and workers, and a general strike, shake a government in May. The chronology dates the events to May. They do not install a new constitution. We do not invent an election day, a percentage, or a quotation. In Czechoslovakia a reform government inside a communist state, a project that can be paraphrased as an attempt at a less repressive socialism, is ended by invasion on 20 and 21 August. The reformers were not trying to be the Paris students. The students were not trying to reform a one-party state from inside its own party. The widget's single dot for 1968 is a piece of drawing. It is not an argument.

What would count against the claim is a shared programme, a shared government, or a single result you can date once and have both events fall out of it. There is none in the chronology. What would also count against it is a showing that one of the two did not happen. Both did. What would not count against it is the true remark that protesters in Paris talked about Vietnam, about empire and about authority in the university and the factory. Talk is a connection. A connection is not an identity. Dien Bien Phu remains 7 May 1954. This lesson does not add an end-date for the later war in Vietnam, because the chronology does not settle one. Protest can refer to a war without becoming that war, and without becoming Prague.
:::

The counter-argument says that 1968 was a world revolution of the young, so splitting Paris from Prague is pedantry, and separating both from Algiers is worse pedantry. The reply is the same as the reply to the gift story. A mood can be wide and the acts still differ. Prague is an invasion. Paris is a revolt that the existing republic survives. Algeria's independence is six year-labels earlier, on 5 July 1962, and it is the result of a war, not of a Parisian May. What would begin to rescue the "one 1968" picture is evidence that the invasion was called by the Paris students, or that the French government fell in the way the Prague reformers fell. Neither is true. You can keep the shared year, which is a fact, and refuse the shared politics, which is a myth.

Lisbon is the worked case that ties the empire back to a European street without blurring it into Paris. On 25 April 1974 officers and crowds bring down the Portuguese dictatorship. Colonial wars in Africa are already under way. We do not invent their start years. The chronology's order is enough: the revolution is 1974, and Angola and Mozambique become independent in 1975 as the empire ends. The army's exhaustion is part of why the dictatorship falls. That is the opposite of a metropole kindly releasing a possession. It is also not May 1968. Different capital, different regime, different result: here the regime actually ends. A student who has one file labelled "protest" will misfile Lisbon, misfile Prague, and misfile the Algerian war as three copies of Paris. [[#thm-1968]] and [[#thm-two]] are the reason to keep four drawers.

:::example Paris beside Prague {#ex-two}
Set May 1968 beside 20–21 August 1968. What may you say they share, and what must you not conclude?
:::solution
They share the year 1968, and on a timeline with one dot per year they share a dot. They do not share a city, a kind of state, or a result. Paris is a revolt of students and workers inside a Western European republic, dated by the chronology to May, and it does not found a new constitution. Prague is a reform inside a communist state, ended by invasion on 20 and 21 August. [[#thm-1968]] is the ban on concluding that one youth, or one revolution, happened twice. The shared year is the whole of the likeness the dates will support.
:::
:::

:::example How long is the closure {#ex-wall}
The wall is closed on 13 August 1961 and opened on 9 November 1989. Find the date difference without listing leap years the chronology does not treat as events.
:::solution
From 13 August 1961 to 13 August 1989 is 28 years. Seven 29 February dates fall inside that span. We do not name the years of those days except where a year is already a settled event. 28 times 365 is 10220. Adding the seven leap days gives 10227, the difference from 13 August to 13 August. From 13 August 1989 to 9 November 1989 is 88 days: August has 31 days, so 13 August to 13 September is 31, September has 30, so 13 September to 13 October is 30, October has 31, so 13 October to 13 November is 31, and 9 November is 4 days earlier, so 31 plus 30 plus 31 minus 4 is 88. Then 10227 plus 88 is 10315. The closure lasts 10315 days on this count. It is still not the start of division, and it is still not the politics of May 1968.
:::

## Where this leads

The wall opened on 9 November 1989 is the first date of [[postwar/from-1989]], and that chapter refuses to treat the opening as the end of history. The Yugoslav wars are the proof inside the decade, which is why a chapter about 1968 must not be told as the last revolt. The treaties that sit before the wall are in [[postwar/welfare-and-treaties]]. The empires this chapter ends are prepared in [[nations/empires]]. How a label such as "the year of Africa" bundles a claim is the craft of [[sources/argument]], and how a local revolt fails to be a continental law is the craft of [[sources/scale]].

:::summary
- Decolonisation is European history because the empires were European, and it is also the history of the societies that were ruled. It is not a gift.
- Pakistan is independent on 14 August 1947, India on 15 August 1947. Partition's violence is very large. No death total is stated.
- Dien Bien Phu is 7 May 1954. The Algerian war begins on 1 November 1954, the Évian accords are 18 March 1962, and independence is 5 July 1962.
- 1960, "the year of Africa", is a label for many independences. It is not the end of the Portuguese empire.
- The Berlin Wall is closed on 13 August 1961. It is not the division of 1949, and it is opened only on 9 November 1989.
- May 1968 in Paris and the invasion of 20–21 August 1968 share a year and not a politics.
- The Carnation Revolution is 25 April 1974. Angola and Mozambique become independent in 1975 as the Portuguese empire ends.
- The United Kingdom, Ireland and Denmark join the Economic Community on 1 January 1973. A market accession is not decolonisation.
