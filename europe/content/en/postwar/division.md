How was Europe divided after the surrender, and by which dates?

The German surrender is signed at Reims on 7 May 1945 and at Karlshorst on 8 May 1945. The Soviet Union marked 9 May, because of the time difference. [[wars/second-world-war]] keeps both 8 May and 9 May rather than correcting one of them out of existence. Those calendar facts end a war. They do not found two republics, and they do not sign an alliance. If you treat May 1945 as "the division", you have skipped the only dates on which the division can actually be checked.

What can be checked is a short list. The Potsdam conference meets in July and August 1945. On 12 March 1947 a speech in Washington, later called the Truman Doctrine, offers a frame for American support. On 5 June 1947 George Marshall, speaking at Harvard, offers aid for recovery. The European Recovery Programme is a programme of 1948, not a sentence in the speech. From 24 June 1948 to 12 May 1949 the western sectors of Berlin are under blockade. Inside 1949 three further acts follow, and they are not the same act: the North Atlantic Treaty on 4 April, the Basic Law of the Federal Republic on 23 May, and the founding of the German Democratic Republic on 7 October. This lesson keeps those dates in that order. A map with one line on it cannot.

We will not tell this as a moral duel with one innocent side. Both military systems coerced people. Both told a story in which the other side was the whole explanation. American power was real, and it was not a sufficient history of European choices. Soviet power was real, and it was not a fate that German, French, British and other governments merely watched. The claim we can defend is narrower than a verdict. Division is a set of institutions with dates. It is not only a line, and it is not only an American decision.

## Occupation is not yet a constitution

:::definition Occupation {#def-occupation}
**Occupation** here means the government of a defeated state by the armies and administrations of the victors. It is a fact about who commands, taxes and polices. It is not yet a constitution of a new republic, and it is not yet a peacetime alliance among the victors.
:::

Yalta, in February 1945, is a wartime conference. Potsdam, in July and August 1945, meets after the surrender. Both are conferences of victors. Neither is the Basic Law, which is dated 23 May 1949, and neither is the founding of the German Democratic Republic, which is dated 7 October 1949. If you subtract the year labels, 1949 minus 1945 is 4. That 4 is a difference of labels. It is not an anniversary. Potsdam has no settled day in this chronology, only the span July–August, so you cannot honestly count days from "Potsdam" to 23 May. You can say something more useful. A conference in the summer of 1945 can constrain what German politicians are later able to do. A constraint is not a statute. The statute has its own day.

The geography that matters is ordinary and easy to blur. Germany was occupied in zones. Berlin, inside the Soviet zone, was itself divided into sectors. The line around a zone and the line around a sector are not the same line. A blockade of the western sectors is a use of access to a city. It is not, by itself, the writing of either constitution. We do not invent a protocol day for the zones. The chronology settles Yalta, Potsdam and the blockade, and we stay with those.

One other legal date falls inside the same months and must not be recruited as a synonym for division. On 9 December 1948 the United Nations General Assembly adopts the Genocide Convention. The word genocide had already been used in Raphael Lemkin's book of 1944. The murder of about six million Jews is the subject of [[wars/genocide]], not of this lesson. The convention is a legal text about a crime. The blockade, which runs from 24 June 1948 to 12 May 1949, is a political use of roads and rails. They overlap in time. They do not explain each other. A reader who folds both into "1948, the new order" has lost both.

Occupation also fails as a moral sorting of the occupied society. Collaboration and resistance had both been real, sometimes in one life, as [[wars/second-world-war]] insists, and the aftermath did not become a single national character. Denazification happened, and so did continuity of careers. This atlas has no settled share for either, so we do not invent a percentage. The absence of a figure is a limit on what we may say. It is not a hint that you should guess.

## Speeches are context

:::definition Context {#def-context}
**Context** here means a dated fact that belongs in the explanation and is still not sufficient for it. A speech can change what governments think is possible. It cannot stand in for the treaties and constitutions those governments later sign or refuse.
:::

On 12 March 1947 the Truman Doctrine speech frames American support as help to governments presented as under pressure. Greece and Turkey are the cases usually tied to that speech. We are paraphrasing a policy, not quoting an address. On 5 June 1947 Marshall's speech at Harvard offers American aid if European governments will organise a recovery. The European Recovery Programme is 1948. Between the Harvard speech and the programme there is a gap in which European ministries answer, bargain and, in some cases, refuse. That gap is the whole point of calling the speech context.

:::theorem Speeches and a programme are not a sufficient history {#thm-context}
The Truman Doctrine speech of 12 March 1947, Marshall's speech of 5 June 1947 and the European Recovery Programme of 1948 belong in any honest account of the division, and they are not a sufficient history of European choices.
:::
:::proof
The evidence for including them is the chronology itself. Two speeches and a programme sit between Potsdam and the blockade, and governments had to respond to an offer of aid. The evidence against sufficiency is the list of acts the speeches do not contain. The North Atlantic Treaty is signed on 4 April 1949 by states that are not Germany. The Basic Law is a German constitutional text dated 23 May 1949. The German Democratic Republic is founded on 7 October 1949 under Soviet occupation. None of these is a paragraph of a speech delivered in 1947.

What would count against the claim is a showing that the Basic Law, the founding of the GDR and the North Atlantic Treaty were American statutes with no European authors and no separate dates. That is not what the record says. What would also count against it is a showing that the speeches were irrelevant, for instance that no government changed course after them and that the recovery programme never operated. The programme is dated 1948, so irrelevance fails too. The claim is the narrow one in the middle. Include the speeches. Do not stop there.
:::

The counter-argument is worth stating at full strength. American armies were still in Europe. American money, whatever the sum, was large enough that refusing it was a strategic choice and accepting it was a strategic choice. This lesson gives no sum, because the chronology gives none, and a false figure would only make the power look more precise than the sources we are using. Power without a number is still power. The Federal Republic's later prosperity is not a date, and it is not a proof that a Harvard speech founded a German constitution. Soviet power is the mirror of the same mistake. The governments in Moscow's reach did not join the recovery programme. That refusal was political, and it was backed by force. It still does not write the Basic Law, which is not a Soviet text, and it is not identical with the founding of the GDR, which has its own day.

From 12 March to 5 June 1947 is 85 days. March has 31 days, so 12 March to 12 June is 31 plus 30 plus 31, that is 92 days, and 5 June is 7 days earlier, so 92 minus 7 is 85. Two speeches inside one season are easy to glue into one "American decision". The count keeps them apart, and the programme's year, 1948, keeps the institution apart from both.

Which statement matches the dates of the speeches and the recovery programme?
- [ ] The European Recovery Programme is the text Marshall read out on 5 June 1947.
- [x] The programme is 1948, and the two speeches of 1947 are context rather than a sufficient history of European choices.
- [ ] The Truman Doctrine speech is the Basic Law of the Federal Republic.
- [ ] Potsdam in July and August 1945 is the same act as the Harvard speech.
:::solution
Marshall's speech is 5 June 1947. The European Recovery Programme is a programme of 1948. [[#thm-context]] includes both, and the Basic Law, and still calls the speeches insufficient, because the constitutions and the alliance have later dates and other authors. Potsdam is a conference of 1945, not a speech of 1947.

:::example Year labels and a summer conference {#ex-labels}
Potsdam is dated July–August 1945. The Basic Law is dated 23 May 1949. Someone says "the constitution came four years after Potsdam". What is the 4, and what is it not?
:::solution
Subtract the year numerals: 1949 minus 1945 is 4. That is a difference of labels, and it is the only subtraction the dating of Potsdam will support. The conference has no settled day, only July–August, so there is no honest day-count from Potsdam to 23 May. Four years from August 1945 would land in August 1949, which is after 23 May 1949. The Basic Law is therefore earlier than a four-year anniversary of the end of the conference. The sentence "four years later" is a label. It is not an elapsed time.
:::
:::

## The blockade is a clock

The Berlin blockade runs from 24 June 1948 to 12 May 1949. Land access to the western sectors was closed. The sectors were supplied by air for that interval. We give no tonnage and no number of flights. The chronology does not, and a heroic total is not the argument. The argument is the place of these two days among the other days.

A conflict over currency in Germany belongs to 1948, the same year as the recovery programme and the start of the blockade. We do not add a statute day for a currency law. The chronology does not list one. What it lists are the endpoints of the blockade. A dispute about money can explain why access was closed. It cannot be swapped for the closure's dates, and it is not the Basic Law.

Read the blockade against 1949 rather than against a legend. The North Atlantic Treaty is signed on 4 April 1949, while the blockade is still in force. From 4 April to 12 May is 38 days. The blockade ends on 12 May. The Basic Law is dated 23 May, which is 11 days later. The German Democratic Republic is founded on 7 October, which is 137 days after 23 May. The ending of the blockade therefore sits in the middle of the political sequence. It is not the last act, and it is not the first. Anyone who says "the air supply saved the city, and so the division was settled" has stopped the clock eleven days early, and has not looked at October at all.

:::example The length of the blockade {#ex-blockade}
Take the date difference from 24 June 1948 to 12 May 1949. Use the difference of the dates, not an inclusive count of both endpoints.
:::solution
From 24 June 1948 to 24 June 1949 is 365 days. The leap day of 1948 falls in February, before 24 June, and 1949 is not a leap year, so that year of the span contains no 29 February. From 12 May back up to 24 June is 43 days: May has 31 days, so 12 May to 12 June is 31, and 12 June to 24 June is 12. Then 365 minus 43 is 322. An inclusive count of both endpoints would be 323. We use 322, the number of days you add to 24 June 1948 to reach 12 May 1949.
:::
:::

:::example Two dates, one year apart on the page {#ex-side}
Set 5 June 1947 beside 24 June 1948. What is the date difference, and why may you not call them one decision?
:::solution
From 5 June 1947 to 5 June 1948 is 366 days, because 29 February 1948 falls inside that span. From 5 June 1948 to 24 June 1948 is 19 days. The difference is 366 plus 19, that is 385 days. One date is a speech offering aid. The other is the start of a blockade of the western sectors of Berlin. They share neither a city nor a kind of act. The 385 days are the gap [[#def-context]] insists on keeping.
:::
:::

## Three acts, not one line

:::definition Division {#def-division}
**Division** in this lesson means the set of institutions that gave Europe two German states and a peacetime alliance with dates in 1949, on top of occupation, a recovery programme and a blockade. It does not mean a single line drawn once on a map, and it does not mean a mood called the Cold War.
:::

The Cold War is a label. Like other labels in [[sources/argument]], it bundles a claim and often hides it. Used well, it names a rivalry between armed alliances, with client states, spies, and two stories about freedom. Used badly, it becomes a moral duel in which one side is innocent and the other is the only agent. We will use the dates instead of the duel. Where the label and the dates disagree, the dates win.

:::theorem Three dates in 1949, not one line {#thm-three}
The division you can date to 1949 is three acts: the North Atlantic Treaty on 4 April, the Basic Law on 23 May, and the founding of the German Democratic Republic on 7 October. It is not a line drawn once on a map, and it is not only an American decision.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the order of the days. On 4 April neither founding date has arrived, so neither German state signs the treaty that day. The alliance is not a clause of either constitution. On 23 May the Basic Law dates the Federal Republic. On 7 October a second state is founded. From 4 April to 23 May is 49 days. From 23 May to 7 October is 137 days. A single line cannot be 49 days long and then 137 days long.

The map is not nothing, and the proof should say so. Zonal borders and sector borders constrained movement. The blockade shows that access could be turned off. What the map does not contain is an author for the Basic Law, a roster of governments answering Marshall's offer, or a distinction between April, May and October. What would count against the claim is a single instrument, dated on one day, that both founded both states and signed the alliance, with no European text among the sources. There is no such instrument in this chronology. What would also count against it is evidence that European governments neither wrote nor refused anything, and that American offices promulgated the Basic Law and the GDR's founding law as purely American statutes. That is not how either date is recorded.
:::

Konrad Adenauer is the political figure this course ties to 1949. He led the early Federal Republic. We do not invent a speech for him, and we do not pretend that one chancellor is the constitution. The Basic Law is a text of a parliamentary republic. The GDR is a separate state, founded under Soviet occupation and ruled as a one-party state. They were not mirror images, and neither was innocent. The Federal Republic carried pieces of the old administration into the new one, and it also built elections and a constitutional court. The GDR offered a rival claim to be Germany, with Soviet troops and a party monopoly. Holding both descriptions at once is the alternative to the moral duel.

A counter-argument says that this is quibbling, because everyone could see the line on the map and the line is the division. The reply is the blockade's clock. If the line were the whole story, ending the blockade on 12 May 1949 would have ended the division. It did not. Eleven days later the Basic Law is dated. In October the second state is founded. Another counter-argument says that only American policy mattered, so the European dates are decoration. Then the refusal of the recovery programme by the Soviet government, the German authorship of the Basic Law, and the separate founding day of the GDR are all inexplicable extras. They are in the record. Decoration does not found a state.

The same clock stops a third shortcut. People sometimes talk as if the Berlin Wall were the division. The wall is a later closure of the sector boundary, dated 13 August 1961 and taught in [[postwar/decolonisation-and-1968]]. Its opening on 9 November 1989 is taught in [[postwar/from-1989]]. The blockade of 1948 and 1949 is neither of those dates. A symbol has been asked to do the work of three different decisions.

:::example An invented chronicle sentence {#ex-invented}
The following sentence is invented. "In 1949 America divided Germany, and the Europeans accepted the line." Read it against the dates. What would you need before it could count as evidence?
:::solution
The sentence collapses three acts into one agent and one object. On 4 April 1949 the North Atlantic Treaty is signed, and neither German founding date has arrived. On 23 May the Basic Law dates a German constitution, not an American statute. On 7 October the GDR is founded. "The line" has no day at all. Before the sentence could count as evidence you would need sources: a text of the Basic Law, a record of who signed the treaty, a record of the GDR's founding, and some document in which "Europe" accepts a line. Even then the sentence would over-claim, because [[#thm-three]] is about three acts. An invented sentence labels a mood. It does not become a source by sounding like a chronicle.
:::
:::

:::quiz
On 4 April 1949, which of these is already true?
- [ ] The Basic Law has been dated, so the Federal Republic signs the North Atlantic Treaty that day.
- [ ] The German Democratic Republic has been founded, and the blockade has been over for months.
- [x] Neither German founding date has arrived, and the Berlin blockade is still in force.
- [ ] Potsdam is meeting in July and August of that same year.
:::solution
The Basic Law is 23 May 1949, after 4 April. The GDR is founded on 7 October 1949. The blockade runs until 12 May 1949, so it is still in force on 4 April. Potsdam is July–August 1945, not 1949. The treaty, the two constitutions and the blockade are four different facts, which is what [[#thm-three]] requires you to keep.
:::
:::

:::warning The moral duel
A common picture makes the years after 1945 a duel of innocence: one camp as liberty, the other as tyranny, with Europeans as the audience. The picture fails on the dates and on the behaviour. The dates are European as well as American and Soviet: a Basic Law, a second German state, answers to an offer of aid, a treaty signed before either German state exists. The behaviour will not sort into one innocent side either. The next chapter's dates already show coercion inside the east, including the rising of 17 June 1953. The western states still held empires, which [[postwar/decolonisation-and-1968]] dates rather than excuses. A duel needs a clean hero. This chronology does not provide one.
:::

If you tell someone the story from the dots alone, you have already made the mistake [[#def-division]] names.

## Where this leads

The institutions dated here are the ground under [[postwar/welfare-and-treaties]]. Welfare was not one European settlement, and the treaties of Paris and Rome were membership lists, not names for the continent. The rising of 17 June 1953 and the Hungarian uprising of 1956 sit on that later page because a founding day is not consent. The surrender dates remain in [[wars/second-world-war]], and the Genocide Convention of 9 December 1948 remains a legal fact beside [[wars/genocide]], not a synonym for the blockade. How a label such as the Cold War hides a claim is the method of [[sources/argument]].

:::summary
- The surrenders of 7, 8 and 9 May 1945 end a war. They do not found either German state.
- Potsdam meets in July and August 1945. Subtracting 1949 minus 1945 gives a difference of labels, not an anniversary.
- The Truman speech is 12 March 1947, Marshall's speech is 5 June 1947, and the European Recovery Programme is 1948. They are context, not a sufficient history.
- The Berlin blockade runs from 24 June 1948 to 12 May 1949, a date difference of 322 days. Ending it does not end the division.
- In 1949 the order is 4 April, then 23 May, then 7 October: alliance, Basic Law, German Democratic Republic.
- On 4 April neither German founding date has arrived, and the blockade is still in force.
- Division is a set of institutions. It is not only a line on a map, and it is not only an American decision.
- A moral duel with one innocent side does not fit the dates or the behaviour.
:::

:::exercise What would defeat the three acts {#exr-defeat level=3}
Suppose a historian claims that [[#thm-three]] is false. Give two findings that would count against it, and say why each finding would force you to rewrite the claim rather than merely add a footnote.
:::hint

:::solution
First, a single instrument dated on one day, founding both German states and constituting the alliance, would make "three acts" false. Second, archival proof that the Basic Law and the GDR's founding were promulgated as American statutes, with no German authorship and no European refusal or acceptance of the recovery programme, would make "not only an American decision" false. You would have to drop that half of the claim. What would not force a rewrite is a true statement that American power was large. [[#thm-context]] already includes that power and still calls it insufficient. A footnote can record power. It cannot turn April, May and October into one American pen.
