Which of Napoleon's acts is a law, which is a crown, and which is a war?

The name Napoleon collects a coup, a civil code, a restoration of colonial slavery, a coronation, a naval defeat, a land victory, an invasion, two abdications and a battle fought after a peace settlement had already been signed. Collected names feel like achievements. The dates do not. A law of property and the family is not a crown, a crown is not a campaign, and none of the three is the declaration by which Haiti left the colonial system. This chapter sorts the acts by kind. It refuses an army's size, and it refuses a portrait of suffering on the retreat beyond the fact that the invasion happened and the retreat followed.

[[revolutions/republic-and-terror]] ended with a republic still in being after 9 Thermidor, reorganised in 1795. On 9 November 1799 that later republic is overthrown. The man who takes the executive is not yet an emperor. The distance from the coup to the crown is part of the argument. So is the distance, inside the single year 1804, from a declaration of independence to a code to a coronation.

## A coup is not a crown

:::definition Kinds of act {#def-kinds}
In this chapter a **law** is a text that changes civil status, property or colonial slavery. A **crown** is the making of a monarch. A **war** is a campaign or a battle. A coup that founds a consulate is none of these three until it is followed by a further act. Putting several kinds in one year, or under one surname, does not merge them.
:::

On 9 November 1799, 18 Brumaire in the revolutionary calendar, the Directory was overthrown and a consulate established, with Napoleon as the controlling consul. The act is a coup inside a republic that kept, for a time, republican language and a concentrated executive. It is not the Civil Code, which does not yet exist. It is not a coronation. It is not the crossing of the Niemen. Calling Brumaire the moment "the empire" began is how later glory edits the calendar. The consulate is a regime of its own. It makes peace and law and police, and it prepares a crown it has not yet put on.

In 1801 the French state settled with the papacy in the Concordat, a religious peace after a decade in which the church had been split and attacked. We do not quote it, and we do not pretend the atlas locked the Concordat as a census event. The year 1801 is available, and the settlement belongs to it. The kind is neither a civil code nor a crown nor a battle. It is a bargain about public worship and clergy paid by the state. A regime that can sign with Rome and, the next year, restore colonial slavery has to be read act by act.

In 1802 at least three things should not be collapsed. Colonial slavery, abolished on 4 February 1794, is restored. Toussaint Louverture is captured. Napoleon takes a life consulate, personal power short of a hereditary crown. A plebiscite was staged. No count of votes is locked, so we invent none. A plebiscite is not a free description of a people. The life consulate is a step towards the crown of 1804 and is not that crown. Restoring slavery is a law of colonial status, an attempt to put the plantation order back, not a step towards Haitian independence. Where French force could enforce it, the attempt mattered. In Saint-Domingue it met a society at war since August 1791, and it failed.

Toussaint died in French captivity in 1803. He did not live to see the declaration of independence. Any sentence that has him proclaim Haiti on 1 January 1804 has the wrong man and the wrong year. Jean-Jacques Dessalines is the name attached, in this course's record, to the new state. The death in 1803 is a fact about a prisoner. We do not describe the captivity. We use it as a date that stops the collapse of two leaders into one legend.

:::theorem The Code, the crown and the wars are not one achievement {#thm-not-one}
The Civil Code promulgated on 21 March 1804, the coronation of 2 December 1804, and the campaigns from Trafalgar and Austerlitz through the Niemen to Waterloo are different kinds of act. A surname and a year do not make them one achievement. The limit: they can serve one government and still be different instruments. Service to one government is not identity of kind.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the instruments. The Code is civil law: property, contract, the end of legal estates as a form of status, paternal and marital power, a narrowing of the divorce the republic of 1792 had admitted. It is not a constitution of political liberty and not an order to a regiment. The coronation, with the pope present, turns a consul into an emperor. A ceremony is not a statute about mortgages. Trafalgar, 21 October 1805, is a naval defeat. Austerlitz, 2 December 1805, is a land victory forty-two days later, so "the wars" are not one outcome even before Russia. The Niemen on 24 June 1812 is an invasion, followed by retreat and failure. Nothing in the Code tells you to cross a river. Nothing in Austerlitz is a rule about married women's contracts.

What would count against the theorem? One text that was at once the civil law, the coronation and a campaign order, or a year in which every dated act was of one kind. 1804 refuses the temptation: Haiti on 1 January, the Code on 21 March, the crown on 2 December. 1805 refuses it another way, defeat at sea and victory on land. Contemporaries could tell a statute from a battle. The later habit of one achievement is ours, and it is the habit this chapter exists to break.
:::

The counter-argument says the split is pedantry, because the acts form one project: law for the interior, a crown to make it hereditary, wars to force it on Europe. A project can be real and still not be one achievement. If the Code and the wars were one achievement, Trafalgar would be a flourish of the same success as Austerlitz, and the retreat after 1812 would be a clause of the Code that failed to apply. Calling failures and laws one jewel is how a biography flatters a regime. [[#def-kinds]] is the antidote. You may say the consulate and the empire pursued order at home and domination abroad. You may not stop at a single noun.

"A law" is not a compliment. The Code gives men a civil status no longer sorted by the old legal estates, and it confirms a wide paternal power. Married women lose capacity they had been gaining. Divorce, admitted in 1792, is narrowed. We do not quote articles. A law that stabilises property and subordinates wives is a social settlement. It is not the Declaration of 1789, not the Terror, and not a gift that justifies a campaign. Some property rules outlive the empire that carried them. Survival is a later fact about legal practice, not a reason to call Austerlitz jurisprudence.

:::example Eighty days in a leap year {#ex-leap}
1804 is a leap year, so February has 29 days. Haiti declares independence on 1 January 1804. The Civil Code is promulgated on 21 March 1804. How many days after 1 January is 21 March? Then say why that gap matters for [[#thm-not-one]].
:::solution
1 January is day 1. 21 March is 31 days of January, plus 29 of February, plus 21, which is day 81. 81 minus 1 is 80, so 21 March is 80 days after 1 January. The gap matters because a declaration of independence and a civil code are different kinds. "In 1804 Napoleon made the modern state" loses Haiti, loses the Code's date, and loses the coronation on 2 December. Eighty days is enough for a law to be a law and a state to be a state.
:::
:::

## Haiti is not a clause of the Code

:::proposition Haiti's declaration is neither the Code nor the crown {#prop-haiti}
On 1 January 1804 Haiti declares independence. That act is not the Civil Code of 21 March and not the coronation of 2 December. It is the political success of a revolution that began in August 1791, against the restoration of slavery in 1802. Toussaint, captured in 1802 and dead in 1803, is not the man who declares it.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the order of persons and of laws. The revolt begins in August 1791. The Convention abolishes colonial slavery on 4 February 1794. In 1802 the consulate restores slavery and captures Toussaint. In 1803 he dies in captivity. On 1 January 1804 Dessalines's state declares independence, before the Code and before the crown. The restoration of 1802 tried to reverse 1794 where French power could reach. In Saint-Domingue it lost. Elsewhere, where the restored law could be enforced, slavery returned. We do not describe the enforcement or count the dead. What would count against the proposition? A Haitian declaration written as an article of the Code, or a declaration signed by Toussaint in 1804. The declaration is 1 January, the Code is 21 March, and Toussaint is already dead. [[states/atlantic]] carries the trade and the two French abolitions, the second in 1848. This chapter's job is to keep 1 January from disappearing into 21 March and 2 December.
:::

The counter-argument says Haiti is a consequence of the French Revolution and therefore a Napoleonic detail. Inheritance is not authorship. The revolt is older than Brumaire. The abolition of 1794 is older than the Code. The restoration of 1802 is Napoleonic policy, and it is the policy independence defeats in Saint-Domingue. Dessalines is not a footnote to a coronation. The reply would fail if the declaration had been an imperial statute proclaimed in Paris. It was proclaimed against that colonial claim. The same months of 1804 show a man becoming emperor and a Caribbean state leaving his legal reach. [[#thm-not-one]] is about that divergence as much as about a statute and a battle.

:::quiz
Who declares Haitian independence, and when, relative to the Civil Code?
- [ ] Toussaint Louverture, in 1802, as an article of the restoration of slavery
- [x] Haiti declares independence on 1 January 1804, under Dessalines, before the Code of 21 March
- [ ] Napoleon, on the day of the coronation, 2 December 1804
- [ ] The Convention, on 4 February 1794, which is the same act as independence
:::solution
[[#prop-haiti]] separates the persons and the dates. Toussaint is captured in 1802 and dies in 1803. The Convention's abolition in 1794 is a French law of freedom, not a declaration of a new state. The coronation is 2 December 1804, after both the declaration and the Code. Independence is 1 January 1804, and Dessalines is the name this course attaches to that state.
:::
:::

## Wars are a third kind

The empire fights, and the fights do not share a result. On 21 October 1805 Trafalgar is a defeat at sea, with British command of the ocean as its strategic consequence. We give no count of ships. On 2 December 1805 Austerlitz is a victory on land against the forces of the Austrian and Russian emperors. The same calendar date as the coronation, one year later, is a battle, which is a useful warning. 2 December 1804 and 2 December 1805 are exactly one year apart and not one event. A mind that remembers "December the second" without a year will mix a crown with a cannon.

:::example Forty-two days, two outcomes {#ex-forty-two}
Trafalgar is fought on 21 October 1805. Austerlitz is fought on 2 December 1805. October has 31 days and November has 30. How many days after 21 October is 2 December? What does the pair do to the phrase "the victory of 1805"?
:::solution
From 21 October to 21 November is 31 days. From 21 November to 2 December is 11 days. 31 plus 11 is 42, so Austerlitz is 42 days after Trafalgar. "The victory of 1805" erases a naval defeat six weeks earlier. [[#def-kinds]] calls both wars, and [[#thm-not-one]] forbids adding them into one achievement. France can lose the sea and win a field in one season. The Code is not in the pair. It was promulgated the previous year.
:::
:::

In 1807 Britain passes an Act against the slave trade, the trade rather than slavery itself, as [[states/atlantic]] distinguishes. The same year sees a peace between Napoleon and Russia that does not last. A British statute and a continental peace are not a Napoleonic battle and not the Code. They stop a year becoming an automatic chapter of his glory. The peace fails as a settlement. The proof, for this atlas, is 24 June 1812, when the Grande Armée crosses the Niemen and invades the Russian empire.

We do not give the size of that army. The chronology names the crossing and refuses the number. The invasion happened, the retreat happened, and the campaign failed. Further campaigns lie between the retreat and the first abdication, and their years are not locked here, so we do not insert them. What is locked is the political end of the first reign: abdication on 6 April 1814. A failed invasion is not, by itself, that signature. We stop at invasion, retreat and failure. Weather, hunger and the look of a road are not this lesson. The crossing is a war, and it is not the Code.

The first abdication does not finish the wars. Napoleon returns in 1815. We do not lock the day he landed, so we do not invent it. The return is enough to make sense of June. On 18 June 1815 Waterloo is a defeat. On 22 June he abdicates a second time, 4 days later. A battle and an abdication are close and not the same act. Wellington and Blücher win a battle. They do not write the Final Act. That act is already signed.

:::example An invented dispatch {#ex-dispatch}
The following sentence is invented. It gives a size this atlas forbids, so the size is not printed even here. "The Grande Armée, of a magnitude we refuse to state, carried the Civil Code across the Niemen, and therefore the Code and the invasion are one achievement." What is wrong with the sentence, apart from the missing number?
:::solution
Even with the number deleted, the sentence makes a civil code into the cargo of an invasion and calls two kinds one achievement. The Code is 21 March 1804. The Niemen is 24 June 1812. They are different instruments, which is [[#thm-not-one]]. A law about property can travel with armies and still not be the reason this river is crossed. Gesturing at a forbidden size is still using it. The sentence would be true only if a campaign order of 24 June 1812 were identical with the Code, and if a strength were locked in the chronology. Neither is.
:::
:::

## Vienna before Waterloo

The congress had been sitting since 1814. Its Final Act is 9 June 1815. Waterloo is 18 June 1815. The second abdication is 22 June. People who remember a sequence called "defeat, then settlement" have the June dates backwards. The settlement is signed while Napoleon is already returned and before the battle that removes him again. [[revolutions/restoration-and-1848]] teaches what the settlement contained. This section teaches only the order, because the order is constantly reversed in memory.

:::theorem The Final Act is earlier than Waterloo {#thm-order}
The Final Act of the Congress of Vienna is dated 9 June 1815, nine days before Waterloo on 18 June 1815. The battle does not produce the treaty. The treaty does not produce the battle. The congress had been sitting since 1814. The limit: diplomats can have expected further war. Expectation is not authorship. A motive does not move a date.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the pair of dates and the congress already at work in 1814, the year of the first abdication on 6 April. From 9 June to 18 June is 9 days. From 18 June to 22 June is 4 days. The second abdication follows the battle. The Final Act precedes it. What would count against the theorem? A clause dated after 18 June and still part of the act of 9 June, or a Final Act misdated to a day after Waterloo. We have neither. Allied intention, before 18 June, to keep Napoleon from ruling can be true and still leave the calendar as Final Act, then battle, then abdication. The next chapter may judge the settlement. It may not move 9 June to the evening of the battle.
:::

If the powers had already decided the map, Waterloo looks like mopping-up, and nine days look like a trick. Mopping-up is still not drafting. A war after a treaty does not make the treaty the child of the war. The return in 1815 threatened the settlement and explains why a battle could still be fought. It does not rewrite the signature. [[#thm-order]] is narrow on purpose. A reader can defeat it with a document. A wide claim, such as "Vienna created the nineteenth century", is what a nickname hides.

:::example Nine days, then four {#ex-june}
The Final Act is 9 June 1815. Waterloo is 18 June 1815. The second abdication is 22 June 1815. How many days after 9 June is 18 June, and how many days after 18 June is 22 June? What political sentence does each interval forbid?
:::solution
18 minus 9 is 9, so Waterloo is 9 days after the Final Act. 22 minus 18 is 4, so the second abdication is 4 days after Waterloo. The first interval forbids "Waterloo produced the Final Act". The second forbids treating the battle and the abdication as one signature. [[#thm-order]] uses the first. The second stops June 1815 collapsing into the single word Waterloo, as 1804 collapses into Napoleon if you forget [[#ex-leap]].
:::
:::

:::quiz
Which order matches the dates of June 1815?
- [ ] Waterloo, then the Final Act, then the first abdication of 1814
- [ ] The Final Act on 18 June, Waterloo on 9 June
- [x] The Final Act on 9 June, Waterloo on 18 June, the second abdication on 22 June
- [ ] The Civil Code, the Niemen and Waterloo, all in 1815
:::solution
[[#thm-order]] and [[#ex-june]] put the Final Act on 9 June 1815, Waterloo on 18 June and the second abdication on 22 June. The first abdication is 6 April 1814, not a June event. The Code is 21 March 1804. The Niemen is 24 June 1812. Only one of the four options keeps the June order and the right years.
:::

:::warning One achievement, and a number for an army
People repeat that the Civil Code and the victories are one achievement, and they often attach a size to the army that crossed the Niemen. The Code is 21 March 1804. Austerlitz is 2 December 1805. The Niemen is 24 June 1812. [[#thm-not-one]] refuses the singular. The size is not taught because it is not locked, and a number would not turn an invasion into a statute. Haiti on 1 January 1804 is the fact a glory story misplaces most easily. It is neither his code nor his crown.
:::

:::history
The Civil Code is promulgated on 21 March 1804, and Napoleon is crowned on 2 December. On 1 January of that year Haiti has already declared independence, with Jean-Jacques Dessalines the name attached to the new state. A code, a crown and a declaration share a year and not a kind.
:::

## Where this leads

[[revolutions/restoration-and-1848]] reads the Final Act as a project of settlement, not as an afterthought of Waterloo, and then follows 1830 and 1848. The abolition the consulate reversed is [[revolutions/republic-and-terror]]. The revolt the empire could not put back into slavery in Saint-Domingue begins in [[revolutions/from-1789]]. The trade, the Code Noir of 1685, the British Act of 1807 and the second French abolition of 1848 are [[states/atlantic]].

:::summary
- 18 Brumaire, 9 November 1799, is a coup that founds the consulate. It is not yet a crown, not yet the Civil Code, and not yet a Russian campaign.
- In 1802 colonial slavery is restored, Toussaint is captured, and a life consulate is taken. These are three acts. Toussaint dies in captivity in 1803 and does not declare independence.
- Haiti's declaration is 1 January 1804, eighty days before the Code of 21 March, and long before the coronation of 2 December. Dessalines, not Toussaint and not Napoleon, belongs with that declaration.
- The Code is a civil law of property, paternal power and a narrowed divorce. The coronation is a crown. Trafalgar on 21 October 1805 and Austerlitz on 2 December 1805 are a defeat and a victory forty-two days apart.
- The Niemen is crossed on 24 June 1812. The invasion and the retreat failed. No army size is stated, and the retreat's suffering is not narrated. The first abdication is 6 April 1814.
- The Final Act of Vienna is 9 June 1815, before Waterloo on 18 June, and the second abdication is 22 June. The congress had been sitting since 1814. The battle does not write the treaty.
- A project of government can use law, crown and war together. Using them together is not the same as one achievement.
:::

A reader says: "The powers meant to settle Europe against Napoleon, so Waterloo comes logically before the Final Act even if the calendar says otherwise. Logic beats a date." Answer using [[#thm-order]]. What would make the reader's "logically before" into a historical before?
:::solution
Intention does not move a signature. The congress sits from 1814, the year of the first abdication on 6 April. The Final Act is 9 June 1815. Waterloo is 18 June, and the second abdication is 22 June. The powers can have meant to stop Napoleon's return becoming a restored empire. That explains why 1815 was a crisis. It does not make 18 June earlier than 9 June. "Logically before" is a story about purposes. [[#thm-order]] is a statement about documents.

The reader's version would become historical before if the Final Act were dated after Waterloo, or if a clause written after 18 June were part of the act of 9 June. Then we would correct the chapter. Until then the sentence is the dull one: the settlement is signed, the battle is fought, the abdication follows. [[revolutions/restoration-and-1848]] may argue about what the settlement did. It may not pretend the battle drafted it.
