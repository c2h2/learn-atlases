What did 1848 settle, and what did it only attempt?

A year can shake many capitals and still not have one result. In 1848 revolutionary regimes rose across Europe. Most of them were defeated. Habsburg serfdom was abolished, and that abolition lasted. A Swiss settlement of the same year lasted. The Orleans monarchy did not return. French colonial slavery was abolished a second time, and that abolition held. A German parliament met and did not become a state. A pamphlet published in London in February did not become a government. Saying the year failed keeps the barricades and drops the laws. Saying it founded socialism as a state keeps the pamphlet and drops the governments that sat.

The path to that year runs through a settlement that was not a return, and through two decades in which politics moved without waiting for 1848. The Final Act of Vienna is 9 June 1815, before Waterloo on 18 June. Restoration is a project. 1830 changes a French dynasty and begins a Belgian state. The British Reform Act of 1832 widens a franchise that stays narrow. The Corn Laws are repealed in 1846 by a parliament, not by a barricade. None of these is "1848" under another name. They are why 1848 cannot be narrated as the first crack in a frozen continent, or as the unique form of change.

## A settlement signed first

:::definition Restoration {#def-restoration}
**Restoration** is the project, after Napoleon's first abdication, of putting kings back and drawing a map that would block a return of French imperial domination. It is not a return to the old regime of 5 May 1789, and it is not a treaty written on the evening of Waterloo. The name sounds like a reversal of time. The acts are choices: which dynasty, which frontiers, which debts to the past to honour and which to ignore.
:::

[[revolutions/napoleon]] established the order and stopped. The congress had been sitting since 1814. On 6 April 1814 Napoleon abdicated the first time. The Final Act is 9 June 1815. Waterloo is 18 June, nine days later, and the second abdication is 22 June. A reader who arrives from the battle has missed the signature. Fear of the emperor's return explains why June was dangerous. It does not let the battle draft the act. [[#def-restoration]] then asks what the project was trying to restore.

Not the morning of 5 May 1789. In 1814 France received a constitutional charter: Louis XVIII, a parliament, not the three estates voting by order. We do not quote the charter. The Civil Code remained the civil law. Legal estates did not return as the skeleton of the state. The project wanted throne and altar in public life, and émigrés compensated in honour and, where it could, in property. Wanting is not rewinding a generation of sales, laws and deaths. A restored Bourbon is a Bourbon after a revolution and an empire. He is not Louis XVI on the eve of the estates. Louis XVI has been dead since 21 January 1793.

The map is a great-power map, not a map of nations. A German confederation is a loose frame, not a nation-state. The Netherlands joins northern provinces to southern ones that will not stay. The Polish commonwealth, erased in 1772, 1793 and 1795, is not restored. A kingdom tied to the Russian emperor is not independence. Italy remains separate states, Austrian influence in the north and restored rulers elsewhere. The powers were settling territory and a ban on a single French empire, not justice to every language. [[states/east]] has the partitions. [[nations/unifications]] has the later states, which this settlement does not pre-write.

:::proposition Vienna is a project, not a postscript and not a rewind {#prop-vienna}
The Final Act of 9 June 1815 is a settlement chosen by great powers, signed before Waterloo, and aimed at preventing a return of Napoleonic domination without recreating the Estates-General of 1789. Restoration names that project. It does not name a successful undoing of every revolutionary change in civil law. The limit: a project can be coercive and still be a project rather than a fate. Later revolutions do not "disprove" the signature. They show that the signature did not freeze politics.
:::
:::proof
The order is the pair already fixed in [[revolutions/napoleon]]: 9 June, then 18 June. Installed: a Bourbon under the charter of 1814, a German confederation, a united Netherlands, redrawn Italian states, no full commonwealth of Poland-Lithuania. Not installed: the three estates, the repeal of the Civil Code, a nation-state for Germans, Italians or Poles. What would count against the proposition? A Final Act dated after Waterloo, or a constitution of 1814 that restored voting by order as on 5 May 1789 and repealed civil equality before the property law. We do not have those documents. The settlement was not mild. Frontiers were imposed, and after the second restoration the reprisals were violent and partisan, sometimes nicknamed a white terror. That nickname is not the Terror of September 1793 to 27 July 1794. No death toll and no procedure. A project and a repression can share a season.
:::

The counter-argument says the Code and a parliament are a surface, and that old elites came back, so "not a rewind" is a lawyers' comfort. Elites did return to land, church and office where they could. We did not say the restoration was socially identical with the republic. The form of authorisation was not the opening morning of 1789. A charter and a civil code are not the Estates-General. A noble buying influence inside those forms plays a new game with old names. [[#prop-vienna]] can absorb social restoration and still forbid the sentence that the old morning had returned. If the three orders and the venal parlement are not the constitution, the old regime is not back.

## 1830, and a narrow reform

The project did not hold still. In July 1830 Charles X, who had tried to govern past the charter, was overthrown. Louis-Philippe, of the Orleans branch, became king of the French. The title is a claim, king of a people rather than of a realm in the old style, not a social description. 1830 changed a dynasty and kept a monarchy. It did not found a republic and did not restore Charles X. The July monarchy fell in February 1848. That is a later fact, not a law known in 1830.

In the same year a revolution began in the southern provinces of the kingdom of the Netherlands. A Belgian state came out of it. This atlas does not lock a later treaty day for recognition, so we do not invent one. What lasted is a polity separate from the Dutch crown Vienna had set over those provinces. The map was revised in the lifetime of the settlement. That revision is not 1848, and it is not the failure of every clause of 1815. Frontiers can move while a German confederation remains.

Not every rising of 1830 founded a state. A Polish insurrection against Russian rule began in 1830 and was defeated. We do not lock the year of its end, so we do not number it. Poles do not receive a state from this season, a fact [[nations/unifications]] will still be facing. 1830 is already a plural: France changes dynasty, Belgium begins, Poland's rising fails. "The revolution of 1830" in the singular has chosen Paris.

:::example Three outcomes in one year {#ex-1830}
Using only 1830, state one result that changes a dynasty without ending monarchy, one result that founds a state, and one result that is a defeated rising. Why does "1830 succeeded" fail in the same way as "1848 failed"?
:::solution
The July Revolution replaces Charles X with Louis-Philippe and keeps a monarchy. The Belgian revolution begins a state out of provinces Vienna had joined to the Dutch crown. The Polish rising of 1830 is defeated and founds no state. "1830 succeeded" fails because Poland is a defeat and because the French result is a dynasty, not a success you can name without saying for whom. "1848 failed" fails for the mirror reason. A year-label with only a verb of success or failure has thrown away the plural. The widget prints "July and Belgium" and does not mention Poland. The figure compresses. The lesson should not copy it.
:::
:::

:::example Two British statutes {#ex-statutes}
The Reform Act is 1832. The Corn Laws are repealed in 1846. What is the difference of the years, and why is neither statute a revolution of the 1848 kind?
:::solution
1846 minus 1832 is 14. Both are acts of a parliament that does not fall. One widens a franchise that stays narrow and excludes women. The other repeals a grain protection, and this chapter states no duty. The contrast is the one the question of the chapter needs: change between Vienna and 1848 is not a synonym for a barricade. Fourteen years of statutes are not a script for February 1848.
:::
:::

Britain did not have that July. In 1832 the Reform Act redistributed seats and widened the franchise inside property. Adult men as a class were not enfranchised. Women were not. The act is a statute of a parliament that survived, the world of the Bill of Rights of 1689 and the Bank of England of 1694, not a Parisian change of dynasty. Later Reform Acts, in 1867 and 1884, still exclude women. They belong to [[nations/mass-politics]]. Naming them warns against the story that 1832 finished democracy. No percentage is locked, so none is stated. [[#ex-statutes]] puts this statute beside the repeal of 1846.

In 1846 parliament repealed the Corn Laws, the protective duties on grain. Robert Peel's government carried the repeal, under pressure from manufacturers and from people who wanted cheaper bread. The decade is also the decade of famine in Ireland. [[nations/industry]] compares 1841 and 1851 and will not state a death total. This chapter invents neither the total nor a start-year the atlas has not locked. The repeal is a statute, not a revolution. A parliament can reverse a major protection without a barricade, in the era when other capitals are about to build them. The repeal does not feed Ireland by itself. It shows that change between Vienna and 1848 is not a synonym for revolution.

The British Act of 1833 against slavery in most of the empire, with apprenticeship to 1838, is the same contrast and is taught as law in [[states/atlantic]]. It is not 1848 and not a civilisation's moral. A labour law of freedom, hedged by apprenticeship, is another instrument. We do not describe plantation labour and we do not count people.

:::quiz
What is true of the British Reform Act of 1832?
- [ ] It gives the vote to all adults and completes democracy
- [ ] It is a republic on the model of the French republic of 1792
- [x] It widens a franchise that remains narrow and does not include women
- [ ] It repeals the Corn Laws, which in fact happens in 1846
:::solution
The Act of 1832 redistributes some seats and widens the electorate inside property. It does not enfranchise women, and it does not enfranchise adult men as such. The Corn Laws fall in 1846, a different statute. The French republic of 21–22 September 1792 is a different country and a different kind of event. Narrowness is the fact the chronology locks, and it is the fact a "triumph of democracy" story cannot use.
:::
:::

## Many revolutions

In February 1848 a revolution in Paris opened the year. Louis-Philippe fell and a republic was proclaimed. Adult men were drawn in by a wide suffrage, and women were not. Workshops opened as a social expedient and then closed. In June the republic fought an insurrection of Parisian workers and suppressed it. We state no death toll. By the end of the year Louis-Napoleon Bonaparte had been elected president. The social republic of the spring did not govern the winter. The July monarchy did not return either. France in December is not France in January, and it is not the republic the June fighters wanted.

The same year abolished slavery again in the French colonies. The first abolition had been 4 February 1794. The restoration of colonial slavery had been 1802. The second abolition, in 1848, holds. It is a legal result of this revolutionary year that does not depend on the June insurrection winning, and it does not make the Second Republic into a permanent regime. [[states/atlantic]] places the three dates in the longer Atlantic sequence, beside the British Act of 1807 against the trade and the Act of 1833. Here the point is local to the question. Abolition outlasts the revolutionary government that passed it. That is one way a year can settle something while its regimes are defeated.

:::theorem 1848 did not fail everywhere {#thm-not-everywhere}
Most revolutionary regimes of 1848 were defeated. The year did not fail everywhere. Serfdom in the Habsburg empire was abolished in 1848 and the abolition lasted. The Swiss settlement of 1848 lasted. Other results, including the end of the Orleans monarchy and the second French abolition of colonial slavery, also outlasted the barricades. The limit: lasting laws are not the same as victorious revolutionary governments. Both sentences are true, and the second does not cancel the first.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the split between regimes and laws. Revolutions in Vienna, Hungary, Bohemia and the Italian provinces shook the Habsburg monarchy. The Hungarian revolutionary government was defeated, with Russian forces used against it. We do not lock a separate year for that campaign and we give no casualty figure. The project of an independent Hungary in the form the revolutionaries wanted did not survive. Serfdom's abolition did. Legal unfreedom ended in that empire in 1848 and stayed ended after the generals had won. A Swiss federal settlement of 1848 lasted. Italian revolutions and a war against Austria did not produce a united Italy, a later creation taught in [[nations/unifications]]. Frankfurt met and did not become a state. Paris saw its February republic beat its June insurrection. The list of defeated regimes is long. The list of lasting changes is not empty. "Failed everywhere" needs an empty list.

What would count against the theorem? A return of Habsburg serfdom, a rapid undoing of the Swiss settlement, the return of Louis-Philippe, and the re-enslavement of people freed in the French colonies in 1848. One reversal would narrow the theorem. All of them would defeat it. The true statement that most revolutionary governments lost is already inside the theorem. The mistake is to let it impersonate the year.
:::

The counter-argument says the lasting results were granted by the governments that won, so they are not revolutionary. A law can be born under revolutionary pressure and confirmed by the authority that then crushes the revolutionaries. Authorship and survival are different questions. Habsburg serfdom ends in the empire that defeats the Hungarian government. Historians argue which decree deserves the emphasis. You may not conclude that a fallen barricade left peasants' legal condition unchanged. The Swiss settlement is the year's own constitutional result, and it lasts. A counter-argument that ignores Switzerland and the peasants has chosen its examples.

Frankfurt is the clearest attempt that did not settle. A German parliament debated a constitution, a smaller Germany without Austria or a larger one with the Habsburg lands, and did not become a state. We do not lock the day a crown was refused, so we do not date it. The absence of a state is enough. The empire proclaimed in 1871 is another project, taught in [[nations/unifications]], not this parliament delayed. Italians who lost in 1848 were not thereby scheduled to win in 1861. Later success is a different chapter.

Britain's 1848 is a mobilisation that does not become a regime. Chartists petitioned for a wider democracy. The government stayed. We give no size of crowd. An Irish rising was defeated in the same year, inside the famine decade, and again no death toll is stated. A year that includes a British non-revolution and an Irish defeat is not one mechanism rolling west to east. The repeal of 1846 did not cause Paris. It left the kinds distinct.

:::example A sentence that fails in one direction {#ex-fail}
The following sentence is invented. It is not a chronicle and not a law. "1848 failed everywhere, so nothing of that year remained by the next season." Which locked results does the sentence erase, and which true clause could you add without making the year into a success story?
:::solution
The sentence erases the abolition of Habsburg serfdom, the Swiss settlement of 1848, the second French abolition of colonial slavery, and the fact that Louis-Philippe did not return. [[#thm-not-everywhere]] is the repair. A true clause you can add is that most revolutionary regimes were defeated, including the Hungarian government, the Italian projects, the June insurrection and Frankfurt as a would-be state. That clause keeps the defeats and does not restore "everywhere". A universal verb met a year that was a set. The repair is a list.
:::
:::

## A document is not a government

:::corollary The Manifesto is not a state {#cor-manifesto}
The Communist Manifesto, written by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels and published in London in February 1848, is a document of that month. It is not a government of Paris, Vienna, Frankfurt or anywhere else, and 1848 is not the birth of socialism as a state. We do not quote the book, and we do not narrate the authors' lives.
:::
:::proof
A pamphlet in London can argue for a revolution. Argument is not office. Paris overthrew a king in February. Its later governors were not the authors and not the programme in power. Frankfurt was a liberal and national parliament, not this text. June did not install the pamphlet as a constitution. No state of 1848 is a socialist state founded by the Manifesto. The corollary would fail if a ministry had adopted the pamphlet as its constitution and governed by it. None did, in the record this course uses. The book still matters as a dated document. Mattering is not ruling. Socialism as a state is a later claim.
:::

A paraphrase fine enough to be a quotation is a quotation, so we do not reconstruct the argument. Two authors published a programme in London in the month Paris revolted, and the programme was not the government that followed. Socialist talk is older than the pamphlet. We lock no birthday for the words. The myth that 1848 begins socialism slides from this book to a state. [[#cor-manifesto]] stops the slide. This chapter will not report a sentence it was not given, or a life. Print settles an argument. It does not settle a constitution.

:::example Printed in the same month {#ex-february}
The Paris revolution that opens 1848 is in February. The Communist Manifesto is published in London in February 1848. What may you infer from the shared month, and what does [[#cor-manifesto]] forbid you to infer?
:::solution
You may infer that a revolutionary outbreak and a socialist programme are contemporaneous. Contemporaneity is not causation and not identity. You may not infer that the Manifesto caused Paris, that Paris enacted it, or that its authors governed. You may not quote it or fill the gap with a biography. The shared month is the same temptation as the day between Valmy and the republic in September 1792: a short gap invites the verb "created". The verb is still wrong. A careful sentence says that in February 1848 a regime fell in Paris and a pamphlet appeared in London. [[#thm-not-everywhere]] says which results lasted. The pamphlet is an attempt in argument, not one of those settlements.
:::
:::

:::quiz
Which statement fits 1848?
- [ ] The year failed everywhere, including the legal condition of peasants in the Habsburg empire
- [ ] The Communist Manifesto became the constitution of France and of the German parliament
- [x] Most revolutionary regimes were defeated, and some results, including the end of Habsburg serfdom and the Swiss settlement, lasted
- [ ] Vienna's Final Act was signed after Waterloo in order to answer the revolutions of 1848
:::solution
[[#thm-not-everywhere]] keeps the defeats and the lasting results in one claim. The Manifesto is a London publication of February, not a constitution, which is [[#cor-manifesto]]. The Final Act is 9 June 1815, before Waterloo and decades before 1848. The wrong options each collapse a plural year, a document, or a chronology.
:::

Usefulness is not evidence about this year.

:::history
In February 1848 a revolution in Paris opened a year of revolutions across many cities. The Communist Manifesto appeared in London the same month, the work of Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels, and it was a document, not a government. Some results outlasted the defeat of the barricades, among them the end of serfdom in the Habsburg empire.
:::

## Where this leads

[[nations/unifications]] takes the German and Italian states that 1848 attempted and did not found, and the Poles and others who still do not receive one. [[nations/mass-politics]] takes the later British reform acts and the franchise 1832 left narrow. [[nations/industry]] takes the census figures, including Ireland in 1841 and 1851, and refuses a famine death total this chapter has also refused. The order of Vienna and Waterloo, assumed here, is argued in [[revolutions/napoleon]]. The old regime that restoration did not restore is [[revolutions/old-regime]].

:::summary
- Restoration is a project of 1814 and 1815, not a rewind to 5 May 1789 and not a text written after Waterloo. The Final Act is 9 June 1815, before the battle of 18 June.
- The settlement builds a great-power map: a chartered Bourbon monarchy, a German confederation, a kingdom of the Netherlands, no restored Polish commonwealth. The Civil Code is not repealed as the price of the king's return.
- 1830 changes the French dynasty and begins a Belgian state. A Polish rising of 1830 is defeated. The year is already plural.
- The Reform Act of 1832 widens a narrow franchise and does not include women. The Corn Laws are repealed in 1846 by statute. Neither act is a revolution of the 1848 kind.
- 1848 is many revolutions. Most revolutionary regimes are defeated. The year does not fail everywhere: Habsburg serfdom ends, the Swiss settlement lasts, the Orleans king does not return, and French colonial slavery is abolished again.
- Frankfurt meets and does not found a German state. Italian unity is not achieved this year. Hungary's revolutionary government is defeated.
- The Communist Manifesto is published in London in February 1848. It is not quoted here, it is not a life of its authors, and it is not the birth of socialism as a state.
:::

[[#prop-vienna]] allows elites to come back and still requires the institutional test. The sentence fails it. It also muddles documents: the charter is French and of 1814, the Final Act is European and of 9 June 1815, and Waterloo is later still.

:::exercise What would defeat the theorem? {level=3}
:::hint
[[#thm-not-everywhere]] already says which reversals would defeat it and which single reversal would only narrow it. Separate a defeated government from a reversed law. The Polish case of 1830 is a useful comparison, not a substitute for 1848.
:::
Suppose a historian showed that Habsburg serfdom was legally restored within a short time, while the Swiss settlement and the second French abolition stood. Would you abandon [[#thm-not-everywhere]] or narrow it? What finding would make you abandon it? Use the Polish rising of 1830 only as an analogy for a year that is already plural.
:::solution
You would narrow the theorem, not abandon it. One reversed result leaves the Swiss settlement and the second abolition, so "everywhere" is still false. The proof of [[#thm-not-everywhere]] already said that one reversal narrows and the full set defeats. You would abandon it if serfdom returned, the Swiss settlement was undone, Louis-Philippe returned and colonial slavery was restored after 1848, or if most regimes had not in fact been defeated. An empty lasting list would make "failed everywhere" fair. That is not the record.

The Polish rising of 1830 is an analogy, not evidence for 1848. One rising failed while a dynasty changed and a Belgian state began. [[#ex-1830]] already banned a single verb. The theorem rejects that verb for 1848 on the contents of 1848, not on the comparison alone.
:::
