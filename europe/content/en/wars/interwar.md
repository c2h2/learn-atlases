Why is 1918–1939 not a corridor to one man? Because the years contain more than one regime, more than one treaty, and more than one crisis, and because the man in the usual story is not in office at the start of them. The armistice is 11 November 1918. Hitler is appointed chancellor on 30 January 1933. The German invasion of Poland is 1 September 1939. Between those dates lie the Paris treaties, a failed putsch, a march on Rome, the formation of the Soviet Union, a financial crash that is not the same day as every European slump, a Spanish civil war, and authoritarian governments that are not German. A corridor story draws a straight line from the Treaty of Versailles to one appointment and then to one war, and treats everything else as scenery. The dates will not sit on that line unless we rub them out.

This chapter refuses the rubbing-out. It also refuses a reparations sum, a vote total, and a death total. Historians disagree about how heavy Versailles was. The disagreement is part of the prose, not a footnote. What the chronology locks is the day of the signature, the names of the other treaties, and the later political dates. A grievance felt in Germany can be real. A grievance is not a timetable that appoints a chancellor. The previous chapters have already refused single causes for 1914 and destiny for 1917. The same discipline applies here, or the interwar years collapse into a preface. They were not a preface. They were the years in which several different kinds of state were made, and in which one German sequence, short and violent, still has to be told in its own weeks.

## Several treaties, not one signature

On 28 June 1919 the Treaty of Versailles is signed in the Hall of Mirrors. The German Empire had been proclaimed in that hall on 18 January 1871. The year numerals differ by 48, because 1919 minus 1871 is 48. Same room, two acts. The second is not the revenge mechanically entailed by the first, and the first is not a draft of the second. Versailles is the treaty with Germany. It imposed territorial losses, military restrictions and reparations, and it included a clause assigning responsibility for the war. We do not state a sum of money, a size of army, or a vote on the treaty. Those integers are not locked. Inventing them would make a false precision look like an explanation.

Versailles is one of the Paris settlements, not the whole peace. In 1919 the treaties of Saint-Germain and Neuilly are also made: Saint-Germain with Austria, Neuilly with Bulgaria. On 4 June 1920 the Treaty of Trianon is signed with Hungary. In 1920 the Treaty of Sèvres is imposed on the Ottoman empire, and it is later revised. The chronology does not lock the year of that revision, so we do not invent one. Anyone who says "Versailles" and means the entire redrawing of Europe has already lost Trianon, Saint-Germain, Neuilly and Sèvres. The Habsburg empire does not end in the German treaty alone. Hungary's losses are Trianon's subject. Confusing the documents is how one German grievance is made to stand for every new border.

The new map included states that had not been sovereign states in 1914. Poland is the clearest case with a longer lock: the partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795 had erased the commonwealth as a state. A Polish state exists again in the settlement. We do not assign a proclamation day, because the chronology does not lock one. Czechoslovakia, a southern Slav kingdom, the Baltic states, and separate Austrian and Hungarian republics belong to the same caution. Name the political result. Do not decorate it with an unlocked anniversary. The point of the treaties, for this chapter, is that they are plural, dated, and earlier than the dictatorships by enough years to block a corridor.

:::definition The Paris settlements {#def-paris}
The **Paris settlements** are the treaties that ended the war of 1914–1918 with the defeated states: Versailles with Germany on 28 June 1919, Saint-Germain with Austria and Neuilly with Bulgaria in 1919, Trianon with Hungary on 4 June 1920, and Sèvres with the Ottoman empire in 1920, later revised on a date this atlas does not lock. Versailles is one of them. It is not their name.
:::

:::definition A corridor story {#def-corridor}
A **corridor story** of these years is an account in which 1918–1939 is only the path from Versailles to Hitler, and every other regime is either a preview of him or an interruption. The story is a label in the sense of [[sources/argument]]. It hides choices, failures and states that do not fit.
:::

:::theorem These years are not that corridor {#thm-not-corridor}
The politics of 1918–1939 cannot be reduced to a path from the Treaty of Versailles to one German appointment. Italy's March on Rome, the formation of the USSR, the failure of the Munich putsch, the Spanish Civil War, and authoritarian regimes elsewhere are dated or named in their own right. A harsh reading of Versailles, about which historians disagree, would not by itself restore the corridor.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the distribution of dates. The March on Rome is 28 October 1922, before Hitler's appointment and before the Wall Street crash. The USSR is formed on 30 December 1922, a different kind of one-party state, not a German copy. The Munich putsch of 8–9 November 1923 fails, so the man later appointed is, in 1923, a man whose bid for power has been stopped. The Spanish Civil War runs from 17–18 July 1936 to 1 April 1939 and is not a German domestic event, though other states intervene. Authoritarian rule in Portugal and in several states of eastern and southern Europe is a fact of the period. We give no coup dates, because inventing a list of them would fake a precision the chronology withholds. Britain and France, bound into the postwar order, do not become that German regime. What would count in favour of the corridor is a demonstration that each of these alternatives was illusory, or that the appointment of 30 January 1933 was fixed by the text of 1919 with no further decision. The alternatives are not illusory, and an appointment is a decision. What would not rescue the corridor is the truth that Versailles was widely resented in Germany. Resentment can be real, and historians can still disagree about the treaty's weight, without the resentment being a sufficient cause. A cause has to be the sort of thing that could have been otherwise. The failed putsch is the otherwise.
:::

:::theorem The appointment is a sequence of weeks, not a vote total {#thm-sequence}
Hitler is appointed chancellor on 30 January 1933. The Reichstag fire is 27 February 1933. The Enabling Act is 23 March 1933. This is a sequence of political acts. It is not a vote total, which this chapter will not invent, and it is not an event of 1919.
:::
:::proof
Count the gaps inside 1933, which is not a leap year. 30 January is day 30. 27 February is day 58, because January has 31 days and 31 + 27 = 58. The fire is 58 − 30 = 28 days after the appointment. 23 March is day 82, because January and February have 31 + 28 = 59 days and 59 + 23 = 82. The Enabling Act is 82 − 58 = 24 days after the fire, and 82 − 30 = 52 days after the appointment. The chronology locks the three dates and no parliamentary tally. What would count against the claim is a locked vote total that showed the chancellorship to have been conferred by a stated ballot on 30 January, or a source that placed the Enabling Act in 1919. There is no such total here, and the Act is 23 March 1933. What would not count against it is the later history of the regime. The sequence explains how a chancellorship became a government that could legislate past ordinary parliamentary limits. It does not explain the Spanish war, the Soviet state, or the Italian monarchy's role on 28 October 1922. Those remain outside the fifty-two days.
:::

:::quiz
Which list is a list of different treaties, not five names for Versailles?
- [ ] Versailles, the Hall of Mirrors, the war-guilt clause, reparations and the Polish border.
- [x] Versailles with Germany, Saint-Germain with Austria, Neuilly with Bulgaria, Trianon with Hungary, and Sèvres with the Ottoman empire.
- [ ] The March on Rome, the USSR, the putsch, the Enabling Act and the pact.
- [ ] Verdun, the Somme, Brest-Litovsk, the armistice and October.
:::solution
The second list is [[#def-paris]]. The first list is a set of topics inside or beside the German treaty, not the other Paris treaties. The third list is later politics, not the peace documents. The fourth list belongs to the war and the revolutions. Sèvres is later revised, and the year of the revision is not locked, which is a limit on the fifth name, not a reason to fold it into Versailles.
:::

## Dates that refuse the straight line

A corridor is a picture of time. The way to damage it is to put the dates back, including the ones that come too early and the ones that fail.

:::example Year labels that will not line up {#ex-year-gaps}
Using year numerals only, compute 1923 minus 1919, 1933 minus 1923, and 1939 minus 1933. Say what each gap separates.
:::solution
1923 − 1919 = 4. Four year-labels separate the Treaty of Versailles from the failed Munich putsch. 1933 − 1923 = 10. Ten year-labels separate that failure from the appointment of 30 January 1933. 1939 − 1933 = 6. Six year-labels separate the appointment from the Molotov–Ribbentrop pact and from the invasion of Poland, both in 1939. None of these gaps is a day-count, and none of them is a vote. Together they are incompatible with a story in which 1919 already was 1933. The putsch has time to fail. The appointment has time not to happen. It happens anyway, by the sequence in [[#thm-sequence]], which is a choice inside 1933, not a maturity of the treaty.
:::
:::

:::example Fifty-two days {#ex-fifty-two}
Repeat the count from 30 January 1933 to 23 March 1933 without a vote total. How many days after the appointment is the Enabling Act, and how do the Reichstag fire's 28 days and the following 24 days fit?
:::solution
As in the argument for [[#thm-sequence]]: the fire falls 28 days after 30 January, and the Enabling Act falls 24 days after 27 February. 28 + 24 = 52. The Enabling Act is 52 days after the appointment. Those fifty-two days include a fire and a law. They do not include a ballot figure, because none is locked. They also do not reach back to 28 June 1919. A reader who calls the Enabling Act "the result of Versailles" has skipped both the failed putsch and these weeks. The weeks are short. Their shortness is the point. The regime's legal break is a sequence you can count, not a fog covering fourteen years. Fourteen is 1933 minus 1919, another year-label gap, and it is not the same object as 52.
:::
:::

:::example Nine days, then a different war in Spain {#ex-nine-and-spain}
The Molotov–Ribbentrop pact is 23 August 1939. Germany invades Poland on 1 September 1939. How many days lie between those dates? Separately, the Spanish Civil War ends on 1 April 1939. How many days after that ending does 1 September fall? Keep the two spans distinct.
:::solution
From 23 August to 1 September is nine days. The invasion follows the pact by 9 days. That proximity is real, and it belongs to the immediate prelude taught at the end of this chapter and the start of the next. It is not a summary of 1919. From 1 April to 1 September: April has 30 days, so 29 days remain after 1 April, then May 31, June 30, July 31, August 31, and 1 September. 29 + 31 + 30 + 31 + 31 + 1 = 153. So 1 September is 153 days after the Spanish war ends. The Spanish war is over before the invasion of Poland. It is not the same war. Treating 1936–1939 in Spain as a German corridor with local colour fails this count.
:::
:::

:::example An invented corridor sentence {#ex-invented-corridor}
Treat this sentence as invented. "In 1919 the German people voted Hitler into power under the Treaty of Versailles, so the putsch of 1923 could not have failed and the appointment of 1933 is only a formality." List the errors.
:::solution
The sentence is invented. It puts a vote in 1919 that the chronology does not record, and this chapter is forbidden to invent vote totals in any year. It makes the treaty and the appointment the same event. It denies the failed putsch of 8–9 November 1923, which is locked precisely as a failure. And it calls 30 January 1933 a formality, against [[#thm-sequence]], which dates a fire and an Enabling Act in the following weeks. A rewritten sentence that survives is dull on purpose: Versailles is signed on 28 June 1919; a putsch fails in November 1923; Hitler is appointed on 30 January 1933, 52 days before the Enabling Act of 23 March. Dullness is the check. The invented sentence is exciting because it deletes the gaps in [[#ex-year-gaps]].
:::
:::

The Wall Street crash is dated twice, on purpose. 24 October 1929 is Black Thursday. 29 October 1929 is Black Tuesday. European slumps are connected to that crisis and are not the same event on the same day. A British labour market, a German banking crisis and a French pattern of trade do not change on the stroke of one New York afternoon. We say they are connected. We do not give an unemployment percentage, because none is locked, and a borrowed percentage would pretend that one number described several countries. The crash is part of the argument against the corridor for a further reason. It comes after the March on Rome and after the formation of the USSR. Those regimes were not born from this panic. The German appointment comes after the panic, in 1933, which is a reason to take the economic crisis seriously and not a reason to call it the treaty under another name.

Italy is the cleanest early counter-example. On 28 October 1922 the March on Rome brings Mussolini's fascists to the edge of power, and the king asks him to form a government. Historians argue about how much was a threatened insurrection and how much was a negotiated transfer. We report that argument rather than inventing the king's words. Either reading blocks the corridor. A fascist government exists in Italy years before 30 January 1933. It is not a German event, and it is not evidence that Versailles caused it. Italy's relationship to the peace was not Germany's.

The USSR, formed on 30 December 1922, is the other early state that a German biography cannot swallow. It is a one-party dictatorship with a different ideology, built after the revolutions of 1917 and the exit at Brest-Litovsk. This chapter does not date later internal campaigns of collectivisation or terror, because those dates are not locked, and it states no death total. The formation date is enough for the claim in [[#thm-not-corridor]]. A map of Europe that contains both Rome in October 1922 and the Soviet Union in December 1922 is already not a corridor to one man. Adding Portugal, where an authoritarian regime governs in these decades, and adding the authoritarian governments of several eastern and southern European states, makes the same point without a fake chronology of coups. The absence of those coup dates is a rule of evidence. It is not a claim that the regimes were mild or that they did not exist.

:::quiz
What does the failure of 8–9 November 1923 do to a corridor story?
- [ ] It shows that Hitler was already chancellor, so the later appointment is a repetition.
- [ ] It shows that Versailles had been revoked.
- [x] It shows a bid for power that failed, years before 30 January 1933, so 1919 did not already place him in office.
- [ ] It is the Spanish Civil War under another name.
:::solution
The putsch fails. Failure is the fact the chronology bothers to lock. The appointment is 30 January 1933, and the year-label gap from 1923 to 1933 is 10, as in [[#ex-year-gaps]]. Versailles is not revoked in 1923. The Spanish war begins in 1936. A story in which the treaty made the chancellorship unavoidable has to erase a dated failure or pretend the failure did not matter. The failure matters because it is evidence that politics could go another way. That it later went this way is a further sequence, not a proof that the other way had been impossible.
:::
:::

Nuremberg and the November pogrom belong in this chapter because they are before the war of 1939 and after the appointment, and because the next chapters must not be told as if persecution began at Wannsee. On 15 September 1935 the Nuremberg Laws define who counts as Jewish for the purpose of the law and bar marriage and sexual relations between Jews and people the law counts as of German blood. That is a paraphrase of the legal exclusion, not a quotation and not the murder taught in [[wars/genocide]]. On 9–10 November 1938 the November pogrom, also called Kristallnacht in the chronology, attacks Jewish life in Germany: synagogues, shops, arrests. We give no death total. The name that speaks of broken glass centres property. "November pogrom" keeps the violence against people in the sentence. Neither the laws nor the pogrom is a vote total, and neither is Versailles.

The Spanish war needs one more distinction. It begins on 17–18 July 1936 and ends on 1 April 1939 with the victory of Franco's side. Germany, Italy and the Soviet Union intervene. We state no troop count. Intervention shows that the war was internationalised. It does not make Spain a province of the German story, and it does not make every Spaniard a combatant of one moral type. From the end of that war to the invasion of Poland is 153 days. Proximity is not identity.

Move the year. With the window at one, you see neighbours, not a fused line. In 1922 the dot has to carry both the March on Rome and the formation of the USSR, two regimes that the corridor would rather file as preliminaries. In 1929 the dot is an American market crash, which the lesson has refused to treat as the unique European day of slump. In 1933 the dot is an appointment, not a treaty. In 1938 it is Munich, not the whole of German expansion and not the November pogrom, which shares the year and is not on the figure. In 1939 it is the pact, nine days before the invasion, and the figure stops. Six year-labels from 1933 to 1939 are what the caption asks you to see. Six is 1939 minus 1933. If the line looks like a corridor, the lesson has failed. The dots are there so that you can refuse the line.

The closing sequence opens the next chapter and does not replace this one. In March 1938 the Anschluss incorporates Austria into Germany. On 30 September 1938 the Munich Agreement concedes the German demand on the Sudeten region of Czechoslovakia. We do not quote the claims then made that the agreement had secured peace. From 30 September 1938 to 1 September 1939 is 336 days. A non-leap year has 365 days, and 365 minus 29 is 336, because 1 September falls 29 days before the anniversary of 30 September. The agreement is less than a year away from the invasion and is not the invasion. On 23 August 1939 the Molotov–Ribbentrop pact binds Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union in a public non-aggression agreement. Historians also read a secret protocol, recovered later, as arranging spheres in eastern Europe. We do not invent the protocol's clauses. Nine days later Germany invades Poland. The pact is part of the immediate cause of that invasion's political setting. It is not a summary of Versailles, and it does not make the Soviet Union into a German satellite. In June 1941 Germany will invade the Soviet Union. That date is the next chapter's. Mentioning it here prevents a reader from ending 1939 as if the pact were permanent. It was a choice, dated, and later broken by another choice.

:::warning The myth that the treaty made the chancellor unavoidable
The myth says that Versailles made Hitler inevitable. It fails the dates even before it fails as a theory of cause. The treaty is 28 June 1919. The putsch fails on 8–9 November 1923. The appointment is 30 January 1933, then a fire, then the Enabling Act, inside 52 days, with no vote total stated in this course. Italy has a fascist government from the March on Rome in 1922. The USSR exists as a union from 30 December 1922. Spain's war runs from 1936 to 1 April 1939. Other authoritarian regimes exist without our inventing their coup days. Historians disagree about how heavy Versailles was, and we lock no reparations sum that would end that quarrel. A heavy grievance is not a sufficient cause. What would count for the myth is a chain with no alternatives and no failed putsch. The alternatives and the failure are the record. Do not let the myth back in by calling 1933 a formality.
:::

:::history
The Treaty of Versailles is signed on 28 June 1919 in the Hall of Mirrors, where the German Empire had been proclaimed on 18 January 1871. It is one of the Paris settlements, not the whole peace. Hitler is appointed chancellor on 30 January 1933. The Reichstag fire is 27 February 1933, and the Enabling Act follows on 23 March.
:::

What would count against [[#thm-not-corridor]] is a source showing that the March on Rome, the Soviet formation, the Spanish war and the other authoritarian regimes were German instruments created in 1919 for one career. They were not. Blame of the treaty, in a speech, is evidence of politics, not the calendar. The worked case remains [[#ex-invented-corridor]]. Keep 28 June 1919, the failed putsch of November 1923, and the appointment of 30 January 1933 apart, and keep Rome and the Soviet Union off that line.

## Where this leads

The pact of 23 August 1939 and the invasion of 1 September are the hinge into [[wars/second-world-war]]. The revolutions that made the state which signed that pact, and the calendars of 1917, are [[wars/revolutions-1917]]. The murder of the Jews, of which the Nuremberg Laws and the November pogrom are not yet the whole, is [[wars/genocide]]. How a label such as "corridor" differs from a cause is [[sources/argument]].

:::summary
- The Paris settlements are plural: Versailles on 28 June 1919, Saint-Germain and Neuilly in 1919, Trianon on 4 June 1920, Sèvres in 1920 and later revised on an unlocked date. One hall also held the proclamation of 18 January 1871.
- Historians disagree about how heavy Versailles was. No reparations sum and no vote total are locked. A grievance is not a sufficient cause.
- The March on Rome is 28 October 1922. The USSR is formed on 30 December 1922. The Munich putsch of 8–9 November 1923 fails. These dates are not German scenery.
- Wall Street's crash is 24 and 29 October 1929. European slumps are connected to it and are not that day, and they have no percentage here.
- Hitler is appointed on 30 January 1933. The Reichstag fire is 28 days later. The Enabling Act is 24 days after the fire, 52 days after the appointment.
- The Nuremberg Laws are 15 September 1935. The November pogrom is 9–10 November 1938. Spain's war runs from 17–18 July 1936 to 1 April 1939. Other authoritarian regimes existed. We do not invent their coup dates.
- Anschluss is March 1938. Munich is 30 September 1938, 336 days before 1 September 1939. The pact is 23 August 1939, nine days before the invasion. None of this makes 1919 into 1939.
:::
