What is the evidence for the murder of the Jews, and what must a lesson not do with it? The evidence is documentary, testimonial and material, and it supports a crime carried out by the German regime and its collaborators during the war: the murder of the Jews of Europe. The figure used by historians, and by the memorial institutions Yad Vashem and the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, is about six million Jews murdered. That figure is an estimate. It is not a list the perpetrators completed, and it is not a licence to invent a second total for anyone else. What a lesson must not do is as much a part of the historical discipline as the dates. It must not describe a killing procedure, a weapon, or the way a camp worked. It must not invent a quotation from a victim or a perpetrator. It must not argue about a present-day state. It must not move the beginning of the murder to a meeting that coordinated a crime already under way.

The name Holocaust is in common use for this murder, and Shoah is another name. Neither name is a substitute for the dates. Legal exclusion begins earlier than the mass killing. On 15 September 1935 the Nuremberg Laws define Jews for the purpose of the law and bar marriage and sexual relations between Jews and people the law counts as of German blood. That is persecution in statutes. It is not yet the estimate of about six million, and it is not a small thing beside the later crime. On 9–10 November 1938 the November pogrom attacks Jewish life in Germany. We give no death total for it. The pogrom is not the systematic murder that follows the invasion of the Soviet Union, and a reader who starts the story at either date still has not started it at the right place if the question is the mass murder. The right place, for the killing, is the summer of 1941, in the occupied Soviet Union, after the invasion of 22 June 1941. A meeting on 20 January 1942 does not move that summer.

Because the figure is an estimate, some people treat the crime as doubtful. Doubt would follow only if the documents, testimonies and sites did not converge. They converge on murder at the scale those institutions publish as about six million Jews. Disagreement about the exact integer inside the estimate is not a denial. The absence of a single completed perpetrator list is why the word is "about", not a reason to discard the convergence.

## Before Wannsee, and at Wannsee

Mass shooting of Jewish civilians in the occupied Soviet Union begins in the summer of 1941. The chronology uses those words. This lesson uses them and stops. It does not describe weapons, units, or the course of any particular action. Summer 1941 is after 22 June and before January 1942. That order is enough to show that the killing is already a fact of the war in the east when officials later sit down to coordinate it. Chełmno begins killing in December 1941. Chełmno is a place of murder. We name it as a place. We do not say how the killing there was done. December 1941 is still before 20 January 1942.

On 20 January 1942 officials meet at Wannsee, with Reinhard Heydrich among them, to coordinate deportation and murder. The record is an administrative document. Coordination is not a beginning. A reader who has only the date of the meeting has a contents word, "Wannsee", of the sort the widget later will show as one dot among earlier dots. The earlier dots are the Nuremberg Laws, the November pogrom, and the start of mass shooting. The meeting belongs in the history because it shows a state organising a crime across offices, not because it is the crime's birth. Deportation is part of what was coordinated. Naming deportation does not require, and will not receive, an account of a journey or of a place's machinery.

Other killing centres are also places of murder. Auschwitz is one of them. The Red Army liberates Auschwitz on 27 January 1945. Liberation is a date on which a place of murder passes out of the hands of the regime. It is not a description of the place, and this lesson does not give one. Historians also name further killing centres, among them Bełżec, Sobibór and Treblinka. They are named here as places. No year is attached to them in the chronology, so none is invented, and nothing is said about how any centre worked. A place-name is evidence that murder had a geography. A procedure would be a different kind of sentence, and it is refused.

:::definition The estimate {#def-estimate}
The **estimate** in this chapter is the figure of about six million Jews murdered, as used by historians and by Yad Vashem and the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. It is not a ledger the perpetrators closed, not a count of every victim of the war, and not a number to which a second group's total may be added from imagination.
:::

:::definition Genocide, the word and the Convention {#def-genocide}
**Genocide** is the word Raphael Lemkin uses in *Axis Rule in Occupied Europe*, published in 1944. On 9 December 1948 the United Nations General Assembly adopts the Genocide Convention. The word and the Convention are a coinage and a legal instrument. They are not the start of the murder of the Jews, which is already under way in 1941, and they are not identical with every later use of the word.
:::

:::theorem Wannsee is not the start {#thm-wannsee}
The Wannsee conference of 20 January 1942 coordinates deportation and murder that have already begun. Mass shooting in the occupied Soviet Union begins in the summer of 1941. Chełmno begins killing in December 1941. The conference is not the beginning of the murder of the Jews.
:::
:::proof
The evidence is the order locked in the chronology. The invasion of the Soviet Union is 22 June 1941. Mass shooting begins in the summer that follows, which is before January 1942. Chełmno begins in December 1941, which is also before 20 January 1942. The meeting's purpose, as the chronology states it, is to coordinate a murder already under way. What would count against the claim is evidence that mass shooting and the killing at Chełmno began on or after 20 January 1942. That evidence is not what the chronology records. What would not rescue Wannsee as a beginning is the importance of the meeting. A coordinating meeting can be historically serious and still be late. The Nuremberg Laws of 15 September 1935 and the November pogrom of 9–10 November 1938 are earlier again, and they are not the mass murder either. [[#thm-wannsee]] does not collapse persecution, pogrom and systematic murder into one dot. It refuses the opposite collapse, in which nothing counts until the officials meet.
:::

:::theorem The evidence can be stated without a procedure {#thm-evidence}
The murder of the Jews is supported by documentary, testimonial and material evidence, and by the estimate in [[#def-estimate]]. A lesson can teach that claim without describing a killing procedure, a weapon, or the working of a camp. Refusing those descriptions does not weaken the claim. Inventing them would break the rules of this course without adding a locked fact.
:::
:::proof
Documents — orders, lists, administrative papers, the Wannsee record — show what offices wrote and, read together, what the policy was. They do not replace a person's experience, and this chapter invents no one's sentences. Testimony can establish a place, a time and a fate. One voice is not the continental total, and it is not weaker than paper by nature. Sites, objects and remains show that places were used for murder. They are not an invitation to reconstruct a method. The estimate gathers these records, including demographic comparison, into the published figure. What would count against the claim is the three kinds of evidence together showing the deported people alive in the places the papers named. That is not the record. Refusing a procedure does not count against it. Friedländer's history treats victims' voices as part of the evidence. We cite that aim and counterfeit no voice.
:::

Which statement matches the chronology's order?

- [ ] The Nuremberg Laws of 1935 are already the estimate of about six million.
- [x] Mass shooting begins in the summer of 1941 and Chełmno begins in December 1941. Wannsee, on 20 January 1942, coordinates a murder already under way.

:::solution
The third statement is [[#thm-wannsee]]. The laws of 15 September 1935 are legal exclusion, not the wartime estimate. The Convention of 9 December 1948 is a legal instrument after the surrender, not the beginning of the murder. Wannsee is a real meeting and a late one. Calling every earlier date "mere background" is how the meeting gets mis-taught as a birth.
:::

## What the estimate is, and what it is not

About six million is a sentence with an "about" in it. The word is part of the claim. Deleting it to make the figure sound harder does not make it more accurate. The perpetrators did not hand historians a single completed list that matches one method of counting. Historians reconstruct from deportation lists, from demographic comparison, and from other records. The institutions named in [[#def-estimate]] publish the result as about six million Jews murdered. A lesson that prints an exact integer and calls it the perpetrators' own total has invented a finished ledger. A lesson that throws the estimate away because it is not that ledger has confused scruple with denial.

The estimate is a figure for Jews. It is not a figure for every person murdered by the regime, and it is not a figure for every death in the war. This chapter states no war-death total. Mixing the two is a common confusion, and it is a way of inventing a number. Polish Jews are inside the estimate of about six million Jews. Non-Jewish Poles are not. They are named below, without a total. Adding a second number, even a sincere one from another book, is forbidden here because the chronology does not lock it. Public availability of other estimates is not permission to import them. The atlas's rule is the same one used for battlefield totals in [[wars/first-world-war]] and [[wars/second-world-war]]: an unlocked quantity is not ours to repeat.

Roma were murdered. Disabled people were murdered in the so-called euthanasia programme, a name that hides the murder of patients and does not mean they had chosen to die. No start day for that programme is locked, and no method is described. Many non-Jewish Poles were murdered, and so were Soviet prisoners of war, and others. "Many" is allowed. A second total is not. The gap is a rule of evidence, not a judgement that those murders were small. Naming the groups and refusing a forged integer are both obligatory.

:::example Three dates before a meeting {#ex-order}
Place these in time, and say which one is not a start of mass killing: the Nuremberg Laws on 15 September 1935; mass shooting beginning in the summer of 1941; Chełmno in December 1941; Wannsee on 20 January 1942.
:::solution
The laws come first, in 1935, and they are legal persecution, not the mass killing. Mass shooting begins in the summer of 1941, after the invasion of 22 June 1941 and before Wannsee. Chełmno begins in December 1941, still before the meeting. Wannsee is last of the four, on 20 January 1942, and it coordinates a murder already under way. The laws are not "the start of mass killing" under another name. The meeting is not the start either. Summer 1941 and December 1941 are the chronology's markers for killing already in progress. We still do not describe the killing.
:::
:::

:::example From the Soviet mark of the surrender to the Convention {#ex-convention-span}
The Soviet Union marked the German surrender on 9 May 1945. The Genocide Convention is adopted on 9 December 1948. How many years and months lie between those two dates, using the shared day of the month? Why is 1948 minus 1945 a different and smaller result?
:::solution
From 9 May 1945 to 9 May 1948 is three years. From 9 May to 9 December is seven months. The span is three years and seven months. Both dates fall on a 9th, so the anniversary method does not depend on whether 1948's leap day is inside the span. Subtracting the year numerals, 1948 − 1945 = 3, keeps only the years and drops the seven months. The Convention is after the surrender. It is not the crime, and it is not in the same month as the surrender. Lemkin's book, which uses the word genocide, is 1944, during the war and before both the surrender and the Convention. A word coined in 1944 cannot be a cause of a murder that the chronology already places in 1941.
:::
:::

:::example An invented beginning and an invented ledger {#ex-invented-start}
Treat this sentence as invented. "The murder of the Jews began at Wannsee on 20 January 1942, and the perpetrators' own completed list shows exactly six million people of all groups." Separate the errors.
:::solution
The sentence is invented. It makes Wannsee the beginning, against [[#thm-wannsee]]: mass shooting begins in the summer of 1941 and Chełmno in December 1941. It turns an estimate of about six million Jews into an exact ledger. [[#def-estimate]] forbids that. It extends the figure from Jews to "all groups", which invents a total the chronology does not lock and erases the distinction between the Jewish estimate and the other murdered groups, who are named without a number. A rewritten sentence that survives is: about six million Jews were murdered; the figure is an estimate used by historians and by two named memorial institutions; Wannsee coordinated a murder that had already begun. The rewritten sentence is dull. That is the test. The invented one is false in its date, its exactness, and its boundary.
:::
:::

:::example What one list, one voice and one site can each do {#ex-three-sources}
Without inventing a quotation or a procedure, say what a deportation list, a survivor's testimony, and the site of a killing centre can each support, and what each cannot support on its own.
:::solution
A deportation list is documentary. It can support a claim about an office's record of people sent, and, with other papers, about a policy. It cannot by itself show that every person named was murdered, and it cannot by itself yield the estimate of about six million. A survivor's testimony can support a claim about a place, a time and a fate, within the limits of any witness. It is evidence. It is not a continental total, and this exercise must not invent the witness's words. A site is material evidence that a place was used for murder. Auschwitz, liberated on 27 January 1945, and Chełmno, where killing begins in December 1941, are such places. The site does not authorise a description of how a centre worked, which [[#thm-evidence]] refuses. The estimate is what becomes available when these kinds of evidence are read together, not what any one of them prints alone. To promote an invented sentence of testimony into evidence, you would need the real testimony, as [[sources/evidence]] requires. This example does not supply one.
:::
:::

Lemkin's book of 1944 coins a term for a crime already in progress. The Convention of 9 December 1948, three years and seven months after 9 May 1945, asks states to prevent and to punish genocide. That is a paraphrase of its job, not a quotation of its articles. A law of 1948 cannot cause a murder placed in 1941, and a course that starts at the Convention and walks back until Wannsee looks like an origin repeats the corridor mistake of [[wars/interwar]]. The Convention did not begin the genocide.

There is an earlier classification that keeps the word from looking as if it could apply to only one crime and only after 1948. On 2 October 1904 an extermination order is issued against the Herero in German South-West Africa. Historians classify the campaign against the Herero and the Nama, to 1908, as genocide. The chronology forbids a death total for that campaign, and this lesson states none. The classification is a historical judgement. Lemkin's coinage is 1944. The Convention is 1948. Three operations, not one: a later legal word, a book, and a campaign historians now classify with the category. The centre of this chapter remains the murder of the Jews. The Herero and Nama case is here so that the category is not trapped inside a single date. It is not here as a comparison of totals, because we have refused the second total and the chronology gives no first total for 1904 either.

What is the status of "about six million"?

- [ ] It is a guess with no documentary, testimonial or material support, so the crime is doubtful.
- [x] It is the estimate of Jews murdered used by historians and by Yad Vashem and the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum, not a finished ledger and not a total for other groups.

The third statement is [[#def-estimate]]. Deleting "about" invents an exactness the institutions do not claim. Calling the figure a baseless guess ignores the evidence in [[#thm-evidence]]. Soviet prisoners of war were murdered, and so were Roma, disabled people in the so-called euthanasia programme, many non-Jewish Poles, and others. None of those groups is given a second total here. The Jewish figure is not transferred to them, and theirs is not transferred to it.

Collaboration and rescue both happened. Officials in more than one state took part in deportation. Some people hid Jews. Some lives did both, in different months. Neither pattern is the character of a nation. German power organised the crime. Collaborators elsewhere joined it. Rescuers existed in more than one society, including inside Germany. What would count against this refusal is a record in which every person in a country stood in one category for the whole of the killing. The archives do not show that. They show decisions, which is the point already made for occupation in [[wars/second-world-war]].

Move the year. The first dot is 1935, the Nuremberg Laws, not 1942. Then 1938 is the pogrom, 1941 the start of mass shooting, 1942 the meeting. If your eye treats Wannsee as the origin, the earlier dots are the correction. 1944 is Lemkin's book, 1945 the liberation of Auschwitz on 27 January, 1948 the Convention. Year labels from 1945 to 1948 differ by three. The span from 9 May 1945 to 9 December 1948 is three years and seven months. The figure carries no death total. About six million stays in [[#def-estimate]], not on the dots.

:::warning Wannsee as a beginning, and a ledger or nothing
Four muddles do the damage. The first makes 20 January 1942 the start, which erases the summer of 1941 and December at Chełmno. The second treats about six million as a completed perpetrator list of all victims, or else as a vague guess that leaves the crime doubtful. It is an estimate of Jews murdered, with the support named in [[#thm-evidence]]. The third sorts nations into rescuers or collaborators by essence. Collaboration and rescue both happened, and neither is a national character. The fourth says that if we will not invent a total for Roma, for disabled people murdered in the so-called euthanasia programme, for non-Jewish Poles, or for Soviet prisoners of war, we have decided those murders were minor. We have decided not to fake a number. The groups are named. A second total is not. None of these muddles is repaired by describing a weapon or the working of a camp. That description is refused, and the refusal is not a gap in the evidence.
:::

:::history
On 20 January 1942 officials meet at Wannsee, Heydrich among them, to coordinate deportation and murder. Mass shooting in the occupied Soviet Union has already begun in the summer of 1941, and Chełmno has begun killing in December 1941. The figure used by historians and by memorial institutions is about six million Jews murdered. It is an estimate, not a list the perpetrators completed.
:::

The historical note does not quote the protocol and does not describe a place. On the widget, Wannsee is not the left-hand dot. What would count against the note is a revision, on the same kinds of evidence, that replaced the estimate or moved mass shooting to 1942. A demand for a procedure, a second total, or a speech would not. The crime is dated from 1941. The word is 1944. The Convention is 1948. Using the Convention to doubt the crime reverses that order. Using the crime as a reason to narrate a method reverses the purpose of the lesson. A list, a testimony and the estimate remain different scales, as [[#ex-three-sources]] says: none of them contains the other.

## Where this leads

The war in which the murder was carried out, including the surrender dated 7, 8 and 9 May 1945 in different senses, is [[wars/second-world-war]]. The laws of 1935 and the pogrom of 1938 sit in the years of [[wars/interwar]], which are not a corridor to one man and are also not yet this crime. The division of Europe after the surrender begins in [[postwar/division]]. How a source differs from an inference, and why an invented sentence is not yet evidence, is [[sources/evidence]].

:::summary
- About six million Jews were murdered. The figure is an estimate used by historians and by Yad Vashem and the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. It is not a completed perpetrator list, and it is not a total for every victim of the regime or of the war.
- The Nuremberg Laws are 15 September 1935. The November pogrom is 9–10 November 1938. Neither is the mass murder, and neither is a minor preface you may skip.
- Mass shooting in the occupied Soviet Union begins in the summer of 1941. Chełmno, a place of murder, begins killing in December 1941. Wannsee, on 20 January 1942, coordinates a crime already under way. It is not the start.
- Auschwitz, liberated by the Red Army on 27 January 1945, and other killing centres are named as places. This lesson does not describe a procedure, a weapon, or how a camp worked.
- Roma, disabled people murdered in the so-called euthanasia programme, many non-Jewish Poles, Soviet prisoners of war, and others were also murdered. No second total is stated. The refusal is not a claim that those murders were small.
- Lemkin's *Axis Rule in Occupied Europe* (1944) uses the word genocide. The Genocide Convention is adopted on 9 December 1948, three years and seven months after 9 May 1945. The word and the law are not the cause of the crime.
- Evidence is documentary, testimonial and material. Collaboration and rescue both happened. Neither is the character of a nation. Historians classify the campaign against the Herero and the Nama, from the order of 2 October 1904 to 1908, as genocide, with no death total here.
