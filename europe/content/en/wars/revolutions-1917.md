Why does 1917 have two calendars? Because the Russian state was still dating its civil life by the Julian calendar when the February and October revolutions took place, while Paris, London and the Gregorian reform of 1582 were already on another count. Britain had made its own switch in 1752. Russia did not switch until the next year: the day after 31 January 1918, Julian, was 14 February 1918, Gregorian. Until that morning, a Russian date and a French date for the same event did not wear the same day and month. The October Revolution is 25 October 1917 Old Style and 7 November 1917 New Style. Both names are one event. Treating either as a mistake is how a true double date gets "corrected" out of existence.

This chapter is about that fact, and about what the revolutions did to the war dated in [[wars/first-world-war]]. Nicholas II abdicated on 15 March 1917 New Style, which is 2 March Old Style. On 3 March 1918 the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk took Russia out of the war. The armistice of 11 November 1918 is still the western stop. None of these dates is a destiny of the Russian people, and none of them is a slogan. A calendar explains why one night has two labels. It does not explain why a government fell, and it does not make the second revolution a necessary sequel to the first. Between abdication and October lay months in which another regime governed. Those months are the counter-evidence to any story in which October was already written on the first day of unrest.

The method is the one in [[sources/time-and-names]]. A date before a calendar reform is not an error. It is a date in the calendar then in use. There is no year 0 in this argument, and we do not need one. We are inside the twentieth century, comparing two counts that differ by a fixed number of days, not crossing from BCE into CE.

## Two styles, two revolutions

**Old Style** means the Julian civil calendar still used in Russia. **New Style** means the Gregorian calendar already used in the states that had adopted the reform. In the twentieth century the Gregorian date of a given day was thirteen days later than the Julian date. That thirteen is not a third calendar. It is the conversion that makes 25 October and 7 November the same day, and that makes 23 February and 8 March the same day. We will compute it, and we will also show the trap in the switch of 1918, where a subtraction of two labels gives fourteen rather than thirteen. The trap is an off-by-one about omitted dates. It is not a reason to doubt the conversion.

The revolution that Russians called February is dated 23 February to 3 March 1917 Old Style. On the New Style calendar those endpoints are 8 March and 16 March 1917. The name "February Revolution" is therefore an Old Style name. In Paris and London the same unrest is a March event. Calling it a mistake on one side or the other confuses a name with an error. The abdication sits inside the range: 2 March Old Style is 15 March New Style, and 15 March falls between 8 March and 16 March, just as 2 March falls between 23 February and 3 March. One abdication, inside one revolution, with two labels. If a textbook prints only "February" and only "15 March", it has mixed the styles without saying so. You should be able to spot that mix.

The revolution called October is later. It is not a second label for February. The two revolutions are two events. The two calendars are two names for each event. Holding both distinctions at once is the whole skill. From the abdication on 15 March New Style to the October seizure on 7 November New Style is 237 days. Day of year for 15 March in a non-leap year: January and February have 31 + 28 = 59 days, plus 15, which is 74. Day of year for 7 November: January through October has 304 days, plus 7, which is 311. Then 311 − 74 = 237. For those 237 days a provisional government ruled after the abdication, while councils of workers and soldiers, called soviets, also claimed a voice. The Bolsheviks, led by Lenin, were one party inside that argument. They were not the government of March. October is the dated seizure by which they became it. A reader who skips the 237 days has turned a sequence into a single burst, and has made the provisional months look like a preface rather than a regime.

:::definition Old Style and New Style {#def-styles}
**Old Style** is the Julian civil dating still in force in Russia through 31 January 1918. **New Style** is the Gregorian dating. In 1917 the New Style date of a day is thirteen days later than the Old Style date. A date written before the switch is not an error in the style it uses.
:::

:::definition The February name {#def-february-name}
The **February Revolution** is the unrest and the change of regime dated 23 February to 3 March 1917 Old Style, which is 8 to 16 March 1917 New Style, including the abdication of Nicholas II on 2 March Old Style, or 15 March New Style. The word February records the Russian calendar. It does not record the month name used that week in Paris.
:::

:::definition Soviet, in 1917 {#def-soviet}
A **soviet** in 1917 is a council, especially of workers' and soldiers' deputies. The word is not yet the name of the union state formed on 30 December 1922. That later state is taught in [[wars/interwar]]. Using the same word for the council and for the union is how a later map gets painted back onto the spring of 1917.
:::

:::theorem The two October dates are one event {#thm-one-event}
25 October 1917 Old Style and 7 November 1917 New Style name one seizure of power, not two revolutions. The February Revolution is a different event, earlier in the same year, and it too has one pair of labels rather than two outbreaks. Neither pair is the destiny of the Russian people.
:::
:::proof
Add thirteen days to 25 October. October has 31 days, so six days remain after the 25th, and the other seven fall in November: the 7th. The chronology states both labels for one revolution and places the calendar switch in the next year, so Russia had not already moved to New Style in October. Contemporary Russian dating and foreign dating therefore diverge by that conversion, not by a second uprising. February is earlier: 23 February Old Style converts to 8 March New Style, and the abdication converts from 2 March to 15 March. What would count against the claim is evidence of two distinct seizures, one on the Julian 25 October and another, thirteen days later, on the Gregorian 7 November. The sources do not show that. They show one night described in two calendars. What would also count against a careless reading is any collapse of February into October. The 237 days between abdication and the October seizure are enough to keep them apart. What would not settle the claim is a quarrel about the word revolution. Some historians stress a coup by a party, others a wider social collapse. That quarrel is about a label, in the sense of [[sources/argument]]. It does not split the night into two dates. Destiny is a further label, and a worse one. A destiny has no counter-evidence by construction. The provisional government is the counter-evidence: it existed, and then it was overthrown. Overthrow is a historical act. It is not a fate that had to arrive in November.
:::

:::theorem Brest-Litovsk is an exit, and it has one calendar {#thm-exit}
The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, 3 March 1918, took Russia out of the war on harsh terms. It falls after the civil calendar had switched, so the chronology gives it no Old Style twin. It is earlier than the armistice of 11 November 1918 and it is not the Paris settlement.
:::
:::proof
The switch is explicit. The day after 31 January 1918 Julian was 14 February 1918 Gregorian. The 3 March 1918 of the treaty is after 14 February, on the new civil count. Unlike October 1917, it does not need a second day-and-month. The treaty ends Russia's war with Germany, which had begun when Germany declared war on 1 August 1914. From 3 March to 11 November 1918 is 253 days, so the western armistice is not this treaty and is not a German victory bought by the eastern exit. The terms detached large territories on the old empire's western edge. We state no population and no area for those losses. What would count against the claim is an Old Style twin for 3 March 1918 locked beside it in the way 25 October is locked beside 7 November, or a Russian state still formally at war with Germany under this treaty on the day of the armistice. The chronology does the opposite: one date for the treaty, and a later armistice. What would not count against it is the later collapse of those territorial terms. They did not become the final map. The final map is the work of the Paris treaties in [[wars/interwar]]. A treaty can be a real exit and still fail to last. Duration is not the test of whether the exit happened.
:::

:::quiz
Which pair names one and the same event?
- [ ] 23 February 1917 Old Style and 25 October 1917 Old Style, because both are revolutions.
- [ ] 8 March 1917 and 15 March 1917, because both fall in the February Revolution's New Style range and therefore must be one instant.
- [x] 25 October 1917 Old Style and 7 November 1917 New Style.
- [ ] 3 March 1918 and 11 November 1918, because both end a war.
:::solution
The October pair is one seizure under two calendars, which is [[#thm-one-event]]. February and October are different events even when both are written Old Style. Inside the February range, 8 March is the New Style start and 15 March is the abdication, not the same instant. Brest-Litovsk and the armistice are different documents, 253 days apart. The quiz is a test of whether two labels have been kept distinct from two events.
:::
:::

## Working the conversion

The arithmetic is short, and it is the part a reader can check without a archive. October and March do not behave like February, because only February is short, and 1917 is not a leap year.

:::example From 25 October to 7 November {#ex-october-convert}
Convert 25 October 1917 Old Style by adding thirteen days. Which New Style date do you reach, and why does the month change?
:::solution
October has 31 days. After the 25th, six days remain in October, the 26th through the 31st. Thirteen minus six is seven, so the count runs seven days into November and lands on 7 November. The month changes because the addition crosses the last day of October, not because a second event has occurred. The chronology's pair, 25 October Old Style and 7 November New Style, is this conversion. Russia had not yet switched its civil calendar. The switch is the following year.
:::
:::

:::example February's name, and the abdication inside it {#ex-february-convert}
1917 is not a leap year, so February has 28 days. Convert 23 February and 2 March, Old Style, by adding thirteen days. Say how the results sit inside the New Style range 8 to 16 March.
:::solution
After 23 February, five days remain in February, the 24th through the 28th. Thirteen minus five is eight, so the date is 8 March. Adding thirteen to 2 March does not cross into April: 2 + 13 = 15, so the date is 15 March. The chronology's New Style window for the February Revolution is 8 to 16 March. Both results sit inside it: 8 March is the start, and 15 March, the abdication, is before the 16 March end. The Old Style abdication, 2 March, likewise sits before the Old Style end on 3 March. The name February belongs to the first label only.
:::
:::

:::example Thirteen omitted labels, not fourteen {#ex-switch-trap}
The day after 31 January 1918, Julian, was 14 February 1918, Gregorian. A reader subtracts the labels and reports that fourteen days were dropped, because 14 February is fourteen days after 31 January on an unbroken calendar. How many date-labels had no civil day in Russia, and why is fourteen the wrong count of those labels?
:::solution
On an unbroken Gregorian calendar, 14 February is fourteen days after 31 January. That fourteen counts the one actual day that passed and the labels that were skipped. The skipped labels are 1 February through 13 February: thirteen dates. Russia went to bed on 31 January and woke on 14 February. One night passed. Thirteen names of days were never used. The chronology's sentence is the evidence: the day after 31 January was 14 February. If fourteen labels had been skipped, the next morning would have been called 15 February. It was not. The off-by-one is the same kind of error as counting both endpoints when the question asks for a difference. Here the question is how many labels were omitted. The answer is 13.
:::
:::

:::example An invented double revolution {#ex-invented-twice}
Treat this sentence as invented. "The October Revolution happened twice: the Russians did it on 25 October, and then, because they were late, again on 7 November, which is why 1917 has two calendars." What has the sentence confused, and what would a real second event have looked like in the sources?
:::solution
The sentence is invented, and it is false. It turns a conversion into a second uprising, and it treats the Julian date as lateness rather than as the civil date Russia was using. [[#def-styles]] forbids that. A real second event would be a separate seizure, with its own actors and its own place, occurring thirteen days after the first and dated as such in both a Russian file and a foreign file once the conversion had been applied. What the files support, once the thirteen days are added, is one night. The sentence also smuggles in a cause, "because they were late", which is not a calendar fact. Two calendars are a fact about states adopting the Gregorian reform at different times, as Rome did in 1582 and Britain in 1752. They are not a fact about Russian tardiness.
:::
:::

The same test applies to an invented headline. Suppose, as a labelled invention, that a Petrograd sheet says "25 October" and a London sheet says "7 November", and a student concludes that the London sheet is a correction of a misprint. The conclusion fails. The two sheets are candidates for the same night if, and only if, the conversion holds and the events described match. To promote either invented headline into evidence you would need the actual newspaper, not a sentence made for an exercise. The rule is from [[sources/evidence]]. An invented sentence can train the test. It cannot pass the test.

:::quiz
After the Russian switch, which statement is true of 3 March 1918?
- [ ] It still needs an Old Style twin, just as 25 October 1917 does.
- [x] It falls after the day that followed 31 January 1918, so the civil date is already Gregorian and the chronology gives it one label.
- [ ] It is the armistice.
- [ ] It is the day the Julian calendar was restored.
:::solution
The day after 31 January 1918 was 14 February 1918. The treaty of 3 March 1918 is later than that morning, so it is dated on the new civil calendar. October 1917 is earlier than the switch and therefore keeps two labels. The armistice is 11 November 1918. Nothing in the chronology restores the Julian civil calendar after the switch.
:::
:::

Move the year with the window at zero. The dot for 1917 can say "two revolutions" and still cannot print 23 February and 8 March, or 25 October and 7 November, as separate numbers. A timeline widget stores one integer per year. The double date is a fact about calendars, and this figure is the wrong instrument for it. That limitation is useful. If the widget could show only 7 November, a reader might think 25 October had been refuted. If it could show only 25 October, a reader abroad would look for an event in the wrong week. The lesson carries both. The dot for 1918 is even thicker: the calendar switch, Brest-Litovsk on 3 March, and the armistice on 11 November are three different acts. The label can name them. It cannot order them. You already can. The switch comes first, in the passage from 31 January to 14 February. The treaty comes on 3 March. The armistice comes on 11 November, which is not a Russian calendar story. It is the western end of the war, taught in the previous chapter and only dated here.

:::warning October was not a destiny, and Old Style was not a misprint
Three muddles travel together. The first says that "October" must mean October on the Gregorian calendar, so 7 November is a later mistake. The conversion, and the switch dated the next year, refute it. The second says that Old Style dates are errors because they are not ours. A date before a reform is a date in the calendar then in force. The third says that October was the destiny of the Russian people, as if the provisional government had been a pause in a script. The 237 days from 15 March to 7 November New Style are the pause that was a government. It was overthrown. Overthrow is evidence of a conflict, not of a fate. We do not invent a slogan to make the conflict sound inevitable, and we do not need one. The dates are enough to keep February, October, the switch and the treaty in order.
:::

:::history
The October Revolution is 25 October 1917 on the Julian calendar and 7 November 1917 on the Gregorian. Nicholas II abdicated on 15 March 1917 New Style, which is 2 March Old Style. Lenin was a Bolshevik leader of the October seizure. Russia moved its civil calendar the next year: the day after 31 January 1918 was 14 February 1918.
:::

The historical note uses only locked equivalences. It does not quote Lenin, because this course does not invent speeches. Paraphrase is allowed, and the paraphrase we use is plain. The Bolsheviks sought an end to Russia's war and treated land as a political question. That is a description of a programme's topics, not a three-word banner. Anyone who offers you the banner as a quotation is offering words this atlas has not locked. Ask for the source. Do not let the rhythm of a slogan do the work of a date.

In the days around the armistice of 11 November 1918 the German imperial regime collapses, and a republic follows. In January 1919 a rising associated with the Spartacists is defeated. That rising is not October repeated in Berlin, and it does not make Germany a soviet republic. We do not date it more finely than January 1919, because the chronology does not lock a day. The German story is one paragraph on purpose. The Russian double date, and the exit at Brest-Litovsk, are the chapter.

What would count against the chapter as a whole? A locked source showing that Russia had already adopted the Gregorian civil calendar before October 1917, or that 3 March 1918 was still Julian in the state's own dating, or that the abdication fell outside the February range once both styles were written down. None of those is in the chronology. What would not count against it is a memoir that remembers only one of the two month-names. Memory often keeps the name it was taught. The check is the arithmetic, not the memory. Thirteen days added to 25 October reach 7 November. Thirteen days added to 2 March reach 15 March. Thirteen labels, from 1 February through 13 February 1918, were never lived. Those three results are the chapter in miniature.

A further caution belongs to scale. The provisional government, the soviets and the Bolshevik seizure are political facts at the scale of Petrograd and of the state. They do not, by themselves, describe every village, every soldier at the front, or every nation inside the old empire. A true sentence about the night of 25 October is not yet a sentence about Finland, Ukraine or Central Asia. [[sources/scale]] applies. We do not invent local chronicles to fill the gap, and we do not pretend the gap is a denial that the seizure happened. Brest-Litovsk is the state's exit from the war. It is not a census of who wanted that exit.

## Where this leads

The map that replaced the treaty's harsh edge is not drawn here. [[wars/interwar]] takes Versailles, the other Paris treaties, and the claim that those years were a corridor to one man. The war the revolutions interrupted is [[wars/first-world-war]]. The calendar rules used here, including 1582 and 1752 and the absence of a year 0, are [[sources/time-and-names]]. How a label such as "destiny" differs from a cause is [[sources/argument]].

:::summary
- In 1917 Russia still used the Julian calendar. New Style dates are thirteen days later than Old Style dates. The civil switch is the next year: the day after 31 January 1918 was 14 February 1918.
- The February Revolution is 23 February to 3 March 1917 Old Style, or 8 to 16 March New Style. The name February is the Russian name, not a Paris month.
- Nicholas II abdicated on 2 March Old Style, which is 15 March New Style, inside that range. From that abdication to 7 November New Style is 237 days of provisional government, not a blank.
- The October Revolution is one event: 25 October 1917 Old Style and 7 November 1917 New Style. It is not Russia's destiny, and it is not a second copy of February.
- Thirteen date-labels, 1 February through 13 February 1918, were skipped. A subtraction that reports fourteen has counted the actual day as well as the omitted labels.
- Brest-Litovsk on 3 March 1918 is after the switch, so it has one calendar. It is Russia's exit from the war, 253 days before the armistice, not the Paris peace.
- A defeated Spartacist rising in January 1919 is not October repeated in Germany. The new states and treaties belong to the next chapter.
:::
