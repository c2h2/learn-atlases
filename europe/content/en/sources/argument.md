What is the difference between a claim, a cause and a label?

[[sources/time-and-names]] took the neutral look off the date. A year can hide a zero, a reform or a verdict, and still be printed as a bare number. This chapter takes the neutral look off the sentence that uses the number. A claim says what happened and how we know. A cause is a claim of a stricter kind: it names something that could have been otherwise, and that helps to explain why this happened rather than that. A label bundles a claim, often several claims, and often hides them. Renaissance, absolutism, and Westphalia as the birth of sovereignty are labels. They sound like places on a map of time. They are arguments wearing a noun.

The difference matters as soon as you try to check a sentence. A claim can be true or false, and the check has a shape: evidence for it, and the evidence that would count against it, stated in the same breath rather than apologised for later. A cause fails the check if it could not have been otherwise, because then it does not explain a difference. It also fails if it is asked to do all the work alone. A label fails the check in a quieter way. It feels too large to be false. Unpacking it into claims is how you get it back inside the room where a reader can disagree. We will not write a history in which one factor does all the work of a later event. We will not call any outcome fixed in advance. The word for that refusal is enough. The lesson is the difference.

## Three jobs for a sentence

:::definition Claim {#def-claim}
A **claim** says what happened, or what a stretch of the past was like, and it says how we know. A checkable claim names its evidence and its counter-evidence together. A sentence that cannot say what would count against it is not yet a claim. It is a mood, or it is a label.
:::

:::definition Cause {#def-cause}
A **cause**, in this chapter, is a factor that helps to explain an outcome and that could have been otherwise. A timetable, a drought, a doctrine or a person's decision can be a cause only in so far as a world without it is imaginable and would have differed in the respect we care about. A backdrop that was going to be there anyway is not yet a cause. One cause is not the whole explanation.
:::

:::definition Label {#def-label}
A **label** is a noun that bundles one or more claims and lets them travel without their evidence. Renaissance, absolutism, and the reading of Westphalia as the birth of sovereignty are labels. The bundle may be useful as a pointer. It is not a substitute for the claim, and it is not itself a source.
:::

The three jobs can sit in one paragraph and still be confused, which is why the figure below keeps them as separate nodes. Evidence ties to the claim. Counter-evidence ties to the claim as well. The label ties to the claim and does not tie, on this drawing, straight to the evidence. That missing line is the lesson. You are not allowed to lean on the noun and skip the trace. Dragging the nodes around will not create the missing line. If a richer argument has more links, the figure has not denied them. It has drawn only the roles this chapter is willing to use.

caption: Drag a node. The edges are the roles in an argument. A label is tied to a claim. It is not a substitute for the evidence.

Read the edges in order. From Claim to Evidence: the sentence is responsible to a trace of the kind [[sources/evidence]] defined, a trace with a maker and a purpose. From Counter to Claim: the awkward fact is part of the sentence, not a footnote you add if a critic arrives. From Label to Claim: the noun is allowed only as a nickname for a claim you can still unpack. There is no edge from Label to Evidence, because a nickname does not become truer by being familiar. [[#def-label]] is that missing edge in words.

## A sentence you can check

The numbered claim of this lesson is itself the example. It is about 476, and it is deliberately dull. Dullness is a virtue in a checkable sentence. The excitement is usually where a label has been smuggled in.

:::theorem In 476 Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus; the eastern empire continued; Julius Nepos died in 480 {#thm-476}
Those three clauses are the claim. The meaning of 476 is disputed, and the claim does not convert the deposition into the end of Roman life, of Roman law, or of the Roman state as such. Counter-evidence is inside the sentence: the east went on, and another western claimant was still alive until 480. 476 is a convention. It is not a witnessed last day.
:::
:::proof
The chronology locks the three clauses and the dispute. Odoacer deposes Romulus Augustulus in 476. That is an act in Italy, with a date, a doer and a victim of the act. The eastern empire continues. Its continuation is not a mood. It is a state that still legislates, taxes and fights, and [[after-rome/byzantium]] will meet it under Justinian without treating it as a footnote. Julius Nepos, a rival claimant, dies in 480. From 476 to 480 is four years on the ordinary CE rule, later minus earlier. A last day does not leave a rival emperor four years of life. Historians disagree about what 476 means. The disagreement is part of the prose, not a whisper under it. Some readers want a fall of the West. The clauses above do not give it to them. They give a deposition, a continuation and a death.

What would count against [[#thm-476]] is evidence that the eastern empire ended in 476, or that Nepos died in 476 rather than in 480, or a contemporary record that makes "Roman political life ended today" a fact witnessed in the way the deposition was an act, rather than a later convention. A chronicle moral that calls 476 the death of Rome would be the writer's lesson, and [[sources/evidence]] forbids promoting that lesson into the event. What would also count against a dishonest version of the claim, one that pretended the dispute was not real, is the pretence itself. The meaning is disputed. We say so. [[rome/later-empire]] tells the longer setting, from Diocletian to Nepos, and it refuses lead, Christianity or decadence as a cause in one sentence. We refuse that here too. The claim is checkable because each clause can fail. A label called "the Fall" cannot fail in the same way, because it has already decided.
:::

Put [[#thm-476]] next to a label and the difference is audible. "The Fall of Rome" bundles a claim that something called Rome ended, a claim that 476 is the day it ended, and a claim that the eastern empire does not count as the thing that ended, or does not count as Rome. None of those is on the surface. The noun does the bundling. Unpacked, the first claim is vague until you say what ended: a court in Italy, a legal order, a military habit, the use of Latin, the life of cities. The second claim is the convention, and it is disputed. The third claim is false of the eastern empire, which continued, and awkward for Nepos, who died in 480. Counter-evidence was available before anyone apologised. Leaving it out of the sentence was a choice, and the choice favoured a story in which the West ends and a middle age begins. Who benefits is the storyteller who needs a clean hinge. [[#def-label]] names the device. [[#def-claim]] names the repair.

Four years is a small arithmetic with a large use. It stops the hinge from being a single point. It does not, by itself, tell you what Italy was like in those years, and it does not tell you that nothing important happened in 476. A deposition is important. Importance is not the same as finality. A checkable sentence can say the deposition happened and refuse the finality. A label cannot refuse anything, because it is not the kind of object that has counter-evidence. That is why the Counter node touches the Claim node and does not touch the Label node. If you want the counter-evidence to matter, you have to be making a claim.

:::example Unpack the hinge {#ex-unpack-476}
Rewrite "Rome fell in 476" as a checkable claim, using only the clauses in [[#thm-476]]. Say which part of the slogan you have dropped, and what dispute you are obliged to mention.
:::solution
A checkable replacement is: in 476 Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus in Italy; the eastern empire continued; Julius Nepos died in 480; the meaning of 476 is disputed, and the deposition is a convention for the end of the western imperial line, not the end of Roman life. The slogan's word "fell" has been dropped, because it bundled an ending of "Rome" without saying which Rome. The dispute has to be in the sentence. Historians disagree about the meaning, and hiding the disagreement while keeping the year would turn the year back into a label. The four years from 476 to 480 are the counter-evidence sitting inside the claim, together with the eastern continuation. [[rome/later-empire]] can add the sack of 410 and the edicts of the fourth century. Those additions are not required for the unpacking, and they are not a single cause.
:::
:::

## What a label hides

The Renaissance is a label for a claim about rebirth. Something, the label says, had died, or slept, and then learned, painted or ruled again in a new way. The claim is doing a great deal of work, and the noun lets it travel without saying what died, where, or how anyone knows. [[crises/city-states]] treats humanism as a practice: classical Latin, education, chanceries, fame. It is not a personality called modernity, and it is not the child of one city's fall. The chronology does not lock a birthday for the Renaissance, and this chapter will not invent one. A nickname with no locked endpoints is already a warning. It can be stretched until it flatters the centuries the writer likes.

Who benefits? A writer who wants a particular generation of scholars, or a particular set of Italian cities, to be the moment Europe woke, benefits from the rebirth story. So does a writer who needs the centuries before that generation to be a trough. The benefit is the plot, the same plot [[sources/time-and-names]] found inside the name Middle Ages. Counter-evidence is easy to state once the label is unpacked, and it does not require a sneer at the learning. Humanist practice is real. It is not a switch thrown on a dark continent. Greek books moving after 1453 are a real connection, and the meaning of that year as a cause of humanism is exactly the sort of extra claim the label hides. Constantinople falls on 29 May 1453. Humanism is already a practice. One sentence that respects both facts is a claim. "The Renaissance begins when the city falls" is a label that has eaten the claim.

Absolutism is a label for a claim about kings. The claim, unpacked, is that royal power in the early modern period was unchecked, and often that one court showed all the others their future. The noun makes the claim look like a period. [[states/composite-monarchy]] refuses the noun as the name of the whole period, because a state could be several kingdoms under one ruler, with several laws. [[states/fiscal-military]] treats a loudly advertised monarchy as one solution to the problem of war, tax and debt, not as the model of all Europe. A republic with a charter and a stock of ships is a counter-example sitting in the same centuries. You do not have to tell that republic's history here to see the logical point. If the label were a claim, the republic would count against it. While it remains a label, the republic is called an exception, and the exception is defined as whatever does not fit. That is how a noun protects itself. Exceptions multiply, and the noun stays. A claim would have to shrink.

Westphalia is the label with a real day attached, which makes it the best test. On 24 October 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was concluded at Münster and Osnabrück. In January 1648 Spain had recognised the United Provinces at Münster. The peace adjusts the Peace of Augsburg of 25 September 1555 and admits Calvinists in the empire. Those are acts, and they can be checked. The meaning of Westphalia is disputed. The dispute, which this sentence is obliged to report, is whether those acts are the birth of modern sovereignty, understood as a finished theory of the state system, or instead a settlement of imperial and religious clauses among powers that already existed. This atlas takes the settlement as the description the documents will bear, and it calls the finished theory a later reading. [[reformations/religious-war]] teaches the war and the treaties. We do not retell them, and we do not give them a single cause. Religion was in the war. It was not the only thing in the war. A label called "the birth of sovereignty" keeps only the later theory and drops the clauses.

Who benefits from that label? Anyone who wants a birthday for the modern state, clean enough to teach in a week, benefits. The treaties become a founding, and everything before them becomes a preface. International lawyers and textbook writers are not villains for wanting a starting line. They are readers who have let a label do a claim's work. What would count against the birth-of-sovereignty reading is what the chronology already says: the peace does not invent that finished theory. What would count against our narrower claim, that the peace adjusts Augsburg and admits Calvinists and recognises a republic Spain had already dealt with in January, would be treaty texts that do not do those things. The texts are the evidence. The nickname "Westphalia" can stay, as a pointer to 24 October 1648, if the pointer is not asked to found a world.

1492 is the same device on a crowded year, and the meaning of 1492 is disputed. The year holds a conquest, an expulsion and a voyage. Calling it the birth of modernity bundles those events into one awakening. [[crises/year-1492]] unbundles them. We do not retell the days here. The logical point is enough. A year-number can be accurate, and every event inside it can be real, while the label wrapped around the year is still a claim that has not been earned. Accuracy about the numeral is not accuracy about the bundle. [[sources/time-and-names]] made that point for calendars. It holds for slogans too.

:::theorem A label does not become evidence by being repeated {#thm-label}
Renaissance, absolutism, and Westphalia-as-sovereignty each bundle a claim and hide the bundle inside a noun. Repetition, familiarity and a place in a chapter title do not promote the noun into a source. The claim can be kept only in so far as it can be unpacked, dated where a date is locked, and exposed to counter-evidence.
:::
:::proof
The three nouns are in constant use, which is the evidence that a label can travel without its reasons. None of them is a charter, a treaty text or a survey. The treaty of 24 October 1648 is a source. "The birth of sovereignty" is not. Humanist books and chancery habits are sources for a practice. "The Renaissance" is not a further source sitting beside them. Royal edicts are sources for what a king commanded. "Absolutism" is not a measurement of how far he was obeyed. [[#def-label]] and [[#def-claim]] divide the pairs. Who benefits is visible once the pair is divided: the rebirth story serves a waking Europe, the absolutist story serves a single model of the state, and the sovereignty story serves a clean birthday. Benefits do not make the claims false by themselves. They explain why the nouns are so hard to put down.

What would count against [[#thm-label]] is a showing that one of these nouns is itself a trace with a maker and a purpose in the sense of [[sources/evidence]], rather than a later bundle. A poem of the period that uses a word is a source for that use. It is not a proof that the modern syllabus-noun was already a fact of nature. What would count against a harsher reading, in which labels are banned, is the harmless use of a nickname as a pointer you immediately unpack. We do not ban the pointer. We ban the moment the pointer replaces the evidence. The figure's missing edge, from Label to Evidence, is the same rule in ink.
:::

:::example The birth that was a peace {#ex-westphalia}
Separate, in a few sentences, the acts of 1648 from the label "Westphalia founded modern sovereignty." What is disputed, and what is the later reading?
:::solution
In January 1648 Spain recognised the United Provinces at Münster. On 24 October 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was concluded at Münster and Osnabrück. It adjusts the Peace of Augsburg of 25 September 1555 and admits Calvinists in the empire. Those are the acts a claim can carry. The meaning of Westphalia is disputed. The reading in which the peace is the birth of a finished theory of modern sovereignty is a later reading, and this atlas does not adopt it. [[#thm-label]] treats that reading as a label tied to a claim, not as evidence. [[reformations/religious-war]] tells the war without making the treaties invent a state system. A single cause, religious or otherwise, is not on offer. Counter-evidence to the birthday includes the limited clerical and imperial business the treaties actually do. Familiarity of the word Westphalia does not close the dispute.
:::
:::

## A cause has to be able to fail

[[#def-cause]] is the part of the lesson most often skipped, because a cause is flattering. It makes the writer look like someone who has found the engine. The test is unkind and it is simple. Could it have been otherwise? If the factor you have named was going to be present in every version of the story, you have named a setting, not a cause. If you have named a real factor and then let it eat every other factor, you have written a single-cause history, which this chapter will not do for any later event.

The July crisis is the forward case, and it is only a pointer. The full narrative is in [[nations/toward-1914]]. We do not tell it here. We do not march through ultimatums, and we do not describe a war. What the method needs is one distinction. Historians disagree about whether that crisis was a set of choices or a timetable. The disagreement is required, not optional. A timetable can constrain a government and still leave choices. A constraint is a cause of the stricter kind only where a different choice was possible and would have mattered. A timetable that is treated as a fate has failed [[#def-cause]], because a fate could not have been otherwise. The chapter that teaches the crisis has to keep the choices and the constraint in the same frame. This chapter only insists that the frame is possible, and that a label such as "the road to war", used as if the road had no exits, is the label problem again. No outcome in that crisis is to be taught, from here, as fixed before anyone acted.

The same test protects 476 from its admirers. The deposition happened. It is not the cause of every later European arrangement, and the continuation of the eastern empire is the fact that stops the sentence from swelling. A cause has a scale, which is the subject of [[sources/scale]]. Here it is enough that a cause which explains everything explains nothing checkable. You cannot say what would count against it.

Counter-evidence is therefore not a courtesy added after the claim has been applauded. It is part of the claim's grammar. [[#thm-476]] would be a worse sentence if the eastern empire and the death in 480 were saved for a rebuttal paragraph. A reader would have been invited to believe the hinge first and to doubt it later, and many readers never reach the later paragraph. Putting the counter-evidence in the numbered claim is the discipline. It makes the sentence longer. It also makes it honest. Labels are short because they have fired the staff who kept the counter-evidence.

:::example An invented engine {#ex-invented-engine}
The following sentence is invented for the example. It is not a source and not a claim this atlas makes. "Westphalia made the modern state, and once that cause was in place the later shape of Europe could not have been otherwise." What is the label, what is the pretended cause, and how does the sentence fail?
:::solution
The label is the birth of the modern state, hiding inside the name Westphalia. The pretended cause is the peace of 24 October 1648, asked to produce a whole later shape by itself. That is a single-cause history of later events, which this chapter refuses. It also fails [[#def-cause]], because a factor that leaves no room for things to have been otherwise is not a cause in our sense. It is a fate wearing a date. The meaning of Westphalia is disputed, and the finished theory of sovereignty is a later reading, so the sentence is false of the treaties as well as empty as an explanation. Counter-evidence belongs in the claim: January 1648 and 24 October 1648 adjust a religious peace and recognise a republic, and they do not found a continent. [[#thm-label]] is the rule the invented sentence breaks. Promotion into evidence, in the sense of [[sources/evidence]], never gets started, because the sentence has no maker but us.
:::
:::

:::example Choices under a constraint {#ex-july}
Without narrating the crisis, say what a timetable can do to a government, what it cannot do, and where the narrative lives. Mention the disagreement the chronology requires.
:::solution
A timetable can constrain a government. It can make some sequences costly, fast or hard to unwind. It cannot abolish choices, and it cannot turn a crisis into a fate. Historians disagree about whether the July crisis was a set of choices or a timetable. The disagreement stays in the sentence. The full narrative is in [[nations/toward-1914]], and it is not told here. [[#def-cause]] is the test you carry into that chapter: a factor earns the name of cause only if it could have been otherwise. A label such as a road with no exits fails [[#thm-label]] in the same way absolutism and the rebirth story fail it. No death total, no ultimatum and no campaign belongs in this answer.
:::
:::

:::warning The noun that ate the evidence
The usual myth says that a period has a spirit, and that the spirit is the cause of what the people in it did. Renaissance, absolutism and the sovereign state born at Westphalia are the myth's favourite nouns. They fail as causes because a spirit could not have been otherwise, and they fail as claims because nothing specified would count against them. They succeed as labels, which is the danger. 476, read as the Fall, is the same myth in Roman dress. The deposition happened. The eastern empire continued. Nepos died in 480. The meaning is disputed. A timetable in a later crisis can constrain a government and still leave choices. None of these outcomes was waiting, complete, inside a noun.
:::

:::history
In 476 Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus. The eastern empire continued, and Julius Nepos died in 480. On 24 October 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was concluded at Münster and Osnabrück. The first cluster is a deposition with counter-evidence attached. The second is a peace whose meaning is disputed, not the invention of a finished theory of sovereignty.
:::

## Keeping the grammar when the story gets loud

The grammar is easy in a chapter about grammar and hard in a chapter about a war, a reform or a revolution. Those later chapters will be full of causes that are genuine: a fiscal crisis, a doctrine, a mobilisation scheme, a vote. The discipline that travels is not a ban on explanation. It is a ban on two shortcuts. The first shortcut lets one cause do every later chapter's work. The second shortcut replaces the cause with a label and then treats the label as too dignified to check. If you find yourself writing that a spirit, an age or a road produced the outcome, you have taken both shortcuts at once.

You can practise the repair on any sentence you admire. Ask what happened, in a clause a stubborn reader could deny. Ask how you know, and name a trace rather than a tradition of textbooks. Ask what would count against the clause, and put that counter-evidence where the reader will meet it before the applause. Then ask whether your factor could have been otherwise. If you cannot imagine the otherwise, you have a setting, and settings belong in the description, not in the engine. [[#thm-476]] is the model only because the facts are small enough to hold in one sentence. The same grammar is what [[sources/scale]] will demand when the facts are a life, a manor, a kingdom or a trade, and a true sentence at one size is being asked to do another size's job.

Who benefits from skipping the grammar is no mystery. A short label fits a title, an examination question and a speech. A checkable sentence fits a margin in which someone can write that Nepos dies in 480 and refuse your hinge. The benefit of the label is speed. The cost is that you can no longer tell a source from a slogan, which is the skill [[sources/evidence]] existed to teach. Dates, claims, causes and labels are four different tools. Using the shiniest one for every job is how a survey of Europe turns back into a myth.

## Where this leads

A claim is now something that can fail in public. [[sources/scale]] asks when a true story at one scale becomes false at another, which is the next place a cause goes wrong. The deposition of 476 is set in a longer Roman history in [[rome/later-empire]]. The peace of 1648 is taught as a peace, not as a theory of sovereignty, in [[reformations/religious-war]]. The July crisis, choices under a timetable, is [[nations/toward-1914]], and it is not told here. The labels Renaissance and absolutism are under pressure again in [[crises/city-states]] and [[states/composite-monarchy]].

:::summary
- A claim says what happened and how we know, and it includes the counter-evidence. A sentence with nothing that would count against it is not yet a claim.
- A cause is a factor that could have been otherwise. A backdrop that was fixed in advance does not explain a difference, and one cause is not a whole history.
- A label bundles claims and hides them. Renaissance, absolutism, and Westphalia as the birth of sovereignty are labels, not sources.
- In 476 Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus. The eastern empire continued. Julius Nepos died in 480. The meaning of 476 is disputed. That is a checkable sentence.
- On 24 October 1648 the peace at Münster and Osnabrück adjusted Augsburg and admitted Calvinists. A finished theory of modern sovereignty is a later reading.
- The meaning of 1492 is disputed. A crowded year is not a birth of modernity just because the numeral is accurate.
- Historians disagree about whether the July crisis was a set of choices or a timetable. A timetable can constrain a government and still leave choices. The narrative is elsewhere.
- The figure ties evidence and counter-evidence to the claim, and the label only to the claim. Familiarity is not an edge to the evidence.
:::

The sentence bundles a rebirth, a cause and a birthday into one noun-driven story. No birthday of the Renaissance is locked, so the sentence invents a joint [[sources/time-and-names]] would already refuse. The fall of the city on 29 May 1453 is a real event. Humanism, as [[crises/city-states]] uses it, is already a practice of classical Latin, education, chanceries and fame. A claim can say both of those things. It cannot let the city's fall be the cause of a practice already under way, and it cannot let "Renaissance" stand in for the evidence. Who benefits is the plot of a continent waking up. :::exercise One noun, several kingdoms {level=2}
Why does a composite monarchy count against absolutism if absolutism is being used as a claim, and why does it fail to count if absolutism is protected as a label?
:::solution
Unpacked, absolutism claims that royal power in the period was unchecked and that one model covers the states. A composite monarchy is several kingdoms under one ruler, with several laws. That fact can be true beside a powerful king, and it counts against the unchecked, uniform model. [[states/composite-monarchy]] refuses the noun as the name of the whole period for that reason. If the noun is only a label, the composite case is filed as an exception, and the exception is defined as whatever does not fit, so the noun never meets the counter-evidence. [[#def-label]] describes that protection. A fiscal-military monarchy can still be one real solution to war and tax, as [[states/fiscal-military]] teaches, without being the pattern of every state. The repair is a claim about a particular crown, small enough to fail.
:::

:::exercise Build a checkable sentence {level=3}
Using 476 and 480 only, write one checkable sentence that includes counter-evidence, states the dispute, and is not the slogan "Rome fell". Then say what would count against your sentence.
:::hint
Three clauses: a deposition, a continuation, a death. The dispute is about the meaning, not about whether Odoacer acted.
:::
:::solution
One acceptable sentence is the claim in [[#thm-476]]: in 476 Odoacer deposed Romulus Augustulus; the eastern empire continued; Julius Nepos died in 480; the meaning of 476 is disputed, and the year is a convention for a western deposition, not the end of Roman life. Counter-evidence is the continuation and the four years until 480, since 480 minus 476 is 4. What would count against it is evidence that the eastern empire ended in 476, or that Nepos died in 476, or a record in which the end of Roman political life was itself the witnessed act rather than a later interpretation. A chronicle moral that simply announces a fall would not count as that record. [[sources/evidence]] keeps the moral on the writer's side of the line. A label called the Fall does not become more checkable by being shorter.
:::
:::

:::exercise The edge that is not drawn {level=3}
The figure has edges from Claim to Evidence, from Counter to Claim, and from Label to Claim. It has no edge from Label to Evidence. Take the label "the birth of sovereignty" and show, with the dates of 1648, what that missing edge forbids you to do. What addition would count against the drawing?
:::hint
The peace has clauses. The birthday does not. Familiarity is not a trace with a maker.
:::
:::solution
The missing edge forbids you to treat the noun as if it touched the treaties by itself. In January 1648 Spain recognised the United Provinces at Münster. On 24 October 1648 Münster and Osnabrück produced a peace that adjusts Augsburg, the peace of 25 September 1555, and admits Calvinists. Those clauses are evidence for a claim about a settlement. They are not evidence for a finished theory of modern sovereignty. That theory is a later reading, and the meaning of Westphalia is disputed. [[#thm-label]] is the missing edge in prose. What would count against the drawing is a showing that the label is itself a source, a trace with a maker and a purpose, rather than a bundle tied to a claim. A chapter title that repeats the noun does not do that. Nor does the usefulness of a short name, so long as the name is unpacked at once. If the treaties had announced the theory in terms the chronology does not give them, the claim about a later reading would have to shrink. They are not given those terms here. The figure may be dragged about. Dragging does not invent the forbidden edge.
:::
:::
