Which nations got a state, and which did not?

A nation-state is a political project: a claim that a state should match a people, carried out by war, negotiation, schools, maps and exclusions. It is not a person waking up, and it is not the completion of nature. Between the proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy on 17 March 1861 and the proclamation of the German Empire on 18 January 1871, two such projects succeeded on the terms their makers set. Poland did not receive a state. Neither did Ireland. Neither did the Czechs. In 1905 the union of Sweden and Norway dissolved, which is a national result of a different kind. The lesson is the list of who was included in a capital, and who was refused one.

The census chapter, [[nations/industry]], has already refused to treat a population total as a nation. Ireland's enumerated fall is not a vote for a state, and England and Wales growing towards 32,527,843 is not a constitution. What follows here is the political history those numbers do not contain.

## A project, not an awakening

:::definition Nation-state {#def-nation}
A **nation-state**, in this lesson, is a political project that claims the state's borders and the nation's membership should be the same thing. It is made by identifiable acts: proclamations, wars, treaties, coronations, refusals. It is not a biological person, and it is not the discovery of a nation that was already a state.
:::

Languages, churches and memories can be older than any of the dates below. Age is not the question. The question is which projects received a state in these years, by what acts, and which projects did not. A metaphor of sleep and waking cannot answer it, because a metaphor cannot fail. An act can.

:::theorem Unification was war and negotiation, not the completion of nature {#thm-project}
The Kingdom of Italy and the German Empire were made by dated acts of war and bargaining. They were not the natural completion of an Italian or a German person, and this lesson does not treat them as the destiny of anyone who spoke the language.
:::
:::proof
The locked acts are these. The Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed on 17 March 1861. Venice's transfer is part of 1866, the year of Königgrätz on 3 July, and this atlas does not lock a separate treaty day for Venice, so none is invented. Italian troops take Rome on 20 September 1870. On the German side, Königgrätz is a Prussian victory over Austria. Sedan, on 1 and 2 September 1870, is a defeat of the French imperial army. The German Empire is proclaimed on 18 January 1871 in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, while the French war is unfinished. The Treaty of Frankfurt, on 10 May 1871, is the peace. Each item is a decision or the outcome of a campaign, not a blossom. What would count against the claim is evidence that either state arrived without war and without bargaining, as the recognition of a single will that already coincided with the later borders. The borders themselves count against that picture. On 17 March 1861 Venice and Rome are not in the new kingdom's hands. In 1871 Austria's German-speakers are outside the empire just proclaimed. A nature that completed itself would not have left those people on the wrong side of the act.
:::

The earlier German parliament belongs in the contrast. In 1848 a Frankfurt parliament met, and most of the revolutionary regimes of that year were defeated. The empire of 1871 is not that parliament reconvened. It is a Prussian-led federation announced after Königgrätz and Sedan. The point of remembering 1848, which [[revolutions/restoration-and-1848]] teaches, is that a national assembly can meet and still not be the state that later wears the nation's name. If nations simply awoke, Frankfurt and Versailles would not be two different kinds of room.

This atlas does not lock days for the campaigns that preceded 17 March 1861, in the north or in the south, so this lesson does not invent them. Naming Garibaldi's expedition, or Cavour's diplomacy, without a year is allowed. Dating them is not. What the chronology does lock is enough for the claim: a proclamation, a later transfer of Venice inside 1866, and Rome on 20 September 1870. The kingdom was not born complete. Completeness is what the awakening story requires, and the dates withhold it.

:::example Ten years on the labels {#ex-decade}
The Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed on 17 March 1861. The German Empire is proclaimed on 18 January 1871. What is the gap between the year-labels, and why is the span of the two days a little shorter than that gap?
:::solution
The year-labels differ by 1871 minus 1861, which is 10. Ten full years from 17 March 1861 would end on 17 March 1871. The German proclamation falls on 18 January 1871, before that anniversary, so the day-span is short of ten years. The widget's "decade" is the gap of the labels. It is not a claim that the two proclamations are one event, or that the second completed the first.
:::
:::

## 1866, 1867, and a dual monarchy

Königgrätz, also called Sadowa, is 3 July 1866. It is a battle between Prussia and Austria. This lesson gives no casualty total. What the battle does in the argument is political. Prussia can go on to make a Germany that excludes Austria. Austria, defeated, bargains inside its own empire. Venice's passage to Italy is part of that same year. Anyone who adds a treaty day for Venice is inventing a date the chronology does not lock. "Part of 1866" is the whole of the allowed sentence.

:::definition The Ausgleich {#def-ausgleich}
The **Ausgleich** of 1867 is the compromise that turns the Habsburg empire into Austria-Hungary, a dual monarchy. Franz Joseph is crowned king of Hungary on 8 June 1867. The settlement recognises a Hungarian kingdom under the emperor. It is not a nation-state of every people the dynasty ruled, and it is not the British Reform Act of the same year.
:::

:::theorem The Ausgleich recognises Hungary and refuses an equivalent to the Czechs {#thm-dual}
The compromise of 1867 and the coronation of 8 June 1867 are acts in favour of a Hungarian kingdom. They do not give the Czechs a matching state. A dual monarchy is a bargain between a dynasty and one kingdom, not the awakening of all its nations.
:::
:::proof
The chronology locks the year of the Ausgleich and the day of the coronation, and nothing parallel for Bohemia. Czech national politics in these years asked for a status comparable to Hungary's and did not receive it. What would count against the claim is a locked act of 1867, or of the coronation's season, that established a Czech kingdom on the Hungarian pattern. There is none in this atlas. What would also count against it is evidence that the dual structure was only ceremonial and that every Habsburg people already had the same public standing as Hungary. The need for a special compromise, and a separate coronation, is evidence that Hungary's standing was being made, not merely described. The British Reform Act of 1867, taught in [[nations/mass-politics]], shares the year and not the act. A franchise statute in Britain is not a coronation in Buda.
:::

The defeat at Königgrätz changes what Vienna can bargain. It does not write the Ausgleich by itself. A military result constrains a court. The court and the Hungarian politicians still make a compromise rather than another arrangement, including the arrangement the Czechs wanted. Constraint is not the absence of a choice. That distinction returns, sharper, in [[nations/toward-1914]].

:::example One year, two kinds of act {#ex-1867}
The Ausgleich is dated 1867, and Franz Joseph is crowned king of Hungary on 8 June 1867. Königgrätz was on 3 July 1866. What may you say about the relation of these dates, and what treaty day may you not add?
:::solution
The year-labels differ by 1867 minus 1866, which is 1. The coronation falls before the anniversary of 3 July, so the day-span is under a year. You may say that the defeat precedes the compromise and changes the bargain. You may not say that Königgrätz is the Ausgleich, and you may not invent a day for a Venetian treaty inside 1866. Venice's transfer is part of 1866 and has no separate treaty day in this atlas. The coronation is a dated act inside the Ausgleich's year. It is not, by itself, a grant to the Czechs.
:::
:::

## Rome, Sedan, Versailles, Frankfurt

The year 1870 carries two markers that are easy to glue together and should not be. Sedan is 1 and 2 September: a French imperial army is defeated, and the second French empire does not survive it as a war government. This lesson does not give a day for the republic that then carries on the war, because the chronology does not lock one. Rome is 20 September: Italian troops take the city. The kingdom proclaimed in 1861 had not held its claimed capital. Taking the city ends the pope's rule over Rome. It does not, in this chronology, settle the quarrel with the papacy, and the lesson invents no later treaty day to finish the story.

Sedan and Rome share a year. Rome's date falls after Sedan's. That order is real. It is not a licence for a single-cause sentence in which one battle is the meaning of the other. French power in Rome had been part of the reason the Italian kingdom waited. The waiting ended in a war France was losing. The Italian government still decided to take the city. A decision made easier is still a decision.

:::example Two dates in September 1870 {#ex-1870}
Set 1–2 September 1870 beside 20 September 1870. What does each date lock, and what would be an over-reading?
:::solution
Sedan locks a defeat of the French imperial army on the first two days of September. Rome locks Italian troops taking the city on 20 September. The gap in the calendar of that month runs from 2 September to 20 September. An over-reading would make Sedan and Rome one event, would invent a casualty total for either, or would treat the taking of Rome as the completion of a nation rather than as the seizure of a city the 1861 kingdom had not held. The proclamation of 17 March 1861 remains an earlier and incomplete map.
:::
:::

The German Empire is proclaimed on 18 January 1871 in the Hall of Mirrors. Wilhelm I takes the imperial title. Bismarck is the minister who has made the Prussian path the path of the empire. The course's own notice of the day says what the room does not cancel: Paris is still at war, and the Commune has not begun. The Commune is March to May 1871, taught in [[nations/mass-politics]]. A proclamation in a captured palace is not the consent of the city outside, and it is not yet the peace.

The Treaty of Frankfurt is 10 May 1871. That day falls inside the months of the Commune. A peace treaty and a city in revolt can share a season without being the same fact. The treaty ends the war between the new empire and the French state. It cedes Alsace and part of Lorraine. This lesson does not state an indemnity. No sum of money is locked, and a guessed sum would only look precise. A proclamation on 18 January and a treaty on 10 May are two acts. Anyone who writes "Germany was unified on 18 January, so the war was over" has collapsed them.

## Who was refused

:::theorem Poles, Irish and Czechs do not receive a state in these years {#thm-refused}
The successes of 1861 and 1871 were not a general rule. The Polish commonwealth had already been erased as a state by the partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795. No Polish state is restored in 1861 or in 1871. Ireland receives no state in these years. The Czechs receive no equivalent of the Hungarian settlement of 1867.
:::
:::proof
The partitions are locked: three acts, and the commonwealth is gone as a state. It had been a large European polity, not a gap on the map. Nothing in the unification dates puts it back. Polish political life continues under Prussia, then the German empire, under Russia, and under Austria-Hungary. Continuation is not a capital. Ireland, inside the United Kingdom, has a national politics and, from [[nations/industry]], a census history. It does not have a separate state on any date this chapter locks. The Czech case is the Ausgleich itself, read from the side it does not include, as [[#thm-dual]] argues. What would count against the claim is a proclamation, treaty or coronation in this run of years that established a Polish, Irish or Czech state. None is in the chronology. What would also count against it is a demonstration that "state" here means something so weak that a denied parliament and a denied kingdom still count as success. They do not. A project can be real and still be refused.
:::

The refusal is not a silence about minds. People in Warsaw, Dublin and Prague argued, organised and remembered. A missing state is evidence about who held sovereignty, not evidence that nobody wanted one. That is the same rule as a silence in [[sources/evidence]]: the archive records who was asked to sign a treaty, not the absence of a wish.

Nor did every successful project include everyone it named. The German empire of 1871 is a Germany without Austria. The Italian kingdom of 1861 is an Italy without Rome until 1870 and without Venice until 1866. Inclusion and refusal are the same political art. The awakening story hides both, because a person who wakes does not negotiate a border.

:::example An invented awakening {#ex-invented}
The following sentence is invented. "In 1861 the Italian nation awoke, in 1867 every Habsburg nation received its kingdom, and in 1871 the German nation awoke, so by 1871 each European nation had its state." Separate the locked dates from the additions.
:::solution
17 March 1861, the Ausgleich of 1867 and 18 January 1871 are real acts, and they are not awakenings. The Ausgleich does not give every Habsburg nation a kingdom: Hungary is crowned on 8 June 1867, and the Czechs are not. "Each European nation had its state" fails [[#thm-refused]]. Poland has had no state of its own since the partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795, and 1871 does not restore one. Ireland receives none. The invented sentence promotes three capitals into a universal rule. The universal rule is the part that would need evidence, and the refusals are the evidence against it.
:::
:::

## A separation in 1905

In 1905 the union of Sweden and Norway is dissolved. That is a national result, and it is not a unification. Two states that had shared a union part. Nobody in this lesson needs to pretend that every national feeling ends in a larger state, or that a union, once made, is nature. The chronology locks the dissolution and does not lock a day for the union's beginning, so the beginning is not given a year here. What matters is the kind of act. 1861 and 1871 build states by proclamation after war. 1905 ends a union. Put them in one list called "the rise of nations" and the list has already lied.

The dissolution also blocks a tidy map of the century in which 1871 is the last word. Later than the Hall of Mirrors, a European union comes apart without being the story of Italy or Germany. National politics has more than one shape. The shapes are dated. They are not a ladder.

:::quiz
The dissolution of the Swedish-Norwegian union in 1905 is best described as which of these?
- [ ] The same kind of act as the proclamation of the German Empire
- [ ] The restoration of the Polish commonwealth
- [x] A national result that separates two states, not a unification
- [ ] The moment Ireland and the Czech lands received kingdoms
:::solution
1905 dissolves a union. It does not proclaim an empire, does not restore Poland, and does not give Ireland or the Czechs a state. [[#def-nation]] allows different acts to be national without forcing them into one plot of awakening.
:::

## A worked case: one palace and a missing capital

Read 18 January 1871 as a worked case, with 10 May beside it and with Warsaw left off the map on purpose.

On 18 January the empire is proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors. The people in the room are princes and ministers of German states, under a Prussian king taking an imperial title. Austria is not among them. The French war is not over. The Commune is still in the future. Nothing in the ceremony is a census of German-speakers, and nothing in it is a Polish constituent assembly. The glamour of the room is not an argument. The argument is the list of who is present and what legal act has, and has not, been completed.

On 10 May the Treaty of Frankfurt completes a different act: peace, and the cession of Alsace and part of Lorraine. From 18 January to 10 May is inside one season. It is long enough for a proclamation and a treaty to be distinct. A student who remembers only "1871" has failed the widget, which gives that year more than one event. The same discipline applied to 1870 keeps Sedan and Rome apart.

Now apply the list to a city that has no dot. Warsaw in January 1871 is under Russian rule. A Polish state is not proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors and not created at Frankfurt. The partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795 remain the last word this chronology allows on Polish statehood. If the case is doing its work, 18 January should look smaller than the myth, not larger. It is a great-power act. It is not the year in which nations as such came into their own.

:::warning Awakening, and the completion of nature
The usual story says that Italy and Germany were nations waiting to stand up, and that 1861 and 1871 are the moments they did. The story cannot see Venice missing in 1861, Rome missing until 20 September 1870, Austria missing from the German empire, or Prague missing from the Ausgleich. It cannot see that Poles and Irish received no state at all. A project can succeed, partly succeed, or be refused. Nature does not proclaim an empire in a palace while the other capital is still at war, and it does not crown one kingdom in 1867 while withholding the same crown from another people. Casualty totals for Königgrätz or Sedan will not repair the story. None are stated here.
:::

:::history
On 18 January 1871 the German Empire is proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles. Paris is still at war, and the Commune has not begun. The Treaty of Frankfurt follows on 10 May 1871. The proclamation is an act of state-making during a war, not the awakening of a German person, and not a state for the Poles.
:::

## Where this leads

The numbers that must not be asked to do this chapter's work are in [[nations/industry]]. The parliament of 1848, which did not become the empire, is in [[revolutions/restoration-and-1848]]. Who could vote inside the states made here, and who was still outside the franchise, is [[nations/mass-politics]]. The empires those states then extended overseas are [[nations/empires]]. The dual monarchy that the Ausgleich built is still the Austria-Hungary that faces the July crisis in [[nations/toward-1914]]. Later borders, including any Polish state this chronology does not date here, belong to [[wars/interwar]] and not to 1871.

:::summary
- A nation-state is a political project made by acts, not a person awakening and not the completion of nature.
- The Kingdom of Italy is proclaimed on 17 March 1861. Venice's transfer is part of 1866, with no separate treaty day. Italian troops take Rome on 20 September 1870.
- Königgrätz is 3 July 1866. The Ausgleich is 1867, and Franz Joseph is crowned king of Hungary on 8 June 1867. The Czechs do not receive that settlement.
- Sedan is 1–2 September 1870. The German Empire is proclaimed on 18 January 1871 in the Hall of Mirrors. The Treaty of Frankfurt is 10 May 1871. No casualty total is stated.
- Poles, Irish and Czechs do not receive a state in these years. The partitions of 1772, 1793 and 1795 had already erased the Polish commonwealth as a state.
- In 1905 the Swedish-Norwegian union dissolves. That is a separation, not another unification. The year-labels of 1861 and 1871 are 10 apart, and each of 1870 and 1871 holds more than one event.
:::

:::exercise The side the compromise leaves out {level=2}
Why is the Czech case a test of the Ausgleich, rather than a footnote to a Hungarian success?
:::solution
The Ausgleich and the coronation of 8 June 1867 make a Hungarian kingdom under Franz Joseph. Czech politics asked for a comparable standing and did not receive it in these years. If the compromise were the awakening of "the nations" of the empire, the Czech refusal would be unintelligible. It is intelligible as soon as the Ausgleich is a bargain with one kingdom. [[#thm-dual]] is that reading. A success for Hungary and a refusal for the Czechs are one settlement seen from two sides.
:::

Why is the dissolution of the Swedish-Norwegian union in 1905 not evidence that 1871 had revealed the normal form of a nation?

The chronology locks the dissolution and does not lock the union's first day, so the answer does not invent one. "Normal form" is what [[#def-nation]] refuses: acts, not a template nature fills in.

:::exercise Language is not the state {level=3}

:::solution
Concede that languages and memories can be older than 1861 and 1871. [[#def-nation]] never said a project invents speech. The counter-argument still has to explain why recognition, if it was mere recognition, left Venice out until 1866, Rome out until 20 September 1870, Austria out of the German empire, and the Czechs out of the Hungarian bargain, and why Polish and Irish speech did not produce states in these years at all. A border that excludes speakers of the language the state claims is a political decision. What would count for the counter-argument is a state whose borders arrived with the language and without war, bargaining or refusal. The locked acts are war, bargaining and refusal. The speeches can be old. The states are dated.
