Chronic inflammation is what happens when the trigger will not leave: **persistent microbes, indigestible particles, autoimmune self-reactions or prolonged exposures keep the inflammatory theatre open, and the cast changes - macrophages, lymphocytes and plasma cells replace the neutrophils, connective tissue grows in, and the same mediators that clear debris now destroy architecture and build scars.** Between the extremes of acute exudate and silent resolution sits the bulk of clinical medicine: fibrosing lung and liver disease, bowel inflammation, granulomatous disorders, most sclerosis syndromes and the inflammatory component of atherosclerosis and obesity.

This chapter covers the mononuclear cast and their activation states, granulomatous inflammation, tissue repair by regeneration and scarring, fibrosis pathways, and the chronic inflammatory diseases as working examples.

::: summary
- Chronic inflammation means persistence: microbes that evade, exposures that endure, or self-reactions that never switch off; the infiltrate is mononuclear and the damage is collateral.
- The macrophage runs the show - recruited, activated, fused into epithelioid and giant cells, and licensed by TH1 interferon-gamma loops; its cytokines injure, and its TGF-beta and PDGF fibrose.
- The granuloma is a strategy against indigestible targets; caseation points to tuberculosis, and every granuloma deserves an organism hunt before an immune label.
- Repair regenerates when the stem cells and scaffold survive; otherwise it scars - and scar biology explains suture timing, contractures, keloids and most chronic organ failure.
- Fibrosis is the common final pathway of organs: liver, kidney, lung, heart and bowel all fibrose through overlapping myofibroblast programmes, and the first licensed anti-fibrotics have arrived.
- Chronic inflammation is also a carcinogen: the inflammation-dysplasia-carcinoma road is paved in bowel, liver, oesophagus and beyond, which is why surveillance clinics exist.
:::
