Filtration presents 180 L/day of plasma-water-with-solutes to a tube that is, a literally hourly reprocessing plant: **the tubule reclaims ~99 % of the water and nearly all of the glucose and amino acids, tunes sodium and potassium and calcium and hydrogen to a fraction of a percent of daily intake, and concentrates or dilutes the urine against gradients it builds itself.** Every transporter here is a drug target (the diuretics, SGLT2 inhibitors, acetazolamide), every genetic defect a named syndrome, and every clinical electrolyte lesson downstream of this chapter's cell biology.

This chapter covers tubular transport principles, the nephron segment by segment, the concentration mechanism, transport maximum, and the tubular syndromes.

## Transport principles

::: definition The tubule cell and its transporter toolkit {#def-transport}
Tubular transport is **transcellular (apical membrane in, basolateral out, across the cell) or paracellular (between cells, through tight-junction strands whose charge-and-pore selectivity varies along the nephron — leakiest in the proximal tubule) and is powered, directly or indirectly, by the basolateral Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase (the 3-Na-out/2-K-in electrogenic pump keeping intracellular Na⁺ ~10-15 mmol/L against ~140 outside — the steep inward sodium electrochemical gradient that is the epithelium's battery — [[physiology/membrane-potentials]]'s Nernst-and-gradient arithmetic]).** The toolkit: **apical sodium entry by (i) Na⁺-coupled symport (the SGLT glucose and NKCC2 and NCC and NaPi and amino-acid carriers), (ii) Na⁺/H⁺ exchange (NHE3 — the proximal workhorse also doing bicarbonate reabsorption ([[renal/acid-base-disorders]]) and the target of the tenapanor-era phosphate research of [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]), (iii) ENaC channels (the aldosterone-tuned distal electrogenic entry — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]'s principal cell), (iv) Cl⁻/base exchangers; efflux by basolateral Na⁺-K⁺-ATPase, K⁺ and Cl⁻ channels (the recycling currents that generate the lumen-positive voltages driving paracellular cation reabsorption), and secondary-driven export (the Na⁺-Ca exchanger and Ca-ATPase of the distal nephron — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]); organic-solute handling by the organic anion (OAT1/3 in), the organic cation (OCT out) and organic-zwitterion and peptide (PEPT) and monocarboxylate systems — the liver's excretion-workforce twin ([[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]]), doing the secretion of drugs, acids and bases almost for free and producing the interaction pharmacology of probenecid-penicillin, cilastatin-imipenem (the dehydropeptidase inhibitor co-drug) and NSAID-methotrexate toxicity ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]); and the endocytic reclaimers (the cubilin-megalin complex salvaging filtered low-molecular-weight proteins, vitamin-B12-transcobalamin and hormone — their failure producing the tubular proteinuria signature ([[renal/filtration]]'s classification)).** Two organising quantities: **the transport maximum (Tm — carrier saturation, with splay — the glucose titration curve of [[#ex-tm]] and the renal threshold and the SGLT2-inhibitor's deliberate glycosuria ([[endocrine/diabetes]])), and the gradient-limited backleak (the maximum gradients the tubule can sustain — the urine-plasma PAH ratio ~40 proving secretion, and the collecting duct's ability to hold a 4.5% urea and 1200-mOsm medullary disequilibrium ([[#def-concentrate]])).**
:::

::: example Reclaiming the glucose load {#ex-tm}
Plasma glucose rises to 22 mmol/L in a diabetic: filtered load = GFR × plasma = 0.125 L/min × 22 mmol/L = 2.75 mmol/min; urinary glucose excretion is 0.7 mmol/min. Estimate the effective Tm, and explain why glucose appears in urine at ~11 mmol/L although saturation is only complete near 22.
::: solution
**Reabsorption = load − excretion = 2.75 − 0.7 = 2.05 mmol/min; at this plasma glucose the carriers are saturated, so the reabsorbed flux estimates Tm(glucose) ≈ 2 mmol/min (~360 mg/min — the textbook ~375 mg/min for a standard adult body size, scaling with nephron mass).** The appearance-at-half-saturation puzzle is **splay: the nephron population heterogeneity (some nephrons' shorter tubules and lower carrier content saturate first — the "splay of the titration curve") plus flow-dependent reabsorption-time reduction — hence the threshold (~11 mmol/L or ~200 mg/dL) sits below the theoretical complete-saturation point, and why the dipstick is an insensitive glycometer ([[renal/filtration]]'s quantification rules) and why SGLT2 inhibitors (raising urinary glucose by ~60 g/day at any glycaemia — the deliberate saturation-collapse) work by lowering effective Tm rather than by blocking all reabsorption ([[endocrine/diabetes]])**
:::
:::

## Segments

::: definition Proximal tubule: the bulk recycler {#def-proximal}
The proximal convoluted-and-straight tubule (the brush-border 50-micron microvilli and mitochondria-rich "pars recta and convoluta") reclaims **~65-70 % of filtered Na⁺ and water (isosmotically — the solute-linked, aquaporin-1-and-claudin paracellular-leak water following), ~80-90 % of bicarbonate (via NHE3 apical H⁺ secretion × luminal carbonic-anhydrase IV/II CO₂-shuttle chemistry and basolateral AE1 Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchange — the acetazolamide-able step ([[renal/acid-base-disorders]] and [[#ex-fanconi]]), essentially ALL filtered glucose (SGLT2 then SGLT1 finish — [[#ex-tm]]), all amino acids and lactate and citrate (the citrate-bicarbonate calcium-stone lever — [[renal/urinary-tract]]), ~85 % of phosphate (the NaPi-IIa/IIc endocytic downregulation by PTH and FGF23 — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]'s hormone axis), most urate (the reabsorb-secrete-reabsorb net that makes urate handling paradoxically retention-prone — the low-dose-salicylate and pyrazinamide and losartan-and-SGLT2 urate effects — [[renal/urinary-tract]]'s gout interface), the low-molecular-weight proteins and vitamins (cubilin-megalin — [[#def-transport]]), and calcium and magnesium paracellularly (the claudin-2/17 solvent-drag pathway — distinct from the loop's voltage-driven machinery)).** The proximal tubule is also **the injury-vulnerable segment (the ischaemia-and-toxin hotspot — the muddy-brown-granular-casts origin ([[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]) and the hypoxia-and-drug-and-heavy-metal-and-viral target (the aminoglycoside-and-tenofovir-and-ifosfamide-and-cisplatin-and-aristolochic-acid list — [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]] and [[pathology/environmental-pathology]])) and the site of the Fanconi syndrome ([[#ex-fanconi]]) and the type-2 (proximal) renal tubular acidosis (the bicarbonate-threshold collapse and the ≤5.5-acidifiable-urine-when-plasma-bicarbonate-is-low hallmark — [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]).**
:::

::: definition Loop, distal tubule and the diluting-and-concentrating machinery {#def-loop}
**The thick ascending limb (TAL) is the diluting segment and the generator: NKCC2 (the loop-diuretic target) takes up Na-K-2Cl; ROMK's potassium back-leak plus ClC-Kb's chloride exit make the lumen positive (+5 to +10 mV), driving paracellular Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ reabsorption (the claudin-16/19 pores — hence loop diuretics waste calcium and magnesium and cause the hypomagnesaemia that perpetuates hypokalaemia ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]])); the water-impermeable TAL deposits solute in the medulla without water — the single-effect engine of the countercurrent multiplier.** **The thin limbs and vasa recta complete the medullary architecture (the passive NaCl-exit descending-limb permeabilities and the urea UT-A1/A3 collecting-duct deposition under ADH — recycled by the UT-B vasa recta — the urea contributing up to half the inner-medullary osmolality ([[#def-concentrate]])), and the juxtaglomerular-short-loop nephrons' division of labour (the cortex nephrons reabsorb, the juxtamedullary concentrate — the surgical and ischaemic medullary vulnerability ([[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]'s papillary-necrosis row and the analgesic-nephropathy ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]])).** **The distal convoluted tubule: the NCC (thiazide-target) reabsorbs NaCl electrogenically in a water-impermeable tube — further dilution — with transcellular calcium reabsorption (the apical TRPV5 and calbindin-D28k and basolateral NCX1 — PTH- and vitamin-D-tuned and thiazide-enhanced: why thiazides lower urinary calcium (the stone-prevention use ([[renal/urinary-tract]]) and loops raise it; and why the Gitelman and Bartter split below ([[#ex-bartter-gitelman]])). The collecting duct: the aldosterone-tuned principal cell (ENaC-apical-and-pump-basolateral electrogenicity drives K⁺-secretion through BK/ROMK ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]) and — with ADH's V2-cAMP-PKA aquaporin-2 insertion (the apical water gate — the final urine concentration step, the Liddle- and AME- and licorice- and renal-pseudohypoaldosteronism-type-1 sodium-transport diseases ([[renal/water-sodium]]) and the diabetes-insipidus V2R-and-AQP2 mutations and lithium-ENaC-entry and acquired-hypercalcaemia-and-hypokalaemia nephrogenic forms ([[#ex-di]]) — [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary])) and the intercalated cells (the type-A H⁺-ATPase-and-H⁺/K⁺-ATPase acid secretion and AE1 basolateral exit, and type-B pendrin bicarbonate secretion — the distal-acidosis-and-alkalosis biology of [[renal/acid-base-disorders]] and pendrin's biology and Pendred syndrome's ear-and-thyroid linkage ([[endocrine/thyroid]] and [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]])), plus the urea- and water-permeability ADH-switch that lets the medulla be used ([[#def-concentrate]]) and the tonicity-response-elements-and-HIF-and-osmotic-stress genes ([[cell-biochemistry/signalling-cell-cycle]]).**
:::

::: definition Concentrating the urine: the countercurrent system and ADH {#def-concentrate}
**The corticomedullary osmotic gradient (cortex ~300 → inner medulla up to 1200-1400 mOsm/kg) is built and maintained by: (i) the active single effect — TAL NaCl (and urea) deposition ([[#def-loop]]); (ii) the countercurrent multiplier — the hairpin geometry turning repeated small single-effects into an exponential vertical gradient (the passive-model of the thin limbs' salt-diffusion and water-abstraction amplifying); (iii) urea recycling — ADH-regulated UT-A1/A3 inner-medullary urea exit depositing ~400-500 mOsm of the interstitial total with UT-B vasa-recta urea-recycling-and-recirculation (and the species-difference in urea-dependence); and (iv) the vasa recta countercurrent exchanger — the low-permeability, hairpin-capillary trick that supplies the medulla without washing the gradient out (the medullary-blood-flow-and-loop-diuretic-and-solute-diuresis gradient-washout clinical levers — the NSAIDs'-and-ageing'-and-sickle-cell'-and-hypokalaemia's concentrating defects and the papillary-necrosis list ([[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]).** ADH (the [[renal/water-sodium]] controller) then decides whether the terminal tubule equilibrates with that stack: **absent ADH → water-impermeable collecting duct delivers ~50-100 mOsm urine (maximal dilution; up to 18 L/day capacity); maximal ADH → AQP2 throughout and UT-A1 urea permeability → 1200+ mOsm (≈0.5 L/day minimum to clear the 600-mOsm daily solute load ([[#def-concentrate]]) — and every step's failure shows in the water-deprivation-and-desmopressin-and-copeptin algorithms of [[#ex-di]] and [[renal/water-sodium]]'s hyponatraemia-and-hypernatraemia chapters; the age-related and sickle-cell and chronic-kidney-disease-isosthenuria and the loop-diuretic concentrating washout ([[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]'s nocturia-and-fixed-specific-gravity findings) complete the clinical reading.**
:::

## The tubular syndromes

::: example The global proximal leak {#ex-fanconi}
A 38-year-old on tenofovir-containing antiretroviral therapy has bone pain and weakness: urine glucose 3+ with a normal plasma glucose, phosphate clearance raised (Tm phosphate low), bicarbonate 16 with a normal anion gap and urine pH 5.2, potassium 2.9, urate very low, and 1+ proteinuria with a normal albumin-specific reading; the child on another ward with the same constellation has rickets and failure to thrive.
::: solution
**Fanconi syndrome — the generalized proximal-reabsorption failure ([[#def-proximal]]): glycosuria-with-normal-glycaemia (the Tm collapse), phosphaturia-and-hypophosphataemic-osteomalacia/rickets (the vitamin-D-and-bone axes — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]] and [[endocrine/bone-calcium]]), type-2 (proximal) RTA (the bicarbonate-threshold defect with an acidifiable urine once plasma falls below threshold — [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]), hypouricaemia-with-high-urate-clearance, aminoaciduria, low-molecular-weight proteinuria and bicarbonaturia-driven K-wasting ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]) — plus secondary hyperaldosteronism from volume contraction ([[renal/water-sodium]]).** Aetiologies and management: **drugs (the tenofovir-and-ifosfamide-and-cisplatin-and-valproate-and-deferasirox-and-aminoglycoside list ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]) and the outdated tetracycline (the expired-doxycline-expirant myth-history)), inherited (the cystinosis (the commonest paediatric cause — the growth-failure and hypothyroid and photophobia corneal-crystal multi-organ picture ([[paediatrics/congenital-genetic]] and [[endocrine/thyroid]]), Wilson ([[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]) and Lowe and mitochondrial and galactose and fructose (the dietary-exclusion enzymes — [[cell-biochemistry/energy-metabolism]]-adjacent) disorders), and the dysproteinaemic (the myeloma light-chain tubulopathy ([[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]])). Treatment: stop-or-dose-reduce the drug (the tenofovir-alafenamide-or-other-agent switch decision with infectious-disease colleagues — [[infectious-diseases/hiv]]), large-dose bicarbonate replacement (the paradoxical-acidosis-if-under-dosed alkalinisation and the thiazide-volume-augmentation trick), phosphate-and-calcitriol for the bone disease, potassium-sparing strategies, and (for cystinosis) the cysteamine treatment era ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]-and-[[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]]-adjacent).**
:::
:::

::: example Two salt-wasters, two transporters {#ex-bartter-gitelman}
Both patients have hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis with normal blood pressure and high renin-and-aldosterone: (a) a 12-year-old with polyuria, nephrocalcinosis, high urine calcium; (b) a 26-year-old with cramps, low serum magnesium and low urine calcium. Two more adults on the ward take diuretics for weight-control.
::: solution
**(a) Bartter syndrome (mutations in NKCC2, ROMK, ClC-Kb or barttin — the loop-of-Henle programme): the loop-diuretic phenotype written into the genome — salt-wasting-with-high-urinary-calcium (the lost lumen-positive voltage ([[#def-loop]])) and prostaglandin-driven polyuria-and-fever (the antenatal hydropic variant's prematurity) and nephrocalcinosis; (b) Gitelman (the NCC loss — the thiazide phenotype): the hypomagnesaemia-hypocalciuria signature (the TRPV5-upregulation and claudin-trio biology ([[#def-loop]]) and the normal-or-low urine calcium distinguishing it from Bartter — the calcium-ratio discriminates-both shortcut). Both: renin-angiotensin activation without hypertension (the salt-wasting defence) and the treatment (salt-and-potassium-supplementation and spironolactone-or-amiloride ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]) and, for Bartter, the prostaglandin-synthesis-inhibitor strategy (indomethacin, with the prostaglandin-inhibition caveats) and the overnight-nephrology-paediatrics-shared-care ([[paediatrics/growth-development]])). The diuretic abusers (the covert-loop and thiazide users — often within the eating-disorder sphere ([[psychiatry/eating-personality-disorders]])) reproduce Bartter or Gitelman exactly; the discriminators are the urine-diuretic-screen (the timed-testing and liquid-chromatography panel), the urine calcium pattern, and the persistence of alkalosis-and-wasting after supervised withdrawal ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]) — hence the differential-diagnosis caution below.**
:::
:::

::: warning Before you label a tubular syndrome {#warn-tubular}
The Bartter-versus-Gitelman urine-calcium reasoning requires ruling out **the pharmacological mimics (loop and thiazide use — including the breastfeeding-mother's and food-fraud and Munchausen-by-proxy possibilities) and the vomiting/diarrhoea-with-diuretics combination**; the urine-diuretic screen and a witnessed-in-hospital-withdrawal-and-electrolyte-trajectory settle it ([[psychiatry/substance-use]]-adjacent — [[psychiatry/eating-personality-disorders]]). Likewise, **never call polyuria "psychogenic" without formal testing** — desmopressin given to a primary-polydipsia patient risks water-intoxication hyponatraemia, and water-deprivation in true diabetes insipidus risks hypernatraemia; the supervised, weight-and-Na-monitored protocol ([[#ex-di]] and [[renal/water-sodium]]) and the copeptin era ([[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]]) exist because both errors are common and injurious.
:::

::: example The double-craniotomy thirst {#ex-di}
Day 3 after severe head injury and pituitary-region surgery, a patient puts out 600 mL/h of clear urine; serum sodium climbs 138 → 154 in 12 hours, potassium is normal, plasma osmolality 322 and urine osmolality 110; thirst is intact and he is drinking, but sedation limits intake.
::: solution
**Central diabetes insipidus (the posterior-pituitary ADH-outflow failure — [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]]'s neurohypophysis anatomy and the post-surgical-and-post-traumatic three-pattern timing: the transient, the triphasic (with the SIADH overshoot phase and its dangerous fluid-switching) and the permanent — the day-3-to-day-7 window is classic) with the diagnostic core values met ([[#def-concentrate]]): hypernatraic hyperosmolality with inappropriately dilute urine — the water-deprivation test unnecessary when the spontaneous plasma osmolality already exceeds the osmostat (the confirmatory logic of [[#warn-tubular]]) and the copeptin (the ADH-co-secretion-marker) or post-desmopressin urine-osmolality response (>10 %? — the >450-and->50 %-rise bands of the modern algorithms) distinguishes central from nephrogenic ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]-and-hypercalcaemia-and-lithium nephrogenic causes in the differential).** Management: **the desmopressin route-and-dose (the intranasal-or-IV-or-oral and the "let it escape once a day" test to avoid water-intoxication hyponatraemia ([[renal/water-sodium]]) and the as-required-not-scheduled principle), free-water replacement guided by hourly sodium and weight (the calculated water deficit and the <10-12 mmol/24h correction discipline for chronic hypernatraemia — the cerebral-odema trade ([[renal/water-sodium]]'s own osmotic-demyelination-and-cerebral-oedema caution)), the oral-intake-and-nurse-input-chart with the clear-fluid-balance-sheet as the safety system, the access-to-water-and-toilet documentation and the monitoring of the triphasic switch (the day-5-to-8 SIADH-phase hyponatraemia trap — the sodium-trajectory graph kept at the bedside ([[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]])), and the long-term map (the anterior-pituitary retesting and the copeptin-prognostic and the desmopressin-card and dilute-urine-annual-urological-overlap ([[renal/urinary-tract]]) and the driving-and-travel-and-intercurrent-illness safety teaching ([[endocrine/endocrine-emergencies]])).**
:::

::: quiz
A patient takes a loop diuretic chronically. Which combination of urinary changes is expected?
- [ ] Low urine calcium, low urine magnesium
- [x] High urine calcium and magnesium, high urine potassium, low urine chloride
- [ ] High urine calcium, low urine potassium
- [ ] Unchanged calcium, high urine bicarbonate
::: solution
**Loop diuretics abolish NKCC2 and with it the lumen-positive voltage that drives paracellular Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ reabsorption ([[#def-loop]]) — calciuria-magnesuria follow, and distal Na⁺-and-flow-delivery raises K⁺ secretion ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]). The low urine chloride is the contraction-and-chloride-depletion state's floor (and the urine chloride's diagnostic use in alkalosis workup ([[renal/acid-base-disorders]]) — with the diuretic-presence caveat: recent dosing falsely raises it during action. Thiazides invert the calcium half of this (the Gitelman and stone-prevention physiology — [[renal/urinary-tract]]).**
:::

::: history
**Homer W. Smith's** reabsorptive-physiology synthesis and the micropuncture demonstrations of glomerulotubular balance; the **Ussing model of the polarized epithelium (1954)** — basolateral pump, selective apical doors ([[physiology/membrane-potentials]]-adjacent); the **countercurrent-multiplier controversy and its 1960s triumph (the Gottschalk-and-Ney micropuncture osmolality measurements and the Epstein-and-Malnic groups' stop-flow and free-perfusion evidence)**; the **calcium-and-magnesium voltage-physiology of the ascending limb and the 1990s channel-cloning era (the Hebert laboratory's NKCC2-and-claudin programs)**; **Bartter's 1962 juxtaglomerular-apparatus-hyperplasia syndrome and Gitelman's 1966 normotensive-hypokalaemia description**, unified in **the positional-cloning decade (1995-2000) that turned both into transporter genetics** ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]); the **aquaporin revolution (the Preston-and-Agre aquaporin-1 cloning of 1992, the Nielsen-Knepp aquaporin-2 trafficking work, and the 2003 Nobel Prize)**; **Liddle syndrome (1963) explained by ENaC gain-of-function in the 1990s**, with **the apparent-mineralocorticoid-excess-and-licorice 11β-HSD2 story (the 1988-1996 Stewart-and-Shackleton era)** and **the pseudohypoaldosteronism-and-Gordon-syndrome WNK-kinase era (the Lifton laboratory's 2001 WNK discovery)**; the **SGLT2 cloning (1998-2000) that led, via phlorizin's old chemistry, to the gliflozin diabetes revolution ([[endocrine/diabetes]])**; and **copeptin's rise as the stable ADH-co-secretion biomarker (the 2000s-2010s diagnostic trials, [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]])** complete the tubule's journey from physiology to bedside gene-and-drug medicine.

::: exercise Why the urine is dilute in polydipsia {level=2}
A psychiatric inpatient drinking 12 L/day has urine osmolality 60 mOsm/kg and normal sodium. Why is the dilute urine appropriate here, and why must the habit not be corrected abruptly?
::: solution
**Appropriate because ADH is correctly suppressed by the low-normal tonicity — the diluting segments (the TAL and DCT — [[#def-loop]]) are generating the maximal free-water clearance the nephron can (~1 L/h); the kidney is doing its job against intake, not failing ([[#def-concentrate]]) — and the polyuria is also washing out the medullary gradient (the washout clause), briefly capping concentrating power. Correction must be gradual: the chronic water-load has driven cerebral cell-volume adaptation ([[renal/water-sodium]]'s cerebral-adaptation lesson and [[psychiatry/psychiatric-assessment]] for the primary-polydipsia/psychogenic driver and its antipsychotic-thirst mechanisms ([[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]])), and abrupt water-restriction unmasks a transient concentrating deficit, while rapid sodium rise risks osmotic demyelination in the mixed-chronicity picture ([[renal/water-sodium]], [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]). Structured fluid-restriction with weights-and-sodium monitoring, behavioural support and medication review is the safe programme.**
:::

::: summary
- The tubule is a polarized-epithelium machine powered by the basolateral sodium pump: segment-specific apical carriers (SGLT, NHE3, NKCC2, NCC, ENaC) decide what returns, and the organic-anion/cation secretion systems handle the body's acids, bases and drugs.
- Reabsorption is saturable (Tm and splay — the glycosuria threshold) and gradient-limited; the proximal tubule reclaims two-thirds of everything, and its global failure is Fanconi syndrome.
- The ascending limb builds the medullary osmotic stack (countercurrent multiplier, urea recycling, protected by the vasa recta) that ADH-driven aquaporin-2 later exploits — the concentrating range from 50 to 1200 mOsm is water-balance's workspace.
- Each transporter has a drug and a disease: loops-NKCC2-Bartter, thiazides-NCC-Gitelman, ENaC-Liddle-AME, AQP2-V2-diabetes insipidus — the genetic and pharmacological phenotypes mirror each other.
- Polyuria and hypokalaemic-salt-wasting workups are transporter-localisation exercises: urine calcium, magnesium, chloride and the diuretic screen do the diagnosing before any gene does.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Power the pump {level=1}
Why is Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition (digoxin) and ATP depletion (ischaemia) proximally toxic, and name two reabsorptive consequences.
::: solution
**The pump sets the inward Na⁺ gradient that powers nearly every apical entry step ([[#def-transport]]): ischaemia collapses Na⁺-glucose (SGLT), Na⁺/H⁺ exchange (bicarbonate) and NaPi (phosphate) reabsorption — the Fanconi-like-and-ATP-and-glutathione ([[cell-biochemistry/energy-metabolism]])-and-cytoskeletal ([[pathology/cell-injury]]) failure cascade whose morphological signature (the brush-border loss and the ATP-and-MDH ([[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]'s enzyme-leakage history) and the muddy-granular-casts and the glycosuria-and-aminoaciduria-and-phosphaturia reappear (the [[#ex-fanconi]] constellation in ischaemic form).**
:::
:::

::: exercise Acid and the brush border {level=1}
Which fraction of filtered bicarbonate does acetazolamide block, where, and why does the drug's acidosis self-limit?
::: solution
**The proximal NHE3/carbonic-anhydrase system (~85 % of reclamation — [[#def-proximal]]): CA inhibition starves luminal CO₂-shuttle H⁺ generation. Self-limits because plasma bicarbonate falls to the reduced threshold (~18-20 mmol/L) — below it filtration-delivery itself limits; distal acidification is intact so urine can still be acidified at steady state (the [[renal/acid-base-disorders]] type-2-RTA distinction and the cystic-fibrosis-and-carbonic-anhydrase-II-deficiency rarities), with the therapeutic uses (the glaucoma-and-idiopathic-intracranial-hypertension ([[nervous-system/headache-pain]])-and-altitude-and-alkalosis-and-epilepsy ([[nervous-system/epilepsy]]-adjacent) applications).**
:::
:::

::: exercise Trace the voltage {level=2}
Explain step-by-step how a NKCC2 mutation causes hypokalaemia (three mechanisms) and hypomagnesaemia-with-hypocalciuria's absence (why Bartter wastes calcium but Gitelman does not).
::: solution
**Hypokalaemia: (i) distal Na⁺-and-flow-delivery raises ENaC-coupled K⁺ secretion ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]); (ii) secondary hyperaldosteronism from salt-wasting further drives it; (iii) the metabolic alkalosis ([[renal/acid-base-disorders]]) shifts potassium intracellularly and perpetuates secretion. Calcium/magnesium: NKCC2's lost lumen-positive voltage switches OFF paracellular Ca-Mg reabsorption (calciuria — the nephrocalcinosis), whereas Gitelman's DCT lesion leaves the TAL voltage intact and enhances TRPV5-distal-calcium-reabsorption ([[#def-loop]]) — hypocalciuria plus hypomagnesaemia (the DCT's Mg pathway via TRPM6 ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]])) mark it; the urine calcium-to-creatinine ratio and magnesium thus localise the transporter before the gene panel ([[renal/filtration]]'s-quantification discipline applied).**

::: exercise Do the water arithmetic {level=2}

::: exercise Lithium's two tubular injuries {level=2}
Give the mechanism of lithium-induced nephrogenic diabetes insipidus and of lithium's chronic tubulointerstitial lesion, and the management levers.
::: solution

::: exercise Localise the glycosuria {level=2}
Glycosuria with normal plasma glucose: build the differential (four mechanisms) with one discriminating test each.
::: solution
**(i) Proximal tubular disease (Fanconi — [[#ex-fanconi]]: generalized low-Tm leak with phosphate-and-bicarbonate-and-urate co-leaks and aminoaciduria); (ii) isolated renal-glucosuria (SLC5A2/SGLT2 loss — benign and pregnancy-unmasked ([[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]); (iii) drugs (the SGLT2 inhibitors (the therapeutic glycosuria ([[endocrine/diabetes]])) and the ifosfamide-and-tenofovir drug list ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]); (iv) the rare developmental causes (the Fanconi-Bickel GLUT2 glycogen-storage disease — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]] and [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]])). Discriminators: full proximal-function panel (phosphate and urate and bicarbonate and LMW-proteins), the drug history, the genetics only when isolated-and-persistent — and always exclude the obvious first: an unrecognised diabetes or a laboratory mishandled sample ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]).**
