The gastrointestinal tract rewards surgeons who think in physiology. **Upper and lower GI surgery is a small set of operations applied to a long list of diseases: acid-peptic disease now medicalised except at its complications, biliary and pancreatic disease organised around drainage and resection, inflammatory bowel disease balanced between excision and preservation, colorectal cancer staged and totalised and staged again, and benign anorectal disease where function is everything. In every corner the principle is the same - control the source, preserve the function, and respect the anastomosis's dependence on blood supply, tension, and infection.**

::: summary
- Upper GI surgery is now mostly cancer, drainage and complications; anti-reflux and ulcer surgery survive only where their indications are rigorously proven.
- Colorectal cancer is run by multidisciplinary staging, quality-defined resection (TME, CME, node counts) and adjuvant decisions; the anastomosis is its vulnerability.
- Anastomotic integrity is a blood-supply-tension-sepsis problem, judged intraoperatively and surveilled on the day-3-7 clock with protocolised rescue.
- IBD surgery preserves bowel and function where possible; failed pouches and fistulising Crohn's belong in high-volume centres with a named plan.
- Stomas succeed or fail in design and support: marking, teaching, output management and reversal planning prevent most readmissions.
- Enhanced recovery, leak bundles, MDT governance and outcome audit are what convert individual technique into reliably safe surgery.

## Taking it further

The visceral reminder for surgical trainees: every decision in this chapter recurs in a patient you will operate on at two in the morning without the consultant, so the algorithms worth memorising are the ones that decide when not to operate - the contained perforation managed conservatively, the obstruction that gets a nasogastric tube and a computed-tomography scan before a knife, the postoperative patient whose tachycardia and oliguria are a leak until the imaging says otherwise. Complication rates reward the surgeon who calls the patient back early and punishes the one who protects the plan. When in doubt, the two questions that keep patients alive are always answerable: is there a collection, and is there ischaemia.
