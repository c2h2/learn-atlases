Trauma care is time organised against biology. **Major trauma kills in three peaks - the immediate haemorrhage and airway catastrophes, the early preventable bleeds and head injuries, and the late septic and organ-failure deaths - so the whole discipline is an argument about sequencing: primary survey over secondary detail, haemorrhage control before imaging, the head and the airway before the limb that frightens relatives, and damage control physiology instead of heroic anatomy when the patient is cold, acidotic and coagulopathic.** From the pre-hospital scene to the trauma-network rehabilitation ward, every minute has a designated job.

::: summary
- Trauma mortality comes in three peaks; the first two are fought with systems (pre-hospital triage, golden-hour surgery, networks) and the third with ICU and ward discipline.
- Primary survey is resuscitation: catastrophic haemorrhage first, airway and breathing decisions before imaging, exposure with warming.
- Bleeding with a soft abdomen and an unstable pelvis is a sequencing problem - CT only for responders, blood not crystalloid, TXA early, calcium and warmth while deciding.
- Damage control surgery trades definitive repair for physiological rescue; the planned second look is the operation, not an appointment.
- The head tolerates no secondary insults: one hypoxic or hypotensive episode doubles the stakes of every later decision.
- The reconstruction and rehabilitation phase determines whether survival is worth living; it begins in the ICU, with the team that will follow the patient for years.
:::
