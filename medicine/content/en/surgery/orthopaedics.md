Bones need biology, mechanics and time. **Orthopaedics is the surgical speciality of the musculoskeletal system: fractures reduced and held while biology repairs them, joints replaced when cartilage fails, infections drained before bone dies, tumours resected with margins that preserve limbs and lives, and the paediatric and neuromuscular deformities corrected before growth finishes. Its two great weapons - fixation and movement - are in constant tension, and its two great teachers are the radiograph and the complication meeting.**

::: summary
- Fracture treatment balances mechanical stability against biological preservation; the implant strategy follows blood supply, soft tissues and the patient's skeleton, not fashion.
- Compartment syndrome, infected joints, open fractures, vascular injury, cauda equina and the slipped epiphysis are orthopaedic emergencies defined by hours.
- Arthroplasty succeeds through patient selection, optimisation, precision, infection prevention and lifelong registry-grade surveillance; infection is its chief adversary and cultures come before antibiotics.
- Bone tumours are managed by networks: image, stage and biopsy where the definitive surgeon can see the plan, because one unplanned excision can cost a limb.
- Children are not small adults: growth plates, remodelling potential and surveillance programmes (DDH, Perthes, SCFE, the cerebral palsy hip) decide outcomes.
- Osteoporotic fracture is a sentinel systemic event; every fragility fracture is an unfinished conversation about bone protection and falls.
