The breast clinic is where cancer medicine is practised most humanely or most cruelly. **Breast and endocrine surgery spans the highest-volume cancer pathway in women and a set of small-organ operations - thyroid, parathyroid, adrenal - where millimetre anatomy and calcium and adrenaline physiology decide whether a day-case becomes a critical incident. The breast unit model - one-stop clinics, triple assessment, multidisciplinary decisions, nurse-led navigation and reconstruction offered as standard - is one of the most successful reorganisations in modern surgery, and its lessons (pathways, named people, measured outcomes) travel.**

::: summary
- Triple assessment means clinic, imaging and pathology agree; when they do not, the discordant pathway (re-review, biopsy, named short-interval follow-up) is the safety net that prevents most missed cancers.
- Breast cancer surgery is now conservation-plus-systemic-therapy for most; sentinel node biopsy and targeted axillary management replaced routine dissection, with lymphoedema numbers in the consent.
- Reconstruction is treatment; offering and delivering it (implant or autologous) with plastics in the MDT is a service standard.
- B3 and indeterminate pathology gets excision, not reassurance; genomic assays guide chemo de-escalation and must be timed before decisions crystallise.
- Thyroid and parathyroid surgery is judged on recurrent-laryngeal nerve rates, hypocalcaemia systems and volume-outcome referral discipline.
- Adrenal and incidental endocrine findings demand biochemical primacy - screens before surgery, alpha before beta, and shared medical-surgical ownership.
:::
