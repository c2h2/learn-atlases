The acute abdomen is a race between pattern recognition and perforation. **An acute abdomen is surgical inflammation, obstruction, ischaemia or bleeding until excluded: the history and examination still localise more reliably than any scan, the blood tests and imaging narrow rather than decide, and the central clinical question is never 'what is it' but 'does this abdomen need a surgeon in the next hours' - because the conditions that kill (perforation, ischaemic bowel, strangulated obstruction, ruptured aneurysm, necrotising infection) declare themselves early to anyone watching for physiology, and the ones that mimic (medical abdomens, functional pain, gynaecological and retroperitoneal impostors) reward patience only when the dangerous ones are being actively excluded.**

**The acute abdomen was surgery's coming-of-age: before the 1880s the inflamed appendix and the strangulated hernia were death-sentences argued over by physicians who bleeding and purged (the 'typhlitis' and 'ilacus' literature and the post-mortem-diagnosis culture), and the field opens with the appendicectomy era - the 1886 Fitz definition and the McBurney and 1890s operative case-series papers that named the disease and its incision and the operation's migration from parlour to hospital, with the great-people-death-anecdotes (the 1902 Edward VII operation before the coronation and Freud's 1923 appendicectomy that made the disease a household phrase) converting appendicitis into a public literacy campaign and the 'promise of a safe operation' that made emergency surgery routine; the strangulated-hernia line - the the femoral-hernia mortality concentrated in thin elderly women and the hernia-campaigns that taught the truss-is-not-a-treatment lesson and the 'never sleep on an irreducible hernia' aphorism; the peritonitis and sepsis science - the 1890s-1930s animal-model peritonitis physiology and the sulfonamide-then-penicillin conversion of suppurative appendicitis from fatal to Tuesday, the 1930s-50s fluid-and-electrolyte revolution (the Darrow-and-Gamble-and-Elman-era saline-and-potassium-and-parenteral-nutrition-adjacency and the postoperative-ileus-and-'surgical-shock' fluid dogmas whose errors the 1960s-70s metabolic-response school (the Kinney-and-Cuthbertson metabolic-response studies) and then the enhanced-recovery movement corrected), the mesenteric-ischaemia-identity-crisis - the 1920s-Sudeck-point-anatomy and the 1950s-'intestinal-angina' descriptions and the 1966-Marston-era-clustering-and-the-Wiley-and-Boley-CT-era-triage with its undiminished mortality that stays the acute abdomen's most honest embarrassment; the imaging-and-laparoscopy-revolutions - the free-air-radiology-era and the 1970s-80s-CT-and-then-the-1990s-2000s multislice-CT that made the acute abdomen a protocol question and the diagnostic-and-therapeutic-laparoscopy-migration-of-appendicectomy-and-cholecystectomy-and-adhesiolysis-and-perforated-ulcer-repair and the antibiotic-first-appendicitis-trials returning 1950s conservatism in evidence-based clothing; and the modern system reckoning - the 2010s-2020s National-Emergency-Laparotomy-audits (the UK and Irish reports showing elderly-frail-patients-operated-late-with-poor-outcomes-and-the-'theatre-at-night-versa-business-hours'-outlier-hunting and the perioperative-medicine-and-geriatric-co-management-responses), the CT-negative-death-reviews that founded the re-examination-ownership rules and the 'time-to-theatre-for-strangulation-is-a-metric-not-a-myth' campaigns, the damage-control and open-abdomen techniques borrowed from war surgery, and the stoma-and-bowel-dysfunction-and-long-term-outcome-research that finally asked survivors what the emergency had cost them, because the acute abdomen's history ends in the question every audit re-asks: was the operation late, was it avoidable, and was the person involved in the choice - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
:::

::: summary
- The acute abdomen is a physiology problem wearing a pain costume: trends in observations, lactate and clinical examination decide more than any single test.
- Four categories drive mortality - obstruction (strangulation clock), perforation, ischaemia (the silent one), and atypical sepsis - and each has a named early signature.
- A normal CT in a peritonitic patient is a re-examination order, not a discharge note; observation needs owners, intervals and escalation criteria.
- The elderly, pregnant, immunosuppressed and medicated present blunted and hide surgical catastrophe inside 'medical' presentations.
- Source control is a clock: antibiotics, fluids, imaging and theatre each have target times, and the audits keep showing the delays cluster in the frail.
- Every acute abdomen ends as either an operation, a documented pathway of watching, or a values-based ceiling conversation - the error is always the unowned watch.
:::
