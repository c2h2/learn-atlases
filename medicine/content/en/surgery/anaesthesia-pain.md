Anaesthesia is the safest branch of medicine and the least forgiving. **Anaesthetic practice is pharmacology delivered to the boundary of consciousness and airway reflexes, monitored continuously, reversed deliberately: the preoperative airway and cardiac assessment, the agents and their haemodynamic and respiratory trade-offs, regional techniques that spare opioids and save enhanced recovery, the management of the crisis minutes (difficult airway, anaphylaxis, malignant hyperthermia, local-anaesthetic toxicity), and the postoperative pain medicine that determines whether patients mobilise, breathe and leave.** This chapter owns the physiology between induction and recovery room discharge.

- General anaesthesia is four drug-classes plus monitoring; context-sensitive half-times, reversal rules and discharge standards are the arithmetic that prevents late harm.
- Regional and multimodal analgesia is the ERAS backbone; neuraxial and fascial-plane techniques carry real benefits governed by anticoagulation timing and local-anaesthetic dose ceilings.
- LAST presents as neurological then cardiovascular collapse; intralipid and adapted ALS save lives when named early.
- A predicted difficult airway fails when anticipation does not become a rehearsed, positioned, help-present plan with a surgical rescue available.
- Epidural plus anticoagulant is a timing disease: haematoma recognition is an emergency MRI and decompression question, never 'wait and see'.
- Anaesthesia safety is an archive of converted disasters: monitors, guidelines, checklists and confidential enquiries each grew from a specific preventable death.
