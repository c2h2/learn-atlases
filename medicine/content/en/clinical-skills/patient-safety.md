Patient safety is the youngest medical discipline and the only one whose raw material is the institution's own failures: it borrowed its ideas from aviation and high-reliability engineering - the just culture, the systems view, human-factors design, checklists, the hierarchy-of-controls for hazards - and discovered that medicine had been running a vast experimental programme on patients without logging the adverse events. **This chapter covers the epidemiology and taxonomy of harm (the one-in-ten-hospitalised-patients figures and their measurement politics, the preventable proportion, never-events and the second-guessing of 'unavoidable' complications), the science of why good people do dangerous things (the attention and memory and judgement properties the human-factors literature maps, the error-trapping that defences should catch, and the Swiss-cheese accident model), the improvement toolkit that actually moves numbers (the significant-event and root-cause analyses with their just-culture discipline, the incident-reporting systems and their learning-loops, FMEA-before-the-launch, the checklist and bundle evidence, the standardisation-and-design hierarchy over exhortation), and the hardest competencies: speaking up and listening (the graded-assertiveness and CUS phrases, the safety-hierarchy-paradox of flatter teams, the duty of candour and the apology), the human cost of harm to clinicians (the second-victim literature), and the measurement-and-governance layer (the harm indicators, the mortality-funnel politics, and why 'no adverse events reported' is itself an alarm).**

## The terrain

::: definition The epidemiology and systems model of harm {#def-epidemiology}
**The numbers and their politics (one-in-ten-hospitalised-patients-harmed' meta-analyses from the 2018 BMJ quality-of-care reviews and their predecessors - the Harvard-METS study's 3.7-percent-adverse-event-rate-and-28-percent-preventable-1991-bombshell, the Queensland and Utah colour-coded chart reviews, the British prevalence surveys (the 2004-planning-versus-measurement debate) which found that the true rate is higher than the complaint rate and that measurement itself is an intervention; the harm taxonomy (the adverse-event-versus-near-miss-versus-no-harm-but-error-was-made definitions because near misses are the free data, the A-E-injury-severity-grades and avoided' versus 'unavoidable' complication debate that clinicians fight because every chart-review flag felt like an accusation at the time it was raised), the never-events-and-serious-harm-indices (the wrong-site-surgery-and-wrong-implant-and-overdose-and-retained-foreign-objects-lists which the health-services treat as system-failure sentinel findings and which still happen at a rate that refuses to reach zero even in the best systems - how is that possible' question that produces each generation's safety barriers) and the harm distribution: medication errors as the volume king (every-step-of-the-medication-lifecycle has a documented failure mode' reality the pharmacology course quotes back), healthcare-associated infections (the device-associated-and-surgical-belt-bundle evidence base), venous thromboembolism and falls and pressure injuries (nursing-sensitive' label which should read 'system-sensitive'), diagnostic error (diagnosis-is-wrong-in-10-15-percent-of-autopsy-era-and-clinical-decision-support-era-literature' and the misdiagnosis-triads of vascular, infectious and malignant mimics - the cognitive half that this chapter's reasoning chapter meets), and the outpatient-and-community-harm-blind-spot which most incident systems never sampled until the harm-research units forced the issue); the accident model (the Reason-Swiss-cheese: the hazard trajectory through organisational-influences-unsafe-supervision-unsafe-conditions-probabilistic-defences layers, active failures of front-line staff carry the blame and latent conditions carry the cause' inversion which is the entire ethics of the field restated, and the normalisation-of-deviance mechanism the safety-science borrowed from the Challenger inquiry: work-as-imagined-by-the-protocol versus work-as-done-by-people, and the gap is where incidents are filed after); and the resilience correction (Safety-I vs Safety-II' High-Reliability-Organisation literature from the aircraft-carrier and nuclear-submarine studies: most work succeeds because people adapt, so counting only failures misses the adaptations that also create the next incident - which is why the modern methods study work-as-done with qualitative surveys and why 'the unit with the most incident reports is not the most dangerous, it is the one that reports', the sentence that makes incident-data interpretation an expertise in itself).**
:::

## The toolkit

::: definition What works: analysis, design, checklists and just culture {#def-toolkit}
**Analysis methods (the significant-event-or-incident-analysis with its 'what happened, why, what will we do' accountability and the root-cause-analysis and SREB-and-SEOP-adjacent-frameworks whose quality depends on whether the panel is allowed to reach the rota and the procurement-committee-with-Power-to-name-them and contributing factors, not the one cause' honesty; the human-factors taxonomy (the slips-and-lapses-of-action, the memory-lapses, the mistakes-of-planning-and-rule-violations-classified-by-why-the-rule-seemed-wrong-at-the-time: the workloads, the conflicting priorities, the bad interfaces); the proactive methods - FMEA (the failure-modes-and-effects-analysis applied before a new drug-delivery-device-ward-layout-launches with the severity-occurrence-detectability ratings that make the change-list tractable) and pre-mortem' and 'what would have to be true for this to kill someone' question which costs minutes and surfaces the staffing assumption nobody audited; the defences in hierarchy-of-controls order (the safety-engineering import that medicine still underuses: elimination and substitution and engineering controls (the design makes the error impossible or the unsafe act unattractive': the enteral-tube-connector-redesign-that-ended-a-decade-of-wrong-route-kills, the spinal-versus-epidural connector, the insulin-pump-and-glucose-infusion 'hard limits' and tall-man' drug labelling and the automated dispensing-cabinet override-audits and the barcode-medication-administration-and-the look-alike-sound-alike naming fights the BNF and ISMP lists wage) versus administrative controls (the policies and education that change knowledge and not the hazard which is why 're-educate the nurse' as an action plan is the field's most-cited bad habit and why the checklist generation - the WHO surgical-safety-checklist from the 2008 Lancet global study and its 'wrong-site surgery did not vanish but complications fell' evaluations and the checklist works if the culture lets the junior speak' caveat which connects this paragraph to the next) ; the improvement science (the Plan-Do-Study-Act-cycles and run-charts-and-control-charts-from-deming-imported 'improvement-breakthrough' method whose evidence for bundles - the ventilator-bundle's '18-months-to-400-lives' Michigan Keystone narrative and its later methodological re-debate which itself is a case study in reading improvement data - beats single-magazine-trial readings; and adoption and spread' literature (evidence that works, hospitals that cannot implement it' problem the improvement-science authors keep restating: the peer-to-peer and collaborative-models and the fidelity-audits) ; and the culture layer that everything runs on (the just-culture definition most bodies now use: supported for human error and near-miss reporting, coached for at-risk behaviour, sanctioned only for reckless behaviour with the substitution of 'blame the person' for 'fix the system' is itself the reckless behaviour' refinement and the fairness-process research showing clinicians accept outcomes of investigations they helped design); the reporting and learning loop (under-reporting is the biggest measurement error' problem: the anonymous-and-named-blended systems and feedback to the reporter within 30 days' design rule because reports vanish into voids and reporting collapses; the incident-report-ingestion-is-not-learning'-criticism of dashboards that count reports instead of closing loops and the Global-Patient-Safety-Levels-and-PSI-adjacent-indicator sets); and the disclosure half that is also safety work (the duty-of-candour-and-apology-adjacent-statutory-duties-in-the-UK-and-Canada-and-australasia-and-many-US-states: apology is not admission' law, families sue silence, rudeness and stonewalling, not complications' litigation research from I'm sorry it happened' communication-and-disclosure-programs (the University-of-Michigan-ann Arbor 'early offer' model's cost-neutrality findings) and telling patients the truth is a patient-safety intervention because near misses get reported where trust exists - and the candour-tour-of-the-inquest-cases where a sentence said on the ward would have prevented the court file).**
:::

## The people

::: definition Speaking, listening, measuring and the human cost {#def-people}
**The voice-and-hierarchy science (the CRM-derived graded-assertiveness tools - CUS ('I'm uncomfortable / I'm scared / stop'), the two-challenge-rule and DOPE- and SBAR-as-escalation-adjacent-scripts, speaking-up for patient safety' surveys whose findings are cross-cultural regularities: junior status, gender, contract type, night shift and immigration status predict silence and adverse outcomes - hierarchical grading of who may stop a list' as the single most fixable predictor of harm; the brief-and-debrief discipline (time-out as a speech act not a tick-box: the senior who answers the junior's question changes the next decade of speaking-up rates in that theatre' observational studies) and the handover-is-a-safety-barrier' SBAR-SOAP-I-P-Assist-adjacent-standardisations which this course's records-and-handover lesson carries further); the human-error countermeasures for individuals (read-back and teach-back' and pause-before-the-push' and two-nurse-check with the actual-drug-and-actual-calculation' rules which are cheap attention scaffolds not incantations - and the honesty that fatigue-and-deprivation-defeat all of them, which is why the junior-doctor-hours-and-nurse-ratio-and-driving-after-night-shift literature is patient-safety literature wearing occupational-clothes); the measurement layer (the harm-indicator toolkit: the trigger-tool chart reviews (give me the patients who received naloxone or a Vanderbilt-adjacent rescue drug' method for finding harm cheaply), the M&M-mortality-and-morbidity-conferences-that-work (systemic factors first, case-based, senior-junior-order-of-speaking' formats versus trial by audience' versions people avoid) and what matters to patients is not your SIRS rate' patient-reported-experience-measures-adjacent question and the NHS-Numbered-Headline-Indicators and HSHI-adjacent-publication era's two distorting effects (the target-fixation that moves the measurement not the harm (gaming-the-4-hour-wait-and-the-cancer-wait' studies) and the benefit that publication forces - both real, both taught), and the mortality-funnel politics (funnel plots do not prove incompetence, they prompt conversation' Bristol-era lesson from Spiegelhalter and the Sherrif-and-Goldhill-reviews that the profession had to learn twice); the second-victim and clinician-cost literature (the clinician who is a patient after an adverse event' syndrome: the intrusive-replay-and-shame-and-alcohol-and-suicide-risk-data-with-the-surgeon-and-obstetric-and-neonatal-and-anaesthesia-suites-with-their-'I-do-not-deserve-to-practise' narratives and what they need: first a person who says 'this happens and you are not alone', not a form', and the peer-support-and-resilience-in-a-day-programs-with-the-caution-that-resilience-training-blames-victims-if-it-replaces-fixing-the-jagged-system' debate), the moral-injury distinction (I was made to do harm-or-could-not-prevent-it-under-imposed-constraints' injury that the burnout literature separated from exhaustion which matters because the fixes differ: constraint-removal not mindfulness); and the governance and advocacy layer (the board's patient-safety-competency-frameworks ('the risk register reviewed with clinical reality-checks', 'the incident data interrogated for patterns not league-tabled', 'the freedom-to-speak policy audited by surveys of the quietest staff', 'the complaint-and-claim data as a hazard source'), the statutory-inquiry-era (the Cantor-and-Bristol-and-Neale-and-Candour-and-Kirkup-reviews-across-the-British-health-systems: the common finding that 'the concern was raised somewhere, somehow, and nothing moved it to a decision-maker' which is why escalation-and-'duty-to-freedom-to-speak'-protections-exist in law for NHS-adjacent-workers and why the clinician's professional duty (the Good-Medical-Practice-and-GMC-adjacent 'raise concerns and protect patients first, loyalty second' clauses) is examinable behaviour not sentiment); and the field's own limits: harm-measurement's crudeness, the improvement-literature's replication failures, checklist as talisman' critique and the honest conclusion that safety is a sustaining activity - the nearest thing medicine has to hygiene, and as vulnerable to boredom.**
:::

::: example The left that was the right {#ex-wrongsite}
A 54-year-old has her right carpal tunnel released; she had asked twice, in clinic and on admission, that the left (dominant, worse-symptom) hand be done; the hospital's list, consent form, mark and the surgeon's memory all said right, and sign-in' ticked.
::: solution
**Every-cheese-hole-aligned ([[#def-epidemiology]], [[#def-toolkit]]): the anatomy of the error - 'the-mark-placed-by-the-house-officer-on-the-wrong-side-following-a-list-inherited-from-a-clinic-letter-with-the-side-reversed-at-transcription (the data-flow trace that root-cause analyses love) and the consent-form-copied-from-that-list and time-out-executed-as-a-chant: the three people who said 'right' out loud were the three who never met the patient awake and the patient who had been told 'trust us' and was too polite to correct a doctor - the asymmetry the consent literature and this chapter's voice literature both name; the barriers that should have caught it - 'the patient-side-question ('which hand are we operating on?') which the WHO checklist added exactly for this and which only works in theatres where a nurse saying it is thanked; site-marked-in-presence-of-patient-with-their-stated-side' rule; the pre-induction-verification-with-the-imaging-and-the-referral-letter-not-with-each-other's-notes and if two documents disagree, the list stops' hard-stop which is an engineering control dressed as a policy; the response - 'the-duty-of-candour-from-the-ward-not-the-complaints-department (we got it wrong; here is what happens now' conversation with the apology that the law in most jurisdictions protects), the-second-victim-support-for-the-house-officer-who-will-never-forget-this-ward-round, the-significant-event-analysis-without-a-villain-that-reaches-the-transcription-and-rota-latent-conditions (locum-list-typing at 11pm, one-clinic-letter-per-consultant-per-month queue') and the fix-list in hierarchy order: transcription-elimination (structured electronic referral with site as a coded mandatory field confirmed against the patient's words) before re-education; and the audit that tells you it worked: wrong-side never happens here' claim checked by near-miss-reporting rates, because the unit reporting 'we nearly cut the wrong hand four times this year' is the safe one.'**
:::
:::

::: example The insulin that ran overnight {#ex-insulin}
A post-operative ward: the infusion pump's background rate was left running when the syringe was changed for the morning; the 61-year-old is found sweating and confused at handover; glucose 1.9 mmol/L; she seizes in the bay; permanent memory sequelae follow. The incident report says 'staff error, re-educate'.
::: solution
**Hard-limits-not-homilies ([[#def-toolkit]], [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]]): the system trace - 'the-change-the-syringe-and-the-line-clears'-myth (the pump dead-space bolus is an engineering property: the line must be cleared with a separate priming step and the syringe-change protocol and the pump-library-and-bolus-limits are the defences); re-educate' fallacy dissected (the same protocol failure recurs across shifts and hospitals because the hazard is the interface not the hands - education changes knowledge, design changes behaviour' literature in one case); the defences in hierarchy order - 'elimination (the ward-subcutaneous-or-predictable-regimens where possible; substitution: closed-system and pre-filled-syringe-cartridges with hard dose limits and glucose-pump-integration; engineering (dose-error-reduction-software and basal-rate-lockout-and-'new-syringe-means-clear-the-line'-forced-steps and independent-syringe-and-line-check made as easy as skipping it - the two-person 'sign-this-line' card at the pump); process (high-alert-medication-list with infusion safety stops at every handover and meal and transfer and glucose-check-frequency protocol attached to every insulin infusion order and point-of-care-testing QC that this trust failed in the same month); culture (the nurse who queried the pump at 2am and was thanked' survey item which predicts whether next time is caught); and the response - 'the-never-events-list-adjacent-requirement (in several systems insulin-intravenous-error is a reportable serious hazard) makes re-educate' action plan unacceptable to the governance layer - and the candour-and-rehabilitation-package: cognitive-rehabilitation-referral and diabetes-insurance-driving-and-DVLA-adjacent-and-capacity-and-deputyship practicalities that 'complication happened' notes never trigger; and the clinician's parallel case: fund-of-knowledge-and-fatigue review of she-seemed-fine-at-midnight' handover which is this incident's SBAR-shaped lesson.'**
:::

## Where this leads

The reasoning errors behind diagnostic harm are worked through in [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], handover standardisation in [[clinical-skills/records-handover]], the prescribing cascade and high-alert drugs in [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]] and [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], escalation physiology in [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], consent and candour law in [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]] and [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]], and the sepsis and VAP bundles in [[infectious-diseases/healthcare-infections]].

::: history
**Medicine's founding safety promise - do no harm - is older than its evidence base, and the discipline of measuring the gap began with three shocks. First, the epidemic framing: the 1972 Barber-Howie committee's report on adverse events and the 1982 Royal Commission of Inquiry on the Thyroid Hormone affair in Canada and the Desirae and blood-product tragedies taught health systems that iatrogenic harm could be produced in bulk. Then the 1991 Harvard Medical Practice Study and the Australian Queensland and Utah series, published by Brenner, Leape, local groups and the Runciman-Buglass-Bell-Lyons-Silber group, applied chart-trigger tools and found the numbers that made 'how do we know it was safe' a research question, and the 1999 US Institute of Medicine report 'To Err Is Human' - with its 44-98-thousand-annual-preventable-deaths estimate - put the issue on newspaper front pages with a figure the field still argues about. The inquiry era followed: the Bristol neonatal-cardiac and the Royals and Cross and other hospital reviews (the Cantor, Keefer, Neale and More-Cambridge reports), the Mid-Staffordshire Inquiry's 2010 death-toll findings and its 'the regulators were satisfied while patients died' conclusion, and the Kirkup-Grenfell-adjacent era's 'the culture was the cause' verdicts. The intellectual apparatus arrived from outside medicine: the Reason-Reasson model's active-versus-latent-failure framework from the 1987-Hillsburgh-adjacent disaster-psychology work and the 1990 normal-accidents-and-high-reliability debates; James Reason's 'Human Error' (1990) and the Swiss-cheese model (1997), Weick and Sutcliffe's high-reliability-organisation studies of aircraft carriers (which produced preoccupation with failure and reluctance to simplify and sensitivity to operations and deference to expertise' principles that the safest room is where the nurse can stop the surgeon' slogan summarises), Dekker's 'field has turned wrong' safety-II critiques and the Hollnagel resilience-engineering school; and the checklist generation's mixed evidence - the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist's 2008 Lancet study and its 'effective-communication-and-sterility-did-the-work-not-the-ticking' re-analyses and the checklist-as-talisman' critique that made implementation science a subdiscipline. The improvement-science import (Breaking the Barriers to Health Care's' and 'Patient Safety and Quality: An Evidence-Based' AHRQ handbooks of 2004 and 2008, the Institute for Healthcare Improvement's 5-Million-Lives campaign's bundle diffusion and its later methodological self-critique about how much improvement was real and how much was regression-to-the-mean, the Keystone ventilator-bundle narrative) taught both the power and the statistical literacy required to read it. The disclosure half moved through law: the apology-not-admission statutes across US states from the 1970s-2000s, Canada's apology legislation, I'm sorry' campaigns, and the UK's statutory duty of candour from 2014 - and the US communication-and-resolution-programmes (the Lexington-Kentucky VA and Michigan-model early-offer studies) showed settlements and seconds down, budgets neutral. Reporting-systems history runs parallel: from the AHRQ patient-safety-indicators and the National Reporting-and-Learning-System-and-RLACs-and-NPSA-Adverse-Event-Reporting-and-Alert-Systems-and-Global-Patient-Safety-Challenges' high-alert-medications-and-care-associated-infection-and-diagnostic-error-campaigns (whose 2023 diagnostic-error thrust finally named the field's oldest and least-measured harm) to the second-victim-movement (the Stanford Centre for Patient Safety's side of harm) and psychological safety' surveys that turned 'do staff speak up' into a measurable predictor. Its unresolved chapters are the ones this course keeps touching: the outpatient and primary-care measurement gap, the diagnostic-error epidemiology's dependence on autopsy-adjacent data, the junior-doctor-hours politics, and the fact that every system's safety data is an estimate produced by whichever reporting culture generated it.** - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
:::

::: summary
- Harm in healthcare is common (about one in ten hospital admissions in the best surveys), substantially preventable, and concentrated in medications, infections, thromboembolism, falls and pressure injuries, procedures, and diagnosis.
- Accidents run through cheese: active failures sit at the bedside, latent conditions (rota, procurement, interface, staffing) sit in boardrooms; blaming individuals for latent conditions is the field's core prohibition - just culture is the compromise.
- The defences that work follow the hierarchy of controls: eliminate and engineer (connector redesigns, pump hard limits, barcode administration, tall-man labelling) before exhorting; checklists and bundles work when the culture lets juniors speak and the implementation is audited.
- Reporting is a trust instrument: near misses are free data, feedback loops keep them flowing, and 'no reports' is a red flag; analysis (SEA/RCA/FMEA) must reach the rota and the procurement committee to count.
- Disclosure is safety work: candour, apology and early-offer programmes reduce both harm's human cost and its litigation cost.
- Harm has two patients: the second-victim and moral-injury literature demands welfare pathways for clinicians, and measurement (triggers, funnels with humility, patient-reported items) is where boards either learn or perform.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Grade the behaviour {level=1}
Under just culture, classify and give the correct organisational response for: (a) an experienced nurse gives drug X instead of Y after the labels were swapped by the supplier; (b) a doctor signs a prescription from memory for a colleague's patient without checking the observations; (c) a registrar alters a controlled-drug record to cover a dispensing gap; (d) staff omit the bed-rail check after the policy was rewritten four times without consultation.
::: solution
**Just-culture-in-four-cases ([[#def-epidemiology]], [[#def-people]]): (a) human error from a look-alike hazard - support, notify the supplier and remove the shared-look-alike labels with an alert; the RCA that stops at 'nurse error' fails the next patient; (b) at-risk behaviour (the shortcut normalised by workload and 'everyone does it') - coach plus redesign (prescriber-can-see-observations-before-ordering' EPR ordering screen is the engineering fix; the conversation asks why signing felt acceptable); (c) reckless-and-deceptive - sanction-and-rehabilitation-process-and-regulator-notification-because integrity failure is different in kind from slip; (d) work-as-done defeating work-as-imagined - system failure: policy fatigue' finding means the policy is the intervention-under-trial, and re-educate' plan is malpractice; and the meta-lesson: the same action (missed check) can be any of the four categories depending on why the actor thought it was right, which is why just-culture panels interview before they judge.'**
:::

::: exercise Design the barcode rollout {level=2}
Your trust is deploying barcode medication administration on 40 wards. Nurses fear it will 'police them', the pharmacy wants compliance statistics, the vendor offers quick wins, and the IT lead wants it live in eight weeks. Write the clinical-safety plan for the implementation.
::: solution
**Implementation-as-a-safety-intervention ([[#def-toolkit]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]]): the FMEA-first stance - 'what-could-go-wrong-before-go-live: the scan-coverage gaps (PRNs, STAT doses, blood products, patient-owned drugs, 'the wristband off for the shower'), the workaround predictions (labels printed and taped to the computer for missing barcodes - today's efficiency, tomorrow's bypass culture), and the two-nurse-check questions that barcode scanning must explicitly resolve rather than silently dissolve for high-alert drugs; the human design - 'nurses as co-designers of the workflow map (study work-as-done on actual shifts before writing the procedure), scanning framed as patient protection not performance surveillance with compliance data decoupled from individual discipline, near-miss scan alerts triaged weekly with visible fixes, and an alert-fatigue budget in the vendor contract so the system is never allowed to train staff to swipe through; the phasing - 'two-ward pilots with observation studies, go/no-go criteria that include network dead-zone surveys and a rehearsed downtime procedure, then wave spread taught by the pilots' own nurses (the peer-spread evidence) rather than the vendor; and the evaluation - 'the right outcome is not the compliance percentage but the intercepted administration errors per thousand scans, the workaround rate, the nurse-confidence survey item ('I can give safe meds on my night with the system as it is'), and a twelve-month look-back for the error types the old paper system used to catch that nobody thought to ask the barcode about.'**

::: exercise Run the significant-event analysis {level=2}
A ward has had three falls with harm in six weeks; the initial notes say 'unsteady, found on floor' each time. Facilitate the SEA so it produces defensible change, and name the three failure modes of bad SEAs.
::: solution
**SEA-done-well ([[#def-epidemiology]], [[#def-toolkit]]): the method - 'reconstruct each event's timeline from the people who were there (the HCA who mopped, the night nurse running two bays, the relative who moved the frame), interview before judgement with the just-culture frame stated aloud; the contributing-factor map - 'the falls-taxonomy audit (orthostatic-drug-load review that names the doxazosin and the hypnotic - polypharmacy lever), toileting-and-call-for-help trajectory (call-bell-response-interval data nobody pulls), footwear-water-bolster-rail-environment sweep at 3am lighting not daylight, delirium-and-continence-and-bone-and-cardiac syncope screens that turn 'unsteady' into a diagnosis); the action plan in hierarchy order - 'elimination-adjacent (bed-alarm-and-low-bed debate with its evidence limits stated), engineering (bed-height-and-rails-and-floor-mat-and-lighting standards), process (post-fall-assessment-protocol with syncope-workup-trigger so the third fall gets an ECG), and only then education - 'each with an owner, a date and a measure; and the three failure modes of bad SEAs: 'the-villain-found-and-minutes-closed' version, action = re-educate' version, and report filed with no feedback to the staff who reported and no re-look at whether the actions bit' - each of which guarantees the fourth fall.'**

::: exercise Read the dashboard {level=2}
Board pack: Ward A reports 2 incidents this year, Ward B reports 61; the complaint volume is identical on both; Ward B's falls-harm rate is half Ward A's; neither has had an RCA. The director proposes 'an incident-reduction target of -20 percent'. Diagnose the dashboard and rewrite the proposal.
::: solution
**Safety-data-literacy ([[#def-epidemiology]], [[#def-people]]): the read - 'report counts measure reporting culture, not harm (Ward B's 61 is the safety signal in the good direction - near misses surfacing; Ward A's 2 with identical complaints is the alarm), and the harm-indicator that matters is the-serious-harm-per-occupied-bed-day and trigger-tool-positives, which the pack omits entirely; the target critique - 'a '-20 percent incidents' number is a textbook Goodhart case (the numbers go down because the reports stop'): suppression of reporting is the predictable behavioural answer and it destroys the early-warning system the moment the target bites; the rewrite - 'replace the target with: (i) reporting-rate-and-quality measures interpreted comparatively with staff-survey psychological-safety scores attached, (ii) harm-prevalence measurement (trigger tools, point-prevalence surveys) as the outcome, (iii) a pattern-review rule ('the five same-ward same-shift reports get a clinician-led review, not a spreadsheet'), (iv) RCA/SEA quality and action-completion audits rather than counts, and (v) an explicit 'the quiet wards get a visit' clause, because the board's job under its patient-safety-competency framework is to notice where the data is missing, not to congratulate where the numbers are small.'**
:::

::: exercise Support the second victim {level=2}
A day after a maternal death her senior midwife was first to attend, the midwife emails the team apologising, stops attending handovers, tells her manager 'I can't do this anymore', and declines the trust's counselling leaflet. As her colleague and as the unit's clinical lead, what do you actually do - and what must the system not do?
::: solution
**Second-victim-support-that-works ([[#def-people]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]): the human sequence - 'the-first-contact-is-a-person-not-a-form'-rule (the evidence-based practice is a trained peer - someone who has been through it - showing up in person within days, saying 'this has happened to good people, you are not alone, tell me yours', which the peer-support-programme evaluations show changes trajectories), the practical shields (paid time, rota protection framed as recovery not exile, a named manager handling the family-correspondence so she never drafts an apology under guidance), and the threshold awareness (the intrusive-replay-and-insomnia-and-alcohol-and-'I-do-not-deserve-to-practise' pattern that needs the confidential clinician-health-and-psychological-services route, the suicide-risk question asked directly, and treatment is career-preserving here' guarantee in writing); the system's don'ts - 'the-leaflet-instead-of-a-person' substitution, resilience training as prevention' decoy (the debate's finding: it blames victims when the jagged system stands), the investigation-calendar that summons her before she has slept, and we have full confidence in our staff' email sent without her story being asked; and the governance that belongs to the same act - the-significant-event-analysis-with-her-invited-not-summoned and the duty-of-candour-owner-named-so-the-family-questions-do-not-land-on-the-most-wounded-clinician and the-return-to-work-conversation-with-the-medicine-for-what-she-can-do-now which is how units keep midwives instead of losing them to other professions at year eight, the quietly largest workforce leak in perinatal care.'**

::: example The overrides nobody reads {#ex-overrides}
The dispensing cabinet logs 4,100 override events this quarter on the surgical wards; the pharmacy's quarterly report shows the number as a percentage and files it. ::: example The 3:12 a.m. call that was answered {#ex-escalate}

**Escalation-is-a-system-property ([[#def-people]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], [[surgery/perioperative-care]]): the three failures - the not right' under-escalated (the unstructured handover-of-worry: SBAR exists for this sentence), the 'the two-hour downward trend invisible to a chart read as numbers not as a line (the 'observation-chart-design-that-plot-the-trend and the NEWS trigger that either did not fire or fired into a system without a mandatory response), and the courtesy call to the registrar' that should not be a favour (the escalation-path-with-a-number-that-answers and the any-nurse-can-trigger-a-review-and-will-be-thanked'-policy that converts a personality-dependent act into a procedure); the medicine of the bleed - the post-pancreatectomy-haemorrhage-with-sick-appears-before-the-haemoglobin-falls and the arterial-blush-on-the-CT-in-the-first-hour-window where the early-alarm-in-ERAS-protocols exists precisely because these patients compensate then crash; the SEA to run - the not-who-but-what'-version (the HO-alone-on-two-wings-at-3am-with-a-pager-list-of-40-beds rota fact, the page-answered-in-words-not-in-person and the trend-flagged-to-no-one'-interface fact) with actions in the right order (the escalation-competency-in-induction-with-simulation and the second-haemorrhage-protocol-card-on-every-pancreatectomy-bed'-design fixes) and the candour-and-support arms for both patient and the 24-year-old HO whose career is at the same crossroads; and the metric worth adopting from the deterioration literature: the time-from-abnormal-to-review and the nurse-initiated-critical-care-review-without-doctor-permission counts, because those two numbers are what 'speaking-up culture' looks like when it is written down.'**

::: exercise Handle the apology request {level=2}
Six weeks after a missed fracture diagnosis that caused a nerve injury, the patient's family telephones asking 'the doctor who missed it' to 'explain himself'. The doctor is terrified, the complaints team wants 'the letter to handle it', and the patient wants an answer about why it was missed and what will change. Advise all three positions and the meeting's design.
::: solution
**Candour-as-a-clinical-skill ([[#def-people]], [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]]): the frames - 'for the family: the statutory-and-moral-duty of candour means a timely, in-person, plain-language explanation with an apology (which in law is not an admission of liability in most jurisdictions) and the 'what-we-have-changed' answer they are asking for is the one only the clinician can give; for the doctor: the apology-feels-like-confession fear is the documented second-victim spiral, and the data from apology-and-disclosure programmes say the meeting that is prepared with coaching and support goes well in the great majority of cases and prevents the complaint-becomes-claim trajectory; for the organisation: 'let-us-write-the-letter' is the 1990s model whose outcomes the research priced; the design - the right-room' (not the hospital where they were treated if avoidable, no bleeps, both clinicians and the PALS-or-equivalent and a family supporter), the timeline-first-then-the-what-I-was-thinking'-structure (families report that hearing the reasoning - 'the X-ray was read as normal and here is why that happens' - matters as much as the regret), the no-speculation-on-compensation'-boundary, and the follow-up-written-summary-and-review-meeting'-promise which converts the meeting into a process; and the after - 'the learning-recorded-and-fed-to-the-reporting-loop so the family's next question ('has anything changed?') has an evidenced answer, and the debrief for the clinician with the same dignity the patient got.'**
