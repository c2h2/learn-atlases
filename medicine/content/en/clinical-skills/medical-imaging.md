Medical imaging has become the whole-body examination performed by a second doctor: the clinician's question travels through a request form, a protocol, a report and a cascade, and the radiation dose, the contrast reaction, the incidental finding and the false negative remain the ordering team's responsibilities. **This chapter covers the modalities as clinical instruments with costs (plain films, CT and its dose philosophy, the iodinated and gadolinium contrast agents and their true risks, ultrasound's operator-dependence, MRI's magnetic ecology, and nuclear medicine's functional window), the choosing logic (decision rules, question-to-modality matching, and the urgency-accuracy-harm arithmetic), the body systems with their named pitfalls (the normal chest film that misses, the head CT that excludes bleed but little else, the ankle rules that work, and the lumbar MRI that can harm), and the reporting contract - the clinical question, the urgent-findings call, the incidentaloma pathway, and the clinician-radiologist dialogue that the serious-incident reports keep finding missing.**

## The instruments

::: definition Modalities, doses and contrast safety {#def-modalities}
**Plain radiography is cheap, fast and honest about its limits: a projectional superimposition that excels at bone, air and fluid-level questions and misses early tumours and subtle fractures, so its legitimate uses are screens where insensitivity is acceptable - the chest film before the CT decision, the trauma series, the line-and-tube check, the obstruction and perforation look. CT is acute medicine's workhorse and the dose leader: effective dose in millisieverts (a chest film sitting at a fraction of a year of natural background and a CT CAP at several years' worth), governed by justification and optimisation - the paediatric and pregnancy protocols, the low-dose and contrast-phase protocols, and the protocol-optimisation audit that most departments now publish. Its clinical arithmetic is population-risk against individual benefit: the lifetime cancer-risk estimates per CT are honest at the epidemiological level and must not deter the dissection or pulmonary-embolism or intra-abdominal-bleed hunt where the untold harm is tonight. CT's artefacts are clinical facts: the poorly timed bolus, the motion and beam-hardening mimics, the single-phase study for a multiphasic question - hence the rule that the clinical question must reach the radiographer and reporting radiologist, not just the requisition. Iodinated contrast has two hazards and a hundred myths. The hypersensitivity spectrum runs from urticaria to cardiovascular collapse: a prior reaction is the strongest predictor of the next, mild prior reactions justify either an alternative study or premedication with observation, asthma is a severity modifier, and 'shellfish allergy' and 'iodine allergy' are folklore that should not block a needed CT. The renal hazard has been revised downward by the controlled studies of the 2010s: contrast-associated kidney injury at standard intravenous doses is largely a myth except in severe pre-existing impairment with volume depletion and in large intra-arterial studies; metformin's hold rule is about lactic acidosis in established AKI, not about the contrast itself; and dialysis patients need no post-contrast emergency session. Extravasation injury and the steroid-and-antihistamine premedication literature finish the ledger. Ultrasound is real-time, radiation-free, operator-dependent and unfairly cheap: its accuracy equals its question (the gallstone and DVT and in paediatric-hip-and-obstetric-and-renal-and-testicular applications its specificity is superb and its 'normal ultrasound does not exclude' indications - the appendicitis and the common-duct-stone - equally well documented); the Doppler layers add vascular and obstetric physiology; and the contrast-enhanced and elastography adjuncts keep moving its frontier. MRI trades radiation and speed for soft-tissue supremacy: the safety screen is a genuine liturgy (pacemakers and loops now increasingly MR-conditional with documented models-and-modes checking, the aneurysm-coil and clip databases, cochlear and neurostimulator implants, metal fragments and orbital screening, pumps and stents and valve documentation), the claustrophobia-and-time-and-sedation-and-monitoring logistics, gadolinium's own safety story (the anaphylaxis-forces-of-NSF-era nephrogenic-systemic-fibrosis near-elimination with the macrocyclic agents and the GFR-threshold policies, and the brain-deposition findings whose clinical significance remains unproven but changed practice toward indication-based use), and its superiority where water and fat and blood products are the answer - the spine, joints, pelvis, liver-characterisation problem, the MS and optic and pituitary questions, and the stroke's diffusion-weighting that finds what the CT cannot see for a day. Nuclear medicine and PET-CT image physiology: the bone scan's sensitivity and low specificity versus MRI, the ventilation-perfusion SPECT era's answer to the contrast- and renal-problem pulmonary-embolism question with its own dose profile, the FDG-PET's oncology-staging-and-restaging-and-infection-and-inflammation indications with the inflammation-mimic false positives, and the therapeutic radionuclide applications (the radioiodine and the PSMA and bone-pain agents) whose radiation-protection counselling is clinical work. Mammography, fluoroscopy and the interventional suite complete the instrument table: the screening mammogram's age-stratified sensitivity and the tomosynthesis and abbreviated-MRI trials, the barium-and-fluoroscopic studies' shrinking-but-real role against CT and endoscopy, and interventional radiology's quiet revolution (the embolisation of bleeds and fibroids and the drain and biopsy and ablation and thrombectomy-adjacent vascular work that made the radiologist a treating specialty and the 'image now, operate later' pathway normal in trauma and stroke).**
:::

## The choice

::: definition Deciding what to image: rules, timing and the systems {#def-choosing}
**The first imaging rule is that the question precedes the modality: suspected scaphoid fracture gets dedicated views or an early MRI rather than 'film the wrist', suspected cauda equina gets same-day whole-spine MRI with the clinical findings documented, suspected stone gets the low-dose CT protocol not the IVU of history, and suspected pulmonary embolism follows the age-and-pregnancy-adapted algorithm (the D-dimer-then-CTPA-versus-VQ-with-the-renal-and-contrast-and-breast-dose-considerations that the guideline wars of the 2010s adjudicated). The decision rules that reduced unnecessary imaging are among patient safety's genuine successes: the Ottawa and Canadian cervical-spine rules, the ankle rules' five-fold drop in films with no missed fractures, the head-CT rules for minor head injury (the Canadian CT Head and New Orleans criteria) that trade a small miss-risk for large radiation-and-incidental savings, and the child-abuse and whiplash and sinusitis appropriateness criteria that the radiology societies' ACR-style lists encode; each rule is a statement of pretest probability management and fails when applied outside its derivation population (the intoxication and the distraction injury and the immigrant-anatomist caveats). The plain-versus-cross-sectional-versus-functional hierarchy per system: chest (the two-day-cough-with-red-flags film, the CT for the PE and aortic and interstitial questions the film cannot address, and the 'film normal, patient sick' reflexes - the pneumomediastinum and early oesophageal rupture that hide on projectional views); abdomen (the X-ray's retirement to perforation-and-obstruction-and-body-map duties, the ultrasound-first biliary-and-renal-and-aortic-screen logic, the CT-with-contrast-without-contrast-with-the-phases-for-the-clinical-question - the appendicitis-and-diverticulitis-and-ischaemia-and-trauma-protocols each a different injection scheme, and the MRCP-and-EUS for the bile duct); the head (the CT-as-the-thunderclap-and-trauma-and-first-episode-seizure-with-features tool, the MRI-as-the-everything-else tool - the tumour-and-demyelinating-and-posterior-fossa-and-early-ischaemia-and-infection-and-metabolic questions, and the honest 'CT negative at 6 hours for SAH' pathway-debate that the radiology and emergency literature still argue about with xanthochromia windows and CTA thresholds); the spine and muscles (the low-back-pain imaging cascade the guidelines fight - red-flag-only early imaging, because the asymptomatic disc bulge is universal after thirty and the report of it converts back pain into a disease - and the sports-medicine MRI indications where the clinical diagnosis was already specific); the breast and paediatric and obstetric fields (the triple assessment, the ALARA-and-justification-in-utero culture, the radiation-pregnancy-questions-with-the-actual-dose-tables-that-reassure-when-appropriate and the ultrasound-and-MRI-first-alternatives); and the oncological restaging economy (the RECIST-and-PET-era serial-imaging intervals, the incidental-and-borderline-findings' reporting rules, and the 'who reads the survivor's scans and who closes the surveillance' question that the cancer-biology chapter inherits). Timing is part of the choice: the diffusion-weighting-that-outruns-CT-in-early-stroke-and-the-plain-film-that-lags-the-scaphoid-fracture-and-the-osteomyelitis-and-the-stress-fracture-weeks; the contrast-timing-phases and the bolus and the fasting and the bladder protocols; and the 'scan when it changes management' rule that retires the admission CT for the two-day-viral picture and books the follow-up for the one that needs interval.**
:::

## The contract

::: definition Requests, reports, incidentals and the dialogue {#def-contract}
**The request form is a legal and clinical document: the Royal-and-NHS-and-accreditation-bodies' requirement for a clear clinical question, the relevant prior imaging and the specific decision sought ('solitary pulmonary nodule management' versus 'pneumonia') changes the protocol and the report's usefulness, and the pregnant-and-breastfeeding-and-renal-function-and-previous-contrast-reaction fields are the safety layer; the 'rule-out cancer because worried' honesty that the appropriateness literature rewards beats the euphemism that hides the red flag. The report is read for three things and written for three more: what is seen, what it means, what to do next - and its audience is the patient's whole future, so the structured reporting era (the radiology-structure-templates with the LI-RADS-and-BI-RADS-and-Lung-RADS-classifications that turn 'indeterminate nodule' into a management algorithm and the 'report language that does not overcall' studies about incidental-adrenal-and-thyroid-and-pituitary-findings' size thresholds) has made the subspecialty re-read the classic 'incidentaloma' literature and write the thresholds into the template. The urgent-findings culture is the contract's fastest clause: the 'critical results' pathway (the pneumothorax and the dissection and the intracranial bleed and the non-arterialised-ischaemic-limb findings that must reach a named clinician by telephone within minutes, with read-back and documentation, because the report-in-the-system-is-not-the-action-on-the-patient and the missed-aortic-dissection-on-a-negotiated-read is a recurring sentinel case); the 'same-shift-critical-findings-communication-audits' that most accreditation regimes now require; and the out-of-hours escalation ladder (the imaging-reading-service-junior-to-registrar-to-consultant-radiologist path and the telephone number that answers). The clinician-radiologist dialogue - the informal corridor conversation the studies show predicts diagnostic recovery - is being rebuilt formally: the radiologist-email-and-phone-open-culture policies, the MDT as scheduled dialogue, the 'please review with the clinical findings' request when the picture does not fit, and the disagreement-resolution paths that protect the patient when the report and the bedside disagree (the 'treat the patient, question the report' rule with its corollary that a confident clinical picture overrides a reassuring scan more often than the reverse). The false-negative reckoning is statistics plus courage: the perceptual-vs-interpretative-vs-knowledge error taxonomy, the double-read-and-AI-assist evidence (the simultaneous-AI-triage-of-intracranial-haemorrhage-and-pneumothorax products of the 2010s-2020s with their regulatory approvals and their 'automation bias makes humans stop checking' caveats), the screening-programme sensitivity audits and recall-rate benchmarks, the national-reporting-schemes (the UK's and Australia's radiation-and-contrast-incident-reporting services), and the duty-of-candour when the missed fracture or the delayed cancer report harms - disclosed like any other harm, because the image is signed and the patient now has access to it. Finally the appropriateness and access governance: the waiting-list-and-triage-and-priority-system (the cancer-suspect-two-week rules and the 'possible-stroke-imaging-within-the-hour' clock that the whole system bends around), the mutual-aid-and-network-imaging (the regional thrombectomy-and-aortic-and-paediatric-neurosurgery-scan-sends), the out-of-hours-minimum-data-set (the 'only the question that changes tonight' doctrine), and the report's last duty - the closing of loops: surveillance intervals that live in a system and not only in prose, recall letters that go, and the 'no significant abnormality' that means what it says in words the referring clinician and the patient both parsed correctly at 3am.**
:::

::: example The ankle that was cleared {#ex-ankle}
A 24-year-old footballer's 'ankle sprain' at 2am: X-ray 'no fracture', discharged; at day nine the persistent lateral-and-midfoot pain and the inability to bear weight since the first night lead to a CT that shows a subtle medial cuneiform fracture and a missed fifth-metatarsal avulsion.
::: solution
**Rules-applied-versus-rules-understood ([[#def-choosing]], [[surgery/orthopaedics]], [[musculoskeletal/fractures]]): the Ottawa logic - 'the ankle rules are excellent at avoiding unnecessary films and useless when the positive findings they encode are ignored after a negative film: this patient had two of the rule's own high-risk markers (inability to bear weight from the injury, midfoot bone tenderness) that mandate imaging - and 'no fracture on the initial view' after an indicated film in a high-mechanism patient is a plan, not a discharge; the anatomy the 2am film misses - 'the midfoot (the Lisfranc-and-cuneiform-and-tarsometatarsal questions with the weight-bearing-film-when-feasible and MRI-or-CT-for-the-occult), the base-of-fifth-metatarsal's zone-2-Jones-versus-zone-3-Avulsion-distinction-that-changes-weight-bearing-advice, and the talar-and-tibial-plafond-pits; the correct pathway - 'the dedicated views for the suspected scaphoid-and-calcaneus-and-templated-views, the 'persistent pain and functional failure after a negative film equals re-imaging-or-MRI-within-days' rule that sports-medicine and the NICE-style-services now run as clinics, and the 'documented weight-bearing-status-at-discharge and follow-up-booked' closure; and the systems reading - 'the 'overnight-silent-discharge' problem (the minor-injuries unit's 're-attend if not better' that nine days of football lost) - so the re-attendance-and-missed-fracture-audit that every department's governance file contains and few act on: the rules reduce overuse; the clinical re-look prevents the underuse.**
:::

::: example The incidental that needed an owner {#ex-incidental}
A routine ultrasound for renal colic notes 'incidental 2.4-cm hypoechoic liver lesion, suggest further imaging'; the patient is stone-free and asymptomatic; the letter reaches her GP, the 'further imaging' is booked as a routine outpatient ultrasound in 14 weeks, and nobody tells her what the lesion might be until the MRI at month four shows a resectable colorectal metastasis from a cancer she had never been diagnosed with.
::: solution
**Incidental-with-a-clinical-question ([[#def-contract]], [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]], [[#def-choosing]]): the radiology side done right and wrong - 'the finding correctly flagged (a new solid liver lesion above 1 cm in an adult is an investigate-it finding on most thresholds), and 'suggest further imaging' without a named modality-and-urgency is the template that the incidentals literature has criticised for a decade: the multiphasic liver-protocol CT-or-MRI (the wash-and-fill characterisation and the primary-search implication) is the next test and the report should say so with the interval the lesion deserves; the referral side's failure - 'a liver lesion is not a routine outpatient: the '2-week-wait-adjacent suspected-cancer pathway for an unexplained liver lesion with a possible-primary workup (the CT-CAP-and-CEA-and-the-colonospy-decision-that-this-patient-should-have-entered-in-week-one-not-month-four), and the owner question answered in the notes ('GP manages the referral' with a named review date or the acute-medical team owns it); the system that prevents it - 'the structured incidental findings' management protocols (the size-and-context thresholds and their mandated intervals that some national radiology strategies publish), the 'recommendation-to-action' closed-loop that reconciles at 6 weeks whether the recommended test happened, and the patient told at the point of the finding what is being chased and by when; and the candour and the case review - 'the 'delay-attribution analysis (the finding-to-treatment interval mapped to where weeks were lost), the resection-that-still-happened-and-the-compensation-conversation-held-together, and the liver-lesion-urgency addendum to the trust's incidental-findings policy, because the incidentaloma is only incidental until someone owns it.**

## Where this leads

::: history
**Imaging began, famously, with a cat: Wilhelm Röntgen's November 1895 discovery of the X-ray, the first image being his wife's hand with her wedding ring, and within a year the medical and popular worlds had adopted 'the photography of the invisible' - along with the burns and the cancers of the unprotected pioneer radiologists (the early radiology cemeteries of epitaphs and the 1900s radiation-protection movement that produced the dose-quantity idea). Contrast media arrived almost immediately (the urinary-tract pyolography of the 1900s and Lipiodol in 1921), angiography was invented almost simultaneously by the accidental and the deliberate (Forssmann's 1929 self-catheterisation and Seldinger's 1953 technique), and the cerebral-arteriography lineage made radiology the only specialty that could see inside living vessels. Ultrasound entered medicine through the war and afterwards: the obstetric plotters' echoes of the 1950s and the Doppler and real-time eras, then the transvaginal and contrast and elastography waves; nuclear medicine followed the isotope age (the iodine-131 therapies and the Technetium-99m generator that became the world's most-used radioisotope); CT's Nobel-winning 1971 introduction (Hounsfield and Cormack, and the immediate replacement of ventriculography) and MRI's 1977-1980 clinical arrival (Damadian's and Mansfield and Lauterbur's work) made cross-sectional imaging central within one lifetime, and the digital-radiology-and-PACS-and-picture-archiving revolution of the 1990s-2000s deleted the film camera and moved the report from typewriter to structured template. The dose politics developed in parallel: the radiation-protection frameworks (the ICRP's recommendations from 1928, the 'as low as reasonably achievable' doctrine from the 1950s, the ALARA-era justification-and-optimisation law of the 1980s-90s directives, the UNSCEAR risk estimates), the CT-dose-creep scare of 2008-2012 (the Annals-of-Internal-Medicine projections on CT-associated cancers and the dose-index-and-CTDIvol-and-dose-audit-registry movements it produced), and the paediatric image-guidedly-better campaigns that turned 'child-size the protocol' into accreditation. The decision-rule era changed who gets imaged: the Canadian and Ottawa ankle and cervical-spine rules (1990s-2000s, with the documented halving-or-more of unnecessary films and the 'rules get misapplied by non-derivers' implementation caution), the head-injury CT rules and NEXUS, the PE diagnostic-management studies (PIOPED and the CTPA-era trials), the Choosing-Wisdom appropriateness lists, and the 'imaging for low back pain' wars in which every guideline body told clinicians what the asymptomatic-disc-prevalence studies (the 1990s-2000s MRI-of-healthy-volunteers series, Jensen's 1994 New England Journal paper showing disc abnormalities in asymptomatic subjects) made undeniable. The overdiagnosis movement (the Welch-adjacent screening-harms literature) and the incidentaloma literature (the adrenal-adjacent 1980s term and the growing cascade-management protocols for thyroid, lung, liver and pancreatic findings with their published size-attenuation thresholds) forced the report to become a management algorithm - the Lung-RADS and LI-RADS BI-RADS-classification project of the 2010s. The contrast-safety corrections are their own evidence-based-medicine lesson: the 2010s-2020s large controlled studies and meta-analyses (the 2017 radiology and nephrology society joint statements) that shrank the contrast-induced-nephropathy scare at intravenous standard doses, revised the metformin rules, and confirmed the near-elimination of nephrogenic systemic fibrosis with macrocyclic agents and GFR screening - after two decades of practice built on case-series fear. The 2020s brought artificial intelligence into the read: the FDA-and-European-approved-adjacent triage products (the intracranial-haemorrhage and pneumothorax and pulmonary-nodule detection algorithms and the diabetic-retinopathy autonomous systems), the automation-bias and generalisability warnings, and the unresolved question of who signs the machine-assisted report. Through every technology the field's own reviews keep naming one failing: the missing clinical question and the unclosed loop - which is why imaging's history ends as a chapter of communication, the same conclusion the 1896 pioneers had already reached about the X-ray's most dangerous component, the optimism of the man holding the tube.** - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[public-health/environmental-occupational]].**
:::

::: summary
- Each modality is an instrument with a cost profile: plain films fast and insensitive; CT fast, decisive and dose-bearing; ultrasound real-time and operator-bound; MRI superior soft tissue with a safety screen; nuclear medicine physiologic with its own dose logic; mammography and fluoroscopy and intervention have their named niches.
- Contrast safety today: prior contrast reaction predicts next reactions; 'shellfish/iodine allergy' is folklore; standard intravenous contrast rarely harms kidneys at modern doses; metformin and NSF rules apply to defined renal-risk groups, not everyone.
- Imaging follows questions: decision rules (Ottawa, CT head, PE pathways) reduce harm; the red-flag-only lumbar-spine rule protects against the asymptomatic-disc-bulge cascade; timing (early MRI for stroke and cauda, repeat films for scaphoid) is part of appropriateness.
- The request must carry the question, the dose-pregnancy-renal fields; the report must carry classifications and thresholds; critical findings travel by telephone with read-back; incidentals need pathways with owners and intervals, not prose.
- False negatives are perceptual, interpretive or knowledge errors: double-reading, AI-assist with automation-bias awareness, sensitivity audits and candour when harm lands.
- The clinician-radiologist dialogue is a diagnostic tool: disagreement is resolved by treating the patient and re-asking the question, and the closed loop (who chases, by when, told whom) is imaging's patient-safety contract.
:::
