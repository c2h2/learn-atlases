A systematic review is what happens when a question is treated as evidence's unit rather than a paper: define the question, find every study that answers it, appraise them, and combine what survives. **This chapter covers the method (questions as PICO-fragments, protocol registration, the search's recall-versus-precision trade and its librarian economics, screening and data-extraction reliability), the synthesis (risk-of-bias tools, meta-analytic models from fixed to random effects to prediction, heterogeneity's diagnosis, subgroup and meta-regression discipline), the publication-and-reporting-bias apparatus (funnels, registries, the 'grey' question, and the network meta-analysis expansion), and the ecosystem built around the method: Cochrane, GRADE and the guideline complex, the overview-of-reviews layer, the living-review response to evidence velocity, and the political economy that makes a method designed for honesty a contested instrument.**

## The method

::: definition Question to evidence base {#def-method}
**The question precedes everything: intervention questions arrive as PICO fragments (population, intervention, comparator, outcome - the framing that determines inclusion criteria and, through the comparator, the review's entire relevance to practice: the 'no-included-study-used-the-available-alternative' rejection class), and diagnostic, prognostic and qualitative questions have their own architectures (PICOS-and-PCC-and-SPIDER-adjacent frameworks) whose absence from protocols predicts the review's failure to answer anything. The protocol, registered prospectively (PROSPERO for reviews, the trial-protocol-anchored comparison - protocol-versus-review-deviation audits showing outcome-switching within systematic reviews mirroring trials' sin), fixes: the search, the criteria, the synthesis plan and the subgroup hypotheses - converting the review from argument to pre-specified measurement. Searching is a recall discipline: MEDLINE, Embase, CENTRAL and the discipline-specific databases, with the search strategy's transparent string, the 'hand-search-and-reference-list-and-grey-literature-and-trial-registry' supplements whose yield the methodological subsamples quantify (the 'five-percent-of-included-studies-from-grey-sources-that-change-conclusions-in-a-sixth-of-reviews' findings), the language-and-date-limit sins and their bias directions, and the citation-candidate deduplication now assisted (and destabilised) by automation - the 'machine-learning-screening-validation-vs-convenience' literature whose verdict is that automation must be validated, measured against dual human screening, and never allowed to silently define the evidence base. Screening and extraction reliability: dual independent review as the standard (the inter-rater statistics that make the flow diagram auditable), single-reviewer-with-verification compromises, and the PRISMA flow diagram as the review's chest X-ray - the identification-screening-inclusion counts whose arithmetic must reconcile. Study appraisal is its own definition's subject (below); the extraction layer's quiet errors (outcome-definition mismatches, unit conversions, the 'response-at-24-weeks-versus-continuous-odds-ratio' synthesis of unlike quantities) are where meta-analyses die methodologically, which is why pilot-extraction forms and adjudication meetings are protocol items, not courtesies. Finally the update economics: reviews age (the 'living-review' response to velocity below; the 'obsolete-by-publication' audits of rapidly-moving fields like antithrombotics and oncology), and the question 'how much new evidence since the search?' should headline every use of a review older than its field's half-life.**
:::

## The synthesis

::: definition Bias appraisal, meta-analysis and heterogeneity {#def-synthesis}
**Risk-of-bias tools formalise scepticism: Cochrane's RoB 2 (randomised trials - the five domains and the overall judgement algorithm, with its 'some-concerns-without-direction' interpretive culture), ROBINS-I for non-randomised studies (the confounding-as-separate-domain architecture), QUIPS-and-QUADAS-2 for prognosis and diagnostics, and the AMSTAR-2 meta-bias tool for reviews-about-reviews; their shared innovations are the domain decomposition (so criticism names a mechanism), the direction-of-effect judgement, and the 'risk of bias is not study quality' campaign against the scorecard era's additive nonsense. Meta-analytic models were introduced in the statistics chapter's decisions definition (inverse-variance weighting, the DerSimonian-Laird-to-REML-restricted-maximum-likelihood lineage, the Hartung-Knapp interval corrections, and the small-study-effect asymmetry that biases random-effects pooling when small harmful studies go missing - the 'RE-versus-FE-in-the-presence-of-publication-bias' counterintuitive results worth re-reading). Heterogeneity is the synthesis's subject-matter: the Q-test's low power, the I-squared's context-dependent reading, tau-squared's own wide interval from few studies, and the prediction interval's honest transportability statement. Where heterogeneity is found, the analytic responses and their rules: subgroup analysis (interaction tests, never within-group significance - the 'subgroups-as-multiplicity-swamp' correction that trial-sequential and false-discovery-rate methods attempt), meta-regression (study-level covariates, ecological fallacy's 'trial-average-data' warning, and the 'few-covariates-many-trials' constraint that makes most published meta-regression descriptive), and the sensitivity programme (exclusion-by-bias-judgement, excluding-unclears-versus-including, the 'unpublished-data-obtained-from-authors' branch that changes conclusions in the audit literature with uncomfortable frequency). The synthesis decisions that are actually judgements, stated as such: combining across clinically heterogeneous populations (the 'averaging-apples-and-oranges-that-are-both-fruit' test - does the pooled mean serve any real decision?), across outcome definitions (the core-outcome-set reform's role), and across risks of bias (the 'low-risk-only' primary with all-studies supportive convention - and its opposite's defence). Network meta-analysis completes the modern toolkit: combining direct and indirect evidence through common comparators with the transitivity assumption (effect-modifier balance across the network - the assumption no statistic tests), the inconsistency checks (node-splitting and design-by-treatment models with their modest power), the ranking-statistics caution (the surface-under-the-cumulative-curve rank and the 'rankograms-not-leagues' advice), and mixed-treatment-comparison's regulatory and guideline entrenchment despite every classic caveat its methodological literature keeps restating.**
:::

## The ecosystem

::: definition Reporting, grades and the economics of syntheses {#def-ecosystem}
**Reporting bias is the synthesis's ecology problem: the trials-never-published literature (the 'trials-in-the-freezer' surveys, the WHO registry-and-ICMJE-policy era's measured effects on registration with incomplete reporting persisting, the 'reporting-completeness-across-registries' audits), within-study outcome switching (the Chan-and-Altman-and-Moore-adjacent registry-versus-paper comparisons), and the methods for their detection and remedy (selection models with their strong assumptions, the 'contact-all-registries-and-sponsors' labour-intensive truth). The 'is-the-abstract-honest' genre (conclusions discordant with own results - the 'spin' literature's quantification across specialties) belongs to this layer too. GRADE is the verdict grammar the ecosystem adopted: certainty per outcome (high-moderate-low-very-low) downgraded for risk of bias, imprecision, inconsistency, indirectness and publication bias and upgraded for large effects and dose-response, with the evidence-to-decision framework separating evidence quality from recommendation strength (the 'strong-recommendation-low-quality-evidence' class where values, equity and resources cross the line - and the arguments that GRADE's arithmetic smuggles judgment into decimals). The institution stack: Cochrane (1993's sedimented programme, its living-review transformation and its licence economics - the 'Cochrane-for-the-global-south-access' debates), the national agencies (NICE and CADTH and their evidence-review production lines), the journal-tier incentive that rewards novel meta-analyses over updated ones (the 'over 6,000-registered-protocols-abandoned-before-publication' audit - the method's own attrition problem), and commercial syntheses whose conclusions correlate with their funding (the industry-sponsored-reviews literature now extending into 'independent-looking' meta-analyses with ghost- and guest-authorship patterns). The living review (the pandemic's methodological gift: continuous surveillance-searched, rapidly verified, versioned updates with the 'trust-in-motion' governance questions - editorial speed versus completeness trade-offs the BMJ-and-Nature-pilots documented), the umbrella-review-and-overview layer (the 'many-review-same-question-proliferation' problem - the 'DEtection-and-ATElition-adjacent' de-duplication methods), and the evidence-gap register (the 'review-as-research-agenda' function the journals' gap-sections formalise) are the current frontier. The chapter's endpoint stays the clinician's: a systematic review is a claim about the sum of human evidence, built from fallible searches, fallible appraisals and fallible statistics, whose authority is exactly the transparency of its contingencies - which is why the critical-appraisal chapter next turns the method on the reviews themselves.**
:::

::: example The guideline citation {#ex-citation}
A guideline recommends drug X over Y 'based on high-certainty evidence from network meta-analysis'. You find: the NMA's transitivity assumption rests on age-balance it does not show; three of nine direct comparisons come from a single trial series sharing an outcome definition; two trials meeting criteria are absent from the reference list and sit unreported in a registry.
::: solution
**Auditing-a-network ([[#def-synthesis]], [[#def-ecosystem]], [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]]): The audit trail: transitivity requires the effect modifiers (age, severity, co-interventions) distributed comparably across every comparison loop - the baseline-characteristics table and the 'did-they-check-the-critical-modifiers-across-nodes' question, not just the statistical inconsistency tests; the single-source-cluster problem (one trial series dominating a direct comparison makes 'network' a misnomer for that edge - the 'contribution-matrix-and-influence-diagnostic' plots that serious software now produces, and the sensitivity analysis dropping the cluster that the guideline summary must report if it changes ranks); and the missing trials (registry-versus-bibliography reconciliation is now a first pass - the 'unreported-but-registered' class, whose effect direction the funnel asymmetry and the trial's own sponsor-report requests fill, with the Cochrane-and-MC-consortium data-sharing mechanisms as the formal route). The GRADE consequences: indirectness and probable reporting bias are downgrade triggers; the 'high-certainty' label is probably moderate at best, and the recommendation's strength (which may be strong despite moderate evidence - that is legitimate under evidence-to-decision) must be re-checked for whether the values-and-costs analysis noticed the same absences. The letter to write: specific (the three named issues with references), constructive (the sensitivity analysis that resolves two of them), and public (the journal's correspondence and the guideline's living-update channel) - the accountability mechanism that has improved at least one antipsychotic and one anticoagulant guideline's transparency.**
:::
:::

::: example The rapid review in the pandemic {#ex-living}
Your hospital's infection-control committee needs, in 72 hours, an evidence summary on UV-C room disinfection; the Cochrane review is four years old and pre-pandemic; three preprints claim benefit; two industry studies are cited in procurement papers.
::: solution
**Evidence-at-emergency-speed ([[#def-method]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): The method triage: the 'rapid-review' adaptations declared (restricted databases with the recall trade stated, single-review-with-check screening, risk-of-bias limited to design-and-confounders, narrative synthesis with meta-analysis only where pooling is obviously defensible), the 'rapid-versus-sloppy' line being transparency about what was skipped. The source census: the living-literature databases and WHO's global research repository (the 'non-indexed-and-preprint-and-paired-data' harvest), the preprints' status labelled (not yet peer reviewed, methods not yet audited - the 'preprint-as-estimate-not-verdict' rule), the trial registers searched for the ongoing studies whose eventual results will rewrite this (the 'evidence-in-motion' register attached to the summary), and industry studies appraised without the conspiracy reflex but with the conflict-of-interest-and-endpoint-definition audit (the 'natural-experiment-versus-controlled-trial' difference in their designs). The synthesis's honest shape: a summary-of-findings table with certainty per outcome (the UV-C literature's 'surface-inactivation-versus-clinical-infection' outcome hierarchy - surrogate endpoints downgraded for indirectness, the 'cluster-design-and-cost-and-maintenance-failure' practicalities included because procurement needs them), the recommendation framed as evidence-plus-values ('no high-certainty clinical-effectiveness evidence exists; surrogate data and mechanism support use as an adjunct where terminal cleaning is delayed; cost per room per year with the maintenance-verified-output assumption'), and the review's expiry condition published (the trigger list that mandates re-review - which is how living-review culture escaped the pandemic permanently). The committee's protection: documenting that the decision was made on the fastest honest evidence available, with the named uncertainties that the next eighteen months will resolve.**

## Where this leads

Bias tools' source material is [[epidemiology-ebm/bias-confounding]]; the statistics of pooling in [[epidemiology-ebm/statistics-for-clinicians]]; trial-level judgement in [[epidemiology-ebm/randomised-trials]]; the appraisal capstone in [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]]; and guideline machinery in [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]] and [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].

::: history
**The systematic review's prehistory is the clinical textbook's footnote tradition - the 'opinions-of-experts-adjacent-editions' problem that by the mid-twentieth century meant a chapter's evidence base was its author's reading list. The methodological ancestors: Karl Pearson's 1904 aggregation of correlation coefficients (the 'is-there-one-correlation' question), the Cochrane-arithmetic-that-wasn't of mid-century cardiology and surgery trials' small numbers, and the 'controlled-trial-documents' era's stubborn finding that famous negative trials (the 'the-famously-failed-therapies-that-worked-slightly' reversals) hid as much as they showed. The modern field crystallised around three 1970s-80s innovations: Archibald Cochrane's 'Effectiveness and Welfare' (1972) with its 'there-is-no-excuse-for-ignorance-of-which-treatment-works' argument for a continuously updated register of trials by specialty, the Oxford controlled-trials-collaborative-group's obstetric registrations, and the meta-analysis coinage (Glass 1976, in education's 'thousand-small-trials' problem) plus the statistics matured by Hedges, Olkin, DerSimonian and Laird (1986's random-effects moment). The institutional build-out: the Cochrane Collaboration's 1993 founding (with its ICBM-and-CCAT-lineage editors' office, its review protocols and the 'risk-of-bias-is-not-quality' culture), the EPPI-Centre-and-CRD's methods production line, Sackett's centre-at-Oxford and the evidence-based-medicine rebrand that made the method clinical medicine's grammar, GRADE's 2000-origin-adoptions (the 'GRADE-Working-Group's-2004-GUIDE'-series), and the PRISMA statement's 2009 revision of the meta-analysis-reporting-Quality-of-Reports-of-meta-analyses lineage. The counter-histories matter too: the 'meta-analysis-in-medical-journals-1970-2010' proliferation (the 30,000-reviews-registered-protocols-many-abandoned audits), the 'wrong-question-right-answer' critiques from clinical specialties, the 'intervention-vs-mechanism-review-confusion' genre wars, and the industry-capture investigations that moved the field to declare funding sources as a GRADE-adjacent domain. The pandemic was the method's forced maturity: the living-review format (the 'evidence-in-motion' versions-with-diffs), the preprint-inclusion debates, the rapid-review-methods-standards (the NICE-and-Evidence-Synthesis-Collaboration-Guideline), and the embarrassing overviews-of-overviews showing dozens of near-duplicate living reviews whose disagreements mostly traced to search dates - a demonstration that speed without registration discipline recreates the literature the method was built to fix. The frontier is machine-assisted and human-accountable: automation validated rather than trusted, review registries audited for abandonment, and the 'review-as-infrastructure-not-publication' repositioning its founders always intended.** - [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]], [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]].**
:::

::: summary
- A systematic review is a measurement of a literature: its question, protocol and search are its apparatus, and its conclusions are only as valid as their recall, appraisal and reporting - which is why registered protocols and PRISMA flows are audit handles, not formalities.
- Risk-of-bias tools name mechanisms with directions rather than issuing quality scores; the synthesis's sensitivity analyses are the places those mechanisms are priced.
- Pooling is modelling: choice of model, of what may be pooled, and of the prediction interval are decisions whose consequences are clinical; heterogeneity is information about context-dependence, not statistical weather.
- Publication bias is an ecology, addressed by registries, grey-literature craft and selection models with their assumptions stated - and the strongest remedy remains getting every trial.
- GRADE and evidence-to-decision separated evidence certainty from recommendation strength, which made guideline reasoning inspectable and, critics note, smuggled values into standardised arithmetic - an argument the field should hold openly.
- The review ecosystem's pathologies (abandoned protocols, duplicative reviews, uncited updates, spin) are methodologically addressable and increasingly addressed - living reviews, umbrella de-duplication, and reporting audits; the clinician's job is to read the newest review's search date before its conclusion.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Write the question {level=1}
Convert to answerable systematic-review questions with PICO and comparator explicit: 'do probiotics help antibiotics?', 'is laparoscopy better than open surgery?', 'what causes postpartum depression?' Name the design filter and the main trap for each.
::: solution
**Questions-that-can-be-answered ([[#def-method]]): (a) In adults prescribed oral antibiotics (P), multi-strain probiotic preparations (I) versus placebo or no probiotic (C), incidence of antibiotic-associated diarrhoea within 8 weeks (O) - trap: strain-and-dose heterogeneity collapsing distinct interventions into 'probiotics' (the 'are-apples-and-oranges-both-fruit' problem) and outcome-definition drift across trials (the 'diarrhoea-defined-per-study' audit). (b) 'Better' must become a primary outcome (laparoscopic versus open colorectal resection: 30-day complications and length of stay, oncological-survival non-inferiority as co-primary in cancer populations) - trap: comparing eras and surgeon experience (the learning-curve and secular-trend confounding the 'open-to-laparoscopic-transition' literature documents) and publication of positive early series. (c) 'Causes' exceeds observational designs' reach - answerable version: risk factors and their adjusted associations (the pre-pregnancy-and-perinatal cohort evidence; the 'depression-defined-by-EPDS-versus-diagnosis' heterogeneity) - trap: reverse causation (early symptoms confounding the antenatal predictors) and the heterogeneity of the outcome's social measurement. The exercise's rule: the comparator and the outcome definition decide whether the review can conclude anything, and questions that skip them are grants, not reviews.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Run the search {level=2}
For 'mechanical ventilation strategies in severe asthma exacerbations', draft the search strategy: the concepts, the term families including free text and subject headings, the filters considered and rejected, the grey and trial-registry sources, and the recall-validation step.
::: solution
**A-search-as-protocol ([[#def-method]]): Concepts: asthma-exacerbation AND mechanical-ventilation (three faces), with the third concept (outcomes) deliberately unsearched - outcome terms narrow recall without adding specificity. Term families: subject headings (MeSH 'Asthma/therapy', 'Positive-Pressure-Breathing-Medical'; Embase's Emtree equivalents) exploded with subheadings, free-text variants on both British and American spellings (asthma, exacerbation, status asthmaticus, intubat*, ventilat*, NIV, BiPAP, ECMO-adjacent as the rescue-therapy question's neighbour), proximity and adjacency operators for the phrase forms, and the search strings published verbatim. Filters considered and rejected: language limits rejected with the 'translation-and-cost-versus-bias' reasoning and recorded; 'humans' and age bands kept only if the question is adult-only (stated); the Cochrane RCT-centrality filter used as a supplement, never as the whole search (the syndrome of reviews that only find indexed trials in this literature where physiology studies and case series carry the management evidence). Grey and trial-registry sources: WHO ICTRP and ClinicalTrials.gov searches for ongoing and completed-unreported ventilation-strategy trials, the ERS-and-ATS-statement-and-consensus-conference bibliographies, thesis and conference-abstract harvests (the 'ICM-conference-abstracts-yield-class' quantified), and citation searches forward and backward from the known trials. Recall validation: the 'known-studies list' method (nine studies the group agrees must be found, checked against every platform), the librarian's second-strategy design (the 'search-as-skill-not-spellcheck' professional standard), and dual independent screening of 10 percent with the kappa and disagreement-resolution log reported. The flow diagram's obligations: deduplication software named, search dates, and the 'who-was-contacted-for-which-trials' correspondence record.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Grade the evidence {level=2}
Grade (high/moderate/low/very-low) with reasons per the GRADE domains: (a) ten consistent low-risk RCTs, total n=800, one outcome definition, precise (risk difference -8 percent, 95 percent CI -10 to -6); (b) the same but the comparator across trials is nothing, and current practice is a different drug; (c) observational cohort evidence, large, consistent, dose-response, confounding plausibly toward null; (d) meta-analysis with funnel asymmetry and two of five trials unregistered.
::: solution
**GRADE-with-reasons ([[#def-synthesis]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): (a) High: risk of bias none serious, inconsistency none (I-squared low), indirectness none, imprecision none (interval excludes harm and trivial benefit), no publication-bias suspicion (symmetric, registered) - a clean high. (b) Moderate at best on directness: the comparator's irrelevance to practice (the 'versus-nothing-when-something-is-available' indirectness) - the effect is real against placebo and unknown against the standard; the recommendation question ('add to standard versus switch') is unanswerable from this evidence, which is indirectness of the PICO-comparator kind. (c) Observational evidence starts low but upgrades for large consistent dose-response effects toward null-biased confounding: moderate, arguably, with the residual-confounding bound stated (the E-value logic) - the 'observational-upgrade-conditions' that GRADE's own guidance reserves for when confounding runs the protective direction and magnitude is large. (d) Serious risk of bias (unregistered trials with unknown outcome definitions), serious suspected reporting bias (asymmetry with missing small-harmful direction), imprecision unassessed - low, with the 'wait-for-registered-trials' rather than 'do-more-reviews' implication. The teaching point: GRADE grades per outcome and per comparison; the grade that changes the guideline is usually the indirectness or imprecision one nobody argued about, and the exercise's answers must survive the 'so what would you do?' test.**
:::

::: exercise Update the review {level=2}
You inherit a 2019 Cochrane review for update. Its search date precedes: two large trials (one registry-only), a retracted trial it included, and a 2022 living review on the same topic from another group. Write the update plan's decisions and their justifications.
::: solution
**An-update-is-a-new-protocol ([[#def-method]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): Question-and-criteria: re-affirm or amend PICO against the field's drift (the 'comparator-is-now-real-practice' update that changes conclusions more than new trials), pre-specified in the new protocol with deviations from the 2019 version listed explicitly. Retrieval: the re-run surveillance search from 2019 forward, the trial registers queried for completed-and-unpublished studies with the sponsor-and-author-request correspondence documented, and the retracted trial excluded with the retraction's forensic note (its effect on the previous pooled result quantified - the 'corrected-previous-evidence' transparency that makes the update citable). The living-review neighbour: the 2022 group's methods appraised (their search, their risk-of-bias, their model), the overlap managed by the 'de-novo-review-versus-living-collaboration-versus-documented-divergence' decision (the Cochrane-and-EPOC-adjacent policy options), with the honest option taken: either merge methods and co-produce, or publish with the comparison of the two reviews' conclusions in the discussion (the 'reviews-about-the-same-question-disagreeing-and-the-literature-not-knowing' situation the overviews-of-reviews exist to flag). Synthesis decisions: the model re-justified (the previous random-effects choice re-audited against current small-study-effect evidence), prediction intervals added, the certainty table rebuilt with the new trials' bias judgements, and the 'what-changed-and-why' summary written for the guideline consumers who will only read that box. The version-control ethic: the 2019 review preserved and superseded (the 'evidence-archaeology' record that lets future audits see when the field changed its mind), with the update's expiry trigger named (the two ongoing trials' expected-readout dates).**
:::

::: exercise Defend against the attack {level=3}
Your meta-analysis of a widely used drug shows a small increased cardiovascular risk (risk ratio 1.12, 1.02 to 1.23) driven substantially by registry cohort studies; trial-only synthesis gives 1.04. The manufacturer calls your result 'observational noise'; a clinician group calls your methods 'a witch-hunt'. Write the response's methodological core.
::: solution
**Between-design-triangulation ([[#def-synthesis]], [[#def-ecosystem]], [[epidemiology-ebm/bias-confounding]]): The numbers stated first: the trial-only estimate (1.04, interval including mild harm and mild benefit) answers 'effect under trial conditions, mostly shorter-term and healthier populations'; the all-evidence estimate (1.12) reflects real-world durations, comorbidities and the cohorts' residual confounding that could run either way (the 'confounding-by-indication-and-severity-who-gets-this-drug' channel with its direction argued explicitly). The response's method core: the pre-specified design-hierarchy decision and its justification (why cohorts were included - rare and long-latency outcomes the trials cannot speak to - the 'trials-are-silent-not-protective' point), the subgroup-and-meta-regression by design with its interaction test reported (the design-effect difference's own confidence interval), the confounder-adjustment audit (the 'most-adjusted-versus-minimally-adjusted' sweep and whether the estimates trend with adjustment quality - the calibration-of-observational-evidence method the pharmacoepidemiology comparisons systematised), the negative-control outcomes (does the exposure also 'cause' conditions unrelated to cardiovascular mechanism in the same cohorts? if yes, residual confounding shares the blame), and the reporting-bias apparatus (the trial registry completeness for cardiovascular safety trials, whose selective-publication asymmetry favours reassurance - the 'safety-signals-get-published-last' literature). The stance: neither 'witch-hunt' nor 'noise' - the estimates differ because they measure different populations and designs with different biases, both imperfectly understood; the clinical translation is honest ('if the drug's benefit in your patients is large, these data do not change use; if marginal, the plausible harm range now includes 12 percent relative'), the research ask is specific (the pre-registered emulated-trial design against a target-trial protocol, and the individual-participant-data requests), and the data and code are published because that is the only currency the attacked should carry.**
:::

::: example The duplicate-review jungle {#ex-dup}
A clinician finds eleven systematic reviews of SGLT2 inhibitors and heart-failure outcomes published since 2019 with overlapping but non-identical conclusions and one that recommends on 'very-high-quality evidence' another rates low. Write the triage.
::: solution
**Reviewing-the-reviews ([[#def-method]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): The first cut is currency and coverage: search dates versus the field's evidence velocity (the 2019 reviews predate the class's renal-and-CKD-trial readouts - a review is a photograph of a moving literature), then the AMSTAR-2-style methodological audit (registration, dual processes, risk-of-bias incorporation, funding declarations - the 'meta-analyses-of-biased-methods-inherit-the-bias' rule), then the question-match test: whose PICO is the clinician's (hospitalisation-versus-mortality composites, the population's ejection-fraction split, the comparator's realism). The overlap quantification: the 'AMSTAR-overlap-and-correction-of-duplicate-publication' methods and the de-duplicated study-count (the 'ten-reviews-three-unique-new-trials' arithmetic that deflates apparent consensus), and the trial-versus-review-count honesty (the 'reviews-are-not-evidence-trials-are' correction for guideline panels). The 'very-high-quality-versus-low' discrepancy resolved by reading the certainty reasoning, not the label: usually different indirectness calls (the composite-outcome-to-mortality translation) or risk-of-bias weighting of the same unblinded trials. The output: the newest methodologically soundest review whose question matches, cross-checked against the living-trial-register for post-search readouts; the duplicates cited only as redundancy evidence; and the 'which-review-should-I-read' sentence the clinician's note writes for next time: registered protocol, search date after the last landmark trial, risk-of-bias tool used and prediction interval shown.**
:::

::: exercise Appraise the search {level=1}
A published meta-analysis's methods say: 'We searched PubMed for articles published in English between 2010 and 2020 on X and Y.' List the recall failures and their probable bias directions.
::: solution
**One-database-one-window ([[#def-method]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): The failures: single-database (the MEDLINE-only recall deficit for Embase-indexed-and-Europe-and-pharmacology-heavy literature with its documented magnitude), English-only (the language bias whose direction tracks which countries' trials publish where - the 'positive-results-in-English-journals-and-local-language-replication' literature), the date window (older trials excluded by fiat - the 'window-fits-the-funding-cycle' tell, and pre-window evidence potentially decisive), no grey or trial-registry sources (the unpublished-trials absence whose direction favours positive effects - the field's most reproducible bias), no published strategy (unauditable), and no hand-searching or backward-citation (the review's inability to find its own field's classics). The bias arithmetic: each filter is a hypothesis that the filtered studies' effects are null or nonexistent - claims that must be argued, not assumed. The remedy template: search the field's databases with strategy published, language limits defended or translated, windows justified by the evidence question, registries and conference abstracts harvested, and the whole flow in the PRISMA diagram. The teachable sentence: a search is a probability sample of the literature with a design and a known selection mechanism - which is why a bad search is worse than a small trial.**
:::

::: exercise Teach the difference {level=1}
Explain to a medical student, in one minute, the difference between a systematic review and a narrative review, and the difference between a living review and a rapidly outdated one. Give the student's reading-order rule for any review.
::: solution
**Three-differences-teaching ([[#def-method]], [[#def-ecosystem]]): The one-minute version: a narrative review is an argument with citations; a systematic review is a measurement of a literature with a protocol - the difference being whether someone else could reproduce your evidence base and disagree with your conclusions from the same numbers (reproducibility is the definition). A living review is a systematic review with a subscription model: continuous surveillance searches and published updates when the evidence moves; a static review is a timestamped verdict whose expiry depends on its field's velocity (the 'read-the-search-date-before-the-abstract' habit). The reading-order rule taught to the student: for any review, read in order - the registered question (was this the question), the search date (is the answer current), the flow diagram (was the evidence gathered honestly), the risk-of-bias summary (what is the evidence made of), the certainty table (what weight the authors themselves gave), then the discussion, then the abstract last - because the abstract is where spin lives, and the other five determine whether the spin is entitled to exist.**
:::

::: example The forest plot lie {#ex-forest}
A conference slide shows a forest plot of eight trials with a striking pooled effect; the slide's plot has no fixed axis range, two trials' weight bars look too wide for their sample sizes, and the pooled diamond's label reads 'pooled OR 0.62***'. Audit what the picture is doing.
::: solution
**Forest-plot-forensics ([[#def-synthesis]]): The axis: log-scaled odds-ratio axes default to symmetric-about-1 ranges; a squashed or truncated axis manufactures drama - check where 1 sits and whether the grid is logarithmic. The weights: inverse-variance weights correlate with sample size and event count, so a wide box on a small trial signals either many events (a case-mix clue worth chasing) or a mis-entered standard error - the 'weight-inconsistency' check (recompute approximate weights from the printed intervals) that conference plots routinely fail. The missing pieces: no prediction interval (the pooled diamond presented as the answer to 'will it work here'), no risk-of-bias column or symbol shading (the GRADE-adjacent cochrane-style plots' feature), the study-label abbreviations hiding population differences, and the three-asterisk convention importing significance theatre into a field that reads intervals. The pooled-label check: recompute mentally from two trials - if the printed diamond disagrees with the plotted squares' weighted centre, the software's model (fixed versus random) is doing something the slide does not name. The verbal audit at the session: 'which model, what prediction interval, and do the weights match the intervals?' - the three-question test that has ended three drug-launch slides' confident arcs in the appraisal literature's collected embarrassments.**
:::

::: exercise Build the de-duplication {level=2}
Your guideline group must reconcile four systematic reviews on the same clinical question with overlapping primary studies, conflicting conclusions and one review including a trial the others exclude for design. Write the reconciliation method.
::: solution
**Overviews-of-reviews-methods ([[#def-method]], [[#def-synthesis]]): The method: extract each review's included-study list and search window (the 'citation-matrix' construction), quantify overlap (the 'corrected-document-overlap-index' and plain unique-trial counts - the number that tells the group how many randomised trials actually exist), and identify each review's search date and the trials published between. Update the evidence to a common date: the 'primary-studies-current-to-today' re-tabulation (which is, in practice, commissioning the overview's own living surveillance rather than adjudicating yesterday's). Adjudicate inclusion disagreements on the protocol: the design-excluded trial examined against each review's registered eligibility rules (the 'one-review-broadened-post-hoc' audit - its deviations documented), with the trial's risk of bias assessed directly rather than inherited. Decide by the pre-specified evidence architecture: one authoritative synthesis (usually a new de-novo or substantially updated review that the group produces or adopts with its own registered protocol - the 'AMSTAR-2-critical-reviews-demoted-to-context' rule), with the four existing reviews cited as the evidence map. The transparency output: the overlap figure, the trial-by-review matrix as an appendix, and the 'why-conclusions-differ' box (search dates, eligibility, model choice, certainty judgements) - the format the overviews-of-reviews-methodology (the 'A-Do-Not-Add-Reviews-without-De-duplication' campaign) now insists guidelines attach.**
