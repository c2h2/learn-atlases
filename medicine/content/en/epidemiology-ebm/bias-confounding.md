If study design determines what a study can show, bias determines what it falsely shows. **This chapter is the error taxonomy every appraiser carries: selection bias (who enters and who stays - Berkson, healthy-worker, loss-to-follow-up), information bias (misclassification of exposure and outcome, differential and non-differential, and the recall-and-interviewer machinery that produces them), and confounding (the third-variable problem with its causal-diagram formalism, its adjustment traps - colliders, mediation, overadjustment - and its residual-and-unmeasured shadow). It closes with the correction toolkit: restriction, matching, stratification and modelling, directed acyclic graphs and their counterintuitive collider lessons, quantitative bias analysis, and the negative-control and falsification habits that separate appraisers from critics.**

## Who gets in

::: definition Selection bias and its eponyms {#def-selection}
**Selection bias is error in who constitutes the study population, and it operates at three doors. Entry: the people who become subjects differ from those who could have - the healthy-volunteer effect that makes cohort participants thinner and less smoky than their postcodes; the healthy-worker effect whose two acts (hiring the fit and retaining the tolerant) depress occupational mortality below the nation's and create the 'the smelter lives longer' absurdities that referent-choice literature exists to debunk; and Berkson's admission-rate bias, whose odds-ratio-distorting mechanism the case-control chapter drew: hospital controls are selected by exposure-influenced admission. Retention: loss to follow-up biases only when the losing is related to exposure and outcome together - the conjunction students should memorise, because dropout percentages alone prove nothing while the two-by-two of loss by exposure and outcome status says everything; informative-censoring corrections (inverse-probability-weighting, worst-case bounds) convert the worry into stated uncertainty. Time-window selection: immortal-time bias - exposure definitions that require survival long enough to be classified (the user-person-time designs in which the interval before first prescription cannot contain death, manufacturing spurious protection that a landmark or test-negative design, or an appropriately lagged definition, removes). The appraisal habits: ask who could have entered and did not; ask what the lost would have had to look like to change the answer (the 'e-value-for-dropout' arithmetic); and ask whether survival, admission or attendance was quietly part of the exposure definition. Selection problems also plague the comparison the study never considered possible: the 'collider-stratification' unity between selection bias and the causal-diagram literature means the same diagram that diagnoses confounding diagnoses these too, which is why this chapter and the next read as one subject.**
:::

## What is measured

::: definition Information bias and misclassification {#def-information}
**Information bias is error in the variables themselves. Misclassification is differential when the error rate depends on the other variable - the case-control's twin terrors: mothers recalling teratogens in proportion to the malformation, interviewers probing cases harder - and non-differential when it is not, which usually attenuates (the toward-the-null theorem) with the textbook exceptions where correlated errors manufacture associations. The quantitative layer appraisers should own: the sensitivity and specificity of exposure and outcome measures applied to a two-by-two (observed odds ratios shrink toward one as classification degrades, which makes imperfect instruments conservative only under symmetry), the correction formulas behind quantitative bias analysis, and the exposure-specific subtleties: recall bias (directional, worse for rare and anxious exposures), social-desirability under-reporting (the weight, alcohol and diet data on which nutritional epidemiology builds its caveats), interviewer bias (blinding as prophylaxis), and diagnostic or surveillance bias - the screened populations have more disease artefact that makes mammography, prostate, thyroid and atrial-fibrillation detection studies hostage to detection intensity (the screening chapter's old friend). Outcome misclassification has its own politics: the hard-endpoint versus soft-endpoint hierarchy (death versus pain scores), the adjudication committees of trials, and the code-versus-medical-record validation studies that registry-based epidemiology must publish or be discounted by. The correction habits: measurement validation subsamples with regression-calibration corrections, instrument standardisation across sites (same scales, same times, same technicians - the metrology discipline multinational surveys fund or regret), and pre-specified handling of missing data (complete-case analysis being a missing-completely-at-random assumption wearing pragmatism's clothes). One more entry for the ledger: regression to the mean follows any variable measured with error at a threshold - the treat-the-highest-readings triage whose extreme values fall by themselves, the phenomenon that inflates apparent benefit in before-after and watchful-waiting comparisons and the reason controls exist.**
:::

## The third variable

::: definition Confounding: the problem, the tools, the traps {#def-confounding}
**A confounder is associated with the exposure, causal for the outcome, and not on the exposure's causal path - the three-clause definition whose third clause is the one clinicians violate when adjusting for a mediator of the drug's own effect (adjusting for oedema when asking whether a calcium-channel blocker lowers blood pressure answers a different question). Confounding is real population structure, not bias: it is correctable in principle by design (randomisation balances it in expectation; restriction removes its variation; matching balances it on chosen variables at the cost of studying those variables and of mandatory matched analysis) and by analysis (stratification - the Mantel-Haenszel tradition and the stratum-versus-crude comparison that defines Simpson's reversal; regression adjustment, with its functional-form dependence and the interpretation shifts that follow what else enters the model). Directed acyclic graphs converted confounding from arithmetic into geometry: backdoor paths and the adjustment sets that block them; colliders - common effects whose adjustment manufactures association (conditioning on hospital admission, on a selection indicator, or on any downstream variable shared by exposure and outcome creates paths that were never there); the mediation decomposition that turns 'is it via BMI?' from a confession into an estimand; and the M-bias and table-bias patterns where naively-safe adjustments open doors. When diagrams are silent (and they usually are about magnitudes), quantitative bias analysis supplies the arithmetic of the unmeasured: the confounder-prevalence-and-strength calculations behind E-values and sensitivity tables - 'how strong must an unmeasured confounder be to explain the result?' is answerable and increasingly mandatory, and the honest answer is sometimes 'implausibly strong' and sometimes 'a smoking-adjacent lifestyle factor'. The tools combine: negative-control exposures and outcomes detect residual confounding empirically (the healthy-user effect discovered by finding the protective association for accidents), active-comparator designs replace the impossible 'takers versus nobody' contrast, within-person designs (case-crossover, self-controlled risk interval, fixed-effects across a person's own time) subtract all stable confounding at once, and triangulation across designs with different assumptions - the law of statistical variations' older cousin - is how causal claims finally mature.**
:::

::: example The healthy-user effect {#ex-huser}
An observational study finds hormone-replacement therapy users have 40 percent lower coronary disease; the randomised trial later finds an increase. Explain the bias structure that produced the observational result.
::: solution
**Confounding-by-the-healthy-who-take ([[#def-selection]], [[#def-confounding]], [[pharmacology/patient-variation]]): the structure: hormone-replacement uptake selected for the whole healthy-user phenotype (higher socioeconomic position, better diet, more exercise, more screening and health-care contact - the takers-versus-non-takers contrast being a comparison of two different ways of being a patient), plus depletion of susceptibles (early events among prevalent users removed by the prevalent-user design), plus timing (the trial randomised older women years past menopause - the 'timing hypothesis' that reinterpreted rather than erased part of the difference). The appraiser's forensic tools, available then and still underused: active-comparator design (users of another healthy-woman medication as controls - the bisphosphonate and vitamin comparator studies that showed healthy-user effects for almost every preventive prescription), negative-control outcomes (accidents and injuries, where a truly cardioprotective drug should do nothing but the healthy-user effect protects there too), and sensitivity analysis (how biased would socioeconomic adjustment have to be to explain 40 percent? implausibly - which was the pre-trial warning). The lesson the field kept: the contrast clinical questions ask is rarely 'takers versus non-takers of anything' but 'this drug versus the realistic alternative for this patient', and the designs that respect the contrast - randomisation, active comparators, within-person designs - are the ones that survive.**
:::
:::

::: example The adjustment that lied {#ex-adjust}
A registry study of a perioperative drug reports mortality benefit after adjusting for 'all recorded covariates including the complication the drug is given to prevent'. Reviewers demand reanalysis.
::: solution
**Mediator-adjustment-and-collider-traps ([[#def-information]], [[#def-confounding]], [[epidemiology-ebm/statistics-for-clinicians]]): the error named: adjusting for a variable on the causal path (the complication is downstream of the drug's mechanism, so conditioning on it estimates a direct effect while the question was the total effect - overadjustment that erases benefit), and worse, adjusting for colliders when the covariate is a common effect of drug assignment and patient fragility (admission-conditioned analyses inducing spurious protective associations - the index-event-bias literature from pharmacoepidemiology). The reanalysis: pre-specify the estimand (total effect of the treatment strategy, the intention-to-treat analogue), adjust only for pre-exposure covariates (the temporal-sequence rule read off a diagram), use the complication in a mediation analysis (how much of the effect travels through it - the informative question) rather than as a confounder, and run the propensity-versus-regression disagreement check as a model-missification alarm. The general heuristics the editorial should state: adjustment lists are arguments, not housework (adjusting for everything is a category error - select variables by diagram, not by software stepwise defaults), and every covariate should carry its causal address: cause, proxy, mediator, collider or descendant. The study's salvage: the honest reframing (no total-effect benefit detectable; direct-effect estimates unchanged) usually fits in the same abstract, if the estimand was written before the modelling.**
:::

## Where this leads

Bias appears inside every design in [[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]; its quantification methods reappear in [[epidemiology-ebm/statistics-for-clinicians]]; confounding control is what randomisation buys in [[epidemiology-ebm/randomised-trials]]; detection-bias twins are in [[public-health/screening]]; and appraisal practice is drilled in [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]].

::: history
**The bias vocabulary accreted around embarrassments. The recall-bias literature grew from the teratogen scares of 1961-62 (the mothers whose memories differed by outcome), and interviewer bias from the same decades' methodological autopsies. Berkson's 1946 paper on hospital controls gave selection bias its eponym and, decades later, its formal collider mathematics. Healthy-worker bias was named from the occupational mortality surprises of the 1950s-60s (the arsenic workers with 'low' mortality from all causes) and instrumented by Checkoway and Kriebel's modelling generation. Confounding had Simpson's 1903 reversal and Yule's critiques of correlation as its early theory; the three-clause definition, the Mantel-Haenszel estimators of the 1959-73 sequence, and Cornfield's blocking-and-adjustment apparatus made it arithmetic; the definitional papers of 1979-83 by Rothman, Greenland, Robins and colleagues settled the 'confounding as bias versus structure' taxonomy. Miettinen's epidemiological tradition (confounding as a design problem, precision as its sibling) and Lilienfeld's textbook fixed the teaching. The causal-diagram revolution arrived from computer science: Neyman-Rubin potential outcomes (1920s-70s), then Greenland-Robins and Pearl's do-calculus (1990s-2000s) turning adjustment into graph surgery and colliding - literally - with the statistics establishment's 'no-causation-without-manipulation' reservations. The collider-catastrophe literature of the 2010s (the body-mass-index-and-fracture, admission-and-outbreak and pregnancy-adjustment retractions that the diagram approach predicted years earlier) converted the geometry into clinical warnings, and the reporting-and-replication reforms of the 2010s made sensitivity analysis a reviewer demand rather than a discussion-section gesture. Randomisation's other half of the story - the confounding it dissolves and the generalisability and honest-blinding problems it keeps - belongs to the trial chapter.** - [[epidemiology-ebm/randomised-trials]], [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]].**
:::

::: summary
- Selection bias is error in who is studied: entry (healthy-worker, healthy-user, volunteer), retention (differential loss requiring the exposure-and-outcome conjunction), and time-window (immortal-time) mechanisms each have named fixes - referent choice, sensitivity bounds, and design hygiene.
- Information bias is error in what is measured: differential misclassification manufactures or reverses associations; non-differential usually attenuates; validation subsamples, blinding, standardised instruments and honest missing-data assumptions are the countermeasures.
- Confounding is population structure, not bias: handled by restriction, matching, stratification and modelling, and diagnosed by comparing crude with adjusted estimates and reading stratum-specific patterns (Simpson reversals).
- Causal diagrams are the adjustment grammar: adjust for causes and their proxies, never for mediators (when asking total effects) or colliders (whose adjustment induces associations studies then 'adjust away').
- Residual and unmeasured confounding are bounded, not dismissed: E-values, negative controls, active comparators and within-person designs let observational claims carry stated uncertainty.
- The appraiser's stance: bias questions are directional ('which way, how much?'), never existential ('therefore nothing is known'), and the best studies pre-empt the answers.
:::

## Exercises
::: example The screening arm's advantage {#ex-leadtime}
A cohort shows screen-detected cancers survive twice as long as symptom-detected ones; the hospital quotes this in its cancer-funding submission. Identify the two classic biases and the analysis that partially rescues the comparison.
::: solution
**Lead-time-and-length ([[#def-information]], [[public-health/screening]]): lead-time bias: screening advances the diagnosis date without changing death, so survival-from-detection inflates by the lead time (the 'same-death-earlier-start' geometry that all survival-from-diagnosis comparisons inherit); length bias: slow-growing tumours are over-represented among screen-detected cases (the sojourn-time distribution that makes screening look effective at indolent disease), plus the over-diagnosis limit (the tumours never destined to cause harm - the prostate and thyroid arcs as the cautionary tales). Partial rescues: compare mortality rates (not survival) in screened versus comparable populations (the randomised-trial endpoint the funding submission should use), estimate lead time directly (modelling or trial reconstruction) and subtract, stratify by tumour grade and biology (the length-bias probe), and report the over-diagnosis excess with the incidence-trend decomposition. For symptom-detected comparisons specifically, the 'interval-cancer' framing (cancers arising between screens, aggressive biology) is the honest reference class. The submission-grade sentence: 'screen-detected cases appear to survive longer by arithmetic alone; the mortality evidence, which corrects the arithmetic, is the number we should be quoting.'**
:::
:::

::: example The statin that looked best in non-adherers {#ex-adherence}
A cohort finds statin adherents (pill-count above 80 percent) have half the mortality of non-adherents; the authors conclude 'statins halve death'. What stratum of bias dominates, and what design corrections exist short of a trial?
::: solution
**Healthy-adherer([[#def-selection]], [[#def-confounding]]): the dominant stratum: adherent behaviour is a marker of the whole survival-favourable package (care-seeking, diet, exercise, fewer interruptions for terminal decline - the 'reverse-causation-at-its-slickest': the dying stop their statins months before dying, the depletion-and-healthy-user blend). Corrections without randomisation: active-comparator new-user design (initiators of statin versus initiators of another chronic drug, contrasting adherence within comparable committers - the design that shrinks the effect dramatically in the classic re-analyses), time-varying adherence with lagged windows (excluding recent stoppers from the 'adherent' risk set - the 'depletion-of-susceptibles-and-early-stoppers' handling), conditional-and-marginal structural models that target treat-and-switch strategies rather than ever-user folk categories, instrumental-variable attempts (the preference-by-prescriber and distance-to-pharmacy instruments - valid instruments are rarer than papers claiming them), and negative-control outcomes (the accident-and-minor-infection tests that expose healthy-adherer residual). The teaching version: adherence is an exposure variable that carries a lifestyle biography and a prognosis; the 50 percent is mostly the biography, some is the drug, and only designs that separate commitment from chemistry - ultimately randomisation - say the split, which is why adherence-informed pragmatic trials and platform designs are the endpoint of this literature's honesty arc.**
:::

Label each scenario (selection, information, or confounding; name the eponym if famous): (a) cases remember past exposures more thoroughly than controls; (b) a factory cohort dies less than the nation overall; (c) adjusting for gym attendance when estimating the effect of a supervised exercise programme on weight; (d) hospital controls over-representing respiratory disease in a lymphoma study; (e) a screening trial reporting longer survival among detected cases.
::: solution
**Taxonomy-drill ([[#def-selection]], [[#def-information]], [[#def-confounding]]): (a) information - recall bias, the case-control differential. (b) selection - healthy-worker effect (hire-and-retention filtering); the referent choice is the fix. (c) confounding-versus-mediator trap: gym attendance during the programme is a mediator (part of the intervention's mechanism), so adjusting for it is overadjustment - the third-clause violation; pre-programme activity would be a legitimate confounder. (d) selection - Berkson-type admission bias (respiratory admissions relate to the exposure of interest, distorting control exposure odds). (e) information/measurement family - lead-time bias with length bias riding along; survival statistics are the vehicle. The exercise's meta-skill: each named bias carries its direction and its countermeasure - (a) blinding-and-biomarker-substudies, (b) internal referents, (c) estimand discipline, (d) population controls, (e) mortality endpoints. The learner's milestone is when the names stop being trivia and start being a checklist that runs while reading the methods section.**

::: exercise Draw the diagram {level=2}
For the question 'does alcohol cause liver cirrhosis in adults?', draw (describe in words) the directed acyclic graph including smoking, socioeconomic position, BMI, healthcare access and a collider; then state the correct adjustment set and two wrong ones with their errors.
::: solution
**A-DAG-with-lesson ([[#def-confounding]]): the diagram: alcohol to cirrhosis (the question's arrow); socioeconomic position to both drinking patterns and cirrhosis (via work, nutrition, healthcare) - a confounder; smoking to cirrhosis and associated with drinking - confounder or mediator depending on structure (for the alcohol question mostly confounder); BMI to cirrhosis and associated with alcohol intake (shared environment) - confounder; healthcare access to cirrhosis diagnosis and associated with drinking (screening and presentation) - confounder for diagnosed-cirrhosis outcomes; the collider: hospital admission, caused by both heavy drinking and advanced cirrhosis - conditioning on it (recruiting inpatients) opens a spurious path. Correct adjustment set: socioeconomic position, smoking, BMI (and baseline-health markers measured before exposure), plus restricting or stratifying sensibly. Wrong set one: add hospital admission (collider - induces drinking-cirrhosis association among the admitted regardless of causation, the admission-rate artefact). Wrong set two: add oesophageal-varices-or-ascites (mediators on the alcohol path - adjust them away and the alcohol effect vanishes with them, the overadjustment that answers 'effect not via decompensation'). The pedagogic close: the diagram is the study protocol's missing page; the paper should contain it, and appraisers should refuse claims without one when the covariate list includes anything a patient could share with an outcome.**

::: exercise Quantify the unmeasured {level=2}
An observed adjusted hazard ratio of 0.70 for a Mediterranean-diet pattern and mortality is claimed. An unmeasured confounder (health-food-shop access) is plausible: assume it is 30 percent prevalent among high-adherence people versus 10 percent among low, and raises mortality 1.5-fold. Estimate the confounded direction, compute the crude-to-adjusted implication, and decide whether the claim survives.
::: solution
**Bias-analysis-arithmetic ([[#def-information]], [[#def-confounding]], [[epidemiology-ebm/statistics-for-clinicians]]): direction first: the confounder is positively associated with the exposure and with harm - it inflates (biases away from null... careful: makes healthy-diet look protective) the protective association, so the true effect is weaker than 0.70. The standard 2x2x2 confounding formula (Rothman/Greenland's): the confounded relative risk equals the true risk ratio times [p1R + (1-p1)]/[p0R + (1-p0)] with p1=0.30, p0=0.10, R=1.5 giving the factor (0.30x1.5+0.70)/(0.10x1.5+0.90)=1.15/1.05=1.095 - the observed 0.70 divides to a true roughly 0.76: the claim's magnitude shrinks but its direction survives these assumptions. Sensitivity sweep: for the claim to be entirely confounded (true 1.0) the hidden confounder would need prevalence ratios near 3 with mortality relative risks near 2.2 - a combination the literature's measured correlates make implausible; that is the E-value-style statement (the observed association's E-value near 1.9 asks how strong an unmeasured confounder must be: above any single plausible named one, below the whole package of them). The verdict format: 'with plausible-but-strong access-to-health-food confounding the effect remains real but modest; the 30 percent reduction claim requires accepting that no unmeasured cluster reaches the sweep's thresholds, which the food-environment literature does not support.' The skill being graded: numbers, not adjectives.**
:::

::: exercise Rescue the case-control {level=2}
Your oesophageal-cancer case-control study recruited controls through general practices, but practices differed in response rates and the exposure (very-hot tea) is culturally patterned by practice population. Name the bias and its mechanism here, and specify three design and three analysis remedies.
::: solution
**Control-selection-meets-cultural-clustering ([[#def-selection]], [[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]): the bias: the controls' exposure distribution must represent the source population's; practice-based sampling with differential response by culture (hot-tea-drinking communities under-answering, or over-represented in participating practices) breaks representativeness - selection bias of the control-sampling variety, and the matching-by-geography that seems to fix it can overmatch (killing the exposure contrast). Design remedies: community-wide probability sampling (the telephone-or-address-frame tradition with honest response-rate reporting), friend-and-neighbour matching at the individual level (preserving exposure-generating environment while restoring representativeness within strata), and two independent control sources whose agreement (or disagreement) diagnoses residual selection. Analysis remedies: participation-weighting (inverse-probability of response using practice- and area-level covariates), restriction to comparable practice populations (the 'same-source-population' framing), and quantitative bias analysis for the exposure-prevalence among non-respondents bounded by area survey data. The interpretation guard: very-hot-tea evidence has a genuine thermal-injury mechanism (the temperature-gradient studies), so the appraisal question is magnitude, not existence - state it that way before the reviewers accuse you of dismissing the finding.**
:::

::: exercise Spot the immortal time {level=2}
A cohort of transplant recipients reports that post-transplant vaccination halves mortality (hazard ratio 0.5) with 'time-independent' analysis. Identify the immortal-time mechanism and rebuild the analysis.
::: solution
**Surviving-to-be-vaccinated ([[#def-selection]], [[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]): the mechanism: if vaccination status is fixed at transplant and the vaccinated are those who lived long enough and well enough to be vaccinated, the interval from transplant to vaccination is immortal (death cannot occur in a vaccinated person's earlier time, or they would be classified unvaccinated) - a guarantee of survival smuggled into the exposure label, whose magnitude grows with the lag's length (the classic simulations showing hazard-ratio-of-half from nothing). The rebuild: time-dependent exposure (vaccination as a state people enter at calendar time, with person-time reassigned - the 'at-risk-at-that-moment' bookkeeping), or target-trial emulation with landmark analyses (landmark at day 180: only those alive enter the risk sets, vaccination status as of the landmark, outcomes after), or risk-set-sampling designs (the nested self-controlled variants). The appraiser's two-minute test for this whole family: ask 'could a patient die before becoming exposed?' - if yes, demand the time-dependent analysis; the 'transplant-and-EPO-and-mechanical-ventilation-and-rehabilitation-referral literatures' each contain papers un-made by this question. The reporting addition: state the immortal-time assessment in the methods, because after the 2010s methodological campaigns its absence is itself a red flag.**
:::

::: exercise Defend the observational finding {level=3}
Your cohort shows a 45 percent asthma reduction with early-life farm exposure. A journal demands proof against confounding; your region's genetics, migration patterns and healthcare are uneven. Write the falsification programme: five analyses with what each rules out and what none can rule out.
::: solution
**A-triangulation-programme ([[#def-confounding]], [[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]): Analysis 1, negative-control exposure: a farm-unrelated healthy-lifestyle marker (organic produce, dog ownership without livestock) - rules out that any rural-affluent lifestyle carries the protective association; rules out nothing about farm-specific mechanisms if it also protects. Analysis 2, negative-control outcome: injuries and eczema-of-non-atopic-type - the hygiene hypothesis predicts selective effect on atopic outcomes; a uniform protective effect across everything signals healthy-user residual. Analysis 3, dose-response and timing: animal-contact intensity from childhood records and the 'moved-to-farm-after-six' contrast - the critical-window structure a confounder rarely imitates. Analysis 4, within-sibling design: farm versus non-farm households with the same parents (the 'fixed-effects-family' models subtract shared genetics, socioeconomic position and household culture - the confounding family-level proxies miss). Analysis 5, Mendelian-randomisation-adjacent or molecular mediation: farm-dust-associated microbial and immune markers in a nested subsample (the mechanism chain: exposure-to-microbiome-to-Treg-to-phenotype - not proof of effect but a chain a confounder must fabricate end-to-end). What none can exclude: a farm-correlated-and-childhood-persistent confounder measured nowhere (the honest sentence), and the possibility the association is only the farm selection into and out of farming by respiratory-susceptible families (the healthy-farmer-and-suitability-alteration bias that occupational epidemiology preaches about). The cover letter's line: causation is not proven but bounded - the surviving explanations are specific, measurable and improbable together, which is as close as population medicine comes.**
:::

::: exercise Correct the trial analysis {level=2}
In a randomised trial, 12 percent lost to follow-up in the intervention arm versus 4 percent in control, concentrated among the poorest and sickest. The published analysis is complete-case with a reassuring 'sensitivity: no significant difference at baseline'. Advise the statistical editor.
::: solution
**Differential-censoring-and-the-complete-case-fantasy ([[#def-selection]], [[epidemiology-ebm/statistics-for-clinicians]]): the diagnosis: complete-case analysis under missing-at-random requires loss unrelated to outcome given covariates - the concentration among the sickest (the highest-event-risk group) makes that assumption not just unproven but directionally threatened (losing the sickest treated patients under-estimates treatment harm or over-estimates benefit depending on structure). The demanded reanalyses: multiple imputation with tipping-point sensitivity (the 'what-if-every-dropped-patient-fared-x-worse' curve made in the paper, not the supplement - the pattern that decides whether conclusions flip), inverse-probability-of-censoring weighting with the loss model's covariates and diagnostics, worst-case and best-case bounds (the 'range-of-defensible-results' presentation), and the 'retrieval-of-minimum-outcome' sweep if any subset can be followed by record-linkage (the death-registry check that converts missing to known for the worst-off stratum). The reporting asks: the loss-by-arm-by-burden table, the reasons-for-loss by arm, and a statement of what the missing patients would have had to look like for the trial's conclusion to reverse - the 'delta' framing that replaces reassurance with quantification. The editor's decision rule: differential loss is not a fatalism ('all trials have drop-outs') but an estimand problem; where the sensitivity curve crosses the null within plausible bounds, the conclusion must be worded within those bounds. The prevention note for future protocols: retention funding and outcome-retrieval plans (registry-consent at randomisation) are design features whose absence this case now bills publicly.**

::: exercise Teach colliders in ten minutes {level=3}
Design a ten-minute small-group teaching segment that makes clinicians feel collider bias, using a single relatable example. Include the numerical demonstration, the common clinical practice it indicts, and the one-sentence rule they should leave with.
::: solution
**Collider-teaching-by-feeling ([[#def-confounding]]): the example: admission to hospital. Both anxiety and major trauma raise the probability of hospital admission; restrict a study to admitted patients and anxiety appears protective against trauma, because in that room everyone is there for a reason, and for the calm patients the reason must be something else. The negative association exists only among the conditioned - created purely by the sampling frame. The numerical demonstration (small 2x2x2, hand-computable): independent exposure-and-outcome in the source population (risk ratios all 1.0), then condition on the common effect with simple probabilities; the admitted-only table yields a visible non-1.0 association - the classroom gasp point when they compute it themselves. The clinical practices it indicts: adjusting for admission, ICU transfer, diagnosis-because-diagnosed, or 'completed-treatment' status; the 'study-of-hospitalised-patients-only' designs; the healthy-survivor filters in registry analyses; and the 'condition-on-the-eligibility-criteria' traps in A/B and service evaluations (the 'online-users-survey-epidemiology' class). The one-sentence rule to leave with: never adjust for, restrict to, or stratify by anything that the exposure and the outcome both cause - and when unsure whether a covariate is a collider, draw the two arrows and look at where they land. The follow-up reading: the collider-paradox case series that made this a journal-club staple (the hypertension-and-body-weight examples where conditioning produced famous reversals), because the lesson sticks when it has victims.**
