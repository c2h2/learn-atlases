Clinical toxicology is medicine where the history, the clock and the antidote outrank the scan: a handful of syndromes (the toxidromes), a handful of time-critical agents (the 'learn-the-lethal-few-then-ring-the-poisons-centre' list), and a handful of procedures (alkalinisation, the antidotes, dialysis) that must arrive at the right minute. **This chapter covers the general approach to the poisoned patient (the priorities and the classic misses), the toxidromes as pattern-recognition, the specific poisons that kill in ordinary quantities at ordinary times (paracetamol's nomogram-and-N-acetylcysteine machinery, salicylate's double acid-base crime, the tricyclic sodium-channel problem, the cardiovascular poisonings' high-dose-insulin era, the toxic alcohols' osmolar-gap trap), enhanced elimination and its shrinking-but-real indications, and the psychiatry-and-safeguarding interface without which the toxicology is unfinished. Its two maxims: 'the poison is rarely the only diagnosis and the only poison is rarely taken', and 'treat the patient, then the level, then never the level alone'.**

## The approach

::: definition The poisoned patient: priorities and toxidromes {#def-approach}
**The priorities (the airway-breathing-circulation-disability-exposure discipline with the glucose and thiamine and oxygen and the 'what else could this be' differential of hypoglycaemia and head injury and infection and metabolic crisis and carbon monoxide-with-the-pulse-oximeter-lying-to-you that makes toxicology a resuscitation-room specialty not a poison-list recital), the exposure history that is half the treatment (the witness and paramedic and family accounts and the empty packs counted and described by colour and imprint and the prescription and over-the-counter and herbal and 'the vet's tablets' list and the route and time since ingestion because elimination and antidote timing and the extended-release trap depend on the hour and the intent question ('how many and why' asked early and kindly because the psychiatric disposition and the observation-period planning start with the why) and the poisons-centre or clinical-toxicology-service phone call which is the cheapest specialist opinion in medicine and is what turns a generalist into a toxicologist at 3am); the investigations that actually change management (the glucose and the paracetamol-and-salicylate levels in every intentional overdose with the 'too early to interpret means repeat, not reassure' rule and the ECG with the QRS and QT intervals read as measurable poison-effects across the sodium-, potassium- and calcium-channel-blocking drugs and the gas with the anion gap and osmolal gap and lactate and the renal and creatine kinase and potassium chemistry (the high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis differential - toxic alcohols, salicylate, iron, lactate from shock or cyanide, ketoacidosis - with the osmolal gap adding the alcohols and the honest caveats that gaps lie at both ends of the timeline) and the urinalysis for oxalate crystals and myoglobin and the honesty that urine drug screens are mostly useful for what they exclude and never for a treatment decision); the toxidromes as pattern-recognition (the opioid toxidrome of respiratory depression and miosis and reduced consciousness with the pulse-oximeter-versus-gas nuance and the anticholinergic delirium - blind as a bat, hot as a hare, with tachycardia, urinary retention and dry axillae distinguishing it from the sweating sympathomimetic and serotonin syndromes - and the cholinergic syndrome of organophosphates and carbamates with its wet bradycardic fasciculating miosis and its atropine-and-pralidoxime medicine and the sympathomimetic agitation-hypertension-hyperthermia syndrome whose management is benzodiazepines, aggressive cooling and avoiding restraint-into-rhabdomyolysis rather than antipyretics alone and the sedative-hypnotic group (the ethanol-benzodiazepine-barbiturate-GHB-baclofen-with-sudden-fluctuating-deep-coma group with the withdrawal corollary that for alcohol, benzodiazepines, baclofen and gabapentinoids the withdrawal seizures are the killer, not the intoxication); and the general decontamination position (activated charcoal within about an hour of a significant ingestion in a protected airway, decided with toxicology advice, with the hydrocarbon and corrosive and iron and lithium and 'sedation-will-lose-the-airway' contraindications; the gastric-lavage-and-syrup-of-ipecac era closed; and whole-bowel irrigation reserved for the body-packers and specific sustained-release, iron and lithium ingestions in monitored patients - niche techniques the poisons centre remembers for you).**
:::

## The time-critical poisons

::: definition Paracetamol, salicylate, tricyclics, cardiovascular poisons {#def-specific}
**Paracetamol first because it is the commonest real poison in the English-speaking world (the therapeutic-misadventure-versus-intentional-overdose case-mix and the alcohol-and-starvation-and-enzyme-inducer risk strata that lower the treated line): the mechanism (the NAPQI-glutathione stoichiometry which is the entire pharmacology explained to an eight-year-old and why N-acetylcysteine is glutathione substrate arriving fast) and the four-hour-level nomogram with its treatment lines and the 'unknown time of ingestion means serial levels and a low threshold to treat' rules and the 2020s treatment-threshold-lowering debate across the British, Australian and US poison services and the shorter and single-bag NAC regimens with the anaphylactoid-reaction lesson 'slow the rate, keep the cure' and the hepatology-arrives-early-not-late principle (the INR-and-creatinine-and-lactate-and-pH trajectory and the King's College criteria for transplant listing with the arterial-lactate and pH inclusions) ; salicylate (the double crime of early respiratory alkalosis and later metabolic acidosis with the mixed picture and near-normal pH as the danger signal not the reassurance and the non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema and hypokalaemia and the CNS-glucose-paradox timeline where tinnitus is the early claim and the acidosis the late bill and the management: serial levels and urinary alkalinisation as the named elimination technique - potassium-repleted bicarbonate with a urine-pH target - and the intubation warning that taking over a salicylate patient's own compensation causes the peri-intubation arrest, and the early haemodialysis decision for the acidotic, comatose, renally-failed or massive-ingestion patient which clears it beautifully); tricyclic and sodium-channel-blocking drugs (the QRS-prolonging-seizure-hypotension triad with sodium bicarbonate for a wide QRS as both antidote and mechanism-test, the 'no physostigmine, no class-I antiarrhythmics, lipid emulsion for refractory collapse' rules and the observation windows for any ingestion with delayed-toxicity potential); the cardiovascular poisonings as the resuscitation-ICU joint (the beta-blocker and calcium-channel-blocker overdoses separated by the calcium-channel-blocker's reflex tachycardia and the beta-blocker's bradycardia and the 'the sustained-release strip presents at hour six or dies at hour nine' observation rule and the treatment ladder that changed practice: calcium and glucagon and vasopressors now sitting alongside high-dose-insulin-euglycaemia therapy - a named protocol with dextrose-and-potassium-and-monitoring logistics - plus lipid emulsion for lipophilic collapse and VA-ECMO as the bridge for a reversible poison with the 'do not prognosticate on vasopressors in a 21-year-old after nifedipine' rule and digoxin with its acute-on-chronic presentations and hyperkalaemia-as-the-killer-marker and Fab antibody fragments (the level becomes uninterpretable afterwards) and the reassessed 'never give calcium in digoxin toxicity' catechism).**
:::

## Elimination and disposition

::: definition Enhanced elimination, the alcohols, and the psychiatric interface {#def-elimination}
**Enhanced elimination as a shrinking, evidence-bounded toolkit (multi-dose activated charcoal for the carbamazepine-dapsone-phenobarbital-quinine-theophylline list with gut-tolerance reality; urinary alkalinisation for salicylate, phenobarbital, methotrexate and chlorpropamide with the modern verdict that haemodialysis often beats forcing diuresis; and the haemodialysis list the renal unit needs at 2am - the toxic alcohols (methanol and ethylene glycol) with alcohol-dehydrogenase blockade by fomepizole or ethanol infusion started empirically when the picture fits because the gaps lie and the levels arrive tomorrow, lithium with its 'the level does not predict the brain' neurotoxicity and rebound kinetics and dialysis triggers, salicylate and theophylline, with the poisons-centre call supplying the cut-offs); the alcohols as a chapter inside the chapter (ethanol withdrawal from the seizure to the delirium-tremens that kills and is prevented not cured - the symptom-triggered benzodiazepine protocols and thiamine-before-glucose culture the addiction chapters teach; methanol with its optic neuropathy and formic acid; ethylene glycol with its calcium-oxalate crystals and renal failure and its 'the metabolite is the poison' fomepizole logic; isopropyl alcohol with its ketosis-without-acidosis and its acetone-not-toxic exception that keeps the differential elegant); the co-ingestant and context rules (the 'every overdose is poly until proven otherwise' reflex with the workup list and the prescribing-review consequence and the therapeutic-dose toxicity mirror (the anticholinergic and serotonergic and parkinsonism loads that present as 'overdose' in renal failure - the polypharmacy chapter's reflection) and the special populations: pregnancy (treat the mother first - the fetus cannot be resuscitated from a dead mother - with the iron and calcium-channel-blocker and tricyclic sensitivity notes and NAC still given), children (the one-dose-can-kill list - tricyclics, calcium-channel blockers, theophylline, iron, sulfonylureas - and the 'a verified dangerous ingestion is observed, not reassured by one normal level' rule and the poisoning-as-neglect safeguarding lens), and the elderly whose accidental double doses are the commonest and least-reported geriatric poisoning); and the psychiatric and safeguarding interface that is the majority of the workload (the hospital self-harm service model with the 'medically fit to a person, not a trolley' principle and the enhanced psychosocial assessment covering lethality, intent, means restriction and protective factors and the 'the tablets were the symptom' discoveries - domestic violence, debt, psychosis, autism burnout; the Mental Capacity Act grammar of refusal in self-harm (she can refuse the charcoal and still need a psychiatric bed, and a capacitous refusal is not a suicide note to be overridden by protocol); the 'aftercare with a phone number, a face and a date' that remains the best-evidenced discharge intervention in the field and is still not commissioned consistently); and the social-medicine coda (the alcohol-price and drug-supply mortality debates, the naloxone-to-corner-shop opioid-overdose era and its fentanyl and novel-psychoactive-substance presentations with their 'the naloxone did not wake them' xylazine-and-benzodiazepine-co-ingestant twists, and the chem-sex and drug-facilitated-crime forensic-toxicology interface that belts up to the medicine in a clinic nobody staffs).**
:::

::: example The 'four paracetamol, it's fine' {#ex-para}
A 26-year-old ingests 'probably 30 tablets' at 20:00; seen at 23:30, well, LFTs normal, paracetamol level at 3.5 hours 'below the line'; the plan is 'discharge with psychiatry letter'.
::: solution
**Four-errors-in-one-discharge ([[#def-approach]], [[#def-specific]]): the errors - 'the level drawn before four hours is uninterpretable on a nomogram built from four-hour points, 'below the line' means nothing yet, a history of 'probably 30 tablets' in an intentional overdose must be treated at the worst credible dose not the estimated one, and a psychiatric letter is not a psychiatric assessment; the correction - 'repeat the level at and after the four-hour mark and apply the nomogram with the history and renal function (co-ingestants that delay gastric emptying - anticholinergics, opioids, sustained-release formulations - justify later peaks and a lower threshold to treat) and start NAC if the line is crossed or the timing or dose is uncertain in the dangerous direction because the drug is remarkably safe and the liver is not, with the poisons centre named in the notes; then the full workup (the salicylate and alcohol levels, the ECG, pregnancy status, the 'what else and where are the other packets' question and observation windows for delayed-toxicity co-ingestants); and the disposition - 'she leaves with a person, not a letter: the psychosocial assessment with her story (intent, means now restricted, protective factors), the follow-up with a name and date and crisis numbers that work at the weekend, the GP informed within 24 hours, and the 'you will be offered help, not judgment' sentence - which is what she came to find out.'**
:::

::: example The amlodipine late-fall {#ex-cacb}
A 44-year-old ingests 'the whole strip' of amlodipine at 22:00; at 06:00 she is anxious but BP 108/64, HR 96, glucose 8; the night team documented 'likely benign, CCBs are safe'; she is on the ward 'for observations' without a plan.
::: solution
**Sustained-release-calcium-channel-blocker-observation-is-a-treatment-plan ([[#def-specific]], [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]]): the physiology-timeline - 'amlodipine's slow peak and the sustained-release formulations mean toxicity arrives at hour six to twelve, often suddenly: the vasodilation with reflex tachycardia, the capillary leak and non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema, the gut that stops and the shock that resists catecholamines; 'the glucose is normal' is not reassurance, it is the pre-dinner normal that will not stay; the plan written now - 'ICU or high-dependency transfer for a dose-history like this (the ward cannot run a high-dose-insulin-euglycaemia protocol at 3am), the charcoal question re-asked (time-window, protected airway, whole-bowel irrigation for sustained-release in a monitored patient with toxicology advice), large-bore access and group-and-save, and the calcium-glucagon-vasopressor-insulin-dextrose-potassium regimen ordered as an anticipation plan, with the VA-ECMO capability conversation made by phone now ('she is young, this poison is reversible, we will not prognosticate our way out of a rescue') and the re-look times documented, because the dangerous hours are the ones where she looked fine.**

::: example The headache household {#ex-co}
Two adults and a child from the same house present over a weekend with headache, vomiting and confusion; the first two are labelled 'flu' and 'migraine'; the third arrives obtunded with a cherry-pink hue and a saturation of 92 percent that does not move with oxygen.
::: solution
**Carbon-monoxide-occupies-the-differential-people-forget ([[#def-approach]], [[respiratory/gas-exchange]]): the pattern - 'a household with simultaneous neurological-and-GI symptoms in cold weather or after boiler or generator or charcoal-brazier exposure is carbon-monoxide poisoning until the detectors say otherwise, and the missed diagnosis is famously serial: each member presents alone to a different clinician and each individual story sounds benign; the confirmation - 'the co-oximetry-measured carboxyhaemoglobin (the pulse oximeter cannot distinguish it and reads falsely comfortable) with the smoking-context for baseline and the gas-and-lactate-and-troponin-and-ECG-and-consider-CT-for-the-neurological-complications and the pregnancy-test-with-the-fetus-affected-at-lower-maternal-levels rule; the treatment and the system - 'high-flow-oxygen-with-the-100-percent-non-rebreather-or-consider-mask-with-PEEP-physiology-debate and the hyperbaric-oxygen-transfer-conversation-per-local-and-national-guidance (the indications contested - syncope, neurological signs, cardiac involvement, pregnancy - and the transfer-decision-is-time-critical-and-specialist-advice-now) and the public-health-must-do: the gas-safety-and-fire-and-rescue-service-and-HSE-adjacent-notification-called-tonight-because-the-house-is-still-poisoning-whomever-returns and the documentation of exposure for the compensation and the inquest and the neighbours; and the cognitive-autopsy - 'the two missed attendances will be in the serious-incident report and the fix is the triage question-'who else in the house is unwell'-which costs one sentence and has ended more carbon-monoxide families'-stories than any chamber.'**

A 33-year-old, two hours after an overdose of her own fluoxetine and 'something for sleep', states she will leave to 'be alone and think'; she is angry, oriented, and refuses bloods, ECG and observation. Manage with the Mental Capacity Act and the poison both in view.

The self-harm psychiatry and aftercare detail is in [[psychiatry/self-harm-suicide-prevention]], the addiction medicine in [[psychiatry/substance-use]], the dialysis logistics in [[renal/acute-kidney-injury]], the cardiovascular resuscitation overlap in [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]], and the prescribing-review angle in [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]].

::: history
**Poisons are the commonest self-inflicted emergency, and their treatment history alternates between heroism and harm. Toxicology became a laboratory science in the 1810s with Orfila's animal experiments and forensic method, and the antidotarium grew from wartime chemistry: British Anti-Lewisite, developed against the First World War arsenical gas and reborn as dimercaprol, founded the chelation lineage that taught medicine a molecule can mop a metal. The cautionary spine is made of reversed treatments: the emetic-everything era of syrup of ipecac and copper sulphate that killed more reliably than many poisons, ended by the charcoal trials and the 1980s-90s consensus that decontamination is a specialist decision; the physostigmine enthusiasm for anticholinergic delirium cooled by conduction-death case reports; class-I antiarrhythmics into tricyclic overdose producing the asystoles that made the measured QRS a textbook rule; and the 'the level is the treatment' fallacies, where a number overruled a patient - lithium, digoxin and paracetamol each supplying examples. The nomogram is the field's greatest achievement and most-stressed artefact: the Prescott-era Glasgow work of the 1970s relating the four-hour paracetamol level to hepatotoxicity risk, the intravenous N-acetylcysteine introduced by the same group, the two-bag and shorter-regimen trials of 2013-23, the anaphylactoid-reaction re-analyses that taught 'slow the rate, keep the cure', and the still-running argument about where the treatment line belongs now that presentations and risk factors have changed. Method restriction supplies the public-health half: the mid-century coal-gas suicide epidemic and its decline with gas decarbonisation, the Edinburgh research showing deadly-poison availability drove self-poisoning deaths, the falling barbiturate mortality with the benzodiazepine replacement, and the paracetamol pack-size legislation - natural experiments conducted on the dead. The current chapter is the opioid and novel-psychoactive-substance crisis (fentanyl and its analogues, the 'two-fluorobenzyl' and 'WO183'-style research-chemical names arriving in toxicology case reports within months of synthesis, naloxone moving from ambulance to corner shop, and xylazine-adulterated supplies producing the 'naloxone did not wake them' presentations) alongside psychiatry's older discovery, made in the 1950s-60s and named 'parasuicide' by Shneidman, Ringland and Gottiparla in 1961 and developed in the Manchester and Edinburgh cohort studies, that far of what presented as accidental or therapeutic poisoning was communication of despair - the finding that created self-harm services, means-restriction counselling and the aftercare guidance that still waits to be implemented. Toxicology's verdict on itself: it is the specialty whose founding treatments include legislation about what may be sold and how, and whose best trials are often policy changes measured in mortality charts.** - [[psychiatry/self-harm-suicide-prevention]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]].**
:::

::: summary
- In poisoning, the history (agent, amount, time, intent, co-ingestants) is the diagnosis-maker; the poisons-centre call is cheap, fast and standard of care.
- The universal workup: airway and glucose, paracetamol and salicylate levels in every intentional overdose (interpretable only from 4 hours), an ECG with intervals measured, a gas with gaps, and the 'what else' question.
- Toxidromes organise the undifferentiated patient: opioid, anticholinergic, cholinergic, sympathomimetic, sedative - and withdrawal from alcohol, benzodiazepines, baclofen and gabapentinoids kills after the intoxication would have.
- The rescues worth their hours: NAC early for paracetamol, urinary alkalinisation and dialysis for salicylate, bicarbonate for tricyclic QRS, high-dose-insulin-euglycaemia plus early intensive care for calcium-channel and beta-blocker overdose, Fab for digoxin, fomepizole for the toxic alcohols.
- Decontamination is a specialist niche (charcoal within the hour, whole-bowel irrigation for selected agents); enhanced elimination is shrinking but real.
- The psychiatric assessment is part of the toxicology: self-harm patients leave to a person with a plan and a date, and the means-restriction conversation prevents the next presentation.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Read the gaps {level=1}
Gas: pH 7.08, pCO2 2.6 kPa, HCO3 7; Na 140, Cl 100: calculate the anion gap; give the five most relevant differentials in a poisoned patient with visual symptoms and a drinking history.
::: solution
**Gap-arithmetic-and-methanol ([[#def-elimination]]): the gap = 140-(100+7) = 33, albumin-corrected aside - a large high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis with compensation that should be checked against Winter's rule and the visual symptoms plus drinking history make methanol the working diagnosis ('snowstorm vision', optic-disc hyperaemia, and an osmolal gap that may already have normalised late because the parent alcohol has metabolised to formate - the 'gaps lie at both ends of the timeline' lesson); the differentials - methanol and ethylene glycol (with the crystals and renals), salicylate (the mixed picture with low pCO2), lactate from shock, cyanide or metformin, and ketoacidosis diabetic or alcoholic (with the ketones and glucose and beta-hydroxybutyrate); and the action - fomepizole or ethanol infusion started empirically now, the haemodialysis team woken, folate/folinic cofactor decided with poisons advice, while the level is centrifuged and the rule 'do not wait for the gas to decide' written where the next doctor will read it.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Manage the cholinergic crisis {level=2}
A farm worker arrives mid-spraying-conversation: salivation, lacrimation, vomiting, diarrhoea, bradycardia 48, wheeze with secretions, fasciculations, miosis, confused. Sequence the treatment and the safety.
::: solution
**Organophosphate-poisoning-execute ([[#def-approach]], [[public-health/environmental-occupational]]): the safety-first - gloves, aprons, removal and bagging of contaminated clothing and the 'who drove in the cab and who did the laundry' decontamination history, because staff casualties of organophosphate arrivals are a documented occupational-medicine genre; the atropine - the litres-not-micrograms logic (repeated escalating IV doses until the chest is dry and the bradycardia answers; the dry chest, not the tachycardia, is the endpoint) and pralidoxime given early because its entire value is before the enzyme ages, with the extended-infusion protocols the research era settled ('more, not less' pralidoxime); benzodiazepines for seizures and agitation; ventilation with the succinylcholine-avoidance trap (cholinergic upregulation prolongs paralysis) remembered; the day-1-to-4 intermediate syndrome (proximal weakness and respiratory failure after apparent recovery) diarised so the ward doctor recognises what the ICU does not yet know about; and the after - suicide-or-accident established (organophosphate self-poisoning in agricultural economies is one of the great preventable-death stories, with pesticide regulation the WHO's cited means-restriction success), mental-health follow-up, cholinesterase monitoring and occupational reporting if accidental.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Decide on dialysis {level=2}
Which get haemodialysis now and which get 'supportive, no role': lithium 3.2 with tremor and renal failure; acute theophylline 1200 mg with tachyarrhythmia; isopropyl alcohol with ketosis; methanol with pH 7.05; sustained-release metoprolol with shock.
::: solution
**Dialysis-decisions ([[#def-elimination]], [[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]): dialyse now - the lithium with neurological features and renal impairment (late distribution and rebound mean prolonged or repeated sessions and a level that lags the brain), the theophylline with arrhythmia and rising repeat levels (it is dialysable and serial levels are obligatory), and the methanol with that pH (fomepizole is a bridge, not a replacement; dialyse on clinical grounds and treat the gap as optional); do not dialyse - the isopropyl alcohol (ketosis-without-acidosis, acetone is poorly cleared and benign-ish: supportive care and airway protection are the consultation) and the sustained-release metoprolol (a large-volume, lipid-partitioned, rebound-prone drug where the treatment is high-dose insulin, vasopressors, lipid emulsion and an ECMO bridge, not a dialyser - the 'dialysis makes us feel busy and helps nobody' lesson toxicology and nephrology teach each other nightly).**
:::
:::

::: exercise Name the syndrome {level=1}
Classify and treat: (a) 19-year-old festival attendee, temp 40.2, HR 130, clonus and hyperreflexia, agitated; (b) 74-year-old started on two new psychotropics, fever 39, rigid, bradykinetic, CK 4000, days into the course; (c) 30-year-old 'body packer' arrested at the airport, then seizure.
::: solution
**Three-mimics-three-clocks ([[#def-approach]], [[psychiatry/substance-use]]): (a) serotonin syndrome (the clonus-and-hyperreflexia-and-hyperthermia-and-agitation with the MDMA-or-co-administered-serotonergics history): stop the serotonergic drugs, benzodiazepines, aggressive cooling, and cyproheptadine where available; (b) neuroleptic malignant syndrome (the lead-pipe rigidity and bradykinesia and days-long evolution after dopamine blockade with the rhabdomyolysis and autonomic instability): stop the causative agent, cooling and dantrolene-or-bromocriptine-or-ECT territory with intensive care, and the distinction from (a) matters because the treatments rhyme but the mechanisms differ by receptor and time-course; (c) the body-packer with a seizure is cocaine-or-amphetamine-packet-rupture until proven otherwise - the whole-bowel-irrigation-in-a-protected-airway decision, the benzodiazepine-and-cooling-and-bicarbonate-for-QRS-if-sodium-channel-blockade and the surgical-or-endoscopic-rescue-plan-for-the-leaking-packet because 'packer' on the triage sheet means 'the dose is enormous and the clock is now', which is the toxidrome approach at its highest stakes.**
