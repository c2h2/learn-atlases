Major-incident medicine is the discipline of doing ordinary medicine in extraordinary numbers: the doctrine that turns chaos into triage, command structure and surge, written in advance because it cannot be invented in the first ten minutes. **This chapter covers the incident-system (the declaration and its phases, the command-and-control architecture, the four Cs, the interoperability and terminology lessons of London 2005 and Manchester 2017), the clinical doctrine under mass-casualty conditions (the START-and-JumpSTART triage grammar with its expectant-category ethics, the field-medicine and decontamination decisions, the injury patterns of blasts, crushes, fires and chemical agents), and the hospital's own major incidents (the internal surge, the cyber-attack that closes systems, the escalation to pandemic-scale) plus the aftermath literature (the recovery phase, staff-welfare and moral-injury data, the exercise and inquiry cycle, and why the after-action report is the most life-saving document in emergency planning).**

## The system

::: definition Declaration, command structure and the four Cs {#def-system}
**The definition and declaration (a major incident is any situation where the incident's demands exceed the service's routine capacity - the relative definition that makes a single-vehicle-crash-on-a-good-day and a-stabbing-with-no-ambulance-free both major incidents when declared by the first-responder-in-charge or the control room or the hospital's on-call executive with the duty-of-candour-and-documentation-rules applying to the declaration itself and its immediate review); the phases (the mitigation-preparedness-response-recovery cycle the UK's Civil-Contingencies-Secretariat-and-NHS-emergency-preparedness-guidance-and-the-US-NIMS-and-Australian-all-hazards-doctrines all teach: the pre-incident work of hazard maps and plans and exercises and stockpiles, the response hours, the recovery weeks of restorers and investigations, and the mitigation where public health argues with engineers about whether the next one is prevented or only survived); the command architecture (the gold-silver-bronze strategic-tactical-operational tiering borrowed from policing and used by fire-ambulance-hospital, the multi-agency coordinating centres and the hospital's incident-crew-or-incident-management-team with its roles named in laminated cards because memory fails at the wrong moment and its physical or virtual incident room with the 'the plan is the people who know the plan' maxim); the four Cs (command, control, coordination, communication, plus the fifth the lessons keep adding: clarity of language) with the two famous failure-syndromes that wrote the doctrine: the London 7 July 2005 responses where the three emergency services could not talk to each other on-air (the inter-operability reviews, the Airwave-style network investment, and the 'Gold-Silver-Bronze, METHANE, HETHUS, WASSHER, ETHANE and the other mnemonics the services now drill endlessly) and the communications-room overload and hospital-arrival-ambiguity (the 'hotline to each receiving hospital' practice after the Manchester Arena 2017 reviews with the 'forward-arrive-information' problem of the London Bridge same-night series where patients self-presented before any service knew); the triage system as shared grammar (the MARCH-or-PRTC-civilian-mover and 'serious-but-walking' doctrines of military-derived tactical-care-adjacent-civilians with the METHANE report (location-type-hazards-access-number-of-casualties-emergency-services-needs) standardised across the UK and the 'hot-hot-hot' and 'hot-warm-cold' arrival triage the EDs drill); the hospital-side surge levers (the internal-major-incident plan's graded actions: the 'call-in and cancel' with the staff-communications-tree-that-must-be-exercised-because-phone-lists-rot, the 'extra-critical-care-and-theatre-and-ward-capacity' plans with the 'surge-database' of equipment and bed counts updated monthly, the 'blood-massive-transfusion-protocol-and-X-ray-and-lab-and-mortuary (the 'HETHUS'-type-casualty-identification-sops with the police-identification-fire-service-adjacent-and-the-reconciliation-exercise-that-nobody-enjoys-and-everyone-who-has-done-says-was-worth-it) and the 'family-reception-centre-kept-far-from-the-ED-entrance'-lesson-of-2005-and-2017-because-family-crowding-the-receiving-hospital-is-a-second-incident-with-a-different-name); and the communication layer that the inquiries keep hitting (the plain-language-radio-protocols-and-avoidance-of-codes-not-understood-mult agency (the '10-4-and-codes-mean-nothing-to-the-hospital'-lesson), the single-information-line-per-incident and 'the hospitals are full' messaging to the public that saves the walking-wounded from self-presenting to the wrong door, the press-officer-as-clinical-safety-role-because-a-broadcast-'come-to-our-ED'-error-floods-a-hospital-and-the-major-incident-communications-training-that-now-includes-joint-information-centre-drills).**
:::

## The clinical work

::: definition Triage under mass-casualty conditions and the injury patterns {#def-clinical}
**The triage grammar (the START (Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment) for adults and JumpSTART for children: the respiratory-rate-and-perfusion-and-mental-status-algorithm-sorting-immediate-vs-delayed-vs-minor-vs-expectant (the 'expectant' category defined not as 'will die' but as 'dying or injuries so severe that survival requires resources beyond what the incident permits, and the ethical and psychological weight of that category on clinicians is planned-for in the training and the debrief, with the doctrine that it is applied under major-incident-authority not by individual bedside choice'), the Sieve and the Canadian Triage-and-Acute-Sensitivity adjacent systems and the 'expectant becomes immediate if capacity frees' re-triage rule that keeps the category honest; the paediatric and special-population corrections (the JumpSTART's-apnoea-after-5-breaths rule and the 'tag the child not the parent's panic' and the burn-and-crush-and-pregnancy-triage-adjacent-nuances and the hospital-at-basement-and-nursing-home-and-prison-and-school-adjacent-population-plans which are local plans that fail locally in exact proportion to how rarely they are exercised); the field medicine (the 'load-and-go versus stay-and-play' tension resolved by the scene's threat (the 'hot-warm-cold' zones and the fire-service 'portable-casualty-decontamination-and-the-90-second-antidote-atropine-pralidoxime-auto-injectors-for-nerve-agent-adjacent-scenarios' and the 'care-under-fire-tactical-bleeding-control' civilian-adjacency (the 'Stop the Bleed' and tourniquet-first lessons of 2017 UK and US terror responses with the lay-bleeding-control-campaigns' evidence of actually saving lives in real time)), the field-triage-point with its treatment-limits (the 'airway-positioning-and-tourniquet-and-oral-airway-and-no-intubations-unless-you-are-an-anaesthetist-sent-for-that'-doctrine), the casualty-clearing-station and the 'ambulance-and-hospital-are-one-system' reality (the ambulance-conveyance-and-destination-decisions-with-the-trauma-network-and-paediatric-and-burns-and-stroke-and-thrombectomy-and-ECMO-centre-adjacent-spread which is why the hotline to receiving hospitals is the clinical decision that matters most) and the secondary-devastation trap (the 'second impact' where responders and survivors die to the second device or the structure collapse, the 'hot-hot-hot' triage of 'care for the first casualty without becoming the second casualty')); the injury-pattern medicine (the blast physics: the primary pressure-wave pulmonary-and-bowel-and-tympanic-injury with the 'the chest X-ray may be normal at first and the abdominal catastrophe declares at hour twelve' observation rule and the secondary-fragment-and-tertiary-displacement-and-quaternary-burn-and-smoke-injury classes, the crush and confined-space syndromes with the crush-injury-medicine of potassium-myoglobin-alkalinisation-dialysis-availability-in-a-resource-collapsed-field-hospital (the amputation-for-crushed-limb-versus-death-by-reperfusion decision which is a triage-ethics-and-nephrology-logistics question at once), the fire-and-smoke syndrome of airway-burns-and-cyanide-and-carboxyhaemoglobin with the 'intubate-early-and-consider-hydroxocobalamin-if-available-and-transport-the-walking-burns-to-burns-units-not-the-nearest-hospital' rules, the chemical and nerve-agent decontamination medicine (the 'the casualty that is still contaminated is a hazard, not a patient' reality and the mass-decontamination-corporates and self-presenting-ambulatory-decon lanes with the 'people are more contagious than most agents' counter-lesson that has closed theatres for a colour and a smell), the stabbing-and-mass-casualty-violence doctrine (the 'do not wait for the ambulance - direct-to-resus major haemorrhage' lessons from the London Bridge 2017 and the 'early tourniquet and pelvic belt and blood first' pre-hospital blood-transfusion programmes' expansion), the pandemic-adjacent 'internal major incident with a rising curve' (the surge-ICU-capacity-and-ventilator-allocation-protocols and the 'reversibility-first triage in scarcity' literature whose ethical architecture was published before 2020 and stress-tested during it) and the child-and-school and mass-gathering-adjacent-planning with its own doctrine).**
:::

## The aftermath

::: definition Recovery, welfare and the learning system {#def-aftermath}
**The recovery phase (the 'stand-down is a clinical event' rule: the phased return to normal with the restock-and-equipment-repair-list-dictated-in-plain-language-and-signed because inventory after incidents fails exactly where the spares cupboard was assumed full, the debrief-and-AAR (after-action review) culture with its no-blame-for-behaviour-under-doctrine-and-full-accountability-for-ignoring-doctrine balance, and the exercises and real-events where the UK's Exercise Watermark and Gold-Shield cycles and the Australian and Canadian and US exercise-report public-summaries keep the doctrine alive between events); the human factor literature (the responder-morale-and-moral-injury research (the 'the first 72 hours are fine; the crash is week three' pattern that the welfare teams must be rostered for, the 'sleep, food, hydration, and a place to sit' leadership science the London 2005 and Manchester 2017 responses taught informally and the pandemic formalised, the bereavement-and-peer-support-and occupational-health pathways with the 'the expectation that stoicism is professionalism' culture change the services are still fighting, and the family-side aftermath: the acute-stress-and-PTSD-and-complicated-grief epidemiology that the disaster-psychiatry literature (the WHO's mhGAP-adjacent mental-health-guidelines-in-emergencies and the 'psychological-debriefing-does-not-work-single-session'-evidence and the 'watchful-waiting-and-CBT-and-scalpels-of-community-recovery' alternatives) which is public health's half of the response) and the 'children and schools after the incident' doctrine with its 'return to routine as treatment' evidence; the learning system (the inquiries and reviews that structure the lessons - the 7/7 reports and the Manchester Arena and London Bridge and the pandemic public inquiries and the US 9/11 and Hurricane Katrina after-action analyses whose findings rhyme: communications, command clarity, surge reality-checks, vulnerable-population plans, and the 'the plan assumed resources that the last audit had already cut' finding) and the resilience and mitigation literature (the Sendai-Framework-for-Disaster-Risk-Reduction-2015-2030 and the 'build back better' and the WHO's hospital-safety-index (the 'a safe hospital in a disaster is the disaster's ICU' logic: structural retrofitting, non-structural anchoring of plant and generators and oxygen plants, and the business-continuity of power-water-and-cyber-adjacent-utilities) which is where disaster medicine and public health and civil engineering meet in one budget line); and the clinician's personal preparedness (the 'know your plan, know your role, pack your bag' personal-readiness doctrine, the family-care-plan-that-lets-you-leave-home-in-a-storm-because 'who looks after my children' has ended more responses than fear has, and the professional and legal literacy of the ordinary clinician in a declared incident: the duty to attend-within-reasonable-safety, the altered-standard-of-care-under-declared-major-incident-doctrine (the 'you are judged against what was possible, not what is ideal' indemnity-and-guidance reality stated plainly so it changes behaviour at the triage point rather than surprising anyone later), and the documentation minimum in chaos (names, times, tags, and the incident log's copies which the civil-claims and inquests after every event prove are read line-by-line years later).**

::: example The Tuesday the systems went white {#ex-cyber}
07:40: the pathology informatics login screen is a red ransom note; within an hour clinical systems across the trust are down, phones are the network, three dialysis clinics and two cath-lab lists are running blind, and the incident room debates whether to declare a major incident 'because it isn't explosions'.
::: solution
**Cyber-attack-is-a-clinical-major-incident ([[#def-system]], [[#def-aftermath]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): the declaration - 'the-'systems-down'-scenario-is-the-textbook-internal-major-incident because the definition is capacity-not-kinetics: every digital process (blood-bank cross-matching, drug charts, theatre lists, CT triage transfer, the ambulance handover board) has been silently carrying safety and the paper-redundancy-that-only-works-if-drilled decides the day; the immediate clinical doctrine - 'the-escalate-declare-paper-and-downtime-forms-out-of-the-locked-cupboard (the downtime packs whose existence and staff familiarity are audited annually in resilient trusts and whose absence is the story in every post-attack review), the clinical-priority-reset (dialysis and chemotherapy and insulin pumps and anticoagulation and the transplant-and-dialysis-network phone tree and the 'no discharge without a printed medication list' and 'no blood without the manual crossmatch protocol' rules with the transfusion lab's manual-issue-sop), the decision-not-to-pay framing for the executive (the ransom decision belongs to national police and government advice, the 'is data exfiltration a patient-safety issue' candour question with the duty-of-candour-and-ICO-adjacent-notifications and the communications that keep the public from converging on the working-but-unpublishable ED); the surge and diversion - 'the-elective-lists-cancelled-as-a-safety-decision-not-a-cost-decision-which-is-the-hardest-call-a-medical-director-makes-on-a-Tuesday and the partner trusts and the ICB-and-region-notified-because-a-cyber-attack-spreads-through-shared-networks-and-suppliers (the pathology-and-blood-and-radiology-networks and the out-of-area-chemotherapy-and-radiotherapy-plans); and the recovery and its traps - 'the-forensic-timeline-preserved-before-the-itchy-finger-reboots-and-the-phased-restoration-with-validated-data-reconciliation (the 'who got which ward's drug chart back' check and the blood-transfusion-record-reconstruction) and the weeks-long clinical-burden-of-manual-working (the staff fatigue that is a patient-safety risk after the acute phase) and the board-level lesson: the attack surface is the hospital's dependence on vendors with worse backups than yours and the resilience investment that looks like IT spending is emergency-medicine spending.'**

::: exercise Judge the standard of care {level=2}

## Where this leads

Triage for the individual patient starts in [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], mass-transfusion logistics in [[blood/transfusion]] and [[surgery/trauma]], the decontamination and toxicology of chemical events in [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]], infectious pandemics in [[public-health/communicable-disease-control]], hospital safety in disasters in [[public-health/environmental-occupational]], and the mental-health aftermath in [[psychiatry/lifespan-psychiatry]].

::: history
**Civilian disaster medicine was written by fires and collapses. The 1919-1920s air-raid-ambulance thinking and London Blitz's casualty-clearing-stations, the UK National Service's Civil-Defence doctrine, and the 1966 Aberfan and 1966-68 series of industrial disasters gave Britain its first serious emergency-planning statutes. The modern era is triage-and-communications: the 1980s mass-gathering medicine (the Heysel and Bradford reviews, the Taylor Inquiry's medical-planning consequences), the 1987 Zeebrugge ferry disaster whose 'the dead were in the forecastle and nobody knew' chaos taught casualty-identification and family-reception doctrine, and the 1988 Pan Am 103 Lockerbie response's multi-jurisdictional lessons. The 2001 wave - the World Trade Center attack (the first documented field use of pre-hospital blood transfusion and the 'command chaos: 80 un-coordinated ambulance arrivals and 'hospital hotlines' and the 'plain-language radio' post-incident reforms that reshaped US emergency communication), the 2004 Madrid train bombings and the 2005 London bombings (the four-bomb-near-simultaneity that produced the three-service communications reviews and the interoperability investment), the 2008 Mumbai attacks, the 2011 Norway Utøya response and the 2015 Paris attacks (the 'tactical-fires-medicine-in-the-civilian-ED' reforms and 'the first ten minutes: running-wounded and direct-to-resus' doctrine). The 2017 Manchester Arena bombing (declared a major incident within 22 minutes) and the London Bridge attacks that same week consolidated the 'hot-warm-cold' zone doctrine, the community 'first-response-first-aid'-campaigns and the 'the NHS's major incident is the model other countries study' self-assessment that the pandemic stress-tested. The technological turn came with the 2017 WannaCry cyber-attack which taught the NHS the 'cyber-major-incident' (the 13 trusts and the 'care-not-security'-reviews and the cyber-resilience-test-and-citizen-certification-adjacent programme) and the 2020s pandemic which made 'surge capacity', 'the four Cs' and 'the exercise-based-preparedness-falls-apart-at-human-factors' a household vocabulary. The disaster-reduction framework grew alongside: the 1990s International Decade for Natural Disaster Reduction, the 2005 Hyogo and 2015 Sendai frameworks and the WHO's hospital-safety programme, whose core finding in earthquake after earthquake - the hospital that collapsed or lost power killed more patients than the seismic event alone - made structural and utility resilience a clinical discipline. The welfare half is the newest: the responder-PTSD and moral-injury literatures (the 9/11-responder-cohorts' decade-plus health registers with their medical-surveillance entitlements, the emergency-services suicide and PTSD studies, the 'the week-three crash' finding) and the disaster-psychiatry correction that single-session psychological debriefing does not prevent PTSD (the 1998-2010 trials) while 'watchful waiting, skills-based support and community recovery' do - a rare case of an evidence-based reversal that changed global guidance. The inquiry era continues: every event generates a review (the Manchester Arena Inquiry's 2022-24 volumes on distribution of emergency resources and the 'the lessons of 7/7 had been learned and partly forgotten' findings), and the discipline's honest summary is that its knowledge decays with each comfortable year and its exams are unannounced.** - [[surgery/trauma]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]], [[psychiatry/lifespan-psychiatry]].**
:::

::: summary
- A major incident is any event whose demands exceed routine capacity; declaration is a clinical safety act with documentation, and the doctrine runs mitigation-preparedness-response-recovery.
- Command structure (gold-silver-bronze), the four Cs, METHANE reports and shared triage language exist because the great inquiries (London 2005, Manchester 2017) found the same failures: services could not talk, numbers moved resources wrongly, families crowded the receiving hospitals.
- Mass-casualty triage (START/JumpSTART) sorts under scarcity; the expectant category is resource-relative, comfort-treated, documented and re-triaged - and its ethics are trained and debriefed, not improvised.
- The injury patterns (blast, crush, fire-smoke-cyanide, chemical, mass-casualty haemorrhage) each have a named clinical clock, and decontamination logic ('the contaminated casualty is a hazard, not a patient yet') protects the rescue effort.
- Hospitals declare their own major incidents: cyber-attack, system-down, internal disasters and pandemics all run on downtime plans, priority resets and mutual-aid networks.
- Recovery is part of the plan: restock, after-action review, responder-welfare ('the week-three crash'), family mental health, and structural resilience of hospitals themselves - Sendai's lesson that a safe hospital is the disaster's ICU.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Write the METHANE {level=1}
You are the first ambulance officer at a coach-crash on an icy motorway: the vehicle is on its side across two lanes, steam rising, around 40 seats believed occupied, several people already outside. Deliver the report and the first three orders.
::: solution
**METHANE-executed ([[#def-system]]): the report - 'M (major incident declared, ambulance control), E (location with junction and direction and the 'eastbound-carriageway-near-the-bridge' landmark for the helicopter), T (type: high-speed-collision rollover), H (hazards: diesel, ice, live hard shoulder, overhead wires if true, the risk to the third lane and the 'approach from upwind-uphill-upstream and 'the 'fire-and-rescue-en-route'-status), A (access: which gate and which lane to stage and the helicopter landing point nominated), N (numbers: 'best estimate 35-45 with multiple critical, ambulatory moving in the lay-by - this is an estimate and will be revised'), E (services needed: fire rescue extrication, multiple ambulances plus the critical-care and HEMS, the major-haemorrhage-and-paediatric-and-burns-aware-notifications, the receiving-hospital-hotlines and the police and the highway authority and the family-reception-venue-and-interpretation-plan-for-coach-passenger-lists); the three orders - 'the-'hot-zone' (the immediate-threat area with the steam-and-debris-and-traffic) managed with 'portable-casualty-collection-point-at-the-safe-embankment', 'the-'first-aiders-are-asked-for-and-the-tourniquet-and-recovery-position-doctrine-taught-to-the-able-bodied-passengers-in-90-seconds', and 'the-'triage-starts-at-the-ambulatory-cluster-not-at-the-wreck'-principle: sort the walking, reach the quiet ones, and the numbers-corrected-as-body-counts-and-seat-counts-diverge.'**

::: exercise Triage the school hall {level=2}
A collapse at a school event: 30 children present with minor injuries, 4 critically injured, 2 apnoeic with pulses and visible perfusion derangement. Apply JumpSTART logic and explain the two places you deviate from adult START.
::: solution
**JumpSTART-the-hall ([[#def-clinical]]): the sort - 'the-apnoeic-children-after-5-rescuer-breaths: with-a-pulse-but-no-breathing-triage-immediate (the-'apnoeic-arrest-from-airway-obstruction-and-hypoxia-is-reversible-in-children'-doctrine-that-defines-the-paediatric-difference); the-perfusion-and-mental-status-adaptations (the capillary-refill-andAVPU-paediatric-version with 'the child who will not look at you is sicker than the child screaming at you'), the ambulatory-sorted-delayed-minor-with-a-'hold-a-ball'-distraction-and-a-named-teacher-per-10-children because paediatric triage is half child-minding; the two deviations - '1: the '5-breaths-then-decide' apnoea rule (adult START gives expectant to apnoea-after-airway-repositioning because adult apnoea in blast-and-crush-is-terminal; children arrest from hypoxia and obstructive causes that rescue-breaths fix) and 2: the 'the parent is a resource and a casualty' management (the ambulatory children moved away from reunification chaos, the 'family-reunification-site-away-from-the-triage-point' rule because paediatric incidents generate an adult-casualty-wave-that-is-itself-a-triage-problem); and the handoffs - 'the-'paediatric-trauma-centre-versus-nearest-hospital'-decision (the 'the child is transported to paediatric-capable not nearest once stabilised' doctrine with the paediatric-transfer-arrangements), the 'tag-not-text' documentation (the name, time, interventions on the paediatric tag), and the debrief with the school's safeguarding lead because the event's aftermath includes the 200 children who watched it.'**

::: exercise Run the stand-down {level=2}
Your hospital's declared internal major incident (chemical spill with 12 decontaminated casualties) is over by 03:00. Staff are elated; the executive wants the all-clear at breakfast. What has to happen before normality is declared?
::: solution
**Stand-down-is-a-procedure ([[#def-aftermath]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): the clinical tail - 'the-decontaminated-but-still-at-risk patients (the delayed-onset agents: the phosgene-and-organophosphate-aged-adjacent-watch-windows and the 'asymptomatic-now-does-not-mean-safe-at-hour-six' observation floor with a named owner), the casualty-count reconciliation (who was here, where are the records, the 'who left against advice' list) and the 'were any of our staff exposed' incident (the nausea-and-headache-in-the-decon-team reported now not next week); the material truth - 'the-equipment-and-stock-recount-signed (the decontamination-shower-consumables and the antidote stock used and the 'the atropine draw-ups that were never documented' audit) and the downtime-forms-reconciliation (the 'paper drug chart from the corridor and the printed blood forms and the incident log vs the notes - the 'documentation debt' that becomes a clinical risk on day three); the humans - 'the-'food-water-rest-and-a-place-to-sit'-immediate-welfare and the 'the 'the-72-hours-are-fine-and-the-week-three-is-the-crash'-welfare-call scheduled with the occupational-health-and-peer-support contacts attached and the 'the manager whose job is to notice the quiet ones' instruction' and the staff whose families were at the incident-site-school (the 'who looks after my children' leave problem named openly); and the governance - 'the-hot-debrief-within-48-hours (what broke, what held) and the restock-and-repair-list-into-the-capital-plan and the 'the-declaration-and-declaration-ending-documented-with-rationale (the incident's start and stop are decisions and get minutes) and the after-action report due-date set with the plan-review-owner named-because-the-incident-is-over and the next one will read whatever you write tonight.'**

::: exercise Advise the resilience board {level=3}
Your region: plans exist on paper, exercises stopped after 2016, staff say they 'wouldn't know who to call', the oxygen plant has had no full-load test, and the cyber essentials were outsourced. Write the six-point programme you would stake a career on.
::: solution
**Resilience-programme ([[#def-system]], [[#def-aftermath]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the audit - 'the-'assumed-resources'-inventory (the plan's implicit supplies and spares and mutual-aid promises verified against current contracts and staffing rosters: post-incident reviews find 'the plan assumed the pre-2015 staffing model' more often than 'the plan was unknown'); the exercise cycle - 'the-graded-rehearsal-programme (the call-tree-test-monthly-which-is-cheap-humiliating-and-the-single-best-investment, the table-top per quarter, the full multi-agency exercise annually with the 'family-reception-and-casualty-identification-included-not-cut' rule and the 'exercise-evaluation-report-published-to-the-board'-discipline the national frameworks demand); the people - 'the-role-cards-and-incident-awareness-training-for-every-clinician-and-manager-with-the-'what-is-my-first-move'-competency (the 'I would follow whoever shouted loudest' finding that the drills fix) and the staff-communications-tree-with-two-person-verification and the welfare plan pre-written-because-welfare improvisation looks like an afterthought and is one; the utilities - 'the-oxygen-plant-full-load-test-and-generator-fuel-and-water-and-LPG-and-medical-gas-network-resilience-testing-on-a-scheduled-cycle-with-the-SHLR-adjacent-duty-holders-named (the Sendai 'safe hospital' logic: non-structural anchoring and utility continuity is clinical care in an earthquake and a winter nor both) and the vendor-and-supply-chain-dependency-mapping (the single-source pathology network and the outsourced cyber layer with contractual evidence of testing); the digital - 'the-downtime-packs-out-of-the-cupboard-twice-a-year-and-the-restore-tested-backups-with-the-'manual-working-week'-simulation (the WannaCry reviews' recurring finding: 'redundancy existed, familiarity did not') and the cyber-incident-playbook-with-clinical-consequences-prioritised (dialysis-chemo-transfusion-identified-first); and the learning - 'the-every-incident-and-exercise-AAR-with-three-findings-max-and-owners-and-dates and the annual board report on the resilience indicators (the 'we can survive a Tuesday' metrics, not 'we have a plan') and the partnership with the region (the mutual-aid rehearsals with the neighbouring trusts, the ambulance and local-resilience-forum and the voluntary sector which in every flood and storm turned out to be the surge capacity the plans keep promising: the British Red Cross and Mountain Rescue and mosque-and-temple-halls experience that resilience literature calls 'the community is first responder' and the NHS calls 'the bit we forgot to plan with'.'**
