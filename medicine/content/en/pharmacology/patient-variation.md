Patients are not healthy volunteers at 70 kg. **Age, weight, pregnancy, organ function, genetics, disease and other drugs shift exposure and sensitivity along curves that prescribing information averages away. Individualised dosing is the practice of asking, for this person today, what target concentration they need, what their volume of distribution and clearance are, and how the measurement will be checked.** This chapter assembles the modifiers, the pharmacogenetic ones in particular, and the systems that turn knowledge into a changed prescription.

## The body itself

::: definition Age, weight and organ function {#def-body}
**Neonates are not small adults: renal function matures over the first year with glomerular filtration and tubular secretion reaching adult rates around 6-24 months, hepatic oxidation is immature while some glucuronidation pathways mature late (the chloramphenicol grey-baby and sulfonamide bilirubin displacement lessons), volume of distribution is proportionally larger so water-soluble drugs need mg-per-kg doses close to adults despite 'immature kidneys', and allometric and maturation modelling now underpins paediatric licensing (the off-licence reality that most paediatric prescribing remains extrapolated, the sucrose-and-benzyl-alcohol excipient toxicity history, and the developmental-neurotoxicity animal-data debate informing why anaesthesia before age three is discussed with parents) - [[paediatrics/newborn]], [[paediatrics/growth-development]], [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]; older bodies change the numbers in the opposite direction: reduced renal reserve with normal creatinine hidden by low muscle mass, reduced hepatic mass and flow, more fat and less water changing lipophilic and hydrophilic distributions, and - the dominant effect - increased brain sensitivity to sedatives, opioids and anticholinergics so that the start-low-go-slow rule is mostly pharmacodynamics - the falls-delirium-hyponatraemia-constipation-retention syndromes that are geriatric pharmacology wearing internism's clothes, with the medication review as the treatable geriatric syndrome (the anticholinergic and benzodiazepine and NSAID and PPI conversations, and the prescribing cascades where a drug treats a side effect of a drug) - [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]], [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]], [[geriatrics-palliative/falls]]; obesity changes dosing drug-by-drug, not by a single rule: lipophilic drugs distribute into fat but fat is poorly perfused, so induction doses follow lean mass while some infusions accumulate, hydrophilic antibiotics need augmented dosing with evidence still thin at the extremes (the anti-Xa monitoring at very high weights, the direct-oral-anticoagulant data gaps above 120 kg, the anaesthesia weight-banding tables and bariatric surgery's absorption rewiring with modified-release formulations post-bypass) - [[pharmacology/absorption-distribution]]; and liver and kidney disease attack different parameters: renal failure lowers clearance and sometimes raises volume of distribution, dialysis adds an extracorporeal organ, while hepatic disease reduces extraction of high-extraction drugs through shunting, alters protein binding, and leaves oxidative capacity variably impaired - so the dose question is which pathway this drug uses and how much of it is broken, not which lab is worst (the acute-on-chronic day-to-day recalculation habit and the frail-kidney perioperative dosing) - [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]].**
:::

## Genes and their clinics

::: definition Pharmacogenetics {#def-pgx}
**Clinically actionable pharmacogenetics clusters by consequence: loss of drug effect through failed activation (clopidogrel and CYP2C19 loss-of-function alleles, reduced active metabolite and higher stent thrombosis in some trials, with genotype-guided de-escalation evidence and the population-frequency caveats) or through rapid inactivation (HLA types do not fit here - that is immune presentation); toxicity through accumulation (TPMT and NUDT15 variants and thiopurine myelosuppression now screened before azathioprine and mercaptopurine, DPYD variants and fluorouracil or capecitabine catastrophe with pre-treatment testing protocols and dose-reduction or avoidance, NUDT15's stronger role in Asian cohorts teaching again about ancestry and panel design); immune idiosyncrasy mapped to HLA (abacavir-B*5701 with mandatory testing and near-elimination of the reaction, carbamazepine-B*1502 in Southeast Asian and Han Chinese patients, allopurinol-B*5801 with screening policies in at-risk groups and the renal-dose interaction, and the flucloxacillin-B*35:01 signal without screening recommendations because of low attributable risk - the absolute-versus-relative-risk lesson in a prescribing context) - [[infectious-diseases/hiv]], [[musculoskeletal/inflammatory-arthritis]], [[pathology/cancer-biology]]; the pharmacodynamic examples ryanodine-receptor CACNA1C-adjacent susceptibility to malignant hyperthermia tested with the caffeine-halothane contracture contract, the succinylcholine-apnoea butyrylcholinesterase variants, warfarin CYP2C9-plus-VKORC1 dose algorithms with modest trial benefit and much guideline ambivalence, the statin myopathy-transporter association); and the framework questions - penetrance and effect size determine whether to test before, reactively, or never; allele frequencies and haplotype definitions differ by ancestry, so panels validated in Europeans miss variants elsewhere and reference standards are being corrected (the ancestry-representation problem as a patient-safety issue); pre-emptive panel testing is becoming economically discussable at health-system scale but demands result-interpretation services, protected storage with re-contact rules when evidence changes, consent for incidental pharmacogenetic information (mostly nil, which is itself reassuring and worth telling patients), and prescribing systems that act on results, because an un-actioned genotype is a medicolegal liability, not an innovation - [[genetics/genetic-testing]], [[genetics/genomic-medicine]], [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]].**
:::

## Everything else that changes a dose

::: definition Interactions, pregnancy and context {#def-context}
**Interactions were mechanised in the previous chapters; here they are a population phenomenon: the average older multimorbid patient carries a polypharmacy interaction burden that scales with prescriber count and transition frequency, which is why the highest-yield intervention is not a new textbook but a pharmacist-led reconciliation at every handover with the OTC, herbal and borrowed-medication history asked properly (the shared-packet and spouse's-tablets confession that only a non-judicial asking produces) - [[clinical-skills/records-handover]], [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; pregnancy changes pharmacokinetics predictably and under-prescribes by reflex: increased volume of distribution and renal clearance, induced glucuronidation (the lamotrigine dose that must rise through pregnancy and fall afterwards), altered protein binding, and the teratogenicity anxiety that causes dangerous under-treatment of epilepsy, depression, asthma and thrombophilia - the risk comparison being nearly always drug-versus-untreated-disease rather than drug-versus-health, with folate, vaccination and dose-adjusted monotherapy as the honest counselling package and pregnancy registries as the evidence base being slowly built - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/early-pregnancy]], [[nervous-system/epilepsy]]; lactation transfers most drugs poorly but not never: the relative infant dose arithmetic, the secreted-drug categories to avoid or monitor, and the breastfeeding-maintenance-of-maternal-therapy framing (the maternal-decompensation-harms-the-infant calculus stated openly); the disease modifiers worth naming because they recur: the asplenia and complement-inhibitor meningococcal-vaccination obligations, the renal-dose and dialysis-timing rules as daily practice, the heart-failure and NSAID and thiazide-and-gout-and-lithium interaction families, and the rare pharmacogenetic diseases that are also metabolic disease (malignant-hyperthermia susceptibility, G6PD-deficiency drug lists and primaquine and dapsone and nitrofurantoin with the quantitative G6PD assays enabling the elimination-era therapies, the porphyogenic drug lists maintained in specialist registries, and succinylcholine sensitivity) - [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]]; and the behavioural layer: adherence is a dose. Half of chronic-disease medicines are not taken as prescribed, so the individualisation algorithm's first input is actual exposure - the once-daily and combination-product and long-acting-injectable design solutions, the blunter-adherence interventions (simplification, aligning with routines, cost and supply checks, reminders with limited durability, motivational interviewing where it works), and the non-adherence-versus-resistance-versus-malabsorption differential that therapeutic drug monitoring settles - with the adherence-evidence hierarchy read with the same critical appraisal as any drug trial - [[psychiatry/treatments-mental-health-law]], [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**
:::

::: example The lamotrigine perinatal plan {#ex-lamotrigine-pp}
A woman with focal epilepsy on lamotrigine 200mg twice daily conceives while on the combined pill; by 10 weeks she reports more auras; her puerperium plan says 'restart pre-pregnancy dose'.
::: solution
**One-drug-two-directions ([[#def-body]], [[#def-context]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]): the oestrogen interaction first - ethinylestradiol induced UGT1A4 glucuronidation drops lamotrigine levels roughly half, so unplanned pregnancy after pill-stop removes the induction while pregnancy pharmacokinetics push levels down further, a double perturbation in the same direction - the auras are a level problem with a safety problem attached (driving, bathing, supervision counselling) - [[nervous-system/epilepsy]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]]; the pregnancy plan - monthly-or-per-trimester levels with dose raised to maintain her personal pre-pregnancy target (the evidence that seizure freedom is achievable for the large majority and the teratogenicity-honesty for lamotrigine's comparatively reassuring numbers with the dose-exposure caveat), folate before conception per guidance, and the specialist-led joint clinic rather than two services guessing - [[paediatrics/congenital-genetic]]; and the postpartum trap - glucuronidation normalises within one-three weeks so the pregnancy dose becomes a toxicity dose (the sedation, diplopia, ataxia reports that look like postpartum everything-else, and the stepwise return-to-pre-pregnancy-dose schedule written before delivery with levels to confirm), with breastfeeding compatible for most anticonvulsants and the infant monitored for sedation and poor feeding (lamotrigine's relatively higher milk transfer read as monitor-not-avoid), the sleep-deprivation-and-contraception-restart counselling and the driving-law conversation per jurisdiction - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The 120 kg patient's medicines {#ex-obesity-dose}
A 120 kg 58-year-old (height 168 cm) is admitted with cellulitis, atrial fibrillation needing anticoagulation, and chronic pain; pharmacy flags three dosing decisions.
::: solution
**Weight-band pharmacology ([[#def-body]], [[pharmacology/absorption-distribution]]): the antibiotic - beta-lactam volume of distribution rises with adipose and oedema while clearance does not fall, so standard doses under-expose; the options are augmented fixed doses within licensed ceilings or weight-banded protocols with the infection-site and renal-function context, and the choice is documented because 'high dose because big' is not a plan (the hydrophilic-drug-distribution nuance and the toxicity margin per agent) - [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[musculoskeletal/skin]]; the anticoagulant - direct oral anticoagulants above 120 kg or BMI over 40 sit in a data gap with lower-evidence alternatives: warfarin with INR, or a DOAC with anti-Xa-level guidance, or weight-banded LMWH with anti-Xa monitoring where bridging; the choice names the evidence, and prophylaxis-vs-treatment dosing stays separate - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]; the analgesia - neuraxial and regional technique anatomy changes with landmarks and needle length (the ultrasound-era practice), lipophilic induction drugs dosed on lean mass to avoid prolonged sedation while infusions are re-titrated as distribution changes, and the weight-based opioids kept on the monitoring-heavy end of the ward with the sleep-apnoea question asked (the opioid-induced respiratory depression risk factor list that obesity heads) - [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]], [[endocrine/diabetes]]; and the equity note - dose-avoidance-because-size is how patients get undertreated, and the solution is protocol, pharmacokinetic literacy and weight documentation, not caution-by-omission, with the bariatric-surgery-post-patient carrying a different absorption problem again (the modified-release and pregnancy-contraception cautions post-bypass) - [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**
:::

::: example The thiopurine dilemma {#ex-thiopurine-pgx}
A 24-year-old with newly diagnosed Crohn's disease of Filipino ancestry is offered azathioprine; TPMT enzyme activity is normal but the lab notes NUDT15 testing is 'not routine here'.
::: solution
**Ancestry-aware pharmacogenetics ([[#def-pgx]], [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]): the science - TPMT deficiency causes thiopurine myelosuppression and screening halves early toxicity, but in East and Southeast Asian populations NUDT15 variants are the more common cause of severe myelosuppression at standard doses, so a normal TPMT result is genuinely reassuring and genuinely incomplete (the population-specific variant frequencies as a patient-safety fact, not a footnote) - [[blood/anaemia]]; the practice options ranked - add NUDT15 testing (increasingly available, cheap against an aplastic episode), start reduced-dose with intensive count monitoring, or choose an alternative induction (the biologic-first conversation with its own immunogenicity and monitoring logic), all defensible once the mechanism is explained to the patient; the wider pattern - warfarin dosing algorithms perform differently by ancestry, carbamazepine and allopurinol HLA screening recommendations are ancestry-anchored because the alleles are, and SSRI and clopidogrel guidance carries population caveats, so 'the guidelines say' without the allele-frequency layer is how panels fail minority patients - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[medical-ethics-law/justice-resources]]; and the counselling - myelosuppression is early and reversible if counts are chased, the TPMT-and-NUDT15 result does not license relaxed monitoring, the allopurinol-and-ACE-neutropenia-and-warfarin interaction reminders, and the reproductive-life question (thiopurines are among the better-studied immunosuppressants in conception and pregnancy, decided with the IBD and obstetric teams, not by leaflet) - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/early-pregnancy]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The anaesthesia consent with a family history {#ex-mh-family}
A fit 19-year-old's brother died during appendicectomy 'something with the muscle relaxant and the fever'; the anaesthetist is asked what to do.
::: solution
**Suspected-malignant-hyperthermia-pathway ([[#def-pgx]], [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]): the history - a heat-death and muscle-relaxant link is malignant hyperthermia until proven otherwise (the RYR1 and CACNA1S variants, autosomal-dominant susceptibility with variable penetrance, and the unmasked-by-suxamethonium-and-volatile-agent mechanism) - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]; the plan - avoid the triggering agents with a clean-machine technique (total intravenous anaesthesia, removed vaporisers and fresh circuits per protocol, no suxamethonium), dantrolene immediately available and its reconstitution drill known to the room, normothermia and temperature monitoring throughout, and no need to delay emergency surgery to 'test' anyone; the family pathway - referral to the MH investigation unit, the caffeine-halothane contracture test and genetic panel with their sensitivity-specificity trade-offs explained, cascade testing offered through the proband, and the patient-held alert card plus registry enrolment that protect the next decade of his operations (the dental extractions and wisdom teeth that are the real-world trigger moments) - [[genetics/genetic-testing]], [[medical-ethics-law/confidentiality]]; and the honest counselling - susceptibility is not a contraindication to anaesthesia, most future anaesthetics are uneventful with precautions, the anxiety and the insurance-and-sport questions the family will ask have clear answers given calmly, and the event itself reported and learned from - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

## Where this leads

Mechanistic detail for each modifier sits in [[pharmacology/absorption-distribution]], [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]] and [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; the monogenic enzyme and receptor diseases in [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]]; implementation of genomics in practice in [[genetics/genomic-medicine]].

::: history
**Pharmacogenetics began as two clinical curiosities read carefully: the succinylcholine apnoea families first reported in 1956 (the enzyme-variant story that founded the field's vocabulary), and the 1950s-60s work on isoniazid and hydralazine acetylator phenotypes by Evans, Price-Evans and Rosenkranz and the concurrent discovery that some people had exaggerated pressor responses to tyramine-rich fish, linking NAT2 to diet and drug handling (the weekend-migraine observation turned into enzyme polymorphism, covered in metabolism) - [[infectious-diseases/tuberculosis]], [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]]; the HLA connection came next - the 1960s observations connecting immune markers to drug reactions, then the 1970s discovery that the HLA system (then called W6/HL-A) predicted agranulocytosis and other idiosyncrasies, framing type B reactions as immunogenetics - [[immunology/adaptive-responses]]; the thiopurine clinic institutionalised it - Relling and Evans's TPMT genotyping programmes of the 1990s-2000s with dose-reduction protocols that removed most early deaths from leukaemia maintenance therapy, the DPYD and fluoropyrimidine literature arriving two decades later with implementation still uneven, and the abacavir-B*5701 pathway from case-series to the mandatory-test policy in under a decade, pharmacovigilance's best modern result - [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]], [[infectious-diseases/hiv]]; the clopidogrel-CYP2C19 era showed the limits - strong pharmacology, replicated genetics, but guideline ambivalence until the ancestry-diverse trial evidence matured, teaching the field that effect size and actionable alternatives decide clinical utility, not p-values - [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]; and the implementation science of the 2010s-20s - CPIC and DPWG guidelines, the eMERGE and PGx-CARRY and U-PGx prospective cohorts and the pre-emptive-panel-versus-reactive-testing economics, with the ancestry-representation correction becoming a safety agenda as panels moved from research to prescription - [[genetics/genomic-medicine]], [[public-health/global-health]].**
:::

::: summary
- Exposure in a real patient is the average of every modifier times the adherence fraction; prescribing information gives you the numerator only.
- Age, weight, pregnancy, organ failure and dialysis each move specific parameters - the dose question is which parameter dominates for which drug.
- Pharmacogenetics is clinically real where effect size is large and the test changes action: thiopurines and TPMT/NUDT15, fluoropyrimidines and DPYD, abacavir and HLA-B*5701, carbamazepine and HLA-B*1502, allopurinol and HLA-B*5801.
- Genetic panels are only as good as the ancestries they were trained on; equity of representation is a dosing-safety issue.
- Pregnancy raises and then removes dose requirements for some drugs (lamotrigine being the teaching case); under-treatment of maternal disease is itself an exposure.
- Non-adherence is the commonest cause of 'drug failure'; measure or ask before escalating.
:::

::: exercise Appraise the race-vs-genetics prescribing debate {level=3}
Some guidelines use ancestry to decide pharmacogenetic testing (carbamazepine, allopurinol) while others avoid racial categories entirely. Appraise the evidence, the ethics and a defensible policy.
::: solution
**Ancestry-in-prescribing-appraisal ([[#def-pgx]], [[medical-ethics-law/justice-resources]]): the science - HLA allele frequencies vary strongly by ancestry because of population history and pathogen-driven selection, so B*1502 screening before carbamazepine is cost-effective in Southeast Asian and Han Chinese populations and near-useless in populations where the allele is rare; the same logic for B*5801-allopurinol in at-risk groups and for NUDT15 in East and South Asian and Hispanic cohorts (the allele-frequency table is the guideline, and self-reported ancestry is a proxy with known mismatch rates) - [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]]; the ethics - universal screening avoids stigmatising categorisation and misses no one but costs more and needs the laboratory capacity; ancestry-triggered asking is efficient but requires clinicians to take ancestries seriously without essentialising them, and the asking itself must be dignified and optional (the clinical-ancestry-versus-social-race distinction written into the script) - [[clinical-skills/history-taking]]; the failure modes - proxy guidelines applied lazily (testing nobody who 'looks local' or testing everyone in a diverse clinic reflexively), panels trained on one ancestry under-performing in others (the real safety risk hiding in this debate), and the documentation gap where a declined test disappears from the record; and the defensible policy - offer genotype-based testing where the allele-or-variant is present (ancestry-informed prompts built into the prescribing system as suggestions, not gates), move toward pre-emptive panels where economically sensible so testing stops depending on how a patient presents, publish the allele-frequency evidence with every recommendation, and audit testing and outcomes stratified by ancestry to catch the equity gaps the policy creates - [[genetics/genomic-medicine]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

## Exercises

::: exercise Which parameter moves? {level=1}
State the dominant pharmacokinetic or pharmacodynamic change and the dosing consequence: preterm neonate and chloramphenicol; 82-year-old and diazepam; second trimester and lamotrigine; haemodialysis day and levetiracetam; cirrhotic shunting and propranolol.
::: solution
**Parameter-movers ([[#def-body]], [[#def-context]]): neonate-chloramphenicol - immature glucuronidation with drug accumulation and mitochondrial toxicity (the grey-baby syndrome, the alternative-agent rule and the dose-per-kg-by-postmenstrual-age maturation charts) - [[paediatrics/newborn]]; elderly-diazepam - reduced clearance of active metabolites plus increased brain sensitivity, so the lorazepam-oxazepam-temepam glucuronide-only switch plus halved starting doses and falls-fracture counselling - [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]; pregnancy-lamotrigine - induced glucuronidation and renal clearance halve levels, so dose up with monthly levels, then back down within weeks postpartum on a written schedule - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]; dialysis-levetiracetam - small, unbound, renally cleared, so significantly removed between sessions with a post-dialysis supplement and an inter-dialytic decline curve (the same rules predicting which drugs need no supplement) - [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]; shunting-propranolol - high-extraction drug with oral bioavailability rising toward parenteral territory when portal blood bypasses hepatocytes, so the oral dose that worked pre-cirrhosis may now overshoot - [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]].**
:::

::: exercise Test or not {level=2}
For each, argue test-before-prescribing, test-on-demand, or neither, using effect size, actionability, latency and cost of missing: thiopurine in a new IBD patient, carbamazepine in a Han Chinese patient, flucloxacillin in a 21-year-old, codeine post-tonsillectomy in a breastfeeding woman, fluorouracil before adjuvant colon cancer therapy.
::: solution
**Testing-strategy-arguments ([[#def-pgx]]): thiopurine - test TPMT and NUDT15 beforehand: large effect, catastrophic miss, actionable alternatives and dose reductions, and the test is cheap relative to a myelosuppression admission (the pan-ethnic-allele argument that NUDT15 raised in Asian cohorts) - [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]; carbamazepine in Han Chinese - B*1502 testing exists to prevent SJS-TEN with a large odds ratio, and with alternatives available, testing is standard for at-risk ancestries (the population-allele-frequency-versus-individual-risk framing and the lamotrigine cross-risk caution documented in labels) - [[nervous-system/epilepsy]], [[musculoskeletal/skin]]; flucloxacillin - do not test: B*35:01's relative risk is high but the absolute risk of idiosyncratic jaundice is low and the reaction is late and unpredictable, so the answer is patient information and jaundice-attention advice plus the reporting duty, not pre-treatment HLA typing - [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]; codeine-breastfeeding-post-tonsillectomy - contraindicated regardless of genotype because CYP2D6 ultrarapid metabolism converts normally to morphine and infant deaths occurred; the policy answer is 'do not use', which is pharmacogenetics applied at population level without testing anyone - [[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]], [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]; and fluorouracil - DPYD testing beforehand is the current direction of travel (the partial-deficiency dose-reduction protocols with early toxicity-avoidance data, and the phenotype-versus-genotype debate about the four common variants versus rare ones, with uracil testing as the alternative) - [[pathology/cancer-biology]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]].**

An 86-year-old is discharged after heart-failure decompensation with: furosemide doubled, spironolactone added, ramipril uptitrated, naproxen continued for 'arthritis', OTC cetirizine for itch, and lithium unchanged for bipolar disorder. Find the five dosing-safety problems and rewrite the plan.
::: solution
**Discharge-review-rewrite ([[#def-body]], [[#def-context]], [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]): the triple potassium hit - spironolactone plus ramipril plus (unstated) dietary and illness variables in an 86-year-old, so the potassium-and-creatinine checks at 48-72 hours and one week are written into the plan with the sick-day rules and the NSAID removed first (the naproxen-bleed-AKI-hyperkalaemia-hyponatraemia-rejection with topical and paracetamol alternatives and the arthritis reassessment that is actually a hand-exercise-and-splint-and-weight conversation) - [[musculoskeletal/osteoarthritis]], [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]; the lithium and the diuretic - loop and thiazide-and-ACE-and-NSAID effects on lithium clearance (the dehydration-toxicity cascade and the level checks with illness and dose changes, the renal-thyroid-calcium surveillance for chronic therapy) - [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]], [[endocrine/thyroid]], [[endocrine/bone-calcium]]; the furosemide logic - doubled for congestion is correct while uptitrating ACE and adding MRA reduces potassium excretion, so the diuretic dose and the potassium plan must move together with daily weights and a clinic phone number, plus the hypokalaemia-with-digoxin-history check; and the cetirizine - second-generation antihistamine is usually fine but in this body the sedation-fall and anticholinergic-add-on question and the renal dose reduction matter, as does whether the itch is dry skin, stasis or drug (the deprescribe-and-treat-the-skin-barrier answer) - [[immunology/hypersensitivity]]; and the system - the discharge conversation done as teach-back with the actual pills, the GP-and-community-pharmacist alerts for the new combination with the one-week bloods tracked to a named person, and the readmission-risk documentation that makes this review findable next time - [[clinical-skills/records-handover]], [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]].**

::: exercise Build the pharmacogenetic service business case {level=3}
Make the case for pre-emptive pharmacogenetic testing at primary-care registration for an adult population. Include clinical, economic, equity, ethical and implementation arguments, and the strongest objections.
::: solution
**Business-case-construction ([[#def-pgx]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the clinical argument - one test reused across a life of prescriptions (the thiopurines, fluoropyrimidines, abacavir, clopidogrel and opioid and antidepressant metabolism guidance where it exists), avoiding both toxicity and the trial-and-error years in psychopharmacology, with the honest statement that most results are 'normal metabolism' and that the actionable minority is where cost-effectiveness lives - [[genetics/genomic-medicine]]; the economic model - test-plus-interpretation-service cost against avoided admissions (the TPMT and DPYD and HLA severe-reaction episodes, the ICU-level toxicity and the litigation), the savings accumulating with reuse and drug-price changes over decades, which is why health-system (not single-department) framing matters, with implementation realism about prescribing-system integration and the interpretation workforce; the equity arguments both ways - pan-ethnic allele coverage requires diverse reference data (the miss-rate problem otherwise), and population-registration testing could equalise access across deprivation gradients that currently decide who gets precision medicine, against the risk it becomes another digitally-excluded service unless channels are plural; and ethics-and-objections - consent for re-contact and data retention, insurability and discrimination worries (mostly answered by law but needing plain answers), the objection that acting on weak-effect evidence medicalises prescribing, the strongest objection being governance failure - an unactioned genotype creating liability and a variant-of-uncertain-significance flood creating confusion - answered by design: result return only with interpretation, prescribing alerts, audit of action rates, and an evidence-change re-contact policy; the pilot-and-evaluation plan with hard endpoints and equity monitoring closes the case - [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]], [[medical-ethics-law/justice-resources]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**
