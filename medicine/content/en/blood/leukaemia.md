Leukaemia is cancer of a blood-forming tissue caught in the act of overproduction: a somatically mutated stem or progenitor cell whose progeny accumulate — by impaired differentiation (the acute leukaemias' differentiation arrest at the blast stage, the marrow crowded out) or by unrestrained production with retained maturation (the chronic myeloproliferative diseases) — and announce themselves as marrow failure, organ infiltration or, on the modern era's commonest pathway, an incidental blood count. The chapter's intellectual spine is the same one the pathology course teaches with solid tumours — mutation, clonal evolution, selection — applied to a cancer that is liquid from birth, and its clinical spine is the therapeutic revolution that made leukaemias the model of molecularly targeted oncology: imatinib, ATRA, the FLT3 and IDH inhibitors, venetoclax and CAR-T.

This chapter covers the classification and biology of the acute leukaemias, myelodysplasia, the myeloproliferative neoplasms and the lymphoid leukaemias.

## Definitions and biology

::: definition What a leukaemia is {#def-def}
**Acute leukaemia**: ≥20% blasts (the WHO threshold; Auer rods or specific genetic lesions diagnose it at any count) in marrow or blood, with failure of myeloid or lymphoid precursors to mature — presenting in days to weeks with the consequences of marrow crowding (anaemia, neutropenic infection, thrombocytopenic bleeding) plus gingival infiltration, bone pain and lymphadenopathy in the lymphoid diseases; **chronic leukaemia**: accumulated mature-ish cells with splenomegaly and high counts, presenting insidiously or incidentally, with an eventual risk of **blast crisis** (the myeloid diseases' terminal acceleration toward acute leukaemia). The biology is clonal and evolutionary: driver lesions either **promote survival/proliferation** (FLT3-ITD, RAS, BCR::ABL1's kinase) or **block differentiation and alter self-renewal** (the core-binding-factor translocations, KMT2A rearrangements, NPM1 mutations), layered over a **leukaemia stem-cell compartment** that regenerates the disease, spares itself from cycle-dependent chemotherapy, and is the true target of transplant and of the immunotherapies — the population whose measurement (**minimal residual disease** by multicolour flow or quantitative PCR of the fusion or Ig-gene rearrangement) is now the strongest prognostic number in the field. Age stays the master variable: the incidence of acute myeloid leukaemia rises through the seventh and eighth decades because accumulated stem-cell clones (the CHIP of [[blood/haematopoiesis]]'s closing exercise) and therapy (alkylator and radiation-related MDS/AML, the latency-and-risk tables of [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]) both feed the soil.
:::

## Acute myeloid leukaemia

::: definition Classification, risk and the treatable lesions {#def-aml}
Diagnosis: the blood film and marrow aspirate (myeloblasts — MPO-positive, Auer-rod-bearing when the lesion permits), **immunophenotyping** (CD34, CD117, CD33, CD13, CD14/64 for monocytic lines) and — decisive — **cytogenetics and molecular studies** (the ELN framework): **favourable-risk** core-binding-factor leukaemias t(8;21) and inv(16), and **NPM1-mutant/FLT3-ITD-negative** disease and **biallelic CEBPA**; **adverse** complex and monosomal karyotypes, −5/−7, RUNX1, ASXL1, TP53 (the therapy-related and MDS-derived biology, the clone that resists everything), with **FLT3-ITD** (the proliferation driver; the ITD allele burden matters therapeutically) and the **KMT2A rearrangements** as the intermediate-to-poor group whose targeted partners (FLT3 and menin inhibitors) now join the regimen. Presenting emergencies are a few syndromes deep: **APL (M3 — t(15;17), PML::RARA)** with its catastrophic coagulopathy (below); **hyperleukocytosis and leukostasis** (blasts >100 × 10⁹/L, the myeloid cells' larger and stickier variety plugging the microcirculation — hypoxaemia, stroke, priapism: cytoreductive emergency with hydroxycarbamide and leucopheresis, prophylactic TLS management); **DIC** (tissue-factor-bearing promyelocytes and monocytes); and **tumour lysis** (the high-turnover bulk after the first dose: potassium, phosphate, urate, calcium — hydration plus allopurinol or **rasburicase**, never in the G6PD-deficient patient — the haemolysis trap of [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]). Treatment remains cytarabine-plus-anthracycline induction ("7+3") with **midostaurin or quizartinib added for FLT3-mutant disease**, liposomal cytarabine–daunorubicin (CPX-351) for therapy-related and myelodysplasia-related disease, and — for the older or frail patient the "7+3 would kill" group — **venetoclax plus a hypomethylating agent**, the BCL-2-inhibitor combination that changed first-line AML in the over-seventies; consolidation by risk (high-dose cytarabine in favourable disease, **allogeneic transplant** in intermediate/adverse first remission and all first-relapses); and **MRD-guided intensification** — the framework's quantitative core.
:::

::: example The bleed that must not wait {#ex-apl}
A 34-year-old woman arrives with gum bleeding, bruising and a disseminated-coagulation picture: platelets 20, fibrinogen 0.7 g/L, INR 1.6, D-dimer very high; the film shows hypergranular blasts with Auer rods in bundles ("faggot cells").
::: solution
**Acute promyelocytic leukaemia until genetics confirms — and ATRA (all-trans-retinoic acid) starts at the clinical suspicion, before the result.** The lesion: **t(15;17) PML::RARA** — the retinoic-acid-receptor-α fused to PML locks the myeloid cell at the promyelocyte stage (a differentiation arrest that is pharmacologically reversible: **pharmacological doses of retinoic acid release the block** — the differentiation therapy that cured a disease whose early deaths were exsanguinations), while the malignant promyelocytes' granule contents (tissue factor, annexin II, proteases) drive the **hyperfibrinolysis-and-DIC coagulopathy** that kills in the first days — hence the aggressive product support (fibrinogen to >1.5 g/L with cryo or concentrate, platelets to >50, the avoid-the-intramuscular-injections nursing rules). Regimens: **ATRA plus arsenic trioxide** for standard-risk disease (WBC ≤10) — chemotherapy-free, curative in the great majority, with QT monitoring and the arsenic precautions — and ATRA/arsenic plus gemtuzumab ozogamicin or idarubicin for the high-risk; **differentiation syndrome** (fever, oedema, dyspnoea, infiltrates, hypotension — the differentiating cells' cytokine storm and capillary leak) is treated with dexamethasone and, if severe, temporary ATRA hold, never mistaken for infection alone; maintenance and intrathecal decisions by risk; and the **PML::RARA PCR** for MRD as the modern cure certificate. The case's one-sentence moral, taught on every haematology unit: **in a bleeding patient with Auer-rod bundles, start ATRA first and let the laboratory agree with you afterwards.**
:::
:::

::: example The breathless patient with 300 white cells {#ex-leukostasis}
A 72-year-old is brought in confused and hypoxaemic; the blood count reader flags WBC 310 × 10⁹/L, the film is full of blasts, the chest shows no infiltrate, and the ophthalmoscopy reveals retinal vein engorgement and haemorrhages.
::: solution
**Hyperleukocytosis with leucostasis** — the myeloid blasts (larger, stiffer, granule-rich than lymphoid) increase blood viscosity and plug the microcirculation: the **lung** (hypoxaemia with a clear radiograph — perfusion block, not pneumonia), the **brain** (confusion, visual change, priapism, and the fundus's sludging), and the kidney, with the added hazard that cytoreduction will unleash the lysis chemistry of the earlier exercise. The immediate physiology-directed management: **hydroxycarbamide in cytoreductive dose (and, for the symptomatic patient, leukapheresis — the mechanical removal that buys days, its trial evidence modest but its role entrenched for neurological and respiratory leucostasis), careful hydration avoiding over-perfusion of the plugged pulmonary bed, TLS prophylaxis running before the count falls, and transfusion held (the older teaching: red cells raise viscosity in the undiagnosed — top up only for critical anaemia)** while the APL question is answered and excluded on sight (the coagulopathy of [[#ex-apl]] would demand ATRA immediately, and leucostasis plus DIC points at the promyelocytic or monocytic variants). Steroids — the lymphoid answer (the ALL blast's corticosteroid sensitivity) — are withheld until the lineage is known, because in an undeclared lymphoid leukaemia they partially treat and mask; and intubation and sedation proceed with the knowledge that hypoxia and acidosis worsen blast deformability and capillary plugging. The case is whole-system rheology: the same Poiseuille-style thinking as [[physiology/homeostasis-body-fluids]]'s viscosity discussions, with the white count as the variable.
:::

## Lymphoid leukaemia and the chronic myeloid diseases

::: definition Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia {#def-all}
ALL is a disease of children (the 2–5-year peak, ~80% of childhood acute leukaemias) and of older adults with worse biology: the arrest is in a lymphoid progenitor — **B-precursor CD10/CD19/TdT-positive** in ~85%, with the cytogenetic risk map of [[genetics/cancer-genetics]]'s translocation lesson: **ETV6::RUNX1 t(12;21)** (the good-prognosis cryptic fusion, the "TEL-AML1" of the old nomenclature), **KMT2A (MLL) rearrangements in infants** (the worst), **BCR::ABL1-like and Philadelphia-positive** disease (now treated with TKI addition to chemotherapy or blinatumomab-based regimens), **Ph-like ALL** (the kinase-fusion group whose targeted partners are found by sequencing) — and **T-ALL** (the adolescent male's mediastinal mass and airway and effusion risks). Diagnosis: the film and marrow, immunophenotype, cytogenetics, and the **lumbar puncture** — the CNS is a **sanctuary site** (the blood–brain and blood–testis barriers exclude systemic therapy, so modern protocols make CNS-directed therapy — intrathecal methotrexate ± cytarabine ± hydrocortisone and risk-adapted radiotherapy now sparing most children — and MRD monitoring the twin pillars that carried childhood ALL from ~10% to >90% five-year survival: the model of curable cancer). Adult ALL adds the blinatumomab and inotuzumab (CD3-bispecific and CD22-immunoconjugate) era and transplant in second remission, with the adolescent-and-young-adult protocols transferred to adult units doing the rest.
:::

::: definition The myeloproliferative neoplasms {#def-mpn}
A **JAK2 / CALR / MPL** driver trio defines the classical **Philadelphia (BCR::ABL1)-negative myeloproliferative neoplasms** — proliferation of one or more myeloid lines with retained differentiation, splenomegaly from extramedullary haematopoiesis and a shared risk of thrombosis, haemorrhage (extreme thrombocytosis's acquired von Willebrand syndrome) and **progression to marrow fibrosis or AML**: **polycythaemia vera** (JAK2 V617F in >95% — erythrocytosis with low EPO; the aquagenic pruritus, facial plethora, hypertension, gout, splenomegaly; thrombosis is the killer and the treatment goal — venesection to haematocrit <45%, low-dose aspirin, cytoreduction with hydroxycarbamide or interferon in the high-risk), **essential thrombocythaemia** (JAK2/CALR/MPL; a platelet count that demands the exclusion of reactive causes — iron deficiency, splenectomy, inflammation, malignancy — before naming a neoplasm, and of masked polycythaemia and pre-fibrotic myelofibrosis, the MPN-1/MIPSSU-scored risk of thrombosis and bleeding driving the aspirin/hydroxycarbamide/interferon decision), **primary myelofibrosis** (the megakaryocyte TPO-signalling clone that fibres the marrow via PDGF/TGF-β dumping — [[blood/haematopoiesis]]'s leuko-erythroblastic film, tear-drops and massive spleen, the constitutional symptoms and **ruxolitinib's** JAK1/2 inhibition of the cytokine machinery — splenic and symptom benefit, no cure — and allo transplant the only curative option), and **chronic myeloid leukaemia**, the exception that named the class: **BCR::ABL1 t(9;22), the Philadelphia chromosome** — a constitutively active tyrosine kinase whose discovery (Nowell and Hungerford's 1960 acute-leukaemia chromosome; Rowley's 1973 translocation proof; the Van Etten–Daley–Baltimore reconstruction of the fusion kinase's transforming activity) ran from description to **imatinib** (an ATP-pocket inhibitor — Druker's programme, phase I's legendary durable responses) in four decades: CML now presents with a high granulocyte count and left shift, basophilia, splenomegaly and **PCR-detectable BCR::ABL1**, is staged by blast percentage (chronic/accelerated/blast crisis — the last an AML- or ALL-like transformation), and is treated with a TKI (imatinib, or second-generation nilotinib/dasatinib/bosutinib with their side-effect signatures — QT, pleural effusion, diarrhoea) aiming for **deep molecular responses sustained off therapy — the closest clinical practice has come to defining "cure by blood test"**.
:::

::: example The itchy retiree {#ex-pv}
A 66-year-old man is referred for "red cells and an itchy back after showers"; Hb 198, Hct 60%, WBC 15, platelets 480, spleen palpable, EPO low, JAK2 V617F positive, saturations normal.
::: solution
**Polycythaemia vera** — the JAK2-driven, erythropoietin-independent proliferation that suppresses EPO (the feedback discriminator that separates secondary erythrocytosis — high or normal EPO, the hypoxic and the rare EPO-secreting tumours, and the high-affinity haemoglobins of [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]] — from the neoplasm whose EPO is low). The work-up closes with the **JAK2 mutation and bone-marrow morphology** (trilineage hypercellularity — the panmyelosis that also raises his WBC and platelets) and, when EPO or the history is odd, the red-cell mass and arterial saturations, a sleep study and a carboxyhaemoglobin (the smoker's apparent polycythaemia is the classic mimic). The symptoms are the hyperviscosity and the basophil/mast-cell and cytokine biology: **aquagenic pruritus** (the shower-triggered, non-rash itch of unknown mast-cell/nerve-fibre mechanism and the most miserable symptom to treat — antihistamines usually fail; the SSRI, and cytoreduction itself, work best), plethora, hypertension, erythromelalgia (the aspirin-responsive digital burning — the microvascular platelet aggregation), gout (the turnover) and the two clinical risks that structure management: **thrombosis** (arterial and venous, the major cause of death — including the unusual-site Budd–Chiari and portal vein thromboses whose JAK2 screening is a rule) and, late, **fibrotic or leukaemic transformation** (small percentages after decades). Management is the risk-stratified triad: **venesection to haematocrit <0.45 (the CYTO-PV trial's clear mortality signal), low-dose aspirin unless the count-related von Willebrand syndrome or bleeding intervenes, and cytoreduction (hydroxycarbamide first; interferon in the young and the pregnant; ropeginterferon the modern long-cycle option) for high-risk disease (age >60 or prior thrombosis — this patient qualifies), with ruxolitinib for intolerant/resistant cases**; the spleen, pruritus and microvascular symptoms are the treatment-response read-outs, and the whole patient is the chapter's lesson that a driver mutation's clinical syndrome is written by which cytokine pathway it constitutively turns on.
:::
:::

::: example The four-year-old with a limp {#ex-all}
A mother brings her fourth-year-old with two weeks of refusal to walk, low-grade fevers and bruising; Hb 78, platelets 22, WBC 34 with "blast cells", bone and calf pain, no mass, normal coagulation.
::: solution
**Acute lymphoblastic leukaemia** — the triad of anaemia, thrombocytopenia and circulating blasts in the age peak, with the bone pain (marrow expansion) that is too often first diagnosed as "growing pains" or, when the film is normal early, as **transient erythroblastopenia or even cerebral palsy-equivalent refusal-to-walk**; the child's pain, fever and bruising pattern is itself a referral rule. The work-up stages: the film and marrow (CD10/CD19/TdT+ B-precursor in most; lymphoblast morphology no longer prognostic), **cytogenetics/molecular risk assignment** (ETV6::RUNX1 good; KMT2A and hypodiploidy poor; ABL-class now actionable), the **lumbar puncture and CSF cytology for CNS status**, and the baseline imaging of the mediastinum and testes. Treatment is the architecture that cured childhood cancer: **risk-adapted multi-agent chemotherapy** (steroid, vincristine, an anthracycline, asparaginase, intrathecal methotrexate, with cyclophosphamide/etoposide and radiotherapy reserved to the historical high-risk groups now being de-escalated), the **day-29 and end-of-induction marrow MRD** deciding escalation (the strongest prognostic variable — below 10⁻⁴ the good-outcome plateau), CNS-directed prophylaxis in every protocol, and the **tyrosine-kinase-inhibitor and immunotherapy additions (blinatumomab, inotuzumab, CAR-T for relapse)** that have made multiply-relapsed childhood ALL a potentially curatable disease rather than a terminal one. The practical paediatric points the case hides: **never transfuse or give steroids before the diagnostic marrow where safely avoidable** (masking the diagnosis is the classic referral catastrophe), **TLS prophylaxis from the first sample**, the asparaginase's pancreatitis, thrombosis and antibody-formation vigilance, and the survivorship agenda — anthracycline cardiomyopathy surveillance, late-effect screening and the educational and fertility work of a disease whose survivors now outnumber its incidences.
:::

::: history
The field's landmarks are therapy's landmarks: **Donné's** 1844 cell counts, **Hughes Bennett's** 1845 blood drawings and **Virchow's** 1847 naming of the "white blood" diseases; then **Ehrlich's** differential stain (1878–81) and **Pappenheim's** morphology that made the acute and chronic, myeloid and lymphoid categories visible; **Farber's** aminopterin remission of childhood ALL (1948 — the page that founded cancer chemotherapy), **Skipper and Schabel's** log-kill mouse-leukaemia doctrine and the NCI combination-chemotherapy programme of **Emil Freireich** and colleagues (childhood ALL and Hodgkin lymphoma as proofs of concept), with the consolidation and maintenance studies that defined the protocols; **Janet Rowley's** demonstration that the Philadelphia chromosome is a translocation (1973, the **BCR::ABL1** gene identified in 1983–87) and **Druker's** imatinib programme (phase I 2001) — the target-to-drug arc that defines molecular medicine; **Wang Zhen-yi's** and Chen Zhu's Shanghai ATRA story (the differentiation discovery that became arsenic combination therapy) and the modern CAR-T era (2012 onward) complete the arc from the 1845 microscope to the engineered cell.
:::

The genetics of transformation in [[genetics/cancer-genetics]]; CHIP and stem-cell ageing in [[blood/haematopoiesis]]; the lymphoid neoplasms in [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]]; transfusion support of every regimen above in [[blood/transfusion]]; the neutropenic emergency in [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]]; palliative integration in [[geriatrics-palliative]].

::: summary
- Acute leukaemias are differentiation-arrest clonal diseases presenting with marrow failure; chronic myeloproliferative diseases are proliferation-with-maturation presenting with high counts, splenomegaly and thrombosis; the blast count and the genetic lesion, not the morphology alone, carry the diagnosis and the risk.
- Cytogenetic and molecular classification (core-binding-factor favourable, TP53/complex adverse, FLT3 and KMT2A actionable) is the treatment protocol and the prognostic instrument; MRD measurement is its follow-up.
- Two presentations are emergencies by mechanism: APL's coagulopathy (start ATRA before confirmation) and hyperleukocytosis/TLS (cytoreduce and hydrate before the first kill-rate dose).
- The myeloproliferative neoplasms (PV, ET, PMF — JAK2/CALR/MPL; CML — BCR::ABL1) are cytokine-pathway diseases whose thrombotic risk dictates management and whose targeted drugs (ruxolitinib, TKIs) are the success story of rational design.
- Childhood ALL is the curable-cancer model: risk-adapted polychemotherapy, CNS sanctuary-site therapy and MRD response monitoring — and the paediatric oncology system that delivers them.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Blast identification {level=1}
Auer rods in a population of blasts establish which lineage, which stains confirm, and which entity needs same-hour treatment?
::: solution
**Auer rods (fused primary granules — peroxidase- and MPO-positive crystallised azurophilic granules) mean myeloid lineage** — the morphological answer that precedes immunophenotyping (CD33/CD117/MPO by flow; the Sudan-black and MPO cytochemistries now confirmatory); lymphoblasts are MPO-negative with TdT and CD19/CD10 or CD3/CD7. The same-hour entity: the **hypergranular promyelocyte picture with Auer bundles — APL** — whose faggot cells, DIC labs and the clinical rule "ATRA on suspicion" appear in [[#ex-apl]] (the t(15;17) result follows, and the FISH interphase test shortens the wait).
:::
:::

::: exercise Classify by marker {level=1}
Assign: CD10⁺CD19⁺TdT⁺; CD33⁺MPO⁺; CD3⁺CD7⁺ with a mediastinal mass; CD5⁺CD19⁺CD23⁺ with lymphocytosis of mature-looking small lymphocytes.
::: solution
**B-precursor ALL; AML; T-ALL (the adolescent's mediastinal mass — check the airway and effusion before sedating); and CLL** — the last the chronic lymphoid leukaemia whose mature phenotype marks it as a disease of the [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]] border (small-lymphocyte lymphocytosis with CD5/CD23 co-expression and smudge cells — the diagnosis whose incidental discovery in an older adult's count is commoner than its symptoms).

::: exercise Manage the first day {level=2}
A 55-year-old with newly diagnosed AML, WBC 180, creatinine 130, urate 640, potassium 5.4, LDH 2,900. Give the prophylaxis plan and the monitoring intervals, and name the mechanism you are preventing.
::: solution
**Tumour lysis syndrome prevention** — the spontaneous and post-chemotherapy release of intracellular potassium, phosphate and nucleic acids (urate from the purine cascade) with secondary calcium-phosphate precipitation: **admit, continuous cardiac monitoring, aggressive intravenous fluids (2–3 L/m²/day-equivalent hydration started before cytoreduction), allopurinol (or rasburicase for the high-risk/established hyperuricaemia — never with G6PD deficiency), hold potassium and phosphate, give calcium only for symptomatic hypocalcaemia (the calcium-phosphate product), q4–6h electrolytes/uric-acid/creatinine for 48–72 hours, and ICU and dialysis access pre-planned**. The mechanism: urate and calcium-phosphate crystal deposition in the tubules driving AKI, which then amplifies every electrolyte; the cytoreduction (hydroxycarbamide, and leucopheresis for the symptomatic leukostasis of the WBC of 180 — the respiratory and neurological micro-occlusion of [[#def-aml]]) starts with the protection running, never after.
:::

::: exercise Why the spinal tap {level=2}
Explain why ALL protocols perform and dose-treat via the lumbar puncture while AML protocols mostly do not give prophylactic cranial radiotherapy, in terms of sanctuary sites and relapse patterns.
::: solution
Lymphoid leukaemias shelter and relapse in immunologically and pharmacologically privileged compartments — the **CNS and testes** — because systemic agents cross the blood–brain barrier poorly and the meningeal and testicular niches select reservoir clones; untreated, meningeal leukaemia was the universal route of childhood ALL relapse, and **intrathecal methotrexate/cytarabine ± hydrocortisone plus systemic agents with CNS penetration (high-dose methotrexate, dexamethasone) plus risk-tuned cranial radiotherapy (now avoided or reduced because of the endocrine, cognitive and second-tumour late effects)** drove CNS relapse from majority to minority. AML's CNS involvement is rarer (higher rates in monocytic and hyperleukocytic disease — where diagnostic LP and intrathecal cover are given), so whole-brain irradiation was never part of its cure trade. The general doctrine — **a sanctuary site must be treated prophylactically or the systemic cure fails there** — recurs in testicular cancer and CNS lymphoma prophylaxis and explains the testes' and the CNS's places in every ALL protocol.
:::
:::

::: exercise TKI resistance {level=2}
A CML patient loses molecular response at year two; kinase-domain testing shows the T315I mutation. Explain the resistance mechanism, the drug-choice logic, and the adherence conversation that must precede both.
::: solution
BCR::ABL1 kinase-domain mutations arise under drug selection (the clone evolved in the pocket); **T315I — the gatekeeper threonine to isoleucine — abolishes binding of imatinib, nilotinib, dasatinib and bosutinib** (the steric and hydrogen-bond loss of the first-and-second-generation agents) while leaving **ponatinib (the alkyne-extended inhibitor designed around the gatekeeper), asciminib (the myristoyl-pocket allosteric inhibitor — a different binding site entirely), and transplant** as the options. The conversation that precedes pharmacology: **non-adherence is the commonest cause of lost response** (the twice-daily, food- and acid-interaction-sensitive regimens — the PPI and antacid absorption traps of [[pharmacology/absorption-distribution]]), and the sequence is recheck adherence and drug interactions, re-mutate-test (the resistant clone and the polyclonal rebound differ), then switch by mutation and comorbidity (dasatinib's effusions, nilotinib's QT and vascular, ponatinib's arterial-occlusive risk management) — the precision-medicine algorithm that also demands the **PCR monitoring schedule (the milestones that make CML the disease managed by blood test)**.
:::

::: exercise Differentiation syndrome versus sepsis {level=2}
Day 4 of ATRA/arsenic for APL: fever 38.6, weight gain 4 kg, hypoxaemia, interstitial infiltrates, rising creatinine. The two differential diagnoses, the discriminating features, and the management of each arm.
::: solution
**Differentiation syndrome** (the differentiating promyelocytes' cytokine release, capillary leak and organ infiltration — fever, weight gain, oedema, hypoxaemia, infiltrates, hypotension, renal impairment; prophylaxis with corticosteroid in high-WBC disease) versus **neutropaenic sepsis** (the APL patient is still neutropaenic — and the line, the central line and the gut translocation of [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]] apply unchanged). Discriminating weight: the syndrome's **rapid weight gain and oedema without a source and with a rising rather than falling count (the differentiating cells)**, and its partial steroid-responsiveness — but the management is **both first, one question later**: **dexamethasone 10 mg twice daily immediately (empiric for the syndrome at the first hypoxaemia), full sepsis work-up and broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluid balance and diuresis or dialysis support, temporary ATRA/arsenic hold in severe cases**, and vasopressor and ventilatory care in the high-dependency bed where the early APL deaths actually happen. The teaching: in APL the treatment-related and infection-related presentations share their whole physical-signature, and both are lethal within hours if untreated — so the protocol treats the two in parallel, never the two by differential.
:::

::: exercise Transplant indications {level=2}
Give the current indications for allogeneic haematopoietic stem-cell transplant across this chapter's diseases, and the two reasons it is both more and less used than twenty years ago.
::: solution
**More used (or used in better-defined niches)**: adverse-risk AML in first remission (the TP53 and complex-karyotype groups where chemotherapy cures almost nobody), relapsed/refractory AML and ALL (now often as a **blinatumomab- or CAR-T-induced remission bridge**), high-risk MDS/secondary AML, and the **matched-unrelated and haploidentical donors' arrival** (the post-transplant cyclophosphamide platform that made the family a donor source) plus **cord-blood and mismatch platforms** extending access beyond HLA siblings. **Less used**: standard-risk CML in the TKI era (transplant all but abolished), favourable-risk AML and MRD-negative ALL (the chemotherapy/immunotherapy cure rates made the transplant mortality unjustifiable), and much of the elderly armamentarium moved to **reduced-intensity conditioning** (the non-myeloablative graft-versus-leukaemia-era strategy trading faster relapse for tolerability — the graft-versus-leukaemia effect as the therapy's active ingredient, and chronic graft-versus-host disease as its tax). The organising principle is the same arithmetic the chapter taught in [[#def-aml]]: transplant replaces the marrow and buys an immune anti-leukaemic effect; it is offered where relapse risk exceeds transplant-related mortality — a comparison the modern targeted drugs keep re-pricing.
:::

::: exercise Design the clinical trial {level=3}
You are designing a first-line trial for untreated AML in patients aged 60–75. Specify the control backbone, one rational experimental arm based on this chapter's biology, the stratification variables and the two endpoints that define modern AML trials.
::: solution
**Control**: the contemporary standard for the age band — **venetoclax plus azacitidine** (the VIALE-A backbone that replaced hypomethylating-agent monotherapy; 7+3 remains the comparator for the fit subset) — with MRD-adapted consolidation and transplant rules protocolised. **Experimental arm (biology-driven)**: e.g. **venetoclax/azacitidine plus an FLT3 inhibitor for the FLT3-mutated stratum, or plus magrolimab/CD47-axis or menin inhibitor for the KMT2A/menin-dependent strata** — chosen for the on-target rationale (the clone's dependency) and the non-overlapping toxicity (the myelosuppression and QT/organ signatures mapped). **Stratification**: age band and fitness (ECOG, cardiac and renal function), ELN genetic-risk group, FLT3-ITD burden, therapy-related versus de-novo disease, baseline MRD assay type, and (increasingly) **CHIP/clonal co-mutations (TP53, PPM1D)** and the MRD method (the NGS versus flow harmonisation problem). **Endpoints**: **composite complete remission (CR/CRi) with MRD-negativity as the key secondary and now the trial-grade primary in many designs, and overall survival — with the patient-reported and transfusion-independence measures the older trials lacked**. The ethical frame: a 60–75 population where "fit for what" is the enrolment criterion, the placebo question is solved by add-on designs (every patient gets active backbone), and the trial's success criterion is not remission rate but **the proportion of patients alive, off transfusion, and out of hospital at two years** — the outcome that made venetoclax-azacitidine a standard despite a survival-curve crossing: this chapter's history is a sequence of exactly such endpoint revolutions.
:::
