The lymphomas and myeloma are cancers of the mature immune system — neoplasms of lymphocytes and plasma cells that carry their normal-programme behaviour into malignancy: the lymphoma reproduces (or corrupts) the architecture, traffic patterns and antigen-responses of the lymph node and mucosal immune system, while myeloma is the plasma-cell clone whose normal secretory function becomes a paraprotein and whose marrow-homing habit becomes bone destruction. Classification follows the cell of origin and its molecular lesion; treatment, since rituximab, follows the surface and checkpoint targets the same biology provides.

This chapter covers lymph-node biology as the template for lymphoma, the Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphomas, and myeloma with its paraprotein complications.

## The node as template

::: definition Lymphoid architecture and the neoplasms it breeds {#def-node}
The germinal centre — where an activated B cell proliferates (centroblast), **somatic-hypermutates** its immunoglobulin genes (AID-driven), selects for affinity on follicular-dendritic-cell antigen (centrocyte), and either exits as a memory B or plasmablast — is the engine of adaptive immunity of [[immunology/adaptive-responses]] and the **cradle of lymphoma genetics**: AID's off-target deletions generate the classic translocations — **t(14;18) IGH::BCL2** of follicular lymphoma (the germinal-centre B cell that cannot undergo apoptosis — the anti-apoptotic lesion whose long MGUS-like latency of decades precedes most follicular lymphomas), **t(8;14) MYC** of Burkitt lymphoma (the proliferative arrest of germinal-centre kinetics — the fastest human tumour, with the highest chemotherapy cure rate — the biology–therapy paradox in one entity), and the mantle-zone, marginal-zone and MALT entities whose cells home to and expand within the compartments they inhabit (the stomach's MALT and its **H. pylori**-driven, antigen-dependent early stage — the one cancer whose first-line therapy is antibiotics; Hashimoto's thyroid-lymphoma risk; Sjögren's and splenic marginal-zone; the coeliac-associated enteropathy-type T-cell lymphoma of [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]). The clinical corollaries of architecture: lymphomas present as **painless enlarged nodes or extranodal masses following the tissue logic of their cell of origin**, spread through lymphatic and blood routes in predictable neighbourly fashion (Hodgkin's contiguous nodal stepwise spread versus non-Hodgkin's earlier haematogenous, multifocal pattern), produce **B symptoms** (fever, drenching night sweats, weight loss — the cytokine output of the malignant and reactive cells, and an staging and prognostic variable), and demand **excisional biopsy with flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry, cytogenetics and molecular studies** — a needle core that shows "atypical lymphocytes" is a diagnosis deferred, not obtained, and the classic missed Hodgkin lymphoma hides in a fine-needle specimen that cannot show the architecture.
:::

## Hodgkin lymphoma

::: definition The Reed–Sternberg cell and its microenvironment {#def-hl}
Hodgkin lymphoma is a tumour mostly of the young adult (15–35, a second rise after 55) in which the neoplastic cell — the **Reed–Sternberg cell and variants**, a crippled **germinal-centre-derived B cell** (clonal IGH rearrangements, often with loss of the B-cell programme: CD15⁺CD30⁺, PAX5-weak, CD20-negative-or-variable — an immunophenotype of escape rather than identity, frequently with **EBV** genome, the mixed-cellularity and HIV-associated varieties) — constitutes a **minority of the mass**, which is mostly reactive T cells, eosinophils, histiocytes and fibrosis: the tumour is a **cytokine organ** (the RS cell's ligands and rosettes recruit and protect it — the JAK-STAT and NF-κB dependencies, the PD-L1 genomic 9p24.3 amplification that the checkpoint inhibitors exploit, and the reason the disease aches with alcohol and burns with B symptoms). Classical subtypes (nodular-sclerosis — the young woman's mediastinal mass; mixed-cellularity; lymphocyte-rich; lymphocyte-depleted) and **nodular-lymphocyte-predominant HL** (the CD20⁺ "popcorn"/LP-cell variant treated like an indolent B lymphoma) carry the treatment logic. Staging (the **Lugano/Pet-CT** era: Ann Arbor I–IV with A/B and bulky modifiers — the staging laparotomy retired by PET) and the **IPS/International Prognostic Score** count risk factors, and therapy is one of oncology's success stories: **ABVD (or the escalated brentuximab-vedotin-plus-AVD regimen for advanced disease) with response-adapted PET-guided radiotherapy decisions**, curing the large majority — leaving **late effects as the modern discipline**: anthracycline cardiomyopathy and the mantle-irradiation-era coronary, valvular, pulmonary and **second-tumour (breast, lung, thyroid) and metabolic syndromes** that make survivorship clinics, echocardiographic surveillance and the radiotherapy-sparing trials (the RAPID and HHSN268 series) the substance of present-day Hodgkin practice — a disease in which the cure rate forces the conversation onto the next thirty years.
:::

::: example The nurse's neck lump {#ex-hl}
A 27-year-old woman has a painless right supraclavicular lump for six weeks, itching after hot baths, drenching sweats and 8 kg loss; the chest film shows an anterior mediastinal mass.
::: solution
**Classical Hodgkin lymphoma, nodular-sclerosis type until biopsy says otherwise** — the age, the contiguous cervical/supraclavicular spread, the mediastinal mass (the nodular-sclerosis signature: young women, thymus-region disease) and the **B symptoms** (fever >38 °C, drenching night sweats, >10% weight loss — here three of three, staging modifier B; the pruritus the stem adds is the cytokine itch of the disease, not specific but classic). Diagnosis: **excisional node biopsy** (the RS cells — CD30⁺CD15⁺PAX5-weak — in their eosinophil-rich, lacunar, septated architecture), baseline **PET-CT, marrow now PET-replaced, bloods including ESR and albumin, HIV and hepatitis serology, echo and lung-function before treatment, and fertility preservation discussed before the first dose** (the gonadotoxic and gonadal-site-radiotherapy legacy — the standard of care conversation). Treatment: early-stage favourable/unfavourable combined-modality or PET-adapted abbreviated ABVD with involved-site radiotherapy decisions, and advanced-stage **brentuximab-vedotin-plus-AVD (the ECHELON regimen)** or A+AVD-class protocols; response-adapted by interim PET (the Deauville score — the five-point visual scale that decides escalation and de-escalation, the image-based biomatisation of the field), relapse managed by **salvage plus autologous transplant** (the historical standard — the HD-series' cure of the majority of chemosensitive relapses) and now **checkpoint blockade (nivolumab, pembrolizumab) and brentuximab** combinations in the refractory and transplant-consolidated settings. The follow-up list the case implies at year twenty: annual breast MRI for mantle radiation in youth, cardiac and thyroid surveillance, vaccination and secondary-counselling — the chapter's larger point that Hodgkin lymphoma is today a chronic-disease-success story whose remaining problems are iatrogenic.
:::
:::

## Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

::: definition The indolent–aggressive axis {#def-nhl}
The WHO/ICC classification sorts NHL by lineage, cell of origin and driver — but the clinical spine is the **indolent versus aggressive axis**: **indolent lymphomas** (follicular grades 1–2, marginal-zone, small-lymphocytic/CLL-SLL, lymphoplasmacytic) are incurable with chemotherapy alone yet survive for years-to-decades, present with painless generalized lymphadenopathy and marrow and spleen involvement, and are managed by **watch-and-wait when asymptomatic** (the formal proof that treating the scan can harm the patient), obinutuzumab- or rituximab-containing regimens (the bendamustine-era front line), **radiotherapy for localising stage I/II disease (curative intent)**, and — for relapse after relapse — the cellular therapies (**CAR-T CD19-directed — ZUMA and TRANSCEND-class remissions in multiply-relapsed follicular and mantle disease — bispecific antibodies, and the EZH2 inhibitor tazemetostat in the EZH2-mutant subset**: the molecular-medicine showcase of an epigenetic driver). **Aggressive lymphomas** (DLBCL — the commonest NHL and the curable one: R-CHOP and the **polatuzumab-plus-R-CHP** advanced-stage update, with double-hit MYC/BCL2-BCL6 high-grade B-cell lymphoma and the TP53-mutant varieties demanding intensification and transplant strategies; mantle-cell (the CCND1/MCL1 t(11;14), the aggressive and blastoid variants and now BTK inhibitors — the Bruton-kinase-dependence exploited by ibrutinib, acalabrutinib and pirtobrutinib, and the non-covalent class); **Burkitt (the MYC-addicted maximum-proliferation tumour — short intensive B-NCC and DA-EPOCH-R-class regimens with TLS prophylaxis and cure rates above 90% in children and the young)**, and the **very-aggressive and T/nk lymphomas** (nasal NK/T and EBV, enteropathy T-cell, adult T-cell leukaemia-lymphoma and its HTLV-1, the cutaneous mycosis fungoides/Sézary axis) round the map. The common biology worth memorising: **CD19/CD20 as the therapeutic surface** (rituximab's lineage-owning antibody, the CD20 CAR-T and the CD19/CD20 bispecifics — and its hepatitis-B reactivation black box), **antigen-dependence as a treatment target (MALT lymphoma's H. pylori eradication, Sjögren's and HCV-associated marginal-zone disease — the HCV-lymphoma viral causation story of the infectious-diseases chapters)**, and **transformation as the indolent disease's exit into aggression** (follicular → DLBCL, CLL → Richter — the new mass, rising LDH and PET-avid focus that demands re-biopsy because the treatment flips to the aggressive protocol).
:::

## Myeloma

::: definition The plasma-cell neoplastic spectrum {#def-myeloma}
Myeloma is the malignant plasma cell in its marrow niche: **MGUS** (M-protein <30 g/L, clonal marrow plasma cells <10%, no end-organ criteria — ~3%/year progression risk tracked by the FLC ratio, M-spike and free-light-chain trends, the premalignant state of the world's oldest adults and the source of the "don't treat the band on the electrophoresis" rule) → **smouldering myeloma** (intermediate burden; selected high-risk biology now treated early on trial evidence) → **symptomatic myeloma**, defined by the **CRAB** criteria (hyper**C**alcium, **R**enal impairment, **A**naemia, **B**one lesions) plus the modern **SLiM** biomarkers (Sixty-percent clonal plasma cells, LC ratio ≥100, >1 MRI focal lesion) that identify the myeloma-defining state before the skeleton pays. The clinical picture is the paraprotein and the niche's damage: **bone disease** (lytic lesions, pathological and vertebral-collapse fractures, the back pain of a thoracic cord compression — an emergency with dexamethasone and radiotherapy/neurosurgery now; the normal alkaline-phosphatase, osteoblast-silenced lytic lesion on biochemistry and the **low-dose whole-body CT or PET-CT/WB-MRI** that replaced the skeletal survey), **marrow failure** (the normocytic anaemia with rouleaux and a high ESR, thrombocytopenia late), **immune paresis and renal loss** (the immunoparetic encapsulated and atypical infections — the leading cause of death, and the light-chain **cast nephropathy** — filtered free light chains precipitated with Tamm-Horsfall protein in the distal tubule, precipitated by dehydration, hypercalcaemia and NSAIDs; the amyloid (AL) and light-chain-deposition glomerulopathies), **hypercalcaemia** (the osteoclast-activation — RANKL and MIP-1α-driven — with the hydration-calcitonin-bisphosphonate/denosumab emergency), and **hyperviscosity** (IgA and IgM — the Waldenström macroglobulinaemia of the lymphoplasmacytic disease, IgM's pentameric volume-expansion: the blurred vision, headache, mucosal bleed and digital ischaemia that makes **plasmapheresis** the immediate answer before any chemotherapy). Diagnosis: the serum and urine electrophoresis and immunofixation, **serum free light chains**, marrow (clonal plasma cells — CD138⁺, the FISH high-risk lesions del17p, t(4;14), t(14;16), 1q gain on MRD-standardised panels), the imaging above, and the PET's role in the solitary plasmacytoma and radiotherapy planning. Treatment has become target-rich: **lenalidomide-bortezomib-dexamethasone (RVd) induction, high-dose melphalan and autologous transplant in the fit (the age ceilings rising), the proteasome inhibitor (bortezomib, carfilzomib — the immunoproteasome-addicted cell's unfolded-protein-collapse mechanism), immunomodulator (the cereblon-axis degradation of Ikaros factors — a molecular-mechanism lesson), the anti-CD38 antibodies daratumumab and isatuximab (the myeloma CD38 target with its transfusion-interference caveat), bispecific T-cell engagers (BCMA- and GPRC5D-directed) and CAR-T for the multiply-relapsed**, the **bisphosphonate/denosumab bone agents with their osteonecrosis-of-the-jaw dental-clearance rule**, the infection prophylaxis (aciclovir with proteasome inhibitors, the IVIG replacement debate in recurrent infection), and the monitoring by electrophoresis and free light chains with **MRD now entering trial and practice** — the outcome a few years ago unimaginable: myeloma's five-year survival curve bending decade by decade.
:::

::: example The retiree's bad back {#ex-myeloma}
A 71-year-old man has four months of thoracic back pain worse on movement; Hb 92, ESR 120, corrected calcium 2.9, creatinine 140, alkaline phosphatase normal; the film shows punched-out skull deposits and a collapsed T8 vertebra with a normal-appearing disc space.
::: solution
**Myeloma until the electrophoresis proves otherwise** — the CRAB picture complete (Calcium raised, Renal impairment, Anaemia, Bone lesions) in the age band, with the **normal alkaline phosphatase the osteoblast-silencing discriminator** against metastatic carcinoma (and the skull's punched-out, non-sclerotic lesions the radiographic twin), the ESR's rouleaux-driven rise the cheap clue. The sequence: **serum/urine electrophoresis and immunofixation and serum free light chains, full blood count, calcium and creatinine, and an urgent whole-spine MRI — because the cord compression (the vertebral collapse with the band-like radiation, the evolving weakness, bladder involvement) is the one emergency: dexamethasone immediately, then radiotherapy or neurosurgical stabilisation within hours, not days.** Then the marrow and FISH staging, the myeloma-defining-events assessment (the 60%/SLiM criteria of [[#def-myeloma]]), and induction per transplant fitness (RVd-class ± anti-CD38 with transplant, or the daratumumab-lenalidomide-bortezomib-dexamethasone quad-regimens in the unfit; the renal-specific bortezomib-dexamethasone backbone if cast nephropathy is present, with hydration, avoidance of contrast and NSAIDs and the light-chain reduction that rescues the tubule). The supportive bundle — bone agents with dental review, infection prophylaxis, the anaemia's work-up for coexisting iron/B₁₂/renal causes, and the VTE prophylaxis when the immunomodulator is on — is as much of the management as the chemotherapy, and the case's teaching is the chapter's summary: **the paraprotein, the skeleton, the kidney and the immune system are one story about the plasma cell.**
:::
:::

::: example The epigastric pain that was not an ulcer {#ex-malt}
A 58-year-old has six weeks of epigastric ache; endoscopy shows a thickened, nodular gastric body with a shallow ulcer; biopsies reveal a dense small-B-cell infiltrate with lymphoepithelial lesions; *H. pylori* is present on staining; CT shows gastric-wall thickening and a few perigastric nodes.
::: solution
**Gastric MALT lymphoma (extranodal marginal-zone lymphoma)** — the [[#def-node]]'s antigen-dependence in a chart: the lymphoid tissue is *acquired* (chronic *H. pylori* gastritis builds MALT where none exists), and the early lymphoma remains **trophically dependent on the bacteria-driven T-cell help** rather than fully autonomous. Hence the first-line therapy is **eradication (the bismuth or standard quadruple regimens of [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]'s *H. pylori* chapter)**, with **endoscopic and histologic regression assessed at ~3–6 months in 70–80%** — molecular remission tracking slower than histology, and predictive negatives being **t(11;18) API2-MALT1** (the translocation that renders the lymphoma antibiotic-independent — treat with radiotherapy or immunochemotherapy), deep or bulky disease, and duodenal ulcer-associated strains' partial immunity from the field effect. The stepwise ladder after failed eradication (involved-site radiotherapy — highly effective and curative in localised gastric disease; then rituximab-based systemic therapy for disseminated or transformed disease) tracks the same biology from antigen-drive to autonomy; the differential the case teaches (lymphoepithelial lesions versus reactive MALT hyperplasia versus diffuse large-cell transformation with its worse prognosis) is the pathologist's version of the same axis. **The one-sentence lesson: some cancers are chronic-infection diseases, and the antibiotic is the oncology.**
:::

::: example The jaw swelling in a five-year-old {#ex-burkitt}
A four-year-old in equatorial Africa has a rapidly enlarging jaw mass doubling in days, fevers, and LDH of 2,400; elsewhere the same tumour presents as a childhood abdominal mass with intussusception.
::: solution
**Burkitt lymphoma** — the geography, the age, the growth fraction (near-100% Ki-67, the "starry-sky" macrophage picture of the tumour's own turnover) and the molecular lesion (**t(8;14) placing *MYC* under the immunoglobulin heavy-chain enhancer — the germinal-centre proliferation-escape of [[#def-node]]**) come together in one of medicine's most elegant causal triangles: **EBV** (endemic African form — the jaw; the malarial immune perturbation of [[infectious-diseases]] as the co-factor hypothesis), **sporadic** form (abdominal — ileocaecal mass, intussusception, ovarian and renal involvement) and the **immunodeficiency-associated** form (HIV — an AIDS-defining illness). The therapeutic physiology is the chapter's paradox at maximum: the same proliferative drive that makes it the fastest-growing human tumour makes it **the most curable with short, intensive, cycle-narrowed therapy** (dose-intensive B-NCC- and DA-EPOCH-R-class regimens, rituximab in adults), with the two management hazards being **tumour lysis** (the [[blood/leukaemia]] emergency — rasburicase, hydration, continuous monitoring before the first dose) and the **abdominal surgical trap** (perforation and haemorrhage during lysis-chemotherapy of bulky bowel disease — the surgeon and oncologist must share the calendar). CNS prophylaxis (intrathecal and high-dose-systemic strategies) completes the protocol — and the historical footnote the case carries: Burkitt's 1958 clinical epidemiology and the subsequent Epstein–Barr virus discovery founded the entire human tumour-virus field, from this one jaw.
:::

::: quiz
The malignant cell of classical Hodgkin lymphoma is:
- [ ] A reactive T lymphocyte driven by cytokines
- [x] A germinal-centre-derived B cell that has largely lost its B-cell surface programme
- [ ] A follicular dendritic reticulum cell
- [ ] An anaplastic histiocyte
::: solution
The Reed–Sternberg cell carries clonal immunoglobulin-gene rearrangements proving B-cell derivation, yet — through the aberrant NF-κB and JAK-STAT signalling and the 9p24.3/PD-L1 and ROSETTE-forming microenvironment — has lost most B-markers (CD20 negative or weak, CD79a often absent) while gaining CD30 and CD15 ([[#def-hl]]). The immunophenotype explains the biology (the crippled germinal-centre cell normally doomed by apoptosis that the BCL2 and EBV-LMP1 lesions rescue), the diagnostic panel, and the therapeutic escape routes (CD30-targeted brentuximab, PD-1 blockade — the loss of the B-cell surface that makes rituximab useless in classical disease while it defines the nodular-lymphocyte-predominant variant's CD20⁺, rituximab-responsive biology).
:::

::: history
**Thomas Hodgkin's** 1832 lecture on the "disease of the lymphatic glands" (the morbid anatomy that later bears his name), **Virchow's** cellular account and the 1890s separation of "granuloma" from leukaemia, **Reed's** (1902) and **Sternberg's** (1926) giant cells, and the electron-microscopy era's B-cell hints that culminated in **Poppema's** 1977 lymphocyte-predominant distinction; then the **Rye (1966) and Rappaport classifications**, the **Working Formulation (1982)**, and the **REAL/WHO** cell-of-origin revolution — including the germinal-centre-versus-activated-B-cell classification of DLBCL from the lymphochip work of Alizadeh, Rosenwald, Staudt and colleagues (1999–2000). Therapy's landmarks: **Henry Kaplan's** radical-radiotherapy era in Stanford and the **DeVita and Freireich combination-chemotherapy programmes (MOPP, then ABVD)** at the NCI that made the first curable adult metastatic cancer; **Dalla-Favera, Tsujimoto and Kroemer's** generation on the t(14;18) BCL2 lesion and apoptosis; **Burkitt's** African jaw-tumour epidemiology (the 1958 thesis that seeded the tumour-virus hypothesis, Epstein and Achong's 1964 herpesvirus and zur Hausen's HPV proof decades later); Waldenström's macroglobulinaemia and the 1960s immunoglobulin chemistry that made myeloma a protein disease; and the rituximab (1997), bortezomib, lenalidomide, anti-CD38 and CAR-T sequence that has turned the chapter's worst-prognosis columns into its most active therapeutic wards.
:::

::: summary
- Lymphomas are germinal-centre and compartment diseases: the AID-driven translocations (BCL2, MYC), the homing of the cell of origin and the antigen-drive (H. pylori, HCV, coeliac) write the clinical map.
- Diagnosis requires architecture — the excisional biopsy — and classification now runs on WHO/ICC molecular lines with cell-of-origin subtyping directing therapy.
- Hodgkin lymphoma is a cytokine-organ tumour of a crippled B cell curable in most, whose modern frontier is late effects and immune-targeted (CD30, PD-1) salvage.
- Non-Hodgkin lymphoma management turns on the indolent–aggressive axis: watch-and-wait versus curative-intent polychemotherapy-plus-antibody versus the BTK-inhibitor, CAR-T and bispecific sequences of relapse.
- Myeloma is the plasma cell's niche and product: CRAB-plus-SLiM diagnosis, the paraprotein's renal, viscosimetric and immune-paretic complications, and a target-rich therapy (proteasome, cereblon, CD38, BCMA/GPRC5D) with bending survival curves.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Why excision {level=1}
A PET-avid axillary node: why is core-needle biopsy sometimes adequate and excision sometimes mandatory? Give two examples.
::: solution
The answer is architecture and entity: **Hodgkin lymphoma** (the RS-cell-in-reactive-milieu pattern, the lacunar septation and nodularity that define the subtype — the fine-needle or short-core "reactive/unknown" rate high, and the subtype deciding therapy) and **follicular-versus-reactive hyperplasia** (neoplastic follicles back-to-back without polarised germinal centres, the BCL2 stain pattern — all architecture-dependent), versus the situations where a good core with **flow cytometry, immunohistochemistry and molecular studies suffices** — a relapse biopsy of a known lymphoma, an obviously carcinomatous node, or a deep inaccessible site where excision carries nerve or organ risk (the EBUS/transbronchial and bone/core-lymph-node routes of modern staging). Rule: **tissue is the diagnosis, architecture is half the tissue, and if the specimen cannot answer the question, re-biopsy rather than treat the assumption.**
:::
:::

::: exercise The B symptoms and the stage {level=1}
Define the B symptoms and the Lugano/Ann Arbor modifiers, and stage: (a) two ipsilateral cervical regions plus spleen; (b) para-aortic nodes plus a bone lesion.
::: solution
**B symptoms**: unexplained fever >38 °C, drenching night sweats, unexplained >10% weight loss in six months (the cytokine trio of [[#def-node]]). Stages: **I** one region, **II** two or more same side of diaphragm, **III** both sides (spleen = lymphatic, so **III**), **IV** extralymphatic (bone, liver, marrow); **A/B** modifiers and the bulky/"X" designation. So: (a) **stage III A/B depending on symptoms** (cervical above, spleen below the diaphragm); (b) **stage IV** (the extranodal bone lesion). The clinical use: HL staging drives combined-modality intensity, and in NHL the **FLIPI and IPI** count nodes, LDH, stage, performance and age — the B symptoms' prognostic weight differing by entity.
:::
:::

::: exercise Watch and wait {level=2}
A 60-year-old with stage III follicular lymphoma, grade 1, no symptoms, Hb and platelets normal, nodes 2–3 cm, LDH normal: the tumour board recommends observation. Justify with the trial evidence and define the triggers that would start treatment.
::: solution
**GELF/BNLI and the PRIMA-era randomised evidence**: immediate versus deferred therapy in low-tumour-burden advanced follicular lymphoma shows **no overall-survival advantage to early treatment** with the cost of toxicity, cytopenias, immunoparesis and (for bendamustine-containing regimens) second-malignancy and infection risk — so observation with review (the "watch-and-wait", the formal name in the guidelines) is the standard for the **asymptomatic, non-bulky, non-organ-threatening** patient. Treatment triggers (the GELF/BNLI criteria as a checklist): B symptoms, large or progressive tumour burden (bulky nodes, effusions), cytopenias from marrow replacement, symptomatic splenomegaly, organ compression or threat (the ureteric obstruction, the cord route of [[#ex-myeloma]]'s spine logic), and the transformation suspicion (focal PET avidity, rising LDH — re-biopsy, because the finding converts therapy to the aggressive-DLBCL protocol). The follow-up includes the FLIPI risk discussion, the second-malignancy and infection vigilance of the treated era, and the honest line that the disease is generally incurable but survivable for many years — the chronic-disease consultation, rehearsed in myeloma's smouldering state with the same ethics.
:::
:::

::: exercise The antibody's two faces {level=2}
Rituximab transformed B-cell lymphoma therapy; it also reactivates hepatitis B and, in myeloma-adjacent CD20-positive disease, causes prolonged B-cell aplasia. Explain each consequence from the target.
::: solution
**CD20** is expressed from the pre-B stage to memory B cells — not on stem cells (which is why B-cell recovery occurs from the marrow reservoir, over months) and not on plasma cells (which is why rituximab spares established humoral immunity — mostly). The therapeutic face: lysis by complement and ADCC, direct signalling and chemosensitisation of the CD20⁺ lymphomas (follicular, DLBCL, marginal zone, MCL — the backbone of the indolent and aggressive combinations; the CD20 CAR-T and bispecific extensions in [[#def-nhl]]). The hazards: **HBV reactivation** — the virus's covalently closed DNA persists in B-lineage cells and its immune control depends partly on the T-B cooperation rituximab disrupts, giving the fulminant hepatitis that made universal **HBsAg/anti-HBV screening and prophylactic nucleoside analogue in the seropositive** a black-box rule (the same logic as checkpoint-inhibitor and BTK-inhibitor hepatitis vigilance); **prolonged hypogammaglobulinaemia and neutropaenia** (the B-cell aplasia, the infection risk — the IVIG replacement in the symptomatic, and the encapsulated-organism vigilance); the **infusion cytokine first-dose reaction** (tumor- and macrophage-cytokine release, premedication and split-dosing — the same pathophysiology, attenuated, of the modern bispecific step-up dosing); and the practicalities — the hepatitis and vaccination timing and the **live-vaccine contraindication window** before and after therapy.
:::
:::

::: exercise Cast and cause {level=2}
A myeloma patient presents with acute-on-chronic kidney injury after a gastroenteritis and a dose of ibuprofen; the urine dipstick shows trace protein but the 24-hour protein is 6 g. Explain the dipstick discordance, the renal lesions of myeloma, and the management.
::: solution
The dipstick detects **albumin**; the 24-hour assay measures total protein including the **light chains** — the discordance is the clue to **overflow (Bence-Jones) proteinuria**. Lesions, by mechanism: **cast nephropathy** (free light chains — filtered, reabsorbed and catabolised via the cubilin/megalin system [[renal/tubular-function]]; when the load exceeds capacity they precipitate with Tamm-Horsfall mucoprotein in the distal tubule forming obstructing, giant-cell-reactive casts — the classic AKI with dehydration, hypercalcaemia, sepsis and NSAIDs as precipitants), **light-chain proximal tubulopathy (Fanconi syndrome — the acquired bicarbonate, glucose, phosphate and amino-acid wasting of [[renal/tubular-function]]'s transport failures)**, **AL amyloidosis and light-chain deposition disease** (the glomerular nodular pictures with albuminuric-range proteinuria and the Congo-red and non-congoophilic-deposition contrast), and the chronic hypercalcaemia/volume-depletion nephrocalcinosis. Management: **remove the precipitants (the NSAID, the calcium, the dehydration), high-rate IV fluids, urgent reduction of the light-chain production (the bortezomib-dexamethasone backbone with dose-adjusted others — the haemodiafiltration and high-cut-off dialysis trials' honest failure and the plasmapheresis's unsettled place), treat the calcium and the infection, and the nephrology transplant conversation for established dialysis-dependent myeloma kidney (the modern myeloma-era answer that these patients are now transplant candidates once in deep response)**. The chapter's physiology lesson again: the paraprotein is not a laboratory curiosity but a filterable, precipitable, depositing molecule whose pathology follows its physical chemistry.
:::
:::

::: exercise The macroglobulinaemia {level=2}
A 74-year-old has blurred vision, epistaxis, headache and a plasma viscosity of 4.2 (normal <1.8); IgM 38 g/L; the marrow shows a small lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with MYD88 L265P. Explain the viscosity arithmetic and the emergency management.
::: solution
**Waldenström macroglobulinaemia with symptomatic hyperviscosity.** IgM is a **pentamer (≈970 kDa)**: it stays intravascular (25% of intravascular volume per gram versus IgG's smaller distribution), so serum levels rise steeply with modest tumour bulk and plasma viscosity climbs **exponentially with concentration** (the non-Newtonian, aggregation-driven curve of [[physiology/homeostasis-body-fluids]]'s and [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]'s rheology sections) — retinal-vein sausage-link engorgement, mucosal bleeding (the protein coats and impairs platelet and clot-factor function — the reason aspirin and anticoagulants are hazardous here), and the neurological triad, with **symptoms typically above relative viscosity ~4 (the IgM threshold varies with binding properties)**. Emergency: **plasmapheresis (the immediate physics — remove the pentamer's intravascular pool, its rebound modest since IgM's distribution is intravascular)** before any red-cell transfusion (transfusing first raises viscosity — the pre-transfusion-exchange rule), then clone-directed therapy (**BTK inhibitors — ibrutinib, zanubrutinib — plus rituximab; the MYD88 L265P-driven MYD88/TLR-hyperactivity and CXCR4-axis biology that also explains the disease's CNS (Bing–Neel) homing**), with the rituximab **IgM-flare caution** (the transient rise that can precipitate viscosity — hold or cover with prior plasmapheresis in high-burden patients). The distinguishing exam pair: hyperviscosity syndromes (IgM's volume effect, the IgA polymerisation, the myelocytomas' WBC of [[blood/leukaemia]]'s leukostasis exercise) all present as flow disorders and are treated as flow disorders first.
:::
:::

::: exercise Richter and transformation {level=2}
A treated CLL patient develops a rapidly enlarging unilateral tonsillar region, B symptoms and an LDH of 900; the PET shows one node with SUVmax 18 while others are 4. What are the two possibilities, and how are they separated?
::: solution
**Richter transformation** — CLL's escape into an aggressive large-B-cell lymphoma (clonally related in most, a de-novo DLBCL in a minority, and the rarer Hodgkin variant) versus the **proliferation centre-flare / infection / second primary** of an immunocompromised host. Separation is **tissue and imaging**: the focal SUV-max discordance (the 18-versus-4 logic — the PET-selected biopsy site) guides the excisional biopsy — the morphology (sheets of large cells) and the **IGH clonality's shared or distinct rearrangement** (the clonally-related, chemo-refractory variety versus the de-novo DLBCL treated as standard R-CHOP-class disease), with the EBV status (the immunosuppression-driven EBV-positive lymphoproliferation of [[immunology/immunodeficiency]]-adjacent literature) and the PET re-staging. The treatment consequence: **clonal-related Richter carries the worst prognosis and demands intensive regimens with early cellular-therapy (transplant, CAR-T, bispecific) consideration; de-novo DLBCL in a CLL patient is treated like DLBCL with CLL-therapy interaction planning** — and the general principle: **never treat transformation on assumption; the re-biopsy changes the protocol and the survival curve.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Design the survivorship clinic {level=3}
You run a clinic for patients cured of Hodgkin lymphoma at 25–40. Build the surveillance plan from the treatment exposures (ABVD, brentuximab, checkpoint inhibitors, mediastinal and mantle radiotherapy), each element justified by the organ physiology and trial-era evidence.
::: solution
**Cardiac** (anthracycline dose-related cardiomyopathy — the [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]'s ejection-fraction physiology — plus the mediastinal-radiotherapy triad of accelerated coronary disease, valvular fibrosis and conduction/pericardial disease): **echocardiographic surveillance per dose and field, aggressive lipid, blood-pressure and smoking control, and a low threshold for ischaemia imaging (the young woman's atypical chest pain is a coronary-history question)**; **pulmonary** (bleomycin and radiotherapy fibrosis — spirometry/DLCO before re-treatment and after symptoms, the operability-anaesthesia warning of the post-bleomycin oxygen-sensitivity literature); **endocrine/metabolic** (thyroid function after neck fields — the hypothyroidism of the mantle era; the metabolic syndrome of the irradiated; gonadal function and fertility — the AMH and spermatogenesis data of [[endocrine/reproductive-endocrinology]]); **second malignancy** (the breast-cancer risk after mantle-in-youth with **annual mammography plus breast MRI from ~25–30 or 8–10 years post-radiation** — the most cost-validated screen of any survivorship guideline; lung cancer with smoking cessation as the highest-yield intervention; the thyroid and GI risks by field; therapy-related MDS/AML — the count surveillance and the earlier CHIP lesson from [[blood/haematopoiesis]]); **bone** (the gonadal-failure and corticosteroid-era densitometry); **psychosocial and financial toxicity, return-to-work and vaccine and infection plans** (the asplenic-function analogue of nodal-field radiotherapy's immune loss). The clinic's justification is the cohort data themselves (the Childhood-Cancer-Survivor and British-Cohort Hodgkin series — premature mortality now often non-relapse), the organising rule is **exposure-based rather than diagnosis-based surveillance** (each modality's dose-and-field record drives the test and interval — the treatment-summary document as the prescription), and the endpoint philosophy is this chapter's: a cured patient is a chronic disease whose comorbidities are treatable, which is the survivorship clinic's whole business.
:::
