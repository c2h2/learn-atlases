Stroke is the commonest single cause of adult neurological admission and one of the few conditions in which minutes measurably convert into preserved function: **roughly a million brain cells die per minute during large-vessel occlusion, thrombolysis and thrombectomy have hard, clock-dependent numbers attached, and the secondary-prevention armamentarium is among the most evidence-dense in medicine.** Almost every clinician, not just neurologists, participates in the first hours.

This chapter covers the vascular anatomy and stroke syndromes, transient ischaemic attack, acute management including reperfusion, haemorrhagic stroke, and secondary prevention and rehabilitation.

## Anatomy and syndromes

::: definition Vascular territories and clinical syndromes {#def-territories}
**The anterior circulation (the middle cerebral artery and its superior and inferior divisions and lenticulostriates - contralateral face-and-arm-predominant hemiparesis and hemisensory loss with homonymous hemianopia and, in the dominant hemisphere, aphasia (the Broca non-fluent and Wernicke fluent syndromes and the conduction and global variants, examined and typed because they change rehabilitation and capacity assessments) and, in the non-dominant, hemineglect with anosognosia and dressing-and-spatial deficits - [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]] for the arterial maps and the watershed zones between territories whose hypoperfusion causes the proximal limb-and-shoulder "man-in-a-barrel" syndrome; the anterior cerebral artery - contralateral leg-predominant weakness with abulia and urinary incontinence and the frontal release signs, and the bilateral ACA-anomaly cases with paraparesis that mimic cord lesions - [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]] for the misrouting); the posterior circulation (the vertebrobasilar system and its brainstem and thalamic and occipital and cerebellar branches - the crossed syndromes in which ipsilateral cranial-nerve deficits pair with contralateral long-tract signs and which non-neurologists misread as "conversion", the "locked-in" ventral pontine state with preserved consciousness and eye movement that must never be mistaken for unresponsiveness in intensive care decisions - [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]; the posterior cerebral arteries - homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing and, when dominant, alexia without agraphia; thalamic infarcts with pure sensory strokes and the Dejerine-Roussy pain; and the cerebellar strokes whose vertigo-and-vomiting-and-cannot-walk triad with direction-changing nystagmus hides behind "peripheral vertigo" until the fourth-ventricle compression and hydrocephalus deteriorate - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the HINTS distinction); the small-vessel syndromes (lacunar infarcts with their pure motor, pure sensory, ataxic hemiparesis and clumsy-hand-dysarthria classic quartet and their hypertension-and-diabetes substrate and the vascular cognitive impairment and gait-apraxis of cumulative white-matter disease - [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]], [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]); the haemorrhagic side (intracerebral haemorrhage with its hypertensive basal-ganglia, thalamic, pontine and cerebellar locations versus lobar bleeds whose cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy and anticoagulation-and-antiplatelet associations change recurrence counselling; and subarachnoid haemorrhage with its sentinel-headache and thunderclap presentation, the aneurysm hunt and the vasospasm-and-hyponatraemia-and-rebleed days 3-12, all handled below); and the mimics that arrive as strokes - seizure with Todd paresis and hypoglycaemia (the two most common, and neither needs a scanner before treatment decisions - check glucose), migraine with aura, functional weakness (hoover and the gait patterns), intracranial tumour and bleed, and the post-ictal and toxic-metabolic encephalopathies - [[nervous-system/epilepsy]], [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]], [[pathology/cancer-biology]].**
:::

## The acute hours

::: definition Suspected stroke: pre-hospital to reperfusion {#def-acute}
**Recognition and systems (the FAST-and-beyond public campaigns and their stroke-mimic over-calls as an accepted cost and the last-known-well clock and the ambulance triage to hyper-acute stroke units with direct-to-CT pathways - the systems-engineering triumph that made thrombolysis deliverable, with the equity caveat that recognition and call-out rates differ by language, age, disability and neighbourhood - [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]]); the first-minute assessment (ABC with the swallow screen before anything oral and the oxygen-only-if-hypoxic rule from trial evidence and glucose measured not assumed and the BP documented in the context of thrombolysis eligibility thresholds and the NIHSS as the shared currency between teams - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]); imaging and decision (non-contrast CT to exclude haemorrhage and the CT-angiography-plus-perfusion mismatch model for the occlusion-and-penumbra question and the MRI-diffusion superiority for posterior fossa and wake-up strokes with the WAKE-UP and EXTEND trial era extending treatment to unknown-onset and up-to-24-hour windows for selected occlusions - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]); reperfusion therapies (intravenous thrombolysis - alteplase and tenecteplase-practice-shift with the 4.5-hour core window and the imaging-selected extensions and the absolute and relative contraindications - recent surgery and trauma and stroke, anticoagulation with elevated INR or direct-oral-anticoagulant use within doses and renal clearance, active bleeding, the do-not-delay-for-platelets-and-INR-until-history-demands rule and the tenecteplase convenience and the door-to-needle metrics and the symptomatic-haemorrhage-rate-of-6%-informed-consent arithmetic; and mechanical thrombectomy - anterior-circulation large-vessel occlusion with good premorbid state within 6 hours and up to 24 hours in perfusion-selected patients, the number-needed-to-treat numbers that justify transferring between hospitals, the anaesthesia strategy debates and the complications - [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]], [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]] for the carotid and arch sources); the immediate aftercare (the stroke-unit floor as a mortality-reducing intervention with its swallow and aspiration and DVT and pressure and glycaemia and fever and rehabilitation-by-assessment content, the permissive-hypertension physiology of the penumbra versus the thrombolysis BP limits, the antithrombotic timing rules after infarct size and thrombolysis status, and the statin and blood-pressure trials of the secondary-prevention phase begun in the same admission - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]).**
:::

::: definition TIA: the warning that pays {#def-tia}
**Transient neurological dysfunction without infarction on modern definitions (tissue-based rather than 24-hour time-based, which is why "TIA with a DWI lesion" is reclassified as minor stroke) is an emergency because early stroke risk is highest in the first 48 hours and is stratifiable with ABCD2-era scores refined by imaging and aetiology. The urgent pathway: same-day assessment (the clinic-and-imaging-and-start-treatment-today model that reduced subsequent stroke by double-digit percentages in the SOS-TIA and express-clinic trials), brain imaging with diffusion and vascular imaging of the neck and head, and prolonged cardiac monitoring for atrial fibrillation (the patch-and-implantable-monitor era that converts "cryptogenic" into cardioembolic and changes antithrombotic choice) with the echocardiographic and, in the young, the patent-foramen-ovale and dissection and vasculitis and thrombophilia evaluations proportionate to age and mechanism - [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]]. Mechanism-matched prevention: carotid endarterectomy or stenting for significant symptomatic stenosis performed early (days, ideally within two weeks once the infarct size and clinical state permit) with the anaesthetic and shunt and restenosis nuances - [[surgery/vascular-urological-surgery]]; antiplatelet therapy (aspirin started acutely at 1-2 g loading where no thrombolysis, and the short dual-antiplatelet courses for high-risk TIA and minor stroke per the CHANCE and POINT era with the haemorrhage-risk trade stated) versus anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation with the direct oral anticoagulants' intracranial-bleed advantage and the timing rules after infarction (the 1-3-6-12-day heuristics from the early-start trials now being pushed earlier) and the left-atrial-appendage-closure niche for the genuinely anticoagulation-intolerant - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; and the risk-factor package (blood pressure after the acute phase, lipids, smoking with the cessation-pharmacotherapy offer, diabetes and weight and activity) which is identical to the completed-stroke package below. Cryptogenic stroke in the young deserves its own list (dissection, cardioembolism from the occult shunt and the paradoxical embolism with DVT and its thrombophilia context, the prothrombotic states and drugs and the reversible cerebral vasoconstriction and angiitis differentials) rather than reflex PFO closure, which remains an evidence-selected intervention - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]].**
:::

::: example The word-finding problem at breakfast {#ex-aphasia}
A 72-year-old retired teacher is brought in 90 minutes after her husband found her unable to get words out; she follows commands, knows him, right face and arm are weak, speech is effortful and halting; glucose 5.8; BP 158/92; NIHSS 7; on no anticoagulants; last known well 02:00, found 08:30 but husband "quite sure she was fine when he got up at 07:45".
::: solution
**Probable left MCA-division stroke with a documented last-known-well of 07:45 - within every treatment window ([[#def-territories]], [[#def-acute]]): the pathway - hyper-acute imaging (CT to exclude haemorrhage, CTA for occlusion, perfusion where the score demands) and the thrombolysis decision with the 4.5-hour arithmetic from 07:45 and the tenecteplase-versus-alteplase local protocol and the blood-pressure and glucose and INR-check constraints that are the only labs that should delay a needle - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; the aphasia caveat - a non-fluent aphasic patient consents through yes-no-verified communication with a deflator of jargon, and the capacity-versus-communication distinction must be made explicitly because "she can't speak well" is not "she can't decide" for thrombolysis and for later consent - [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]; if an M1 occlusion is found - thrombectomy candidacy discussion and inter-hospital transfer with the neurointerventional centre, and the "still worth it after thrombolysis" bridging logic per current evidence with the family told what the two treatments do differently - and if not - the permissive-hypertension and antithrombotic-after-24-hours-and-stroke-unit-admission pathway with the swallow screen before breakfast trays; and the secondary-prevention and rehabilitation openings made that week - the AF monitoring and lipid and BP plan and the speech-and-language-therapy and occupational-therapy referrals and the driving notification, which for a teacher is an identity conversation, not a form - [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]] - keep vascular cognition rows here light; cite [[public-health/prevention-promotion]] for the smoking-and-alcohol-adjacency and [[clinical-skills/records-handover]] for the discharge-letter content that the GP must be able to act on (medication changes, pending monitors, therapy goals, driving status).**
:::

::: example The worst headache of her life {#ex-sah}
A 49-year-old woman has abrupt maximal-intensity headache during housework, neck stiffness, and one vomit; GCS 15, no focal signs; CT head shows basal cistern hyperdensity; she took 800 mg ibuprofen and "felt worse".
::: solution
**Aneurysmal subarachnoid haemorrhage - a rebleed-prevention race and a vasospasm watch ([[#def-territories]]): the first 24 hours - neurosurgical or neurointerventional referral with the CT-angiography aneurysm hunt and the grading scales (Hunt-Hess and World Federation scores) that predict and communicate risk, analgesia and antiemetic and stool-softener prescription, nimodipine started for the neurological-complication reduction, the avoid-NSAID-and-antiplatelet logic with the ibuprofen history noted for reversal-planning, and the neuro-observations with the hydrocephalus-clinical-drain rationale and the hyponatraemia monitoring for the SIADH-versus-cerebral-wasting trap - [[renal/water-sodium]], [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]]; the definitive aneurysm treatment - coiling versus clipping decided by aneurysm anatomy and centre expertise with the trial-era outcomes stated and the timing usually early, with the EVD-versus-lumbar-drainage and the decompressive-craniectomy-for-malignant-infarct decisions if vasospasm day 4-10 delivers a delayed ischaemic deficit (the induced-hypertension-and-euvolaemia-era haemodynamics replacing triple-H dogma, the endovascular options) - [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]], [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]]; the look-backwards - the unruptured-incidentals-and-family-screening-and-genetic-context questions (the rare connective-tissue and polycystic disease associations, the smoking-and-hypertension modification, the contralateral-anxiety-scan debate) and the disclosure of the small-but-real screening-detected-aneurysm overtreatment problem - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[medical-ethics-law/justice-resources]]; and the survivorship - the fatigue-and-cognition-and-mood-and-return-to-work reality that follows even "excellent-grade" SAH, the driving rules and the follow-up angiography for recanalisation after coiling and the hydrocephalus-that-presents-months-later gait-and-bladder story - [[nervous-system/headache-pain]], [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]].**
:::

::: example The stenosis that needs a date {#ex-carotid}
A 67-year-old man has 15 minutes of left eye monocular blindness (amaurosis fugax) yesterday, now resolved; CTA shows 85% ipsilateral internal carotid stenosis; LDL 4.1; still smoking; NIHSS 0.
::: solution
**Symptomatic high-grade carotid stenosis - the operation is timed like a drug ([[#def-tia]], [[surgery/vascular-urological-surgery]]): risk stratification - the 12-week recurrence risk after symptomatic stenosis concentrated in the first days, the ABCD2-era refinements and the imaging confirmation of the culprit (and the "why not the other side" question answered by the ocular rather than hemispheric anatomy of ophthalmic-artery embolism) - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]; the urgent pathway - same-admission or same-week carotid endarterectomy (or stenting per anatomy, age and surgical fitness with the transfemoral-versus-transcarotid nuance) because benefit decays weekly after the event and the "outpatient-in-six-weeks" referral is the commonest system failure in this syndrome - [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]]; the perioperative medicine - the BP-lability-and-nerve-injury-and-hyperperfusion counselling, the antiplatelet-and-statin loading and smoking-cessation-pharmacotherapy started now rather than at discharge, and the contralateral-occlusion-and-arch-disease documentation - [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[cardiovascular/hypertension]]; and the long game - the LDL target with high-intensity statin plus add-on therapy if needed, the BP strategy after revascularisation (and the cerebral-hyperperfusion-syndrome warning for the early-post-op headache patient), and the amaurosis-recurrence-red-flag teaching with same-day-return instructions that prevent the completed stroke - [[#def-prevention]], [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The bleed on apixaban {#ex-doac-ich}
A 81-year-old on apixaban for AF falls and hits her head; two hours later she is vomiting with a GCS of 11 and a large right putaminal haemorrhage on CT; last apixaban dose was 6 hours ago; creatinine 105.
::: solution
**DOAC-associated intracerebral haemorrhage - reverse, resuscitate, reassess ([[#def-ich]]): the reversal decision - andexanet alfa where commissioned (with the thrombotic-risk-and-rebound-anti-Xa monitoring caveats) versus four-factor prothrombin-complex-concentrate as the widely available strategy, dosed by agent-and-dose-and-time-since-last-dose, with the anti-inhibitor-co-factor activity measurement where available and the do-not-give-both-by-reflex caution and the tranexamic-acid-era-trial-results-stated-honestly - [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]], [[blood/transfusion]], [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; the hour-one bundle - airway protection decision with that GCS and the anaesthetic-and-neurosurgical joint call, BP control to the trial-derived systolic target with titratable agents, avoidance of iatrogenic hypotension and hypovolaemia, attention to glucose and temperature and the ICP-basics for a deteriorating patient - [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]], [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]], [[cardiovascular/hypertension]]; the surgery question - deep haemorrhage in an elderly patient rarely benefits from evacuation (versus the deteriorating superficial lobar lesion) so the honest conversation is comfort-versus-continue-escalation with the family using baseline-function-and-age-and-haematoma-volume prognostication done transparently, including the withdrawal-of-lifelong-antithrombotic implication - [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]; and the follow-through - the AF-and-falls reconciliation (appendage closure discussion for the faller, the anticoagulation-restart-versus-never decision rehearsed with the earlier data on warfarin-ICH and the newer DOAC-ICH natural history), the medication-record correction (was the dose ever too high for her renal function?), and the incident report that checks whether a head-injured-anticoagulated patient should ever wait two hours for a scan - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[renal/filtration]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**
:::

::: quiz
A 68-year-old on warfarin for atrial fibrillation, INR 2.4, arrives 2 hours into a right-hemisphere syndrome with NIHSS 14 and no haemorrhage on CT. The correct action regarding thrombolysis is:
- [ ] Give alteplase because her INR is below 3
- [x] Withhold IV thrombolysis because of anticoagulation, but assess immediately for thrombectomy
- [ ] Reverse warfarin then thrombolyse
- [ ] Give alteplase at half dose
::: solution
**Therapeutic anticoagulation with elevated INR is a contraindication to IV thrombolysis (the >1.7 threshold in most protocols), but it does not contraindicate mechanical thrombectomy, which is the point of CTA-first pathways for anticoagulated patients - occlusion means transfer, no occlusion means the early-anticoagulation-reversal-and-permissive-BP stroke-unit pathway ([[#def-acute]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]); half-dose thrombolysis is not a thing, and reversal-then-lysis wastes the clock; and the AF means her long-term plan is anticoagulation-timing decisions, not antiplatelets ([[#def-tia]], [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]).**

## Prevention, haemorrhage and recovery

::: definition Intracerebral haemorrhage management {#def-ich}
**The bleed patient: reversal first (warfarin with four-factor prothrombin complex concentrate plus vitamin K dosed by INR and weight with the thrombotic-risk acknowledgement; direct oral anticoagulant reversal with idarucizumab for dabigatran and andexanet or factor-concentrate strategies for the Xa inhibitors with their cost-and-availability and prothrombotic caveats; antiplatelet-associated bleeding where the platelet-transfusion trials were negative and desmopressin remains a centre-by-centre habit; and the coagulopathy-of-liver-disease and thrombocytopaenia versions with their product-strategy debates - [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]], [[blood/transfusion]], [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]); blood pressure lowering (the intensive-systolic-target trials showing haematoma-stasis benefit with the J-curve and renal and coronary cautions, delivered as titratable IV agents in monitored beds and the withdrawal-versus-permissive phase logic - [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]); the escalation decisions (GCS trajectory and clot size and location and age-and-premorbid-state and do-not-escalate-versus-decompressive-surgery evidence for superficial lobar bleeds with deterioration and the IVH-and-hydrocephalus external-drainage question, with the neurosurgical and palliative conversations held in parallel because both may be right answers for different patients); the secondary causes to exclude before labelling "hypertensive" (the lobar-and-anticoagulant-and-amyloid combinations, the vascular malformations and tumour bleeds and the sympathomimetic-drug-and-vasculitis-and-venous-thrombosis differentials with the CTA/MRV-and-MRI-with-swar-for-cavernomas-and-amyloid workup) - [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]], [[pathology/cancer-biology]]; the restart-dilemma counselling (anticoagulation for AF after ICH: the recurrence-fear-versus-thromboembolism-numbers, the timing and the appendage-closure alternative and the shared decision documented with the patient's values, because this is one of medicine's genuinely value-laden choices) - [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]; and the rehabilitation-and-outcome framing (haemorrhagic strokes are disproportionately fatal early but survivors can do well, and the same stroke-unit and rehabilitation principles apply with the added cognitive-and-fatigue burden of lobar and deep bleeds) - **[[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]] for the older-frailty selection questions and [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]] for the vascular cognitive load.**
:::

::: definition Secondary prevention and rehabilitation {#def-prevention}
**Antiplatelet-versus-anticoagulation logic after non-cardioembolic ischaemic stroke - aspirin 75-325 indefinitely (or clopidogrel or dipyridamole-aspirin as alternatives with their modest differences) and the short-course dual-therapy indications and the anticoagulation-only-for-AF-and-select-sources principle with the PFO-and-recurrent-events nuances; the blood-pressure lowering (thiazide-like and ACE-inhibitor-centred trial evidence, the permissive-acute-phase caveat and the long-term magnitude of recurrence reduction that dwarfs every antithrombotic comparison); the lipid strategy (high-intensity statin with the ICH-haemorrhage-trade-off acknowledged and the ezetimibe-and-PCSK9 add-ons for those not at target, and the haemorrhagic-stroke-patient where the answer differs by mechanism); and the lifestyle-and-comorbidity package (smoking with pharmacotherapy, weight and activity, diabetes with the newer agents' stroke-relevant trial evidence, alcohol reduction, sleep apnoea detection in the vascular phenotype, and the generalisation caveat when applying trial results to an 85-year-old) - [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]]; the rehabilitation core - early multidisciplinary mobilisation with the physiotherapy-and-OT-and-SLT-and-psychology-and-social-work-team and the goal-scaling instruments, the stroke-unit-to-discharge-to-community continuity and the neuro-rehabilitation-versus-nursing-home decisions that shape where a patient recovers, and the dose-response evidence that more therapy hours buy more function up to a point - [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]; and the sequelae clinic items - spasticity management (stretching-and-posture-and-oral-baclofen-and-tizanidine-and-toxin-for-focal-patterns-including-the-hidden-thumb-and-axillary-hygiene indications and the intrathecal-baclofen-and-selective-rhizotomy-for-severe-cases) - [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]] for the tone-and-contracture-adjacency and the shoulder-hand-and-subluxation-and-painful-arm syndromes; post-stroke fatigue and the mood disorders (depression affecting a third of survivors, screened and treated with SSRIs that also carry the old-caveats-and-the-real-evidence, and the pseudobulbar affect and apathy variants) - [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]]; epilepsy after stroke with its early-versus-late-seizure prognostic split and driving consequences - [[nervous-system/epilepsy]]; and the recurrence-prevention adherence problem, where the polypill idea, once-daily prescribing and pharmacist and nurse follow-up are the practical answers to a statistics problem about 30% discontinuation - [[pharmacology/patient-variation]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**
:::

::: exercise Run the aphasic consent {level=2}
A 55-year-old with global aphasia after a large left MCA stroke has a decision about early rehab intensity and a PEG for unsafe swallow; his wife says "he understands us".
::: solution
**Capacity is decision-specific and communication-impaired is not decision-impaired ([[#def-territories]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]): the assessment - verify comprehension with yes-no-and-pointing-and-constant-choice-error checks and the awareness that aphasia may coexist with intact understanding and with anosognosia that distorts it, and the SLT present to certify what was and was not understood - with the documentation of that process rather than a signature alone - [[clinical-skills/communication]]; the PEG conversation - the "unsafe swallow now is often safe in 2-6 weeks" trajectory and the NG-versus-PEG timing trial evidence (early PEG in predicted prolonged dysphagia), the honest trial lesson that feeding tubes do not prevent aspiration pneumonia in advanced dementia, and the benefits framing as medication-and-nutrition-and-rehabilitation-tolerance rather than "feeding" - [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]] - keep dementia-comparator to one clause: the advanced-dementia comparator; the substitute-decision layer if capacity is genuinely absent - the wife's role as closest relative-and-her-not-being-the-decision-maker-alone, the best-interests and prior-values exploration, and the second-opinion and advocacy routes when family and team diverge - [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]; and the rehabilitation intensity agreement - the goal scaling set with the patient using whatever communication channel works, the mood-and-apathy screen that predicts participation, and the family-carer training so that "therapy" happens after the therapy hours - [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]], [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]].**
:::

::: exercise Design the stroke service {level=3}
A 900,000-population region has no hyper-acute network. Design one: pathways, workforce, equity, evaluation.
::: solution
**Networks-beat-beds ([[#def-acute]], [[#def-prevention]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the model - hub-and-spoke hyper-acute units with direct-to-CT ambulance bypass and telemedicine-equipped spoke scanners (the drip-and-ship-versus-mothership-and-direct-to-angio-suite comparisons with their transfer-time arithmetic), and the stroke-unit-for-all-and-rehabilitation-tier-structure-with-community-neuro-rehab - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; the workforce and quality spine - the hyper-acute nursing ratios and the therapy-hours-per-patient standard and the on-call neurology-and-neuroradiology-and-neurosurgery rotas with their burnout-and-retention reality and the clinical-network governance that forces disclosure of outcome-by-unit - [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]; the equity layer - public recognition campaigns in the languages and media that reach the communities with the highest stroke burden, ambulance-call bias audits, the disability-and-older-patient eligibility questions that quietly exclude people from thrombectomy, and the rural-and-transport-cost analysis that predicts who will actually receive the network - [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]]; and the evaluation - door-to-needle and door-to-groin distributions not averages, thrombolysis-and-thrombectomy rates and complication rates, 90-day functional outcomes with mortality reported and risk-adjustment transparency, 12-month readmission-and-recurrence and rehabilitation access and PROM and carer-burden measures, and the audit-and-feedback loop with the ambulance service as a partner rather than a supplier - [[epidemiology-ebm/measuring-disease]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

::: exercise Sort the headache {level=1}
Decide "thunderclap workup now" versus "migraine clinic": (a) 32-year-old with 10-year history of unilateral throbbing headache with visual aura, now typical; (b) 58-year-old with sudden maximal headache during sex, neck pain, normal exam; (c) 29-year-old post-partum day 8 with thunderclap headache and two brief visual disturbances; (d) 44-year-old with new daily persistent headache since a car accident 6 weeks ago, normal exam.
::: solution
**Red-flag-driven imaging ([[#def-territories]], [[nervous-system/headache-pain]]): (b) is SAH until CT-and-if-needed-LP-or-CTA say otherwise - the sentinel-headache literature exists because this patient gets discharged and rebleeds; (c) demands urgent imaging for the post-partum differential - reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome, venous thrombosis, pituitary apoplexy and eclampsia - [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; (d) needs imaging once for the trauma-and-new-daily-persistent context and then a headache-medicine plan rather than repeat scans; and (a) is migraine with aura - no imaging, but the oestrogen-containing-contraception-and-aura discussion and the thrombolysis-eligibility-counselling for her future self - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**

::: exercise Manage the aspiration fever {level=2}
Day 4 post-stroke, 74-year-old right-hemisphere patient with NIHSS 16 becomes febrile and tachypnoeic; the urine is clean, the cannula site is fine; SpO2 93% on air.
::: solution
**Aspiration-pneumonia-until-excluded-in-a-dysphagic-post-stroke-gut ([[#def-acute]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): the confirmation - chest examination with the reduced-air-entry-and-coarse-crackles pattern and the chest radiograph (or bedside ultrasound where trained) and the sputum-and-blood-cultures-and-urine-antigens and the swallowing-status-audit since admission (was the diet graded? who fed her? was the head-up rule kept) and the early CT if no consolidation with persistent fever - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[respiratory/respiratory-infections]]; the treatment - aspiration-protocol antibiotics with severity scoring and oxygen and the speech-and-language re-review with nil-by-mouth-and-alternative-access decisions and the mouth-care protocol that has real trial evidence for preventing the next episode - [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]; and the fever hunt's other entries - the drug fever and the DVT-and-PE (she is on prophylaxis, isn't she?) and the UTI-from-the-bladder-training regime and the pressure-sores nobody is looking at because she cannot complain - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[renal/urinary-tract]], [[surgery/wounds-surgical-infection]]; and the escalation conversation - which deterioration triggers antibiotics-versus-transfer-versus-comfort-focus, held earlier with the family who were told "the next few days are the risky ones" - [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**

::: exercise Teach the polypill argument {level=2}
A 60-year-old two years post-minor-stroke admits he stopped "half the tablets because I feel fine"; he takes antihypertensive and statin inconsistently. Convince him with numbers.
::: solution
**Recurrence-risk-numbers-not-scolding ([[#def-prevention]], [[pharmacology/patient-variation]]): quantify - his approximate annual recurrence risk (5-10% after index events, higher early) and the relative-risk reductions blood-pressure-lowering (about a quarter fewer recurrences), statins and antiplatelets add, then convert to absolute numbers per 100 patient-years which is what lay brains can hold - [[epidemiology-ebm/measuring-disease]]; name the silent-treatment problem - BP and statins have no day-to-day felt benefit and the symptoms he does feel (muscle aches, ankle oedema) get attributed to the pills, so ask what he notices and reframe each side effect as a swap-not-stop conversation with a menu of solutions - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; simplify - once-daily combinations and the fixed-dose-rewrite conversation with the GP, pharmacist-led review, and the "fewer bottles, same protection" logic that the polypill trials support in adherence terms; and respect autonomy - the goal is an informed chooser, so state his own priorities (independence, travel, not becoming a patient again), agree the two medicines he will never stop and the one he will renegotiate with you, and book the follow-up that makes the plan real - [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**

## Where this leads

The vascular substrates in [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]] and [[cardiovascular/hypertension]]; the arrhythmia sources in [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]; anticoagulation pharmacology in [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]; neuroanatomical maps in [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]; rehabilitation and frailty interfaces in [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]] and [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]].

::: history
**Stroke medicine's history is the story of neurology losing an therapeutic-nihilism reputation it held for a century: the vascular localisation canon of the nineteenth century - Armand Trousseau's aphemia-and-artery descriptions, the Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysm theory that dominated thinking about hypertensive bleeds for 120 years before being revised down to size - [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]], [[pathology/haemodynamics]]; the carotid endarterectomy lineage beginning with the 1950s thromboendarterectomies for "cerebral ischaemia" done almost by accident and the NASCET and ECST trials of the late 1980s and early 1990s finally proving what surgeons had long claimed, one of the cleanest surgery-trial collaborations in history - [[surgery/vascular-urological-surgery]]; the thrombolytic battle: the European and Australian and US attempts and the NINDS 1995 result and the years of "the time window is too narrow, the system cannot do it" objections that were answered not with better drugs but with redesigned ambulances and CT suites, the great lesson that acute-medicine innovation is operations research as often as molecular biology - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; the thrombectomy renaissance of 2015 - five trials, one meta-analytic message, and stent-retriever technology vindicating the neuroradiologists who had been doing "futile" thrombectomies through the anti-endovascular wilderness decade - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]; the stroke-unit evidence from the 1950s-60s Scottish and Swedish wards through the SRR and subsequent Cochrane reviews - the unglamorous finding that organised nursing and therapy floors reduce death and dependency more than almost any drug in this chapter; and the atrial-fibrillation-anticoagulation arc - from the warfarin-trial era with its INR-tightening and intracranial-bleed fear through the DOAC non-inferiority programme and the reversal-agent generation, ending where the patient's own values decide the restart question - [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]].**
:::

::: summary
- Think vascular territory before vessel: aphasia and neglect and crossed syndromes localise faster than any scan and change triage.
- Time is quantified biology: last-known-well drives thrombolysis, perfusion imaging drives thrombectomy, and both metrics belong in the handover.
- Anticoagulation excludes lysis but never excludes thrombectomy; CTA-first pathways exist to answer that question.
- TIA is a diagnosis with a 48-hour deadline: same-day imaging and mechanism-matched prevention beat every other intervention in this chapter.
- ICH management is reversal, blood pressure, and the two parallel conversations - neurosurgical and palliative - with the restart-anticoagulation decision made with data and values.
- The stroke unit, the swallowing screen, the therapy hours and the polypill-level prevention adherence are the difference between surviving and recovering.
:::
