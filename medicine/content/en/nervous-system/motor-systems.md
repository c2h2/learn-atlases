The motor system is the one part of neurology you can examine to a certificate with a stethoscope bag and a couch: **weakness has a pattern, and the pattern names the level - muscle, neuromuscular junction, peripheral nerve, anterior horn cell, root or plexus, corticospinal tract, or cortex.** The upper motor neuron versus lower motor neuron distinction is the first great sorting exercise of clinical medicine, and the pyramidal-versus-extrapyramidal contrast moves you into the next chapter.

This chapter covers the anatomical levels and their signs, stroke as the commonest cause (handled separately), nerve injuries and palsies, myasthenia and the junction, and the motor neurone group.

## Levels of the motor system

::: definition Upper and lower motor neuron signs {#def-umnlmn}
**Lower motor neuron lesion (flaccid weakness with wasted muscles and fasciculations and reduced or absent reflexes and no Babinski - the anterior horn cell, root, plexus, nerve or muscle signature, with the segmental myotome map and the peripheral nerve territory map as the two grids you overlay on the history, and the electrodiagnostic confirmation - nerve conduction studies and EMG with their fibrillations and positive sharp waves and motor-unit morphology - in [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]] and [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]); upper motor neuron lesion (spastic weakness with increased tone in the velocity-dependent clasp-knife pattern and hyperreflexia and the extensor plantar and often Hoffmann and clonus, preserving bulk apart from disuse - the corticospinal signature, with the acute-phase spinal or cerebral shock that makes an acute cord or hemispheric lesion initially flaccid and areflexic before the tone returns, which is the single most misread examination finding in acute medicine); the discriminating details that trainees lose marks on - pyramidal distribution in the arm (extensors weaker than flexors) and leg (flexors weaker than extensors) versus myopathic proximal weakness versus the give-way weakness of pain or poor effort, the wrist-drop-versus-radial-versus-posterior-interosseous anatomy, the patterns of hand intrinsic wasting that separate ulnar from C8-T1 from anterior horn cell disease; and the two syndromes that sit above the motor neurone and change its signs - the extrapyramidal movement disorders in [[nervous-system/movement-disorders]] (rigidity without spasticity, normal reflexes, no Babinski) and the cerebellar world of incoordination-without-weakness and hypotonia and pendular reflexes and the intention tremor and dysdiadochokinesis that families call "weak" when strength testing is near-normal. Sensory co-localisation is the necessary companion: a purely motor deficit is a different list (motor neurone disease, multifocal motor neuropathy, myasthenia, myopathy, some nerve lesions) from a mixed sensorimotor one (polyneuropathy, plexus, root, cord, brain), and the tendon-reflex-and-babinski pair plus the sensory level in a stocking pattern are the four findings that decide where the lesion is. [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the sensory pathways; [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]] for the tract maps; [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]] for the manoeuvres.**
:::

::: definition Weakness by time course {#def-timecourse}
**Hyperacute weakness (seconds-to-minutes onset names vascular and traumatic mechanisms - the stroke-and-TIA arithmetic in [[nervous-system/stroke]], the traumatic cord and root and plexus injuries with their surgical timetables - [[surgery/orthopaedics]], the spinal haemorrhage and compression emergencies of epidural abscess, haematoma on anticoagulants and metastatic cord compression where steroids-and-imaging-and-neurosurgery-within-hours decide walking outcome and the bladder alongside the legs - the metastatic pages of [[pathology/cancer-biology]], the sepsis rows of [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]] and the clinical picture of [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]); acute-to-subacute (days-to-weeks - Guillain-Barré syndrome and its variants with ascending areflexic weakness and respiratory and autonomic monitoring and the albuminocytologic-discordance CSF and the IVIG-versus-plasma-exchange equivalence and the post-infectious molecular-mimicry mechanism, transverse myelitis, vasculitic neuropathy and mononeuritis multiplex, myositis, botulism with its descending pupil-involving paralysis, and the tick-and toxin lists - [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]], [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]], [[microbiology/bacterial-pathogenesis]]); the relapsing pattern (multiple sclerosis and its dissemination in time and space with the optic and sensory and cord presentations and the disease-modifying-treatment era - [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]); and the insidious patterns (months-to-years - the muscular dystrophies and metabolic myopathies with their CK and genetic and biopsy pathways - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[cell-biochemistry/proteins-enzymes]]; the compressive and degenerative spine myelopathies with cervical spondylotic change and the gordon Holmes hand, peripheral nerve tumours in the neurofibromatoses - [[genetics/cancer-genetics]]; motor neurone disease combining upper and lower signs without sensory loss; and Parkinsonism as a slowness masquerading as weakness - [[nervous-system/movement-disorders]]). Symmetry and gradient do much of the work: distal symmetric (length-dependent neuropathy), proximal symmetric (myopathy, myasthenia, motor neurone disease variants), asymmetric multifocal (mononeuritis multiplex, radiculoplexus neuropathy in diabetes, inclusion body myositis, multimotor neuropathy) and focal (structural). The exercises below turn this grid into referrals and prescriptions.**
:::

## Peripheral nerves, roots and the junction

::: definition Nerve injuries, entrapments and radiculopathies {#def-peripheral}
**Focal nerve lesions (median at the wrist in carpal tunnel with the nocturnal paraesthesia and thenar wasting and the pregnancy-and-hypothyroidism-and-diabetes-and-dialysis associations - [[endocrine/thyroid]]; ulnar at the elbow with the digits-four-and-five numbness and the Fromén and Wartenberg signs and the cubital-tunnel-vs-deeper-lesion logic; radial with the classic fracture-and-crutch-and-saturday-night palsy; common peroneal at the fibular neck after weight loss, labour and casts; the lateral femoral cutaneous nerve of meralgia paraesthetica and its obesity-and-tight-trouser history; and the entrapped-plus-generalised picture where a polyneuropathy unmasks entrapments - [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]]); root lesions (the dermatomal pain-and-numbness-and-myotome-and-reflex triads of the cervical and lumbosacral radiculopathies - L5 versus S1 versus L4 discrimination, the straight-leg raise and its crossed variant, the cauda equina red flags of saddle anaesthesia and urinary retention and bilateral sciatica demanding same-hour MRI and decompression with a documented bladder scan, and the paradox that most acute discs and most "sciatica" improve without surgery while the imaging finding must match the clinical level - [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]); plexus syndromes (the diabetic and post-infectious neuralgic amyotrophy of Parsonage-Taylor with its days of scapular agony followed by patchy profound weakness and the immunological mechanism, the radiation-induced and neoplastic infiltrations with Horner and hand oedema and pain, the obstetric brachial plexus injury with its long surgical rehabilitation, and the positioning palsies of anaesthesia and ITU that should never happen twice on the same ward - [[surgery/perioperative-care]], [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]); and polyneuropathy as a medical history written well - the length-dependent "stocking-glove" positive and negative symptoms with distal weakness and early ankle-reflex loss versus the demyelinating and axonal patterns on nerve conduction, and the aetiology shortlist of diabetes and alcohol and B12 and folate and thiamine (wet beriberi and refeeding) and renal failure and hypothyroidism and coeliac and the drugs (vincristine, platinum agents, nitrofurantoin, metronidazole, isoniazid with pyridoxine, the antiretrovirals and the amiodarone-era statin-myopathy confusables) and the immune and infective causes (CIDP with its treatable proximal-and-distal pattern, HIV, hepatitis C cryoglobulinaemia, leprosy where relevant - [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[blood/anaemia]], [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]], [[infectious-diseases/hiv]], [[infectious-diseases/tropical-infections]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]) and the urgent hunt when it is acute, asymmetric or accompanied by systemic features such as weight loss and fever - vasculitis, amyloid (with its autonomic and cardiac clues and the do-not-miss-treatable point), sarcoid, malignancy - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], the amyloid rows of [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]] and the paraneoplastic discussion in [[pathology/cancer-biology]].**
:::

::: definition Myasthenia and the junction {#def-nmj}
**Myasthenia gravis is fatigable weakness of ocular, bulbar, limb and respiratory muscles - ptosis and diplopia that drift and vary through the day and with sustained upgaze, speech that becomes nasal and dysarthric as a sentence proceeds, a chew that tires, neck flexor weakness, limb-girdle fatigability - with normal sensation, normal or brisk reflexes and normal tone, which is the diagnostic fingerprint. The immunology is the point: autoantibodies against the postsynaptic acetylcholine receptor in most generalised cases, MuSK in a bulbar-ocular-respiratory-predominant subset with its different antibody-mediated mechanism and poorer pyridostigmine response, LRP4 rarely, and the seronegative minority requiring single-fibre EMG and specialised assays. The thymus is the adjuvant - thymic hyperplasia in young-onset disease and thymoma in a minority whose discovery changes oncological management and whose paraneopastic associations (myasthenia, red-cell aplasia, hypogammaglobulinaemia) deserve their own screen - [[immunology/tolerance-regulation]], [[blood/anaemia]], [[immunology/immunodeficiency]]. Diagnosis - the pattern plus AChR antibody, the ice-pack test and the rest-and-fatigue examination at the bedside, single-fibre EMG's jitter, and the repetitive-nerve-stimulation decrement - and the pharmacological proof of pyridostigmine response with its honest limitations. Treatment: pyridostigmine for symptoms, corticosteroids and steroid-sparing agents for the immune disease, thymectomy by trial evidence in AChR-positive generalised disease with its years-to-benefit lag, plasma exchange and immunoglobulins for crises and preoperative optimisation, and the newer FcRN inhibitors and complement blockade in refractory disease with their infection and vaccine caveats - [[immunology/vaccines-immunotherapy]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]]. Myasthenic crisis (bulbar failure and aspiration risk and a falling vital capacity tracked serially on the ward - the 20-15 rule for ventilation consideration and the "never trust a single FVC in a fluctuating disease" rule) versus cholinergic crisis in the overtreated era that history now tells; and the drugs that unmask or worsen myasthenia (beta-blockers, aminoglycosides, macrolides, magnesium, quinolones, the neuromuscular blockers of anaesthesia with their dosing catastrophe potential and the postoperative-ventilation-plan duty - [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]). The junction's other disorders: Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome with its proximal-leg weakness and autonomic dryness and reflex-potentiation after exercise and the small-cell lung cancer search that the antibody panel sharpens - [[pathology/cancer-biology]], [[respiratory/lung-cancer-pleura]]; the congenital myasthenic syndromes with their genetics and drug-responsiveness that differs from autoimmune disease - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]; and botulism and organophosphate poisoning covered in [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]] and [[microbiology/bacterial-pathogenesis]].**
:::

::: example The double vision that tires by dinner {#ex-mg}
A 34-year-old woman has three weeks of vertical diplopia worse in the evening and ptosis "when I'm tired at the supermarket", a nasal voice by the end of the history, normal pupils, normal reflexes, fatigable neck flexion and a positive ice-pack test.
::: solution
**Ocular-generalised myasthenia - confirm, then scan the chest before the next step ([[#def-nmj]]): the immediate workup - AChR-binding antibody with modulating and blocking antibodies and MuSK if negative and single-fibre EMG or repetitive stimulation and the MRI/thoracic-window-CT of the mediastinum for thymoma with the breast-and-thyroid-and-automimmune co-screen - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]], [[immunology/autoimmunity]], [[endocrine/thyroid]]; the safety screen at presentation - serial vital capacity and single-breath count, bulbar and neck assessment, and the admission threshold for a change in speech, swallow or breathlessness that converts a clinic patient into a ward patient, with the do-not-discharge-a-fluctuating-nasal-voice rule - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; the treatment sequence - pyridostigmine with honest expectations about the ceiling of benefit, immunotherapy discussion (steroid-start caution that weakness may transiently worsen, steroid-sparing plan, thymectomy referral if AChR-positive generalised disease and the timing conversation), and crisis-prevention before elective surgery or infection with the multidisciplinary pre-op letter - [[surgery/perioperative-care]], [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; and the counseling that reduces panic and A&E attendance - the day-to-day variability explained as the disease rather than doubt, the sick-day-and-infection-and-pregnancy planning in a young woman with a family plan (the neonatal-transient-myasthenia and drug-choice details with obstetric and neonatal colleagues), and the driving and occupation advice written and dated - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[paediatrics/newborn]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The ascending numbness after a cold {#ex-gbs}
A 41-year-old has 5 days of progressive symmetrical leg weakness now reaching the hips, distal tingling in hands and feet, double vision, a hoarse voice, HR 118, BP swinging 90-170; 2 weeks earlier she had diarrhoea; reflexes are absent and plantars are mute.
::: solution
**Guillain-Barré syndrome with autonomic and bulbar involvement - this patient is admitted, monitored and scored, not investigated slowly ([[#def-timecourse]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]): the airway-breathing-circulation frame - serial vital capacity (lying and sitting) and the fall-to-15-20-mL/kg or 1-L rule and the bulbar and aspiration assessment and the early-anaesthetist-conversation-about-elective-intubation-versus-a-crash, with the autonomic labile-hypertension-and-bradyarrhythmia monitoring and the caution against beta-blockade for the tachycardia - [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]], [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]; the supporting tests - CSF with albuminocytologic discordance that may be normal in week one and the NCS/EMG pattern of the AIDP-axonal-GBS variants, plus the antecedent Campylobacter and CMV and Zika and immunisation-attribution context with the honest vaccine-risk-versus-benefit numbers used in public conversations - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]], [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]], [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]]; the treatment decision - IVIG over five days or plasma exchange with the no-steroids-alone finding and the timing argument that earlier treatment shortens the ventilator phase, plus the supportive package - neuropathic pain, DVT prophylaxis once immobile, pressure care, bowel and bladder, and the ICU-ward liaison - [[immunology/adaptive-responses]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[renal/urinary-tract]]; and the long arc - the ICU-acquired weakness and shoulder-pain-and-hand-swelling and contracture prevention, the rehabilitation goal-setting with the walking-and-hands-and-work timeline families ask for and the 1-in-5 residual-disability reality stated honestly, the relapse-versus-CIDP-conversion diagnosis at 8-plus weeks, and the pain-and-fatigue management that outlives the weakness - [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]].**
:::

::: example The foot that slaps {#ex-footdrop}
A 58-year-old woman with 2 months of right foot drop and 15 kg of weight loss has painful patchy numbness in the right fourth finger and left sole; ESR 62, no diabetes, urine with protein and microscopic haematuria.
::: solution
**Mononeuritis multiplex with systemic inflammation is a vasculitis until proven otherwise ([[#def-peripheral]]): the localisation - two named nerve territories (deep peroneal, tibial/plantar, digital) in an asymmetric pattern separating this from length-dependent neuropathy and from a single root, with the electrophysiology confirming an axonal multifocal process and excluding the silent second nerve - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]], [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]]; the urgent aetiology - ANCA-associated vasculitis and polyarteritis nodosa and cryoglobulinaemic and connective-tissue and infective and malignant associations with the renal and pulmonary and ENT and skin and nerve-conduction-and-nerve-or-sural-biopsy and the CT chest-abdomen-pelvis and the do-not-wait-for-tissue-to-start-treating-in-a-rapidly-deteriorating-nerve rule - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], [[renal/urinary-tract]], [[pathology/diagnostic-pathology]], [[respiratory/respiratory-infections]]; the treatment race - immunosuppression initiated with rheumatology and neurology jointly (steroid induction plus the steroid-sparing choice by severity and organ set, plasma exchange and rituximab and cyclophosphamide contexts), the nerve-recovery-window teaching that hours-to-days of delay convert reversible conduction block into permanent axonal loss, and the infection-and-vaccination-before-rituximab discipline - [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]], [[microbiology/antimicrobials]]; and the functional and safety half - the ankle-foot orthosis and physiotherapy and falls assessment and the skin-check of the insensate foot and the driving notification for a foot you cannot lift - [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[geriatrics-palliative/falls]].**
:::

::: example The clumsy hands and the wet feet {#ex-cervical-myelopathy}
A 67-year-old has 8 months of progressive finger clumsiness (keys, buttons), unsteady feet, urgency and mild neck pain; examination shows wasted small hand muscles, slow finger taps, hyperreflexia in legs, bilateral extensor plantars and a vague C5-T1 sensory reduction.
::: solution
**Cervical myelopathy with segmental hand changes - the combination that names the cord ([[#def-umnlmn]], [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]]): the reading - atrophy and areflexia in the hands at the level of the compression (anterior horn and root involvement) with spasticity and Babinski below (corticospinal) plus bladder urgency is the classic mixed picture that fools clinicians into "peripheral" diagnoses, and the Lhermitte and numb-hands variants deserve the same reflexes asked about - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]], [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]]; the workup - MRI cervical cord with the cord-signal change question and the differentials of B12 and folate and thyroid and syphilis and demyelination and motor neurone disease (the pure-motor mimic) with bloods and, when doubt persists, neurophysiology - [[blood/anaemia]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]], [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]; the surgical decision - operable myelopathy graded on function-and-trajectory rather than image drama, with the moderate-severe-or-progressive cases going to decompression (anterior-versus-posterior and levels and the C5-palsy-and-reperfusion-injury counsels) and the mild-stable cases to surveillance with documented red flags, and the timing honesty that established bladder and hand-intrinsic loss recovers poorly while gait improves first - [[surgery/orthopaedics]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]; and the non-surgical care - falls and hand therapy and driving and the "do not manipulate a myelopathic neck" rule for chiros and physios stated in the letter, plus the adjacent-segment-and-ligamentous-ossification context for the rheumatology-adjacent patient - [[musculoskeletal/bones-joints]].**
:::

- [x] Fatigability with normal reflexes and normal sensation that improves after rest

## Where this leads

Stroke in all its vascular forms in [[nervous-system/stroke]]; demyelination and inflammatory neuropathies in [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; the spinal and surgical compression emergencies in [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]] and [[surgery/orthopaedics]]; the movement disorders built on basal ganglia circuits in [[nervous-system/movement-disorders]]; neuromuscular genetics in [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]].

::: history
**Motor localisation is one of physiology's great detective stories: Bell and Magendie's 1811-1822 separation of anterior (motor) and posterior (sensory) roots performed on animals and argued in the face of contemporaries who thought one fibre did both, and the clinical root-lesion syndromes that followed; Brown-Séquard's half-cord physiology in French soldiers and the eponym that survives the caveats; the Charcot school - Duchenne's functional neurology and the arm-chair diagnosis of a century's worth of stroke and cord disease, Charcot's own teaching of ALS with his patient at the couch and the "great mimicker" role of hysteria he also dissected ; and Marie and Foix's tract anatomy - [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]; the peripheral-nerve era - the carpal-tunnel-descriptions-of-the-WW1-era-and-the-surgical-release-lesson-and-the-phalen-and-tinel-eponyms-and-the-nerve-conduction-station-that-made-entrapment-measurable - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]; myasthenia's journey from a fatal "bulbar palsy" of young women (the Lewis, Erb and Jolly descriptions; the thymectomy discoveries from wartime wound surgery; Simpson's 1960s antibody hypothesis and Patrick and Lindstrom's receptor immunisation model) to the modern antibody-defined and complement-targeted disease, one of the best-documented triumphs of bench-to-bedside immunology - [[immunology/autoimmunity]], [[immunology/adaptive-responses]]; the Guillain-Barré-Banneix-Strohl triangle described in 1859-1860s and re-drawn after the 1976 swine-flu surveillance attention and the Campylobacter-molecular-mimicry work that converted a post-infectious mystery into a specific anti-ganglioside mechanism with a diagnostic blood test and an animal model; and the electrodiagnostic revolution - nerve-conduction studies and EMG and single-fibre jitter turning the clinical map into a physiological one, and now the ultrasound era imaging nerves and roots directly.**
:::

A 61-year-old on atorvastatin 80 mg after PCI reports 6 weeks of symmetrical thigh heaviness climbing stairs; CK 38, no weakness on testing; she wants to stop everything.

::: summary
- Sort weakness by level first (UMN versus LMN versus junction versus muscle versus cerebellar-extrapyramidal), then by time course, then by symmetry and gradient.
- An acutely flaccid areflexic limb can still be an acute UMN lesion in spinal or cerebral shock; re-examine.
- Painful patchy multifocal deficits with systemic features are vasculitis until excluded; nerve recovery is time-critical.
- Fatigable ocular-bulbar weakness with normal reflexes is myasthenia until the antibodies say otherwise; check the mediastinum and the vital capacity.
- Ascending areflexia is an airway, autonomic and ventilation problem before it is an immunology problem; treat early.
- Red-flag motor syndromes are emergencies by clock: cord compression, cauda equina, myasthenic crisis, GBS with bulbar or respiratory signs, botulism.
:::

A 66-year-old with 18 months of numb feet, erectile dysfunction, diarrhoea, carpal tunnels bilaterally and a first-degree AV block: build the differential and the two tests you want today.
::: solution
**Autonomic-plus-neuropathy-plus-cardiac-plus-bilateral-CTS-is-amyloid-amyloid-amyloid ([[#def-peripheral]]): the pattern argument - length-dependent axonal sensory and autonomic neuropathy with bilateral entrapments and conduction disease and unexplained weight loss or cardiomyopathy or macroglossia or periorbital purpura, versus the diabetes, B12 and myeloma-related alternatives - [[renal/urinary-tract]], [[blood/anaemia]], [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]]; the two tests now - serum and urine immunofixation with free light chains and the fat-pad or minor-salivary-gland biopsy, plus the ECG and echocardiogram looking for the thick-walled low-voltage discordance (and the cardiac MRI or scintigraphy for the transthyretin question in the older patient) - [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]; the typing that decides treatment - ATTR versus AL with the haematology and cardiologist co-management and the AL-as-oncological-emergency logic (rapidly effective clone-directed therapy) and the ATTR-modifier and silencer era and the transplant-centre conversations - [[pathology/cancer-biology]], [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]; and the honest counselling - why "we are looking for a protein" is not a diagnosis and what a negative result would mean, plus the neuropathic-pain and orthostatic-hypotension management with the falls and driving implications - [[geriatrics-palliative/falls]], [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]].**

::: exercise Handle a myasthenic admission {level=2}
A known myasthenic on azathioprine is admitted with pneumonia, increasing nasal speech and a vital capacity falling from 2.0 to 1.2 L over 6 hours. Manage the next two hours.
::: solution
**Escalate-with-the-team-now-not-the-ward-round-later ([[#def-nmj]], [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]]): the respiratory line - repeated FVC and single-breath-count and gas and pH with the recognition that a normal ABG is late and reassuring in this disease, and the senior decision on elective intubation in a monitored bed versus non-invasive ventilation as a bridge with the "NIV does not protect a bulbar airway" caveat and the anaesthetic-difficulty awareness of the myasthenic's weak muscles and exaggerated blocker sensitivity - [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]; the treatment package - urgent plasma exchange or immunoglobulin per the crisis protocol and the infection treated with a myasthenia-safe antibiotic (avoiding aminoglycosides and fluoroquinolones and the macrolide caution) and the aspiratory-care and NG or swallow decision and the DVT and pressure care - [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]; the drug-chart reconciliation - pyridostigmine timing and the tube-related dose absorption problem, the steroid dose stress adjustment, the azathioprine infection decision with the haematology-and-infection-team view, and the medication-review of the beta-blocker that someone started for the rate - [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]; and the communication - explaining to the patient, who knows this disease better than the rota, what is happening and why, and documenting their previously stated ceiling-of-care and ventilation preferences in a crisis where they may lose the ability to speak - [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]].**

::: exercise Run the MND clinic {level=3}
Design a multidisciplinary motor neurone disease service from diagnosis to end of life: assessments, interventions, evidence and ethics.
::: solution
**Service-design-for-a-terminal-neurological-disease ([[#def-timecourse]], [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]]): the diagnosis pathway - neurologist-with-specialist-interest disclosure protocol, the EMG and imaging that exclude the treatable mimics (multifocal motor neuropathy with conduction block, cervical spondylotic amyotrophy, myasthenia, Kennedy disease and the structural lesions), the baseline respiratory, nutritional, speech and swallowing measures, and the second-opinion and time-to-diagnosis honesty - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]]; the disease-modifying layer - riluzole's modest survival evidence and the sodium-benzoate-and-tirasemton-era-caveats read as uncertain, the genetic-counselling pathway for familial cases and the C9orf72 and SOD1 and TARDBP era with the presymptomatic-testing ethics - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[genetics/genetic-testing]], [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]]; the interventions that change outcomes - non-invasive ventilation for the symptomatic respiratory failure and the PEG decision timed on weight-and-FVC-trajectories rather than crisis, the multidisciplinary clinic with named respiratory, dietetic, SLT, physio, OT and specialist nursing input and the equipment-and-adaptations pipeline (wheelchair, communication aids including eye-tracking before speech is lost, environmental controls) - [[respiratory/respiratory-failure]], [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]; and the palliative and ethical spine - anticipatory prescribing for secretions, cramps, dyspnoea and anxiety, the advanced-care-planning conversation repeated at each milestone about intubation and BiPAP ceilings and tube feeding and place of death and the VSED-and-assisted-dying debates stated neutrally per jurisdiction, the family-carer support and bereavement pathway, and the clinical-trial-and-research-participation offer as an act of hope management rather than false promise - [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]], [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]], [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]].**
