The neuromuscular chapter is the distal half of the motor system: **root, plexus, nerve, junction and muscle each fail with a characteristic pattern of weakness, reflex change, sensory involvement and time course, and the electrodiagnostic and antibody and genetic tests have turned most of the old "progressive muscular atrophy" wards into named diseases with named treatments - some curable, some modifiable, all worth diagnosing accurately.**

This chapter covers the neuropathies beyond the stroke-and-diabetes basics, the myasthenic and congenital junction disorders' muscular siblings, myopathies and dystrophies and channels, motor neurone disease's lower-motor-neuron face (cross-referenced from [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]), and the neuromuscular emergencies.

::: history
**Neuromuscular medicine is where clinical description met the microscope met the gene, in three visible waves: the nineteenth-century nosology - Duchenne's 1868 memorandum on chronic muscular atrophy with his brothers' photography and the family-based inheritance arithmetic he drew before Mendel was translated, Erb and Kussmaul's myopathies and the progressive muscular atrophy that Gowers catalogued with his famous hand-drawn plates - [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]; the nerve-muscle physiology wave - the Sherrington reflex-arc synthesis and the Katz and Marden and Harvey-and-Masland neuromuscular-translation studies that produced the myasthenic decrement and the tetanic facilitation with which we still certify junction physiology, and the the Bernstein-Hodgkin-Katz membrane physics that made the channel diseases thinkable - [[physiology/membrane-potentials]], [[physiology/synapses]]; the enzymology and ultrastructure wave - the McArdle's 1951 career-ending cramps case (the runner-patient who became the disease eponym) and the phosphorylase assay and the biopsy-era taxonomy of nemaline and central-core and ring-fibre diseases that made histology a diagnostic organ, and the Engel-and-Rowland myoglobinuria and metabolic-muscle work that linked sport, fasting and rhabdomyolysis to defined enzymes - [[cell-biochemistry/energy-metabolism]]; the molecular wave - the 1985-87 dystrophin cloning from the Becker-Duchenne translocations as one of the first reverse-genetics triumphs of the genome era (the sequence-first-then-protein inversion of the sickle-cell logic) and the anticorresponding-gene therapy pipeline from mdx-mice micro-dystrophins to exon-skipping antisense (the eteplirsen-era regulatory argument that still echoes between patients and agencies) and the CMT1A duplication and the myotonic-repeat discoveries and now the SMA Nusinersen-Spinal-gene-therapy and the DMD CRISPR trials that moved this chapter from eponyms to prescriptions; and the treatment-culture lesson - the IVIG-versus-plasma-exchange-equivalence trials for Guillain-Barré and the corticosteroid-failure and the Cochrane-era evidence culture (and the patient-led charity funding of basic science in the UK and US that built the field's infrastructure decades before the state and industry arrived) - [[public-health/health-systems-economics]], [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]].**
:::

::: summary
- Polyneuropathy is a history exercise: length-dependent and symmetric is medical; asymmetric, multifocal, motor-only, sensory-neuronopathic or rapid is a hunt for the treatable and dangerous.
- Proximal painless weakness with raised CK is a myopathy; check the endocrine, the drugs, then the antibodies, then the genes - and always screen for the malignancy and ILD that accompany some of them.
- Myotonia, ophthalmoparesis, recurrent rhabdomyolysis and exercise intolerance each name a channel, metabolic or mitochondrial disease with specific drug and anaesthetic dangers - record them where the on-call team will see them.
- Neuromuscular respiratory failure is silent on pulse oximetry: serial FVCs, morning symptoms and CO2, not saturations, decide the ventilation conversation.
- Anaesthesia is the neuromuscular patient's recurring operation: the myasthenic, myotonic, dystrophinopathic, RYR1 and mitochondrial patients each need written, individual, pre-visit plans.
- Multifocal motor neuropathy, CIDP, myasthenia, inflammatory myopathy and the metabolic myopathies are treatable; missing them converts manageable disease into permanent disability or death.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Split the weak patient {level=1}
Give the likely level and next test: (a) 40-year-old with distal wasting, cavus feet and a slow-growing beard of family history; (b) 60-year-old with 6 weeks of dysphagia and diplopia and fatigable proximal arms; (c) 30-year-old with 3 months of calf cramps, myalgia and CK 4000 after starting a statin, no weakness; (d) 55-year-old with wrist-drop, foot-drop and mononeuritic sensory loss with asthma and eosinophilia.
::: solution
**Levels-to-tests ([[#def-neuropathy]], [[#def-myopathy]], [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]): (a) hereditary motor-sensory neuropathy - examine parents and nerve conduction velocities then genetics - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]; (b) neuromuscular junction - AChR-and-MuSK antibodies, ice-pack and single-fibre EMG and chest imaging and vital capacity; (c) statin-associated myotoxicity versus immune-mediated necrotising myopathy if it persists after stopping (anti-HMGCR) - the stop-and-recheck discipline and the CK-trajectory - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; (d) EGPA mononeuritis multiplex (asthma-eosinophilia-neuropathy) - ANCA and urgent immunosuppression and the ENT-and-lung-and-cardiac assessment - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], [[respiratory/asthma-copd]].**

::: exercise Write the steroid myositis plan {level=1}
A 62-year-old with new polymyositis is started on prednisolone 60 mg. List everything that must accompany the prescription.
::: solution
**Co-prescription-medicine ([[#def-myopathy]]): the bone-and-glucose-and-eye-and-infection package - calcium-and-vitamin-D-and-a-bisphosphonate-by-risk-or-age-with-dental-check-before-bisphosphonate and the glucose monitoring with diabetes-team input and the annual-eye-review-for-cataracts and the PJP-prophylaxis threshold at 20 mg for 3 weeks with co-trimoxazole or dapsone or atovaquone options and the vaccination update before deeper immunosuppression - [[endocrine/bone-calcium]], [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]], [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]]; the taper-and-monitor plan - the creatinine-kinase-and-manual-muscle-testing-and-CDAI-style-tracking and the steroid-taper-ladder with relapse-recognition teaching and the steroid-card-and-adrenal-insufficiency-education and the intercurrent-illness-stress-dosing and the never-stop-abruptly rule - [[endocrine/adrenal]], [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; the steroid-myopathy trap - the week-3-4 patient who gets weaker as CK falls: steroid myopathy versus relapse decided by CK-and-MRI-and-EMG-and-exercise-testing rather than by panic, because adding steroids to steroid myopathy doubles the weakness; and the myositis-specific additions - the malignancy screen appropriate to the antibody subtype, the ILD screen (HRCT-and-PFTs and the antisynthetase-and-MDA5 urgency with the rheumatology-and-respiratory shared care) and the swallowing-and-aspiration and the hand-and-grip rehabilitation - [[pathology/cancer-diagnosis]], [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]], [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]].**

::: exercise Prepare the myasthenic for surgery {level=2}

::: solution
