Headache is the commonest neurological complaint and the specialty's most under-rated skill: **the diagnosis is made by history alone in almost every case, the red flags are a short list worth memorising, and the treatment fails more often from overuse of acute drugs and underuse of prevention than from any mystery disease.** Facial pain adds the trigeminal syndromes and the dental and sinus industries.

This chapter covers primary headache disorders, the secondary headaches and red flags, trigeminal and other facial pains, and the medication-overuse trap, with analgesia principles woven through.

## Primary headaches

::: definition Migraine {#def-migraine}
**Migraine is a recurrent 4-72-hour unilateral-or-bilateral pulsating headache with nausea and photophobia-and-phonophobia and functional impairment, worsened by movement, driven by trigeminovascular and hypothalamic-brainstem mechanisms in a genetically susceptible brain (the calcitonin gene-related peptide story that produced the gepants and the antibody programme is one of modern therapeutics' cleaner bench-to-bedside arcs) - [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]], [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]]; the phases that patients rarely volunteer - prodrome (yawning, mood and food changes a day ahead), aura (10-60 minutes of spreading visual scintillations and tunnel and zigzag phenomena, sensory march and dysphasia, sometimes without headache - the late-onset "aural without headache" that frightens everyone including the patient's own GP), headache, and postdrome with its cognitive hangover and neck stiffness that misleads examiners into meningitis searches - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the visual cortex mechanics; the variants that need naming rather than imaging - vestibular migraine (the overlap with the vertigo clinic - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]), hemiplegic migraine (familial channelopathy forms with motor aura and the do-not-thrombolyse certainty trap and the CACNA1A-ATP1A2-SCN1A genetics - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[nervous-system/stroke]] for the mimicry), basilar-type with its bilateral brainstem aura symptoms, retinal migraine as the monocular-visual-loss differential, abdominal and cyclic vomiting in children with the later headache conversion, and the established-without-aura-but-real persistent aura without infarction; the diagnostic criteria applied with judgement (the International Classification of Headache Disorders' own counting of attacks and the recognition that most "atypical" migraines are typical migraines in unpractised histories) and the red flags that must never be blamed on migraine: first or worst or changed headache, new aura after 40 or with stroke features, positional and exertional and cough and Valsalva triggers, thunderclap onset, headache with fever-or-neck-stiffness-or-immunocompromise, headache with papilloedema or progressive neurological deficit, pregnancy and post-partum and malignancy and anticoagulation contexts, age-over-50 with temporal tenderness and jaw claudication (giant cell arteritis - steroids before biopsy, visual loss preventable only by speed) - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; acute treatment with a stratified ladder - simple analgesia taken early and in adequate doses (the failed-ANT trial lesson that most patients are under-treated rather than over-treated), triptans and the newer ditans and gepants with their contraindication lists (triptan vascular cautions and the medication-overuse ceiling of 10 days per month for triptans and combination analgesics and 15 for simple analgesia - [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]] for the coronary caution and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]] for the pregnancy-safe options), antiemetics with their dystonia and QT caveats - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]; preventive treatment offered at 4-plus monthly or disabling days - beta-blockers and topiramate and valproate (with the pregnancy-capacity restrictions that now dominate this conversation) and amitriptyline and candesartan as the cheap-and-effective classics, the CGRP antibodies and oral gepants as the newer targeted class with their honest cost-effectiveness debate, and the non-drug layer with the strongest evidence per pound - sleep regularity, exercise, the headache diary that distinguishes true patterns from recall bias, and the psychological therapies for the pain-catastrophising cycle - [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]] for the analgesia ladder logic; and the hormonal interface - menstrual migraine's oestrogen-withdrawal mechanics and the contraception choices (the aura-oestrogen-stroke contraindication with its absolute-vs-relative risk numbers discussed properly - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]]), the pregnancy improvement in most and the post-partum worsening and eclampsia's headache trap - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]].**
:::

::: definition Tension-type, cluster and other primaries {#def-other}
**Tension-type headache - bilateral pressing non-pulsating mild-moderate pain without nausea (photo-or-phonophobia allowed), episodic versus the chronic form where the diagnostic line versus new daily persistent headache and medication-overuse blurs, the pericranial muscle tenderness that is a sign not a cause, the postural-and-work-load and stress contributors, and the treatment paradox that simple analgesia helps the episodic form while causing the chronic one, leaving amitriptyline prophylaxis (with its dose-sleep-and-dry-mouth bargaining) and the exercise-sleep-and-psychological package as the honest tools and the muscle-relaxant-and-manipulation industries their marketing - [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]] for the neck-headache interface and the upper-cervical referral patterns; cluster headache as neurology's most painful and most misdiagnosed syndrome - strictly unilateral orbital and temporal pain 15-180 minutes with ipsilateral autonomic features (lacrimation, injection, ptosis, miosis, rhinorrhoea, agitation - the patient who cannot lie down distinguishes it from migraine), circadian and circannual periodicity with its hypothalamic clock signature and the alcohol trigger during bouts, the male skew now narrowing, the suicide-risk honesty about pain severity - [[psychiatry/self-harm-suicide-prevention]] for the risk framing, [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the autonomic anatomy including the Horner's; its acute treatment - high-flow oxygen at 12-15 L through a non-rebreather mask for 15-20 minutes (under-prescribed, safe, cheap) and subcutaneous or intranasal sumatriptan with the do-not-give-both-together-within-hours caution and the nasal irritation trade-off - and its prevention - verapamil with the ECG-and-constipation-and-anti-arrhythmic-interaction monitoring culture and the steroid-and-greater-occipital-nerve-block bridging and lithium as third-line with its monitoring choreography and the neuromodulation frontier of vagal stimulation and sphenopalatine-ganglion interventions and the controversial hypothalamic targets; the trigeminal-autonomic-neuralgia family sharing cluster's features - paroxysmal hemicrania's absolute indomethacin response as a diagnostic test, the short-lasting unilateral neuralgiform headache attacks (SUNCT and SUNA) with their autonomic flooding and treatment difficulty, and the hemicrania continua that daily-responds-to-indomethacin - [[nervous-system/motor-systems]] for the brainstem reflex anatomy; and the primaries the public names - exertional and headache-with-cough (imaged because a minority hide Chiari and posterior-fossa lesions - see the posterior-fossa rows of [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]) and the sex-and-weight-and-positional combinations that point to idiopathic intracranial hypertension with its papilloedema and pulsatile tinnitus and the acetazolamide-and-weight-loss-and-shunting-and-stenting era - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the visual-loss emergency logic and [[endocrine/reproductive-endocrinology]] for the PCOS-and-weight adjacency and [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]] for the carbon-monoxide-flue-check headache presentation that is asked about too rarely.**
:::

## Secondary headache and facial pain

::: definition Red flags and the dangerous few {#def-redflags}
**The red-flag list that justifies its own imaging economy (the SNNOOP10-style teaching made practical - systemic symptoms and fever and malignancy and immunocompromise and neuro deficits and papilloedema and sudden-onset and older-age-onset and positional and precipitated-by-cough-exertion-sex and pattern change and eye pain with haloes and jaw claudication and scalp tenderness and neck stiffness, each with its named disease behind it: subarachnoid haemorrhage (thunderclap, CT-then-LP-or-CTA era with the sentinel-headache literature - [[nervous-system/stroke]]), cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (post-partum and prothrombotic and ear-infection contexts, the headache-with-papilloedema-and-seizures-and-focal-deficits combination, MRV diagnosis and anticoagulation even with haemorrhagic infarction - [[nervous-system/stroke]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]), giant cell arteritis (the over-50 urgent-temporal-and-occipital-headache-with-scalp-jaw-and-visual-symptoms-and-raised-ESR-and-CRP where same-day steroids save sight and the ultrasound-and-biopsy pathway follows treatment not precedes it - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]]), acute angle closure and other ophthalmic causes (the haloes and the painful red eye triage in [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]), spontaneous intracranial hypotension (the postural headache with neck pain after dural puncture or spontaneously, the pachymeningitis-on-contrast-MRI signature and the blood-patch pathway - post-dural-puncture headache prevention and treatment in [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]), meningitis and encephalitis (the hours-matter aciclovir-and-antibiotics rule - [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]], [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis])), the hypertensive and pre-eclamptic and eclamptic headaches with their visual phenomena and magnesium-and-antihypertensive pathways - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], the carbon-monoxide-checklist (heads in a flue-services household and a warm room), the traumatic and post-concussion headache with its vestibular-and-cognitive-and-sleep constellation and the return-to-activity-not-rest consensus - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]] - post-concussion belongs to sport too; keep with [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]]; and the tumour headache myth-corrected - headache alone rarely signals a tumour, and the red flags are the ones above, which is exactly the reassurance patients need when the internet says otherwise - [[pathology/cancer-biology]]. The imaging decisions worth memorising: thunderclap gets same-day CT-or-LP protocols; new persistent headache with any red flag gets imaging; and migraine with unchanged typical aura does not need serial scans, the overuse of which is itself a health- harm with incidentaloma anxiety attached - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**
:::

::: definition Trigeminal neuralgia and facial pain {#def-facial}
**Trigeminal neuralgia - paroxysms of seconds-long electric-shock pain in one or more trigeminal divisions triggered by light touch, chewing, talking, brushing and wind, with pain-free intervals and normal examination in the classic form; the pathophysiology of neurovascular compression (usually superior cerebellar artery on the root entry zone) with demyelination and ephaptic cross-talk, the MRI-with-protocol-sequences question and the multiple-sclerosis-and-lesional secondary causes in the young and bilateral patient - [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; the treatment ladder - carbamazepine and oxcarbazepine as first-line with the hyponatraemia-and-inducer-and-HLA-typing cautions and the slow-reintroduction-after-pain-free-period strategy - [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]], [[nervous-system/epilepsy]] for the shared sodium-channel logic, and the add-ons (lamotrigine, baclofen, the old-physostigmine-era folklore abandoned) and the procedural options with their durability-and-numbness-and-recurrence trade-offs - glycerol rhizotomy and balloon compression and radiofrequency thermocoagulation and peripheral branches and gamma-knife radiosurgery and the microvascular decompression that offers the longest pain-free interval with its craniotomy and rare serious risks quantified honestly; the atypical and secondary facial pains that get mislabelled neuralgia - persistent idiopathic facial pain (the constant-dull non-dermatomal pain that does not respond to carbamazepine and needs antidepressant-and-psychological management and a dentist-sparing history - [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]]), post-herpetic trigeminal neuralgia with its zoster-history and allodynia and the vaccine-eligibility conversation - [[microbiology/viruses]], [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]], [[immunology/vaccines-immunotherapy]], glossopharyngeal neuralgia with its throat-and-ear trigger zones and syncope attacks and the same-surgery-and-radiosurgery considerations, the short-lasting unilateral neuralgiform conditions in [[#def-other]], and the sinus-and-dental-and-TMJ differentials - maxillary sinusitis with its facial-lead-and-ostial-pathology and the airplane-triggered barotrauma, dental and odontogenic pain referred across the trigeminal map, and temporomandibular disorder with its jaw-clicking and morning clenching and its genuine-but-overdiagnosed role in "sinus headache" referrals that ENT and neurology both see weekly - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the referred-pain anatomy, [[musculoskeletal/back-pain]] - cervical referral rows and [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]] - keep out.**
:::

::: example The headache with a new word-finding problem {#ex-gca}
A 74-year-old woman has 3 weeks of right temporal headache, new difficulty chewing toast, an itchy scalp when brushing hair, and this morning blurred colour vision in the right eye for 10 minutes; ESR 68, CRP 42.
::: solution
**Giant-cell-arteritis-with-impending-ophthalmic-loss - treat before you prove it ([[#def-redflags]], [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]): the same-hour action - high-dose corticosteroids now (pulse IV methylprednisolone for the threatened-eye picture per vasculitis guidance, oral otherwise), the ophthalmology and rheumatology phone calls in the same hour, and the documentation that steroids preceded biopsy because sight is lost between investigations, never because a biopsy was late - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], steroid mechanics in [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]]; the confirmation pathway - urgent temporal-and-axillary ultrasound with the halo and compression-sign literature and the biopsy-with-long-segment-and-concurrent-steroid-does-not-erase-histology teaching and the large-vessel-vasculitis screen for the aortic-and-subclavian phenotypes - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[pathology/diagnostic-pathology]]; the steroid campaign - the slow-taper protocol with the relapse-on-treatment-recognition rules and the PMR co-prescription, and the steroid-sparing tocilizumab-with-methotrexate-context with its infection vigilance and vaccination updates and the bone-and-glucose-and-eye protection bundles in a 74-year-old - [[endocrine/bone-calcium]], [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]]; and the safety-netting - the immediate-return-if-visual-symptoms instructions and the driving conversation and the Polymyalgia-and-arteritis-awareness letter to the GP with the taper schedule shared, plus the honest explanation that the other eye and the aorta are the follow-up, not the headache - the visual-loss pathways of [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]].**
:::

::: example The thunderclap after the new drug {#ex-rcvs}
A 42-year-old woman has three thunderclap headaches over 36 hours, one during a hot shower, with transient visual disturbance; neurological examination and GCS are normal; non-contrast CT and LP are normal; she started a stimulant weight-loss supplement and has a history of migraine.
::: solution
**Reversible cerebral vasoconstriction syndrome on the differential with SAH and CVST already excluded ([[#def-redflags]], [[nervous-system/stroke]]): the pattern - recurrent thunderclap headaches over 1-3 weeks with normal initial CT-or-LP and the triggers of showering-and-exertion-and-emotion-and-the-sympathomimetic-and-serotonergic-and-cannabis-and-SSRI-drug-lists and the post-partum and cerebral-artery-aneurysm-adjacent contexts, the complication risk of segmental vasoconstriction producing stroke-and-convexity-SAH-and-seizures in the second week - [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]], [[nervous-system/epilepsy]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]; the workup - CTA-or-MRA demonstrating the multifocal-segmental-narrowing (with the normal-initial-vascular-imaging caveat that early distal involvement can hide, so repeat imaging matters) and the full secondary-exclusion sweep for angiitis-and-PHA and phaeo and the posterior-parenchymal-pressures - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[endocrine/adrenal]]; the management - the calcium-channel-blockade (nimodipine-or-verapamil) with the trigger-avoidance prescription (no vasoconstrictor drugs, no vasoactive supplements, no strenuous strain), the analgesic choice with the opioid-and-triptan caution, and the blood-pressure-avoiding-hypotension nuance for the vasoconstricted territory - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; and the follow-through - the 3-month-imaging-demonstrates-reversibility rule that converts the scary picture back into anatomy and distinguishes RCVS from the angiitides whose treatment is immunosuppression, with the recurrence counselling and the supplement-withdrawal documentation and the weight-management conversation redirected to safer options - [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**
:::

::: example The headache that is worse lying down {#ex-iih}
A 31-year-old woman, BMI 38, has 3 months of daily pressure headache worst on waking and when bending, double vision on far distance, and a "whooshing" in the right ear in time with her pulse; examination shows bilateral papilloedema and a left sixth-nerve palsy; visual fields show enlarged blind spots.
::: solution
**Idiopathic-intracranial-hypertension with sight in the balance ([[#def-other]], [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]): the urgent sequence - same-week neuro-ophthalmology with formal perimetry and optical-coherence-tomography baselines (the vision loss is often peripheral and unnoticed until it is not), MRI brain with sequences excluding venous thrombosis (MR venography, plus the dural-sinus-stenosis question) and secondary causes, then lumbar puncture with an opening-pressure measurement after imaging safety - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[nervous-system/stroke]] for the CVST overlap, [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the field tracking; the treatment - acetazolamide titrated to tolerance with the paraesthesias-and-metal-taste-and-renal-stone-and-pregnancy cautions and topiramate's dual headache-and-weight advantage with its own cognitive caveats, the weight-management programme offered as the disease-modifying therapy rather than a moral aside, and the pain-medication rationalisation that avoids stacking a medication-overuse headache on top - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[endocrine/reproductive-endocrinology]]; the sight-saving escalation - the fulminant course (rapid field loss) demanding admission with serial perimetry, acetazolamide-and-steroid-bridging-not-limb and the surgical options - optic-nerve-sheath fenestration and CSF shunting and the transverse-sinus-stenting era with its pressure-gradient-patient-selection nuance and restenosis honesty with the indication selection made jointly with the neurosurgical and neuroradiology teams; and the longitudinal reality - the medication-taper-and-weight-maintenance-and-field-surveillance years, the depression-and-quality-of-life burden, the contraception-and-pregnancy planning (acetazolamide counselling, the second-trimester eye checks) and the documented driving status during double vision - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**
:::

::: example The daily tablet for a daily headache {#ex-moh}
A 44-year-old with 15 years of migraine now has a dull daily headache from waking, takes co-codamol most days and ibuprofen "to balance it", nurofen-plus for her "real migraines" twice weekly, and is convinced she "cannot function without them"; MRI for a separate worry is normal.
::: solution
**Medication-overuse headache - the diagnosis is the prescription count ([[#def-migraine]]): the mechanics stated kindly - 10-plus monthly days of triptans, ergots, opioids or combination analgesics (15 for simple agents) for 3 months drives receptor-and-central-sensitisation changes that make the next headache easier to spawn, the opioid-and-codeine versions being the worst offenders and the most difficult to withdraw, with nausea-and-sleep-disruption-and-rebound-pattern teaching so she hears mechanism rather than blame - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[physiology/synapses]]; the withdrawal conversation - the honest 2-10-week worsening prognosis and the preventive-cover plan (start or optimise preventive before or during withdrawal rather than asking her to white-knuckle with nothing), whether outpatient versus day-unit versus inpatient withdrawal fits her work and caring duties, and anti-emetics-and-NSAIDs-where-allowed-and-a-named-contact-as the rescue scaffolding - [[clinical-skills/communication]]; the substitute acute plan - a defined weekly triptan budget with a written rule, an antiemetic strategy, and the behavioural tools (the graded-return-to-exercise and sleep regularity that do the work codeine was pretending to do) - and the relapse framing: this is a relapsing condition like the migraine itself, so annual re-checks and an early-help number beat the "never take another tablet" sermon that guarantees secret overuse - [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]]; and the comorbidity sweep - the depression-and-anxiety-and-trauma-and-sleep-apnoea prevalence in chronic daily headache, the treat-the-person-and-not-just-the-tablets prescription, and the documentation of the agreed plan with a follow-up diary review that makes the numbers visible to both of you - [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]], [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] for the obstructive-sleep interface, [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**

::: exercise Treat the status in the ED {level=1}
A 36-year-old has had 30 hours of severe migraine with vomiting, unresponsive to her own triptan at hour 2; BP 158/95; no red flags; what does the next hour of emergency management look like, and what should you not do?
::: solution
**Rescue-therapy-not-opioids ([[#def-migraine]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]): the first-line ED regimen - an IV or IM dopamine-antagonist (prochlorperazine or metoclopramide with the dystonia-and-younger-female risk and the buccal-prochlorperazine alternative and the co-beneldopa-or-anticholinergic rescue knowledge) plus an NSAID (ketorolac-or-diclofenac with renal-and-GI cautions) plus IV fluids and an antiemetic-friendly dark quiet bay - [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]], [[renal/filtration]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; the second-line - subcutaneous or nasal triptan if not yet adequately tried (her "one triptan at 2 hours" is under-dosing not failure), IV dexamethasone for the 24-72-hour recurrence reduction (the modest but real evidence) and dihydroergotamine where local practice and vascular safety allow - and the do-not list that the audit keeps finding: IV opioids (worse outcomes, more attendance, medication-overuse factories), escalating imaging for a stable known migraine without red flags, and discharge with only paracetamol and optimism; and the exit plan - a named preventive referral, a written acute plan with day counts, the safety-net red flags in plain words, and the follow-up call that makes the difference between an attendance and an episode - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**

::: exercise Headache after a dural tap {level=2}
Day 3 after an obstructed labour with an accidental dural puncture, a 33-year-old has a disabling frontal-occipital headache within minutes of sitting up, with neck stiffness and tinnitus; afebrile, normal neurology.
::: solution
**Post-dural-puncture-headache - the diagnosis that mandates an anaesthetic conversation ([[#def-redflags]], [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]): the pattern - postural onset within days of the puncture with the neck-and-tinnitus-and-hearing-fullness-and-diplopia (sixth-nerve traction) extensions, the conservative natural history (most resolve by 1-2 weeks) and the conservative package - paracetamol-and-NSAIDs-and-caffeine-with-its-rebound-caveat-and-fluids-and-the-honest-evidence-on-"bed-rest"; the definitive fix - an epidural blood patch for disabling or persistent symptoms, its high single-shot success rate, the anticoagulation-and-sepsis contraindication timing rules and the informed discussion about the second procedure on an already traumatic birth week - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]], [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]]; the differentials that are not postural - the post-dural-puncture differentials that must not be missed - meningitis (fever and non-postural component), pre-eclampsia and the cortical-vein-thrombosis-with-its-non-postural-and-seizure-and-focal-progression, and the rare subdural haematoma from intracranial hypotension, so "typical PDPH" still requires an examination and a documented change-if-non-postural warning - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]], [[nervous-system/stroke]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; and the duty-of-candour - the accidental-puncture disclosure, the incident reporting that keeps the difficult-airway-and-labour-epidural audits honest, and the note for future anaesthetics (the smaller needle and the paramedian approach lessons), because this is one of obstetric anaesthesia's most litigated and most preventable events - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**

::: history
**Headache's history is largely the history of people being believed or disbelieved: the ancient trepanation skulls with their healed margins as proof of survival and of the theory that holes release headache-demons; Galen's cephalalgia classifications and the centuries of humoral bile-and-fumes therapy that left "megril" and the migraine-names etymologies (hemicrania, hemigrim, megrim) as their fossils; the 1783 monograph that first separated the sick-headache from the simple kind and the 19th-century neurology of Latham and Gowers - Gowers's own migraine diaries and his textbook's aura descriptions still quoted verbatim in the literature ; the ergotometry arc - from the midwife's ergot for labour and Stork's and later Warton's ergot-poisoning headaches (the 1859-1894 "St Anton's fire" epidemics) through the 1920s-30s purification of ergotamine and the 1960s sumatriptan design at Wellcome informed by the serotonin and vascular-vasoconstriction work of the mid-twentieth century, arguably the first drug made by a hypothesis about a headache mechanism rather than found by observation - [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]]; cluster headache's name-journey (Horton's histamine cephalalgia, the "suicide headache" of patient folklore, the long misdiagnosis as "sinus" and "trigeminal" and the oxygen-reintroduction by Sjaastad after the 1960s trial - the classic case of an effective therapy sleeping for two decades; and the CGRP decade - the peptide's vascular discovery, the epidemiology of the triptan non-responders, the antibody programmes and the regulator-accepted preventive indications - one of the few headache stories with an unusually clean translational line, and a reminder that a billion pain-years were logged in diaries before the market moved - [[physiology/synapses]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
:::

::: summary
- Headache is diagnosed by history; the red-flag list (SNNOOP10 in any memorable form) decides who gets scanned, and typical migraine with unchanged aura does not get serial imaging.
- Treat migraine early, at adequate dose, on a counted monthly budget; the medication-overuse trap is the commonest iatrogenic cause of chronic daily headache.
- New headache after 50 with scalp and jaw symptoms is giant cell arteritis until steroids say otherwise; sight waits for no biopsy.
- Thunderclap onset means same-hour subarachnoid protocols and, when those are clear, the RCVS and CVST differentials and their drug-trigger histories.
- Cluster headache deserves oxygen and verapamil with ECG monitoring, not a lifetime of migraine tablets; its suicide risk is a real clinical variable.
- Trigeminal neuralgia is a symptom with a cause hunt (MS and lesions in the young) and a carbamazepine ladder to microvascular decompression at the top.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Sort the referrals {level=1}
Image now, image routinely, or reassure: (a) 25-year-old with 8-year typical migraine, unchanged, seeking "a scan for peace of mind"; (b) 55-year-old with new daily headache and jaw claudication; (c) 33-year-old with thunderclap 6 hours ago, normal CT; (d) 40-year-old with migraine with aura whose pattern changed after a long flight and now has neck pain.
::: solution
**Pretest-probability-in-practice ([[#def-redflags]]): (a) reassure-and-treat with an explicit explanation of why a scan does more harm (incidentalomas, anxiety, cost) at this probability - the conversation is the intervention - [[clinical-skills/communication]]; (b) same-day ESR-CRP-and-steroids-then-imaging-biopsy pathway as above; (c) the CT-negative-thunderclap still needs the LP-or-CTA-within-window protocol because sensitivity falls with hours - SAH rules out with tests, not with one test - [[nervous-system/stroke]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; and (d) the change-plus-neck-pain-plus-prothrombotic-context picture gets venous imaging for CVST and arterial imaging for dissection - both treatable, neither "just migraine" - [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]].**
:::

::: exercise Write the preventive plan {level=1}
A 38-year-old has 6 migraine days per month, is on amitriptyline 10 mg at night for sleep, uses rizatriptan 11 days a month, and wants "something new".
::: solution
**Fix-behaviour-before-adding-drugs ([[#def-migraine]]): the overuse check first - the 10-days-per-month triptan ceiling with the withdrawal-prediction (a 2-10-week flare before improvement and the plan to ride it with preventive cover), and the diary audit that finds the caffeine-and-sleep-debt-and-skip-meal architecture; the preventive optimisation - amitriptyline titrated to a genuinely therapeutic dose (10 mg is a sleep dose) or switched to propranolol or topiramate or candesartan per comorbidity (sleep versus weight versus blood pressure), with the pregnancy-capacity questions asked before any valproate conversation - [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]]; the acute-plan rewrite - triptan plus antiemetic at onset in adequate dose with a day-count rule and a named "escape" strategy for failures, which paradoxically reduces total consumption - [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]; and the newer tier - CGRP antibody or gepant candidacy if two-plus preventives fail or are intolerable, with the honest discussion of cost-effectiveness thresholds, the long-term-safety data horizons, and the trial-end question of what happens when access ends - [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
:::

::: exercise Manage the pregnant migraineur {level=2}
A 29-year-old at 24 weeks with severe migraine 4 days per week, previously well-controlled on topiramate (stopped per patient choice) and propranolol; now uncontrolled; asking what is safe.
::: solution
**Pregnancy-headache-risk-balance ([[#def-migraine]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]): the reassurance and the work - most migraineurs improve by the second trimester, so non-improvement earns a red-flag review and a pre-eclampsia-aware blood-pressure-and-proteinuria check at later visits, and the topiramate discontinuation is validated (teratogen) while the "stopped everything because leaflets" decision is revisited with numbers - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; the safe list - paracetamol in standard doses, metoclopramide and prochlorperazine for nausea, magnesium and riboflavin as low-risk adjuncts, and the triptan evidence base (sumatriptan's registry data being the most reassuring) reserved for severe attacks with the vascular cautions and documented shared decision - [[pharmacology/patient-variation]]; the preventive conversation - propranolol's placental-growth considerations versus the untreated-disease harm (dehydration, poor intake, falls in pain), the amitriptyline option and the CGRP antibodies where the data genuinely are not there and she must be told so rather than enrolled by silence - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]; and the logistics - the anaesthetic letter for labour with the her-own-triptan plan, the post-partum-sleep-deprivation strategy (the worst week is the delivery week), the breastfeeding transfer figures for each chosen drug, and the VTE-and- aura-and-oestrogen-contraception follow-up booked before discharge from the maternity unit - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]].**
:::

::: exercise Run the facial-pain clinic {level=2}
A 63-year-old man reports 6 weeks of right cheek electric-shock pains triggered by wind and brushing teeth, with a persistent dull ache between attacks and slightly reduced light touch in V2.
::: solution
**Classical-versus-secondary-trigeminal-neuralgia-decided-by-the-details ([[#def-facial]]): the two signals - the constant dull ache and the sensory loss are the "this one gets scanned" features - structural lesions (vestibular schwannoma and meningioma and metastasis and nasopharyngeal carcinoma invading the cavernous sinus and foramen rotundum with its V2-predilection) and the MS-partially-myelinating-plaque picture in the younger patient - MRI with protocol and the nasal-and-dental-and-sinus examination with the red flags (unilateral serous otitis and neck mass meaning ENT referral for the nasopharynx) - [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; the treatment start - carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine titration with the sodium-and-HLA-and-FBC-platelet monitoring culture and the interaction review (the warfarin and the statin and the transplant drugs) and the driving-sedation advice - [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]], [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]]; the escalation conversation - when pain outpaces drugs, the anaesthetic-and-neurosurgical menu ranked by durability versus numbness versus recurrence (decompression's long pain-free curve versus the percutaneous techniques in the frail and secondary causes) with the expectation-setting that "cure" and "control" differ, and the referral-now-not-after-six-months rule for the suicidal-in-pain patient - [[psychiatry/self-harm-suicide-prevention]]; and the trap to avoid - the dental cascade (extractions done for undiagnosed neuralgia because the trigger zone was mistaken for a tooth) which the history alone prevents, and the persistent-idiopathic-facial-pain patient who should be spared procedures and offered the antidepressant-and-psychological pathway instead - [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]].**
:::

::: exercise Audit the headache service {level=3}
Your trust's headache pathway: 60% of triptan prescriptions lack a day-count rule, neuroimaging referrals are 3x the regional mean, and the preventive-visit default is "add another drug". Design the improvement programme.
::: solution
**Headache-services-are-behaviour-change-programmes ([[#def-migraine]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): measurement - prescribing-audit-definitions that count triptan and compound-analgesic days-per-patient from primary-care extracts, imaging-referral rates with their indication coding and the incidentaloma-and-repeat-scan tail, and the preventive-visit note-audit for the three questions that must appear (attack frequency, acute-drug days, disability score) - [[epidemiology-ebm/measuring-disease]], [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]; the intervention set - a template note with the ICHD prompts and a red-flag checklist and a medication-overuse warning that fires in the prescriber's screen, the nurse-led diary-and-titration clinic, the imaging-referral guideline embedded in the request form with a "why now?" free-text, and the deprescribing protocol for overused drugs with the preventive cover and the follow-up call - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[pharmacology/patient-variation]]; the education and culture layer - the GP-lunch-and-lunchtime teaching on the red flags and the reassurance scripts, the neurology-registrar imaging-stewardship rotation, and the patient-information that explains why fewer scans make care better - [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]; and the evaluation with guardrails - acute-days-per-patient and imaging rates per 100 new referrals and 6-month MIDAS-and-HIT-6 scores and the do-not-miss-SAH-and-giant-cell-arteritis metric as the safety counterweight to referral suppression - [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**
