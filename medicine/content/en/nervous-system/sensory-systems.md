The special senses are the parts of neurology that leak into every other specialty: the blurred child, the double-visioning driver, the elderly man who fell because his vestibular system failed, the patient whose "functional" weakness is a field defect nobody charted. Vision occupies most of this chapter because most sensory complaints are visual, and because the visual pathway is the longest tract in the body with the richest localising value. Hearing and balance follow with their own geometry, and the chemical senses arrive mostly as ENT and systemic disease.

This chapter covers the eye and its emergencies, refractive and retinal disease, the visual pathway and cortical blindness, hearing loss and vertigo, and taste and smell.

## The eye and sight-threatening disease

::: definition The painful red eye and the painless visual loss {#def-eye}
**Approach by three questions - painful or painless, vision lost or preserved, discharge present or absent - because the red eye is an anatomical triage of cornea, anterior chamber, conjunctiva and adnexa before it is a differential diagnosis. Sight-threatening causes with their discriminators: acute angle closure glaucoma (older, hypermetropic, Asian or female patient with severe unilateral pain, haloes, vomiting, mid-dilated fixed pupil, steamy cornea and a rock-hard globe - an emergency treated by lowering pressure and laser peripheral iridotomy, with the other eye always scheduled for prophylaxis because the anatomical predisposition is bilateral); keratitis and corneal ulcer (contact lens wearers, pain out of proportion, fluorescein uptake, pseudomonas risk, do not patch, do not use topical steroids without ophthalmology, scrapes for culture when the ulcer is central or purulent); anterior uveitis (photophobia, ciliary flush, small sluggish pupil, keratic precipitates, the HLA-B27 back and bowel associations that make the rheumatological history part of the examination); scleritis (boring pain waking the patient, red vessels that do not blanch with phenylephrine, the vasculitis hunt); endophthalmitis (post-procedure, post-traumatic or endogenous, hypopyon, vitritis, intravitreal antibiotics within hours); and orbital cellulitis (fever, proptosis, painful restricted motility, the sinus-adjacent ethmoid origin in children, contrast imaging of sinuses and orbit, drainage decisions, cavernous sinus thrombosis as the feared extension - contrasting with the benign preseptal version that does not restrict eye movement or displace the globe). Painless visual loss divides by speed and permanence: vascular events (central retinal artery occlusion - cherry-red spot, ophthalmic emergency of minutes-hours with stroke workup because the carotid that threw the clot can throw a hemispheric one; central and branch retinal vein occlusion - "blood and thunder", the diabetic and hypertensive associations, neovascular follow-up); vitreous haemorrhage (red-free loss, no view of fundus, diabetes and anticoagulation and posterior vitreous detachment with trauma to the retina); retinal detachment (flashes, floater shower, curtain with progressive field loss, surgical emergency for the macula-on retina); and the slow painless list - cataract (glare, gradual, opacified lens, surgery when it matters to the patient rather than when a number says so) and the maculopathies below. Red flags for immediate ophthalmological referral are gathered in the exercises. [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]] for how to look; [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]] for the vasculitic eyes; [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]] for the diabetic fundus; [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]] for the embolic sources.**
:::

::: definition Retina, optic nerve and visual pathway {#def-pathway}
**Diabetic retinopathy is a microvascular census: background changes (microaneurysms, haemorrhages, hard exudates, venous beading), proliferative disease (new vessels at the disc elsewhere, vitreous risk), maculopathy (the actual commoner of blinding mechanisms, graded by exudation and oedema around the fovea), and the treatment grammar of laser photocoagulation for proliferative risk and intravitreal anti-VEGF for macular oedema and neovascularisation. Screening programmes count because lesions are asymptomatic until they are catastrophic; pregnancy, insulin requirements, hypertension and renal disease alter tempo, and sudden worsening after rapid glycaemic correction is real and worth warning patients about ([[endocrine/diabetes-complications]], [[renal/filtration]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]). Age-related macular degeneration divides into dry (drusen, atrophy, the AREDS formulation nuance, low-vision services) and wet (metamorphosis on the Amsler grid, sudden distortion, anti-VEGF regimes that preserve rather than restore). Glaucoma - the optic neuropathy of cupping and arcuate defects with pressure as a treatable risk factor rather than the disease - needs perimetry and OCT and field comparison over years, and its asymptomatic early phase is the argument for opportunistic measurement in the over-fifties and relatives of glaucoma patients. Optic neuritis (young woman, pain on eye movement, days of desaturation and colour loss, disc normal in retrobulbar cases so "the patient with a normal fundus and no vision", MS associations and the MRI decision, the steroid-accelerated-recovery-not-final-outcome point, and the alternative diagnoses that mimic it - ischaemic optic neuropathy in the vascular elderly with altitudic field loss and no pain, papilloedema with bilateral transient visual obscurations, leber hereditary optic neuropathy in teenage boys) is covered with its demyelinating family in [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]. The pathway beyond the eye localises with the chiasmal crossing in mind: monocular loss to optic nerve or retina; chiasmal lesions to bitemporal hemianopia with the pituitary and craniopharyngioma differentials of [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]]; retrochiasmal defects to congruent hemianopia with macular sparing in occipital infarcts, the quadrantanopias of temporal ("pie in the sky") and parietal ("pie on the eye") radiations, and cortical blindness with pupillary preservation and Anton denial in bilateral occipital damage. Field defects that respect neither anatomy - spirals, tunnel vision that improves with distance - are functional, examined with the mirror and the tangent test rather than argued with ([[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[psychiatry/mood-disorders]] for conversion contexts).**
:::

## Refraction, prescribing and protection

::: definition Errors of refraction and the squint {#def-refraction}
**Refractive errors are optics, and their clinical content is who gets them and what they hide: myopia (distant blur, axial elongation, the epidemic of near-work and outdoor-light deprivation, and the high-myopia complications of retinal detachment, myopic maculopathy and glaucoma that make childhood control a serious topic - atropine drops, orthokeratology and defocus spectacles with their real-but-modest effect sizes), hypermetropia (accommodation-driven eye strain and headaches in the young, the amblyopia risk of significant uncorrected far-sightedness, late presbyopia tipping compensated patients into blur), astigmatism (corneal shape, ghosting, the corneal-clinic scar and keratoconus associations with eye-rubbing and atopy - [[musculoskeletal/skin]] for the atopic march), and presbyopia (the universal lens stiffening of the forties that every emmetropic professional experiences as the menu-and-phone syndrome). Contact lenses bring their own medicine: microbial keratitis risk with overnight wear and water exposure (Acanthamoeba in swimmers and tap-water rinsers - the Acanthamoeba pages of [[microbiology/fungi-parasites]]), giant papillary conjunctivitis, dry eye and the contact-lens-abuse-ulcer that any SHO should flag on red flag criteria. Amblyopia ("lazy eye") is a developmental cortical phenomenon from strabismus, anisometropia or deprivation (cataract, ptosis, haemangioma), which is why newborn eye checks look for the red reflex and why patching works only while plasticity lasts - the surveillance-and-treatment programme logic sits in [[paediatrics/growth-development]] and [[public-health/screening]]. Squints: comitant deviations with the cover test versus the paralytic squints of third, fourth and sixth nerve palsies - each with localising meaning (painful third with pupil involvement is an aneurysm until proven otherwise; isolated sixth in raised intracranial pressure is a false localiser; the diabetic microvascular palsies that spare the pupil); and the nystagmus families (congenital, vestibular with fast phase naming the direction, downbeat and upbeat with brainstem and toxic causes, and the seesaw variants of sellar disease). Diplopia is binocular until one eye is covered; that five-second test saves more time than any scan. [[nervous-system/motor-systems]] for the brainstem nuclei; [[endocrine/thyroid]] for the ophthalmopathy with lid retraction and restricted elevation; [[cardiovascular/hypertension]] and [[blood/haematopoiesis]] for the retinal haemorrhages that arrive as "floaters". The prescribing content - who may drive after a diplopia episode, what the eyes-only disease of giant cell arteritis demands within hours (steroids before biopsy, jaw claudication and scalp symptoms in the over-fifties, the contralateral eye at risk), and the medicolegal duty to notify licensing authorities - threads through the exercises and [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The halo morning {#ex-angle}
A 68-year-old woman wakes with a rainbow-halo right eye, brow pain, two episodes of vomiting and nausea; she has always needed "reading glasses only"; the eye is quiet-red, the pupil mid-dilated and fixed, the cornea steamy; she has taken an antihistamine for a rash.
::: solution
**Acute angle closure until proven otherwise - and the drug history is the trigger ([[#def-eye]], [[#def-refraction]]): recognition - the haloes, pain, vomiting (often mis-sorted as gastro or migraine), fixed mid-dilated pupil, steamy cornea and the hypermetropic small-eye phenotype, with the anticholinergic (antihistamine, antidepressant, antispasmodic, sympathomimetic) as the classic precipitant and the dark-dilated pupil of cinema-and-dusk timing as its cousin - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[clinical-skills/history-taking]]; the immediate minutes - urgent same-hour ophthalmology (do not wait for the ward, do not treat as conjunctivitis), pressure-lowering topicals and systemic agents per protocol (beta-blocker drops with asthma caution, alpha-agonist, carbonic anhydrase inhibitor, sometimes hyperosmotics), antiemetics, avoid miotics until pressure falls and pain permits, oral or IV acetazolamide with its sulfa and renal cautions - [[pharmacology/dosing-principles]], [[renal/filtration]], [[musculoskeletal/skin]] - the sulfa-asthma history lives in [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]; the definitive act - laser peripheral iridotomy once the cornea clears, with the prophylactic laser on the fellow eye discussed today because the anatomical risk is bilateral and the medicolegal literature is unambiguous, and documentation of the teaching given about dark rooms and new drugs - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]]; and the differentials that imitate: neovascular glaucoma from the diabetic or occluded-ischaemic retina (new vessels on the iris, the opposite of the flat iris of primary angle closure), the painful glaucomato-cyclitic crises, uveitic secondary closure with synechiae - [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]], [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]] for the uveitic bowel connection.**
:::

::: example The curtain in the evening sky {#ex-retinal-detachment}
A 55-year-old highly myopic man notices 2 days of flickering lights in the left periphery on waking, then a shower of twenty new floaters, and now a grey curtain creeping up from below that "hasn't moved since last night"; vision central 6/6.
::: solution
**Rhegmatogenous detachment - inferior field means superior break, and "central vision still good" means operate today ([[#def-pathway]], [[#def-refraction]]): the mechanism - posterior vitreous detachment tearing a peripheral retina at a lattice-degenerated site, the myopic and post-surgical and traumatic predispositions, the photopsic-then-floater-then-curtain sequence as the most reliable history in ophthalmology - [[#def-eye]] for the red-flag list; the same-day pathway - emergency vitreoretinal assessment with dilated fundoscopy and indentation (the curtain does not wait for a routine slot), ultrasound if media are opaque, nil-by-mouth advice and positioning discussion pending review, and the clear instruction not to drive with a hemifield lost - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]; the surgery and after - tamponade (gas or silicone oil) with the positional rules and flight-and-altitude contraindications for gas and the cataract acceleration that follows, versus scleral buckle in selected younger phakes, with the one-eye-at-a-time counselling about the other eye's risk and the symptoms to return for; and the follow-on medicine - the fellow eye's lattice examination, the rare bilateral simultaneous detachment after trauma or surgery, and the low-vision referral if the macula has been detached long enough that "successful reattachment" still leaves distortion - the low-vision and workplace conversation belongs with [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The child who sits at the front {#ex-amblyopia}
A 6-year-old is referred by the school vision screening for "squint and poor vision right"; examination shows a manifest convergent squint and a best-corrected acuity of 6/18 right, 6/5 left; the fundoscopy is normal.
::: solution
**Strabismic amblyopia with a squint that still needs its cause separating ([[#def-refraction]], [[paediatrics/growth-development]]): the workup - cycloplegic refraction to unmask hypermetropia (accommodation hides it in children, and uncorrected far-sightedness drives both the esotropia and the amblyopia), full acuity charting per eye, ocular alignment measurements and the exclusion of the rare organic mimics including retinoblastoma (leukocoria, always check the red reflex before labelling "lazy eye" - the medicolegal classic), and the growth and neurological context in [[paediatrics/growth-development]] and [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]; the treatment ladder - glasses first and full-time (a significant hypermetropic esotropia may straighten with correction alone), then patching or penalisation of the good eye measured in hours-per-day with the adherence-and-schoolwork negotiations that are the actual barrier, then squint surgery once acuity is maximised because operating before amblyopia therapy trades alignment for a wasted chance - [[public-health/screening]] for why school screening exists, [[clinical-skills/communication]] for the patching contract with a child who hates being patched; the surveillance logic - the critical-period neuroscience behind acting before roughly seven to eight years, the relapse rate that mandates review after successful treatment, and the adult patient presenting "too late" for whom the honest answer is that plasticity is reduced rather than gone - critical-period visual-cortex physiology in [[physiology/membrane-potentials]]-adjacent chapters and the developmental rows of [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]; and the safety-education layer - the one-eyed future if the good eye is ever injured, so eye protection during sport is a discussion worth having in every amblyopia clinic, with the sports-vision rules of the ophthalmology and optometry literature and the sports-vision rules of that literature and sports-vision and the workplace-protective-goggles logic of [[public-health/environmental-occupational]].**
:::

## Hearing, balance, and the chemical senses

::: definition The ear: conductive, sensorineural, and vestibular {#def-ear}
**Hearing loss is divided with a tuning fork or an audiogram before anything else - conductive (outer and middle ear: wax, otitis media with effusion "glue ear" as the commonest cause of childhood hearing loss and the adenoid-and-grommet era, otosclerosis with its autosomal-family and pregnancy-worsening lore and the excellent stapes surgery, malignant otitis in the diabetic elderly with pseudomonas and cranial nerves - [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]]), sensorineural (presbycusis - bilateral high-frequency, the hidden disability of group consultations and the hearing-aid acceptance problem; noise-induced with its 4 kHz notch and occupational-claim reality - [[public-health/environmental-occupational]]; Ménière disease - episodic vertigo with low-frequency loss, fullness and tinnitus, the endolymphatic hydrops model, betahistine-and-diet-versus-intratympanic-steroid-versus-destructive-procedures ladder; sudden idiopathic SNHL - a steroid-within-days emergency misfiled as "blocked eardrum"; ototoxicity - aminoglycosides with the drug-level era's reduced-but-real risk, cisplatin with its paediatric-survivor burden and cochlear-implant planning, loop diuretics, quinine-era lore, and salicylate tinnitus that reverses - [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[pathology/cancer-diagnosis]]; acoustic neuroma/vestibular schwannoma - unilateral asymmetric loss with poor discrimination and tinnitus, the MRI internal-meatal-protocol question, NF2 in the young bilateral case - [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]) and mixed; and central versus peripheral vertigo - the acute vestibular syndrome triad test of head-impulse, nystagmus pattern and skew deviation (HINTS) being more sensitive than early MRI for posterior-circulation stroke, BPPV with its positional seconds-long torsional-upbeat nystagmus and the Epley manoeuvre as bedside therapy, vestibular neuritis versus labyrinthitis by the hearing question, the migraine connection with its own diagnostic gymnastics, and the rehabilitation logic of vestibular suppressant stop-dates because the brain compensates only when allowed to. Tinnitus is a symptom with a short dangerous list (pulsatile - vascular and glomeroid causes, imaging territory), a long benign list, and a management chapter of habituation therapy and sound enrichment rather than tablets. Sudden deafness, unilateral loss, pulsatile tinnitus and any vertigo-with-neuro-signs get urgent referral. Smell and taste: conductive versus sensorineural loss (the viral and post-infectious anosmia era with its parosmia phase and olfactory training evidence; the neurodegenerative smell-loss-as-prodrome research direction in [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]]; drugs, zinc, sinus disease, and the safety questions - gas alarms, spoiled food, burn scars - that dominate quality-of-life discussions), with taste mostly riding on smell plus trigeminal sensation, and the true taste-bud list (metal taste with ACE inhibitors and metformin and zinc supplements, burning mouth syndrome, and the painful glossitises of nutritional deficiency in [[blood/anaemia]] and [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]). [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]] for the ear and cranial nerves; [[nervous-system/motor-systems]] for the brainstem; [[psychiatry/anxiety-disorders]] for the persistent-dizziness phenotype.**
:::

::: example The vertigo that is a stroke {#ex-hints}
A 61-year-old hypertensive man has abrupt continuous vertigo with vomiting and cannot stand without support; nystagmus changes direction on left and right gaze; head impulse test is normal to the right; he slurs slightly when talking.
::: solution
**HINTS-fragile posterior circulation stroke until imaged ([[#def-ear]], [[nervous-system/stroke]]): the bedside signal - direction-changing gaze-evoked nystagmus and a normal head-impulse in continuous vertigo point central, not peripheral, and the early DWI-negative window of small brainstem infarcts means a normal first MRI does not overturn the clinical signs - [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]; the stroke-system response - same-hour stroke assessment with NIHSS documentation and the thrombolysis-and-thrombectomy-window arithmetic and CTA of head and neck for the vertebral dissection-and-occlusion questions that present as "ear pain and vertigo" - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]]; the admission medicine - aspiration screening with that dysarthria, DVT and pressure and glycaemic care while "lying still feels safer", fall-proofing the bathroom, and the early mobilisation-versus-holding debates with stroke-unit-level-of-care logic - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]; and what must not be done - prochlorperazine-and-promethazine-for-days-which-delay-compensation-and-sedate-the-examination, the discharge with a diagnosis of "viral labyrinthitis" when the eyes never fit, and betahistine-as-insurance - plus the clinic-after: vestibular rehabilitation once the acute phase passes, the migraine-and-anxiety overlays that can keep a patient dizzy for a year, and the driving advice with the licensing letter written the same week - [[psychiatry/anxiety-disorders]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: example The wax, the pain, and the perforation {#ex-ear}
A 34-year-old with hearing loss "since childhood, worse in winter" has severe ear pain, fever and purulent discharge for 3 days; the tympanic membrane bulges; the previous audiogram showed a 30 dB conductive loss.
::: solution
**Two diseases in one ear - chronic eardrum pathology plus acute otitis media on top ([[#def-ear]]): the acute management - analgesia-first medicine with regular dosing rather than prn, the discharge-that-appeared-as-perforation-decompression and whether antibiotics are indicated (age, severity, systemic features, immunocompromise and the perforation-with-discharge pathway where topical quinolone drops become the agent of choice with a hearing-safe explanation - aminoglycoside drops and cochlear risk), and the safety-net teaching on mastoid tenderness, protrusion and neck stiffness - [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[microbiology/bacteria]] for the S pneumoniae-H influenzae-M catarrhalis cast; the hearing story - the conductive loss "since childhood" demanding a proper examination for the perforation, cholesteatoma (attic retraction, foul discharge, bone erosion on CT, the surgery-when-it-can-be-organised-not-when-it-leaks rule) versus ossicular fixation and glue, and the occupational and driving implications of unilateral loss - [[public-health/environmental-occupational]], [[microbiology/infection-control]] - keep the cholesteatoma pathway with ENT-surgery rows in [[surgery/wounds-surgical-infection]]-adjacent; write: the surgical pathway is described in ENT practice under [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]]; and the follow-through - repeat audiometry after the acute episode settles (conductive losses that persist needENT action), the water precautions for a perforation and the "never syringe an unknown drum" rule for the practice nurse and the emergency department alike - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

::: exercise Design the vertigo clinic {level=3}
Your service sees 600 "dizziness" referrals a year, half persisting beyond 6 months, most labelled vestibular. Redesign the pathway with assessment, treatment and evaluation.
::: solution
**Dizziness-is-a-syndrome-not-a-diagnosis ([[#def-ear]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the-taxonomy-first-clinic - the four-games classification (acute continuous, episodic spontaneous, episodic positional, and symptomatic orthostatic/presyncope) driving targeted tests and forcing the history questions the drug list and the lying-standing BP answer before any canalith-repositioning is booked - with the cardiovascular and autonomic contributions from [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]] and [[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]] and [[renal/water-sodium]]; the rule-out rules - the HINTS-based urgent-stroke criterion, the new-unilateral-hearing-loss pathway, the papilloedema check on every "chronic dizziness", and the drop-attack-and-diplopia red flags with their neuroimaging triggers - [[nervous-system/stroke]], [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]; the treatment spine - BPPV repositioning training as a staff competency with the recurrence-counselling, vestibular rehabilitation delivered by trained physiotherapists with home-programme adherence engineering, Ménière stratified care, and the vestibular-migraine protocol with its prophylaxis and sleep-and-diet work - and the persistent-postural-perceptual-dizziness phenotype whose treatment is CBT-plus-mobilisation-plus-suppressant-withdrawal rather than more tests, with the psychiatric collaboration honest about mechanism not blame - [[psychiatry/anxiety-disorders]], [[nervous-system/headache-pain]]; and the evaluation - time-to-diagnosis and proportion-diagnosed-at-first-attendance, suppressant-prescribing-rates, rehab access and attendance, falls and ED re-attendance, PROM instruments (Dizziness Handicap Inventory) sampled not universal, and the re-referral rate that reveals whether patients were reassured or merely tested - [[epidemiology-ebm/measuring-disease]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

## Where this leads

The neurological examination and its localising grammar in [[nervous-system/motor-systems]] and [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]] ([[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]); demyelination and papilloedema in [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]]; stroke and the visual field in [[nervous-system/stroke]]; the diabetic eye in [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]; screening programmes in [[public-health/screening]]; hearing as a paediatric and genetic topic in [[paediatrics/growth-development]] and [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]].

::: history
**Eye and ear medicine carry some of medicine's oldest instruments and oldest inequalities: cataract couching from antiquity through the eighteenth century and Daviel's operative extraction and the plane-by-plane progress toward the modern phacoemulsification-and-lens-implant surgery that made blindness a solvable problem in rich countries - [[surgery/perioperative-care]]; Helmholtz's ophthalmoscope of 1851 turning the living retina into a readable organ within a year of its invention and launching the era of signs-with-a-camera, and the diabetic-retinopathy-screening-photography-era that followed a century later; the trachoma-control-movement as the first mass public-health-eye-programme and the still-open equity wound of avoidable blindness - [[public-health/global-health]], [[musculoskeletal/skin]] - trachoma belongs with neglected infections in [[infectious-diseases/tropical-infections]]; the audiogram's military origins in hearing-conservation-audiology after the gun-decks and the jet era and the compensation law it created - [[public-health/environmental-occupational]]; Ménière's 1863 hydrops description and the century of surgical destruction it licensed before micro-instrumentation and the endolymphatic-sac-and-vestibular-nerve alternatives; the BPPV story - Barany's cupula physics, Epley's canal repositioning ignored for fifty years until Semper's and Brandt and Darlow's laboratory-era vindication, one of medicine's clearest examples of effective bedside therapy lost to habit; and the modern sensory-neuroscience arc from Hubel and Wiesel's visual-cortex electrode cat to cortical implants and the cochlear-implant programme, which turned deafness from a sensory fate into a surgical timeline - [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]] itself, [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]] for the connexin-26 deafness genes and the newborn-screening debates, and [[immunology/vaccines-immunotherapy]] - rubella-defeated-deafness belongs with vaccination programmes in [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]].**
:::

::: summary
- Triage the red eye by pain, vision and discharge, and act on the sight-threatening five: angle closure, keratitis, uveitis-urgent-causes, endophthalmitis, orbital cellulitis.
- Sudden painless visual loss is vascular or retinal until proven otherwise and is same-day medicine; curtain-like field loss with photopsia is a detachment until the retina says otherwise.
- Localise visual loss along the pathway with the chiasmal crossing in mind; the field defect is the lesion's portrait, and non-anatomical fields are examined, not argued with.
- Diplopia that disappears on covering either eye is ocular misalignment; an acutely painful third with a dilated pupil is an aneurysm until imaged.
- Split hearing loss by fork or audiogram before treating; sudden sensorineural loss is a steroid emergency and pulsatile tinnitus is a vessel until disproved.
- Acute continuous vertigo needs HINTS before reassurance; BPPV deserves repositioning, and vestibular suppressants deserve stop-dates.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Sort the diabetic emergencies {level=1}
Which is the acute ophthalmic emergency: proliferative diabetic retinopathy, new vitreous haemorrhage, painless gradual blur over months, or sudden fixed dilated pupil with headache?
::: solution
**Different-speed emergencies ([[#def-eye]], [[#def-pathway]]): the headache-with-fixed-pupil is angle closure (or third-nerve aneurysm) - hours matter and it is not diabetes-specific; vitreous haemorrhage is same-week with ultrasound and surgical timing per whether the retina is attached; proliferative disease is a months-clock laser-and-anti-VEGF problem whose emergency is that it is silent; the slow blur is cataract or maculopathy - book it and explain it - [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**
:::

::: exercise Chart the field defect {level=1}
Give the lesion site for: bitemporal hemianopia; right homonymous hemianopia with macular sparing; left upper quadrantanopia; arcuate defect with enlarged blind spot in one eye.
::: solution
**Lesion-portraits ([[#def-pathway]]): chiasm (classically pituitary and craniopharyngioma, with the endocrine and sellar imaging pathway of [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]]); left occipital infarct (PCA territory, the sparing from macular-cortical dual supply) - [[nervous-system/stroke]]; right temporal lobe Meyer's loop ("pie in the sky", the tumour and hippocampal-surgery contexts) - [[nervous-system/epilepsy]]; and an optic nerve process with disc change - papilloedema and glaucoma and disc oedema differential, with pressure and imaging checks - [[#def-eye]], [[nervous-system/neuroinflammation]].**

::: exercise Handle the chemical exposure {level=2}
A welder splashes alkali into his eye at work; at presentation the conjunctiva is beefy and the cornea hazy; he has rinsed for "a minute or two".
::: solution
**Alkali-burn-is-a-ph-time-emergency ([[#def-eye]]): irrigation-to-neutrality - topical anaesthetic first, Morgan-needle-or-tear-drop-continuous-irrigation-until-pH-normal-for-30-minutes-off-irrigation and the litmus-paper-discipline and the eversion-and-sweep-for-particulates - [[clinical-skills/physical-examination]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]; the grading and admission decision - the limbal-ischaemia-and-corneal-clarity grading (Roper-Hall-Duque) predicting outcome, the overnight admit-and-drop-and-cycloplegic-and-antibiotic-protect-regimen with the ascorbate-and-citrate-collagen-narrative and the early anterior-segment-procision-about-amniotic-membrane-grafts - [[surgery/wounds-surgical-infection]] - keep the tissue-repair logic with [[pathology/chronic-inflammation-repair]]; the occupational half - the eye-protection failure and RIDDOR-style reporting and workplace assessment and the other-eye-incident-history that makes this case bigger than one eye - [[public-health/environmental-occupational]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]]; and the follow-up weeks - the symblepharon and lid and conjunctival-heel problems, the stem-cell-deficiency-keratopathy diagnosis that threatens the late result and demands the specialist limbal-reconstruction conversation - [[musculoskeletal/skin]].**

::: exercise Manage the glue ear childhood {level=2}

::: exercise Decide about driving {level=2}
State your approach for each: (a) the 52-year-old with a sixth nerve palsy from a microvascular cause; (b) the 24-year-old with newly diagnosed epilepsy and a normal scan; (c) the 70-year-old with homonymous hemianopia after stroke; (d) the 45-year-old with Ménière attacks twice monthly.
::: solution
**Fitness-to-drive-as-a-clinical-duty ([[#def-eye]], [[#def-ear]], [[nervous-system/stroke]], [[nervous-system/epilepsy]], [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]]): (a) no driving until diplopia resolves and cover-testing normal, with the "one-eye-driving-skills-are-not-restored-by-habit" explanation and occupational-health liaison for HGV and PSV classes which have stricter standards - [[clinical-skills/communication]]; (b) seizure-free-period rules set by the licensing authority and jurisdiction, the immediate-notification duty for group-seizure-risk occupations, and the documentation of advice when the patient disagrees - the conversation that must be in the record; (c) visual-field standards after hemianopia - the field-perimetry criteria and compenstraining nuance and the practical-assessment referral for suitable cases, with the OT and optometry shared roles; and (d) the episodic-incapacitation rules: attack-free intervals for private entitlement, no commercial licensing while attacks persist, and the honest counselling that "rare" and "unpredictable" are different things. In all four: the duty is to advise, document and notify where the law requires, never to police by force - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**
