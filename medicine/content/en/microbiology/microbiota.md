The human microbiota is an organ you borrow: **some hundred trillion organisms supplying metabolic, barrier and immunological services whose disruption tracks with an almost suspicious number of diseases - while the one proven, repeatable, mechanism-based therapy for its acute collapse, faecal microbiota transplantation, remains the field's most striking success and its most overhyped promise.** This chapter covers normal microbiology, the antibiotics-microbiota interface, Clostridioides difficile, and the evidence-graded frontiers.

This chapter covers the resident microbiota and its functions, dysbiosis concepts, C. difficile infection, microbiota-based therapeutics and the diet, probiotic and faecal-transplant evidence base.

::: summary
- The microbiota is a metabolic and immunological organ; antibiotics are its most common environmental insult and the ecological cost is measurable for weeks to years.
- Dysbiosis associations are graded: C. difficile, IBD and hepatic encephalopathy have therapeutic proof; most brain, immune and metabolic claims remain associative.
- C. difficile is managed as a recurrence continuum - severity staging, fidaxomicin or vancomycin strategies, bezlotoxumab, and microbiota restoration early in the recurrence series.
- Never test for cure, never treat asymptomatic carriage, and treat ileus with negative stool as severe disease; anti-motility drugs worsen colitis.
- Faecal microbiota transplantation is the proof of principle that restoring an ecosystem cures a disease; it is also the cautionary tale about unregulated delivery and infection transmission.
- Probiotics are strain-specific medicines, not health food: proven in narrow niches, potentially harmful in fragile patients, and always inferior to the antibiotic-stewardship they advertise themselves against.
:::
