Virology rewards a structural way of thinking: **genome type decides replication strategy, replication strategy decides the drugs, and the host's response to an infected cell decides most of the symptoms - which is why "viral" illness feels the way it does and why only a handful of the hundreds of human viruses have treatments.** This chapter walks virus biology through the infections you actually manage.

This chapter covers virus structure and replication, antiviral principles, the respiratory and systemic RNA viruses, herpes and DNA viruses, hepatitis viruses, and the special cases of HIV, measles and emerging viruses.

::: summary
- Genome type is destiny: replication strategy determines tropism, mutation rate, persistence pattern and which drug classes can exist.
- Symptoms are mostly immunity: acute viral illness is interferon and dying epithelium, which is why timing of antivirals (before the peak) is everything.
- Latency rewrites the natural history - herpesviruses guarantee lifelong exposure; immunosuppression selects which virus reactivates and where.
- Drift versus shift explains influenza's annual versus occasional-pandemic behaviour; reassortment of segmented genomes is the shift mechanism.
- Vaccines, not drugs, conquered most human viruses; where drugs exist (HBV, HCV, HIV, herpes, influenza, SARS-CoV-2) they reflect specific viral enzymes discovered by structural biology.
- The laboratory does the diagnosis - PCR panels, antigen tests, serology timing and sequencing - with pre-test probability and specimen choice as the clinical skills.
:::
