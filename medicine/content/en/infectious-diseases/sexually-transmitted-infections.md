Sexual health medicine is general medicine with the history taken properly: **most STI syndromes are a handful of organisms in repeatable patterns, testing is cheap and good, treatment is short, and the difficult part is the conversation - confidentiality, partner notification, consent, power, coercion and stigma determine whether the pharmacology ever happens.** A clinician who can ask about sex without flinching and examine without shaming has covered half the syllabus.

This chapter covers consultation and testing principles, the ulcerative and discharge syndromes, viral STIs, HIV as an STI, and public health control.

## The unfinished agenda

Three honest footnotes close the chapter. First, syndromic management still rules much of the world: where NAATs and culture are unavailable, WHO algorithms of coloured cards treat presumptively on signs, and they save lives and cause overtreatment in the same breath - the right response is investment in affordable point-of-care testing (the mycoplasma and gonorrhoea assays now landing) rather than moralising about the choice. Second, STI stigma is a clinical determinant: people delay, partners go untraced, and clinics that feel like inquisitions produce worse public health than clinics that feel like pharmacies; every quality improvement that shortens the distance between "I think I have something" and "I have been treated" is an intervention with a number-needed-to-tell. Third, antimicrobial resistance in Neisseria gonorrhoeae is the canary that sings for all of infectious disease - an organism that has sequentially defeated every drug class tried against it, against a background of asymptomatic pharyngeal carriage acting as a global mixing vessel; the last-resort cephalosporin will eventually join the list, and the race between that date and a gonococcal vaccine is the most consequential single contest in this chapter's future.


## Confidentiality, adolescents and the record

Two recurring dilemmas deserve rehearsal because every clinician meets them weekly. The adolescent consultation is governed by competence, not by a birthday: the under-16 who understands the decision, cannot be persuaded to tell a parent, and will otherwise go without care or go elsewhere unadvised, can be treated confidentially on the Fraser principles - document the reasoning, offer the parent-involvement conversation, keep the safeguarding lens open for exploitation, and remember that confidentiality has named limits (risk of serious harm, safeguarding duties) that must be stated at the start, not discovered at the breach ([[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[medical-ethics-law/confidentiality]], [[paediatrics/safeguarding]]). The adult disclosure dilemma - a patient who refuses to inform a partner with a newly diagnosed infection - has a graduated answer: explore the barriers and offer to notify through health advisers or anonymous SMS services, escalate through senior and public-health advice when there is ongoing high-risk exposure, and treat forced disclosure as an exceptional, documented, proportionate decision taken with senior, legal and public-health input, never as a solo act in a closing consultation ([[public-health/communicable-disease-control]], [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]], [[clinical-skills/records-handover]]).

One prevention detail belongs to every puerperium and every family-planning visit rather than to the sexual-health clinic: antenatal syphilis and HIV screening, with reliable result circulation and third-trimester re-screening where prevalence or risk justify it, has near-eliminated congenital syphilis and vertical HIV in systems that do it properly, and every case that slips through traces back to a broken workflow, never to biology.

The record itself is an STI instrument. Site-of-exposure notes make the next clinician sample correctly; window-period dates make the next negative result interpretable; partner-number-and-notification status makes audit possible; and a routine flag for vaccination status turns every future attendance into a catch-up opportunity. Conversely, the vague note - "discharge, treated" - without sites, dates, results or partner status, guarantees that the next clinician repeats the whole hunt or, worse, guesses from the wrong sex. Good STI notes read like a public-health dataset with a person in them, because that is exactly what they are - a dataset other clinicians, advisers and auditors will rely on when the patient cannot tell the story herself.

**STI control is as old as public health and as contested as personal liberty: the biblical quarantine of skin disease and the levitical laundry policy as proto-infection control; the great syphilis panic after 1495 and Fracastoro's poem that named it, followed by centuries of mercurial cure-that-tormented; the Contagious Diseases Acts of the 1860s in Britain and their repeal after Josephine Butler's campaign - the founding case that state regulation of women's bodies was both unethical and ineffective, a precedent that still frames STI ethics - [[medical-ethics-law/justice-resources]], [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]]; the gonorrhoea-and-syphilis-therapeutic-revolutions - Salvarsan's-aromatic-arsenic-then-penicillin's-total-conversion-of-syphilis-treatment and the epidemiological intelligence of the serological tests with their false-positive-lupus-antibody-adjacencies - [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]], [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]; the venereal-disease-clinic-model-of-the-World-Wars-era contact-tracing-machines and their moralised confidentiality compromises; the HPV story - zur-Hausen's-cancer-virus-deduction-maintained-through-decades-of-disbelief-to-the-Nobel-and-the-vaccines-and-the-screening-programmes-that-are-now-actually-eliminating-cervical-cancer-in-organized-countries - [[pathology/cancer-biology]], [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]]; the antibiotic-resistance chase in gonorrhoea - silver-proontosil_then_tetracycline_then_penicillin_then_fluoroquinolone_then_cephalosporin - the fast-evolving-organism that forces reinvestment in vaccines and stewardship - [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[infectious-diseases/stewardship-outbreaks]]; and the PrEP and U=U era that turned HIV prevention from a moral test into a prescription - [[infectious-diseases/hiv]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**

::: summary
- The sexual history is a clinical instrument: five Ps, confidentiality explained, interpreters and chaperones offered, coercion screened, and dates written down.
- Sample every exposed site; blood does not diagnose chlamydia or gonorrhoea, and the missed pharyngeal swab is the commonest cause of apparent treatment failure.
- Treat syndromes to cover both chlamydia and gonorrhoea when likelihood is high, then de-escalate on results; resistance in gonorrhoea is a permanent emergency.
- Syphilis serology is a two-test story and an interpretation, not a number; pregnancy makes every discordant result urgent.
- Ulcer plus node plus travel or network history needs HSV PCR, treponemal pairing and an eye on LGV, chancroid and donovanosis.
- Vaccination (HPV, hepatitis A and B), partner notification, screening offers and PrEP are the clinical acts that end outbreaks; the prescription is the easy part.
:::

## Taking it further

The infectious-diseases mindset in one paragraph: think in source, route, host and time. Identify the source before naming the syndrome; ask what the exposure history permits and what the local resistance map makes likely; adjust everything for the host standing in front of you; and treat the clock as a drug - the sepsis, endocarditis and meningitis outcomes are functions of hours. Then audit yourself against the two failure modes of the specialty: anchoring on the first plausible organism while the atypical presentation evolves, and de-escalating never - the narrow-spectrum switch that should happen on day three and does not. Both failures are curable by the habit of re-asking, at every review, what the story said yesterday that it no longer says today.
