Arrhythmias are the electrophysiology of [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]] turned pathological: ordinary circuits misused (re-entry), ordinary cells firing improperly (enhanced automaticity and triggered activity), or ordinary channels mutated (the channelopathies). The clinical architecture follows three questions in order — **is the rhythm dangerous now (the unstable patient gets electricity), why is it happening (substrate, trigger, modulator: ischaemia, valve disease, thyroid, drug, gene), and how do we stop it recurring (rate and rhythm control, ablation, devices, and the stroke and sudden-death prevention that matter more than the rhythm itself in the long run)**.

This chapter covers mechanisms, the atrial and ventricular tachycardias, bradycardia and block (whose pacing logic was built in the ECG chapter), syncope, and sudden cardiac death.

## Mechanisms: three ways to misfire

::: definition Re-entry, automaticity, triggered activity {#def-mechanisms}
**Re-entry** — the impulse circulating a closed (or functionally closed) path — needs **two pathways with different refractoriness and conduction velocities, unidirectional block in one, and slow enough conduction that the other has recovered when the wavefront arrives** (the refractory-gradient arithmetic of [[physiology/membrane-potentials]]'s excitability cycle and [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]'s excitable-gap logic). The classic demonstrations: **atrioventricular nodal re-entrant tachycardia (AVNRT — the dual AV-nodal physiology, a "slow–fast" circuit in 90 % of cases, the discontinuity on the atrial extra-stimulus curve in the widget), atrioventricular re-entrant tachycardia through an accessory pathway (the WPW circuit of [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]), atrial flutter (the cavotricuspid isthmus macro-re-entry — the ECG chapter's example), peri-infarct ventricular tachycardia (the scar channels of [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]'s remodelling theorem), and atrial fibrillation (multiple wavelets and rotors maintained by atrial stretch, fibrosis and short refractory periods — the "fibrillation begets fibrillation" self-substrate)**. **Enhanced normal or abnormal automaticity** — depolarised or diseased cells firing their own phase-4 (the junctional escape and its acceleration, the atrial and ventricular ectopic foci, the **digoxin-toxicity double** of increased automaticity plus depressed conduction, and the catecholamine-driven triggered automaticity of exercise-induced ventricular ectopy). **Triggered activity** — afterdepolarisations that reach threshold: **early afterdepolarisations (EADs) at long action potentials and slow rates (the pause-dependent, short-coupled torsades de pointes of the congenital and drug long-QT — the reverse use-dependence that makes sotalol and dofetilide proarrhythmic at low heart rates and why pacing and magnesium and isoprenaline rescue them), and delayed afterdepolarisations (DADs) from calcium-overloaded sarcoplasmic reticulum (the digitalis and catecholamine and the outflow-tract and idiopathic ventricular tachycardia syndromes — the right ventricular outflow tract's cAMP-mediated focal automaticity cured by ablation)**. The **arrhythmia triangle — substrate (scar, stretch, hypertrophy, mutation), trigger (ectopy, pause, catecholamine, rate), and modulators (ischaemia, electrolytes, autonomic tone, drugs)** — is the reasoning template for every case below and for the ECG chapter's emergencies ([[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]] and [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]] supplying the modulators).
:::

## Atrial fibrillation: the common sustained arrhythmia

::: definition AF: mechanisms, stroke, and the three management axes {#def-af}
**Atrial fibrillation** — organised atrial rates above ~350/min producing chaotic mechanical standstill and an irregular ventricular response — arises from **triggers (the pulmonary-vein sleeves' ectopy — the embryological vein–atrium cuff tissue and the reason pulmonary-vein isolation is the ablation operation) acting on a substrate of atrial stretch, fibrosis, inflammation and refractory shortening (hypertension, the valvar and failure atria of [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]], obesity and sleep apnoea, alcohol, thyrotoxicosis — [[endocrine/thyroid]], age and the epicardial fat and autonomic ganglia)**, with the paroxysmal → persistent → long-standing-persistent and permanent natural history driven by the atrium's own remodelling ([[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]'s renin–angiotensin and [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]'s fibrosis as substrate amplifiers). Management runs three axes: **(1) Rate versus rhythm: rate control (beta-blocker, digoxin's nodal potentiation for the sedentary and the failure patient — digoxin-toxicity-adjacent ([[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]), the non-dihydropyridine calcium-blocker in the structurally normal heart, and ablate-and-pace with a resynchronisation or His-bundle-pacing backplane for the refractory — [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]'s block chapters) versus rhythm control (the electrical cardioversion with its anticoagulation-window rules — transoesophageal-echo-guided or three-weeks-therapeutic, the pill-in-the-pocket strategy for the infrequent paroxysms, the antiarrhythmic drugs chosen by substrate — no structural disease: flecainide or propafenone with an atrioventricular-nodal blocker to prevent the 1:1-flutter disaster; structural and coronary and failure disease: amiodarone and dofetilide — the Vaughan-Williams classification's pharmacology home in the drug chapters), and catheter ablation with the rhythm-and-heart-failure outcome evidence updated by the early-rhythm-control trials (the EAST-AFNET 4 finding of cardiovascular benefit from treating within a year of diagnosis) and the AF-and-failure ablation trials' signal (the CASTLE-AF line)); (2) Stroke prevention — the embolic stroke of the left atrial appendage thrombus (the spontaneous echo contrast and the appendage's pectinate architecture) risk-scored (the CHA₂DS₂-VA–style scoring and its modern replacement by direct risk-factor lists as the guidelines migrate off the score's gender artefact), and prevented with **direct oral anticoagulants (the factor-Xa and thrombin inhibitors of [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]'s pharmacology — dosed by renal function, age and weight, with the antiplatelet-combination caps the trials set) or the left-atrial-appendage occlusion devices for the genuinely anticoagulation-intolerant, and the mechanical-valve and moderate-to-severe-rheumatic mitral stenosis exceptions (the warfarin-only cohorts of [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]]); (3) Risk-factor and upstream management — the weight, alcohol, blood pressure, sleep apnoea and exercise evidence (the REVERSE-RAFT and LEGACY-era risk-factor trials and the obesity-phenotype ablations: the soft axis with the hardest recurrence effects).** The clinical traps: **the pre-excited fibrillation (ECG chapter's contraindication), the rate-related cardiomyopathy of years of uncontrolled ventricular response (a reversible failure cause — [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]'s tachycardia-mediated list), the regular ventricular rhythm during AF (the digoxin-toxicity or complete-block giveaway), and the "lone" AF in the young (the thyroid and the alcoholic holiday and the vagal and adrenergic patterns — and the increasingly-found atrial-genetics question, [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]]).**
:::

::: example The palpitations at the concert {#ex-svt}
A 26-year-old postgraduate has four episodes this year of sudden-onset regular pounding at rest resolving after 20 minutes of sitting still; during one episode a paramedic recorded a regular narrow-complex tachycardia at 190; she is well between episodes with a normal ECG and normal bloods.
::: solution
**Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia, almost certainly AVNRT** (the young-woman predominance, the sudden on/off, the regular narrow complex, the neck pounding of atrial contraction against a closed AV valve during the circuit — the cannon waves of [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]] made symptomatic). The work-up ladder: **capture (a smart-watch or loop-recorder tracing of an episode — the ambulatory-monitoring era has demoted the empirical tablet), then the choices by frequency and preference: (1) self-terminated — teach the vagal manoeuvres (the modified Valsalva's enhanced success and the refreshed-strain technique) with a documented safety net; (2) treat acute episodes in clinic or emergency department with adenosine (the EC chapter's diagnostic-and-therapeutic tool — [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]) or DC where necessary; (3) prevent — the as-needed pill-in-the-pocket (the beta-blocker or the class Ic plus nodal blocker once pre-excitation and ischaemia are excluded), long-term beta- or calcium-blocker or the drug-free option that most young patients choose: **catheter ablation of the slow pathway (>95 % success, a small nodal-block and recurrence conversation, and the definitive answer the patient's age argues for)**. The between-episode ECG's job: **exclude the delta wave (the WPW that changes every drug decision — [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]'s example), the long QT and the Brugada and early-repolarisation signatures, and the pre-excitations that make adenosine-for-undifferentiated-tachycardia a monitored decision.**
:::
:::

::: example The old man who faints shaving {#ex-carpark}
A 78-year-old, twice fallen in the bath and once down at the car wash while shaving his neck, has pauses on ambulatory ECG of 4.2 seconds during which he felt the light go; daytime pressures show a 30/15 postural fall and he takes doxazosin and amlodipine.
::: solution
**Mixed syncope: carotid-sinus hypersensitivity plus drug-contributed orthostatic hypotension, with a documented symptomatic pause.** The physiology: **an over-responsive carotid sinus baroreceptor arc ([[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]] and [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]) — afferents from the sinus nerve of Hering along the glossopharyngeal to the nucleus tractus solitarius, efferent vagal (cardioinhibitory — the pause) and sympathetic-withdrawal (vasodepressor — the pressure fall) limbs, exaggerated with age, atherosclerosis and neck stimuli; the doxazosin's α-blockade removing what vascular compensation remains ([[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]] and [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]'s orthostatic exercise).** The sequence: **stop the doxazosin and re-measure (the vasodepressor component may be wholly drug-made), the ECG and monitoring correlation of symptoms and pauses (the 4-second symptomatic pause with a cardioinhibitory phenotype), carotid-sinus massage in a monitored, resuscitation-ready setting for the reproducible response (the classic 3-second-asystole-plus-50-mmHg definition, done only when the diagnosis will change management), and the pacing decision — dual-chamber (closed-loop-stimulation) pacing for the symptomatic cardioinhibitory subtype, with the honest caveat that pacing fixes the pause and not the pressure fall (the vasodepressor fraction stays faint-ish, which is why the drug and physical-counterpressure and compression measures come first).** The general syncope law the case teaches: **treat the mechanism that can be treated, remove the drug that mimics disease, and never pace a reflex before the doxazosin audit is closed ([[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]'s own indications document and [[geriatrics-palliative/falls]]).**
:::
:::

::: example The eighty-year-old's fast irregular rhythm {#ex-af-old}
An 81-year-old care-home man is admitted with a week of cough; he is in fast atrial fibrillation at 148, BP 104/70, saturating 94 %, febrile with a right lower-lobe consolidation; NT-proBNP and troponin are mildly raised; no chest pain; echo shows EF 55 % and a normal-sized left atrium.
::: solution
**Atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response driven by sepsis — the type-2 troponin and the peptide rise are passengers, not captains.** The reasoning: **the infection (fever, catecholamines, hypoxia, inflammation) is the trigger on an aged conduction system ([[#def-mechanisms]]'s modulator arm); this is not "new primary AF needing rhythm control" — it is rate-and-cause management with the anticoagulation decision done soberly.** Management: **treat the pneumonia (antibiotics, oxygen, fluids judiciously — the [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]] bundle); rate-control gently (the IV beta-blocker or digoxin loading with the blood-pressure and the septic-myocardium caveats — the combination's hypotension arithmetic; aim symptom-and-rate control, not sinus conversion), replete magnesium and potassium ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]), avoid empirical amiodarone boluses in the vasodilated, and expect spontaneous conversion or acceptable rates as the fever settles.** **Troponin and BNP: serial, not alarming — the demand-injury and rate-strain patterns of [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]] and [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]'s peptide confounders.** **Anticoagulation: score the stroke risk (his age alone qualifies), weigh the bleeding risk and the falls-and-capacity-and-goals context ([[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]), start a direct oral anticoagulant when the acute illness and renal function declare themselves, and record the recurrence pattern for the outpatient follow-up (the sepsis-provoked AF's substantial long-term recurrence and stroke statistics — the "it converted, stop the tablets" mistake)**. Conversion, if it fails and the rate stays refractory, is a days-not-minutes elective decision after the [[#def-af]] cardioversion safety rules.
:::
:::

::: definition Ventricular tachycardia, fibrillation and the unstable patient {#def-vf}
**Ventricular tachycardia** (three or more ventricular beats at >100/min, wide and regular) is **scar-related re-entry (the peri-infarct channel — monomorphic VT in a known infarct is scar until proven otherwise), channelopathic (the catecholamine-sensitive bidirectional and exercise-coupled tachycardias — [[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]'s catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia and long-QT subtypes), or outflow-tract and idiopathic (the adenosine-sensitive, radiofrequency-curable foci)** until proven otherwise, and **its first treatment in the unstable patient is synchronised DC cardioversion with sedation when time allows** (the defibrillator's asynchronous shock for the pulseless or polymorphic — the [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]] algorithm's two switches), while the stable patient gets **procainamide, sotalol or amiodarone (the amiodarone-versus-procainamide trial's verdict favouring procainamide's acute efficacy in the stable wide complex) and then the substrate work-up and the secondary-prevention device.** **Polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with a normal QT is ischaemia until proven otherwise (the acute-occlusion arrhythmia — catheter laboratory); with a prolonged QT it is torsades (pause- and bradycardia-dependent: magnesium first-line even when the serum magnesium is normal, overdrive pacing and isoprenaline and potassium-high-normal and withdrawal of the culprit — the ECG chapter's list); with a short QT and family history it is the rare short-QT syndrome.** **Ventricular fibrillation** — the arrest rhythm (the shockable pair with pulseless ventricular tachycardia) — has its epidemiology, survival chain and ICD secondary-prevention rules in the resuscitation chapters; the electrophysiologist's jobs are **the reversible causes (ischaemia, electrolytes, drug, the acquired long-QT and the post-arrest-targeted-temperature and reperfusion arithmetic of [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]] and [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]) and the storm (the sympathetic amplifier — beta-blockade (the left-stellate-block and thoracic-epidural and surgical-sympathectomy ancient-modern ladder), deep sedation, and urgent ablation of the triggering and substrate foci, with mechanical support where the shocks are many).** The antiarrhythmic pharmacology's organ ledger — **amiodarone's thyroid (the hypo- and hyper- of the iodine-loaded molecule — [[endocrine/thyroid]]), lung (the chronic pneumonitis and organising patterns — [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]), liver, cornea, skin and nerve, and its CYP-and-P-glycoprotein interactions (the warfarin and digoxin dose reductions it forces — [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]])** — belongs to the drug chapters and to every prescribing conversation about it.
:::

::: example The athlete's aborted death {#ex-arvc}
A 22-year-old basketball player collapses in training; the witnessed shockable arrest is survived; the ECG after resuscitation shows T-wave inversion in V1–V3 with an epsilon wave, the MRI shows a fibrofatty right ventricular wall with regional akinesia, and his uncle "died young in his sleep".
::: solution
**Arrhythmogenic right ventricular cardiomyopathy (ARVC) with resuscitated sudden death** — the inherited desmosomal disease (plakoglobin and desmoplakin and the plakophilin and junction lines — autosomal-dominant with variable penetrance: [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]'s pedigree, [[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]'s variable-expression lesson) in which **myocyte–cell-coupling failure under mechanical stress gives myocyte death, fibrofatty replacement and the re-entrant substrate of exercise-triggered ventricular tachycardia — the mechanism behind the "athlete died young" statistics (the young-athlete sudden-death differentials: hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (the sarcomere's [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]]-discussed asymmetric septum and its myocyte disarray), the anomalous coronary ostia of [[anatomy/thorax]], the myocarditis, the channelopathies and commotio cordis).** Management: **secondary-prevention ICD (the class-one, unquestioned one), exercise restriction counselling with the contested shared-decision era (the living-guidelines argument about competitive sport — the ethics of autonomy ([[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]), beta-blockade and catheter ablation for recurrent shocks (the endo-epicardial substrate approach; the disease progresses so ablation is adjunctive), the family cascade (ECG and MRI and genetics screening — the proband's variant the anchor, [[genetics/genetic-testing]]), and the athletic-screening and defibrillator-access public-health debate ([[public-health/prevention-promotion]]).** The chapter's exam hook: **the epsilon wave (the post-excitation notch from delayed right-ventricular activation) and T inversion beyond V1 in an adult athlete — both normal-ish in V1 and both abnormal beyond it — the same reading discipline as the Brugada and early-repolarisation patterns of [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]].**
:::

The rationale for pulmonary-vein isolation in atrial fibrillation is:

**Haïssaguerre's demonstration that paroxysmal AF usually fires from ectopic sleeves of myocardium extending into the pulmonary veins (embryological atrial cuffs — [[anatomy/thorax]]) made the veins the target: the circumferential antral isolation (balloon or point-by-point) eliminates the triggers**, which is why success is best in younger, paroxysmal, less-remodelled atria and worse in long-standing persistent disease (the substrate has taken over — the "begets fibrillation" progression of [[#def-af]]), and why the procedure is rhythm control, not stroke prevention: **anticoagulation decisions continue on risk scores after an apparently successful ablation** ([[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]).

::: history
The mechanistic century: **Mayer and Wenckebach and Lewis's** circus-movement and clinical-mapping experiments (the flutter circuits of the 1920s — the ECG chapter's history), **Schmitt and Erlanger's** re-entry diagrams with the refractory-period geometry, **Rosenblueth's** and then **Moe and Abildskov's** multiple-wavelet mathematics of fibrillation, **Wit and Bigger's** and **Surawicz's and Rosen's** triggered-activity and digitalis-arrhythmia electrophysiology, and **El-Sherif's and the Spear-and-Moore** reperfusion-arrhythmia era; **the catecholamine and cyclic-nucleotide understanding of the triggered arrhythmias and the torsades-clinical-trial era of the 1990s (the d-sotalol failure and the DIAMOND and EMIAT and CASH trials of the automatic-defibrillators' dawn — Moss and Schwartz and Vincent and Keating's molecular long-QT lineage — [[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]); the atrial-fibrillation surgery of Cox's maze procedure and Haïssaguerre's 1998 pulmonary-vein triggers (the mechanism-to-operation pipeline of the quiz) and the ablation-tools era (the cryoballoon, the pulsed field)**; **the device era (Mirowski's capacitor and the Sudden Death and MADIT and SCD-HeFT primary-prevention programmes and the subcutaneous-defibrillator generation)** and **the Holter and loop-recorder and telephone-transmission monitoring lineage (the syncope diagnosis's technological revolution)** complete the chapter's toolkit history.
:::

## Where this leads

The ECG patterns and emergencies in [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]; the refractory and action-potential biophysics in [[physiology/membrane-potentials]]; autonomic modulation in [[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]]; anticoagulation pharmacology in [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]; the heart-failure interface in [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]; the ischaemic substrate in [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]; channelopathy and cardiomyopathy genetics in [[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]; resuscitation and defibrillation in [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]]; syncope's falls overlap in [[geriatrics-palliative/falls]].

::: summary
- Three mechanisms explain the tachyarrhythmias: re-entry (the circuits — AVNRT, AVRT, flutter, scar VT, fibrillation), abnormal automaticity and triggered afterdepolarisations (the EAD/torsades and DAD/digitalis-and-catecholamine syndromes); substrate–trigger–modulator is the case-analysis template.
- Atrial fibrillation is managed on three axes — rate or rhythm (with ablation and early-control evidence strengthening), anticoagulation by stroke risk (the appendage's emboli and the direct-oral-agent era), and upstream risk factors — never on the rhythm alone.
- Ventricular tachycardia is a scar, channel or focal diagnosis; instability means synchronised electricity, polymorphic-with-prolonged-QT means magnesium and rate, storm means sympathetic blockade and ablation.
- Syncope is a mechanism triage (reflex, orthostatic, cardiac); the carotid-sinus and pause stories are baroreflex physiology with a drug list attached, and pacing treats only the cardioinhibitory limb.
- Sudden death in the young is cardiomyopathy, channelopathy or anomaly until proven otherwise: the ICD, the genetics cascade and the family are as much the treatment as the shock.
:::

::: exercise Mechanism spotting {level=1}
Assign the mechanism (re-entry / triggered / automatic) to: AVNRT, digoxin-toxic ventricular ectopy, torsades, outflow-tract ventricular tachycardia, atrial flutter, pause-dependent early-afterdepolarisation ectopy.
::: solution
**Re-entry: AVNRT and atrial flutter** (the fixed circuits — the discontinuity-curve substrate and the isthmus-dependent macro-re-entry — both catheter-curable because the circuit is anatomically defined). **Triggered, delayed afterdepolarisations: the digoxin-toxic ectopy (the calcium-overload afterdepolarisations of the heart-failure chapter's digoxin example ([[cardiovascular/heart-failure]])) and the exercise-coupled bidirectional ventricular tachycardia of catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia ([[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]'s ryanodine-receptor disease).** **Triggered, early afterdepolarisations: torsades and the pause-dependent ectopy of long QT (the reverse-use-dependence logic of [[#def-mechanisms]]).** **Focal automaticity or triggered micro-circuits: the outflow-tract ventricular tachycardia (the adenosine-sensitive cAMP mechanism, ablatable).** The exercise's point: **mechanism predicts the treatment — electricity for re-entry, magnesium and rate and drug-withdrawal for triggered, beta-blockade and ablation for the automatic and catecholaminergic.**

::: exercise Score and prescribe {level=1}
Compute the stroke-risk verdict for a 72-year-old woman with hypertension, diabetes and no prior stroke (CHA₂DS₂-VA-style terms), and state the anticoagulation recommendation and the two situations where a direct oral anticoagulant is the wrong drug.
::: solution
**Risk factors: female sex (1 — and the score's own deprecation of this — the modern risk-factor lists ask sex-adjusted questions), hypertension (1), age 65–74 (1), diabetes (1) = ≥3: anticoagulation clearly indicated (the threshold being ≥2 in men, ≥3 in women historically, with female sex alone never an indication).** Recommendation: **a direct oral anticoagulant (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran or edoxaban — dose-adjusted for renal function, weight and age — [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]'s pharmacology) over warfarin unless contraindicated.** The two direct-oral-anticoagulant-wrong situations: **the mechanical prosthetic valve (and the moderate-to-severe rheumatic mitral stenosis) — warfarin-only cohorts — and severe renal failure with the dose-floor problem plus antiphospholipid syndrome (the thrombotic-phenotype trial failures — [[immunology/hypersensitivity]]-adjacent and [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]]'s valve-and-pregnancy anticoagulation discussion)**; the other near-exclusions are the cost-and-adherence reality and the interacting drugs (the strong P-glycoprotein-and-CYP3A4 inducers and inhibitors — [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]]).

::: exercise Rate or rhythm, and when {level=2}
A 55-year-old with first-detection AF at 4 months, EF 45 % and disabling palpitations prefers rhythm control. Give the drug choices by substrate, the cardioversion safety rule, and the ablation argument.
::: solution
**Substrate-based drugs:** no structural disease beyond the mild rate-related EF reduction (the tachycardia-mediated component makes recovery plausible — [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]): **flecainide or propafenone (always with a nodal blocker — the 1:1-conducted atrial flutter it can create without one is the syllabus trap) or dronedarone (the non-iodinated amiodarone cousin with its heart-failure and permanent-AF exclusions), or sotalol and dofetilide (the QT and inpatient-initiation caveats — the ECG chapter's reverse-use-dependence), or amiodarone (effective, and its organ ledger — [[#def-vf]] — argues for the young patient's ablation-first preference).** **Cardioversion safety:** three weeks therapeutic anticoagulation before (or a transoesophageal-echo-excluded appendage thrombus) and four weeks after (the atrial mechanical function's stunning and the post-cardioversion thromboembolic window — independent of CHA₂DS₂-VA score for the periprocedural rule, and continuing after it by the score), plus the thyrotoxicosis and the digoxin and potassium checks before shocking ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]'s toxicity-with-electricity caution). **Ablation argument:** the early-rhythm-control trial data, the high single-procedure success in paroxysmal AF of this duration and atrial size, the possibility of reversing the mild failure phenotype and the patient's age and preference — with the honest recurrence statistics, the complication rates (the tamponade, the rare atrio-oesophageal fistula, the phrenic and the stenosis), and the need to continue anticoagulation by risk score regardless of apparent rhythm success ([[#def-af]]).

::: exercise Amiodarone monitoring {level=2}
List the baseline and annual monitoring for amiodarone, and explain the mechanism of its thyroid toxicity (with the two opposite phenotypes) and its warfarin and digoxin interactions.
::: solution
**Monitoring: thyroid, liver and renal and electrolytes and glucose baseline and 6-monthly-to-annual; chest radiograph and pulmonary function (with the diffusing capacity) and ophthalmology (the corneal microdeposits are near-universal and harmless; the optic neuropathy is rare and stop-the-drug); ECG and blood pressure and skin exam and the annual symptom review for the insidious breathlessness and the ataxia and the photosensitivity; drug-level assays where available for adherence questions.** **Thyroid: the molecule is ~37 % iodine by weight and structurally thyroxine-like — type 1 (iodine-induced hormone synthesis in an autonomous or latent-Graves gland — treated with the antithyroid drugs, the radioiodine-uptake-limited problem) and type 2 (the destructive thyroiditis releasing preformed hormone — the steroid-responsive one), with the hypothyroid phase (the Wolf–Chaikoff failure) commoner in iodine-sufficient regions — [[endocrine/thyroid]]'s and [[immunology/autoimmunity]]'s joint case.** **Interactions: inhibition of CYP2C9, CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein raises warfarin exposure (halve the dose empirically — the INR-monitored negotiation) and digoxin levels (halve; the toxicity-potentiation double of hypokalaemia-independent mechanisms), and it adds to the QT of others while paradoxically rarely causing torsades (the homogeneous action-potential prolongation — [[#def-mechanisms]]'s dispersion concept — [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]], [[pharmacology/drug-receptor]]).**

::: exercise The shock-happy device {level=2}
An ICD patient has six appropriate and two inappropriate shocks in a month. Sort the causes and give the management by category.
::: solution
**Appropriate shocks (true ventricular tachycardia/fibrillation — the device working):** the causes are progression of the substrate (ischaemia, failure, storm), drug failure or non-adherence, and the reversible modulators (the electrolytes, the QT drugs, the dehydration) — management: **interrogation and stored-ECG review of every event, the ischaemia and failure re-audit, antiarrhythmic intensification (amiodarone-plus-beta-blocker, the mexiletine add-on) and urgent ablation for scar-related VT (the VANISH and post-hoc trial logic), with the storm pathway of [[#def-vf]] (sedation, intubation, temporary mechanical support, percutaneous and surgical sympathectomy in extremis).** **Inappropriate shocks:** **atrial-fibrillation or flutter with rapid conduction (the discriminator algorithms' failure in the fast-irregular zone — nodal-ablation-and-pacing or AF ablation the answers), sinus tachycardia undersensed into the rate zone (exercise reprogramming and the rate-onset-discrimination settings), T-wave and myopotential and lead oversensing (the fracture — the shocking-lead-revision and the subcutaneous-device conversation) and noise** — management by category: **reprogramming first, lead revision for the fracture, and the magnet-and-shock-free-padded-bra era of palliation and safety ([[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]]'s device-deactivation ethics belongs here and in the medical-ethics chapters — [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]).** The shared rule: **never let device interrogations and the patient's account run on separate tracks; every shock has an electrogram and a reason ([[clinical-skills/records-handover]]).**

::: exercise Syncope triage {level=2}
Give the three mechanism classes with two discriminators each, and the red flags that admit rather than reassure.
::: solution
**Reflex (neurally mediated):** the prodrome (warmth, nausea, tunnel vision, the prolonged standing and pain and emotion triggers) and the pallid-sweaty recovery; subtypes vasodepressor, cardioinhibitory (the pause of [[#ex-carpark]]) and mixed, with the carotid-sinus and swallowing-and-cough situational variants. **Orthostatic:** the postural timing and the drug, dehydration, autonomic-failure (the neurogenic [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]-exercise syndrome) context and the absence of the protective tachycardia. **Cardiac:** the exertional or supine or triggerless abrupt onset, the palpitation preceding, the structural and family and channelopathy history, the abnormal ECG and murmur, and the injury-without-prodrome ("dropped"). **Red flags (admit or same-day assessment): the syncope during exertion or supine, chest pain or dyspnoea preceding, the family sudden-death history, the abnormal ECG or reduced EF, the severe anaemia or hypoxia or acidosis, the injury, the age over 65 with comorbidity and the recurrent unexplained events** — the ESC syncope-rule architecture; the work-up's ambulatory-monitoring tier (patch, implantable loop recorder) for the infrequent events and the tilt-table's selected role in reflex-syncope labelling ([[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]]).

::: exercise Arrhythmia in pregnancy {level=2}
A 29-year-old at 14 weeks has recurrent SVT; outline the acute and preventive management with the fetal considerations, and the two drug classes to avoid with reasons.
::: solution
**Acute: vagal first, then adenosine (safe — the very-short-half-life and no uterine effect; the same protocol as the non-pregnant — [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]), and synchronised DC cardioversion without hesitation for the unstable (the fetus tolerates the shock better than the maternal hypotension — the monitoring and left-tilt and tocolysis-after viability details aside).** **Preventive: the low-dose beta-blocker (the preferred agents, with the fetal-growth and bradycardia-and-hypoglycaemia surveillance and the placental-transfer pharmacology) or the verapamil alternative; ablation only for the drug-refractory storm, with the abdominal-shielding or zero-fluoroscopy electroanatomic technique when needed ([[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]'s dose logic).** **Avoid: amiodarone (the neonatal thyroid and neurodevelopmental effects of the iodine load — [[endocrine/thyroid]] and [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]] — reserved for life-threatening refractory cases) and the ACE/ARB/neprilysin class and the direct oral anticoagulants (the teratogenic and placental-transfer problems — warfarin-versus-low-molecular-weight heparin negotiation in the anticoagulation-requiring gravida — [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]).** The postpartum layer: the breastfeeding-safety table for the beta-blocker choice, the magnesium-for-torsades and adenosine continuities, and the peripartum cardiomyopathy-AF overlap of the heart-failure chapter ([[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]).

::: exercise Design the sudden-death workup {level=3}
A 19-year-old dies during a football match; the autopsy is near-normal. Design the post-mortem investigation programme, the family pathway, and the responsibilities of the club's clinician.
::: solution
**Post-mortem: the specialised cardiac pathologist's examination (the blocks and the conduction system and the subtle ARVC and myocarditis and early HCM sampling — the "near-normal heart" is a sampling and histology and genetics question, not an ending), toxicology and biochemistry (the stimulant and supplement screens and the tryptase and metabolic stored-product tests), and **the retention of material for molecular testing (the panel-and-targeted approach to the channelopathy and cardiomyopathy genes — the post-mortem genetic analysis of negative-autopsy sudden-death cohorts finds an actionable variant in a substantial minority — [[genetics/genetic-testing]]'s forensic-adjacent territory).** **Family pathway: the cascade — first-degree ECG and echo and, where a variant is found, predictive testing with genetic counselling (the borderline-phenotype ethics: the athlete's eligibility, the insurance and the reproductive questions — [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]'s autonomy discussion and the predictive-testing-in-minors limits); the family's own screening for the shared substrate (the ARVC and HCM phenotypes can declare late) and the bereavement support with its arrhythmia-specific guilt-managing content.** **Club and community responsibilities: the emergency-action-plan audit (the AED location, the minutes-to-shock chain that made this a survived-possible event — [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]] and [[public-health/prevention-promotion]]'s public-access-defibrillation evidence), the return-to-play policy for team-mates if a shared cause (the commotio and viral-myocarditis screening week), and the documentation and duty-of-care communication.** The physiology frame: **the sudden death in a structurally normal young heart is usually a channel or a coupling mutation unmasked by the catecholamine and pH and repolarisation reserve of maximal exercise — the same [[#def-mechanisms]] triangle, running in a family tree.**
