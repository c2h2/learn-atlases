Heart failure is the syndrome in which the heart fails its two jobs — generating output and accepting return without excessive filling pressures — and its two clinical faces, **congestion and hypoperfusion**. The modern concept (the fourth universal definition's and the ESC and ACC/AHA guidelines' architecture) is three-layered: a **stage** (A–D, from risk to refractory disease, mostly irreversible forward), a **phenotype by ejection fraction** (reduced, mildly reduced, preserved — partly a measurement convention, mostly a genuine pathophysiological divide), and an **aetiology** (ischaemic, hypertensive, valvar, toxic, genetic, infiltrative, inflammatory, peripartum — the question that changes management most and the one most often left unanswered). Around the phenotype sits the **neurohormonal-mechanical spiral** — the body's own compensations of [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]] becoming the disease's engine — and the four-pillar pharmacology that interrupts it, the most successful translation of physiology into mortality benefit in adult medicine.

This chapter covers the pathophysiology of the failing pump, the clinical syndrome and its diagnosis, the four pillars and the decongestion craft, and the advanced and palliative ends of the arc.

## The spiral: from molecule to syndrome

::: definition The failing ventricle and its adaptations {#def-spiral}
Any aetiology that kills myocytes (infarct — [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]), loads pressure (aortic stenosis, hypertension), loads volume (regurgitation, shunts, anaemia and obesity's high-output demand), poisons contraction (alcohol, the anthracyclines and trastuzumab of [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]-adjacent oncology, cocaine), infiltrates (amyloid — the AL of [[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]] and the ATTR of the old and the under-recognised, iron, sarcoid), inflames (viral and giant-cell myocarditis) or mutates the sarcomere (the dilated and hypertrophic cardiomyopathies' genetics — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]] and [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]]'s HCM discussion) ends at the same cellular physiology: **disrupted excitation–contraction coupling (the [[physiology/muscle]] calcium-handling failing — SERCA down, leaky ryanodine receptors, slow sodium-calcium exchange), impaired energetics (the failing heart's substrate shift and ATP-transport failure — [[cell-biochemistry/energy-metabolism]]'s accounting in deficit), cytoskeletal and extracellular-matrix remodelling (the collagen and stiffness), and myocyte death and fetal-gene reprogramming (the [[cell-biochemistry/signalling-cell-cycle]] and [[genetics/genomic-medicine]] stress-hypertrophy programme)**. The whole-organ response is the spiral: **falling ejection and rising volumes → wall stress (Laplace again — radius begets radius) → baroreceptor-unloaded sympathetic surge, renin–angiotensin–aldosterone activation and vasopressin and natriuretic-peptide counter-regulation → sodium and water retention and arteriolar constriction that restore pressure at the price of filling pressure (congestion) and afterload (more failure), while aldosterone and catecholamines remodel myocardium, kidney and vasculature (the fibrosis, hypokalaemia and arrhythmia milieux) → the Frank–Starling curve flattened and descended (the widget), the EDPVR steepened (congestion at normal volumes), the ventricle spherical and the mitral annulus dilated (functional regurgitation — the loop that begets itself, [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]]'s PV-loop disease)**. The **natriuretic peptides (BNP and NT-proBNP — myocardial-wall-stress hormones, the diagnostic biomarkers and the therapeutic peptide that neprilysin and its inhibitor sacubitril protect)** are this spiral's biochemical shadow: useful numbers with their own confounders (the obesity that lowers, the age, renal impairment, atrial fibrillation and pulmonary hypertension that raise — exercise-built reasoning).
:::

::: definition Four phenotypes, four diseases wearing one name {#def-phenotypes}
**HFrEF** (reduced ejection fraction, ≤40 %): the low-output, dilated, neurohormonally-hot phenotype with the mortality-proven quadruple therapy of [[#def-treatment]]. **HFpEF** (≥50 %): the **stiff, thick, age-and-hypertension-and-obesity-and-atrial-fibrillation-and-female phenotype** — a chamber (and systemic) compliance disease: the steep EDPVR of [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]] means small volume shifts produce large pressure swings (the flash-oedema physiology, the exertional dyspnoea from an exercise-filling-pressure that rockets while the ejection fraction "looks normal"), on a substrate of coronary microvascular inflammation (the systemic-comorbidity paradigm), titin and collagen stiffening, atrial and ventricular interdependence and chronotropic incompetence — **the phenotype whose trials (the ARB, aldosterone and neprilysin blocks that proved neutral for death) famously failed and whose first signals of benefit came from the metabolic drugs: SGLT2-inhibitors and the GLP-1-agonist and weight-loss trials (the STEP-HFpEF programme — obesity-phenotype improvement even independent of weight)** ([[endocrine/diabetes]]). **HFmrEF** (41–49 %) behaves enough like the reduced side to deserve most of its drugs; **recovering and recovered ejection fraction** (the myocarditic, takotsubo, peripartum and tachycardia-mediated cardiomyopathies) demands the reverse reasoning — time-limited therapy-withdrawal decisions instead of lifelong escalation ([[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]'s stunned myocardium, [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]]'s peripartum cardiomyopathy). Finally the **stage axis (ACC/AHA A–D)**: A (risk, no disease — treat the hypertension and diabetes), B (structure or peptide, no symptoms — the pre-chemotherapy cardio-oncology and post-infarct remodelling clinics), C (symptomatic — the hospital's population), D (refractory — the advanced-therapy and palliative conversation of [[#def-advanced]]), which is the **guideline's way of making cardiology a prevention programme**, and the reason heart failure is the commonest discharge diagnosis of people who were never diagnosed.
:::

::: example The wet and the cold {#ex-acute}
A 72-year-old is brought in gasping, pink froth, BP 186/100, saturating 78 % on air, crackles to the roots, an S3 and a jugular venous pressure to the angle of the jaw; the ECG shows sinus tachycardia and old inferior Q waves.
::: solution
**Acute pulmonary oedema, "warm and wet" (perfusing, congested) hypertensive flash oedema — the acute decompensation whose treatment is the [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]] and [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]] lectures compressed into an hour.** The physiology: a stiff-or-failing ventricle facing an afterload surge (the sympathetic vasoconstriction, often with renal sodium retention and a missed-dose or NSAID or ischaemia or arrhythmia trigger) → wedge pressure rockets → Starling-capillary failure (the [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]] equation running in reverse, plasma into alveoli) → hypoxia and work-of-breathing → more sympathetic surge — the circle to break at as many points as possible, fast: **oxygen and non-invasive ventilation (CPAP/BiPAP — the alveolar recruitment and, elegantly, the reduced preload and afterload of the raised intrathoracic pressure — the [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]] pressures doing haemodynamic work), nitrates sublingual then intravenous (the venous capacitance fill — Guyton's stressed volume moved out of the circulation's central compartment, plus arteriolar afterload relief at higher dose), intravenous loop diuretic (the furosemide double effect — an early venodilation before the natriuresis that is the [[renal/tubular-function]] loop-diuretic pharmacology — and the dose logic: the home-dose × 2.5 rule, the urine-output-at-two-hours as the titration metric, and the resistant-congestion escalation of thiazide-potentiation to metolazone and to ultrafiltration's last resort), the treat-the-cause list (the ECG and troponin for ischaemia, the arrhythmia, the sepsis, the aortic and mitral lesion, the drug history), and the avoid list: morphine (the harm signal), routine oxygen without ventilation support, the beta-blocker started or continued in shock-prone fluid shifts, and the fluid bolus for a blood pressure that is 186.** Position, monitor, the catheter and echo plan for the non-responders, and the discharge prescription that never repeats the admission: the four pillars of [[#def-treatment]], the daily-weight and salt and fluid architecture, the heart-team follow-up — **the readmission curve as the true endpoint of every acute-heart-failure hour.**
:::

## Recognising and labelling the syndrome

::: definition Diagnosis: symptoms, signs, peptides, chambers {#def-diagnosis}
No single test makes the diagnosis: the **pre-test reasoning (the revised heart-failure clinical score and its poor discrimination in primary care, where the symptoms — exertional dyspnoea, fatigue, ankle swelling — are the commonest complaints of the obese, deconditioned, anaemic and pulmonics)** drives the modern algorithm: **natriuretic peptides as the gatekeeper (high sensitivity, useful for ruling out; the confounders of [[#def-spiral]] and laboratory reference thresholds read before ordering — [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]], [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]]), then echocardiography as the arbiter (ejection fraction, diastolic parameters and the E/e′ filling-pressure estimate, valve and wall and pericardial assessment — the cause-hunting study), with chest film (the cardiomegaly, cephalisation, Kerley lines and effusions that are the X-ray version of the Starling equation), ECG (the arrhythmia and infarct and LBBB signals that double as therapeutic targets), and the aetiology panel (the ischaemia imaging, the iron and amyloid and thyroid and alcohol and drug histories, the sleep study, and the genetics conversation where the dilated or hypertrophic or arrhythmogenic phenotype demands it — [[genetics/genomic-medicine]]'s cascade screening)**. The **bedside remains the staging instrument**: the elevated JVP (with the hepatojugular reflux and the abdominojugular reflex — [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]]'s right-side examination and its Kussmaul and tamponade variants), the third heart sound (the rapid-filling slosh of the dilated ventricle — specific, insensitive), the displaced and hyperdynamic or silent apex, the pulsatile liver and the dependent and sacral oedema, the Cheyne–Stokes respiration of central sleep apnoea ([[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]) and the narrow-pulse-pressure cold periphery of the low-output state; the **right-heart catheter** reserves its role for the uncertain filling pressures, the cardiogenic-versus-other shock question, the transplant and LVAD work-up and the pulmonary-hypertension phenotyping (the post- vs pre-capillary arithmetic of [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]). Cardiac MRI completes the tissue diagnosis (the oedema and late-gadolinium enhancement patterns of myocarditis, infarct and the infiltrations — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]).
:::

::: example The breathless grandmother with a "normal" ejection fraction {#ex-hfpef}
An 80-year-old (BMI 36, hypertension, permanent atrial fibrillation, two admissions for "chest infection") has NT-proBNP 1,400, ejection fraction 62 %, grossly enlarged left atrium, and E/e′ 17; her legs and sacrum are swollen and she cannot climb one flight.
::: solution
**HFpEF with congestion — the diagnosis the ejection fraction alone keeps missing.** The physiology reads the case: **the hypertensive, aged, obese, atrial-fibrillation ventricle with a steep EDPVR (congestion at ordinary volumes — the [[#def-phenotypes]] paragraph), the loss of atrial kick and the rate-related diastolic-shortening of the fibrillation (the filling time and the [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]] atrial-contribution arithmetic — decompensated by every tachycardia), and the "chest infections" that were pulmonary venous hypertension and the effusions**. The diagnostic discipline: **exclude the mimics and contributors (the anaemia, the thyroid, the renal sodium retention, the pulmonary hypertension's pre-capillary share, the aortic stenosis, the OSA, the deconditioning and obesity mechanics), confirm the diastolic physiology (the E/e′ and the LA volume and the TR velocity, the exercise or stress-echocardiographic or catheter haemodynamics when resting studies are equivocal — the HFpEF literature's whole complaint)**, then treat what is treatable: **the SGLT2-inhibitor (the symptom, first-hospitalisation and cardiovascular-death benefit that made it the class-first for this phenotype — and the GLP-1 and weight programme for the obese phenotype, and the diuretic titrated to weight and sacral examination — not to the ankle), rate control and consideration of the rhythm strategy (the AF-ablation-in-HF trials' signal that sinus rhythm may matter more in this phenotype than the beta-blockade's rate dogma allowed — [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]), the blood-pressure and heart-rate architecture, and the exercise/rehabilitation referral (the skeletal-muscle and peripheral-oxygen-extraction component of the dyspnoea — the "heart failure with skeletal-muscle myopathy" footnote of the exercise-physiology literature — [[physiology/integrative-physiology]]).** The teaching sentence: **an ejection fraction is a number about systole; this woman's disease is about diastole, volumes and comorbidity, and her management must be about those three things.**
:::
:::

::: definition The four pillars and the decongestion ladder {#def-treatment}
Chronic HFrEF therapy is now **four mortality-and-hospitalisation-reducing classes started in the weeks after diagnosis rather than titrated serially to tolerance over years**: **(1) renin–angiotensin system blockade, preferably as the angiotensin-receptor–neprilysin-inhibitor sacubitril/valsartan (the natriuretic-peptide-potentiating concept of [[#def-spiral]] made drug — the PARADIGM trial's supremacy over ramipril and the 36-hour ACE-inhibitor washout rule), or ACE/ARB where not tolerated or affordable; (2) evidence-based beta-blockade (bisoprolol, carvedilol, sustained metoprolol, nebivolol in the old-hypertensive variant — the sympathetic-toxicity reversal whose early negative inotropy means starting low, after decongestion and off inotropes, and doubling weekly; the mechanism's elegance: rate, demand, anti-arrhythmia and [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]-theorem-style reverse remodelling); (3) mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonist (the aldosterone-fibrosis axis, potassium and creatinine watched — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]); and (4) the SGLT2-inhibitor (an osmotic-diuretic and natriuretic, cardiorenal-metabolic drug whose benefits across phenotypes and aetiologies still outstrip its fully-worked-out mechanism — the [[renal/filtration]] tubuloglomerular-feedback and sodium-handling and energetics hypotheses, and its genital-infection and rare euglycaemic-ketoacidosis cautions, [[endocrine/diabetes]]).** On this skeleton hang the **loop diuretic for symptoms and congestion (morbidity and comfort, not mortality — the dose-metric of the 24-hour urine and weight), the additions by phenotype (hydralazine–isosorbide dinitrate in the Black-population and ACE-incompatible and pregnancy-safe slots — [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]; ivabradine for the rate-signature patient; digoxin for symptom-and-admission reduction with its narrow therapeutic window and its [[physiology/muscle]] contractility mechanism and renal and potassium sensitivities; vericiguat and the iron infusion for the deficient and recently-decompensated), the devices (the implantable cardioverter-defibrillator for the EF-≤-35-and-worse-prognosis arithmetic of [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]'s sudden-death rules, cardiac resynchronisation for the LBBB-and-wide-QRS dyssynchrony whose mechanism is [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]'s septal-to-lateral contraction delay made therapeutic, the conduction-system-pacing era's refinements, and the atrial shunts, baroreflex devices and tricuspid clips living in their trial slots), and the aetiology-specific cures (the revascularised ischaemic ventricle, the clipped or replaced valve, the alcohol stopped, the haemochromatosis bled or chelated, the amyloid typed and treated, the tachycardia-mediated fibrillation ablated, the myocarditis's immunosuppression, the familial cardiomyopathy's genetics clinic).**
:::

::: example The young man with a dilated heart {#ex-dcm}
A 34-year-old presents with 6 weeks of fatigue and orthopnoea; echocardiography shows a 6.5 cm dilated left ventricle with 15 % ejection fraction, functional mitral regurgitation, and normal coronary angiography; he drinks "a few pints most days" and takes no drugs; his father died suddenly at 44.
::: solution
**New dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) — the case is a work-up and a family, not just a prescription.** The immediate management is decongestion plus the four pillars introduced cautiously (the low-output patient may need the inotrope-and-advanced-therapy assessment of [[#def-advanced]] if the pressure limits the titration), plus the **aetiology hunt that can change everything: the alcohol history quantified honestly (a toxic-reversible cause in a minority, and ablation of the substance often followed by remarkable reverse remodelling — the recovering-ejection-fraction category of [[#def-phenotypes]]), the viral and immune history and cardiac MRI tissue pattern, the haemochromatosis iron studies and the thyroid and HIV and Chagas and peripartum and checkpoint-inhibitor questions, the stimulant and cocaine and anabolic-androgenic steroid and chemotherapy exposure list, the sleep and the family tree, and the genetic panel** (the titin truncating and lamin and desmosomal and lamin A/C and other DCM/LVNC genes whose identification means: the **arrhythmia risk may exceed the EF's apparent comfort (the lamin and desmosomal arrhythmia-first cardiomyopathies get the defibrillator conversation early — [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]), and the cascade family screening is preventive medicine ([[genetics/genomic-medicine]]'s and [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]'s pedigree tool).** The father's sudden death at 44 makes the genetic result a medical-ethics teaching case too (the predictive-testing-in-adults-at-risk discussion, the partner's screening choice, and the driving and sport counselling), and the heart-failure team's advanced-therapy and transplant listing (the mechanical-support bridge and destination logic of [[#def-advanced]]) becomes real if the pillars and the abstinence do not reverse him inside the three-to-six-month remodelling clock ([[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]] for the transplant immunology).
:::
:::

::: definition Refractory failure: devices, transplant, and the honest horizon {#def-advanced}
Stage D is the physiology of the last figure's bottom curve: **diuretic resistance (the [[renal/tubular-function]] distal-nephron hypertrophy, the gut oedema and the cardiorenal vasoconstriction), inotrope dependence, recurrent admissions, and the end-organ toll (the congested kidneys, the cardiac cirrhosis, the cachexia of the tumour-necrosis-factor-and-appetite axis)**. The options are **mechanical circulatory support (the continuous-flow left ventricular assist devices — the bridge-to-transplant, bridge-to-recovery and destination-therapy indications, and their price: the driveline infections, the pump thrombosis and stroke demanding anticoagulation ([[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]'s most demanding patient), the acquired von Willebrand syndrome — the shear-protein unfolding that causes the gastrointestinal bleeding and aortic-regurgitation and arteriovenous-malformation triad, and the right-ventricular failure that the device cannot fix — [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]]'s septal and interdependence physiology back as a surgical complication); heart transplantation (the gold standard for the selected: the work-up's age and comorbidity and compliance and psychosocial arithmetic, the donor-matching and allocation immunology and the lifelong triple immunosuppression's infections, skin cancers, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease, diabetes, and the cardiac allograft vasculopathy that is the chronic-rejection vasculopathy of [[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]]), and — for the majority who will not reach either — the palliative-medicine integration (the prognostic markers and the surprise question, the advanced care planning and the preferred place of dying, the symptom-management partnership that diuretics and low-dose opioids share for breathlessness, and the honest readmission-versus-quality conversations — [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]] and [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]]).** The British-registry truth behind all three paragraphs: incident heart failure carries multi-year mortality rivaling common cancers, half of patients die within five years of diagnosis, and only a quarter ever reach a specialist failure clinic — so the internist's, geriatrician's and GP's prescribing of the four pillars at first contact is, epidemiologically, the most important page of this book's cardiovascular section.
:::

::: example The nausea after the dose change {#ex-digoxin}
A 79-year-old on digoxin 250 µg daily and furosemide and ramipril, two weeks into a trimethoprim course for a chest infection, is sick, confused, and seeing yellow-tinged vision; pulse 46, potassium 3.2, creatinine up from 95 to 160, and the ECG shows a regularised atrial tachycardia with a slow, regular ventricular response.
::: solution
**Digoxin toxicity in its textbook setting — the narrow therapeutic index meeting its three interactants.** The pharmacology: **renal excretion unchanged in amount while the trimethoprim blocks tubular secretion and the dehydration and renal-artery-and-age physiology drop the glomerular filtration, the loop diuretic's hypokalaemia potentiating cardiac glycoside binding at the [[physiology/muscle]] sodium–potassium-ATPase (the potassium–digoxin receptor competition that makes the serum potassium, not just the level, the severity read), and the low muscle mass and hypoalbuminaemia shrinking the volume of distribution ([[pharmacology/absorption-distribution]], [[pharmacology/metabolism-excretion]]'s renal-clearance chapter in living form).** The manifestations as physiology: **the gut (anorexia, nausea, vomiting — the chemoreceptor trigger zone and the gut oedema), the eyes (xanthopsia and the blurred halos), the arrhythmias of increased automaticity plus depressed conduction — the "regularised atrial fibrillation" of the stem is the pathognomonic gift (the atrial tachycardia with block, the bidirectional ventricular tachycardia of the catecholamine-plus-glycoside milieu, the high-grade block and the slow junctional escape — [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]'s automaticity-versus-triggered-activity lesson), and the hyperkalaemia of massive acute poisoning (the pumped-out gradient) which is the opposite prognostic sign from the chronic patient's hypo-kalaemic potentiation.** Management: **stop the drug and the interactants, atropine or pacing for the symptomatic bradycardia, magnesium and potassium correction (and the careful potassium in the hyperkalaemic overdose — the binding fragment wins that argument), and digoxin-specific antibody fragments for the life-threatening arrhythmia, the potassium over ~5.5–6 in acute poisoning, or the malignant dose-plus-renal picture** — with the chronic-dose lesson: **the trial-era digoxin role is symptom-and-admission reduction with a narrow window (the low-dose serum-target strategy in renal impairment and the every-other-day and 62.5–125 µg reality of old kidneys — [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]'s potassium and [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]'s deprescribing list) and the whole case belongs to the drug-history question of the decompensation audit above.**
:::

::: quiz
Which heart-failure drug class relieves congestion but has never been shown to reduce mortality in HFrEF?
- [x] Loop diuretics
- [ ] Beta-blockers
- [ ] Mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonists
- [ ] SGLT2-inhibitors
::: solution
Diuretics (and indeed all decongestive measures including ultrafiltration) **control symptoms and signs — comfort, lungs, weight — but carry no mortality trial proof; the four pillars (renin–angiotensin-system blockade including the neprilysin combination, beta-blockade, mineralocorticoid-receptor antagonism and SGLT2 inhibition) plus, in selected patients, devices, are the mortality-and-hospitalisation evidence.** The clinical corollary is the dosing philosophy: **the diuretic at the minimum dose that keeps the patient dry (under-diuresis admits them with congestion; over-diuresis admits them with hypoperfusion and renal injury — [[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]), and never as a substitute for the disease-modifying four.**

::: history
**Withering's** digitalis (the foxglove's 1785 trial account applied to "dropsies", the dose-toxicity discipline and the mechanism's [[physiology/muscle]] sodium-pump inhibition still debated by historians from the same pages), **Hope's and Laennec's** and then **James Mackenzie's** venous-pressure and clinical-failure synthesis (the congestion-first cardiology before the left-heart era), **Christison and Bright's** renal-cardiac interweavings and the **Sir Thomas Lewis** school's mechanics; the **salt-restriction and mercurial-and-xanthine diuretic era (Kempner's rice-diet experiments of the 1940s — the first modern proof of sodium's role — and the chlorothiazide revolution of the 1950s that created the modern treatment of congestion)**; the **vasodilator revolution (the Cohn and Chatterjee-and-Parmley nitroprusside and afterload-reduction physiology and the V-HeFT trials of the 1970s–80s)** that converted "inert heart failure" into a haemodynamic disease; the **neurohormonal era's clinical trials (the CONSENSUS and SOLVD and the beta-blocker trials that flipped the class from contraindicated to cornerstone — the CIBIS and MERIT-HC and COPERNICUS programmes, the RALES and EPHESUS and EMPHASIS aldosterone line, the CHARM and PARADIGM-HF neprilysin and the DAPA-HF, EMPEROR and DELIVER SGLT2 waves — every one of them physiology-based, every one a mortality claim verified)**; the device line (**Barnard's** transplant, the total-heart and then the **DeBakey-and-Devries-era continuous-flow ventricular assist devices and the BerlinHeart era**, the **MIRACLE and CARE-HF resynchronisation trials** born of the same afterload-reduction concept), and the modern HFpEF and obesity-and-metabolic trial era finally returning the "nothing works" phenotype to the table with SGLT2-inhibitors, GLP1-agonists and weight-phenotype medicine.
:::

## Where this leads

The pumping mechanics in [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]]; the compensation physiology in [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]; the diuretic and neurohormonal pharmacology with their renal targets in [[renal/tubular-function]] and [[renal/water-sodium]]; cardiorenal syndrome in [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]; the ischaemic substrate in [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]; the arrhythmia and device interface in [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]] and [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]]; transplant immunology in [[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]]; the palliative arc in [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]].

::: summary
- Heart failure is stage (A–D), phenotype (reduced/preserved ejection fraction and the middle and recovering bands) and aetiology — and all three labels change management; finding the cause is treatment.
- The spiral — injury, neurohormonal compensation, remodelling, congestion and hypoperfusion — is the same physiology as [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]] read pathologically, and it is why four drug classes that interrupt it reduce mortality in HFrEF.
- Congestion is treated by moving fluid off the circulation (ventilation support, nitrates, diuretics) with the cardiac output curve in mind; hypoperfusion is a different, scarcer, more dangerous axis with its own ladder.
- HFpEF is a compliance and comorbidity disease whose management is decongestion, metabolic and rhythm strategy, and the new SGLT2 and weight evidence — not the HFrEF quadruple copied.
- Refractory disease is a choice between mechanical support, transplantation and honest palliation, and the population-level lever is early four-pillar prescribing by every clinician who sees these patients first.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Sort the oedema again {level=1}
A swollen, breathless patient: list three non-cardiac causes that mimic heart failure and the discriminator for each.
::: solution
**Chronic kidney disease and nephrotic syndrome** (the facial and periorbital distribution, the urine protein and albumin, the eGFR — and the raised JVP absent unless combined: [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]'s Starling terms); **chronic liver disease** (the ascites-out-of-proportion, the stigmata, the low albumin, and the high-output circulation's flow murmur — [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]); **chronic lung disease with cor pulmonale** (the clubbing and wheeze and cyanosis and the raised JVP with clear lungs and a normal wedge — the pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension of [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]], versus the post-capillary failure); plus the venous incompetence, the calcium-channel-blocker and the obesity-hypoventilation and the lymphoedema periphery. The discriminator is the **filling-pressure and chamber evidence (the JVP character, the echo, the peptide)** — the chapter's method: separate syndromes by the curve they broke.
:::
:::

::: exercise Interpret the peptide {level=1}
Give two false-negative and three false-positive contexts for raised natriuretic peptides, and state what the test is best used for.
::: solution
**False negatives: obesity (the adipose clearance receptor and the suppressed secretion — the lower thresholds the guidelines mandate), and the acute-flash or chronic-low-output state with a normal peptide, plus pericardial-constriction and the obesity-masked HFpEF presentation**; **false positives: atrial fibrillation (the atrial stretch), age and renal impairment (the reduced clearance of the pro-peptide especially), pulmonary embolus and pulmonary hypertension (the right ventricle), sepsis and critical illness, and myocardial injury of any cause.** Best use: **the negative predictive value in undifferentiated acute dyspnoea (ruling out) and serial monitoring in advanced disease trials rather than as a screening test in primary care** — the likelihood-ratio arithmetic of [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]] and the reason the algorithm puts the peptide before the echo, not after the guess.
:::
:::

::: exercise Constriction versus restriction {level=2}
Both present with right-sided congestion and a normal ejection fraction. Give three clinical and three imaging/catheter discriminators between constrictive pericarditis and restrictive cardiomyopathy (the amyloid patient).
::: solution
**Clinical: the pericardial history (the tuberculosis, radiotherapy, cardiac surgery and haemothorax of constriction) versus the low-voltage-and-carpal-tunnel-and-biceps-tendon-rupture phenotype of ATTR or the macroglossia-and-periorbital purpura of AL amyloid ([[blood/lymphoma-myeloma]]); the Kussmaul sign (both), the pericardial knock versus the restrictive third sound, and the preserved systolic function with the thick walls (restriction) versus normal-thickness walls (constriction). Imaging: the CT and MR pericardial thickness and calcification and the septal bounce (constriction — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]); the diffuse late-gadolinium enhancement and the abnormal technetium-pyrophosphate and the fat-pad and genetic tests (amyloid typing). Catheter: the dip-plateau equalisation of diastolic pressures with ventricular discordance (the ventricular interdependence of [[cardiovascular/cardiac-cycle]]'s tamponade logic — constriction) versus concordance and a pulmonary-artery wedge above the right-ventricular end-diastolic with elevated filling pressures and low output (restriction).** The stakes: **constriction is surgically curable (the pericardiectomy's own selection problem — the transient and the burned-out myocarditis variants), while amyloid now has type-specific medical therapy** — hence the pair of diagnoses this chapter's examiners love most ([[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]]'s and [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]'s adjacent physiology, and [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]] for the ATTR-in-the-elderly epidemiology).

::: exercise The decompensation hunt {level=2}
A chronic HFrEF patient returns wet for the third time this year. List the eight-question audit that finds the cause in most admissions.
::: solution
**(1) Which tablets did you actually take, and when? (the adherence and cost and confusion audit — [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]] and the adherence-programme evidence); (2) Salt and fluids — the real diet, including the soup-and-bread load and the effervescent-sodium tablets; (3) NSAIDs and steroids and the thiazolinedione and the new amlodipine and the gabapentin (the sodium-and-water list — [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]); (4) Weight trend and diuretic dose-response (the diuretic-resistance and wrong-molecule question — [[renal/tubular-function]]); (5) Ischaemia and arrhythmia (the ECG, the peptide and troponin trend, the palpitations and the pacemaker and device interrogations — the AF-with-rapid-response and the RV-paced-dyssynchrony decompensations); (6) Infection, anaemia and thyroid (the precipitants — [[blood/anaemia]], [[endocrine/thyroid]]); (7) The alcohol and the stimulant and the missed dialysis (the exposures); (8) What does the heart actually look like now (the progression of the lesion, the valve, the remodelling, the new pulmonary hypertension — the echo and clinic review). The exercise's point is that admissions are usually causes with names, and the third admission is a phenotype question — the refractory-and-palliative or advanced-therapy crossroads of [[#def-advanced]].**

::: exercise Which device {level=2}
For each, name the device and its physiological criterion: (a) EF 28 %, LBBB 162 ms, NYHA III on quadruple therapy; (b) EF 30 % 5 months post-infarct, no symptoms; (c) EF 33 %, narrow QRS, AF with controlled rate.
::: solution
**(a) Cardiac resynchronisation therapy (biventricular or conduction-system pacing) — the LBBB ≥150 ms (and the ≥120-with-other-criteria variants) plus persistent symptoms and reduced EF: the septal–lateral dyssynchrony of [[cardiovascular/electrophysiology-ecg]], the wasted-work and functional-regurgitation reduction, and the CRT super-responders and their reverse remodelling (the CRT-D versus CRT-P decision by age, frailty and prognosis conversations); (b) an implantable cardioverter-defibrillator — the post-infarct, >40-day, ≥3-month, EF-≤-30-and-otherwise-fair primary-prevention indication (the MADIT-II/SCD-HeFT arithmetic, and the shared decision about shocks-versus-years in the older and the comorbid); (c) no device on these facts — narrow QRS removes the resynchronisation rationale, and the AF-plus-EF combination invites the ablation-and-rhythm strategy and rate-control re-audit first ([[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]]), with re-check of the EF after optimisation deciding the defibrillator question later.** Each answer is a curve or a circuit, not a number — the chapter's method restated.

::: exercise The breathless pregnant patient {level=2}
A 31-year-old at 35 weeks develops orthopnoea and an EF of 28 % with no prior history. Diagnose, give the prolactin-pathophysiology hypothesis, and the treatment logic including the breastfeeding decision.
::: solution
**Peripartum cardiomyopathy** — the last-months-to-five-months post-partum systolic failure with no other cause (the pre-eclampsia, multiple-pregnancy, older-mother, West-African-heritage and socio-economic risk set — [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]). Mechanism hypothesis (the Hilfiker-Kleiner work): **an oxidative-stress-driven cleavage of prolactin into a cardiotoxic 16-kDa fragment with capillary and myocyte damage — the reason bromocriptine (the prolactin-block, plus anticoagulation because of the thrombotic risk) entered trial and practice, and the reason the recovery and the recurrence risk discuss future pregnancy honestly**. Management: **deliver and stabilise by the obstetric–cardiac team; the four pillars adapted to lactation and to the fetus if still pregnant (the ACE-inhibitors' post-partum-but-not-in-pregnancy status and the lactation-safety tables — enalapril and captopril acceptable post-partum, the ARBs and sacubitril not; beta-blockade with fetal-growth surveillance; the AT1 antagonists avoided in pregnancy; the diuretic lowest dose), the anticoagulation question with the very-low-ejection-fraction and the bromocriptine's thrombotic caveat, the contraindication-to-further-pregnancy conversation where recovery fails (the maternal-risk and the teratogenic-drug timing), and the recovery follow-up that puts the weaning decision (the prolactin-cessation logic argues against breastfeeding on the bromocriptine pathway) on the informed-consent list.** (the family-planning logic sits in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]]).

::: exercise The inotrope decision {level=2}
A cold-and-wet patient on maximal diuretics and nitrates has systolic 78, lactate 3.4, rising creatinine and a cardiac index of 1.7. Lay out the pharmacological ladder, each drug's receptor logic, and the two escalation decisions it precedes.
::: solution
**The "cold and wet" quadrant (low output plus congestion — the widget's bottom curve's live version):** secure the rhythm and rate and valve lesions first (the acute mitral regurgitation and the severe aortic stenosis are mechanical answers), then **inotropy with receptor logic: dobutamine (β₁-dominant — contractility and rate and some vasodilation; the arrhythmia and ischaemia costs — [[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]]'s dose-response in dangerous dress), milrinone (phosphodiesterase-3 inhibition raising the [[physiology/muscle]] calcium and the cyclic nucleotide — inotropy plus arteriolar and venous dilation and its long half-life and hypotension in renal impairment), levosimendan (the calcium-sensitiser-plus-KATP-vasodilator hybrid with its active-metabolite days), and adrenaline or noradrenaline added by the perfusion-pressure and shock-depth question ([[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]]), with mechanical support (the intra-aortic balloon pump's diminishing evidence and the percutaneous micro-axial and centrifugal devices) for the drug-refractory. The two preceding escalations: **the temporary mechanical support-to-decision bridge versus primary inotrope-based palliation (the goals-of-care conversation with the prognosis data), and the advanced-therapy referral (the LVAD/transplant work-up of [[#def-advanced]] before the third admission makes it impossible — the timing problem that is stage D medicine's central skill).**

::: exercise Design the heart-failure service {level=3}
A region of 500,000 people (incidence ~10/1,000/year over 65) asks for a heart-failure plan. Build it: prevention, rapid-access diagnosis, discharge pharmacology, readmission reduction, advanced pathways and workforce, with the physiology/evidence reason each element exists.
::: solution
**Prevention (stage A/B):** hypertension and diabetes control (the [[cardiovascular/hypertension]] and [[endocrine/diabetes]] architectures — the renin–angiotensin-and-SGLT2 arms literally labelled "heart-failure prevention" by the trials), the cardio-oncology surveillance (the anthracycline and trastuzumab protocols — [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]), and the post-infarct remodelling prescription ([[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]); **rapid access:** the peptide-gated chest-and-breathlessness clinic with open-access diuretic titration and same-week echocardiography (the diagnostic-delay evidence and the primary-care trial family), and the heart-failure specialist nurse as the service's spine (the multidisciplinary-readmission-trial literature — the same evidence base behind every guideline's class I recommendation); **discharge pharmacology:** the four-pillar initiation in-hospital with 2-week post-discharge titration (the STRONG-HF collaborative-care and rapid-up-titration trial and its safety-architecture lesson — the discharge is the consultation, not the bed), the heart-failure medicines reconciled against the NSAID and the metformin and the diuretic doses ([[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]); **phenotype-specific tracks:** the HFpEF and obesity-metabolic and AF-rhythm pathway, the amyloid- and iron- and ischaemia-cause services with the genetic clinics ([[genetics/genomic-medicine]]); **advanced:** the single regional LVAD/transplant centre with referral criteria taught to the district hospitals (stage-D recognition before the fourth ICU week), and the palliative-medicine integration with the preferred-place-of-care recording ([[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]]); **accountability:** the readmission-and-mortality and four-pillar-prescribing rates at the clinician level, patient-reported function, and the equity audit (the deprivation gradient of incidence, late diagnosis and device access — [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]]). The physiology through-line: every element attacks the [[#def-spiral]] spiral at a different node, at the earliest node the system can reach.
