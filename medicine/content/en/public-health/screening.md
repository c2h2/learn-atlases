Screening is the branch of prevention where medicine's good intentions are audited hardest, because its benefits are delayed and statistical while its harms are immediate and personal: the false positive, the overdiagnosis, the interval cancer, the cascade. **This chapter covers the principles (the Wilson-Jungner lineage and its modern revisions, the three biasses that made screening trials necessary - lead time, length and selection - and the overdiagnosis concept), the national programmes themselves (cervical, breast and bowel cancer screening with their test-level epidemiology, the abdominal-aortic and sickle-cell-and-thalassaemia and newborn-screening programmes and the diabetic-retinopathy and lung-screening additions), and the delivery science (uptake and its inequalities, quality assurance from sampling to double-reading, informed choice and opt-out ethics, and the benefit-harm communication numbers that make screening honest).**

## The principles

::: definition Why screening needs trials: criteria and biasses {#def-principles}
**The classical frame is Wilson and Jungner's 1968 principles, revised repeatedly since (the last major WHO update keeping the condition-test-treatment-programme structure): the condition should be important, its natural history understood, a recognisable early stage exist, treatment at the early stage be better than later, the test be acceptable and reasonably accurate, its cost balance with overall care be reasonable, and - the clause that took medicine fifty years to internalise - screening should be a continuous programme with quality assurance, not a test without a system. The ethics added the modern criteria: informed choice (the screen-detected and the unscreened must both be able to say what they were told of benefits and harms), equity of access, and the programme-evaluation duty. The three biasses are the reason 'early detection saves lives' is not a syllogism: lead-time bias (survival-from-diagnosis lengthens when diagnosis moves earlier even if death is unchanged - the reason the endpoint is disease-specific or all-cause mortality, never five-year survival), length bias (slow-growing, less-lethal cases are over-represented among screen-detected cancers, flattering outcomes further), and selection bias (the health-conscious who attend screening would have lived longer anyway); the methodological answer is the randomised trial of screening versus no screening (the HIP breast trial of 1963 and the colorectal and prostate and ovarian programmes that followed, with their contested re-analyses) and modelling (the Mikulski-adjacent and CISNET-style simulation consensus that adjudicates breast and colorectal and lung screening benefits when trials are impossible or old). Overdiagnosis is the harm that arrived with the evidence: the screen-detected disease that would never have symptomatic - quantified per programme (the breast estimates ranging across the methods from a few percent to double digits; the prostate-adjacent and thyroid and melanoma and now the lung-screening debates), driven by sensitivity itself (the better the test and the longer the horizon, the more indolent disease is found), and made ethically salient by the treatment harms it triggers (the surgery, radiotherapy and their sequels in men whose cancers would never have killed them). The taxonomy of screening types belongs here too: mass, selective and high-risk (the familial and occupational and imaging-surveillance programmes); case-finding (the symptomatic-adjacent opportunistic testing that is not screening and follows different rules); screening with prevention built in (the cervical and bowel programmes that remove the disease they detect - the 'screen-and-treat' model whose positive predictive value thresholds decide what gets excised); and the modern frontier (the polygenic-risk-score and multi-cancer-early-detection blood-test debates whose evidence standards the principles chapter of epidemiology already condemns them to fail until randomised).**
:::

## The programmes

::: definition The national screens: tests, numbers and harms {#def-programmes}
**Cervical screening remains the success story and the live experiment: the cytology era's mortality fall (the 'screening saved more British women than any other cancer programme' arithmetic), the HPV-primary-testing transition (the higher sensitivity and longer safe intervals and the triage algorithms - self-sampling options increasing coverage in the under-screened), the vaccinated-cohort future (the cohorts with near-elimination-level HPV prevalence now entering screening age, raising the 'de-escalation' question the programmes are drafting answers for), and the harms ledger (the false-positive colposcopy cascade, the over-diagnosis-and-overtreatment-of-lesions-that-regress problem - with the 'management-to-reduce-overtreatment' guidelines, and the reproductive harms of excision that the age-and-parity communication must carry). Breast screening: the mammography programmes (the invite ages and intervals across nations, the 'reduction in breast-cancer mortality around a fifth to a quarter' trial-and-modelling synthesis with its Cochrane-sceptic counterweight), the harms (the false-positive recall cumulative risk - higher in younger and dense-breast populations, the interval cancers as the programme's blind spot, and the overdiagnosis estimates that the benefit-harm numbers must quote), and the technology-in-evaluation (the digital and tomosynthesis upgrades with their recall-rate effects, the AI-double-reader trials with their 'non-inferiority promising, workflow-proven' status, and the dense-breast-supplementary-screening equity debate). Bowel screening: the faecal-occult-to-FIT transition (the haemoglobin-immunoassay's better specificity-and-quantitative-threshold design, the colonoscopy-as-the-follow-up-with-its-own-quality-metrics-adenoma-detection-rate), the one-step-and-multitarget-stool-DNA tests' uptake-versus-colonoscopy-capacity trade, and the age-window trials (the under-50-increase-of-early-onset-cancer epidemiology that reopened the start-age debate amid much heat); the programme's real-world outputs depend on the colonoscopy pathway's capacity and the symptomatic-referral firewall (the 'screening is not for bleeding' rule). The abdominal-aortic-aneurysm programme (the one-male-screen-at-65-and-surveillance-intervals-by-diameter logic - a clean example of prevalence deciding predictive value and the 'number-needed-to-screen' being small because the treatable-prevalence is high in the target sex-and-age), and the sickle-cell-and-thalassaemia-and-haemoglobinopathy antenatal-and-neonatal programmes (the carrier-screening-ancestry-based-offer debate and the 'condition-imported-from-lowland' equity history of the tests). The newborn blood-spot programme (the phenylketonuria-and-endocrine-and-SMA-and-SCID-additions era with tandem-mass-spectrometry's expanded menu, the false-positive-parenting-and-condition-identification-versus-screening-for-risk debates, the hearing screen-and-critical-period-linguistics case and the pulse-oximetry-critical-congenital-heart-disease-screen's contested evidence). Diabetic-retinopathy screening (the graders-and-mydriasis-and-AI-autonomous-read-era programmes whose real failure mode is the loss-to-follow-up between detection and treatment, 'screened-and-not-referred' being the morbidity's new name), and lung cancer screening (the low-dose-CT mortality trials' ~20-percent lung-cancer-mortality reduction in the eligible - with the overdiagnosis and false-positive-nodule-cascade and radiation and equal-access-in-deprived-smokers problems the programmes inherit; the 'screening-works-worst-for-those-it-helps-most-unless-designed' lesson generalising). The infectious-screening programmes (the antenatal HIV-and-syphilis-and-hepatitis-B-with-the-prevention-of-vertical-transmission-arithmetic, the blood-donation screen-and-nucleic-acid-testing-arms-race with window periods, and the prison-and-migrant-and-contacts-active-case-finding programmes - HIV-testing-normalisation and the latent-tuberculosis-screening-of-contacts and high-incidence-group approaches and their 'number-needed-to-screen' realities); and the optic-and-orthoptic-and-deaf-and-glaucoma-and-osteoporoporosis-adjacent-conditional-programmes (the FRAX-and-screening-detection-thresholds-and-the-WHO's-screening-and-treatment-of-osteoporosis-positions and the 'screen-for-fragility-fractures-not-for-BMD' direction of travel) each demonstrate the principles in one organ's clothing.**
:::

## The delivery

::: definition Uptake, quality and the numbers of informed choice {#def-delivery}
**Uptake is the programme's first output and its equity X-ray: coverage gradients by deprivation, ethnicity, language, migration status, disability and learning difficulty mean the population programme can be excellent in the aggregate and failing in the postcode (the inverse-care-law pattern in every programme's data - the cervical and breast and bowel screens' lowest quintiles sitting 15-30 points below the highest), so the delivery evidence base is a public-health speciality: the reminder-and-recall systems, the text-and-mailers-and-navigator-programmes, the community-and-pharmacy-and-workplace-and-school-settings, the opt-out-versus-opt-in-and-self-sampling-and-home-kits-and-'invite-at-attendance' designs, the interpreters-and-easy-read-materials-and-'trusting-relationships'-qualitative literature, and the requirement that the programme's equity dashboard be a board metric, not an academic appendix. Quality assurance is the invisible scaffolding: the pre-analytical (the sampling-and-transport-and-labology-standards), the analytical (the reagent-and-control-and-external-quality-assessment), the reader level (the double-reading-in-breast-and-the-second-reader-audit, the adenoma-detection-rate floors-and-ceiling-feedback for colonoscopy, the colposcopy-and-histology correlation audits, the grading-certification and re-certification loops for retinal graders), the programme level (the interval-cancer reviews and the death-certificate-and-screening-history linkage and the national screening committees' appraisal processes), and the ethics of thresholds (the positive-cutoff-and-referral-protocol choices that are resource rationing in laboratory clothing and must be stated as such). The numbers of informed choice are the chapter's clinical core and must be quotable per programme: the absolute benefit (the deaths averted per thousand screened over the programme horizon), the harm counts (the false positives per thousand and their investigation cascade, the overdiagnosis per thousand and its treatment morbidity, the interval cancers), the comparison frames (the 'numbers needed to screen and to treat' and the 'life-years gained' versions), and the presentation research (the balanced, absolute-frequency icon-array formats that the risk-communication literature shows support genuine choice against the single-number advocacy formats). The opt-out ethics and the data layer: the NHS-style 'call-and-recall by registration' default (its 'you are invited because you are registered' transparency and the migrants-and-the-unsourced-and-the-overseas-visits populations the system drops - the registry-maintenance duty), the screening registers and record linkage and the secondary-use-of-screening-data-for-research (the population-cohort-and-biobank-adjacent governance that the research-ethics chapter claims), and the 'introduced-to-screening-and-forgot-to-stop' class of harms (the screening that continues past life-expectancy or age limits, and the disinvestment programs that make stopping as clinical as starting). Finally the frontier's governance: the multi-cancer blood tests and polygenic risk and wearables arriving as 'screening without a programme' - the principles chapter's requirements (the randomised mortality evidence, the equity and capacity plans, the harms ledger and the affordability question) are the only defence against a prevention-industrial-complex in which the worried well are screened and the worried poor are not, and the clinician's role is to answer the patient's 'should I have the whole-body scan / the gene panel / the Apple watch atrial-fib search' with the same three questions this chapter was written to install: what does it find, what happens next, and who proved it helps.**
:::

::: example The mammogram that caused a biopsy {#ex-falsepositive}
A 47-year-old first-attender is recalled for 'ultrasound and core biopsy' after a screening mammogram; the recall letter says 'nothing is likely to be wrong'; the biopsy shows atypical hyperplasia; she asks whether the screening harmed her.
::: solution
**The-both/and-answer ([[#def-principles]], [[#def-delivery]], [[surgery/breast-endocrine-surgery]]): the arithmetic she is owed - 'per thousand women screened per round at her age, the recall rate runs in the tens and a substantial share of recalls finish 'all clear' (the 'letter is right that most recalls are innocent' fact, with its honest twin: the recall's anxiety is now measured to persist for years in some women - 'screening harms are not only physical); the pathway's justification - 'the image's positive predictive value at this age and density is modest but the alternative (tolerating the cancer probability) is worse at the programme's chosen threshold, and the biopsy's atypia result is precisely why the cascade exists (atypical hyperplasia is a genuine risk marker and sometimes a harboured malignancy - the excision-and-surveillance decision is now evidence-based medicine, not cascade); the harm accounting - 'both statements true: the screening programme detected something that needs managing (if the MDT says so) or it triggered an investigation whose result is risk-stratification rather than cancer (the 'over-investigation-not-overdiagnosis' nuance), and either way the programme's duty was the balanced numbers before the first mammogram and the clear explanation now; and the system check the letter should trigger - 'the 'recall-communication-quality-audits (the 'what does 'likely' mean to patients' studies), the equity-of-recall-experience (the interpreter-and-anxiety-management supports), and her own next choices made with the risk-reduction-not-fatalism framing that the atypia literature's high-quality decision aids support.'**
:::

::: example The bowel screen and the bleed {#ex-fitness}
A 63-year-old mails a FIT kit showing 45 µg/g (abnormal threshold 15); the system books a colonoscopy in eight weeks; meanwhile he mentions to his GP 'the usual bleeding, piles'; the GP reads 'screening' and defers - and the interval cancer arrives at week ten.
::: solution
**Two-systems-one-patient ([[#def-delivery]], [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]], [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]]): the collision - 'the screening pathway (asymptomatic, FIT-threshold, capacity-planned wait) and the symptomatic pathway (rectal-bleeding, two-week-wait, must-be-seen-now) ran parallel because a flag said 'screening' and the bleeding was 'haemorrhoidal by assumption'; the rules - 'a screening result never downgrades a red-flag symptom and a red-flag symptom never gets routed through the screening queue (the 'screening-is-not-for-symptomatic-patients' firewall the programmes publish but practices miss), and the FIT-positive-with-bleeding is two independent indications, each sufficient; the medicine of FIT - 'the haemoglobin-threshold's cancer-and-advanced-adenoma detection figures and its right-sided-and-intermittent-bleeding limits (the 'negative FIT never excludes the symptomatic' rule) and the interval-cancer concept the programme monitors; the fix - 'the GP-and-screening-system interoperability that reads the FIT result as a clinical object (the 'urgent-re-look-when-bleeding-reported' flag), the eight-week-wait-and-2-week-wait-priority-rules reconciliation (a bleeding FIT-positive belongs in the fast lane - the 'upgrade-on-new-symptom' SOP), the safety-netting letter on every screening appointment ('new bleeding, pain or weight loss - ring your GP now, not after the appointment'), and the interval-cancer review that will name the 'deferral by label' as its cause; and the habit for every clinician: when a 'screening' patient reports symptoms, they have left the screening population - the epidemiology no longer describes them, and the pathway must change with the population.'**
:::

## Where this leads

The test-accuracy mathematics behind every programme is in [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]] and the trial-and-modelling evidence in [[epidemiology-ebm/randomised-trials]] and [[epidemiology-ebm/systematic-reviews]]; the cancer-specific disease detail is in [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/gynaecological-cancer]], [[respiratory/lung-cancer-pleura]] and [[surgery/breast-endocrine-surgery]]; antenatal and newborn screening meets [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]] and [[paediatrics/newborn]]; and the risk-communication craft is in [[clinical-skills/communication]].

::: history
**Screening as a defined public strategy begins with the tuberculin mass-tests of the 1920s-40s schoolrooms and workplaces - the first time 'test the well to find the sick' was organised at scale - and with the parallel, older practice of antenatal and school medical inspection. The term 'mass screening' is credited to the Soviet public-health physician Vladimir Maksimenko in the 1920s; the modern era opens with the breast trial (the HIP study randomising 1950s New York women to mammography and clinical examination, its 1966 mortality finding making 'early detection' evidence rather than intuition), followed by the decade's 'screening crusades' (the cervical cancer smears' population rollouts, the neuroblastoma urine-test campaign that later became a textbook false triumph - screened cohorts 'survived' better while stage distribution shifted, and the trial-free programmes were stopped after the overdiagnosis was exposed in the 1990s-2000s, the single most instructive screening lesson never taught in medical school). Wilson and Jungner's 1968 WHO principles arrived exactly as the crusades multiplied, and their criteria (updated by the WHO in 2020 with equity and evidence-based-decision-making added) became the world's shared checklist; national screening appraisal bodies (the UK's National Screening Committee lineage, the US Preventive Services Task Force's grade system from 1984) institutionalised the arguments. The trial-and-controversy catalogue wrote the field's epistemology: the prostate-PSA saga (the randomised trials reporting weak mortality effects against modelling consensus, the overdiagnosis quantification, the 'informed choice' era and now the risk-stratified renaissance of 'PSA with MRI-first pathways'); the ovarian-screen trials (UKCTOCS's null-with-nuance result and the biomarker-and-imaging programmes it discouraged); the colorectal line (the faecal-occult randomised trials proving mortality reduction, the flexible-sigmoidoscopy-and-CT-colonography trials, and the FIT transition now standard); the lung-screen reversal (the low-dose-CT NLST and NELSON trials' 2010s mortality reductions converting a decades-old 'screening cannot work in smokers' consensus into national programmes being built in real time with their harms dashboards attached). The newborn-screening programme is the quiet success: Guthrie's 1961 heel-prick phenylketonuria test (the bacteria-in-a-tube assay that created the field and the 'newborn blood spot' as an institution), the tandem-mass-spectrometry expansion, and the treatment-advances (the SMA and SCID additions whose 'early-treatment-transforms-outcome' evidence finally answered the condition's oldest ethical objection). Cervical screening's history merges with virology's: the cytology mass-screen era's mortality fall, zur Hausen's HPV-causation work (the 2008 Nobel prize) enabling HPV-primary testing and vaccination - the first cancer-screening programme with a retirement date built in, and the newest ethical frontier being de-escalation-while-others-still-die. Overdiagnosis itself became a public concept through the 2000s-2010s epidemiological work (the Welch and the 'Overdiagnosed' and 'Less Is More' campaigns and the International Agency for Research on Cancer's overdiagnosis-classification working group), shifting the field's language from 'early detection is good' to benefit-harm ratios quoted in absolute numbers before consent. The delivery-science chapter - uptake gradients, self-sampling, reminders, opt-out-versus-opt-in trials, the navigator programmes - is now where most of the 'effectiveness gap' between what screening can do and what it does is being closed, and the 2020s frontier (polygenic-risk-stratified and multi-cancer blood tests and AI-reading) is arriving with the same promise and the same checklist it always should: show the mortality, show the harms, show the equity, and pay for the capacity.** - [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]], [[pathology/cancer-diagnosis]].**
:::

::: summary
- Screening is a programme, not a test: Wilson-Jungner and its 2020 updates demand an understood natural history, a better-treated early stage, an acceptable accurate test, quality assurance, equity - and randomised or otherwise rigorous mortality evidence, because survival curves lie.
- Lead-time, length and selection biasses plus overdiagnosis explain why 'early detection saves lives' needs a trial behind it; overdiagnosis scales with test sensitivity and is the harm every programme must quantify and disclose.
- The national programmes (cervical HPV-primary, breast mammography, bowel FIT, aortic-aneurysm, newborn and antenatal, retinal and lung) each have their own benefit, harm and capacity numbers; the screen-and-treat designs (cervical, bowel) convert detection into prevention and must manage overtreatment thresholds.
- Uptake is the equity X-ray: coverage gradients track deprivation, ethnicity and disability, and the delivery evidence (reminders, settings, self-sampling, navigation, opt-out designs) is where realised benefit is won.
- Quality assurance is the invisible treatment: double-reading, detection-rate floors, interval-cancer reviews and grading certification are clinical safety, and the referral thresholds are rationing that must be stated.
- Clinicians answer the screening questions with three numbers and three questions: absolute benefit, absolute harms, comparison; who proved it, what happens next, and who pays for the follow-up - and symptoms always exit the screening population.
:::

A multi-cancer early-detection blood test (one draw, '20+ cancers, signal-specific source prediction') seeks health-system adoption. Your country's cervical programme is debating its vaccinated-cohort future: women born after 2008 have near-zero high-risk-HPV prevalence; the committee must choose between continuing HPV-primary screening at 25, moving to self-sampling every five years, or first-screen-at-35 with self-samples.

::: example The newborn screen with no treatment {#ex-nbsx}
The national committee considers adding a condition to the newborn blood spot that has a licensed gene therapy: ultra-rare, devastating if late, treatable only in the first weeks, at a cost-per-child-screened that dwarfs every existing item, with the therapy's own evidence limited to a small cohort.
::: solution
**Screening-for-a-launch-price ([[#def-principles]], [[#def-delivery]], [[genetics/genetic-testing]]): the classic criteria still do the work - 'the condition qualifies (severe, understood, detectable in the presymptomatic window, earlier treatment plausibly better); the programme questions are new: the therapy's evidence (a conditional-approval drug's real-world effectiveness becomes the screening programme's evidence base - the 'screening-to-uncertain-treatment' inversion the SMA addition survived because its treatment trials were strong), the economics (the cost-per-QALY of population screening for an ultra-rare condition is dominated by the therapy price; the 'screening-creates-a-market-and-the-market-sets-the-test's-value' critique and the 'who-negotiates-first' governance question), the consent-and-justice layers (the 'screen-everyone-pay-for-the-few' cross-subsidy's fairness argument versus the 'rare-disease-solidarity' defence, and the equity of 'a postcode-dependent gene therapy'), and the harms of the diagnosis-without-certainty (the borderline-internal-reference-range class and the parents' 'my child is a candidate' limbo); the decision structure that is emerging from the 2020s appraisals: the 'condition-and-treatment-appraised-together' pathway (the UK's and Germany's newborn-screening-and-HTA joint pilots), the 'evidence-development-agreements-with-treatment-registry-requirements (the 'screening-approval-conditional-on-outcomes-data' model), the 'centralised-testing-with-named-treatment-centres' delivery that makes the cascade survivable, and the honest public reasoning: this is where screening meets rationing and hope, and the committee's answer is legitimate only if the numbers (costs, benefits, uncertainties) are published - because the alternative is a private pay-per-screen market for the same tests, which reliably worsens the equity the programme exists to protect.'**
:::

::: exercise Explain the interval cancer {level=1}
A 55-year-old's bowel-screen FIT was negative; eighteen months later she presents with an obstructing colon cancer. She sues the programme. Explain, for the claims letter: what a negative FIT is and is not, and the three reasons interval cancers happen.
::: solution
**The-negative-result's-meaning ([[#def-programmes]], [[#def-principles]], [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]]): what a negative FIT means - 'at the assay's threshold, haemoglobin below the cut-off on that single sample; against colorectal cancer it carries a sensitivity of the order of half to two-thirds (cancer-specific, higher for advanced disease) and a high negative predictive value only because prevalence is low - 'a good test for populations, a probabilistic one for people'; the three interval-cancer mechanisms - '1) biology: right-sided and flat lesions bleed intermittently or not at all (the FIT's known weakness for the proximal and mucinous cancers), 2) the single-sample limitation (the repeat-or-quantitative-threshold designs that programmes trade against participation), and 3) the incident interval (cancers arising between screens - the 'fast' tumours length bias exists to hide, and the symptomatic-interval pathway that must catch them); the defence's and the programme's shared honesty - 'the guidelines' position that negative screening never excludes symptoms' cause: the record will be read for whether her (perhaps undocumented) symptoms between the kit and the presentation triggered the 'exit-the-screening-pathway' rule, and whether the safety-net message was given; the learning response - 'the interval-cancer review committee (the programme's mortality-and-stage audit of its own misses, the site-specific and symptom-pattern analysis that feeds the threshold-and-age-window debates), and the candour letter explaining all of this to her, because most screening-related litigation is about the unexplained.'**
:::

::: exercise Answer the screening-demand patient {level=2}
A 44-year-old asymptomatic low-risk patient demands a cardiac CT angiogram 'because my father had a heart attack at 50'. Apply the screening framework to the request and give the alternative offer.
::: solution
**Opportunistic-imaging-as-quasi-screening ([[#def-principles]], [[#def-delivery]], [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]]): the framework - 'this is screening outside a programme: asymptomatic, low-intermediate-risk individual, test with radiation-and-contrast-and-incidental findings and no randomised mortality evidence for the asymptomatic-low-risk population (the guideline positions against routine coronary-imaging screening); the father's history is the real object - 'family history of premature coronary disease is a genuine risk-enhancer, and the evidence-based route is the risk assessment (the QRISK-and-ESC-risk-modifier frameworks explicitly include it), the lipoprotein(a) and lipid-and-glucose workup, the blood-pressure-and-smoking-and- lifestyle conversation, and the statin-decision-that-needs-no-artery-photograph; where imaging legitimately enters - 'if the 10-year risk calculation lands near the treatment threshold and the patient and clinician want reclassification, the calcium-score argument (the 'no-contrast-no-radiation-lite-decision-refiner' literature) is at least an evidenced option, with the incidental-finding disclosure; and the refusal-with-a-plan grammar - 'I can see why you want the scan; the problem is what it will do to you: the radiation, the incidental nodules you will now chase for years, and the blockage-or-clear result that changes neither of the things that actually determine your risk; here is what changes my and your certainty: the lipid and Lp(a) numbers, the blood pressure, the smoking, and a repeat risk calculation in five years - and if those numbers or your family history deepen, the discussion about imaging is genuinely open.'**
:::

::: exercise Run the programme board {level=2}
Your breast-screening unit reports: recall rate up 2 points after the AI-assist pilot; cancer detection unchanged; radiologist sign-off time down 15 percent; two interval cancers reviewable; GP letters now mention 'AI used'. Advise the board on continuation, with the evidence conditions.
::: solution
**AI-in-a-screening-programme-governance ([[#def-delivery]], [[#def-principles]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]): the read - 'the pilot measured process, not outcome (the 'recall-up-detection-flat' pattern is the classic first-year signature of a sensitivity-threshold shift, and the '15-percent-faster' gain is the economic case, and the interval cancers must be reviewed for AI-related attribution because 'the machine did not flag it' will be the claim); the continuation conditions good programmes set - '1) the outcome protocol prespecified before scale-up (the 'cancer-detection-and-interval-cancer-and-PPV-and-recall-by-reading-mode' series with the sample size that detects the differences that matter, over screening rounds not months), 2) the human-factors monitoring (the automation-bias probes: does second-reader disagreement drop when the AI disagrees - the 'over-reliance audits' the AI-safety literature demands), 3) the false-positive-harms tracking (the recall-anxiety-and-attendance-consequence data the recall-rate rise buys), 4) the transparency standard the GP letters started (the 'AI used, radiologist responsible' disclosure and the patient-facing explanation), and 5) the accountability clause (the 'who signed' remains the radiologist and the 'AI-error-incident-class' defined in the risk register); the decision template: 'continue in evaluation mode with those conditions and a named clinical lead, with de-implementation written into the contract - because the alternative failure mode is worse: the AI quietly becomes the programme, and the evidence base stays where the pilot left it.'**

::: exercise Set the boundary of screening {level=3}
A charity proposes free population prostate, pancreatic and ovarian ultrasound screening in shopping-centre vans for men and women over 50, citing 'early detection saved lives' stories. Write the public-health response: the per-cancer appraisal, the equity and opportunity-cost framing, and the offer you would make instead.
::: solution
**The-van-vs-the-programme ([[#def-principles]], [[#def-delivery]], [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]): the per-cancer appraisal - 'prostate: ultrasound is not a prostate screen (the PSA-and-MRI pathway debate has the evidence; PSA in a van without counselling and MRI capacity is the worst of both); pancreatic: asymptomatic-average-risk screening has no validated test and no mortality evidence (even the high-risk familial cohorts - the CAPS-adjacent MRI-and-endoscopic-ultrasound programmes - apply only to gene-confirmed families and referral is the correct answer there); ovarian: the two large randomised trials (the US PLCO and the UK GGTS) showed mortality nulls with the CA-125-and-ultrasound strategy and thousands of women with false-positive operations - the clearest 'screening-harms-without-screening-benefit' demonstration in the field; the narrative problem - 'the saved-lives stories are selection-of-the-detected testimony, survivorship and lead-time in human form, sincere and statistically worthless - which is the communication challenge, not the science; the equity framing - 'the van model diverts the charity's and the system's money toward the low-yield-visible and away from the proven-underfunded: finishing bowel-screen colonoscopy capacity, lifting cervical coverage in the under-screened, and the lung-screen rollout for smokers are the interventions with mortality evidence and coverage gaps; the counter-offer - 'support the charity's reach for the programmes that work (mobile cervical and bowel and immunisation units with the same vans and the same goodwill), fund the navigation posts that close the uptake gaps, and give the public the symptom-awareness messages the cancer services need anyway (the 'what-to-look-for' campaigns with their modest-but-real upstaging effects); and the sentence the letter ends with: 'we cannot let the desire to help outrun the duty to do good - the evidence of what screening does and does not do is the charity's best argument for the offers it should be making.'**
