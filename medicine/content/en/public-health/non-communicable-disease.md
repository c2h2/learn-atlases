Non-communicable disease causes about three quarters of deaths in high-income countries and a growing majority worldwide, and the public health discipline built around it has to work on the slowest timescales and the deepest causes of any in medicine. **This chapter covers the four disease clusters and their shared behaviours (tobacco, alcohol, diet, physical inactivity, and the metabolic mediating pathway of obesity, hypertension, dyslipidaemia and glucose intolerance), the causal framework (the social determinants acting through commercial and environmental exposure across the life course, the commercial determinants as a studied field, and the health-in-all-policies machinery), the prevention strategy (the Best Buys, the risk-factor management clinics, the screening overlaps, and the WHO targets and their tracking), and the care-delivery half (the chronic-disease-model architectures, multimorbidity and polypharmacy as population problems, and the rehabilitation and palliative integration that the NCD narrative too often forgets).**

## The burden

::: definition The four clusters, five behaviours and the metabolic middle {#def-burden}
**The NCD framing groups cardiovascular disease, cancers, chronic respiratory disease and diabetes as the four major diseases, with mental illness and musculoskeletal and neurological conditions increasingly counted in the same accounting because they share the risk architecture and the care-model problem. The behavioural determinants are few and enormous: tobacco (still the largest single preventible cause, with the addiction economics, the second-hand-smoke evidence that closed workplaces, and the industry's documented playbook now a case study in commercial determinants), alcohol (the dose-response on cancers and liver disease, the marketing and availability and pricing elasticity, and the industry-tactics convergence with tobacco that produced the same critique), diet and physical inactivity (the salt-and-blood-pressure arithmetic and the industry reformulation programmes, the ultra-processed-food epidemiology with its formulation-versus-overconsumption arguments still open, sugar-sweetened-beverage taxes and their evaluation literature, and the obesity environment that the energy-balance physiology chapters own clinically and the environment owns programmatically). The metabolic mediators are where population medicine and clinical biochemistry meet: the blood-pressure distribution shifted left by population salt, the lipid distributions moved by fat-quality reformulation, the adiposity and glucose pathways with their life-course origins (the maternal-nutrition-and-birthweight-and-development-programming literature). And the geography: NCD mortality is concentrated in the working-age years of low-income and middle-income countries (the premature-mortality dividend argument), the risk-factor transition proceeds faster than the income transition, and the double burden of infection and chronic disease is now the norm - which means the NCD chapter is a global-health chapter with an equity heartbeat.**
:::

## The causes of the causes

::: definition Commercial determinants, environments and health in all policies {#def-determinants}
**The behavioural risks do not distribute themselves. The proximate causes are commercial: the tobacco industry's death-and-denial playbook (the internal-documents litigation and the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control's treaty response, the illicit-trade and nicotine-product debates since), the alcohol industry's shift to the Global South and its sponsorship-and-lobbying pattern (the 'best-buy' price-and-availability measures most consistently opposed), and the food-and-ultra-processed-beverage industry's political economy (the reformulation-versus-regulation arguments, the front-of-pack-labelling battles the Chilean and UK trials evaluated, the marketing-to-children evidence and its weak voluntary codes). The structural causes are built: the walkable-versus-driven city (the transport-and-planning-and-housing chapters of epidemiology), the food environment measured in outlet density and price ratios, the air pollution now recognised as a major NCD risk factor in its own right (the particulate-and-cardiopulmonary-mortality arithmetic that links this chapter to the environmental one), the alcohol-and-nicotine-availability licensing, and the workplaces-and-schools-as-settings. The life-course causes add timing: the determinants of maternal health and early child development setting cardiometabolic trajectories, the cumulative-exposure-and-latency logic that makes occupational and environmental cancers a 30-year public health problem, and the 'delayed-returns' economics that make NCD prevention chronically unattractive to electoral cycles. The governance responses are consequently constitutional in flavour: health-in-all-policies (the 'Heath-in-all-policies' tradition from the Marmot-review-era White Papers, the health-impact-assessment tools and their political reality), the whole-of-government NCD platforms and parliamentary-committee-accountability models, the 'Best Buys' compact costed interventions (the salt reduction, tobacco taxation and marketing controls, physical-activity advertising, and the harmful-usage taxes), and the NCD targets (the '25-by-25' premature-mortality goal and the Voluntary Targets' tracking - the monitoring literature's verdict that most countries miss most targets and that accountability without consequence is theatre). The clinician's role in this layer is not incidental: the prescriber is a node in the commercial system, the consultation is where the environment becomes a biography, and the documented 'smoking-stopped-reverses-risk' and blood-pressure-lowering curves are the evidence patients get to hear as permission to expect their choices to matter.**
:::

## The response

::: definition Prevention architecture and chronic-care delivery {#def-response}
**Upstream, the economics did the arguing: the Disease-Control-Priorities and WHO Best-Buy analyses give tobacco and alcohol taxation, salt reduction, and physical-activity mass campaigns the cheapest cost per disability-adjusted-life-year in all of medicine, and the clinical interventions with population reach (the blood-pressure control at treatment thresholds, statins and aspirin after myocardial infarction, and the polypill-and-fixed-dose-combination adherence logic) deliver the 'big-wins' strategy of moving treatment thresholds and treatment coverage together. Service delivery is a design discipline: the chronic-care model (the Wagner framework's interaction redesign - the self-management support, the clinical information systems, the delivery-system design and the skill-mismatch fixes), the practice-nurse-and-pharmacist-and-health-worker task-shifting evidence (the hypertension-titration protocols that community health workers ran to trial-grade blood-pressure control in low-income settings), the registry-and-recall architectures for diabetes and chronic kidney disease (whose recall systems are screening's first cousins), the 'care-planning-and-anticipatory-guidelines' layer (the stroke and heart-failure and COPD and dementia care pathways the NICE-style guidelines write in populations of comorbidity rather than single organs), and the rehabilitation and palliative integration - the functional-decline half of NCD burden that hospital-centred systems under-count and cost studies now force into view. Secondary prevention and case-finding overlap the screening chapter (the atrial-fibrillation opportunistic-checking and the cardiovascular-risk-calculation programs like QRISK-and-assign-adjacent tools with their equity-of-use audits) and carry a constant tension: treating by risk threshold treats the many modestly rather than the few severely, which is epidemiologically rational and individually strange to explain - a conversation the risk-communication literature has actually studied. The accountability instruments close the loop: the NCD observatories and dashboards, the 'best-buy-tracking' scorecards, the 'premature-mortality-by-region-and-deprivation' data that make the equity gap visible quarterly, and the civil-society-and-patient-organisation watchdogs whose documented role in tobacco and diabetes policy is the field's only reliable engine. The pandemic added the lessons now canonical: the NCDs as the severity risk factor (the obesity-diabetes-cardiac-renal-transplant-risk tables), the deferred-diagnosis backlog and its cancer-stage shift (the 'lost-diagnoses' audits), and the telemonitoring-and-community-pharmacy expansion whose evidence the field is still sorting from the emergency-era marketing.**
:::

## Where this leads

The clinical halves of these diseases live in [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[cardiovascular/coronary-artery-disease]], [[endocrine/diabetes]], [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[respiratory/asthma-copd]] and [[pathology/cancer-biology]]; the risk-factor physiology in [[physiology/homeostasis-body-fluids]]; the screening interfaces in [[public-health/screening]]; the determinants science in [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]]; the environmental exposures in [[public-health/environmental-occupational]]; and the economics of the Best Buys in [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].

::: history
**The NCD era began as a surprise. Nineteenth-century public health was built for infection, and the early twentieth century's falling infectious mortality was read as civilisational advance; by the 1930s the insurance and actuarial statistics were already showing the rise of the 'degenerative' diseases, and the post-war Framingham Heart Study (begun 1948, the term 'risk factor' coined there) invented the cohort logic that made blood pressure, cholesterol and smoking population objects. The dose-response evidence came fast and adversarially: the British doctors' study and the Doll-Hill smoking studies of 1950-56 and the 1964 Surgeon-General report's heresy of causation; the diet-and-coronary-disease controversies (the Keys-era fat-and-sugar arguments whose replicas run today); the MRFIT and the multiple-risk-factor-intervention trials whose modest effect sizes taught the field that clinics alone do not change distributions; and the Rose-theorem (the 'sick-individuals-approach-versus-population-approach' framing of 1981) that remains the field's favourite sentence and its hardest implementation. Treatment evidence arrived in parallel: the antihypertensive trials converting 'essential' hypertension from destiny to managed risk, the statin era after the 4S trial of 1994, secondary-prevention aspirin-and-beta-blockade-and-statin bundles, and the polypill idea from 2009 that Wald-and-Law's provocative arithmetic turned into trials. Prevention science learned to study the causes: the Black Report (1980) and the social-determinants lineage, the 'delayed-returns' problem named in the health-commissioning literature, the 'commercial-determinants-of-health' coinage (the 2016 Public-Health-England-adjacent and Lancet-commission formalisation), the tobacco-internal-documents litigation and the Framework-Convention-on-Tobacco-Control of 2003 (global health law's first treaty), the alcohol-industry-tactics research importing the tobacco playbook studies, and the ultra-processed-food epidemiology (the Monteiro-classification-era NOVA studies) with its contested mechanism debates. The governance architecture followed the targets: the 2011 UN General-Assembly political declaration on NCDs (the health-development argument's '25-by-25' premature-mortality goal), the WHO Global Action Plan and its Voluntary Targets, the Best-Buy and costed-intervention compendia, the Global NCD Observatory, and the pandemic-era reckoning (the NCDs as the severity gradient, the diagnosis-backlog audits and the 'build-back-better-for-NCDs' advocacy). The unfinished business is written in the gradient: the field's cheapest, best-evidenced tools (tobacco-and-alcohol prices, salt reformulation, activity environments) are the ones its opponents can block, and its clinical half now spends more on the multimorbidity-and-ageing-of-the-survivors than on the diseases that created the survivors.** - [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[public-health/global-health]], [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]].**
:::

::: summary
- NCDs are four disease clusters (cardiovascular, cancer, respiratory, diabetes/metabolic) with mental and musculoskeletal conditions sharing the architecture; a small set of behaviours and exposures - tobacco, alcohol, diet, inactivity, pollution - act through metabolic mediators over decades.
- The risks are manufactured by markets and environments, not chosen in vacuums: the commercial determinants (pricing, availability, marketing, formulation) explain most of the population distribution, and the social gradient in NCD mortality is among the largest effects in epidemiology.
- The cheapest effective tools are fiscal and regulatory (tobacco and alcohol taxation, salt and fat reformulation, marketing control, smoke-free law, activity environments) - the Best Buys - with clinical protocols (blood-pressure and lipid thresholds, fixed-dose combinations, secondary prevention) delivering the treatment half.
- Delivery requires redesign, not more clinics: chronic-care models, self-management support, task-shifting with protocols, registries and recall, and the rehabilitation-and-palliative integration that hospital systems omit.
- Risk-threshold treatment is population-rational and individually counter-intuitive; explaining number-needed-to-treat and absolute risk is a core clinical skill of the NCD era.
- Elimination-style 'solutions' do not exist for NCDs; the honest metric is premature-mortality gradients by place and deprivation, tracked quarterly with consequences attached.
:::
