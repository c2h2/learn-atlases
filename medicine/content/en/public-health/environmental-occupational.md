Environmental and occupational health is the oldest and the youngest of the public health disciplines: oldest in its tools (sanitation, clean water, and workplace law built most of the twentieth century's life-expectancy gains) and youngest in its problems (the climate crisis, the chemical exposures we are running unconsented, and the occupational morass of precarious and platform work). **This chapter covers water and sanitation and their unfinished two-billion-person business, ambient and household air pollution, chemical exposures from lead to endocrine disruptors, the built environment, and climate and occupational health as the fields where medicine's preventive and curative halves must meet.**

## Water and air

::: definition Water, sanitation, hygiene and particulate matter {#def-water-air}
**The water-borne world still drives a large share of preventable disease in the poorest countries: the faecal-oral infections and their prevention by the sequence of sanitation, piped water and chlorination. The cholera, typhoid and childhood-diarrhoea arithmetic makes water supply the greatest medical technology in history, and its maintenance is never finished anywhere: source protection, treatment, distribution integrity, chlorine residuals, biofilm control, and the lead pipes that the Flint crisis used to teach wealthy countries they had not finished learning. Water safety in clinics and schools is infection control's foundation, with its trachoma, schistosomiasis and worm links, and the post-disaster water-safety manual that climate shocks keep re-printing. Air contributes two exposures with opposite politics. Ambient particulate matter carries the epidemiology inherited from the 1952 London smog through modern cohort and time-series studies: a log-linear concentration-response with no demonstrable safe threshold. The measurement debates distinguish fine mass from composition and ultrafractions; the mechanism chapter runs through systemic inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, autonomic tone and plaque stability, and the evidence now extends to lung cancer, diabetes, birthweight and cognition, with cardiopulmonary death dominating the burden. Household air pollution from solid fuels supplies the cooking-smoke data on childhood pneumonia, COPD, low birthweight and lung cancer; the cookstove trials taught that measured exposure reduction, not stove distribution, is the endpoint. The clinical consequences are a counselling list: air-quality-index asthma and COPD action plans, wildfire-smoke advice (filtration and proper respirators, not surgical masks), activity reduction during episodes, and an exposure history taken as routinely as smoking status. The policy instruments carry the causality: the clean-air acts with their low-emission-zone evidence, and the World Health Organization guideline revisions whose argument about feasibility versus evidence is applied ethics built on epidemiological fact.**
:::

## Chemicals and buildings

::: definition Contaminants, toxins and dose-time-response logic {#def-chemicals}
**Environmental toxicology supplies the field's grammar. The dose-makes-the-poison principle carries modern complications: the non-monotonic curves of endocrine disruptors, the mixtures problem, and the critical windows of development, against reference doses derived from adults. Exposure reconstruction is its own science - biological monitoring, the biomarkers of exposure and effect, the exposome programme, and the retrospective reconstruction from records that turns chemical disasters into cohort studies. Latency is what makes this field legally feared: asbestos and mesothelioma (the latency that outlaws the injunction), lead and childhood intelligence (the repeated 'no safe level' revisions), benzene and leukaemia, thalidomide and diethylstilbestrol and the developmental window, Minamata's mercury and itai-itai's cadmium as court-recognised environmental diseases, and Flint as the modern case study in the epidemiology of trust. The exposure inventory still matters clinically. Occupational: crystalline silica and silicosis with its tuberculosis multiplier, coal workers' pneumoconiosis, asbestos and artificial mineral fibres, solvent neurotoxicity, and the latency tables for vinyl chloride (liver angiosarcoma), arsenic (skin, lung, bladder) and aromatic amines (bladder) that make occupational cancer history-taking an exercise in decades; organophosphate cholinergic syndromes with the developmental-neurotoxicity debate. Consumer and domestic: flame retardants, phthalates and bisphenols with their body-burden epidemiology; the per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances and the kidney, testicular and immune findings; microplastics, where the honest sentence is 'present everywhere, effects unclear'; radon as the second lung-cancer cause; carbon monoxide with its detector prescription; and damp and mould with their asthma evidence and housing-first remedy. The regulatory machinery is where medicine argues with statistics: risk assessment versus the precautionary principle; threshold versus non-threshold carcinogens and the acceptable-daily-intake doctrines; the burden-of-proof question separating the European registration system from the older American approach; occupational exposure limits set by triangulation between agencies, industry and unions; and the epidemiologist as expert witness. The PFAS litigation is teaching in real time that 'no evidence of harm' often means 'no one looked', and that mixtures are regulated one molecule at a time while exposure arrives as a cocktail.**
:::

## Climate and work

::: definition Climate change and occupational health as synthesis fields {#def-climate-work}
**Climate closes the loop between populations and patients through four pathways. Direct: heat and its interactions with cardiopulmonary and renal disease and with drugs that block thermoregulation (the beta-blocker, diuretic, anticholinergic and antipsychotic lists), the urban heat island mediated by housing quality and the right to cool, and heat-health action plans whose evaluations show that warning is easy and acting on warnings hard. Infectious: vector and zoonotic range shifts arriving in the notifiable-disease data - the autochthonous dengue and the travel-history-negative cases - with attribution science honest about its limits: naming a single outbreak 'caused by' a heatwave is a moral, not a scientific, statement. Nutritional: the crop-yield and micronutrient-decline projections feeding the malnutrition crisis. Displacement and mental health: the post-disaster psychiatric epidemiology (the disaster-distress-displacement sequence with its PTSD, depression and suicide tails) and the eco-anxiety and solastalgia literature that psychiatry now treats as a signal, not a symptom. The co-benefits frame makes the medicine persuasive: the strategies that cut emissions also cut disease immediately - active travel (with its coronary, diabetes, depression and obesity co-benefits), cleaner cooking and heating, and diets shifted away from red meat. Occupational health is the field's other synthesis. Its history is the control hierarchy - engineer the hazard out before mandating equipment, a ladder industry routinely inverts. Its present is the changing workforce: platform and gig work whose employment status decides whose lungs get protected, and the construction, agriculture and care-work exposures whose illnesses are under-reported because reporting costs contracts. Its clinical practice is unique in medicine: the occupational physician serves a population with two masters, and fitness-for-work opinions, surveillance regimes and disclosure dilemmas belong to the ethics chapters. Its epidemiological practice remains the gold standard of causation in court, from the asbestos judgments to the night-shift controversy. Its future problem is written in the determinants: precarious work, long hours and heat increase exactly where protection is thinnest.**
:::

::: example The boiler that was not flu {#ex-co}
A GP practice books six elderly patients with headache and vomiting across two adjacent streets in November; two return with confusion and ataxia; one is found collapsed at home by a neighbour.
::: solution
**Carbon monoxide is a cluster diagnosis ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-water-air]], [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]]): the pattern that should trigger the call is household or street clustering, the 'everyone with flu at once, plus the pet, plus the boiler last serviced in 2009' history, and the headache-vomiting-confusion sequence. The cherry-pink colour is an autopsy photograph, not a sign, and the pulse oximeter reads beautifully because it cannot distinguish carboxyhaemoglobin from oxyhaemoglobin - co-oximetry on blood gas is the exam fact that saves the misdiagnosed. The immediate medicine: remove and ventilate, 100 percent oxygen, poison-centre discussion for hyperbaric consideration in the neurological, cardiac and pregnant classes, and counselling about the delayed neuropsychiatric syndrome and its follow-up. The public health response belongs to the same minute: the gas, buildings, fire-and-rescue and health-protection notifications (the possible-environmental-exposure duty), the gas-board emergency attendance, street-wide metering and the neighbouring-flat sweep, because cluster carbon monoxide is a building fault, not a coincidence. The system lesson: a same-symptom-same-week syndromic flag should have rung by patient three, and deaths following a missed cluster are attributable to the missing signal - which is why these cases end in serious-incident reviews and prevention-of-future-deaths reports.**
:::
:::

::: example The builder with the breathless chest {#ex-asbestos}
A 68-year-old plasterer from a demolition firm presents with progressive breathlessness; his occupational history is full of 'old finishes, tiles and textured coating'; the radiograph suggests plaques and a lower-zone reticular pattern.
::: solution
**The latency catches up ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-climate-work]], [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]): the clinical work is the differential of asbestosis against usual interstitial pneumonia, pleural disease, mesothelioma and lung cancer - the HRCT and lung function and the multidisciplinary discussion that owns the asbestosis-versus-IPF question, with the occupational history deciding which criteria apply. Smoking interaction must be counselled carefully: the multiplicative asbestos-and-smoking lung-cancer risk is stated to motivate cessation, not to assign blame. The compensation work is also clinical care: the industrial-injury benefit claim (the prescribed-disease lists for asbestosis, pleural plaques and mesothelioma, and the occupational-disease reporting forms that double as surveillance), the 'date of knowledge' medico-legal conversation about limitation periods, and the tracing of former employers' insurers - the problems that specialist claimant firms and support organisations exist for. Surveillance obligations follow: the occupational-health referral, the exposed-and-still-working cohort from the same firm, and the risk-communication dilemma of a diagnosis that is also a verdict on someone else's 1980s. And the public health truth, restated each generation: the banned material is still in the built environment, and the demolition-and-refurbishment exposure story repeats exactly as often as the inspections lag.**
:::

## Where this leads

The clinical respiratory consequences are in [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]] and [[respiratory/asthma-copd]], the poisoning medicine in [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]], infectious climate shifts in [[infectious-diseases/tropical-infections]] and [[public-health/communicable-disease-control]], the determinants framing in [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], the pollution risks of [[public-health/non-communicable-disease]], and the global architecture in [[public-health/global-health]].

::: history
**The field's founding victories are statistical: John Snow's broad-pump cholera work (1854) and the registrar-general's bills of mortality that made environment measurable; the Great Stink, the Bazalgette sewers and the Chadwick report's sanitation-as-statecraft; and the public-health acts that created medical officers of health with their notification and vital-statistics empires. Occupational chapters opened with factory and mine inspection after the accidents and the children's-commissioners' testimonies of 1842, then the industrial toxicology record: Ramazzini's physicians-of-trades tradition rewritten by Alice Hamilton's strike-bound analyses, the phossy-jaw of match workers, and the radium dial-painters' jaw necroses that made the 'body-burden' concept. Radiological protection built the low-dose apparatus through the Radium Girls' case law, the Hiroshima Life-Span Studies and the linear-no-threshold debate, and the dose-conversion culture of the radiation-protection commissions with their as-low-as-reasonably-achievable doctrine. Lead supplies the arc: the clean-air battles over tetraethyl petrol, the revision of 'safe' blood-lead levels downward for four decades, and the intelligence-cost benefit analyses that eventually made abatement obvious. Air pollution's legal era begins with the 1952 London fog and the Clean Air Act of 1956, the American environmental-agency founding and the national-standard revision fights, and - in water - the Bangladesh handpump catastrophe (the arsenic in the wells that was meant to end diarrhoea) as the largest mass poisoning ever diagnosed by geologists. The industrial-accident regime followed Seveso and Bhopal with their conventions on notification, land-use planning and process safety. Rachel Carson's Silent Spring (1962) and the DDT story created the modern chemical-safety and environmental-movement intersection, while the 'Six Cities' and American-Cancer-Society cohorts of the 1990s turned particulate matter from a visibility nuisance into a mortality risk factor, and the household-air-pollution reckoning made the Lancet call cooking smoke a leading global risk. Climate health's institutional history runs from the meteorological-mortality time series and the 1995 Chicago heatwave (the birth of the heat-action-plan experiment) through the World Health Organization quantifications, the Lancet Countdown's annual accountability reports, and the slow recognition that health was absent from the first decade of climate diplomacy - an absence the recent health declarations and programmes are correcting. The PFAS and microplastic eras now repeat the oldest lesson of the field: exposure precedes evidence, evidence precedes regulation by a generation, and medicine's contribution is the exposure history, the cohort study, and the willingness to say so in court.** - [[public-health/global-health]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]].**
:::

::: summary
- Water, sanitation and hygiene remain the world's unfinished medical project where they are missing, and their maintenance is never finished anywhere.
- Air pollution is a top-tier cardiometabolic and respiratory risk with a log-linear concentration-response and no demonstrated safe threshold; the clinical interface is action plans, episode advice and exposure history-taking.
- Chemical exposures are governed by dose, timing and latency: developmental windows make adult extrapolation dangerous, mixtures defeat single-substance regulation, and the classic cases show latency outrunning both law and memory.
- Occupational protection works best up the control hierarchy (engineer it out) and fails where work is precarious - the gig economy is now an exposure problem.
- Climate acts through heat, infection, nutrition, displacement and mental health; its most persuasive clinical frame is co-benefits, because the emission-reduction strategies are also this decade's best disease-prevention strategies.
- Every exposure question is answered in the history: occupation, housing, water, fuel and the neighbourhood map belong in every consultation - the exposure history is environmental medicine's stethoscope.
:::

## Exercises
::: exercise Take the exposure history {level=1}
A 45-year-old warehouse cleaner with chronic cough, headaches and poor concentration has had 'four normal chest X-rays' and three antibiotic courses; she works nights at a distribution centre. Write the exposure history you would take and the hypotheses it tests.
::: solution
**The history that orders uncertainty ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-water-air]]): take it as a day in the life - the substances by trade name, what is mixed with what, the ventilation, and the work pattern. The specific warehouse questions: bleach or hypochlorite cleaners used with acidic descalers (the chlorine-gas generator), isocyanates in spray adhesives, dust from cardboard and corrugated packaging, exhaust from the forklifts, and the pest-control schedule. Then the symptom map by place and time: better on holidays is a diagnosis, and colleagues with the same cough is an outbreak notification. Then home and hobbies: mould and damp, the gas boiler, weekend painting and solvents. The hypotheses the history tests: occupational asthma and irritant-induced airways-dysfunction syndrome; chronic intermittent gas or vapour exposure; building-related damp illness; and the non-occupational alternatives the same history keeps in play (reflux, post-nasal drip, anxiety hyperventilation). The actions: serial peak-flow at work and home on the validated occupational-asthma grid with referral to occupational health carrying the diary; the employer's risk-assessment documents requested as of right; and the simultaneity of treat, report and protect - the inhalers, the statutory exposure notification where required, and workplace changes that should not cost her the job. The consultation length is justified by the arithmetic: four normal X-rays cost more than the ten-minute history that would have explained them.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Advise during a heatwave {level=2}
It is day four of a heat event forecast to reach 39 degrees for five more days. Your polyclinic serves a deprived, ageing population of top-floor flats on cardiac and psychiatric polypharmacy. Write the operational plan: clinical, pharmaceutical and communication.
::: solution
**A heat plan is a drug plan ([[#def-climate-work]], [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]]): the clinical register first - the medications that break thermoregulation and fluid balance (anticholinergics and antipsychotics inhibiting sweating, diuretics and ACE inhibitors and NSAIDs in the dehydration and acute-kidney-injury arithmetic, beta-blockade limiting cutaneous flow, stimulants and alcohol), with lithium toxicity and sedation masking confusion as the classic pairs. Write 'heat-day rules' per patient as you would sick-day rules: fluid and salt targets, activity windows, cool-room plans, and the check-in call list. The operational layer: the register of heat-vulnerable patients (the frailty and severe-mental-illness and post-stroke lists), proactive phone-or-visit rounds on the hottest days, the clinic re-configured (vaccinations and bloods to the morning, waiting areas cooled, water points, the day centre and library as refuges with the transport solved), the escalation criteria written with the ambulance and acute trusts (when to cool actively, when to admit), and the staff rota and PPE burden addressed because heat breaks the carers too. The communication: warning messages that state the specific action (the 'close the curtains at 9am, open everything at 10pm' advice beats 'drink water'), delivered through the channels the population actually uses in that deprivation profile (the neighbour-network and the mosque-and-pub-and-shop messengers, not the app). The evaluation afterwards: the calls made versus patients seen, the emergency attendances by category, and the deaths reviewable - because heat mortality is excess deaths in the register, and the audit is the plan's exam.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Rebuild after Flint {level=2}
A post-industrial city's water inquiry finds blood-lead levels rising in four wards after a source change and corrosion-control lapse; you join the health response at month three, when the argument is between reassurance and alarm. Advise the medical-lead team on the evidence, the clinical programme, and the trust problem.
::: solution
**Lead, windows and trust ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-water-air]], [[paediatrics/growth-development]]): the evidence tier: the dose-response for childhood intelligence and behaviour extends below any historical 'safe' level (the 'every microgram has a slope' literature), so the clinical question is not poisoning but developmental risk; adults are a smaller but real hypertension-and-kidney-and-pregnancy story; the corrosion chemistry explains the exposure (the phosphate-control lapse and the lead-service-lines' particulate sampling, whose episodic release the standard 1-litre tests under-detect). The clinical programme: blood-lead testing targeted by age and address (the pregnant-and-6-to-72-months cohort first, the capillary-then-venous-confirmatory pathway, the result-communication scripts that do not blame parents for paint), the developmental-services surge (the early-intervention and school-support entitlements activated by result, the nutrition counselling on iron and calcium adequacy), the 'water-advice ladder' with the filters-certified-for-lead-and-the-cold-tap-and-no-boiling rules stated precisely, and the register for longitudinal follow-up because the harm is educational, not acute. The trust problem is clinical: the inquiry will find the reassurance messages preceded the sampling reform, so every statement must carry its own data provenance ('these are third-party verified'), and the messenger must be the paediatricians and nurses, not the utility. The legal-and-compensation interface: the documentation standards that serve both medicine and claim, and the 'no-child-loses-a-claim-because-nobody-tested' testing programme. The structural fix the health service must demand: the service-line replacement schedule with funding, the corrosion-control fail-safes with alarms, and the public dashboard - because the epidemiology of trust showed the second exposure (to institutional denial) does more harm than the first did by month three.**
:::

::: exercise Cost the retrofit {level=2}
Your city must choose between a housing-insulation-and-damp retrofit programme and a fuel-poverty payment scheme; the treasury prefers payments because they are 'targeted'. Write the health submission: the evidence, the co-benefits arithmetic, and the distributional analysis.
::: solution
**Warm-homes-evidence ([[#def-water-air]], [[#def-climate-work]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]]): the health evidence - the cold-home trials and Welsh-and-New-Zealand-style retrofit evaluations showing respiratory and cardiovascular admissions falling, mental-health and sleep improvements, and the mould-and-asthma effect sizes, against the payment scheme's honest weakness (energy prices and house physics do not move with a lump sum, and the 'heat-or-eat' behaviour persists). The co-benefits arithmetic: the retrofit cuts fuel demand permanently (the payment buys one winter), cuts emissions (the climate line), creates local jobs and supply chains (the treasury's own language), and prevents the hospital bed-days that are the health budget's expensive item - the 'whole-system cost-benefit' studies from the English commissions quantify these and consistently favour property measures when hospital costs are counted. The distributional analysis the treasury cares about: payments reach the registered poor, retrofits reach the physical worst (the poorly-insulated private-rented and owner-occupied hard-to-treat homes where the sick actually live), and the two maps overlap imperfectly - so the answer is not either-or but 'fabric first, payments as bridge.' The design details that decide success: the 'one-visit whole-house' standard versus the drafty partial measure, the ventilation-companion rule (insulation without airflow manufactures mould), the tenant-consent and landlord-coercion questions, the quality-assured-install-and-warranty-and-maintenance regime, and the health-service role (the 'prescription-for-warmth' referral pilots and the admission-data feedback that proves the programme to next year's treasury). The closing line: a payment treats the symptom of a cold house for one winter; a retrofit is the only intervention in this chapter that prescribes on the building.**
:::

::: example The farm summer {#ex-pesticide}
A district health office in an farming region notes twelve cases of 'gastroenteritis' and three 'mystery tremor' clinic visits among shepherd families within ten days of the sheep-dipping season starting; two farmworkers' children live near the dipping race.
::: solution
**Organophosphate-and-pesticide-season ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-climate-work]], [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]]): the pattern - the cholinergic syndrome arrives as 'gastroenteritis' (the vomiting, diarrhoea, sweating, salivation, miosis, bradycardia or tachycardia, fasciculations, and the smell of the product on clothing as the examination finding nobody documents), the tremor-and-anxiety cluster in children near the dipping race, and the intermediate syndrome and delayed neuropathy as the complications the first 48 hours miss. The immediate actions: the poison-centre and occupational-and-environmental-notification calls, the confirmation (the plasma-and-erythrocyte-cholinesterase levels with their timing caveats, and the product identification from the container), the atropine-and-pralidoxime availability briefed to the rural clinics with the 'treat-the-clinical-syndrome-not-the-number' rule, and the decontamination discipline (the gloves and gowns that stop the clinician becoming case thirteen). The exposure investigation: the dipping-race-and-spray-drift-and-reentry-interval questions, the 'children-and-animals-and-watercourse' pathways, the product-registration-and-label-compliance check (the illegal-or-mislabelled-class problem in several regions), and the farm-workers' own account as the exposure epidemiology. The prevention programme the season demands: the re-entry-interval and PPE education delivered in the workers' languages before the next season (the evidence says post-incident training converts nobody), the notification-and-surveillance forms that feed a real register, and the agricultural-advisory services engaged as public-health partners rather than adversaries. The developmental footnote that makes the children urgent: the childhood-organophosphate-neurodevelopment literature's slope on attention and memory, which is why 'mild' exposures in the young are programme events, not reassuring clinic letters.**
:::

::: example The office with the headaches {#ex-sbs}
Over one winter term, a school's infant unit reports a headache-and-eczema cluster and two asthma exacerbations; staff mention 'the building smells sweet when the weather turns'; the portable classroom was installed after mould was painted over in 2019.
::: solution
**Sick-building-is-a-diagnosis ([[#def-chemicals]], [[#def-water-air]], [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]): the method - the 'symptoms-by-room-and-time' map (better at weekends is the school version of the holiday sign), the ventilation audit (the air-change-rates and the CO2-as-proxy data that most school buildings fail), the damp-and-water-ingress survey (the painted-over mould's history, the roof-and-gutter-and-groundwater faults that recurs each wet season), and the source hunt behind the smell (the old solvents and sealants in the portable, the 'off-gassing-and-ventilation-on-arrival' construction-commissioning failures, and the VOC and formaldehyde questions). The clinical layer: the respiratory-and-skin-and-headache phenotyping (the asthma-control reviews and the inhaler-supply-and-action-plan-and-exercise-tolerance audit the school nurse runs, the eczema-steroid-and-wet-wrap support), and the honest statement that some symptom clusters remain unexplained after a perfect investigation ('sick building syndrome' as a real experience with a weak exposure science - the phrase's own history of dismissed patients is a caution). The actions that work: remediate the source and the ventilation before any medical reassurance (the 'fix-the-building-first' rule), the interim measures (the portable vacated or the air-cleaners-and-enhanced-ventilation with CO2 verification), the statutory-standard compliance (the education-department-place-of-returns-assessment duties), the transparent communication with parents (the data posted, the remediation timeline, the symptom-reporting channel), and the surveillance follow-up (the asthma-and-absence data as the outcome measures). The governance lesson: 'a painted-over mould problem and a sweet-smelling portable are a capital-planning story with a paediatric presentation; the school health service's report is the only place the two get read together.'**
:::

::: exercise Write the radon campaign {level=1}
Public Health maps show your region's granite belt with 15 percent of homes above the action level; lung cancer rates in never-smoking women are the country's highest. Plan the radon-awareness and testing campaign: the audience, the message, the logistics and the evaluation.
::: solution
**The-invisible-gas-campaign ([[#def-chemicals]], [[public-health/screening]]): the audience - 'the high-radon-postcode households (the area-based targeting that made the UK and Irish and US campaigns' cost-per-test work), with the equity add-on (the older homes, the solid floors, the under-nins and crawl spaces, and the renters who cannot test what they do not own), and the sub-audience that matters: the 'my-aunt-died-of-lung-cancer-and-never-smoked' women whose family story is the campaign's own message. The message design: radon as the 'second-lung-cancer-cause-and-the-only-one-you-can-measure-at-home', the risk stated multiplicatively with smoking (the 'if you smoke and live in a high-radon home' sentence that carries the cessation message for free), and the mitigation promise (the sump-and-membrane-and-sealed-floor systems whose effectiveness the post-mitigation retest proves). The logistics: the postal-test kits with prepaid return and the door-knock-and-pharmacy-and-library-and-GP-waiting-room distribution, the test conditions stated (the long-term test, the ground-floor bedroom-and-living-room pair, the 'keep-the-windows-as-normal' rule), the results-service with the action-level explanation and the mitigator referral, and the mitigation-grant schemes where they exist (the 'who-pays-for-the-sump' question decides participation). The evaluation: the tests-returned-and-mitigations-by-area, the retest-verified-success rates, and the long-line outcome (the 'radon-and-lung-cancer-models' estimate the lives-saved-per-mitigated-home; the campaign's own mortality data take decades, so process metrics and modelling carry the accountability). The honest caveat for the brief: the campaign must not imply that avoiding radon resembles avoiding smoking in effect size - it is the cheapest lung-cancer prevention available to a postcode, and that sentence is both true and sufficient.**

::: exercise Design the heat-standard case {level=2}
Your country has no occupational maximum indoor temperature or work-stoppage rule for extreme heat. Draft the consultation response: the evidence, the international comparison, the feasibility arguments, and the enforcement design.
::: solution
**A-heat-rule-for-work ([[#def-climate-work]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the evidence - 'the occupational-heat-mortality-and-morbidity data (the construction-and-agriculture-and-delivery-and-foundry-and-kitchen death series, the kidney-disease-of-unknown-origin epidemic in Mesoamerican and other agricultural workers now attributed to recurrent heat-and-dehydration), the physiological ceiling (the wet-bulb-and-core-temperature literature and the 'humid-heat-beyond-human-workability' projections), and the work-loss economics the employers respect (the ILO-style output-loss estimates per degree above comfort). The international comparison: the WBGT-index-based regimes (the American industrial-hygiene thresholds and the Gulf states' midday summer work bans with their documented enforcement), the European OSHA guidance, the Australian 'safe-working-environment' heat duties, and the 'many countries regulate nothing and lose workers quietly' contrast. The feasibility answers: the phased application (the outdoor-and-heat-exposed-sectors first), the wet-bulb-globe-temperature instrument standard with simple metrics for small firms, the duty structure ('risk-assess-and-control-and-provide-water-shade-rest-and-acclimatisation-and-training' as a code of practice before prosecution), the acclimatisation protocols (the 'new-and-returning-workers-graduated-exposure' rules that kill the heatstroke cases), and the 'cooling-and-scheduling-and-rotations' control hierarchy applied honestly (the shade engineering that beats the electrolyte poster). The enforcement design: the labour-inspectorate resourcing (the self-inspection-plus-spot-audit-plus-whistleblower-protection mix that the construction studies show outperforms the complaint-driven model alone), the 'no-retaliation-for-stopping-unsafe-heat-work' clause, the temperature-and-incident-data returns, and the public naming register. The health-service line in the consultation: the emergency-department admissions, the ambulance demand and the dialysis-clinic disruptions on heat days arrive at NHS-scale cost annually - the rule is one of the few occupational interventions whose health economics are already measurable in this year's accounts.**

::: exercise Judge the air-quality legal case {level=2}
A mother of an asthmatic seven-year-old sues the state after a court finds roadside pollution exceeded legal limits for four years near her school; she cites his admission episodes and your clinic's records. Advise as the expert: causation levels (population versus individual), what the records can and cannot show, and the honest opinion you would sign.
::: solution
**Two-causations-one-patient ([[#def-water-air]], [[#def-chemicals]], [[epidemiology-ebm/bias-confounding]]): the framework - 'population causation is settled (the concentration-response coefficients say excess pollution caused excess admissions in the city - the 'necessary-component-cause' logic), individual causation is a probability statement about this child's episodes, and the expert's job is to keep the two levels visible rather than smuggle one into the other. What the records show: the exposure timeline (the school-frontage NO2 and PM monitoring against his attendance years, the 'near-road-versus-background' gradients), the outcome timeline (the symptom diaries and inhaler usage and admission dates and their seasonality-and-viral-epidemic confounding - the honest table), and the intervention natural experiments (the family's move, the clean-air-zone implementation, the summer-holiday patterns) which are the individual-level version of the exposure-response study. The confounders named before the other side names them: the second-hand smoke and mould and bedding-mite and respiratory-virus and socio-deprivation list. The opinion you can sign: 'on the balance of probabilities, the excess roadside pollution more than trivially contributed to the exacerbation burden of this asthmatic child during these years' - defensible when the child is exposed, susceptible, documented, and the epidemiological coefficient is large, with the quantitative contribution estimated (the fraction-of-effect arithmetic from the concentration-response function with its uncertainty stated). The additions that make the report useful: the developmental literature (the 'traffic-related-exposure-and-lung-function-deficits-in-cohort-studies' as the background that harm is not only exacerbations), the mitigation-and-compensation-adjacent recommendations (the 'filter-and-window-and-advice' and the 'school-frontage-traffic-management' measures), and the sentence on uncertainty that survives cross-examination: the science says the population effect is certain and the individual effect is probable, and the law only ever asked for the second.**
