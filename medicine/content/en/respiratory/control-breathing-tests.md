Ventilation is the autonomic system that answers to two masters: the brainstem's metabolic controller — a negative-feedback loop regulating CSF and arterial H⁺ (chiefly via CO₂), with peripheral oxygen sensing as the emergency input — and the cortex, which sings, talks, holds its breath, panics and breathes-against-will. Almost every abnormal breathing pattern is the loop's parameters made visible: its **gain** (how hard ventilation answers a given CO₂), its **threshold** (the CO₂ at which it answers), and its **plant** (the lung and chest wall that convert effort into gas exchange). Sleep disorders, opioid respiratory depression, periodic breathing and the breathlessness of anxiety are all loop-and-state diseases.

This chapter covers rhythm generation, the chemoreflex arcs and their interactions, the clinical tests of control, and sleep-disordered breathing.

## The generator and its feedback

::: definition Rhythm generation and behavioural override {#def-rhythm}
The respiratory rhythm arises in the **brainstem central pattern generator — the pre-Bötzinger complex and the ventral respiratory group's inspiratory and premotor neurons (the calcium-activated pacemaker-and-network bursting — [[physiology/membrane-potentials]]'s conductance-reserve physiology)**, patterned by the pontine pneumotaxic and apneustic centres (the switch-like shaping of the inspiratory off-point and the apneusis of pontine lesions), relayed to the diaphragm (phrenic, C3–5 — [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]) and intercostals and accessory motor nuclei, with the **retrotrapezoid nucleus and related medullary chemoreceptive neurones serving as the brain's CO₂/H⁺ sensors (the central chemoreceptor population)**. Three control layers coexist: (1) **automatic metabolic control** (the loop below), (2) **behavioural/voluntary control** (the corticobulbar and corticospinal pathways — the reason you can voluntarily hyperventilate to unconsciousness, sing through expiration, and the painful dissociation of the "air hunger" mismatch of [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]'s corollary-comparison dyspnoea), and (3) **state and limbic inputs** (the wakefulness drive — the behavioural motor tone that sleep withdraws, explaining why central apnoeas live in sleep; the emotion, pain and anticipatory tachypnoeas — [[psychiatry/anxiety-disorders]]). Reflex shapers: the **Hering–Breuer inflation reflex** (pulmonary stretch receptors truncating inspiration — potent in the neonate ([[paediatrics/newborn]]) and during exercise and with positive-pressure inflation; its vagal afferent arm in the diving-and-deflation reflexes), the irritant and J-receptors (the rapid-shallow reflexes and cough of [[respiratory/asthma-copd]] and [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]), and chest-wall and muscle mechanoreceptors feeding the dyspnoea percept ([[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]'s corollary loop).
:::

::: definition The chemoreflex arcs {#def-chemo}
**Central chemoreception:** CO₂ diffuses freely across the blood–brain barrier into CSF and cerebral extracellular fluid, where carbonic-anhydrase-generated H⁺ (the barrier's poor H⁺ permeability is the point — arterial H⁺ barely reaches the sensor; [[physiology/acid-base]]'s CSF-pH logic) drives the retrotrapezoid and medullary sensors: **the dominant controller of minute-ventilation — the CO₂ response curve's steep near-linearity (the widget), its threshold at the apnoeic point (~PaCO₂ 40 at sea level, reset by pregnancy's progesterone-driven hyperventilation — [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], altitude acclimatization's renal bicarbonate loss ([[#ex-cs]], and training)), and its gain modulated up by hypoxia, exercise feedforward, catecholamines and fever and down by opioids, alcohol, sedatives, anaesthetics and sleep (the µ-receptor actions on the pre-Bötzinger rhythm — [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]] and [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]).** **Peripheral chemoreception:** the **carotid bodies (the glomus type-I cells' oxygen-sensing — the hypoxia-inhibited potassium currents, calcium entry and dopamine/ATP release onto the sinus nerve of Hering to the nucleus tractus solitarius — the same HIF-and-channel biology of [[cell-biochemistry/energy-metabolism]]'s oxygen sensing; the aortic bodies contributing in animals and the human aortic component minor)** are **the only substantial hypoxia detectors: acute hypoxia stimulates ventilation (the hyperbolic hypoxic-response curve — needing concurrent hypercapnia-or-alone interactions: hypoxia's stimulation is potentiated at high CO₂ and blunted at low), causes the brief hypoxic ventilatory decline, and drives the acute hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]]); chronic intermittent hypoxia (the sleep-apnoea carotid body — and the chronic continuous hypoxia of altitude and failure) potentiates the loop's gain (the hyper-responsive phenotype and its sympathetic and blood-pressure consequences — [[cardiovascular/hypertension]]'s sleep-apnoea chapter)**; the carotid sinus's baroreceptor neighbour ([[cardiovascular/circulation-blood-pressure]]) shares the nerve — the surgical and radiating-therapy deafferentations' clinical combinations. The two arcs interact multiplicatively (the classic CO₂×O₂ response-surface experiments), and **the clinical syndromes map onto them: the lost central drive of opioid overdose and central hypoventilation syndromes ([[#ex-ondi]], the obesity-hypoventilation loop-gain question — [[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]'s type 2 chapter), the sensitised peripheral arc of heart failure's and sleep-apnoea's high-gain periodic breathing ([[#ex-cs]]) and the carotid-body hyperactivity that causes the rare central-hypercapnic-with-hyperventilation-and-hypertension phenotype (the carotid-body resection trial era)** — with the H+ note that **metabolic acidosis drives peripheral (carotid-body) and central sensors together (the Kussmaul breathing of [[renal/acid-base-disorders]] and [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]) and that the peripheral arc also senses hyperkalaemia ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]])**.
:::

::: example The crescendo-decrescendo breaths {#ex-cs}
A 71-year-old with EF 25 % heart failure is noted by his wife to pause breathing for ~15 seconds, then overbreathe with arousals, overnight; daytime ABG shows PaCO₂ 4.1 kPa, and the sleep study's AHI is 38/hour with central events and a sawtooth flow pattern.
::: solution
**Cheyne–Stokes respiration with central sleep apnoea — the control loop oscillating about its set point (the loop-gain formalism made audible).** The mechanism: **heart failure's prolonged circulation time and hyperventilation put the apnoeic threshold just below daytime PaCO₂ (his 4.1 kPa sits near it — [[#def-chemo]]); each ventilatory overshoot (from high loop gain — the sensitised carotid bodies, raised chemoreflex gain and the delayed feedback of slow flow) drives CO₂ below threshold → central apnoea → CO₂ climbs and oxygen falls → the generator re-ignites → the crescendo (the patient's and bed-partner's oscillation — the same physics as the altitude periodic breathing of [[respiratory/gas-exchange]]'s acclimatisation and the high-gain phenotype that survives as the post-hyperventilation apnoea of breath-hold divers ([[#ex-blackout]]-adjacent)) — the arousals and the sympathetic surges fragmenting sleep and raising blood pressure (the [[cardiovascular/hypertension]] and [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]] interactions).** Management: **treat the failure hard (the four pillars — the EF and the congestion and the loop gain improve together — [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]), fix the oxygen and the opioids and the altitude contributors, CPAP as the first device (the stabilising pneumatic splint and CO₂-threshold raising), and the honest modern caveat on adaptive servo-ventilation: the SERVE-HF signal (increased cardiovascular mortality in the reduced-ejection-fraction central-apnoea phenotype — the device's CO₂-lowering overshooting the already-low threshold — a loop-physiology lesson written as a black-box warning), with the supine-position and sleep-hygiene and pharmacological (acetazolamide, theophylline — the respiratory-stimulant adenosine-blockade) options trial-by-trial ([[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]'s ventilatory-support chapter) — and the driving-and-sleepiness safety conversation ([[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]'s notification rules).**
:::

::: example The baby who stops breathing {#ex-ondi}
A term neonate has two apnoeic spells with desaturation and no effort on the monitor; between episodes the baby feeds and cries normally, but the spells recur in sleep; the mother recalls "he never breathed properly when asleep since birth".
::: solution
**Sleep-state-associated central hypoventilation — test for congenital central hypoventilation syndrome (CCHS, Ondine's curse).** The mechanism: **loss-of-function in the PHOX2B homeobox gene (the autosomal-dominant, mostly de-novo polyalanine-repeat expansions and the worse non-polyalanine phenotypes — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]'s de-novo-dominance and repeat-expansion teaching) destroys the retrotrapezoid/medullary CO₂-sensor–pathway development — the central chemoreflex is absent or blunted (the [[#def-chemo]] loop's failure mode) while behavioural (awake) cortical breathing persists — the classic "breathing with the eyes open" dissociation of the eponym's curse: apnoea in sleep, near-normal wakefulness, the danger at every sleep onset.** The clinical net: **polysomnography-and-transcutaneous-capnography demonstrating sleep-dependent hypoventilation (the end-tidal CO₂ rise without effort), the PHOX2B test, and the associations to hunt (Hirschsprung disease — the neural-crest partner [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]], the neuroblastoma and ganglioneuroma tumour screen, the autonomic-dysregulation phenotype (temperature, mood and pain oddities).** Management: **assisted ventilation during sleep (the tracheostomy-and-ventilator or negative-pressure and modern mask strategies, the capnography-home-monitoring programme) for a lifetime of sleep-dependent breathing, the parental safety-net teaching and the emergency-illness admissions thresholds, the genetic counselling (the mosaic-parent recurrence and the prenatal testing options — [[genetics/genetic-testing]], [[medical-ethics-law/beginning-of-life]]) — a single gene's window onto the whole controller chapter.**
:::

::: definition Testing the controller and the sleep airway {#def-tests}
The clinical tests of this chapter: (1) **arterial blood gases read as controller output** (the PaCO₂ as the ratio of CO₂ production to alveolar ventilation — the type-2-respiratory-failure thresholds and the bicarbonate compensation's timeline separating acute from chronic — [[physiology/acid-base]], [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]; the "normal PaCO₂ in an acidotic or hypoxic or breathless patient" as the alarm of exhausted compensation — the asthma and obesity patterns of [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]); (2) **capnography and the bedside capnograph** (the end-tidal waveform and gradient — the dead-space and embolic and hypoventilation signatures in theatre and ICU — [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]], [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]]); (3) **respiratory inductance plethysmography, nasal-pressure and pulse-oximetry sleep studies — home polygraphy versus attended polysomnography (the EEG arousal scoring, the apnoea-hypopnoea index thresholds (5/15/30 per hour defining mild/moderate/severe sleep-apnoea) and the oxygen- and arousal-index partners, the hypopnoea-definition caveat that changes the index by design — the epidemiology of the "epidemic" diagnosis — [[epidemiology-ebm/measuring-disease]]'s drift-of-thresholds lesson)**; (4) **the respiratory-muscle and drive adjuncts (the sniff nasal-inspiratory pressure and maximal inspiratory/expiratory pressures — the neuromuscular-respiratory interface of [[nervous-system/motor-systems]], the morning-headache-and-NOCTURNAL-hypoventilation recall, the transcutaneous and end-tidal CO₂ monitoring, and the phrenic-conduction and diaphragm-ultrasound for the paralysed [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]-chapter patient)**; and (5) **the research-grade controller tests (the CO₂-rebreathing and iso-hypercapnic-hypoxia protocols measuring the slope and intercept of the widget, the loop-gain measurement by ventilatory-oscillation analysis)** — mostly physiologist's tools whose clinical descendants are the sleep-study reports. The **obstructive sleep apnoea** entity itself: **the repetitive collapse of the pharyngeal airway at sleep onset (the dilator-genioglossus tone withdrawal — the upper-airway anatomy's dependence on its muscle, the collapsibility promoted by the short posterior airway space, the retropalatal and retrolingual levels, the obesity's fat and the menopause's loss — the STOP-BANG-style pretest scores and their poor-alone discrimination — [[epidemiology-ebm/diagnostic-tests]]), the negative-effort-against-closed-airway swings (the large-pleural-pressure swings' cardiac sequelae — the afterload-and-atrial-stretch and AF-and-failure-and-pulmonary-hypertension links of [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]] and [[cardiovascular/arrhythmias]], and the micro-arousal sympathetic surges driving the resistant-hypertension phenotype of [[cardiovascular/hypertension]]'s exercise) and the cardiovascular-risk evidence landscape (the SAVE-and-other-trials' mixed primary-prevention verdicts versus the symptomatic-drowsiness and road-safety indications)**; treatment: **weight and alcohol and position and driving rules, CPAP (the pneumatic splint and the adherence-metric hours-per-night and the humidification-and-mask-interface craft), mandibular devices, the surgical and the hypoglossal-nerve-stimulation and the nasal-inspiratory-muscle-targeted era, and the drug era's arrival (the combination of a weight-reducing agent and the upper-airway muscle-targeted agents — the tirzepatide SURMOUNT-OSA programme, the oxybate lineage) — with the sedative-avoidance logic supplied by this chapter's loop physiology**.
:::

::: example The driver's daughter {#ex-blackout}
A 21-year-old is brought in after a shallow-water blackout while freediving; her boyfriend says she "did twenty fast breaths before each dive"; she recovered in a minute on the surface but is confused now.
::: solution
**Hypoxic blackout from voluntary hyperventilation before a breath-hold — the controller's sensors fooled by its own input.** The physiology: **breath-hold duration is limited chiefly by the CO₂-driven air hunger and the motor-surge build-up ([[#def-chemo]] — not by oxygen), so pre-dive hyperventilation lowers PaCO₂ below the apnoeic threshold and delays the alarm while the O₂ content falls on the tissue-oxygen-uptake clock ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]]'s stores and consumption arithmetic) — the diver loses consciousness to hypoxia before CO₂ ever reaches the waking threshold (the shallow-water blackout, the drowning-without-struggle pattern); the same physiology explains why asthmatics "breathless" with a normal CO₂ are dangerous ([[respiratory/asthma-copd]]) and why the "training" of breath-hold lengthening carries a hypoxic-injury risk that no technique removes.** Management: **the hypoxic-ischaemic assessment (the confusion marking the hypoxic-encephalopathy end of the spectrum — the aspiration, the immersion and the decompression-in-free-diving variants), the oxygen-and-glucose-and-temperature basics ([[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]), and the prevention prescription (never dive alone, no pre-dive hyperventilation, the black-out-and-swallow reflexes of the surface interval).** The teaching generalises: **the controller's set points can be manipulated — by drugs (the post-operative opioid pattern), by altitude ([[#ex-cs]]), by training, by disease ([[#ex-ondi]]) — and each manipulation has its characteristic failure mode; hypoxia-without-air-hunger is the deadliest of them.**
:::
:::

::: example The normal carbon dioxide {#ex-normal-co2}
An acute-severe-asthma patient has been dyspnoeic for 14 hours; RR 32, pulsus paradoxus 20 mmHg, SpO₂ 91 % on high-flow oxygen; the ABG: pH 7.38, PaCO₂ 5.3 kPa, PaO₂ 9.8 kPa.
::: solution
**The "normal" PaCO₂ that is the most abnormal result in asthma.** The controller logic: an attack of this severity should be hyperventilating to a PaCO₂ of 3–3.5 kPa (the [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]] hyperventilation-and-work trade — [[#def-chemo]]'s intact drive); a "normal" 5.3 in that context means **the compensation is failing — the work of breathing, the air-trapping threshold-load and the muscle mechanical disadvantage ([[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]) are reducing effective alveolar ventilation despite the rate — the pre-arrest pattern of fatigue, not improvement.** Management consequences: **this ABG plus the clinical picture is the intensive-care and intubation-trigger conversation (the [[respiratory/asthma-copd]] severity criteria and the [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]] lung-protective, permissive-hypercapnia strategy — [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]'s auto-PEEP and plateau-pressure arithmetic), the repeat-gas-and-continuous-monitoring discipline, and the "do not be reassured by oxygen" rule (the supplemental-oxygen-flat-top hiding the rising CO₂ — [[respiratory/gas-exchange]]'s oximetry lesson and [[#def-tests]]'s capnography rationale).**
:::

::: quiz
The central chemoreceptors drive ventilation chiefly in response to:
- [ ] Arterial hydrogen-ion concentration
- [x] Hydrogen ions generated from CO₂ diffusion into the CSF and cerebral extracellular fluid
- [ ] Arterial oxygen tension
- [ ] Plasma bicarbonate
::: solution
Arterial H⁺ crosses the blood–brain barrier poorly; **CO₂ crosses freely and is hydrated (carbonic anhydrase) to H⁺ at the sensor — which is why the chronic hypercapnia of COPD is buffered (the renal–CSF bicarbonate compensation, [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]) and why the acute CO₂ rise is such a potent drive ([[#def-chemo]] and the widget)**. Arterial O₂ acts only peripherally (the carotid bodies), bicarbonate is the regulated rather than the sensing variable, and [[#ex-normal-co2]] is the bedside reading of the whole loop.

::: history
**Legallois's and Traube's** 19<sup>th</sup>-century medullary slice-and-respiration preparations and **the Lumsden's** 1920s pontine and bulbar respiratory centres-in-sequence experiments, **the Hering and Breuer reflexes (1868)** and their twentieth-century clinical anatomy, **Cunningham and the Crossed-Andes?** — the Carotid-Body: **Heymans' carotid-sinus physiology (the 1938 Nobel)**, **von Euler and Sjolund's** 1960s single-chemoreceptor-unit recordings, and **the glomus-cell oxygen-sensing channel biology of the Lahiri, Lopez and Prabhakar era (1990s–2000s)**; **the central-chemoreception line — the Loepky and the Mitchell and the Rehder?** — the medullary surface chemo-sensitivity studies, **the retrotrapezoid-nucleus identification of the 2000s (the Nijima-and-Feldman-generation physiology and the PHOX2B-and-channel-sensor work)** and **the pre-Bötzinger-complex era of Feldman and colleagues' rhythm-genetics (the Dbx1-lineage and the network-versus-pacemaker debates)**; **the sleep-medicine birth: the 1960s–70s polysomnography schools, Guilleminault and Dement and Roth's narcolepsy-and-sleep-apnoea clinics (the 1970s), and Sullivan and Berthon-Jones's 1981 Sydney CPAP paper — the single apparatus that created sleep-disordered-breath medicine**; **the CCHS/PHOX2B story (the Amiel-led and International CCHS-consortium work culminating in the 2001 polyalanine-repeat discovery — [[genetics/non-mendelian-inheritance]]'s repeat-expansion family)**; and **the opioid-respiratory-depression's modern receptor anatomy (the pre-Bötzinger µ-receptor work and the negative-allosteric-modulator drug-development programmes — the "breathing-sparing analgesia" goal — [[pharmacology/drug-receptor]])**.
:::

## Where this leads

The pump that the controller drives in [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]; the gases it regulates and senses in [[respiratory/gas-exchange]]; type 2 failure and ventilatory support in [[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]; the reflex cough and irritant arms in [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]; the acid–base chemistry of the sensor in [[physiology/acid-base]] and [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]; the neural anatomy in [[anatomy/neuroanatomy]] and the motor pathways in [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]; opioid pharmacology in [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]] and [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]; autonomic consequences in [[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]].

::: summary
- Rhythm generation is a brainstem central-pattern-generator product (pre-Bötzinger bursting), shaped by pons, state, emotion and voluntary override — hence apnoeas live in sleep and songs live in wake.
- CO₂ (via CSF H⁺) is the dominant regulated variable; the peripheral carotid bodies are the only meaningful oxygen sensors and interact multiplicatively with the CO₂ response; the loop's threshold and gain are the variables every disease and drug moves.
- Periodic breathing (Cheyne–Stokes, altitude, post-hyperventilation) is the same high-gain-and-delayed-feedback loop, and its treatments (and their SERVE-HF-era cautions) follow the loop's parameters.
- Sleep-disordered breathing is an anatomy-in-sleep disease with systemic sympathetic, cardiac and hypertensive consequences; diagnosis is scored sleep study, and treatment spans weight, devices and the new drug era.
- The controller is testable (blood gases, capnography, CO₂ and hypoxic response curves, sleep studies, muscle and drive adjuncts) and it is manipulable — by drugs, altitude, training and disease — with the manipulated-sensor blackouts of freediving as the cautionary tale.
:::

::: exercise Reflex arcs {level=1}
State the receptors, afferents and effects of the Hering–Breuer inflation reflex and the carotid-chemoreceptor hypoxic reflex, and one clinical situation where each dominates.
::: solution
**Hering–Breuer: pulmonary stretch receptors (slowly-adapting) → vagal (depression-of-inspiration) afferents → inspiratory-off switching — dominant during positive-pressure ventilation (the Hering–Breuer-mediated apnoeas at high tidal volumes — [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]) and in the neonate (the vagal apnoeas of the overstretched and the air-leak baby — [[paediatrics/newborn]]); its absence in the adult at rest is why we do not cycle on lung volume. Hypoxic reflex: glomus cells of the carotid body → sinus nerve of Hering → glossopharyngeal → nucleus tractus solitarius → increased ventilation and sympathetic outflow — dominant at altitude (the acclimatisation driver ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]])), after the bilateral carotid-body resections' apnoeic responses to hypoxia (the historical catastrophe), and in the neonate's periodic and gasping breathing ([[paediatrics/newborn]]).** Both arcs are the [[#def-chemo]] chapter's clinical anatomy ([[anatomy/neuroanatomy]]).

::: exercise Loop parameters {level=2}
For each — opioid overdose, heart-failure Cheyne–Stokes, pregnancy, obesity-hypoventilation — name the parameter moved (threshold, gain, slope, plant) and the predicted CO₂-response curve shift.
::: solution
**Opioid: reduced slope (blunted gain) and often raised threshold — the flattened, rightward line (the widget's second curve) with the rising-CO₂-no-distress phenotype ([[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]]); heart failure: increased gain (steep slope) with a low-ish threshold and a delayed feedback (the plant's circulation time) — the line pivots steeply, producing the oscillation ([[#ex-cs]]); pregnancy: leftward threshold shift and increased slope (progesterone's gain) — the chronic mild hypocapnia (PaCO₂ ~4.1) and the dyspnoea-perception of normal ([[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]); obesity-hypoventilation: acquired central blunting (reduced CO₂ response) plus the plant load (the chest wall's work — the [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]] compliance terms), the phenotype that separates from simple obesity by the awake hypercapnia and the bicarbonate ([[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]).**

::: exercise Score the study {level=2}
Home polygraphy: AHI 24/hour, nadir SpO₂ 82 %, no EEG. A 6-hour attended study in the same patient shows AHI 41 with arousals. Explain the discrepancy's mechanisms and what it changes.

::: exercise The post-operative pause {level=2}
A morphine-patient on a surgical ward has a respiratory rate of 7, a capnography trace of slow regular waves with rising baseline end-tides, and is "easy to rouse". Explain why the pulse oximeter stays normal and why rousability is not safety.
::: solution
**The [[#def-rhythm]] state layer and the [[#def-chemo]] drug layer together: opioids blunt the central CO₂ response (the widget's flat line) and slow the rhythm generator while the behavioural wake-system still answers voice — hence "easy to rouse, then apnoeic again" (the arousability-with-lost-automaticity pattern that naloxone must be dosed around — the renarcotisation window and the pain-crisis of overdose-naloxone boluses ([[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]])). The oximeter stays normal because **supplemental oxygen (surgical wards' routine) fills the flat top of the dissociation curve ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]]'s widget) while CO₂ climbs unmeasured — oxygen without ventilation is the trap the capnograph exists for (the end-tidal trend preceding the saturation fall), the reason monitoring protocols for the sedated-and-opioid patient specify capnography or continuous gas measures, the observations chart's NEWS-style escalation rules and the PCA-programme's selection criteria (the obesity-sleep-apnoea and the opioid-sparing multimodal anaesthesia of [[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]]).**

::: exercise Kussmaul and the sensors {level=2}
A type-1-diabetic in ketoacidosis breathes at 32 with no hypoxia. Explain the gas-hunger arithmetic, the expected ABG pattern (with the respiratory-compensation ceiling) and the intubation danger.
::: solution
**The acidosis drive: H⁺ stimulates the carotid bodies and (once crossing into CSF as CO₂-equilibrated H⁺ — the buffering timelines of [[physiology/acid-base]]) the central sensors → the ventilatory compensation (Kussmaul's deep-fast breathing — the plant pushing the CO₂ down the buffer curve; expected ABG: low pH, very low bicarbonate and a compensatory PaCO₂ near the Winter's-formula target ([[physiology/acid-base]]'s arithmetic), PaO₂ normal — the "breathless with pink lungs and normal saturations" differential of [[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]-as-chapter). The ceiling: compensation is limited by the work and the fatigue of the muscles ([[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]) and by the fact that CO₂ cannot go below ~1.3–2 kPa sustainably — an apparent "normalising" PaCO₂ in a profoundly acidotic patient signals exhaustion, not improvement (the gas-exchange chapter's alarm pattern ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]])).** The intubation danger: **sedation and paralysis remove that ventilation; the periprocedural minute must match the patient's own — the pre-oxygenation-denitrogenation and apnoea-limitation and the "bag-breathe-maintain-acidosis-avoiding-ventilation" anaesthesia arithmetic ([[surgery/anaesthesia-pain]], [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]]): the failure is the iatrogenic acidosis-cardiac-arrest sequence ([[renal/acid-base-disorders]], [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]).**

::: exercise Design the controller-safe analgesia plan {level=2}
Write the peri-operative respiratory-risk plan for an obstructive-sleep-apnoea patient having major abdominal surgery: pre-assessment, intra-op, analgesia strategy and monitoring, each justified from this chapter.
