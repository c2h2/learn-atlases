Oxygen gets from the barometric pressure to the mitochondrion by four serial steps — ventilation, diffusion, vascular transport with haemoglobin's loading arithmetic, and capillary-to-cell delivery — and each step has its own failing signature on the blood gases, the transfer factor and the response to oxygen. Carbon dioxide travels the reverse road by different chemistry (the bicarbonate buffer and the red-cell chloride shift), and obeys different laws: its elimination is ventilation, its content barely limits anything, and its arterial tension is the respiratory system's single most information-rich number.

This chapter covers diffusion and transfer factor, the ventilation–perfusion distribution and its zones, the alveolar–arterial arithmetic of hypoxaemia, and the gas-transport chemistry of both respiratory gases.

## Crossing the membrane

::: definition Diffusion and transfer factor {#def-diffusion}
The alveolar–capillary membrane is a **0.6-µm average diffusion barrier (the fused basal laminae of the epithelium–endothelium syncytium)** across which gas flux follows Fick's law (area × diffusion coefficient × tension-gradient / thickness); the **diffusion coefficient carries the solubility-over-√molecular-weight (Graham's) term that makes CO₂ ~20× more diffusible than O₂**, and O₂'s uptake additionally limited by its **combination rate with haemoglobin (θ × capillary volume) — the Roughton–Forster decomposition of the measured diffusing capacity into a membrane conductance (Dm) and a reaction-and-volume term (θ·Vc)**. The clinical instrument is **DLCO (the single-breath carbon-monoxide transfer factor — CO chosen for its affinity so that uptake is diffusion-and-reaction-limited at near-zero back-pressure)**: **low in loss-of-surface (emphysema, resection), thickened-barrier (fibrosis — [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]), vascular (pulmonary vascular disease, anaemia — hence the haemoglobin correction), and up in increased pulmonary blood volume (left-to-right shunts, polycythaemia, supine exercise) and alveolar haemorrhage (the free haemoglobin's CO sink — the DAH signature)**; and because **resting O₂ transfer normally completes in ~0.25 s of a ~0.75 s capillary transit (the Krogh-cylinder reserve)**, pure diffusion limitation reveals itself only under stress (exercise desaturation and the falling end-capillary PO₂ of fibrosis and emphysema — the six-minute-walk's oximetry and the cardiopulmonary exercise test's DLCO-and-desaturation phenotype — [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]).
:::

::: definition V/Q matching, zones, dead space and shunt {#def-vaq}
Ventilation (≈4 L/min) and perfusion (≈5 L/min) match regionally (the V/Q ratio averaging 0.8) by three mechanisms: **the gravitational zones (West's: pleural-pressure gradients make apical alveoli larger-at-rest and less-perfused — zone 1's alveolar-arterial-venous pressure hierarchy and its extension in positive-pressure ventilation, hypovolaemia and pulmonary hypertension — the ventilated-wasted-unit of alveolar dead space — V/Q infinity), hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction (the alveolar oxygen-sensor's Euler–Liljestrand mechanism diverting blood from poorly ventilated units — physiology's own shunt-prevention, inhibited by anaesthetics and vasodilators and overwhelmed in pneumonia, and maladaptive in the high-altitude and COPD pulmonary hypertensions — [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]), and the matching of ventilation to metabolism by local CO₂ and pH.** The pathological ratios: **low-V/Q units (the near-normal range's biggest hypoxaemia generator — asthma, COPD, pneumonia, failure's airway and interstitial oedema) respond briskly to supplemental oxygen (unlike shunt), and **shunt (V/Q = 0: the entirely unventilated-perfused unit — consolidation, atelectasis, oedema — or the anatomic right-to-left (the intracardiac and intrapulmonary shunts of [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]] and [[#ex-shunt]]) responds poorly to oxygen: the classic shunt-fraction arithmetic of [[#ex-shunt]]. **Dead space (ventilation without perfusion — pulmonary embolism's [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]], the zone-1 extension, and the added mechanical dead space of circuits and ETTs and the physiological-dead-space fraction from the Bohr-Enghoff Pa–PeCO₂ difference — the Vd/Vt number whose rise in the ventilated patient predicts failure-to-wean)** — the three derangements sharing one hypoxaemic expression and separating on the oxygen-response, the gradient and the imaging.
:::

::: example The gradient decides {#ex-a-a}
A 55-year-old post-thyroidectomy patient on 21 % air: PaO₂ 7.4 kPa, PaCO₂ 4.8 kPa; and in the next bed, a 30-year-old opioid-overdose: PaO₂ 8.6 kPa, PaCO₂ 8.0 kPa. Both look "hypoxic".
::: solution
**Compute the alveolar gas equation and let the gradient decide: PAO₂ = FiO₂ × (PB − PH₂O) − PaCO₂/0.8 ≈ 0.21 × 100.6 − PaCO₂/0.8 (kPa at sea level). Patient one: PAO₂ = 21.1 − 6.0 = 15.1, so A–a = 15.1 − 7.4 ≈ 7.7 kPa — against an age-expected ~2–3 kPa (≈4 + age/4 mmHg), clearly raised: the lung or its vessels are the problem (the post-operative atelectasis of [[surgery/perioperative-care]], the aspiration, the pneumonia, the embolus — the V/Q-and-shunt list). Patient two: PAO₂ = 21.1 − 10.0 = 11.1, so A–a = 11.1 − 8.6 = 2.5 kPa — essentially normal for her age: the hypoxaemia is CO₂'s arithmetic, alveolar oxygen displaced by the rising CO₂ up the alveolar equation (pure hypoventilation preserves the gradient — the opioid, sedative, neuromuscular and obesity-hypoventilation patterns of [[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]] and [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]), and the treatment is ventilation support and naloxone, not oxygen alone — oxygen in hypoventilation can unmask the rising CO₂ (the [[#def-transport]] trap). The four-mechanism hypoxaemia table (low inspired PO₂ — normal gradient ([[#ex-altitude]]); hypoventilation — normal gradient; diffusion limitation, low V/Q and shunt — raised gradient, separating on oxygen response) is this chapter's most-examined box, with the A–a caveats: its calculation's assumptions (the 0.8 respiratory quotient fed by substrate mix and the F-factor approximation) and the FiO₂-dependence of shunt detection ([[#ex-shunt]]).**
:::
:::

::: example The mountain headache {#ex-altitude}
A 26-year-old flies to a 3,800 m town, within 24 hours develops headache, ataxia and breathlessness at rest, SpO₂ 76 %; chest clear, and her sea-room ABG on arrival shows PaO₂ 5.8 kPa, PaCO₂ 3.1 kPa, pH 7.48.
::: solution
**Hypobaric hypoxia with appropriate hyperventilation and respiratory alkalosis — plus two acclimatisation and catastrophe lessons.** The arithmetic: **the inspired PO₂ falls with barometric pressure (Dalton — at 3,800 m the inspired PO₂ ≈ 0.21 × (460 − 47) ≈ 87 mmHg ≈ 11.6 kPa and the PAO₂ with her hyperventilation's help ~9–10 kPa — her PaO₂ 5.8 with a normal-for-altitude gradient: no lung disease, a low-PiO₂ problem ([[#ex-a-a]]'s first row) — and the A–a gap stays normal because the lung itself is well; the hypoxaemia drives the carotid bodies ([[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]) to hyperventilate, lowering CO₂ and raising pH (the acute alkalosis that limits the ventilation until renal bicarbonate excretion acclimatises over days — [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]'s compensation timeline, acetazolamide's carbonic-anhydrase-inhibitor acceleration of the diuresis-and-acidosis programme).** The complications: **her ataxia and headache are acute mountain sickness's cerebral component; the progression risk is the high-altitude cerebral and pulmonary oedemas (HACE and HAPE — the exudative, overperfusion-and-hypoxic-vasoconstriction-heterogeneity pulmonary oedema of [[pathology/environmental-pathology]] — descent-and-oxygen-and-sildenafil-and-dexamethasone, the at-risk prophylaxis and the gradual-ascent rules), and the same altitude unmasking the unrecognized shunts and sickle traits ([[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]'s high-altitude splenic and pulmonary crises).** The descent physiology is the ascent in reverse (the transient sleep-disordered breathing and periodic breathing of the acclimatised — [[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]'s Cheyne–Stokes discussion and the [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]-overlap).
:::

## Transport, measurement, and the false numbers

::: definition Content, carriage and the pulse-oximeter {#def-transport}
Oxygen content = **1.34 × Hb × saturation + 0.003 × PO₂ (mL/dL)** — the two-term equation that explains **anaemic and carboxylaemic hypoxaemia with normal PaO₂ and saturation-on-pulse-oximetry (the dissolved term's invisibility and the oximeter's two-wavelength assumption: carboxyhaemoglobin read as oxyhaemoglobin (the ~85 % plateau trap — [[#ex-co]], and methaemoglobin's pull toward ~85 % too — the saturation gap of the refractory "cyanotic" patient with normal-looking blood — [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]] and [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]'s dyshaemoglobinaemias), the perfusion, motion, nailbed-and-dye artefacts, the wavelength-overlap at high saturations and the calibration-curve statistics the device's "±2 %" means),** and **hyperbaric oxygen's arithmetic (three atmospheres' 6 mL/dL of dissolved oxygen — enough for resting aerobic metabolism without haemoglobin — the carbon-monoxide and clostridial and flap indications of the chamber)**. Carbon dioxide travels as **bicarbonate (the chloride-shifted red-cell carbonic-anhydrase machinery — [[physiology/acid-base]]'s buffer chemistry), carbamino-haemoglobin (the Haldane effect: deoxygenation raises CO₂ carriage — the V/Q-mismatch consequence of high-FiO₂-induced hypercapnia in the COPD patient's hypoxic-constriction release and Haldane-and-dead-space physiology — [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]'s target-range oxygen therapy), and dissolved**, with the CO₂ content curve's near-linearity within physiological range the reason elimination, not carriage, is ever the problem; and the **mixed-venous saturation (the Fick-derived mirror of delivery-versus-consumption — ScvO₂/SvO₂ trending in shock resuscitation ([[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]]) and the extraction-failure signatures (the high-ScvO₂ septic and cyanide-shunt circulation — [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]] and [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]])).**
:::

::: example The chaperone's headache {#ex-co}
A family arrives from a cabin holiday: headache, vomiting and confusion; the 8-year-old is sleepy; all three pulse oximeters read 97–99 %; the blood drawn is bright red; the roadside heater was flue-blocked.
::: solution
**Carbon-monoxide poisoning — the transport chapter's ambush: normal saturation, normal PaO₂, low content.** The arithmetic: **CO's haemoglobin affinity ~200× oxygen's (and its left-shift of the remaining sites' dissociation curve — the [[#def-transport]] widget's left-shifted line) drops content and off-loading alike; the PaO₂ (dissolved oxygen) and the two-wavelength SpO₂ (reading COHb as oxyHb) are normal-to-falsely-comfortable — the saturation-gap rule (measured co-oximetry COHb fraction versus pulse oximetry, and the AB-machine's calculated saturation lying the same way); the bright-red venous blood and the headache-cluster-from-a-shared-airspace are the bedside clues.** Management: **high-flow 100 % oxygen (halving COHb half-life from ~4–5 hours to ~60–90 minutes — the content-restoration-by-dissolved-oxygen arithmetic of hyperbaric's stronger version (the three-atmosphere minutes-to-clear and the neuromyopathy and in-utero-fetal-haemoglobin (the fetal COHb-left-shift double-jeopardy — [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]) indications and the transfer logistics — [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]]), the neurological observation and the delayed-neuropsychiatric-syndrome counselling, the source-extraction and the gas-safety and prevention prescription (the smoke-and-CO alarm public-health line — [[public-health/environmental-occupational]]).** The differential that shares the mechanism: **methaemoglobinaemia (the dapsone, local-anaesthetic-prilocaine-benzocaine, nitrate and aniline exposures — the chocolate blood and the ~85 % saturation-gap signature, methylene-blue's reductase pharmacology — [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]])** — together the two hypoxaemias-of-content-with-normal-tension, and both invisible to the cheap monitor.
:::
:::

::: example The shunt that oxygen will not fix {#ex-shunt}
A 24-year-old is 5 months post-partum with breathlessness; SpO₂ 89 % on air improving only to 91 % on 15 L/min; the ABG shows PaO₂ 8.4 kPa on FiO₂ 1.0 after denitrogenation; CT pulmonary angiography is clear; the bubble-contrast echo opacifies the left atrium after four cardiac cycles.
::: solution
**Intrapulmonary arteriovenous shunting (the pulmonary arteriovenous malformation — PAVM) with a large right-to-left shunt fraction.** The reasoning: **failure to normalize PaO₂ on 100 % oxygen (the shunt-fraction calculation Qs/Qt = (Cc′O₂ − CaO₂)/(Cc′O₂ − CvO₂), the denitrogenation technique's 1.0-FiO₂ absorption-atelectasis caveat included) locates the lesion at a shunt, not a low-V/Q bed ([[#def-vaq]]'s oxygen-response rule), and the bubble echo's delayed (3–6 cycle) left-atrial opacification distinguishes intrapulmonary (transit-through-the-capillaries) from intracardiac (immediate — the PFO/ASD of [[cardiovascular/valve-congenital-disease]] and the orthodeoxia-platypnoea of hepatopulmonary syndrome via basal dilations — [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]])** — the PAVM's own aetiology list dominated by **hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (the endoglin/ALK1 pathway's autosomal-dominant telangiectasia syndrome with its epistaxis, mucocutaneous and cerebral and hepatic AVMs, pregnancy-enlargement risks and the brain-abscess-and-stroke paradoxical-embolism hazards — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]'s locus classicus).** Management: **the pulmonary-angiographic and contrast screening of the lungs and the family, the transcatheter embolisation (the occlusion-device era that converted PAVM surgery to a procedure — and the closure thresholds by feeding-artery size), the endocarditis-and-paradoxical-embolism prophylaxis counselling (the air-bubble caution on IV lines), the pregnancy planning ([[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]) and the HHT programme's nose, gut and cerebral surveillance.**
:::

::: history
**Fick's** diffusion and content frameworks and **Graham's** effusion law (the diffusion-coefficient's solubility-over-root-mass term), **Barcroft's** decade of the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve and the fetal-haemoglobin difference and **Bohr, Hasselbalch and August Krogh's** CO₂-and-pH carriage and the capillary-recruitment physiology (the 1904–1920 Copenhagen school); **Haldane's** carbamino and CO₂ findings and the **August-Douglas Haldane-and-Zuntz isoresistance** chemistry, **Roughton's** stopped-flow and **Forster's** two-resistance analysis giving the DLCO its modern equation, and **the carbon-monoxide transfer-factor technique (the Forster-and-Sandoval-era single-breath method of the 1950s–60s)**; **West, Dollery and Nadel's** 1964–66 regional V/Q and zone modelling (the multiple-inert-gas-retention era's refinement — the O'Neilly-Riley distribution-of-ratios mathematics), **Euler and Liljestrand's** 1946 hypoxic-vasoconstriction demonstration and the physiological-dead-space fraction from **Riley and Cournand's** 1949 classical triad papers (the normal values for ventilation, distribution and diffusing capacity), and the shunt-fraction oxygen techniques of the classical-description era; and **the pulse-oximeter's lineage (Shaw's 1940 eight-wavelength ear instrument, the 1970s transmission instruments of Shimizu and Nomaguchi, and Takuo Aoyagi's 1977 two-wavelength algorithm)** — the cheapest bedside physiology monitor in history and the subject of the era's equity-of-signal-quality investigations ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]'s device-validation lesson).
:::

## Where this leads

The ventilation that supplies the gradient in [[respiratory/mechanics-breathing]]; the reflexes that set the CO₂ in [[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]; the failure types and oxygen prescribing in [[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]; the membrane diseases in [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]] and the vascular ones in [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]; haemoglobin chemistry in [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]; acid–base and CO₂ buffering in [[physiology/acid-base]] and [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]; altitude and environmental extremes in [[pathology/environmental-pathology]].

::: summary
- Oxygen transfer is diffusion-plus-reaction (Roughton–Forster), measured as DLCO and interpreted with haemoglobin, volume and haemorrhage corrections; its large resting reserve means pure diffusion limitation shows on exercise.
- Hypoxaemia sorts by the alveolar–arterial gradient: low inspired PO₂ and hypoventilation leave it normal; diffusion limitation, low V/Q and shunt raise it — and oxygen response separates V/Q from shunt quantitatively.
- Perfusion is diverted from poorly ventilated alveoli by hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction — the good physiology behind shunt prevention, altitude and COPD pulmonary hypertension and its pharmacological disruption.
- Content, not tension, is what tissues receive: anaemia, carboxy- and methaemoglobinaemia produce hypoxia with normal PaO₂ and falsely reassuring pulse oximetry.
- CO₂ is a ventilation-and-buffering story (bicarbonate, chloride shift, Haldane), near-linear in content and clinically eliminated, never transported-inadequately.
:::

::: exercise Compute the gradient {level=1}
Sea-level room air: PaO₂ 10.5 kPa, PaCO₂ 5.3 kPa, age 64. Compute the A–a gradient and judge it.
::: solution
PAO₂ = 0.21 × 100.6 − 5.3/0.8 = 21.1 − 6.6 = **14.5 kPa**; A–a = 14.5 − 10.5 = **4.0 kPa**; age-expected ≈ 4 + 64/4 = **20 mmHg ≈ 2.7 kPa** — mildly raised (the borderline zone: repeat with the FiO₂ and clinical context, since the gradient widens with age, feeding and F-factor assumptions — [[#ex-a-a]]'s caveats; the expected-value formulae are population approximations).
:::

::: exercise Why the transfer factor falls {level=1}
Give one mechanism of reduced DLCO each from: emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis, anaemia, and left-to-right shunt — and the one that rises it.
::: solution
**Emphysema: loss of alveolar–capillary surface and bed (area ↓ — [[respiratory/asthma-copd]]); fibrosis: barrier thickening (Dm ↓ — [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]); anaemia: the reaction term (θ·Hb) falls (correct for haemoglobin — [[blood/anaemia]]); left-to-right shunt and polycythaemia: increased capillary volume raise measured DLCO (the ↑ example) — as does alveolar haemorrhage (the free-Hb CO sink) and the supine/exercise recruitment states ([[#def-diffusion]]).**

::: exercise Shunt arithmetic {level=2}
On FiO₂ 1.0: PaO₂ 12 kPa, haemoglobin 150, SaO₂ 98 %, mixed-venous 6.8 kPa/68 %; computed content difference suggests ~20 % shunt. Explain why 100 % oxygen is given before the calculation, and what the absorption-atelectasis caveat is.
::: solution
**The shunt equation requires end-capillary content ≈ pulmonary-capillary content ≈ fully-saturated, tension-equalised blood — 100 % oxygen "denitrogenates" and maximises alveolar PO₂ so that perfused units are saturated and any arterial desaturation must come from units ventilation never reaches ([[#def-vaq]]); in normal lung the PaO₂ on 1.0 exceeds 60–80 kPa — anything far below implies shunt-like physiology.** Caveats: **the nitrogen-washout itself causes absorption atelectasis (the V/Q worsener that historically made the test semi-quantitative), the 0.8-respiratory-quotient assumption shifts with substrate, and the modern substitutes are the PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio and the ARDS-definition oxygenation indices ([[respiratory/respiratory-failure]]) — but the shunt-versus-low-V/Q logic remains the chapter's cleanest quantitative lesson.**
:::

::: exercise Oximetry physics {level=2}
Give four reasons a pulse oximeter misreads, and the clinical reflex each should trigger.
::: solution
**(1) Dyshaemoglobinaemias — COHb (reads ~oxyHb — the falsely-high plateau) and MetHb (pulls toward ~85 %) → the saturation-gap rule (co-oximetry) and the poisoned/think-outside-the-lungs reflex ([[#ex-co]]); (2) poor pulsatility and motion (shock, vasopressors, arrhythmia, shivering, tremor and transport artefact) → look at the plethysmograph quality and the extremity, use an ear-and-nose probe, and trust the ABG when the wave is junk ([[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]]); (3) the flat top of the dissociation curve (the widget) → SpO₂ 99 % says nothing about PaO₂ 40 versus 200 — hyperoxaemia avoidance and the CO₂-relevance in type-2-risk patients ([[respiratory/asthma-copd]]'s target ranges); (4) venous congestion, dyes (methylene blue, indigo carmine — the very antidote temporarily ruining the monitor) and nail polish/circulating dyes — and the chronic-signal-quality lesson on skin pigmentation bias (the occult hypoxaemia studies): the device-verification and health-equity audit ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]). Each rule: **oximetry is a monitored estimate, not a measured gas.**
:::

::: exercise The hypercapnia of oxygen {level=2}
Explain the modern (multi-mechanism) physiology of oxygen-induced hypercapnia in COPD and derive the target-saturation policy from it.
::: solution
**The mechanisms, ranked by evidence: (1) hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction release redirecting perfusion to poorly-ventilated units (raising physiological dead space and therefore CO₂ retention — the dominant effect in the human oxygen-physiology studies, (2) the Haldane effect (oxyhaemoglobin's lower CO₂ carriage displacing bicarbonate stores into plasma — the [[#def-transport]] chemistry), (3) the small true reduction in hypoxic drive (the carotid-body contribution — [[respiratory/control-breathing-tests]]), and (4) the small reductions in metabolic CO₂ production and the dead-space-load effects — minor contributors. Policy derivation: **titrate oxygen to 88–92 % in the CO₂-retainers and at-risk phenotypes (the BTS target-range rule and the post-exacerbation and pre-hospital oxygen protocols), re-gas at 30–60 minutes (the iatrogenic-narcosis and rising-CO₂-acidosis detection), and escalate to non-invasive ventilation for the acidotic failure ([[respiratory/respiratory-failure]], [[emergency-critical-care/airway-ventilation]]) — the same reasoning that makes the emergency "controlled-oxygen" mask and the long-term-oxygen survival-criteria prescription ([[respiratory/asthma-copd]]'s LTOT trial lineage) part of one transport-physiology family.**
:::

::: exercise Hyperbaric arithmetic {level=2}
Compute oxygen content breathing 3 atmospheres of 100 % oxygen (Hb 150, saturation 100 %, PaO₂ ~1,900 mmHg → 253 kPa), compare with the normal arterial content, and identify the therapeutic logic and the two toxicities.
::: solution
Dissolved = 0.003 × 1,900 ≈ **5.7 mL/dL**; plus Hb-bound 1.34 × 150 × 1 ≈ **20.1 mL/dL** → total ≈ **26 mL/dL** versus ~20 normal — and, crucially, **the dissolved fraction alone (~5.7 mL/dL) approaches the arteriovenous difference (~5 mL/dL) that sustains resting aerobic metabolism — i.e. enough without any haemoglobin function (the CO and the near-exsanguination and the swap-boat indications: the [[#ex-co]] half-life-shortening-plus-off-loading-repair argument and the clostridial-and-necrotising and the compromised-flap and the decompression-and-gas-embolism (Boyle-volume) uses — [[emergency-critical-care/poisoning]], [[surgery/wounds-surgical-infection]]).** Toxicities: **the CNS oxygen seizure (the rate-dependent, hyperbaric-collapse-within-the-chamber, airway-safe-by-design mitigation) and the pulmonary oxygen toxicity (the tracheobronchitis-then-ARDS-like dose-dependent vital-capacity fall — the same reactive-oxygen-and-absorption-atelectasis biology as the FiO₂-1.0 atelectasis — the fractional-exposure-time limits of the chamber protocols).**
:::

::: exercise Exercise desaturation {level=2}
A fibrotic patient saturates 97 % at rest and 86 % at the sixth minute of walking. Explain with the diffusion-and-V/Q reserve of [[#def-diffusion]] and the transit-time concept, and why the desaturation persists briefly into recovery.
::: solution
**The three-part physiology: (1) resting capillary transit (0.75 s) exceeds the prolonged equilibration time across the thickened, sparse bed — saturation completes at rest because the reserve is spent, not absent; (2) exercise shortens transit (the high-output recruitment failing in the destroyed bed) and raises oxygen consumption (steeper mixed-venous desaturation and content demand) while the fibrotic V/Q heterogeneity worsens (the stiff lung's ventilation redistribution and the high cardiac output's unmasking of low-V/Q units — the [[#def-vaq]] exercise analogue), so end-capillary equilibration fails — diffusion-limited plus V/Q desaturation; (3) the persistence into early recovery is the continued low mixed-venous and the slow re-equilibration of the poorly-ventilated units and the ongoing elevated extraction — the 6-minute-walk-desaturation and the nadir-recovery-time as the ILD severity and progression metrics ([[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]'s trial endpoints).** The companion lesson: emphysema desaturates by the loss of surface and the ventilation-perfusion mismatch of a different mix — the DLCO-with-normal-spirometry search (vascular disease, early fibrosis, anaemia) completes the [[#def-diffusion]] interpretation algorithm.
:::
