Beginning-of-life ethics is the field where medicine's capabilities outran its consensus fastest: from IVF's first decades (when the 'test-tube-baby' reaction set the argument's terms) to genome-editing's heritable-line red lines drawn and debated within years of the technique's arrival. **This chapter covers the moral-status argument and its structure (potential-person and species-membership and sentience and viability accounts, and why the 'when-does-personhood-begin' quarrel resists resolution), the reproductive applications in current practice (contraception's controversies, preimplantation genetic testing's selection questions, selective reduction, NIPT and the disability-rights challenge, abortion's arguable-consensus-over-procedure problem), the governance layer (the licensing-embryology-act model, professional-body lines and their enforcement, the global-variance-as-natural-experiment literature), and the frontier (mitochondrial-replacement-technique's germline-trojan-horse argument, CRISPR-heritable-editing's He-Jan-fallout consensus and its loopholes, and the artificial-uterus and gamete-from-stem-cell technologies approaching the same questions with new physics).**

## The argument

::: definition Moral status and its accounts {#def-status}
**The bad-objectivity problem first: the moral-status debate's structure explains its permanence - the rival accounts each cohere internally and answer different questions. The potentiality account (the zygote's active developmental trajectory grounds its claim) inherits the continuum difficulty (the same trajectory that makes the embryo a person makes the blastocyst's destruction in twinning-and-twinning-recombination cases a moral absurdity the account cannot flag); the species-membership account (human-organismhood from conception) is descriptively clean and philosophically isolated (the 'moral-status-without-sentience-is-speciesism' charge from the utilitarian tradition, and the 'the-gametes-are-humans-too' reductio its defenders absorb); the sentience account (capacity for experience, arriving with thalamic structures at a defensible if fuzzy second-trimester threshold) does the least work at conception and the most at birth and is the only account with a neurodevelopmental timetable to argue about (the 'birth-as-sentience-continuity-event' literature and the sensory-cortex-onset data); and the relational-and-social accounts (status constituted by relationships and projects - the wanted-unwanted-realism that most actual moral life runs on) describe the phenomenology accurately but risk making personhood a matter of degree that the law cannot tolerate. The pragmatic-regime theories (the gradualist 'increasing-protection-as-development-proceeds' model most legal systems actually implement, the 'woman-as-the-first-environment' frameworks that locate early decisions in the pregnant person's legal personhood, and the threshold-plus-viability-oversight architectures the abortion-law jurisprudence keeps rebuilding) are attempts to legislate without settling, which is either statesmanship or evasion depending on your account of the argument. The clinician-facing synthesis this course defends: the metaphysical question will not be settled in your career, the practical questions were settled without it (contraception, PGT, research on defined embryo-windows, abortion provision under varying law) by procedures that bracket it - which means the professional duty is to argue the procedures honestly (who-decides, what-information, whose-costs) rather than to refight the unresolvable, and to recognise that patients' positions are usually commitments, not conclusions, and deserve the counselling competence the disagreement implies.**
:::

## The applications

::: definition Contraception through selection: the practice controversies {#def-applications}
**Contraception's ethics is mostly the ethics of access and of two residual fights: the mechanism-and-conscience layer (the 'is-it-contraceptive-or-contragestive' argument over post-fertilisation action - a dispute in which both sides mostly agree on the biology and disagree on moral status, which is why it relocates to pharmacy-conscience-rules and formulary politics rather than resolving), and the long-acting-method consent problems (the coercive-history-of-LARC-provision - the indigenous-and-disability-sterilization abuses whose reparations-litigation is beginning, the 'contraception-as-condition-of-discharge-or-of-community-care' ethically-impermissible-adjacent practices the safeguarding reviews keep finding, and the capacity-and-contraception interface the consent chapter owns). Assisted reproduction's questions follow the technology's sequence: donor-gamete-and-surrogacy ethics (the anonymity-ending-reforms' evidence - donor-conceived-people's-own-registrations-and-advocacy changing law across Europe, the 'right-to-know-your-origins' versus contractual-parentage arguments, and commercial-surrogacy's exploitation debate whose positions harden by jurisdiction and soften by evidence about actual arrangements); the add-ons industry (the 'evidence-free-fertility-clinic-extras' audits and the regulator's patient-harms remit - the ethics here is near-standard consumer protection dressed in reproductive solemnity); and preimplantation genetic testing, the selection chapter's core (the disorder-seriousness-spectrum that clinics actually navigate - from Tay-Sachs through the deafness-and-blindness end of the seriousness spectrum, the late-onset-conditions and the 'is-brca1-a-selection-against-or-for' questions; the sex-selection-for-family-balance fights about reasons and harm; the HLA-matching-and-saviour-sibling literature's 'child-as-reserve-with-rights' nuance; and the disability-rights critique - that PGT expresses a judgment that disabled lives are not worth living - whose honest answer is that the critique applies coherently to parents' reasons while changing nothing about the alternative's cruelty for the families facing the actual condition, a disagreement the genetics-counselling-philosophy literature models better than either camp's rhetoric). Selective reduction in higher-order multiples (the ethics of saving some by ending some in higher-order-pregnancy reduction, with the fetal-loss-history counselling load it carries) and NIPT (the non-invasive-testing-and-trisomy-21-termination-connection and its disability-community's political critique versus the reproductive-autonomy-and-accurate-information defence, the sex-imbalance-through-sex-selection-termination-in-specific-populations data that converts the argument from bioethics seminar to public health, and the 'what-does-a-low-risk-result-mean-for-what-you-would-have-known' consent-design problem that the screening chapter's principles reappear in obstetric form). Abortion ethics in this chapter's space is the argument-about-arguments: the volume-violinist and personhood and interests-and-futures positions (Thomson's bodily-availability formalisation, the 'a-future-like-ours' account's critics), the gestational-threshold politics (why the law's lines land where medicine's viability moved, and the 'post-viability-fetal-pain-and-neither-knowing-nor-consensual' debates that the sentience-timetable data keeps interrupting), and the clinician-conscience-versus-access architecture (the 'effective-access-override' duty the professional bodies increasingly adopt: conscience accommodated where alternative provision is real, refused where it converts the patient's right into a search - the two-tier rule whose implementation the reproductive-health services audit).**
:::

## The governance and the frontier

::: definition Regulation models and heritable editing {#def-governance}
**The licensing-embryology-act model (the UK's HFEA architecture - statutory licence, the 14-day culture rule as a symbolic boundary whose compliance-ease and norm-setting-power the comparative literature credits, per-centre-per-procedure approval and the register) competed through the 1990s-2000s with the professional-guidelines model (the American common-law-and-ASRM-ethics-committee approach) and the prohibition-variety (the German-and-Italian-embryo-protection-statutes whose effects on research and on patients' travel the comparative studies measured); the convergence since has been hybrid (statutory floors, professional detail, licensing for the heritable-adjacent technologies) because every pure model failed somewhere. The mitochondrial-replacement-technique case study: the 'three-person-baby' naming battle (the technique's actual genetics - mitochondrial DNA's 37-genes-not-appearance-or-personality - vs the public framing), the safety-and-ethics-commission-reviews' two-gate design (license-the-technique-then-approve-the-patient-and-track-the-children - the 'first-person-of-a-new-lineage' pharmacovigilance analogy), and its germline-trojan-horse-argument-management (the distinction between editing and replacing that the heritable-editing opponents contested as rhetorical); the 2023 birth's quietness versus the 2016 vote's drama is governance-by-exhaustion as policy style. Heritable genome editing: the 2018 He Jiankui rupture (the 'CRISPR-babies' scandal's forensics - the ethics-review-fabrication, the inadequate-safety-case, the HIV-discordant-couple-selection's unnecessary-risk finding), the response architecture (the WHO's expert-committee recommendations and governance-framework, the National-Academies' 2020 heritable-editing-conditions-for-trials-never-currently-met formulation, the international-observatory-and-moratorium-proposals' range from 'call' to 'impose'), and the loophole-gravity (the 'the-clinics-in-unregulated-jurisdictions-and-medical-tourism-for-editing' scenario the field's own wargames run, the 'somatic-heritable-boundary-erosion' argument that germline-adjacent-somatic-techniques-like-testicular-stem-cell-work raise, and the enhancement-versus-therapy-line's re-crossing when the therapy-list lengthens). The approaching set keeps the same shape with new physics (the IVG - in-vitro-gametes - ethics of unlimited-embryo-production-and-its-consent-and-sex-selection-and-kinship-system consequences; the artificial-uterus and its 'abortion-argument-reversal' debates on whether ectogenesis voids or extends the bodily-autonomy rationale; and the gene-therapy-for-fetal-diseases convergence that moves treatment before birth and returns the chapter to its oldest question - who consents for the patient who is, epidemiologically, two).**
:::

::: example The saviour-sibling question {#ex-saviour}
A couple with a transfusion-dependent thalassaemic daughter ask about IVF with HLA-matched embryo selection 'to help our child, not to harvest her'; they have been told no clinic nearby will do it.
::: solution
**Selection-for-another-and-consent-for-both ([[#def-applications]], [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]], [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]): The ethics as practised: HLA-matching-without-implantation-selection-for-genetic-disease (the child would be healthy regardless - the 'saviour-sibling' literature's settled middle: permissible with safeguards in most frameworks, prohibited in a minority of jurisdictions) turns on the safeguards, so counsel the family through them: the donation-limits-by-informed-consent-of-the-recipient-procedure-at-age-of-competence question (the cord-blood-first-and-bone-marrow-last design documented to the donors' children), the sibling's own future consent and welfare (the 'donation-without-harm-to-donor' rules and the psychological-follow-up-adjacent-cohorts' reassuring-with-caveats data), the parents' reasons-under-pressure audit (the 'already-sick-child-and-marital-and-family-expectation-dynamics' counselling that the licensing bodies require), and the 'what-if-no-match-and-what-if-match-but-not-needed' plan the family needs before the first cycle. The access-inequity the case exposes (the 'clinics-nearby-say-no' travel-to-permitting-jurisdictions pattern) is the regulatory-arbitrage problem beginning-of-life governance has never solved - counsel toward the clinics whose ethics-and-licensing paperwork you can actually read, the opposite of the fertility-tourism warning label. The documentation: the counselling's-own-record (both-parents'-independent-advice-available, the-welfare-of-the-future-child-weighed-as-a-person-not-a-resource-in-the-notes, and the long-term-follow-up-commitment the register carries), because the saviour-sibling literature's durable finding is that families do well when the process made the child's-independence-an-explicit-part-of-the-plan rather than a reassurance offered under pressure.**

::: example The NIPT result {#ex-nipt}
A low-risk-pregnancy patient's cell-free DNA screen reports 'high risk for trisomy 21, 1 in 4'; she is 12 weeks, alone, and asks you to 'arrange the reduction'. The confirmatory pathway is amniocentesis at 15.
::: solution
**Screening-is-not-diagnosis-clinically-and-ethically ([[#def-applications]], [[public-health/screening]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]): The arithmetic first: even a 1-in-4 post-test figure from a screening test in a low-risk population carries a substantial false-positive share (the positive-predictive-value-versus-prevalence lesson from the diagnostics chapter - NIPT's sensitivity is high, its specificity nearly so, but at low prevalence the ratio of false to true positives still matters; the number she needs is not 'high risk' but 'your chance that the baby truly has trisomy 21, given this result, is about X percent - and here is what the next test tells us'), and the confirmatory step (the CVS-or-amniocentesis-with-its-own-procedure-risk-quantified) is not a delay but the diagnosis she does not yet have. The counselling craft: the same-day-or-next-day structured session (the 'non-directive-with-numbers' standard), the partner-and-time-questions (the request's speed is information about her prior - the 'was-I-already-deciding' conversation that the termination-for-aneuploidy-autonomy literature insists is legitimate and the disability-rights critique insists is conditioned - a disagreement she should meet as information, not as a lecture), the 'what-our-numbers-mean' leaflets from the organisations run by and for families of children with the condition (the balanced-information duty the screening-programme guidelines now write with named sources both ways), and the pathway-neutrality rule: the confirmatory diagnosis opens all doors equally (continuation-with-support, adoption-planning, termination-within-law-and-service-availability) and the clinician's job is the map, not the destination. The system honesty: the two-week wait for amniocentesis is an ethics variable (the later-termination-risks and the decision-fatigue data), so the pathway's design - rapid-access-counselling, next-available-confirmatory-slot, and the 'come-back-together-after-the-result' appointment - is the intervention this chapter can actually prescribe.**
:::

::: example The conscientious referral {#ex-conscience}
A general practitioner in a small town declines, on conscience grounds, to refer for termination of pregnancy; the nearest alternative clinician is 80 miles and the local media learn of 'doctors who refuse'. A 19-year-old needs a same-week referral.
::: solution
**Conscience-within-access ([[#def-applications]], [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]): The settled framework: practitioners may decline personal participation in a lawful service on conscience, but the duty of effective access sits with the service and the professional body - the referral must happen, not merely be possible: the objection-with-immediate-handover model (a named alternative who will act, contacted before the patient leaves, with the record showing the handover not the refusal), the 'no-lecture-no-moralising-no-delay' conduct rule the codes enforce (the patient's emergency is the clock, and the media story is not hers), and the duty to know the local-access map before the first objection is voiced (the conscientious-practitioner's own obligation: a refusal without a working alternative is a service failure the objector personally causes). The town-level problem is the real one: single-clinician practices make conscience a public barrier (the 'rural-conscientious-objector-arrangements' the reproductive-health services solve by telehealth-first-trimester-pathways, locum-rota coverage, and the pharmacy-and-telemedicine routes), and the professional body's quiet enforcement (the registration-condition approach: the referral-duty is a condition of practice, not advice). The young woman's care this week: the same-hour-arranged-appointment-elsewhere (the travel-and-cost-and-accompaniment supports that the 'practical-barriers-to-access' literature says decide whether a lawful right is a real one), the STI-and-contraception-and-continuity care the visit still delivers with dignity, and the 'you will not be judged here or there' sentence from the GP who declines but arranges - because the objection is to the act, and the duty to the patient is total, and the difference is exactly what a professional conscience is for.**

## Where this leads

The obstetric practice in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/early-pregnancy]] and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]] and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]], genetics services in [[genetics/genetic-testing]] and [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]], the screening principles in [[public-health/screening]], the consent machinery in [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], research governance in [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]], and the status-argument frame in [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]].

::: summary
- The moral-status argument is permanent because its accounts answer different questions; practical ethics and law proceeded by bracketing it - gradualist protections, decision-procedures and delegation to the pregnant person - which is neither cowardice nor evasion but the only consensus plural societies sustain.
- Reproductive technologies' controversies are usually the same three variables in costume: who decides (parents, clinicians, the state, the future child), on what information (screening versus diagnosis, balanced or otherwise), and who bears the costs (of child-rearing, of disability services, of the technologies themselves).
- Selection technologies (PGT, NIPT) convert private reproductive decisions into public questions - about disability, sex ratios, and the meaning of 'informed' - and the defensible clinical posture is numbers-plus-all-options-plus-neutrality-of-pathway, not neutrality of feeling.
- The licensing-model of governance (statutory floors, per-procedure oversight, symbolic boundaries like the 14-day rule, registers) has outperformed both pure professional self-regulation and prohibition, and its revision under new embryo-model and IVG technologies is the field's next argument.
- Heritable genome editing's post-He consensus (conditions-not-met, international-observatory governance, the therapy-versus-enhancement line held in the text and tested in the clinics) is a live experiment in whether a technology can be governed before its reasons mature.
- Clinician competence here is counselling competence plus honesty about the disagreement: patients at these frontiers need the numbers, the options, the services, and company in an argument their society has not finished.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Explain the disagreement {level=1}
Two residents argue in the stairwell: one says 'the embryo is a person, full stop', the other 'it is a clump of cells, full stop'. Give the attending's sixty-second intervention that makes both useful to the ward.
::: solution
**Disagreement-as-competence ([[#def-status]], [[#def-applications]]): The intervention: 'you are both repeating positions, and neither is the one your patients hold - most people's moral views about pregnancy are commitments about relationship and future, arrived at before any metaphysics, and your job is not to grade their ontology but to work inside their reasons with safe medicine and honest options. And notice the practical consensus you already share with each other and with almost every law: it is wrong to destroy an eight-cell embryo carelessly, and not the same wrong as the third-trimester question, and women are the decision-makers for early pregnancy in the systems we practise; that gradualist, procedure-focused agreement is where all your actual ward decisions will live. So: whichever position you keep privately, the clinic's answers - the counselling, the numbers, the referrals, the non-judgment, and the conscientious-duty-and-effective-access rule when you personally cannot provide a service - are identical for both of you, and the patient in room four should never learn which of you is which.' The pedagogy point: this is the 'conscience-within-competence' curriculum position the reproductive-health bodies defend (accommodate the clinician, never the patient's delay), and the stairwell argument is best ended by assigning both residents the next shared counselling session with the numbers script and the debrief.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Write the consent for the IVF add-on {level=2}
Your clinic offers endometrial-embryo-adhesion-molecule 'IMLI' and 'assisted-hatching-adjacent' services; the evidence of benefit is weak. Draft the clinic's consent paragraph and the internal rule about when an add-on may be offered at all.
::: solution
**Add-ons-as-evidence-question ([[#def-applications]], [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]]): Consent paragraph: 'This service is not proven to improve your chance of a baby. The studies are small, the methods weak, and major-registry analyses show little-or-no benefit; you will be offered it only with these sentences in writing and a chance to decline without any effect on your care. Its risks (the procedure's own and the delay's) are small but real, its cost is not reimbursed, and the research-consent alternative (join a properly designed trial) is offered where one exists.' The internal rule: an add-on may be offered when its evidence meets a stated bar (at minimum a randomised-or-large-registry signal of benefit), or when it is provided transparently within approved research with its own consent and no charge - the 'either-proven-or-studied-never-simultaneously-overpriced' standard the fertility-society ethics committees and the regulator's advertising rules converge on. The governance teeth that make the rule real: the clinic's success-rates disclosure separated by add-on use (the transparency-the-register-forces model), the patient-facing 'what-works-and-what-doesnt' list at the first appointment, and the audit that the add-on's uptake falls when the consent sentence is read aloud - because the add-ons literature's mechanism is desperate-people-compliance, and the ethical design goal is that the evidence, not the desperation, decides.**
:::

::: exercise Handle the trisomy-18 call {level=1}
A sonographer's scan at 20 weeks shows soft markers; the diagnostic result returns trisomy-18. The parents ring you at 19:40, before any counselling appointment. Draft the call's structure and the two sentences you must not say.
::: solution
**Bad-news-by-telephone-protocol ([[#def-applications]], [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]): The call's structure: confirm-receiving-the-information-with-a-person-present-when-possible, give-the-diagnosis-in-one-clear-plain-sentence (no 'abnormality-suggested' hedging - the euphemism-lag literature shows it lengthens the shock without softening it), then silence-then-answer-the-first-question, then the concrete-next-thing (the named-midwife-and-the-morrow's-counselling-appointment-and-what-that-meeting-covers: the-numbers-about-life-and-health-as-best-known, the-options-including-continuation-with-neonatal-palliative-planning-and-termination-and-adoption, the-parental-support-organisations-that-are-actually-useful), and close by naming what they should watch for tonight (their own shock - the 'sleep-and-eat-and-tomorrow-is-arranged' sentence). The two forbidden sentences: 'you can always try again' (the replacement-frame that some parents experience as the cruelty of the arithmetic) and 'the baby won't suffer' (a prognostic promise the evidence cannot make and the future inquest of their memory will replay). The follow-through the call commits you to: the counselling appointment with the fetal-medicine-and-neonatal-and-palliative-inputs-sequenced-right, the partner-and-interpretation needs booked, and the referral made to the condition-specific-parent-support bodies (the 'families-who-have-walked-this' information right - both the medical outcomes and the lived-experience sources, which is the balanced-information duty in practice).**
:::

::: exercise Advise the regulator {level=2}
A biotech applies for a licence to create 28-day-culture embryo models (no genomic imprinting corrected, stem-cell-derived, no gametes involved) for implantation-studies. Advise the licensing body: the status question, the rule-revision question, and the oversight design that survives the public argument.
::: solution
**Models-versus-embryos-governance ([[#def-status]], [[#def-governance]]): The status question handled administratively: the model is not an embryo by the law's constitutive definition (no-paternal-and-maternal-genomes-in-conception, no-organism-continuity-in-the-statute's-terms - the licensing acts' definitions were written for IVF's entities, and this is the definitional gap the 14-day-rule revision debates actually fear), so the honest answer is not 'it is an embryo' nor 'it is just cells' but 'it is a new entity whose moral claim is uncertain and whose research-use can proceed under a purpose-bound licence with the symbolic boundary respected in form.' The rule-revision question: the 14-day culture limit's function was a bright-line compromise whose science-era assumption (no culture beyond without primitive streak forming anyway) has inverted; the options the body must weigh - extend the clock with new conditions, keep the clock and accept the models' limbo, or replace the time-rule with a development-stage-rule (no-streak-and-no-neural-plate-and-no-functional-organization, monitored by defined assays) - and the stage-rule's advantage (it tracks the moral-intuition content, individuation-and-sentience-precursors, rather than a calendar) against its enforcement cost (the assay-definition-arguments the field will litigate). The oversight design: the purpose-list licence (implantation and placentation disorders, early-pregnancy-loss - the diseases the model uniquely addresses, because the justification the public accepts is the disease, not the knowledge), the destruction-at-stage-limits condition with audit, the prohibition on uterus-transfer-and-trying-to-make-a-person written absolutely, the stakeholder-consultation-with-disability-and-faith-and-patient-bodies-actually-summarised-in-public (the Care.data lesson: the consultation's visibility matters more than its count), and the periodic-revision-sunset that brings the licence back before the same committee as the science outruns it. The advising sentence the committee should accept from you: license the research, move the boundary from the calendar to the organism's-development, say plainly that you are moving it, and keep the prohibition that everyone actually cares about.**
:::

::: history
**The field's chronology is capability-then-argument. The contraceptive decade (the Pill's 1960-61 licensing and the 'the-birth-control-to-contraception-politics' court eras), then the reproductive-technology shock: Brown's 1978 birth (the Steptoe-and-Edwards programme whose ethics committee approved in fifteen minutes, the tabloid 'test-tube baby' framing that set the public register for a generation, and the 'whether-to-destroy-spare-embryos' question that arrived with the first vials before anyone had a vocabulary); the Warnock Report (1984) and the Human Fertilisation and Embryology Act (1990) inventing the licensing-embryology-model in one legislative breath (the 14-day rule's drafting by committee-hours, the donor-register-opening-of-anonymity's 2005 revision decades later); the American counter-model (no statute, the professional-ethics-committees-and-ASRM-practice-committee regime, the 'the-tremor-of-the-defining-cases-coming-through-tort-instead-of-bioethics' - Mary Beth Whitehead's Baby M surrogacy case teaching contract law to the cradle). The prenatal strand: the aminocentesis-and-alpha-fetoprotein era's screening-birth (the 'screening-for-neural-tube-defects-consent-before-testing' lessons the 1970s wrote early), the amniocentesis-versus-chorionic-villous-sampling risk debates, the ultrasound-imaging-ethics' quiet arrival (the 'fetal-as-second-patient' framing's critics-and-defenders), and the NIPT shock (2011's validation-to-market in five years, the 'trisomy-21-termination-rates-and-the-disability-rights-protest' that made a blood test a political actor, and the sex-selection-bans'-legislative-aftermaths' argument). The abortion argument's modern shape came from the jurisprudence (Roe-and-Casey's-viability-architecture and its 2022 demolition's demonstration that the settlement was always legal-positional; the 'viability-moves-with-technology' observation's vindication by the 22-week-intensive-care-outcome data; and the global liberalisation wave's counter-current - Ireland-and-Poland-and-Argentina's-opposite-decades documenting the 'law-travels-both-ways' comparative literature). Embryo-research governance's fights: the 'therapeutic-cloning-and-STEM-cell-research-funding-wars' of the 2000s, the iPSC bypass (2006-07's Yamanaka-logic that dissolved one boundary-maintenance argument overnight, the field's lesson in how technology de-escalates metaphysics), the embryo-models' current 14-day-rule pressure, and the mitochondrial-replacement-technique's 2015-vote-and-2023-birth arc as the licensing model's most complete performance. CRISPR's two moments: 2015's somatic-editing euphoria and the He-Jan 2018 rupture (the 'the-China-scandal-and-its-race-to-govern' verdicts, the 2020-and-2021 National-Academies-and-WHO-framework answers, and the 'de facto moratorium by conditions-never-met' consensus the field polices with its second-look at its own governance), closing with the frontier waiting in the wings (IVG, ectogenesis, and the base-editing clinical trials whose germline-adjacent questions the chapter's arguments will inherit before their indications are written).** - [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]], [[genetics/genomic-medicine]].**

::: example The 22-week question {#ex-22week}
A fetus with isolated trisomy-18 at 22 weeks; the parents want continuation 'whatever happens'; the neonatologists' local practice is 'comfort care only at this diagnosis and gestation'. The midwifery team asks you, the obstetric consultant, to 'make them understand'.
::: solution
**Goals-versus-ceiling-versus-cruelty ([[#def-applications]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[paediatrics/newborn]]): The distinction the room needs: a treatment ceiling is a clinical judgment about what interventions can achieve (the 'CPR-at-trisomy-18-mortality-and-ventilation-outcome' data that genuinely justify limits), not a decision about the child's worth, and the parents' continuation choice is fully protected between those facts. The craft: the joint family meeting (obstetrics, neonatology, midwifery, and the palliative-care-and-parent-support-organisation voices in the room before the argument), the outcomes stated as ranges and lived realities (the 'median-survival-days-to-weeks-with-a-small-long-term-tail' literature and what intensive care would and would not change), the plan built as a pathway not a verdict (the antenatal counselling, the birth-plan-with-comfort-at-birth-versus-time-limited-trial-versus-full-intensive-decisions-pre-agreed-with-review-points - the 'shared-decision-with-prognostic-honesty' model the neonatal-ethics literature converged on after the 'withdraw-limit-versus-aggressive' wars), the conscience-and-staff-support layer (the nurses who will carry the chosen plan, the moral-distress debriefs, and the 'no-team-member-coerced-into-interventions-they-judge-harmful-with-transfer-of-care-as-the-realistic-accommodation' rule), and the 'make them understand' sentence refused (the parents are understanding; they are deciding, which is their role, and the clinician's job is the map and the honest prognosis and then loyalty to whatever they choose within what medicine can ethically do). The service design the case exposes: the perinatal-palliative-medicine pathway (the 'which-hospital-can-honor-a-22-week-comfort-plan-well' transfer question) is the difference between a death-as-failure and a death-as-care, and its absence is the actual clinical problem.**

## Exercises

::: exercise Date the argument {level=2}
Map these positions to their era's technology: 'donors must remain anonymous', 'the 14-day rule must become a 21-day rule', 'intersex infants should not undergo cosmetic surgery', 'the embryo created for research is a person', 'NIPT should be on the NHS'. Explain what each argument was really about beneath its stated object.
::: solution
**Technology-decodes-the-argument ([[#def-applications]], [[#def-governance]]): Anonymity-mandatory (the donor-insemination-boom era, 1960s-80s: the argument was actually the clinic's supply-economics and the parents' legal-parentage anxiety, resolved by the donor-conceived-people's own testimony and the register model when the identity-in-question became a civil-rights question). 14-to-21-days (the embryo-models era, 2021's scientific petition: actually the conflict between the rule's calendar-form and its moral-content - the stage-versus-clock argument about where the symbolic boundary lives). Intersex-infant-surgery (the patient-advocacy era of the 2000s-10s: the older 'parents-need-a-sex-to-register' administrative convenience versus the child's bodily-integrity claim, and the chapter's slow lesson that consent-timing is the whole ethics). 'Research-embryo-person' (the IVF-spare-embryos-and-stem-cell-debates of the 1990s-2000s: the position about moral status that the movement's energy came from protecting-potential-life-in-the-abortion-contest's-transferred-frame - the two arguments have rarely been separated honestly). NIPT-on-the-NHS (the genomic-medicine-era rationing debates: the stated argument about test-performance and cost-effectiveness, the real argument about whose-reproductive-decisions-the-state-facilitates and which-births-the-system-wants-informed-about. The exercise's point: beginning-of-life arguments almost never are what they say; the analyst's task is naming the technology that made the question newly-askable and the interest the answer serves.**

::: exercise Counsel the oncology patient {level=2}
A 29-year-old with newly diagnosed breast cancer wants fertility preservation but treatment must start in days; the IVF-stimulation pathway takes two weeks and costs what she does not have. Write the same-day plan's ethics and logistics.
::: solution
**Fertility-preservation-as-oncology-urgency ([[#def-applications]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]): The medicine: the random-start-stimulation protocols (the 'letrozole-primed-and-progestin-triggered' regimens that made the two-week pathway into days and the 'no-treatment-delay-finding' the onco-fertility trials settled), the oocyte-versus-embryo-versus-ovarian-tissue choices (the tissue-cryopreservation route for pre-pubertal and impossible-to-delay cases), and the cost-architecture (the public-fertility-preservation-for-medical-indication schemes whose eligibility the team should know by heart, the charity-and-trial-funding fallbacks, and the 'what-you-can-afford-determines-what-you-can-save' honesty when that is the fact). The ethics sequence: the same-day-preserved-fertility conversation (the 'fertility-affected-by-cancer' communication literature's findings on information-timing - she will remember little, so written-numbers-plus-named-contact-plus-second-conversation-booked), the partner-and-consent-questions that cannot be improvised later (the 'posthumous-use-and-relationship-change' consent forms completed while calm, the cryopreservation-period-and-review rules), and the non-abandonment clause (the 'if-we-proceed-without-preservation-because-of-timing-here-is-what-we-lose-and-who-decided' documentation that protects her future trust in the team). The logistics that are the ethics: the onco-fertility-fast-track referral already agreed between the cancer-and-reproductive services (the 'door-to-clinic-within-72-hours' pathways' outcome data), the drug-acquisition-and-cycle-monitoring arranged around her actual treatment dates, and the named-coordinator because a patient in the fertility-oncology gap between two services loses more time than either team notices. The clinical bottom line: 'your cancer treatment starts when it must; the fertility conversation happens today anyway, because this is the part of your care where the decision cannot be reversed later.'**

::: exercise Argue the sex-selection ban {level=3}
One jurisdiction bans all sex-selection reproduction; another bans it only below a gestational threshold; a third permits it for family-balancing after two of one sex. Grade each law against this chapter's framework: autonomy, harm, women's-equality and effectiveness evidence.
::: solution
**Three-laws-one-evidence-base ([[#def-applications]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]]): The evidence inputs: the sex-ratio-distortion data (the 'son-preference-and-birth-order' analyses from the sex-selective-termination-available-eras - the averted-daughters estimates whose best work shows the practice tracks availability more than culture-change), the travel-and-unsafe-alternative effectiveness literature (bans' effects: reduction in travel for some, determination-for-others, and the never-counted-backyard-endpoints), and the autonomy-and-equality argument's collision (the reproductive-liberty position versus the 'sex-selection-is-bodily-level-sexism-and-consent-under-patriarchal-pressure-is-the-question-not-the-answer' feminist critique - the two camps' genuine disagreement about whether individual choices aggregate to harms). The grading: the total ban fails the effectiveness-and-women's-safety tests (the pregnancy-continuation-or-clandestine-abortion effects fall on the women least able to travel) and overreaches autonomy at the family-planning end while symbolically condemning a practice; the gestational-threshold variant is internally incoherent (the sex is known early; the threshold targets the wrong variable); the conditional-permission model (family-completion context, counselling requirements, clinic-regulated routes) tracks the actual harm-question's shape (the individual-family-versus-population-ratio problem) but is administratively brittle and philosophically uncomfortable (the state ranking reasons-for-having-children - which no other reproduction law does). The honest synthesis for a legislator: the ratio harm is real but population-level and addressed better by daughter-security policy (the inheritance-and-safety-and-old-age-data on son-preference's drivers), the individual decision sits within accepted procreative liberty, and the law that survives review of all four criteria is the counselling-plus-no-clinic-advertising-plus-monitoring-ratios package rather than a prohibition on the indication. The chapter's sentence: this is the one selection question where the aggregate and individual harms genuinely diverge, which is why every easy answer is wrong twice.**
:::

::: exercise Design the post-mortem gamete policy {level=2}
A bereaved partner requests sperm retrieval and use after her husband's sudden death; he left nothing written. Draft the clinic-committee procedure: the consent question, the child's interests, the family objections, and the decision's reviewability.
::: solution
**Posthumous-procreation-procedure ([[#def-applications]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]): The consent question is the law's hardest and the ethics' easiest: retrieval is a bodily intervention on the dead (the authority-route: the partner's-in-the-moment-consent-versus-the-regulator-versus-court authorisation that varies by jurisdiction - retrievals proceed routinely; the dispute is always about use), and use turns on the deceased's documented or convincingly-inferred wishes (the 'embryos-and-sperm-and-the-written-consent-acts'-architectures where no-consent-means-no-use, and the jurisdictions' court-overrides whose pattern favours the partner where the couple's fertility-treatment-journey proves intent). The child's-interests limb (the 'deliberately fatherless-child' objection answered with the actual-welfare-literature - family-process-over-family-structure, but honestly weighed where it bears on the register-and-inheritance-and-support-arrangements the applicant must navigate). The family objections (the grandparents-who-want-no-legal-heir-uncle question - the estate-and-parentage consequences that make the committee's legal-advice limb mandatory, and the partner's own counsel independent of the clinic's). The procedure that makes it reviewable: the urgent-committee-convening-within-the-biological-window (the retrieval's hours-limit naming the clock honestly), the evidence-standard-written-down (anything-in-writing-or-recorded-versus-testimony-of-fertility-plans-and-its-graded-weight), the applicant's-independent-advocate, the decision-published-anonymised, and the appeal-route-to-a-court because a clinic is the wrong final arbiter of a child's legal beginning. The drafting principle for the policy: the couple's procreative project survived one partner; the committee's job is not to grant or refuse grief but to decide whether reproduction here is the continuation of a shared decision or the beginning of someone else's - and to say, in writing, which it found and why.**
