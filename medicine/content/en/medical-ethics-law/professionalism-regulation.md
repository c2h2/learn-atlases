Professionalism is the chapter where the previous seven become an institution: the reason confidentiality, consent and candour are enforceable rather than aspirational is that a profession exists with the machinery to enforce them, and the reason that machinery is trusted (or not) is how it behaves when trust is expensive. **This chapter covers the social contract of the professions (privilege exchanged for self-policing, its guild history and its modern enforcement stack of appraisal, regulation and law), speaking up (whistleblowing structures, colleague-concern routes and the just-culture template), the integrity of influence (industry relationships, gifts, conflicts and the disclosure architecture), and the profession's own competence management (appraisal and revalidation, the revalidation-evaluation literature, remediation, ill-health pathways and the detection problem nobody has solved).**


::: definition The professional compact and its enforcement {#def-compact}
**Professionalism is best understood as a bargain rather than a demeanour: society grants a self-governing occupation monopoly-ish privileges (who may practise, price partly outside markets, control entry, hold information asymmetries and confidential knowledge) in exchange for competence, altruism and self-policing that markets and pure law cannot supply - the historic patient-centredness-not-privilege charter language, the 'professionalism-as-social-contract' literature (the Parson-and-Goodin-versions), and the critics' reminder that the compact historically delivered oligopoly with a halo (the registration-acts' professional-control origins, the guild-economics and the 'ethics-code-as-barrier-to-entry' histories). The modern enforcement stack has three layers worth distinguishing: employment-and-appraisal governance (clinical-negotiation-and-named-senior-clinician systems - the post-Mid-Staffordshire-and-Hinchingbrooke-and-Candour-architecture where the named-clinician is the accountability-unit's smallest unit), external regulation (the GMC/NMC-and-American-state-board-and-AHP-regulator family: registration-as-public-protection-not-membership-honour, with the fitness-to-practise apparatus - the 'the-doctor-is-not-safe' threshold-versus-'the-doctor-was-rude' grievance-capture problem the regulators' reform-programmes fight, the 'no-fault-and-low-threshold-and-early-resolution'-proposals, and the 'regulators-punish-communication-failure-more-than-cognitive-error' finding that the system would rather sanction the rude than fix the tired), and law (the professional's own criminal exposure - the false-certification andprescription-and-billing frauds, the 'above-in-conduct' prosecutions, and the regulator's increasing parallel-proceedings practice). The compact's current frictions: the 'duty of candour' statute-and-professional-code era (a legal duty to tell patients when things go wrong with an apology and a written statement - the post-Stavanger-and-Mid-Staffordshire-legislation, its compliance-audits-finding-the-notification-half-working-and-the-apology-half-genuine, and the defensive-medicine-debates-never-really-answered because the data on litigation-savings-stay-ambiguous-but-the-ethics-was-never-about-litigation), the revalidation-and-appraisal systems (the 'three-year-cycle-with-multi-source-feedback-and-monthly-engagement' architectures - the international revalidation diffusion after the UK case, and its evaluation: useful for the conscientious, theatre for the rest, expensive as a trust-indicator), and the transparency wave (publication of outcomes and complaints, the 'named-consultant-and-surgical-audit-publication' policies, and the 'does-transparency-drive-quality-or-defensiveness' literature whose honest verdict is both). What clinicians should actually know how to do with the compact: when to escalate outside the institution (the whistleblowing next definition), when the regulator deserves the referral (the 'dangerous-now' threshold and the duty-to-report-others duty with its 'colleague-code-silence' pathology), and when to refuse the privilege (the corruption-and-gifts definition) - the professional-autonomy that makes the bargain defensible is exercised most seriously in these three unglamorous acts.**
:::

::: definition Speaking up: whistleblowing and speaking-for patients {#def-speak}
**Whistleblowing law and professional duty protect the act of reporting danger when internal routes fail or are the problem; medicine's own experience (the Bristol-and-Staffordshire-and-Morecambe-line, the Harold-Shipman-shadow over 'the-doctor-who-reported-and-lost-his-contract', and the pediatric-surgery-and-unit-closure-cases-where-the-referral-was-delayed-for-decades) supplies the field's settled diagnosis: the typical failure is not courage but structure - the reporting route that runs through the people being reported, the 'local-resolution' expectation that functions as suppression, the absence of any named-independent-recipient, and the culture that files concern as personality-conflict. The protective architecture that works, per the comparative law-and-practice literature: a protected-disclosure route with a recipient outside the line-management-chain (the prescribed-person regime - regulators, the ombuds, named-officers), interim anti-retaliation protections that act fast because retaliation is fast and litigation is slow, the 'reasonable-belief-in-imminent-danger-to-patients' threshold that does not require the whistleblower to be right (the detrimental reliance on incomplete information being the normal condition of clinical foresight), confidential-and-anonymous-channels-with-feedback-loops (the 'speak-up-reforms' from financial-regulation imported into health - the 'psychological-safety'-survey literature and its outcome correlations), and the duty-to-act-on-reports-half that every system neglects (the 'reports-received-reports-closed-without-action' audits the safety-inspectorates now publish). The professional-duty edge is the referral-of-a-colleague question (this chapter's neighbouring definition's hardest act): the duty to raise concern about an unsafe colleague runs first to the patient-facing-instant (stop-the-clinical-exposure-now - the list, the supervision, the removal-of-duties route), then to the proper-authority where the local response is absent or self-protecting, and the 'collegiality-versus-loyalty' argument is settled against silence by every code written after the cases in which silence was the mechanism of harm. The personal cost accounting is not optional ethics: whistleblower-health-and-family-and-career-outcomes-are-the-moral-bill-society-runs-on-the-people-who-protect-it, and the chapter's professional-ethics verdict is that institutions are judged by what happens to the person who complains, which is why the 'duty-of-candour-to-staff-as-well-as-patients' framing and the 'just-culture' template (blame-the-system-unless-the-individual-act-was-beyond-the-pale - the Reason-to-Regan-decimal ladder of unsafe-acts, the 'substitution-test-would-another-competent-clinician-have-done-that'-criterion) are the two instruments that determine whether speaking up is a career-exit or a routine clinical act. The trainee-facing version: report-through-channels-that-exist-and-are-recorded-not-through-hopes; document-what-you-saw-in-plain-fact-language-with-dates-and-numbers-because-the-investigation-will-be-about-your-contemporaneous-notes; find-one-ally-with-a-role (the medical- director, the nurse-at-the-top, the union, the regulator - not a friend at dinner); and know that being transferred-or-sidelined-after-raising-concern-is-itself-reportable-and-legally-actionable-in-proper-systems, which is the smallest thing the law can do for the act that keeps everyone else safe.**
:::

::: definition Industry, gifts and conflicts: the integrity of influence {#def-conflicts}
**Medicine's dependence on industry (research funding, devices, formulary supply, the training-courses-and-conference-ecosystem) makes conflict-of-interest management a core professional competence rather than an etiquette question, and the evidence base is not subtle: prescription-and-formulary-behaviour shifts with even modest hospitality (the pen-and-lunch-and-meeting-seats literature, the industry-payment-public-disclosure datasets' findings - the 'small-payments-most-numbers-largest-total'-shape), guideline-and-consensus-panel-capture (the 'conflict-of-interest-in-continuous-infusion-and-diabetes-and-psychiatry-panels' analyses and the WHO-and-IOM-conflict-management-standards - chair-and-voting-majority-rules rather than disclosure-by-itself), and the ghostwriting-and-seeding-research machinery (the 'the-publication-was-the-marketing'-cases, the 'trials-designed-to-flatter' registration-audits, and the sponsored-symposium-to-formulary-advocacy pipeline). The regulatory responses' spectrum: the Sunshine-Act-and-EU-transparency-code-and-Association-of-British-Pharmaceutical-Industry-code-and-Purchasing-Association-and-Academic-Medical-Centers'-constraint-families - from 'ban-all-promotional-contact-and-most-industry-funded-CME' (the strict-institutional-and-departmental-policies with their 'education-survived'-evaluations) through 'disclose-everything-and-manage' (the register-and-declaration model - the journals', committees' and regulators' declaration architectures) to 'name-and-shame-in-public-databases' (the disclosure-as-social-norm-re-engineering hypothesis, with its honest finding that disclosure-changes-reader-belief-more-than-writer-behaviour). The chapter's operational rules for the individual clinician: the gift test ('would I be comfortable if this appeared in the local paper with my name and their product', and the stricter institutional version - no-personal-gifts-none-of-those-lunches), the relationship test for your own prescribing (notice the drug you chose that you did not notice choosing, and audit it against the guidelines), the committee test (declare-before-deliberation-not-at-publication, recuse-from-chairs-and-votes-and-drafting-with-teeth), the consultancy question (industry-paid-speaker-and-advisory-board-roles-as-individual-freedom-versus-institutional-risk, the 'who-owns-your-expertise-when-it-is-rented'-problem, and the medical-school-faculty-conflict-of-commitment-and-accountability-regimes as the honest middle), and the disclosure-honesty-over-disclosure-volume rule (a real list of what-might-influence-you-beats-a-page-of-boilerplate that immunises the form-signer and misleads the reader). The structural point the profession cannot opt out of: the industry relationship is the research-funding-and-innovation-pipeline's price, so the integrity work is design not abstinence - firewalled-endowment-models-for-CME, sponsor-blind-trial-conduct-with-registered-protocols-and-independent-statisticians (the 'trials-unit-model' the cardiology-oncology-networks proved could keep bad-drugs-out-and-good-drugs-credible), public-registration-and-pooled-negotiation-and-pricing-transparency, and the open-science-architecture (pre-registration, data-sharing, registered-reports) as the discipline that makes influence visible where it is exercised. The chapter's verdict, in the compact's own language: society tolerates the relationship because it needs the products, and trusts the profession to have designed the firewalls honestly - the day the public believes the design is theatre, the relationship gets regulated by people who understand medicine worse and the innovations get slower and more expensive, which is the harm to patients that makes conflict-management a clinical duty, not a compliance chore.**
:::

::: definition Revalidation, appraisal and the competence duty {#def-revalidation}
**The professional's own competence is the compact's load-bearing obligation and the least trusted, so modern systems replaced virtue-assumption with documented cycle: appraisal (the annual employer-run conversation - the NHS's responsible-officer structure, the multisource feedback, personal-development-plan and the extermal-review of a sample - with its documented weaknesses: box-ticking among the willing, no signal for the struggling-who-look-fine, and the 'appraisal-as-applause' finding from the quality-assurance surveys), revalidation (the GMC's three-year cycle of portfolios and attestations and the licence-to-practice renewal question - the international diffusion after the UK model and the evaluations' honest verdict that it reassures the public more than it detects the rare dangerous doctor, which is a design-truth not an indictment since detection is the regulator's job and reassurance-with-a-trigger-is-the-appraisal's), and the remediation-and-ill-health pathway (the 'ill-health-not-misconduct'-first architecture every reformed regulator now claims and few execute well - the 'sick-doctor-policy' literature on why doctors hide illness from their own systems: the revalidation-portfolio-and-sickness-record-panics, the confidential-hearing-loss-disaster-cases, and the 'employers-as-patients-not-police' occupational-health-design-fix). The measurement problem is the field's permanent embarrassment: no system reliably detects the deteriorating clinician before a pattern-of-harm event (the outcome-data-mining-and-peer-comparison-and-'statistical-signals-versus-small-numbers' literature - the individual-surgeon-audit's-power-problem that the 'regulation-by-averages' critics sharpen with the anesthesia-and-cardiac-surgery-early-retirement-analyses), so the honest design purpose of appraisal-and-revalidation is a conversation-with-consequences-that-makes-early-conversation-possible (the 'difficult-conversations-about-decline-before-the-crisis'-trainings, the colleague-concern-routes that land somewhere competent, and the 'no-surprises-at-the-regulator'-ideal). The trainee-relevant duties the literature generates: know your appraisal is a governance document (write the development plan you will actually execute, name the things you cannot do, and collect the multisource feedback that will hurt - the 'feedback-avoidance-as-safety-risk' finding applies to you most when you are doing well), keep a contemporaneous practice diary because revalidation-attestations-and-reflections-are-only-credible-as-a-habit, treat the health-and-addiction-and-bereavement-questions-on-the-forms as the safety-instrument they are (the physician-health-programmes' outcome data are uniformly better than their reputation and their reputation is the reason men do not call), and understand the numbers around your own practice (your outcome dashboards, your complaint-and-claim history, your prescribing outliers - the autonomy argument dies when you cannot discuss your own data).**
:::

::: example The named clinician's bad week {#ex-named}
You are the named consult for a ward whose infection deaths are climbing; your medical director asks for 'the clinical governance narrative before the board'; two registrars mention, separately, that the ward 'stops escalating over weekends'; your own clinic audit shows documentation gaps in exactly those escalations.
::: solution
**Accountability-without-scapegoating([[#def-compact]], [[#def-speak]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): The named-clinician-role's purpose is exactly this moment: the accountable person who is not asked to be omnipotent but is asked to be accurate, so the narrative's spine is the sequence the governance literature rewards: describe-the-signal-precisely (the infection-case-audit-with-dates-and-escalation-timings-not-adjectives, the weekend-pattern-as-a-testable-hypothesis-from-two-independent-accounts-and-your-own-data), state-your-own-unit's-contribution-honestly (the documentation-gap-finding-in-first-person - candour about your patch is what buys the credibility for the parts above your pay-grade), name-what-is-beyond-you (the staffing-establishment-and-weekend-decision-rights you do not hold - this is the 'responsible-officer-with-mandate-matching' argument the post-Mid-Staffordshire-reforms were supposed to fix), and commit-to-a-measurable-cycle (the escalation-protocol-with-weekend-simulator-drills-and-audit-of-time-to-antibiotics-and-time-to-senior-review-with-monthly-dashboard-to-the-board). The two conversations the week requires: the staff-side (the 'your-concerns-are-the-evidence-and-here-is-the-plan-and-here-is-what-happens-if-it-does-not-work' meeting that converts fear-of-escalation into escalation-with-support, and the psychological-safety-literature's finding that naming-the-authority-you-yourself-lack-is-what-makes-junior-speaking-believable), and the patient-facing duty-of-candour (the families of the deaths deserve the honest-account-with-the-apology-and-this-is-what-we-changed-because-the-law-now-says-so-and-because-the-alternative-is-what-Staffordshire-taught-the-price-of). The line that keeps the role honest: the narrative goes to the board with your signature and its data-appendix-attached - not 'the team', not the infection-doctor's-adjacent-heads-of-service as a chorus, and if the board wants a throat, offer the system-design-choice (the weekend-rota-decision-they-make-not-yours) because that is the truthful causal-location-of-blame-and-the-only-version-of-accountability-that-changes-the-next-month.**
:::

::: example The conference invitation {#ex-conf}
A device company offers to fund three departmental places at its international symposium (fees, travel, a nice hotel) with 'faculty' roles for your consultant and you; the same device is under your trust's procurement review, and the journal you both publish in requires conflict declarations.
::: solution
**Hospitality-arithmetic([[#def-conflicts]], [[#def-compact]]): The evaluation: this is not a gift, it is a procurement-adjacent relationship with two clinicians attached, so the tests run in order - the transparency test (the Sunshine-and-ABPI-and-EU-code-registers-will-record-it-by-name-with-the-company's-product-attached, so 'nobody need know' is unavailable), the role test ('faculty' at a sponsor's own meeting is marketing-with-a-lanyard; genuine educational faculty-roles survive being named and paid by the university or the society), and the decision test (you are inside the procurement-review's influence-radius - the recusal-or-full-disclosure fork the committee-rules apply to you personally the moment the review is live). The permissible version, because purism also has a cost (the education-and-networking-and-industrial-partnership-the-department-genuinely-needs): accept only what your institution's written policy permits with prior approval, route the funding through the department's-education-account-not-individual-purse-not-travel-agent-stipend, publish the relationship on the procurement-submission-and-the-trust-register-and-every-future-paper-and-talk, and take the science not the suite (the abstracts and the technique-videos do not require the hotel's spa). The consultant conversation that ends the negotiation: 'the device might be good for our patients; if we take this trip, we have to be able to say so afterwards and be believed - so we go only in the sunshine version, and I will not be on the review panel either way.' The systems-fix worth proposing while you are in the room: the department's standing industry-engagement rule (what-we-will-take-what-we-never-take-and-who-approves-in-writing - the policy that spares every future associate-head the same Friday-afternoon argument, and the trust-level register where the procurement office can actually see who the company has been flying - the disclosure architecture only works when the discloser and the decider read the same page).**
:::

::: example The colleague with the tremor {#ex-tremor}
Your consultant's hand tremor has worsened; two central-line attempts this month went to a second operator; a trainee mentions the parking bumps. You have seen the colleague twice since; he is well-liked, recently bereaved, and preps your lists.
::: solution
**Loyalty-is-what-you-do-for-someone-not-what-you-avoid-about-them([[#def-speak]], [[#def-revalidation]], [[#def-compact]]): The sequence that is both brave and kind: talk-to-him-first-and-for-real (one private conversation from a peer, not a delegation: what you have seen in dated factual terms - the line attempts, the parking if you are friends enough - the bereavement acknowledged, and the ask stated as medicine: a health check and a temporary reduction in the procedures his hands do, framed the way you would want a colleague to frame it for you; and hear the answer with the possibility that he already knows), escalate-on-action-not-on-gossip (if he agrees: support the conversation with the clinical director that reorganises the work before anyone else notices; if he refuses and the attempts keep failing: the duty has moved past him - the responsible-clinician-or-clinical-director-with-the-patient-safety-case-in-facts-and-dates-not-adjectives, and the interim step is always the clinical one: someone-else-does-those-procedures-now-which-is-a-rota-conversation-not-an-accusation). The ill-health-not-misconduct frame is not softness, it is epidemiology (the bereavement-depression-sleep-deprivation-and-neurological-onset differential is why every reformed regulator now runs health-first pathways and why the occupational-health referral route with confidentiality carve-outs exists); the governing-meson that keeps you honest is that none of your sentences should be about whether he is a good doctor - they are about whether these hands can do this procedure today - because that is both the whole question and the one formulation that leaves his dignity a place to stand. The things not to do: the ward-wide whisper network (it converts a solvable health problem into a competence tribunal with an audience), the anonymous letter first (skip straight to him unless he is an immediate danger to a current patient), and the heroic self-supervision of 'I will just quietly do all his lines myself' (a cover that delays the only intervention that helps him and protects nobody's patients). The ending the system was built for: the Parkinson's-diagnosis-at-a-treatable-stage, the modified practice he keeps with his knowledge-and-consent-not-over-his-objection, the trainees who watched a colleague handle it properly, and the lesson they will re-test on each other for thirty years.**
:::

::: example The viral compliment {#ex-viral}
A patient's grateful video about 'the doctor who saved me' goes local-news viral; the clip shows your ward, names your registrar, and the hospital's press office wants to re-post it with the consultant quoted; the registrar has anxiety and did not consent to being the face.
::: solution
**Professionalism in the attention economy ([[#def-compact]], [[medical-ethics-law/confidentiality]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]): The analysis in order: the patient may of course share their own story (their experience, their image of her own choosing), but the clip showing identifiable others on a clinical ward involves the co-patients'-and-staff'-confidentiality-and-the-filming-rules-that-exist-for-the-dignity-reason-not-the-bureaucracy-reason (the ward should have asked before cameras appeared, and 'it is a compliment' does not change the data-protection-and-dignity arithmetic of a bed-space), while the registrar's own consent to be named-and-amplified-is-hers-to-give (the 'praise you cannot decline' problem the professionalism literature names: viral praise of a named trainee is a hazard to them - the inbox that follows every public face, the future-adversary-who-will-find-the-video, and for the anxiety-sufferer specifically, the exposure-panic loop the trust's own occupational-health vocabulary could describe). The handling: thank-the-patient-and-the-producer-genuinely, explain-what-can-be-reposted (the story without the ward-footage-or-the-junior's-name-until-she-chooses - a re-cut she is offered first), get-the-consents-that-are-required (co-patients-visible-in-frame-and-the-staff), let-the-registrar-lead-on-herself-and-record-it, and give the hospital its win honestly (the 'we are proud of this team; here is what they actually did; the names are theirs to use' statement that re-posts with dignity and performs exactly the culture the recruitment pages pretend to have). The teaching layer for everyone watching this negotiation: the professional reputation the trust is marketing is made of individual humans' rights to decline the marketing (the GMC-era codes' line about 'acting with dignity and respect online applies to institutions amplifying people', and the press-office-collaboration-SLA - consent-before-share, no-bedside-footage, staff-naming-by-staff-choice - that turns this from a Friday crisis into a form). The closing sentence to the producer, warm and final: 'the best version of this story is the one where nobody in it was asked.'**

::: history
**The profession's regulatory history is a pendulum between guild and guardianship: the Victorian registration acts (1858's Medical Act creating the register - a consumer-protection statute that the profession captured into a membership-privilege, the 'who-registers-the-regulators'-question born on day one), the early ethic-codes' clinical-decorum (the 'no-advertising-no-patronage-no-divided-loyalty'-canons whose enforcement-with-teeth-arrived-late-and-selectively), and the twentieth century's divorce of ethics from etiquette as the scandals accumulated (the 'GMC-of-the-1970s-rarely-striking'-era-convictions-for-the-undramatic-and-the-post-Shipman-2001-Forster-and-Walker-inquiries-inventing-performance-and-health-proceedings-because-the-old-misconduct-only-jurisdiction-had-literally-no-category-for-a-doctor-who-was-bad-at-his-job-innocently). The patient-safety turn rewrote the diagnosis: the American-to-err-is-human-1999-report-and-the-Leape-and-Wächter-patient-safety-publications, the Bristol-baby-heart-inquiry's-verdict-that-the-profession-protected-itself-before-its-patients (the 'culture-of-secrecy-and-blame'-finding and the clinical-governance-and-duty-of-candour legislative aftermath), Mid-Staffordshire's-2010-and-2013-reports-making-'the-organisation-tells-the-truth-or-the-bodies-tell-it-for-it'-the-management-science-of-the-decade, the 'just-culture-and-psychological-safety-and-speaking-up-and-the-public-concerns-database-and-the-concern-at-work-helpline'-apparatus, and the 'regulators-must-see-staff-wellbeing-too'-NHS-Perspectives-survey-era-with-the-medical-director-for-wellbeing-crop. The revalidation-and-appraisal arc: the Shipman-triggered-Goodman-review-recommending-regular-licence-renewal-against-the-profession's-initial-horror (the 'three-year-cycle-coming-to-America-too'-board-of-medical-specialties-and-state-board-movements), and the implementation-compromises-everyone-lives-with-now. The conflicts half: the IOM-2009-conflict-of-interest-and-industry-relationship-report-and-the-Angell-and-Friedberg-'the-bite-from-inside'-era-arguing-and-the-PhRMA-code-revisions-and-Sunshine-Act-2010-and-Government-Purchasing-Payment-Prescriber-Payer-and-European transparency codes-and-the-journals'-ICMJE-declarations-and-the ghostwriting-and-seeding-research court and congressional dossiers, the CME-firewall model, and the pen-and-lunch disclosure datasets. And the current, unsettled layer: the post-pandemic-trust-rebuilding-agenda ('the-public-inquiry-era's-transparency-and-accountability-lessons-not-yet-legislated-everywhere'), the AI-and-medico-legal-accountability-frontier ('who-is-the-responsible-officer-for-a-model'), the international-recruitment-and-regulatory-parity-questions ('do-our-regulators-protect-patients-everywhere-or-only-at-home'), and the 'professionalism-as-observed-behaviour-versus-performed-virtue'-assessment-research-argument-about-whether-any-of-this-is-teachable. The clinician's throughline stays: every protection now resented as bureaucracy is a grave with a name, and the compact is renewed only by the acts that cost something - candour, escalation, and refusing the nice hotel - [[medical-ethics-law/ethical-principles]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**
:::

## Where this leads

Patient-safety systems and human-factors in [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], the records-and-handover discipline in [[clinical-skills/records-handover]], communication's duty-of-candour applications in [[clinical-skills/communication]], the prescribing-conflict interface in [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]], the research-integrity layer in [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]], and confidentiality's enforcement in [[medical-ethics-law/confidentiality]].

::: summary
- Professionalism is a compact: privileged practice exchanged for competence, altruism and honest self-policing - and it is audited not by declarations but by what happens when speaking up costs the speaker something.
- The enforcement stack (appraisal, regulation, law) fails in predictable ways - rudeness sanctioned while exhaustion is celebrated, revalidation reassuring more than detecting - and the competent professional knows the stack's blind spots well enough to report into them deliberately.
- Speaking-up systems work through structure, not heroism: independent recipients, fast anti-retaliation protection, reasonable-belief thresholds, and action-on-reports audits; institutions are judged by the fate of the person who complains.
- Industry relationships are the innovation pipeline's price and the integrity work is design, not abstinence: prior approval, sunshine registers, recusal with teeth, firewalled education, and disclosure lists a reader can actually use.
- Candour and the named-responsible-officer roles convert transparency from virtue to routine; the apology and the honest account are cheaper than the inquiry, and were never about the cost.
- The compact's final test is inward: a profession that polices its members' health with the compassion it would want for its own, and reads its own dashboards before a lawyer does, has earned the autonomy it keeps invoking.
:::

A student asks why doctors 'have their own rules' instead of just following the law and hospital policy. Give the social-contract answer in ninety seconds, including one historical fact that makes the privilege less flattering.
::: solution
**The-compact-explained-plainly([[#def-compact]]): 'Three things society cannot get from markets or general law: it cannot check whether the person stitching your tendon is competent (nobody in the queue can), it cannot stop you from exploiting the patient's ignorance or secrets, and it cannot compel you to keep learning after qualification. So society delegates: it lets the profession control entry, set standards and police itself - in exchange for practising competence and loyalty to patients over profit or convenience. The uncomfortable history: the delegation was won partly as a guild protection - the 1858 registration act that created the British register was captured into a members'-privilege for decades, and much of the twentieth century's self-regulation protected practitioners more reliably than patients; the Shipman-and-Bristol-and-Staffordshire-era reforms exist because that imbalance kept being discovered. Which is why the modern bargain's enforcement is external as much as internal - regulators with public-protection mandates, appraisal and revalidation, candour law - and why your professional obligations you will actually feel (candour after harm, reporting the unsafe colleague, refusing the funded weekend) are the exact points where your self-interest and the patient's diverge. The compact is real, it is bought with those inconvenient acts, and every generation of doctors inherits it with the same small-print audit duty.'**
:::

::: exercise Grade the systems {level=2}
Compare three colleague-concern routes: tell the consultant and hope; the hospital's confidential concern hotline; the regulator's public-concerns channel. Grade each on speed, protection, detection-power and patient-safety economics, and give the escalation rule you would teach interns.
::: solution
**Route-forensics([[#def-speak]], [[#def-compact]]): Tell-the-consultant: fastest and often right (most concerns are rostering-and-load problems solved locally), but zero independence when the consultant is the concern, and no record - which is why it is the first step, never the only one when danger persists. Hotline: independence-and-record with variable trust (the hotlines' real-world problem is the action-side audit - reports-closed-without-action statistics, the anonymity-that-costs-the-caller-credibility, and the feedback-loop-absence that teaches staff reporting changes nothing); detection-power is decent for patterns, poor for crises. Regulator-channel: the correct destination for the the dangerous-now colleague when the employer is not protective (the public-protection jurisdiction with fitness-to-practise powers and interim-orders capability), but slow by design (its threshold is public-protection-not-interpersonal-friction) and personally costly without the law's anti-retaliation protections behind you. The rule for interns: patient-facing-now (stop the exposure: escalate clinically, list-and-supervision, the the on-call consultant with the power to pause the work), local-record-today (the the incident report and the dated factual note - contemporaneous documentation is the whole later case), and outside routes when local action has not moved within the time the risk allows - with the two sentences that make it survivable: keep your reports factual, dated and copied, and know that the whistleblowing-protection law in your jurisdiction exists for the shift where hospital loyalty looks like patient endangerment.**
:::

::: exercise Refuse the offer gracefully {level=2}
Draft the email declining a pharmaceutical company's paid advisory-board seat (5,000 per afternoon, your specialty's leading drug under your committee's review) while preserving the legitimate relationship your department needs, plus the one-sentence register entry if you had accepted.
::: solution
**Refusal-with-relations([[#def-conflicts]]): Model email: 'Thank you for the invitation to the advisory board. I must decline: [Drug] falls under active review by the committee I sit on, and a paid advisory relationship with its manufacturer would compromise both my position on that review and the appearance of it - a problem for you as much as me. I am glad to continue engaging through the legitimate routes: submitting our unanswered trial-questions through your scientific-liaison channel, the society-funded education sessions where I hold no commercial relationship, and the published-data discussions our specialties share. I hope you understand why I must record this exchange, and I would welcome the day the evidence makes the case for you.' The discipline: the decline names the conflict not the company's character (relationships survive accurate objections), it offers the real alternatives (the science has lawful channels and saying so raises their cost of grey-zone recruiting), and it is itself recorded in your institutional register (refusals of conflicted offers belong in the transparency system, because tomorrow someone will ask what you took). The accepted-version sentence had you stayed on the committee and taken the seat, your journal submission, committee declaration and the public payment register would each need: 'I have received 5,000 in the past 24 months from the manufacturer of the product under this committee's review; I have not participated in deliberations on that product.' The exam footnote: disclosure's function is to move the conflict from invisible to manageable - the second clause (not-participated) is what makes the first (the number) honest rather than a getout-of-jail card.**
:::

::: exercise Prepare for appraisal {level=2}
A high-performing surgeon's appraisal file: multisource feedback never requested twice, CPD logged as conference receipts, reflective accounts that all end 'no changes needed', and a prescribing outlier she has never opened. Coach her, in one page, to a defensible portfolio - and state which item a regulator would notice first.
::: solution
**Appraisal-as-governance-not-theatre([[#def-revalidation]]): The coach's page: (1) multisource feedback, and the point is the friction (the tool detects blind spots and drifting behaviour in the teams that watch you tired - so request it from the anaesthetists and theatre sisters, and put the 'feedback avoidance as risk signal' line in her personal-development-plan with a date), (2) CPD logs are inputs, not evidence (convert two receipts into one change she made: the technique she adopted or abandoned and why, the audit she closed the loop on - the revalidation standard's 'evidence of engagement, not attendance' wording exists for exactly this), (3) reflection that ends in 'no changes needed' is the form telling you it is unread (one real case with a real uncertainty and what she did about it is worth five spotless paragraphs - the portfolios that protect doctors are the ones where the learning is visible), (4) the prescribing outlier gets opened today (the outlier report is her data and either her defence or her indictment - the rule of thumb: a clinician who cannot discuss her own dashboard has no standing to invoke clinical autonomy at any meeting that matters), and (5) attestations from people who actually see her (the 'responsible person who guesses' is the weak link the GMC reforms target). The regulator-notice-order: the unopened-outlier-first (an unexamined-prescribing-or-practice-signal-is-the-classic-precursor-pattern-in-harm-inquiries), then the never-requested feedback - the avoidance signal the Shipman-era reforms built appraisal partly to catch. Her honest counterargument deserves the honest answer: all of this is theatre when nothing rests on it - which is why the portfolio's value is that it is discoverable in the one year when it will be read aloud, and the surgeon with a real file is the one who survives that reading intact.**
:::

::: exercise Write the candour moment {level=2}
A retained swab after hysterectomy, discovered day four; the patient is now septic, re-operated, recovering. She asks 'how could you do this to me?' Write the conversation's structure: the words that carry the duty of candour and the two sentences that breach it.
::: solution
**Candour-under-harm([[#def-compact]], [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): Structure: sit, name yourself, then answer the question straight (yes, we left it, that should never have happened, I am sorry - the apology-first rule of the statutory-duty design: say what happened, say sorry, do not qualify the apology with 'but you must understand'), the account as far as is known tonight (how it happens with swab counts, unknowns stated honestly, and the full investigation to follow in writing - because the draft account is not the story's end), the consequences fairly stated (what the sepsis and the second surgery mean for her recovery, and what is known about the long term), the practical commitments (a named contact, the later meeting, documentation offered rather than awaited, and her records plus the complaints-and-compensation routes explained - because candour that withholds those routes is concealment with manners), and the change sentence (what is already different on that ward - this happened to her, not to the next patient, and prevention is the only ethical use of having harmed someone). The two breaching sentences: 'these things happen' (the fatalistic dissolution of responsibility the compensation-litigation-ethics literature identifies as the phrase patients remember for years) and 'I need to be careful what I say because of the lawyers' (the defensive-adjacent-confession-that-reframes-the-apology-as-strategy-and-converts-the-statute's-moral-revolution-into-its-opposite-in-front-of-the-bed). The follow-through: the written-account-within-days, the investigation findings actually shared - notifying and then hiding the report is the breach all over again, and a recurring inquiry exhibit, and-the-staff-side-support (the second-victim literature applies to the scrub team most that day, because candour cultures that abandon their own humans stop being candour cultures quietly).**

::: exercise Advise the editor {level=2}
A reviewer alerts you (the journal editor) that a published trial's supplementary analysis differs from its registry-listed primary outcome, the sponsor-employed authors added a post-hoc subgroup, and a medical-communications company authored the draft. Advise: the published-record actions, the author responses required, and the institutional referrals that are not your call but that you must trigger.
::: solution
**Editorial-integrity-machinery([[#def-conflicts]], [[medical-ethics-law/research-ethics]]): Record-first principle: the scientific record gets corrected on evidence, proportional-to-findings - the ladder: expression-of-concern (the-discrepancy-under-investigation-notice-attached-to-the-article - used when the audit will take months and readers are prescribing now), correction-or-erratum (a registered-versus-published outcome mismatch is not a typo - the registry was the pre-commitment, and the change needs a published explanation attached to the original), or retraction-with-the-reason-published (if the primary analysis was switched without registration and the headline claim rests on it - the Committee on Publication Ethics flowcharts exist to make this procedural, not personal). Author-process: the corresponding and guarantor authors asked for the statistical analysis plan, the registry records, and the documentation of who analysed and who wrote; declarations re-run under journal policy, not manuscript legend; the medical writer named per ICMJE rules - the ghosting finding by itself rarely retracts and rarely punishes, but the ledger matters. Your-not-your-call-referrals-with-a-named-owner-each: the authors' institutional research-integrity office (the-undeclared-writing-service-and-outcome-switch-is-their-jurisdiction-not-an-editor's-zeal), the-ethics-committee-that-approved-the-study (protocol-deviations-reach-them), the-funder-and-any-regulator-where-a-submission-relies-on-the-claim (the-trial-data-may-live-in-a-dossier-somewhere), and the-registry itself (results-record-corrections-run-through-the-sponsor-who-must-explain-them). The transparency that protects the journal: publish the timeline of your own process (the concern date, the authors' responses, the final notice - the 'editors defend the record, not people' posture that survives the defamation letter and, more importantly, the next prescription written from the page).**

::: exercise Handle the regulator's letter {level=3}
You receive a regulator's request following an anonymous complaint: your patient numbers, an outcome they consider an outlier, three clinical letters and your appraisal record. You have practised carefully; the complaint names a death you still think about. Respond: the practical steps, the emotional truth you should not hide from yourself, and what you may lawfully and wisely not do.
::: solution
**On-the-other-side-of-the-letter([[#def-compact]], [[#def-revalidation]]): Practical sequence: acknowledge it and diarise the deadline (regulators-deadline-and-proportionality-and-their-duty-to-be-fair-are-all-better-served-by-your-scheduled-response-than-your-panic), notify your defence organisation or union and your employer's named senior clinician per contract, because-the parallel processes you are unaware of are the ones that trip people (the employer may have duties of its own on the same events), gather the record from the primary sources, not your memory (the contemporaneous notes, the audit and the MDT minutes tell the case better than your best recall, in any direction), get the statistical claim properly analysed (small-denominator outlier reports are noisy, and the 'regulation by averages' critique is a real defence when the numbers are few - but have the analysis done by someone credible and keep the correspondence - cherry-picking your own statistician is how colleagues get additionally investigated for the wrong reasons), and answer only in the documentary form the case demands (the professional, unemotional tone every regulator says matters most, and which clinicians find impossible precisely about the death they cannot stop thinking about). The emotional truth to hide from nobody important: the case that stays with you is the same thing as being a good doctor, not evidence of guilt (the second-victim and moral-injury literature exists because this combination of care and exposure breaks people quietly - an occupational-health-or-peer-support-or-GP-with-a-protected-diagnosis-route-is-clinically-sensible-not-strategic - and never falsify anything to help the letters - the doctor who was open about  and the doctor who admits burnout and gets help is treated far fairer than the one whose cover-up creates a second case). The lawful-not-wises: no communicating with the complainant about the complaint outside official channels (contact reads as intimidation even when it is grief, and the professional-advocacy codes are clear), no rewriting notes (the backdating sentence that converts a clinical case into a fitness-to-practise case plus possible fraud - the one rule every defence lawyer recites first), and no signing a quiet departure before taking advice (the settlements clinicians sign to make it stop become the public record they did not know they were creating). The ending worth aiming for: a file that shows careful-practice-in-the-regulator's-own-ordered-way, and a you who still practises afterwards - because the process can be survived badly by the innocent as easily as the guilty, which is the argument for why the compact's procedures deserve your full participation even when they feel like betrayal by the profession.**
:::

::: exercise Design the policy {level=3}
Write your department's industry-engagement and transparency policy: one paragraph each on (1) education funding, (2) individual relationships and registers, (3) committee and formulary conflicts, (4) research collaborations and publication freedom, (5) enforcement and review - each with the sentence a hostile journalist could not improve.
::: solution
**A-policy-with-teeth([[#def-conflicts]], [[#def-compact]]): Education-funding: 'CME and teaching is department-designed and delivered by faculty; industry funds nothing directly to individuals or courses, contributions (if any) pass through the university's unrestricted educational fund with the funder named on the public page and no say in content, speakers or topics' (the-IOM-and-firewall-model's-sentence). Individual-relationships: 'every clinician declares all industry payments, honoraria, advisory roles and travel annually into the institutional register, published in aggregate and individually searchable; undeclared relationships found in public disclosure databases trigger automatic re-declaration of the affected papers and committees' (the-Sunshine-Act-datasets-as-policy-backstop - the-hostile-journalist-cannot-write-it-because-the-database-he-would-use-is-quoted-in-the-policy). Committees-and-formulary: 'any role in a company relationship disqualifies, for its duration and one year, from chairs, votes, evaluations or advocacy concerning that company's products; recusals are recorded and published in the committee minutes that name the product' (the-manage-dont-just-disclose-rule - the-minutes-are-the-enforcement-document). Research-collaborations: 'sponsored studies run under the university or trust contract with registered protocols and pre-specified analyses, independent custody of data, a publication-freedom clause with only a thirty-day confidential-content review, named-guarantor authorship per ICMJE including the medical-writer disclosure, and results published irrespective of direction; no departmental agreement without these terms is signed' (the-clause-every-clean-trial-network-already-lives-by). Enforcement-and-review: 'the policy is enforced by the research-integrity-and-governance office with sampling audits against public payment databases and journal declarations, published annual compliance statistics, a stated sanction ladder from correction to removal from committees, and an annual public review by a panel including patient representatives with power to amend' (the-audit-the-register-against-reality-design - because the policy that never cross-checks itself against the company's own published payments list is the policy the journalist rewrites for free). The meta-lesson for your file copy: every clause is written to be checked by someone who does not trust you - which is exactly how the profession keeps the autonomy it says it values.**
