::: summary
- Neonatal transition is a circulatory rerun done without a bypass: delayed clamping, warmth and effective ventilation cover most of birth's emergencies, and Apgar scores describe, never predict.
- A collapse at days 2-7 with a normal-looking start is sepsis or a duct-dependent cardiac lesion until proven otherwise; prostaglandin and antibiotics run together, and pre-ductal saturations alone miss left-heart disease.
- Vomiting with hyponatraemia and hyperkalaemia at 1-4 weeks is a salt-wasting crisis; no blood-spot screen overrules the baby in front of you.
- Preterm care is antenatal steroids and magnesium, gentle ventilation, caffeine, breast milk, temperature, family integration and honest periviability counselling with palliative care as a service.
- Jaundice is managed by risk of bilirubin encephalopathy, and persistent or conjugated jaundice after the phototherapy era is biliary atresia until proven otherwise.
- Newborn screening and examination are population medicine with individual duties: they need funded downstream pathways, equity of uptake, and transparency about what they do not detect.

## Taking it further

Three habits finish the newborn curriculum. Rehearse the resuscitation drill until the sequence is muscle memory, including the team roles and the decision to stop - the outcomes of delivery-room chaos track speaking practice far more than equipment. Guard the temperature and the glucose minute-by-minute in the first hour, because most preventable newborn harm in resource-rich settings is thermal and metabolic, not hypoxic. And write the examination that documents the hips, the red reflex, the palate and the murmur you actually heard, because the newborn examination is the only full physical assessment many people will ever receive intact, and its medicolegal history is a list of things done carefully by someone who expected to be asked.

## Taking it further

The infectious-diseases mindset in one paragraph: think in source, route, host and time. Identify the source before naming the syndrome; ask what the exposure history permits and what the local resistance map makes likely; adjust everything for the host standing in front of you; and treat the clock as a drug - the sepsis, endocarditis and meningitis outcomes are functions of hours. Then audit yourself against the two failure modes of the specialty: anchoring on the first plausible organism while the atypical presentation evolves, and de-escalating never - the narrow-spectrum switch that should happen on day three and does not. Both failures are curable by the habit of re-asking, at every review, what the story said yesterday that it no longer says today.
