Delirium is the mind going short-term on top of an ill body, and dementia care is what medicine does when the mind's decline is the background rather than the event; in hospitals the two are managed badly together more often than either is managed well apart. **This chapter covers delirium (the mechanism, the predisposition-precipitation arithmetic, the missed hypoactive subtype, the 4AT, the cause-hunt, the non-pharmacological core and the honest pharmacology of the agitated minority), dementia as it presents on medical wards (behaviours that communicate, the antipsychotic evidence and its Lewy-body and stroke-and-mortality cautions, the deprescribing that follows), and the legal-ethical layer that geriatric wards live inside (capacity, restraint, the Mental Capacity Act's machinery, and the delirium-superimposed-on-dementia patient whom every system mishandles). The chapter's two sentences: 'she is just tired' is delirium's funeral epigraph, and every behavioural symptom in dementia is a sentence written in a language the ward has stopped trying to read.**

## Delirium

::: definition Delirium: mechanism, detection and the cause hunt {#def-delirium}
**The mechanism in one paragraph worth teaching: an acutely ill body produces a vulnerable-brain syndrome - the neurotransmitter story (the cholinergic deficit against the dopaminergic and inflammatory noise) and the network story (the attention-and-awareness networks of the frontoparietal and thalamic systems going offline together) explain the clinical cards: acute onset, fluctuating course, inattention and disorganised thinking or altered awareness - which is the 4AT's four items and the CAM's four features, and which is why delirium is a bedside diagnosis that no blood test replaces. The epidemiology that should scandalise: a third to a half of hospitalised older patients develop delirium (higher in ICU, hip fracture, post-cardiac-surgery), it is present in a quarter-plus at admission and missed in most of those, and it is independently associated with the outcomes that matter: longer stay, functional and cognitive decline some of which never returns, institutionalisation, death; and the two-way traffic with dementia (the demented brain deliriates ten times easier, and a delirium episode unmasking or accelerating dementia is one of medicine's genuinely unsettled prognostic questions). The arithmetic: delirium = predisposition x precipitation - the frail, demented, deaf, elderly woman deliriates on a bedpan and a hip fracture while the robust 70-year-old tolerates the same sepsis clear-headed, which is why the prevention literature (identify the high-risk admission, remove three precipitants, mobilise, orientate, hydrate, glasses-on) produces trial-grade reductions and costs almost nothing and is still the most-implemented-in-name-only bundle in hospital medicine. The detection failure modes: the hypoactive subtype (half or more of cases, the 'quiet good girl' with the pillow-over-the-face-chart, missed while the hyperactive patient gets the lorazepam that will worsen everything), the fluctuation that visits at teatime and is documented as 'alert and oriented x4' on the morning round, the baseline unknown so nobody can call the change (which is why the family's 'she is not herself' is a diagnostic instrument the ward should treat as a lab result), and the naming problem (the confusion written as 'sepsis', 'UTI', 'social', 'just tired' - the diagnosis is missed when it is not named, and the outcome improves when it is). The cause hunt done as a set piece: the hypoxia and the hypotension and the hypoglycaemia-and-hyponatraemia-and-calcium-and-uraemia-and-hepatic-encephalopathy and the infection hunt (the urine and the chest and the wound and the mouth and the C. difficile and the line, with the catheter-infection-that-is-the-catheter as the self-fulfilling classic), the drugs (the anticholinergic and opioid and benzodiazepine and steroid load and the withdrawal states - alcohol-with-the-5-day-rule-and-the-clonidine-and-baclofen-and-benzodiazepine-rebound-reactions - and the abrupt-agonist-stops including the Parkinson's meds and the baclofen whose withdrawal is its own emergency), the retention-and-impaction (the bladder-scan-and-the-digitals-the-lowest-tech-highest-yield-tests-medicine-owns), the pain (undertreated pain deliriates and the regular paracetamol-is-delirium-medicine evidence), the sensory deprivation (the glasses and the hearing aid off for the scan and never back on), the intracranial event (the subdural and the stroke and the seizure and the encephalitis when the story or the signs say, with the honest note that most delirium never needs a scan and the minority that does benefits from it being today). Management is the cause list minus the precipitants plus the non-negotiables (the fluid and the nutrition and the sleep architecture and the mobilisation and the reorientation and the one-face-she-knows), with the antipsychotics demoted to their evidence position: not preventive, not routine, short-term-low-dose-only-for-the-distress-or-danger-with-the-cardiac-and-seizure-and-Lewy-body-safety-checks-and-the-physical-health-monitoring-and-the-stop-dates-because-the-ward's-'she-is-settling'-is-a-reason-to-stop-the-drug-not-continue-it.**
:::

## Dementia on medical wards

::: definition Dementia care in general settings: behaviours and the antipsychotic question {#def-dementia-care}
**The premise: most dementia care is not neurology - it is the management of a person's remaining communication in environments designed by people who have never been confused and frightened at scale. The types are the nervous-system and psychiatry chapters' (Alzheimer's and vascular and Lewy body and frontotemporal with their distinctive delirium-drug-traps noted below); what this chapter adds is the ward grammar. The behavioural and psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD) read as sentences: the resistance-to-care that is pain or constipation or the fear of the water (the UTI and the fracture and the toothache that present as 'she is difficult' until someone examines her properly and prescribes paracetamol), the pacing that is the afternoon-sundowning-against-a-dark-corridor, the 'stealing' complaints that are confabulation and loss of object-permanence, the aggression that is a flooded threshold in a person who cannot edit a response, and the withdrawal that is depression or delirium or grief and is treatable in the ways those things are treatable. The assessment instrument that matters is the ABC or a equivalent (Antecedent-Behaviour-Consequence) and a nurse who is given the time to fill it in, because BPSD intervention research keeps showing the same answer: the environment, the staff training and the individualised activity plans work and the psychotropics mostly do not (the NICE and Cochrane dementia-symptoms evidence that the psychotropics' benefit over placebo in BPSD is small-to-none at the group level, with the real-world harms concentrated in exactly this population). The antipsychotic ledger, kept honestly: they reduce some aggression and distress short-term; they carry the cerebrovascular-event and mortality class warnings (the small absolute risk increases from the dementia-pooled trials that made every guideline say 'licensed only where there is harm to self or others, after non-drug options, with a review in weeks', the stroke risk real but numerically modest and the mortality signal roughly one-to-two-percent-per-year absolute over placebo which is a conversation she or her attorney should have in her numbers not in the leaflet's), the cholinesterase-inhibitor-and-memantine-evidence-which-is-modest-and-disease-specific-and-not-a-replacement-for-the-social-prescriptions, the benzodiazepine trap (the disinhibition and the falls and the delirium that 'calms' by worsening the disease), the anticholinergic load quietly accumulating in the bladder-and-allergy-and-antidepressant prescriptions, and the deprescribing campaigns with their genuine achievements (the US CTQ and the UK STOMP and the NICE audits and the falling antipsychotic-prescribing curves in care homes where the peer-comparison feedback was real - which is the public-health version of this chapter's bedside message). The Lewy body caution earns its own sentence wherever it belongs: neuroleptic sensitivity in DLB can be catastrophic (the severe worsening of parkinsonism and consciousness with typical and some atypical agents), so the antipsychotic-naive-demented-patient-with-the-fluctuating-cognition-and-the-visual-hallucinations-and-the-falls history is the one you check before you prescribe, and the pimavanserin-and-parkinson's-psychosis-adjacent-questions-belong-to-specialists. The care architecture: the dementia-friendly ward features with actual trial or quasi-trial support (the single-room-and-the-visibility-of-nature-and-the-dementia-nurse-specialist-and-the-Dementia-Care-Mapping-observation-tool-that-showed-what-fifty-hours-of-unstructured-tedium-a-week-looks-like, the reorientation-and-validation-not-arguing-with-the-time-and-place-she-cannot-hold, the mealtimes-as-intervention (the hand-over-hand-and-the-coloured-plates-and-the-sit-with-her evidence), the one-staff-member-one-task continuity that lets a frightened person predict the world again, and the carer as the ward's most under-used diagnostic and therapeutic resource - the spouse who knows she has always slept in the chair and the daughter who can calm the panic in forty seconds that the whole team takes an hour to fail at). Finally the interface list: driving (the licence-and-DVLA-or-equivalent duty when diagnosis impairs), the disclosure and capacity sequences (who hears the diagnosis and how, covered in the ethics chapter), the advanced-planning-early (the 'what matters to you' while she can still answer), and the outpatient follow-through that keeps the ward's gains (the community dementia team and the Admiral Nurse and the day services and the carer's own health, because dementia is a two-patient diagnosis).**
:::

## The legal and ethical machinery

::: definition Capacity, restraint and the delirium-dementia interface {#def-capacity}
**The Mental Capacity Act's four principles are ward furniture, not exam filler: capacity is decision-specific and time-specific (the same patient has capacity to refuse the bloods and lacks capacity to manage the insulin tonight); the presumption of capacity with a duty to support it (the glasses and the hearing aid and the quiet room and the daughter and the one-question-at-a-time that convert many 'lacks capacity' notes back into capacitous decisions); the unwise-conclusion-is-not-incapacity rule (the right to fail safely at 84 is real and repeatedly re-litigated at 2am); and the best-interests process with its least-restrictive-option requirement and its advance decisions and its lasting power of attorney which the clinician must actually look for before acting. The applications on this chapter's wards: the delirious patient who pulls at the drip (the restraint ladder - the repositioning and the distraction and the 1-to-1 and the familiar face and the removal of the line that does not need to be there, and the restraint that is used with a document, a stop-review, a name and a family told the same day; the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards era and its replacements with the 'she is not free to leave and cannot consent to staying' question the hospital finally has to answer in paperwork that means something); the covert-medication question (putting the antipsychotic in the juice, which the Act addresses directly and which most ethical failures in dementia care commit with good intentions); the refusal of the cannula that is really needed (the capacity assessment for that decision at that time, documented in the MCA's own grammar - understand, retain, weigh, communicate - with the supports used named); and the interface cases: the patient with early dementia who signed the care-home place-shifting paperwork with capacity or without, the fluctuating-capacity patient's advance decision, and the Aintree (Bethel) and Wye and NJ case law that keeps the withdrawing-and-withholding line in its legal place which is nowhere near where hospital folklore puts it. The delirium-superimposed-on-dementia (DSD) patient completes the chapter's ethics because she is where every system fails at once: the baseline nobody documented (so the delirium goes undiagnosed), the consent nobody revisits (because 'she never had capacity' becomes a ward slogan covering last year's capacitous refusals and this year's treatable cause), the discharge nobody plans (because 'you cannot go back there' arrives with a social-worker's shrug rather than a reablement plan the family can try), and the prognosis nobody states (because the dementia trajectory and the delirium trajectory are genuinely entangled and the honest sentence - 'this illness may cost her more function than it gives back, and here is what we will do and here is what we will not' - requires a clinician with time). The chapter's ethical position: the treatable cause gets treated even in severe dementia (the chest infection and the impacted bowel and the painful fracture are best-interests questions with obvious answers), and the not-treatable trajectory is planned early in the patient's own terms (the ceilings-of-care conversations and the CPR-in-disease-trajectory discussion which belong in a sunny afternoon clinic and not in the 3am arrest), and both of those sentences are said together, because the system that only says one of them is the system the families and the inquests keep describing.**

::: example The 82-year-old who was 'just tired' {#ex-hypoactive}
Two days post bowel resection, 'sleeping well', not attending to physio, new incontinence, refusing breakfast; the night nurse says 'she was fine yesterday, she is just tired'; obs chart normal except a rising respiratory rate the analgesia sedation explains away; family not told anything because 'she is settling'.
::: solution
**Hypoactive delirium is the delirium that dies of politeness ([[#def-delirium]], [[#def-capacity]]): the recognition - 'the 4AT done now: the acute change (yesterday-fine is the history), the fluctuating-not, the inattention she cannot count backwards or name the months, the altered awareness hiding as quietness; the rising respiratory rate on a "settling" post-op patient is the body asking the question the chart keeps answering "tired"; the workup: the hypoxia and the anastomosis-leak-sepsis and the retention-and-impaction and the sodium and the drug load (the opioid and the anticholinergic) with the imaging driven by the signs (the chest and the abdomen and the wound and the line) and the glucose and the calcium because the chart does not run itself'; the management - 'the cause list plus the non-negotiables plus the medication subtraction: the opioid rotated or reduced with regular paracetamol and the nerve-block-anaesthesia-adjacent-multimodal-ask, the catheter out, the mobilisation restarted today with a named person, the glasses and hearing aid on, the daughter called as the ward's best nurse, and the pillow-off-the-face protocol (the lights and the clocks and the orientation and the sleep protection and the day-night rhythm - all of it ordered as treatment with the same seriousness as the antibiotic)'; and the system - 'the delirium screen on the obs chart with the nurse able to trigger the review, the family told same-day in words they can use ("her mind has become confused because of her illness; here is what we are doing and here is what to expect") because the family-not-told version of this admission becomes the complaint that says "nobody noticed", which is technically true, and the mortality-and-function data that make early recognition a clinical priority rather than a documentation preference.**

::: example The 76-year-old with 'agitation' and a label {#ex-dlb}
Care-home resident labelled 'Alzheimer's with aggression' on risperidone 2 mg, twice fell, now stiff, shuffling, hallucinating animals in the hallway, fluctuating alertness; the new antipsychotic for the hallucinations is 'being discussed'; family says 'she was not like this before the home'.
::: solution
**Probable-dementia-with-Lewy-bodies-in-a-restraint-pyramid ([[#def-dementia-care]], [[#def-capacity]]): the diagnosis hiding in the drug list - 'the fluctuating cognition and the visual hallucinations and the parkinsonism and the neuroleptic-sensitivity-while-on-a-moderate-dose-antipsychotic is the DLB phenotype, and DLB is the dementia where antipsychotics can cause irreversible deterioration, which makes the "being discussed" escalation the most dangerous sentence in her notes'; the medication reversal - 'the risperidone tapered (not stopped in one move at this dose-and-duration-with-the-cholinergic-rebound-and-the-re-emergent-distress-planned-for), the anticholinergic and sedative load audited, the review with the old-record-collateral (the "was she like this before the home" answered with the timeline that the fluctuation and the falls were there before the drug or grew with it), and the DLB-specialist-referral-for-the-diagnosis-because-it-changes-everything-she-will-be-given-for-the-rest-of-her-life'; the symptoms treated their way - 'the hallucinations-in-DLB-are-often-not-distressing-while-the-dark-hallway-and-the-poor-contrast-and-the-hearing-loss-are-which-makes-the-lighting-and-the-mirror-and-the-companionship-and-the-music-the-first-line-antipsychotic; and if the psychosis is genuinely distressing after the environment work, the specialist-managed options (the cholinesterase-inhibitor evidence in DLB and the pimavanserin-adjacent-questions-some-countries-allow-not-the-typical-agents-ever)'; and the home - 'the "before the home" question heard properly: part of the decline is the drug and part may be the unmet-pain-and-boredom-and-loneliness-of-the-institution-and-the-Care-Mapping-style-audit-of-what-her-hours-actually-contain and the family's inclusion in a re-designed care plan with the named staff who know her; and the annual-review-with-a-stop-date culture the home's prescribing now needs, because the antipsychotic that started as 'aggression' has become a diagnosis she never had.**

::: example The 88-year-old with a 'urinary tract infection' every month {#ex-recurrent-uti}
Recurrent 'UTIs' treated with three antibiotic courses in eight weeks in a woman with moderate Alzheimer's; each course followed by 'clearer urine but cloudier head'; the daughter says she has been 'off her food and holding on' since the move to a new flat; on review she is faecally impacted, drinking little, and the dipstick is cloudy with a chronic asymptomatic bacteriuria.
::: solution
**The-urine-as-the-ward's-favourite-scapegoat ([[#def-delirium]], [[renal/urinary-tract]]): the read - 'the persistent bacteriuria is colonisation (the old catheter, the age, the female urethra) and the fluctuating confusion is delirium from the constipation, the dehydration, the bereavement-of-her-house and the anticholinergic burden - and each antibiotic course treated a dipstick, worsened the gut and the microbiome and the C. difficile risk, and left the causes standing; the "clearer urine but cloudier head" is the sentence that indicts the whole strategy'; the correction - 'the bladder-scan-and-impaction-exam done properly and the bowel-regimen-with-a-maintenance-laxative-and-review, the fluid plan with the measured-intake-diary-and-the-who-drinks-with-her question, the move-and-her-life reviewed (the new flat with no one passing and the day-service she is not attending - the social prescription is part of the delirium treatment), the antibiotics stopped or never started for the asymptomatic bacteriuria per the stewardship rule the renal chapter teaches, and the urine-culture-not-repeated-until-the-next-symptomatic-episode-with-a-clean-specimen-technique-the-carers-have-been-taught'; and the safety net - 'the written delirium-and-constipation-and-hydration plan the daughter holds, the "when to worry" list, and the recorded advance statement now because the trajectory needs her words while she has them - because the next crisis will be at 2am with a stranger reading a drug chart that says 'recurrent UTI' six times'.**

::: example The discharge that became a readmission {#ex-dsd}
An 81-year-old with vascular dementia, lived alone with a package, admitted 11 days with sepsis and delirium; now 'medically fit', still needing help with meals and continence she did not need before; the ward says 'she cannot go back yet'; the family say 'she was fine before' and refuse a care home; day 19 she is on a corridor trolley.
::: solution
**The delirium-superimposed-on-dementia discharge trap ([[#def-capacity]], [[#def-delirium]], [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]]): the honest frame - 'the delirium has partly cleared and the dementia and the deconditioning have taken some of the ground; "medically fit" describes the bloods, not the person, and "cannot go back yet" is a system sentence masquerading as a prognosis - the function may still improve for weeks or months and the longer the institutional stay, the less of it comes back; the plan this woman needs instead of a trolley - 'an interim bed in the right place (the intermediate-care or reablement unit, or the hospital-at-home with the intensity named), the OT-led goal-writing with the pre-admission function documented and used as the benchmark, the reablement package trial-at-home with the real hours the daughter can accept and the community-team key-worker she has now met as a person, the equipment that arrives before she does, the continence and nutrition and medication regimens written for the new capability, and the community-dementia-team and Admiral-Nurse involvement for the family's fear, which is legitimate and contagious'; and the capacity-and-process layer - 'if she lacks capacity for the residence decision, the best-interests process with the family consulted and the least-restrictive-option tested by actually trying the home package rather than asserting it cannot work, the care-home-placing process only after the rehabilitation-realistic trial, and the written review dates so "not yet" becomes "by when"; and the system accounting - 'the 19 corridor-days are a social-care failure with a medical label and this exact pattern (the delirium-with-residual-functional-loss in the person who lived alone) is why the discharge-to-assess-and-reablement pathways exist and why their absence belongs on the board's risk register next to the bed figures'.**

::: exercise Catch the withdrawal delirium {level=1}
Day 4 of admission, agitated, sweating, misidentifying the nurse as her husband, HR 112, tremor; the history was not taken because 'she is from the care home'; the home's records show nightly lorazepam 2 mg 'for many years' - omitted at admission. What is the diagnosis and the protocol?
::: solution
**Iatrogenic-withdrawal-delirium ([[#def-delirium]]): the diagnosis - 'the day-3-5 timing and the autonomic signs and the misidentification fit the benzodiazepine-withdrawal-delirium, and this is a medication-error case wearing a psychiatric costume; and the alcohol question asked the same way (the collateral histories that never arrive are how both diagnoses hide in hospitals); the protocol - 'the reinstatement with a proper reducing regimen (the benzodiazepine reloaded at the withdrawal-suppressing dose then tapered at the pace her nervous system sets, the CIWA-or-equivalent-symptom-triggered-dosing-where-used, the thiamine-and-electrolytes-and-the-dark-quiet-room, and the antipsychotic only as a careful adjunct because it lowers the seizure threshold it now risks and does not treat the withdrawal itself); and the systems fix - 'the drugs-reconciliation-at-admission-as-a-safety-process-with-the-care-home-medication-administration-record-fetched-and-a-nurse-who-telephones-instead-of-assuming and the hospital's-wide-safety-alert-because-long-term-benzodiazepine-and-baclofen-and-clonidine-and-Parkinson-med-withdrawals-are-the-repeating-avoidable-harms-on-older-people's-wards-which-is-also-a-2020s-deprescribing-chapter-irony: the drug that we now know causes these admissions is the one the same campaigns are (rightly) trying to stop prescribing to new patients'.**

::: exercise Treat the agitated delirium safely {level=2}
A 79-year-old in pain after a femoral fracture is pulling at dressings, frightened, calling for his dead mother; he is on tramadol and amitriptyline; nurse requests lorazepam 'to let him sleep'. Decide the order of everything.
::: solution
**The-agitation-with-reasons ([[#def-delirium]]): the order - 'first the pain (the fracture is the diagnosis and the undertreatment is the delirium: regular paracetamol, the nerve block or the opioid-dose-that-actually-covers-movement-with-the-bowel-and-nausea-plans attached), second the removals (the tramadol's and amitriptyline's anticholinergic-and-hyponatraemic-and-sedative contributions and the lorazepam-request-declined-because-benzodiazepines-worsen-delirium-outside-withdrawal-and-catatonia-and-the-paradoxical-disinhibition-is-coming-if-you-say-yes), third the person (the daughter at the bedside, the orientation, the chair-by-the-window, the glasses, the hearing aid, the daylight, the one-nurse-one-job, the toilet-adapted-rather-than-bedpan, the leg-moved-gently-by-physio-with-the-analgesia-first); and only then the drug - 'if he is still a danger to his repair or himself, the low-dose-antipsychotic-with-the-ECG-and-the-QT-and-the-Lewy-body-screen-and-the-seizure-and-cardiac-history-with-the-stop-date-at-days-not-weeks-and-the-same-day-review-of-whether-the-pain-plan-worked-because-it-usually-has'. The lorazepam answer said kindly: 'that is the drug that will make the operation-day confusion into a week of it - let's fix the pain and the people first and use the tablets for the failure of those two, not before them'.**

A care-home spreadsheet shows 41 residents on antipsychotics: 12 for 'behaviour', 9 with documented Parkinson's or DLB features, 27 with no review date in 12 months, 6 on valproate 'for aggression' with no licence, and the home's night staffing cut two years ago. Plan the audit meeting and the deprescribing with its risk management.
::: solution
**The-psychotropic-audit-with-hands ([[#def-dementia-care]]): the meeting - 'prescriber, pharmacist, home manager, the nurses who do the night shifts, and the residents' families invited - with the peer-comparison data (the national care-home rates and the homes-that-have-been-there) because this is the intervention the STOMP-era English data found moves prescribing: numbers, faces and a date; the stratification - 'the-urgent-stops-or-tapers (the 9 with parkinsonism or DLB features because of the neuroleptic-sensitivity risk - which is a safety-calls-today not audit-adjourns list), the valproate-6 reviewed against the unlicensed-use-and-hepatology-and-hyponatraemia-and-falls data, the no-review-date-27 given their review with doses reduced toward minimum effective and the behavioural-formulation-redone-for-each ("what was happening before the drug?" is the question that finds the pain and the constipation and the 7am-wash and the night-shock), and the 'behaviour' diagnoses re-examined for the boredom-and-loneliness-diagnosis (the activity-provision-and-the-one-to-one-hours-which-are-what-the-staffing-cut-actually-cut-which-is-why-41-residents-now-have-41-prescriptions)'; the deprescribing - 'gradual-with-a-plan-and-a-rescue-plan (the slow taper, the family briefed on what rebound distress looks like and for how long, the non-drug package in place before the dose halves, the named clinician to ring, and the re-escalation criteria written so a setback reads as information not failure); and the governance - 'the monthly report (rates by home, reviews done, distress and injury and falls tracked as the outcome so the family see what "reduced prescribing" bought, which in good programmes is fewer falls and no more aggression - the evidence that answers the manager's real question) and the staffing business case written from the audit because 41 prescriptions are a paybill for the rota the home stopped having'.**

A 68-year-old with newly diagnosed posterior-cortical atrophy (early, capacity fully intact, sharp, terrified, a retired barrister) asks you to 'write down that I am not to be kept like that' and gives you a folder. ::: solution
**The-advance-plan-with-a-lawyer-in-the-room ([[#def-capacity]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]]): the pace - 'this is the consultation where you have time twice and make it anyway: his request is a document-drafting task with grief inside it, and the folder he brought is the beginning of the conversation not the end'; and the treatable-trough caveat - the depression-that-accompanies-early-diagnosis-changes-what-people-write-down and this folder gets revisited at six months with the memory-clinic and the advocate he has already asked for'; and the medicine of the visit - 'the diagnosis-with-a-future-in-it: the research trials he is eligible for while he can consent (which he will want for the reason he gives and for the reason he does not), the driving-licence-and-DVLA-duty-discussed-today-not-deferred, the work and pension and insurance conversations signposted, and the fact that he is the only person in the room who knows what he will find unbearable - and your job is to transcribe that knowledge into a form the 2am admission will honour, which is a legitimate and testable clinical outcome for this clinic'.**

## Where this leads

The dementia subtypes and their neurology are in [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]], the psychiatric differentials in [[psychiatry/lifespan-psychiatry]], capacity law in [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], the frailty model in [[geriatrics-palliative/ageing-frailty]], and the deprescribing science in [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]].

::: history
**Delirium's history is a lost-and-found story: the Hippocratic mania and phrenitis, the seventeenth-century 'delirium' as a fever-symptom of the medical wards, and then the psychiatric colonisation of confusion (the nineteenth-century asylum literature and the mid-twentieth-century habit of calling every agitated old person 'confused senile' and treating them with the sedatives of the decade) - until the 1970s-80s rehabilitated it as a diagnosable medical syndrome: the DSM's-attention-deficit-formalisation, the Inouye and the Charltson-and-the-Yale-Others-of-the-1990s whose Boston-group created the CAM (the Confusion Assessment Method) and the longitudinal outcome studies of the 1990s-2000s (the Inouye 1993 cohort and the Davis and Bellelli follow-ups) that proved delirium predicted dementia-institutionalisation-death and, crucially, could be prevented (the HELP and the Cochrane-review-era bundles reducing cases by a third for the price of glasses and walkers and family time). The detection failures were studied as a science in themselves (the incidence of undiagnosed delirium running at half to two-thirds in the classic series, the hypoactive subtype's invisibility, the nurse-recognition-versus-doctor-recognition gap which made 'ask the nurse' a clinical aphorism). The psychopharmacology followed the evidence backwards: the antipsychotics introduced for delirium on faith, shown by trial after trial not to shorten episodes or improve outcomes (the BOLD-III and MAP-DOC delirium trials of the 2010s-2020s which found no benefit of haloperidol or risperidone over placebo on duration or severity, and the atypical's own safety tail), and the benzodiazepines' role reduced to withdrawal states and specific indications while the dexmedetomidine and melatonin and ramelteon literature matured into an interesting adjunct debate - the history's summary: the ward learned to stop shaking the patient awake and start removing the causes. Dementia's care history moves from custodial to person-cented: the institutional-warehousing critique of the 1960s-70s, the Maudsley school of Roth, Isaacs and Blessed, which named the dementias properly and proved that nursing-home 'old-age dementia' often missed treatable disease and depression, the person-centred-care-revolution-of-Tom-Kitwood's-1990s-psychotherapy-of-carework ('malignant social psychology' as the diagnosis of the institution), the Dementia-Care-Mapping-audits-of-the-2000s (the 'forty-six minutes of activity a week' figure that shamed services) and the naming of BPSD (the Cohen-Mansfield and the BEHAVE-AD instruments), and the giant policy-era (the national dementia strategies from 2009 onward and the Prime-Minister-summits and the 'dementia-friendly' everything including the supermarkets). The antipsychotic chapter within it is the most instructive: the 2004-06 FDA and MHRA class warnings (the approximately 1.6-1.7-fold mortality signal and the stroke risk from the pooled atypical-trials-in-elderly-dementia meta-analyses of Schneider and the Ball and the categorical-warnings that followed), the prescribing-drop-that-never-fully-happened (the 31 percent care-home reduction in England driven by the Commissioning Quality and Innovation payments and STOMP and CHOICES, with the Danish and Australian registries showing the same lever works which proved that public peer-comparison-of-prescribing-rates is a potent clinical intervention), the covert-medication-and-restraint-scandals-and-their-inquiries (the the Winterbourne View report of 2012, the Whorlton Range and Paterson reviews and the Transforming Care programme - with the finding, in every country's records whatever the flag, that the same system that overprescribes psychotropics understaffs the alternatives), and the DLB-neuroleptic-sensitivity-literature (the McKeith and Perry school's criteria work and the catastrophic-reaction case series - where the card that says 'it is not Alzheimer's' saves lives). The legal architecture is the final layer: the Mental Capacity Act 2005 and its Irish-and-Scottish-and-commonwealth-cousins (the best-interests-versus-substituted-judgment-arguments-still-running-in-the-CPRH-and-Crugie-and-Wye-and-James-Raeburn-case-law-adjacent-literature) and the UN-Convention's-interpretations-that-turn-'best-interests'-into-'will-and-preferences'-and-back-again which is the philosophy of this whole chapter in slow motion. The current frontier is prognosis and honesty: the delirium-to-dementia-trajectory-research, the 'what happens next' conversations that dementia trials of communication have begun to measure, and the recognition that most of the deaths of people with dementia in this century will occur in institutions whose delirium-detection-bundles we are still writing - which is why this chapter keeps saying the same two sentences: the quiet patient has the loudest syndrome, and the behaviour is the vital sign.** - [[nervous-system/dementia-neurodegeneration]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]], [[psychiatry/lifespan-psychiatry]].**
:::

::: summary
- Delirium is an acute, fluctuating inattention syndrome of a vulnerable brain; it is a bedside diagnosis (4AT/CAM), frequently missed when hypoactive, and independently predicts death, dementia progression and institutionalisation.
- Treatment is cause-removal plus the bundle (mobilise, orientate, hydrate, glasses and hearing aids, sleep, family, stop the offending drugs); antipsychotics are for severe distress or danger only, with monitoring, stop-dates and Lewy-body vigilance.
- Prevention works (multicomponent bundles roughly a third fewer cases) and costs almost nothing, which makes its non-implementation a governance fact.
- BPSD is communication: pain, constipation, infection, boredom and fear dressed as aggression; the environment and staff-training interventions beat psychotropics, whose benefits are small and whose harms (stroke, mortality, sedation-falls) are class-wide.
- Capacity is decision-specific and supported; restraint and covert medication are legal processes with names and reviews, not bedside habits.
- Delirium-superimposed-on-dementia is the system's stress test: document the baseline, treat the treatable, plan the trajectory early, and tell the family both halves of the truth together.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Score the 4AT {level=1}
Post-op day 2: not observed to be clearly drowsy, AMT 6 (date and year wrong), 'months of the year backwards' fails after two letters, no acute change documented but nurse says 'she keeps trying to get out at night'. Score it and act.
::: solution
**The-4AT ([[#def-delirium]]): the items - alertness normal (0), AMT-8-failed-or-uncertain (1), the months-backwards-accelerated-test-abnormal (2, the attention item), acute-change-or-fluctuation-plus-the-overnight-behaviour-noting-that-'not-documented' is a system gap and the nurse's collateral is the positive finding (2): total 5 which is 'possible delirium - investigate' (4 or more is the actionable threshold and 9-plus is probable). The act - 'the delirium cause-hunt protocol (the vitals-with-the-oxygen-and-the-urine-output-and-the-drug-load-and-the-bladder-scan-and-the-bowel-and-the-wound-and-the-sodium-and-glucose), the non-pharmacological bundle written as orders, the family called, and the confusion flowchart in the notes with the review-times because the 4AT's second job is serial: the score that normalises is the outcome and the score that climbs at teatime is the fluctuation the morning round missed'.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Decide about the antipsychotic {level=2}
A nursing-home resident with moderate Alzheimer's strikes a carer during washing, twice; the home has requested quetiapine 'to make care possible'. Non-drug review: pain on hip movement, constipation, washing at 7am with two staff in a hurry, no prior attempts to change the routine. Advise the prescriber.
::: solution
**The-prescription-that-is-a-staffing-problem ([[#def-dementia-care]], [[#def-capacity]]): the sequence - 'the antipsychotic is not the next step, the ABC-formulation is: the striking happens at washing (the antecedent), at the time when she is stiffest (the hip pain) and fullest (the constipation) with the routine she cannot predict or refuse (the two busy people at 7am); the interventions write themselves: the analgesia-schedule-with-pre-wash-paracetamol-and-the-laxation, the time-and-staffing-change (one familiar carer, ten unhurried minutes, the afternoon-wash-if-that-is-her-awake-window, the towel-and-the-camera-free-room-and-the-choice-offered-in-words-she-retains), and the carer-training-approach (the validation-and-distraction-and-hand-under-hand-washing-methods-the-nurse-educators-teach-where-they-have-been-taught)'; the drug conversation - 'if-after-all-that-the-striking-continues-and-injury-loomed, a short-defined-antipsychotic-trial-is-legitimate-with-her-MCA-and-capacity-process-documented-who-consents-for-her-and-what-would-make-us-stop-which-is-where-the-quetiapine-and-the-licensing-and-the-mortality-warning-and-the-review-in-4-weeks-and-the-stop-date-in-the-plan-actually-happen; and the honest framing for the home: "if the drug is being asked for so that the morning can be staffed as it is, we are treating the rota, and that is a conversation we will have, because it is the conversation she would have if she could". The follow-through - 'the pain and the bowels treated today, the routine changed this week, the incident-log-kept-by-the-home, and the review appointment with the prescriber and the family on the same day - because the alternative history of this case is the quetiapine that becomes the diagnosis, which is the last example's opening sentence'.**
:::

::: exercise Run the capacity assessment {level=2}
An 84-year-old with a hyperactive delirium in A&E refuses IV antibiotics for pneumonia and wants to go home to 'feed the birds'. Document the capacity reasoning and the lawful options if the refusal does not stand up.
::: solution
**The-assessment-in-its-own-grammar ([[#def-capacity]], [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]): the functional test for this decision now - 'does she understand the information relevant to the decision (the infection, the proposed treatment, the alternatives including the oral route and the going-home risk), retain it long enough, weigh it (the delirium is precisely a weighing-machine failure - inattention and fluctuation), and communicate the decision; and the supports first: the quiet room and the daughter and the glasses and the one-sentence-at-a-time version of 'your chest is infected and the drip is the fastest way to treat it', because capacity assessment without support is not the Act's version of the assessment'; the delirium's legal weight - 'an acute confusional state usually but not always negates capacity for complex decisions, and here the refusal is inseparable from the illness and the 'feed the birds' ideation shows the weighing is not happening: this is a decision that does not meet the test at this time, not an unwise capacitous refusal'; the lawful routes - 'treatment under best-interests for the delirious-incapacitous patient with the emergency-or-urgency analysis (the IMCA where no appropriate adult, the attorney consulted if one exists, the least restrictive option, the oral-antibiotics-and-go-home-with-district-nursing-option-actually-compared-because-that-is-the-balance-the-ward-skips) and the deprivation-of-liberty-process-if-she-would-be-held-against-her-will (the urgent-authorisation-route-with-the-named-owner-of-the-clock-and-the-review-which-is-what-the-law-requires-and-the-ward-that-just-holds-her-breaches-it); and the documentation that must exist by the end of the shift: the assessment itself in the four abilities, the supports attempted and their failure, the decision and its reasons, the family and attorney involvement, the restraint used or avoided with the reasons, and the review-time, because the point of MCA paperwork is that the next clinician inherits the reasoning and not just the conclusion'.**
:::

::: exercise Design the delirium service {level=3}
Your district general hospital: delirium not screened on general wards, present-and-missed in half of cases on review, antipsychotic use 3x the peer median, 40 percent of delirium episodes never documented, families report 'nobody explained anything', and the dementia wards have no liaison model. Build the improvement programme and its measurement plan.
::: solution
**Delirium-as-a-service ([[#def-delirium]], [[#def-dementia-care]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): the diagnosis of the system - 'this hospital treats confusion as a nursing observation and a psychiatry consult instead of a medical syndrome with a pathway, and the three numbers (the missed cases, the prescribing, the undocumented episodes) are the same failure counted twice; the prescribing half - 'the-antipsychotic-dashboard-by-ward-weekly-with-the-peer-line-and-the-pharmacist-with-authority-to-call-the-prescriber, the stop-date rule (no antipsychotic started without a review date in the last 100 admissions review - and audit the Lewy-body-check for every one), the benzodiazepine-restriction-to-withdrawal-and-the-catatonia-adjacent-exceptions-and-the-regular-paracetamol-and-bowel-and-bladder-prescribing-that-replaces-half-the-PRN-calls, and the staff-training-in-the-communication-skills-that-make-sedation-last-resort-real (the validation-and-the-one-person-one-task-and-the-dementia-nursing-skills-with-backfill-hours-because-the-skills-need-time-more-than-the-ward-has-given-them);
