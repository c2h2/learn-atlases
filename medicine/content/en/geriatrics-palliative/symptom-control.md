Symptom control is palliative medicine's pharmacology: the same opioid-receptor science that makes a morphine dose prescribable also explains its constipation, its nausea, its myoclonus and its rescue-by-rotation; and the same assessment grammar (what the symptom is doing to this person, what is driving it, what has already been tried) does more work than any single drug. **This chapter covers pain (the mechanistic read - nociceptive, bone, visceral, neuropathic - the analgesic ladder's modern form, opioid prescribing craft with conversion and rotation and adverse-effect management, the adjuvants and the interventions), breathlessness and cough and secretions, the nausea-and-vomiting pharmacology chosen by mechanism, constipation as the symptom that kills patients nobody is killing deliberately, cachexia-and-anorexia with its honest limits, and delirium and refractory symptoms including the palliative-sedation procedure done properly. Its method sentence: treat the mechanism, name the expectation, and review with a number - 'better' is not a dose; 'pain 3/10, walked to the garden, slept five hours' is.**

A 70-year-old with end-stage liver failure from cholangiocarcinoma becomes acutely agitated at 8pm: pulling at lines, terrified of the ceiling, not recognising her husband; obs stable, afebrile; on lactulose-and-rifaximin at home, last bowel motion four days ago; the family are frightened and the ward wants 'haloperidol and restrain'.
::: solution
**Delirium-in-the-last-days-with-a-liver:[[#def-other]], [[geriatrics-palliative/delirium-dementia-care]]): the reversible-pass-that-fits-the-goal - 'four days without a bowel motion in hepatic encephalopathy is a treatment target and the rectal-lavage-or-enemas-and-lactulose-given-by-whatever-route and the stopping of any constipating or sedating contributor and the bladder-scan-and-hypoglycaemia-and-infection-and-hypoxia-and-hyponatraemia-and-bleed-swift-check are all still indicated because they treat distress, not days; the environment - 'the family-in-the-room-with-a-plan (the husband told what this is and what to do: calm voice, familiar object, the light left on because darkness is worse, the reorientation he can keep doing and the staff who will keep letting him), the one-nurse-one-task continuity and the removal of the lines that do not matter tonight; and the family's-terror-medicine - 'this is the symptom that most often converts a good death into a bad memory, so the explanation (the liver-and-toxins-and-reversible-and-manageable version, honest about what is and is not fixable), the permission to stay, the offer of the soother-drug-for-him-too-if-he-cannot-sleep, and the named-doctor-present-by-10pm-are-part-of-the-prescription-sheet'.**

**Prescribing-quality-as-clinical-medicine ([[#def-pain]], [[#def-other]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): the honest framing - 'these five numbers are one number: symptoms treated by habit instead of mechanism, and the QI design follows the same logic the chapter teaches (make the right thing the default and make the wrong thing visible); ## Where this leads

The dying-phase-specific management (secretions, agitation, artificial nutrition at the end) is in [[geriatrics-palliative/end-of-life-care]], the opioid science in [[pharmacology/autonomic-drugs]] and the prescribing culture in [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]], the sedation ethics in [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]], and the respiratory procedures in [[respiratory/lung-cancer-pleura]].

::: history
**Symptom control's history is the story of medicine's most effective drugs being feared, rediscovered and finally dosed properly: the late-nineteenth-century patent-morphine and cough-mixture era and the addiction scare that followed it (the Musto and Hart histories), which made American prescribing prohibitive after the 1914 Harrison Act and gave twentieth-century pain undertreatment a long head start, the decline of medical narcotics use in Britain that St Christopher's own early caseloads documented, and Cicely Saunders' case notes recording how cancer pain went unexamined and untreated, and the modern rescue, which came from two directions at once: the 1970s-80s endogenous-opioid-receptor science (the Hughes and Kosterlitz enkephalin work and the receptor pharmacology that made rational rotation and side-effect prediction possible) and the public half (the 1980s audits that showed cancer pain routinely going untreated, the 1980s Lancet and BMJ editorials against 'the cruellest myths' of opioid fear (the Foster and Ferriman letters and the pain-society campaigns) and the later framing of pain relief as a human right, and the WHO-cancer-pain-ladder-of-1986-which-was-copied-universally-because-it-was-simple-but-misread-globally-as-an-escalation-guarantee-instead-of-a-mechanism-and-adjuvant-menu - as this chapter keeps correcting), and the essential-medicines and global-access literature - the Lancet Commission findings that a handful of high-income countries consume the bulk of the world's morphine while most of humanity never accesses essential pain medicine, and the model-list and African and Asian palliative-formulary work that this chapter's drugs depend on. The adjuvant-science-arrived-by-specialty (the gabapentin postherpetic-neuralgia trial of 1998 and the antidepressant and anticonvulsant trials that followed, catalogued agent by agent by the Moore-Derry-Wiffen Cochrane pain group with a regularity that made neuropathic analgesia one of the most audited corners of pharmacology), the antiemetic-receptor-cartography (the 5HT3-receptor work that turned the vomiting-centre physiology of Wang and Borison into ondansetron, and the NK1-antagonist and olanzapine eras of antiemetic trials that the oncology chapter borrows), the breathlessness evidence - the Abernethy palliative-oxygen trial, the bedside-fan studies and the Cochrane reviews establishing opioids for refractory dyspnoea and scepticism about oxygen in non-hypoxic symptoms - which is the rare case of a non-drug device (a battery box pointing at a face) beating a drug (the palliative-oxygen) for the indication - and the evidence being taught anyway. The dark-side-history sits here too: the opioid-epidemic's-own-palliative-adjacency (the Purdue and Sackler story and the notorious two-sentence 1980 letter that seeded 'addiction is rare in patients given opioids' - and the way the resulting American crisis made clinicians flinch from legitimate cancer-pain prescribing at the exact decade the access movement was winning abroad, a whiplash the 2022 CDC guideline revision and the palliative-care societies' critiques re-told for a general audience); the Canadian and American evidence that 'fear of opioids kills more patients than opioids do' is now quotable as a literature not an aphorism; and the palliative-sedation-and-euthanasia-adjacent-debates (the Cochrane and van der Heide and Dutch and Belgian registry literature on sedation and euthanasia, which the ethics chapter gives its own paragraph). The history's shape for a student: almost every 'obvious' rule in this chapter (the laxative with the opioid, the fan for breathlessness, the octreotide for obstruction, the rotation for neurotoxicity, the steroid for bone pain) exists because somebody audited the undertreatment, somebody ran the trial, and somebody in a hospice in London, Houston, Bangalore or Nairobi was already doing it by hand when the evidence caught up.** - [[pharmacology/autonomic-drugs]], [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]], [[public-health/global-health]].**
:::

::: summary
- Read the pain mechanism first (somatic, bone, visceral, neuropathic, total): the ladder sets the strength, the mechanism sets the drug mix and the adjuvant list.
- Opioid craft is arithmetic plus anticipation: scheduled background plus proportionate rescue doses, conversion-with-reduction then re-titration, proactive management of bowel, nausea, sedation and neurotoxicity, and renal-function-informed drug choice.
- Breathlessness is treated with opioids for refractory symptoms, a fan, oxygen only when hypoxaemic, and calm explanation; terminal secretions need repositioning, an antisecretory and the explanation more than suction.
- Antiemetics are chosen by pathway (prokinetic, chemoreceptor-trigger, antisecretory, central), and a prokinetic against a mechanical obstruction is a prescribing error with a name: malignant bowel obstruction is octreotide, antisecretory, steroid, analgesia and venting, not metoclopramide.
- Constipation never tolerates: the stimulant-based regimen starts with the first opioid dose and the refractory case gets an examination and a cause audit, not a fourth laxative.
- Cachexia has modest short-term drugs and no cure; comfort feeding is care; refractory distress is managed by proportionate palliative sedation with consent, concurrently with ordinary care.
:::

::: exercise Calculate the rotation {level=1}
A patient on oral morphine 60 mg modified-release twice daily plus 10 mg soluble breakthrough x4 daily is switched for neurotoxicity to oral oxycodone (local ratio 1:1.5 oral morphine:oxycodone total daily dose) with a 33 percent reduction for incomplete cross-tolerance. ::: solution
**The-arithmetic-shown ([[#def-pain]]): the 24-hour morphine = 120 + 40 = 160 mg; the oxycodone equivalent = 160/1.5 (i.e. 240 oral-morphine-equivalent check aside, the ratio here is written morphine:oxycodone = 1:1.5 for total daily doses, meaning oxycodone dose = 160/1.5... state your convention explicitly - this is the skill) = about 107 mg/day; the cross-tolerance reduction (33 percent) gives ~70 mg/day; the practical conversion: the modified-release oxycodone 30 mg bd (60/day background) with 10 mg soluble breakthrough (roughly a sixth of background daily dose) up to q1-2h-prn-with-a-diary; and the two exam-marks-nobody-awards-anymore-but-clinicians-need: writing the convention used (ratio applied to total daily dose, oxycodone being the more potent molecule so its number is smaller in the alternative convention - tables differ and misreading one is a real overdose mechanism) and reviewing at 24-48 hours with the rescue-dose-consumption-re-dosing-the-background.**
