The last days of life are where medicine's technical and moral vocabularies have to fit together in real time: recognizing that dying has begun, withdrawing what no longer serves, treating what distresses, telling the family the truth in sentences they can survive, and then - after death - caring for the body, the paperwork, the relatives and the staff with the same competence. **This chapter covers the recognition of the active dying phase and the reversals it demands (fluids, feeds, targets, tablets, antibiotics), the day-and-night management of the dying person's symptoms and the family's presence, the identification and certification of death and the care of the body including the cultural and religious imperatives, the coroner's and organ-donation interfaces that intersect dying in hospitals, and the bereavement-and-staff aftermath that determines whether the next family gets a good death. Its standard of care is a sentence: nobody should die frightened, in pain, alone against their wishes, or with their culture unconsulted - and every one of those four failures is a system property, measurable tonight.**

## Recognising that dying has begun

::: definition The last days: recognition and reversal {#def-recognition}
**The recognition problem: prognosis in days is harder than prognosis in months, and the last-week signs are learned on wards where nobody teaches them: the escalating exhaustion and sleeping most of the day, the loss of appetite-and-thirst-she-does-not-appear-suffer-from, the falling blood pressure and cooling extremities and the mottling that creeps up the knees, the Cheyne-Stokes-or-ataxic breathing and the new secretion-noise, the confusion or the quiet-removal-from-conversation-some-families-read-as-'she's-saying-goodbye' and some studies suggest is its own phenomenon, and the trajectory-context-that-makes-sense-of-all-this (the advanced cancer that has stopped responding, the organ-failure patient whose last admission recovered less than the previous, the frailty-dementia patient with the second aspiration or the new fracture or the non-healing pressure area - the NICE-and-Gold-Standards-Framework-style 'last days of life' recognition criteria and the prognostic-signs-literature-of-the-Lund-and-Yale-adjacent-clusters (the huddling-list of the Glasgow-Blazeby-and-Blanche-Adams-and-Hanson-and-Lynn-adjacent-end-of-life-recogniser-tools) whose honest verdict is that they are modest individually and decent as a constellation and useless unless acted upon); the surprise-question-failure-mode (most older inpatients could be said to be 'possibly dying', which is why the good services convert recognition into a named document - the last-days-of-life-care-plan with its own proforma and its own anticipatory medicines discussion - rather than a mood in the corridor). The reversals the recognition demands, each with its own conversation: fluids (the subcutaneous-saline-that-may-worsen-the-secretions-and-congestion-and-oedema-versus-the-mouthcare-that-treats-the-thirst-sensation-because-the-dying-brain's-thirst-is-mouth-not-volume-and-the-evidence-basis-of-the-hydration-at-end-of-life-debate which is genuinely contested and should be argued with the family not quoted at them), nutrition (the artificial-nutrition question in the last days answered by physiology - the body shutting down cannot use the feed and the aspiration and oedema and refeeding-harm are the risks of the intervention, and the comfort-feeding-instead-of-tubes principle extended: the spoon of ice cream is medicine at this stage - with the exception cases where starvation-delirium-or-a-reversible-cause-is-probably-driving-things, which is why recognition is a clinical task and not a formula), the deprescribing cascade in reverse (stopping the statin and the bisphosphonate and the digoxin-that-may-be-making-her-nauseated and the diabetic-insulin-reduced-toward-hypoglycaemia-avoidance-not-glycaemic-orthodoxy (the sliding-scale-not-slave-scale principle with the blood-glucose-monitoring-stopped-in-the-unmonitored-home-death) and the antihypertensives-falling-away-with-the-blood-pressure and the micro-management-of-lithium-and-levodopa-and-steroids-which-are-the-stopping-exceptions-with-their-own-rebound-and-withdrawal-pharmacology-named-in-the-polypharmacy-and-psychiatry-chapters), the antibiotics question (the 'treating the chest infection for comfort-versus-for-life' decision that families experience as 'are you giving up' and is genuinely sometimes-hope-as-a-bridge-a-visit-a-wedding and genuinely sometimes just more diarrhoea - the decision recorded with its reasoning and its stop point, because the last-weeks-oral-versus-IV-versus-neither-question is goals-of-care-medicine and the answer differs between two patients identically sick), and the monitoring-and-interventions-audit (the obs-chart-with-a-purpose question and the blood-sugars-at-4am question and the venepuncture-in-a-dying-person question, each answered 'does this change what happens to her tonight' - the reversal list is the deprescribing chapter applied at maximum gradient).**
:::

## The dying phase

::: definition The last hours: symptom and presence management {#def-dying}
**The care-bundle reality: the dying patient needs fewer interventions and more attendance - the physical management is short (repositioning for comfort and the pressure areas and the mouth-care-schedule-that-is-the-most-neglected-effective-treatment-in-dying-care (the thirst sensation, the cracked lips, the candida treated, the mouth-swabs-offered-honestly-better-than-sips-in-a-choking-patient), the eyes-closed-gently, the bladder-bowel-plan (the retention-in-a-dying-person-is-still-a-reversible-agitation-cause worth one check and the incontinence managed with pads and dignity and a changed-word-list)), and the drug management is anticipatory rather than reactive: the just-in-case-syndrome (pain-and-breathlessness-responding-to-the-opioid-already-titrated, the levomepromazine-or-equivalent for nausea-and-distress, the midazolam-for-acute-agitation-or-seizure-with-the-same-dose-titration-taught-and-confidently-executed-because-agitation-in-the-dying-is-terror-managed-as-medicine-and-never-restraint-and-the-antisecretory-for-noisy-breaths-with-the-explanation-below); the secretions question again because it is the family's worst sound: the death-rattle is painless, is not drowning, is not always fixable, and responds more to repositioning and to stopping artificial fluid than to escalating antimuscarinics - the explanation is the primary treatment and the antimuscarinic is offered with hope calibrated to its modest effect (and the honest note that some traditions and some families want the sound named out loud before it happens, which is a conversation the nurse who has time can have and the nurse with three other patients cannot, which is staffing as a place-of-death determinant); the breathing-pattern conversation (the Cheyne-Stokes pauses that make relatives ring 'is she gone' - explained as pattern-not-crisis, the apnoeic pauses that are part of dying not a resuscitation event, and the terminal-restlessness-versus-hypoactive-delirium distinction that determines whether to treat or simply to sit); the family-in-the-room as clinical material (the presence-permission ('you can touch her, she can likely hear you - the hearing is thought to be the last sense to go' which is unproven and universally comforting and true enough), the-roles-given (the hand-held-mirror-of-purpose: the moistening of lips, the music, the telling-of-stories, the sitting-in-turns-rotas-which-is-how-good-palliative-units-convert-helpless-relatives-into-the-care-team), the-children-and-the-grandchildren-included-with-honest-words-per-the-children-in-palliative-family-care-literature, and the estranged-family-and-the-'who-does-she-want'-check-done-now-because-the-dying-person's-relationships-are-not-legacy-administered-in-deference-to-whomever-answers-the-phone); and the environment-and-logistics-competence (the bed-at-home-versus-hospice-versus-ward-decision-rechecked-against-what-the-night-will-need, the 'can she die at home' question answered honestly when home is a fourth-floor-maisonette with a stairlift that breaks on Sundays, the equipment-and-medicines-actually-in-the-house-tonight-because-a-prescription-that-arrives-tomorrow-is-a-bet-the-family-should-not-have-to-take, the funeral-director-who-is-not-yet-needed-told-what-time-it-is-not-when-time-is, and the chaplaincy-and-cultural-requests-as-same-hour-arrangements (the priest, the imam, the rabbi, the rainbow-family, the blessing-or-the-silence - the spiritual care team is a rapid-response service in this phase and the ward's default of asking nobody is the failure every faith's review finds). Last, the vital-signs-of-the-room: the staff's own behaviour is an analgesic or a stressor and 'the team that sits down to explain' is the intervention families report most - the dying are monitored by the family more accurately than by the chart, and the ward that admits that gets better at everything in this definition.**
:::

## After death

::: definition Death: identification, paperwork, body and bereavement {#def-after}
**The identification of death: the clinical exam (the absent heart sounds for ten full minutes in a monitored setting or with the auscultation-device-competence-and-the-absent-breath-sounds-and-the-fixed-dilated-pupils-and-the-tanning-of-early-rigor being the NICE-2023-and-academy-of-medical-royal-colleges-criteria-era-reaffirmation which replaced the two-doctors-cardiac-respiratory-brain-stem-formalism-for-death-outside-the-neurological-context-and-ended-the-litigation-era-of-the-'60-minutes-of-asystole'-debate-with-reasoned-plain-physiology-while-the-organ-donation-interface-adds-its-own-ancillary-tests-in-some-jurisdictions-and-settings-which-are-a-transplant-medicine-topic-more-than-a-bedside-one), the timing-and-notification-duties (the relatives-arrive-before-or-after-the-identification-question-answered-with-culture-and-circumstance-not-habit-because-some-families-need-to-have-been-present-and-some-traditions-will-not-wash-the-body-until-a-relative-arrives which is why the identification is a procedure and a piece of social work simultaneously), and the honest edge cases (the unattended hospital death with no next of kin - the property-listed-and-searched-with-a-second-person and the burial-of-the-destitute-duty which is a public-health function nobody photographs); the certification layer (the Medical Certificate of Cause of Death written from the notes and the actual sequence - Part I the immediate cause down to the underlying condition with the interval line and Part II the comorbidities that contributed not the shopping-list, the 'I cannot exclude' phrasing that invites the coroner and the 'uncertain-onset' problem the death-certification-reform-debates-are-about, the medical-examiner-versus-coroner-model-visions-and-the-scandinavian-and-scottish-examples, and the doctor's legal-duty when the death is violent-unnatural-or-unattended-where-the-coroner-referral-rules-and-the-police-and-funeral-director-choreography-must-not-be-allowed-to-delay-a-family-for-days-beyond-the-legal-minimum); the organ and tissue donation interface (the death-in-hospital-with-donation-potential-sequence: the early-referral-to-the-specialist-team-because-the-window-is-minutes-to-hours and the brain-stem-and-circulatory-donation-pathways with their own physiology-protecting-ICU-phase-which-is-the-immunology-transplant-chapter's-detail-and-the-opt-out-consent-systems-now-running-in-Wales-England-Scotland-and-the-adjacent-nations with the family-knives-out-conversations-that-still-happen-when-the-request-is-timed-badly-or-the-death-was-the-morgue's-not-the-clinicians', and the tissue-donation-timeline-eyes-and-skin-and-bone-and-the-coroner's-objections-where-applicable which is a specialist conversation that generalists must at least not botch); the care of the body and its cultural medicine (the respect-protocol-whose-omissions-become-complaints: the body checked and washed with the dignity-of-the-name-and-the-curtains and the prostheses-and-jewellery-and-the-'do-we-cover-the-face' question-answered-by-religion-not-ward-custom (in Muslim and Jewish practice the washing-and-shrouding-are-the-family-and-faith-community's-obligations-and-the-clock-and-the-body-handling-requirements-are-part-of-the-clinician's-basic-competence-and-the-cooperation-with-the-faith-washers-is-the-intervention-not-the-hospital's-own-washing), the mortuary-with-a-family-room-and-a-quiet-route-and-the-photographs-some-families-ask-for-with-their-child-dead-which-is-what-the-perinatal-bereavement-protocols-taught-everyone, and the post-mortem-examination-requests-explained-in-plain-English-legal-mechanics), and the bereavement-and-aftermath-medicine (the 'at risk of complicated grief' identification in the family - the sudden-and-violent-and-stigmatised-and-bereavement-by-suicide-and-by-child-and-the-carer-spouses-who-were-the-secret-family and the-mention-of-the-bereavement-services-and-General-Practitioner-and-Macmillan-and-Samaritans-and-faith-community-support-in-the-discharge-papers-that-get-read-or-not-and-the-nurse's-phone-call-in-week-two-which-the-evidence-says-is-the-highest-value-bit, and the staff-debrief-culture-that-this-specialty-invented-because-attending-four-deaths-a-week-without-language-becomes-a-workforce-hazard-with-a-calling-in-ten-years and the mortality-and-morbid-mortality-reviews-that-should-ask-'how-was-here-and-her-family-treated'-as-seriously-as-'was-the-right-antibiotic-given'-because the audit of the last week is the audit a hospital should be proudest of failing).**

::: example The admission that was a homecoming {#ex-lastdays}
A 74-year-old with end-stage heart failure is admitted 'for optimization'; she sleeps all day, eats nothing, BP 82/50, creatinine rising, two family crises already on the ward; the cardiology team wants 'another echo before we decide'; the daughter says 'my mother keeps asking if we are waiting for something'.
::: solution
**Naming-the-last-days-then-clearing-the-table ([[#def-recognition]], [[#def-dying]]): the recognition-and-honesty-move - 'the daughter's sentence is the diagnosis: the patient has known for weeks what the team has not said, and 'optimization' of an ejection fraction nobody can optimize is the treatment plan's euphemism; the next 24 hours should contain a consultant-led conversation with both of them (the trajectory described, the 'are we there - dying' question answered in the same register it was asked, the decision about what the hospital can still do (which is symptom control and her daughter's chair, not inotropes unless the palliative-cardiology team is comfortable that they serve a named goal like getting home)) and the echo is not the next action; the reversal list performed visibly - 'the tablets-stop-list-explained-one-by-one (the statin, the aspirin if there is no recent stent, the digoxin-that-may-be-the-anorexia, the diuretic reduced toward comfort and reviewed against her breathlessness-not-her-weight) and the glucose-and-blood-pressure-targets-retired and the obs-frequency-cut-to-what-changes-care-tonight and the 'what if she has a chest infection next week' conversation with the antibiotics-honest-both-ways; the logistics as treatment - 'the discharge-to-die-at-home-is-not-a-discharge-it-is-a-transfer-of-care-with-a-package: the district nurse with the syringe-driver-and-the-anticipatory-medicines-and-the-24-hour-number, the hospital bed in the front room with the oxygen-if-hypoxic-and-the-commode-route-lit, the hospice-at-home-visits-booked, the funeral-and-financial-and-grandchildren's-school-run-logistics-which-are-really-clinical-medicines-at-this-point, and the promise kept that the ambulance crew will be able to read the plan when she rings; and the daughter's question re-asked in four weeks of this chapter's language: 'what did you need from us that you didn't get' - because that question in the family meeting while she can still answer is the last clinical act available.'**

::: example The 3am ward death, reviewed {#ex-nightdeath}
An 86-year-old nursing-home resident with advanced dementia dies alone in a side-room of an acute surgical ward at 03:40, sedated on PRN lorazepam given twice for agitation, restraint belt in place 'for her safety'; family arrived at 05:20; the notes say 'settled, peaceful'; the son is a police officer and asks for the records.
::: solution
**The-serious-incident-is-in-the-chart-already ([[#def-after]], [[#def-dying]], [[geriatrics-palliative/delirium-dementia-care]]): the review's findings in order - 'the death was probable and the dying was unrecognised (no last-days plan, no anticipatory medicines, no preferred-place-of-care conversation ever documented), which converted a predictable trajectory into an emergency; the management that night was the opposite of dying-care (the PRN lorazepam for undifferentiated agitation without a cause-hunt - the bladder and bowel and pain and hypoxia - repeated escalation without medical review, and the restraint belt which is never a comfort measure and is a legal event for a person who could not consent); and the documentation is the worst part: 'settled, peaceful' when no one examined her after the second dose and the family were telephoned late is the record that converts a bad death into a claim and, worse, is probably false'; the process the son will trigger - 'the duty-of-candour-in-motion (the coroner-notification-rules-check, the records-secured-uncorrected-with-an-audit-trail-note, the family-meeting-with-the-consultant-and-the-nurse-in-charge-and-the-risk-team-with-their-actual-questions-answered-not-the-lawyer's-language, and the serious incident review whose terms of reference include the questions the son is asking rather than the ones the trust prefers); the fixes that are not 'more training' - 'the dying-person's-observations-that-include-the-restraint-and-PRN-psychotropic-audit-triggers, the nursing-home-liaison-and-last-days-flagging-so-a-resident's-final-admission-is-not-a-surgical-ward-surprise, the 'could-die-today' palliative-reflex-and-the-anticipatory-prescribing-in-homes-and-wards-eagerly, the post-falls-and-post-agitation review that reaches all the way to 'should she have been here at all', and the staff-debrief-of-03:40-because-the-night-team-will-remember-this-death-longer-than-anyone-in-this-review-and-a-debrief-is-how-a-service-keeps-the-people-who-do-this-work'; and the family's-own-aftermath - 'her-wishes-reconstructed-from-the-record-and-the-daughter-who-lived-near-and-the-hymn-she-sang-in-the-home-and-the-body-washed-with-the-son-present-when-he-asked-because-the-son's-first-question-after-the-records-was-'did-she-suffer'-and-the-only-honest-answer-coming-from-a-service-that-will-be-better-because-he-asked'.**

::: example The daughter who was 'the carer' {#ex-bereaved}
Six months after her husband's death in your care, a 71-year-old woman is admitted with falls and 'just tired'; she has not been to the GP since; the housekeeper says she has lost 6 kg and stopped answering the phone; on the ward she refuses to talk about him and apologises for being 'a nuisance'.

**The-bereavement-clinic-that-starts-as-a-medical-admission ([[#def-after]]): the read - 'this is a bereavement presentation with a falls-and-malnutrition-skin: the widowhood-widow-danger-studies are blunt - early mortality-and-morbidity climbs after a spouse's death, particularly in the surviving partner who was the carer, because the person whose job was keeping someone alive has lost the job, the schedule and the witness; the "nuisance" apology is the risk-flag; the inpatient jobs - 'treat the reversible medical things and ask the non-medical questions the last 48 hours exist for: what has happened since he died, who is here, is there food in the house, is she sleeping, did she ever get the two-week GP appointment the discharge letter promised, and the depression-versus-grief-differential-with-honest-boundaries (the persistent self-hatred and the two-years-plus patterns belong to treatment and the first months belong to mourning which does not mean nobody measures it)', the structures that answer falls and weight-loss - 'the-bereavement-services-named-as-a-prescription (the Macmillan-or-age-UK-or-community-bereavement-project with a warm handover and the carer-support-group-where-she-is-a-person-again and the GP letter that says as much; and the practical medicine the district nurse delivers (the weighing, the pills-box, the lift-to-the-memorial-service-which-is-treatment)), and the permission - 'the one conversation where the ward tells her what his last hours were like - if she wants to know and no one has said it plainly - because the families of hospital deaths carry unfinished-questions for years (the "was he scared, did you do everything, why did it happen at that minute" list) and the good services offer the follow-up meeting with the doctor six weeks on which is when most of these questions actually arrive'. Finally the staff mirror: the nurse who cared for him asks about her - because bereavement-adjacent-grief-in-staff-who-felt-they-failed-then-died-anyway is the chapter's last subject and the debrief culture is partly for exactly this moment.'**

::: exercise Teach the family the dying sounds {level=1}
The husband of a patient drifting in and out asks, in the corridor, 'is she suffocating - that noise'. Give the sentences you would actually say.
::: solution
**The-rattle-conversation-in-sixty-seconds ([[#def-dying]]): the three sentences - 'first the fact in her body's language: "that sound is saliva collecting in the back of her throat because she is far too relaxed and tired to swallow it - it does not hurt her and it is not choking; she is not aware of it the way you are." Second the action: "we can change how she is lying, which often helps, and there is a medicine that dries it up some of the time - it is not perfect, but we will use it if the sound is worrying you more than it is worrying her." Third the permission and the truth: "you are allowed to sit with her, talk to her, hold her hand - hearing is thought to be one of the last senses to go, and even if that is folklore, it is the kind that does no harm. If you want us to give her something for restlessness, say so; you will never be bothering us."' The craft marks: no jargon, no false certainty, the sound named, the family's role included, and the offer made before the 2am panic-ring - which is the same conversation at ten times the volume.**

Write the protocol your acute surgical ward will use for a patient recognised as dying overnight: staffing, medicines, escalation, family, faith, documentation, and who calls whom at 03:00.

**The-protocol-that-survives-3am ([[#def-dying]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]]): staffing-and-competence - 'the night nurse's-delegated-competence-documented (the dying-persons' observations-and-interventions-and-first-doses-without-waiting-for-a-doctor under a signed protocol), the second-nurse-or-hcca-for-the-two-person-tasks-because-a-dying-patient-should-not-cost-a-118-call, and the junior-doctor-who-knows-this-is-their-most-important-call-not-theirs-most-anxious; medicines - 'the anticipatory set on the ward's dying-patient drug chart by default (the analgesic, antiemetic/sedative, antisecretory, midazolam), the syringe-driver-and-fine-bore-kit-in-the-fridge-not-in-pharmacy-stocks-at-03:00 and the out-of-hours-pharmacist-number-that-answers-because-compatibility-questions-are-real; escalation - 'the "comfort-first" single-page plan (the emergency-treatment-and-resuscitation-plan's-care-below-medical-recommendations-half, the ceiling-stated-not-gold-plated, the obs-frequency-reduced-with-reasons, the bloods-stopped), and the one-line rule that a dying person in distress is a doctor-called-now event never a wait-and-see; family and faith - 'the 'could die tonight' phone-call-scripted-conversation-and-the-visit-any-time-permission-and-the-side-room-not-the-corridor-and-the-tea-and-the-chairs-written-into-the-plan as nursing orders, and the faith-leader numbers on the wall with the 'asked about by 18:00' rule so nothing waits for the morning; documentation - 'the 'last hours' nursing narrative with the medicines-given-and-effect-on-the-family-in-the-room-recorded-and-the-'family-was-present-and-understood'/'not-yet' honesty-and-the-doctor-attended-by-name; and the post-death choreography (the identification-competence-per-the-national-criteria and the quiet-room and the body-care-preferences-followed-and-the-coroner-question-asked-early-and-the-bereavement-leaflets-here-not-posted). The protocol's test-question, printed at the top: 'if this were your mother at 3am, what would you want this ward to already have done'.**

::: exercise Defend the place-of-death record {level=3}
Your trust's data: 58 percent of over-85s die in hospital; 4 percent have a recorded preferred place of care; deaths are concentrated in side-rooms and at night; restraint and psychotropic use in the last week is triple the peer unit. The board calls this 'a community-care failure'. Write the memo that makes it a clinical-quality programme.
::: solution
**Place-of-death-as-a-clinical-indicator ([[#def-after]], [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]): the reframe with the board's own numbers - 'the community is a supply-chain for the place-of-death outcome (the district nurse and GP out-of-hours and hospice-at-home and equipment-and-medicines-delivery), but the recorded-preference figure of 4 percent is not a community failure - it is a clinical-record failure happening on our wards and in our clinics months before the death, and the triple restraint rate is not community-care at all: that is our nursing, our staffing and our delirium-blindness, reported in the least-protected people in the building'; the programme's five workstreams with named clinical owners - 'the preference-and-plan-4-percent-to-60-percent-workstream (the 'what matters to you' recorded in the EPR and visible to out-of-hours and ambulance-triage-crew with the last-years-of-life-review-incentive-used-properly plus the 'is she dying' flag on our own ED and admission-charts-because-a-preference-documented-at-the-fracture-admission-changes-the-discharge-destination); the recognition-and-reversal-workstream (the 48-hour-recognition-audit-and-the-medication-review-on-dying-recognition-protocol-which-is-this-chapter's-definition-of-good-medicine-audit); the side-room-at-night-workstream (the 'dying alone is a quality indicator' policy: the last-hours family-presence-permission-with-the-night-staff-rostered-for-it-because-the-room-count-is-dignity-maths-and-the-night-obs-in-a-dying-person-should-be-comfort-checks-not-a-ritual); the restraint-and-psychotropic-workstream (the audit with the peer line published weekly, the delirium-cause-hunt-before-sedation rule, the Transforming-Care-style-reduction-target-for-deaths-and-near-deaths, and the Lewy-body-card-for-every-prescriber-who-orders-PRN-haloperidol); and the aftermath-workstream (the death-audit-of-the-previous-quarter-with-family-interviews-reported-to-the-board-with-one-case-read-aloud-because-place-of-death-statistics-only-move-when-executives-have-heard-the-3am-version-in-a-voice). The closing paragraph for the paper: 'a society's death statistics are a clinical-quality output, and a hospital that externalises them to "the community" has admitted it treats the death rather than the dying - the four-percent number is our staph-rate equivalent, and we will move it the same way: measurement, ownership, and shame applied in the right direction'.**

## Where this leads

The symptom pharmacology of the dying phase is in [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]], the service and early-referral case in [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]], capacity and best-interests at the end in [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]] and [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]], and donation and transplantation in [[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]].

::: history
**The history of dying in institutions is this chapter's pathology report: the nineteenth-century default of dying at home - with its Victorian mourning industry and its post-mortem photography, itself a kind of palliative technology - and the charities that appeared when home dying meant dying in a workhouse, the twentieth-century migration of death to hospital (the 1940s-1970s western shift that the sociologists of 'awareness contexts' and the denial-of-death literature tried to name, and that the British hospital-board era of corridor beds and rationed visiting-hours institutionalised). The audit era answered it: the US Institute of Medicine reports, the 2014 English National Audit of the Last Weeks of Life, and the Australian and Canadian sibling reviews, whose findings were so consistent across four countries they could be one document with the flags changed - pain untreated, recognition absent, families uninformed, dignity an afterthought; the response machinery that grew from the audits (the Gold Standards Framework and the End-of-Life-Care-Nets and the 100000-Better-Deaths-and-the-Ambulatory-Sensitive-Conditions-adjacent-ambulance-and-place-of-death-programmes and the 'preferred place of care recorded' indicators which are this chapter's public-health-instrumentarium, the Australian-Ending-Life-Every-Dying-Person-matters-programme's national indicators and the Scottish-Ambulatory-Care-and-Just-Care-at-the-End-review-and-the-World-Health-Organization's-quality-of-dying-resolution-which-made-place-and-manner-of-death-a-global-health-metric); the recognition science of the last week - the prognostic tools built in hospice and care-home cohorts (the 'death is possible' prompts and the various last-days indicator scores) and the sober accuracy reviews that found no single tool strong enough for a standalone verdict but a decent constellation when a clinician reads it - alongside the intensive-care palliation commissions' shared conclusion that recognition bundles beat optimism; the care-of-the-body-and-beyond (the mortuary-science-and-the-return-of-'handling-the-dead-with-respect'-to-a-taught-competence-and-the-coroner-and-medical-examiner-systems-history-from-the-medieval-deodand-to-the-modern-referral-rules-and-the-2019-2023-English-death-certification-reform-arguments-about-who-should-sign-what-and-the-Scottish-medical-examiner-rollout-as-the-quiet-revolution-in-how-a-death-gets-explained-without-a-court), and and the organ-donation era, whose ethics debates about whether donation-consideration distorts death-recognition medicine answers unanimously that it does not, while the opt-out legislative wave (Wales 2015, England 2020, Scotland 2021, with the Spanish and Swiss models studied en route) made not-botching the referral conversation a public duty; and the current frontier which is the same argument this whole book keeps having, conducted around a deathbed: the medical-assistance-in-dying laws (the Oregon Death with Dignity Act of 1997 and the widening American state tapestry that followed and the Canadian MAID legislation and the disability-rights protests around it, which belong to the ethics chapter's analysis) whose palliative-care-reception-history-matters-clinically-because-the-specialty's-own-evidence (the Oregon and Washington annual reports that found loss of autonomy and fear of the future, rather than pain itself, as the recurring motives, and the terminal-sedation-frequency debates that both camps cite) is cited by both camps and belongs to neither as property. The chapter's through-line is simpler than the debates: every country that audited its deaths found the same unfed families, the same undertreated pain, the same corridor, and the fixes were the ones Cicely Saunders named - recognition, symptom science, the family as unit, and staff who are helped to care - and the reason this chapter is last in the clinical sequence is that it is where all the other chapters' claims about person-centred care are finally invoice-checked.** - [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]], [[medical-ethics-law/end-of-life-ethics]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
:::

::: summary
- Recognising the last days is a clinical act with a checklist behind it: trajectory, sleep, intake, circulation and breathing patterns, read together and converted into a named plan, because days-prognosis is never certain and that is an argument for planning, not for deferring.
- Recognition triggers reversal: fluids and feeds and targets and most tablets reconsidered one by one, antibiotics argued as a goal-decision, monitoring kept only where it changes tonight.
- The dying phase is treated with presence more than procedures: mouth care, repositioning, anticipatory medicines, an antimuscarinic plus an explanation for the rattle, and roles given to the family.
- Identification of death is a taught procedure; certification is a clinical document written from the record; coroner and donation pathways have clocks that medicine must respect.
- Care of the body is cultural medicine: the washing, timing and handling requirements of faith families are clinical-competence-level knowledge, and the quiet route and the family room are infrastructure, not luxury.
- The aftermath is part of the death: candour with the family, the records secured, the staff debriefed, and the review asking 'how was she treated' in the same voice as 'was she treated right'.
:::

::: exercise Write the last-days plan {level=1}
On the ward round you judge a patient with metastatic breast cancer to be in the last days. List the plan's rows: medicines to stop, medicines to start, monitoring, family, faith, logistics.
::: solution
**The-plan-on-one-page ([[#def-recognition]], [[#def-dying]]): stop-list-with-reasons (the statin, the bisphosphonate, the non-essential antihypertensives, the laxative-reviewed-against-the-opioid-still-running, the insulin-reduced-toward-no-hypo-targets, the tablets-continued-being-the-ones-treating-symptoms-she-actually-has (the opioid, the antiemetic, the anticonvulsant-for-her-brain-mets-with-the-steroid-continued-then-reviewed)); start-list (the anticipatory subcutaneous set - analgesic, antiemetic/sedative-for-distress, antisecretory, midazolam-for-agitation-or-seizures - prescribed as doses and routes, held where nurses can reach them at 3am, and the mouthcare-and-skin-and-positioning-regimen-written-as-nursing-orders); monitoring (the obs frequency matched to the plan and the blood-tests-stopped-with-the-stop-list and the one-line-prognosis-with-review-time ('dying - review 12h or sooner if distress') and the escalation-plan-that-is-not-CPR-by-default-written-as-an-emergency-treatment-and-resuscitation-plan-legal-where-applicable); family (the 'she may die today/today-tonight' told in the room with the words the family will use with their own children, the visiting-flexibility-and-overnight-presence-permission, the roles-offered (lips, music, the rota of sitting), the phone-numbers-on-paper); faith (the chaplain-and-own-minister-offered-by-name-not-assumed-against-religion-field-ticked-years-ago, the ritual-requests-with-a-time-clock, the washing-preferences-recorded-now-because-they-will-be-asked-in-an-hour-when-everyone-has-forgotten-everything); and logistics (the bed-position-and-the-window-and-the-quiet-ward-ask, the funeral-director-and-bereavement-leave-information-given-as-if-it-mattered-because-it-does, and the discharge-home-plan-if-she-dared-want-home-with-the-package-named). One page, one owner, copied to the notes, the GP and the family's phone camera - which is how a good death gets staffed.**

::: exercise Answer the hydration question {level=2}
The family of a dying woman insist on 'a drip, she is thirsty': she has secretions and ankle oedema and is semi-conscious. Convince them properly or find the compromise.
::: solution
**The-thirst-is-in-the-mouth ([[#def-recognition]]): the physiology in their language - 'at the end the body cannot use the water: a drip adds to the secretions she is already noising through and the swellings she cannot complain about, while the feeling of thirst lives in the dry mouth, which we can treat - often better - with the swabs and the spray and the sponge and the ice-chip-if-safe that the hospice movement has spent forty years perfecting (and here you demonstrate, not describe: the mouth-care is done in front of them so they can see the technology of it); the honest both-ways - 'subcutaneous fluid sometimes helps a reversible component (the delirium-adjacent-dehydration and the drug-clearance-nausea and the days-when-she-seems-to-wake-after-a-bag) which is why the trial-with-review-rule version exists: a defined small infusion, a named thing we are watching for, and stopping if it is not buying what it appears to - the plan is a question asked with the family's help, not a refusal delivered to them; and the grief under the request - 'the drip is often the last thing they feel able to do, which is why the alternative must be an action (the mouth-care-rotas-and-'you-can-do-this'-handover) and why 'we are not doing anything' is never true in this room and should be said out loud with the list of what we are doing instead'. The compromise answer: a time-limited, goal-named trial of fluids with explicit stopping criteria, or the mouth-care-with-compassion-and-no-fluids version when the secretions are already telling you which way it will go; both are defensible, the unspoken refusal is not'.**

::: exercise Handle the donation conversation {level=2}
A 34-year-old has catastrophic intracerebral haemorrhage, is progressing toward brain stem death testing, and her parents (next of kin) say 'she was in a car crash, she will not be a donor, please stop asking'. The transplant coordinator has rung twice. What happens now?
::: solution
**Donation-medicine-in-a-profoundly-serious-conversation ([[#def-after]], [[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]], [[nervous-system/stroke]]): the immediate rules - 'the request for donation is made by trained specialist nurses, at the right time and place, after the prognosis is understood - not twice by telephone pressure and never in the resus-room first minutes, and the "stop asking" sentence is answered once, properly: "I will stop; if at some point you want to know what being a donor would mean, I can have someone explain it once, and the answer stays no as long as you say no" - because the evidence on family refusal (the organ-donation literature on request timing and dedicated-requester-models and the well-documented fact that a poorly-timed or entitled request hardens refusal even in donor-willing families) is about doing it right, not about pushing'; the parallel duties that continue regardless - 'the brain-stem-death-testing-and-donation-are-separate-conversations-and-separate-decisions (the donation question does not touch the testing question, and the parents need that wall stated explicitly because the fear that doctors-will-stop-looking-to-declare-her-dead-for-her-organs is the fear that kills donation as much as refusal does and is answered with transparency about the testing-team's-independence and the lawful framework she may or may not be covered by (the opt-out-consent-architecture and the family-consultation-versus-family-veto distinction which differs by nation and must be stated accurately, and her own recorded views checked - the donor register and her own words to friends which the parents may not know)'; the path if the answer stays no - 'which is legitimate and honoured, full stop - the end-of-life care proceeds to the parents' timeline, the coordinator told, the potential donors other organs never-referenced-again-in-front-of-the-family-because-the-dignity-of-the-refusal-is-part-of-what-wins-the-next-family'; and the path if they ask again - 'which a good number of grieving families do within the week - and this is why the specialist nurses are patient and why the ICU keeps the physiological-support-competence (the donation-after-circulatory-death-pathways-where-the-circulation-arrest-window-is-minutes-not-hours-which-is-why-"we-stop-asking"-must-never-become-"we-stop-being-ready-to-answer").**

::: exercise Audit your unit's last week {level=3}
Design a death-audit for your ward: the sample, the data sources, the questions, the ethics of reading other people's dying, and the fixes worth reporting to the board.
::: solution
**The-mortality-and-dignity-audit ([[#def-after]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[geriatrics-palliative/palliative-care]]): the sample and sources - 'every inpatient death in a period the team can genuinely review (a quarter at unit level) with the record, the nursing and palliative notes, the GP and care-home documents, the medication-chart audit trail and - the part that makes this different from a mortality meeting - the family-approached-for-interview-by-a-separate-person-or-an-independent-cancer-and-bereavement-worker because the record contains the staff's version and the family carries the rest;
