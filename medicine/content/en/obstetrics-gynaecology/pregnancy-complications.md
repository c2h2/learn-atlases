Pregnancy's complications arrive in predictable families - hypertensive, placental, growth-related, preterm, and the medical-and-surgical emergencies that happen to pregnant women as well as because of pregnancy - and each has a protocol whose first word is usually magnesium, steroids, admit, or deliver, and whose last word is a consultant conversation. **The chapter's logic is that the fetus makes common conditions dangerous and pregnancy makes uncommon conditions present atypically: a normal blood pressure does not exclude pre-eclampsia, a normal CTG does not exclude the growth-restricted fetus, and the appendix moves. Master the hypertensive protocols, the growth-versus-timing arithmetic, the preterm-birth bundle, and the rule that undifferentiated collapse in a pregnant woman is haemorrhage or thromboembolism until disproved.**

## The hypertensive diseases

::: definition Pre-eclampsia, eclampsia and the hypertensive spectrum {#def-hypertension}
**Classification first: chronic hypertension (pre-existing or before 20 weeks), gestational hypertension (new hypertension after 20 weeks without proteinuria or organ features), pre-eclampsia (new hypertension after 20 weeks plus proteinuria or maternal-organ involvement - and note proteinuria is no longer required: renal, hepatic, neurological, haematological or uteroplacental dysfunction features define it), superimposed pre-eclampsia on chronic hypertension, eclampsia (seizures), and HELLP (haemolysis - rising LDH and bilirubin, falling haptoglobin and fibrinogen; elevated liver enzymes; low platelets) which can present with epigastric pain and normal blood pressure and is diagnosed by a blood count and film and liver and renal and coagulation screen interpreted with the physiological baselines from the antenatal chapter. Pathophysiology in one line: abnormal placentation with maternal endothelial dysfunction, vasoospasm and capillary leak explains the hypertension, the proteinuria, the oedema and the complications - cerebral (headache, visual disturbance, seizures, posterior reversible encephalopathy), hepatic (capsule stretch, rupture), renal, haematological and placental (growth restriction, abruption, stillbirth). Assessment: blood pressure measured properly and quantified repeatedly, urinalysis plus protein-creatinine ratio, bloods (FBC, urea and creatinine, liver transaminases, urate), fetal assessment with growth and Dopplers, and the daily 'severe features' questions - headache, visual symptoms, epigastric or right-upper-quadrant pain, breathlessness, oliguria - which are the symptoms that convert surveillance to delivery. Management by protocol: mild-to-moderate hypertension in pregnancy treated with labetalol (first-line in most guidelines), modified-release nifedipine or methyldopa with target blood pressures around the 130s-140s/80s rather than normalisation (placental perfusion and the small-trial evidence for tighter targets now settling the debate toward treatment at 140/90 per recent trials); severe hypertension as an emergency treated within minutes-to-an-hour with IV labetalol, or hydralazine, or oral nifedipide per the acute-protocol charts - because the maternal-stroke risk is immediate and the fetus tolerates the pressure fall better than the mother tolerates the spike; pre-eclampsia itself only cured by delivery, so the real decisions are steroid lung-maturity timing, magnesium sulfate for eclampsia treatment and for prophylaxis in severe disease (the loading-and-4-hourly-maintenance protocol with the calcium-gluconate antidote and the reflexes-and-respirations-and-urine-output monitoring), antenatal corticosteroids for 24-34(36) weeks, and the delivery-timing table that balances prematurity against the maternal disease (at 34-37 weeks with severe features or uncontrollable blood pressure or organ dysfunction, deliver; earlier with the same features plus fetal compromise, deliver after stabilising; expectantly only with mild disease and inpatient-or-day-unit surveillance and daily fetal-and-maternal checks). The postpartum tail people forget: blood pressure peaks again in days 3-5, pre-eclampsia can start after a normal delivery (the readmission headache), magnesium continues 24 hours postpartum, antihypertensives need breastfeeding-compatible choices (labetalol and nifedipine compatible, atenolol less so), and the long-term truth to tell her at discharge: pre-eclampsia raises her lifetime cardiovascular risk meaningfully - blood pressure check yearly, weight, and the next-pregnancy aspirin plan that this one paid for - [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[renal/filtration]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]].**
:::

## Placenta, growth and timing

::: definition Fetal growth restriction, abruption and placenta praevia {#def-growth}
**Small does not equal growth-restricted: the small-for-gestational-age fetus may be constitutionally healthy (the biggest determinant is maternal size and parity - customised charts try to capture this and remain contested) or placenta-failing; the clinically important distinction is made with serial growth, amniotic fluid (oligohydramnios travels with placental failure), umbilical-artery Dopplers and the CTG or biophysical profile - and the Doppler ladder tells the delivery-timing story: raised pulsatility index, then absent end-diastolic flow, then reversed end-diastolic flow, each step worse and shorter-dated, with the middle-cerebral-arterial brain-sparing ratio, the ductus-venosus waveform and nuchal translucency adding information for the tertiary team deciding whether a 29-weeker is better out today or in for three more monitored days. Management belongs to a fetal-medicine unit: steroids, magnesium for neuroprotection when delivering before about 30-32 weeks, inpatient-versus-day-unit surveillance by severity, and delivery timing that follows the trial evidence (the larger frameworks from the growth-restriction trials give the honest shape: at 36-37 weeks for early-onset with normal Dopplers and reassuring surveillance, 34-36 for absent end-diastolic flow, 30-34 or immediate for reversed flow or abnormal ductal or CTG events - individualised, not table-worshipped). Abruption (painful bleeding with a tense uterus, the clinical diagnosis, sometimes concealed - the falling haemoglobin and the DIC picture that turns fatal in the posterior uterus) ranges from the observation-and-steroids admission to the category-one caesarean with massive-transfusion protocol and the anaesthetic conversation about the coagulopathy; recurrence risk is real so the next pregnancy's growth surveillance starts early. Praevia (placenta over the os - painless bleeding, transvaginal scan mapping at the anomaly scan with a re-look at 32 weeks because most low-lying placentas resolve) becomes dangerous with accreta-spectrum disease - the previous-caesarean-plus-anterior-placenta combination that produces placenta accreta, increta and percreta, whose management is a planned early delivery (often 34-36 weeks) in a centre with interventional radiology, cell salvage, blood-bank pre-alerts, and a consultant obstetric-and-anaesthetic team that has run the simulation, because the morbidly adherent placenta is one of the two or three conditions where British maternal mortality concentrates - blood loss, hysterectomy, and the ICU night that a good pathway exists to make boring - [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]], [[paediatrics/neurodevelopment]].**
:::

## Preterm birth and the emergencies

::: definition Preterm birth, prelabour rupture of membranes and maternal emergencies {#def-preterm}
**Preterm birth - labour or cervical change before 37 weeks, with periviable decisions from 22-24 weeks - runs on four interventions: antenatal corticosteroids (betamethasone or dexamethasone for 24-34 weeks, and the late-preterm 34-36-week dose where local policy adopts the trial evidence, remembering the neonatal-hypoglycaemia trade-off), tocolysis used briefly and deliberately (nifedipine or atosiban where used, not to delay the inevitable but to buy the steroid window and the transfer; contraindicated in chorioamnionitis and limited in severe pre-eclampsia or abruption), magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection when birth before 30-32 weeks looks imminent, and transfer to a unit whose neonatal survival and outcomes match the gestation - because the evidence for centralised neonatal care is as strong as anything in perinatal medicine and the perimortem transfer of an unscreened mother is the failure the reports repeat. Prevention is partial: cervical-length screening with vaginal progesterone or history-or-length-indicated cerclage for the short cervix or the classic painless second-trimester loss pattern, and cerclage decisions made with transvaginal ultrasound and a written plan because rescue cerclage in a contracting, infected uterus is how iatrogenic extreme prematurity happens. Preterm prelabour rupture of membranes: the diagnosis is sterile-speculum history and pooling and ferning or IGFBP1 rather than a digital examination, the management depends on gestation - from 34-36 weeks (local cut-offs vary) deliver after steroids; before that, the expectant pathway with oral erythromycin (the ORACLE evidence), no routine repeat antibiotics, temperature-and-pulse-and-bloods-and-CRP daily, the single dose of steroids, group-B-strep status thinking, and the honest counselling that the clock is against her - chorioamnionitis and abruption and cord prolapse are the reasons the plan changes at night. Maternal emergencies in pregnancy follow surgical rules with two patients: appendicitis (the displaced pain and the normal white count that rises anyway, and imaging that starts with ultrasound or MRI rather than refusing to look), cholecystitis (the third-trimester lipid storm, and the laparoscopy-versus-conservative debate that now leans toward same-admission surgery), pyelonephritis (admit; the sepsis and preterm-labour spiral), thromboembolism (the LMWH dosed on booking-weight thinking and the epidural-timing arithmetic that changes the induction plan), epilepsy seizures (benzodiazepines then levetiracetam or the local pathway, and the fetal-bradycardia-after-maternal-seizure that usually resolves with the mother treated), trauma (the mother-first resuscitation with left lateral tilt, the Kleihauer for the rhesus-negative, the CTG even when the mother looks fine, and the perimortem caesarean-at-five-minutes rule whose logic is that the fetus is the second patient and only the first one's circulation saves either), and collapse - the amniotic-fluid-embolism and catastrophic-haemorrhage resuscitations in which the algorithm is identical to the non-pregnant one plus the tilt, plus the call for the senior obstetric and neonatal teams at the start, not after the second shock dose - [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]], [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]].**
:::

::: example The 32-week epigastric pain {#ex-hellp}
A 32-week primigravida is sent in by her GP for 'reflux and indigestion': BP 148/94, platelets 78, ALT 142, LDH 890, proteinuric, baby on the 2nd centile with raised umbilical pulsatility index, and she is 'just hungry, really'.
::: solution
**HELLP until disproved, and it is not ([[#def-hypertension]], [[#def-growth]]): the recognition - 'epigastric pain plus hypertension plus thrombocytopenia plus transaminitis plus haemolysis markers is HELLP syndrome, the fetal growth restriction proves the placentas have been failing for weeks, and the normal-ish blood pressure in a young primigravida is the most dangerous number on the page; this woman was sent home once because nobody put the four results in one sentence'; the bundle within the hour - 'the consultant obstetrician, the anaesthetist and the neonatal team all told now; magnesium sulfate started; IV antihypertensives to bring the severe-range pressures down safely; the steroids given and the platelet-and-coagulation-and-fibrinogen trend watched with crossmatch sent; fluid restriction with the oliguria-and-oedema caution; and the delivery decision made for the right reason - not the number but the trajectory, with HELLP at 32 weeks plus growth restriction plus abnormal Dopplers generally pointing to delivery after maternal stabilisation and steroid effect, by caesarean if the cervix or fetal state demands it'; and the four corners of the conversation - 'with her: "this is a serious pregnancy complication, we are going to deliver your baby soon, here is why tonight is safer than next week"; with anaesthesia: the platelet count for the spinal decision and the day's trend, not a single number, decides; with neonatology: a 32-weeker with growth restriction handles transition worse than a weighty one; and with the notes: the long-term follow-up booked - the blood-pressure check, the cardiovascular-risk discussion and the next-pregnancy aspirin plan that start on the day she leaves, because the disease is over and her risk is not' - [[cardiovascular/hypertension]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]].**

::: example The 26-week waters and the long conversation {#ex-pprom}
A 26+2 multigravida reports a gush and ongoing leak; the speculum shows pooling, the CTG is reassuring, and she asks 'how long can I stay and what are the odds'; the same afternoon she spikes 38.2 and her pulse is 112.
::: solution
**PPROM-at-the-edge ([[#def-preterm]]): the split-second reframe - 'a temperature and a tachycardia convert an expectant-pathway conversation into a chorioamnionitis-and-preterm-labour emergency: at this gestation the answer is treat, stabilise, and deliver after steroids if safely possible - prolonging a septic pregnancy kills the very baby she is protecting, and that is the sentence she needs first, not the survival statistics'; the immediate bundle - 'bloods and cultures, IV broad-spectrum antibiotics per the local sepsis-in-preterm-labour protocol, the single steroid dose if delivery is not immediate enough to omit it, magnesium neuroprotection started, the tocolysis deliberately not given despite the contractions because infection is the classic contraindication, the senior anaesthetic-and-neonatal teams briefed, and the group-B-strep prophylaxis thinking in place'; the delivery and after - 'mode decided by her state and the fetal state - chorioamnionitis is not itself a caesarean indication, vaginal birth with operative delivery for fetal indications when needed; the placenta sent for histology; the baby's septic workup coordinated with neonatology, and her own postpartum surveillance for the endometritis-and-continuing-fever that this picture predicts; and the documentation of the pathway - the one antibiotic course, the timed observations, the discussions in her words - because the same night, managed differently, is the claim'; and the equity-and-counselling footnote - 'these conversations happen repeatedly at this gestation in some families and never in others; the periviability numbers should be given as ranges with the outcome categories neonatologists actually use, and the same clinician should give them at 26 weeks and at 24, because consistency is the only mercy the statistics cannot provide' - [[paediatrics/newborn]], [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]].**

::: example The readmission headache {#ex-postpartum}
Day 4 after an 'uneventful' 38-week birth, a woman returns with thunderclap headache, BP 165/110, visual snow and hyperreflexia; the midwife's notes from the birth unit say 'pre-eclampsia excluded antenatally, blood pressures normal'.
::: solution
**Postpartum-pre-eclampsia ([[#def-hypertension]]): the rule - 'pre-eclampsia can present de novo up to six weeks after birth and peaks in days 3-6, and normal antenatal blood pressures do not exclude it; thunderclap headache plus visual symptoms plus hypertension plus hyperreflexia in a new mother is eclampsia-imminent and gets the magnesium-and-antihypertensive protocol now, with the differential - cerebral-venous-thrombosis (postpartum, dehydrated, headache-plus-seizure-plus-focal-signs, so image the veins when the picture is atypical or the CT is normal and the suspicion stays), posterior-reversible-encephalopathy, and the anesthetic puncture headache that is postural and none of the above - chased in parallel, not instead'; the management - 'the same bundle as the antenatal disease: IV or oral acute antihypertensives, magnesium 24 hours, bloods including the HELLP screen, and the decision about how much investigation the headache deserves made on examination and trajectory, with a low threshold for imaging because the medicolegal literature on postpartum headache is a catalogue of missed CVTs'; and the system lesson - 'her blood pressure at discharge and at the day-5 postnatal check were both someone's job: the RCOG-adjacent standard is that women with hypertensive disease in pregnancy get community blood-pressure monitoring with explicit thresholds and a phone number that rings, and the ones who present de novo postpartum usually did not have normal pressures - they had no pressures - which is why the day-5-to-8 blood-pressure check is being put back into every postnatal guideline this decade' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]], [[nervous-system/headache-pain]].**

::: example The twin pair and the discordant scan {#ex-ttts}
Monochorionic-diamniotic twins at 24 weeks: the donor has an empty bladder and oligohydramnios, the recipient polyhydramnios with a visible heart, and the mother asks whether she is 'going to lose one'.
::: solution
**Twin-to-twin-transfusion ([[#def-preterm]], [[paediatrics/newborn]]): the diagnosis and its honesty - 'this is stage-II-or-more TTTS on the Quintero-style staging the fetal-medicine unit uses, the natural history untreated is mortality for both in a majority of advanced cases, and the answer to her question is "you may lose one or both without treatment, treatment gives both a real chance, and the treatment can itself cost the pregnancy - the numbers are: laser ablation of the anastomoses leaves at least one survivor in roughly 80-90 percent of treated cases and two survivors in a majority, and selectives depend on stage and gestation"'; the pathway - 'same-day transfer to the fetal-medicine centre, the detailed anatomy-and-placenta-and-ductal assessment, the laser-versus-serial-amnioreduction-versus-expectant-for-stage-I discussion (laser is the evidence-based answer for advanced disease and the amnioreduction-is-temporising truth said plainly), the tocolysis-and-steroid-and-cerclage-if-bulging decisions taken by the operating team, and the post-laser surveillance schedule for the two classic complications - recurrent TTTS and the sudden twin-demise that leaves the survivor at neurological risk, which is why the follow-up includes neonatal-adjacent brain imaging rather than only growth'; and the counselling architecture - 'one clinician owns the serial conversations, the partner hears them, and the language is staged not binary: "the scan today is worse than last week, here is what changes" - because parents of high-risk twins live between weekly scans and the service that only speaks at crises teaches them that silence is safety' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[paediatrics/newborn]].**

::: exercise Treat the 30-week IUGR today {level=1}

::: exercise Write the steroid-versus-delivery decision {level=2}
At 33+2, severe pre-eclampsia is stabilised but the blood pressure needs three drugs; the fetus has reversed end-diastolic flow. The consultant on call says 'give the second steroid dose tomorrow and deliver then'. Agree or correct.
::: solution

## Where this leads

The antenatal detection of these conditions is in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], labour decisions in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]], the puerperium (eclampsia-after-delivery, wound and VTE issues) in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]], critical care in [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]], and neonatal outcomes in [[paediatrics/newborn]].

::: history
**The history of pregnancy's complications is the history of learning which ones medicine can fix and which ones it must merely anticipate. Pre-eclampsia - 'pre-eclamptic toxaemia', the 'monster of hypertension' whose seizures killed primigravidas in lying-in hospitals from the eighteenth century - resisted every treatment until the diagnosis was moved earlier than the convulsion: the 1920s-30s antenatal-clinic movement built the 'triad' (oedema, weight gain, proteinuria, the first two later demoted by evidence), the methyldopa trials of the 1970s proved treatment of mild hypertension could be safe, the Magpie trial (2002) made magnesium standard in one publication, and the English-and-Welsh confidential enquiries turned the 1990s-2000s maternal deaths into the 'save the mother' messages about early severe disease, HELLP recognition and the postpartum-eclampsia trap; aspirin prevention arrived from 1985's hypothesis through three decades of discordant trials to the 2017 FIGO-NICE adoption, the archetypal slow science. The preterm story ran through its own reversals: the bedside cervical-examination 'competence' diagnosis replaced by transvaginal ultrasound, the routine bed-rest prescriptions abandoned (they harm), ritodrine's beta-agonist harm era giving way to atosiban and nifedipine's modest utility, the steroids proven by Liggins and Howie's 1972-7 lamb experiments into one of perinatal medicine's great exports, and magnesium neuroprotection's 2000s trials adding a neurology outcome to an obstetric protocol. The fetal-growth-restriction revolution is the newest: the Doppler era from the 1980s turned 'small baby' into a staged placental-failure diagnosis with testable timing rules, and the growth-restriction trials of the 2010s-2020s - GRIT's ethics committee of the womb debates, then the Digitisation/HYPITAT-era timing studies and the Dutch and British trials - made the 29-week-versus-36-week decision a published curve instead of senior mood; the same era produced the stillbirth-autopsy-and-placenta-histology programmes that keep returning the same lesson, that a third of term stillbirths have placental pathologies we can name after the fact and predict only some of before it. The surgical emergencies history is smaller and meaner: the maternal appendicectomy mortality that fell not with surgery but with anaesthesia (the difficult-airway and aspiration lessons behind every pregnancy fast-list and the 2017 difficult-airway guidelines' cricoid-pressure nuances and the decision-to-deliver-in-arrest rule formalised in resuscitation councils' algorithms), and the placenta-accreta-into-ICU pathway born in the 1990s-2000s from the fact that caesarean-section's rise created a new disease's incidence and centres of excellence its only effective treatment. The through-line from every confidential enquiry: the women rarely die of unrecognisable conditions; they die of conditions recognised late by services organised to be convenient for staff - which is why the 'triggers' list, the 'what would worry you' phrase, and the escalation policy are in this chapter as much as the drug doses - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[paediatrics/newborn]].**
:::

::: summary
- Pre-eclampsia is diagnosed by the whole picture and cured only by delivery; treat severe blood pressure in minutes, give magnesium for eclampsia, and remember the disease can debut after a normal birth.
- HELLP presents as epigastric pain with normal-ish pressures; four abnormal blood results in one sentence is the diagnosis.
- Growth restriction is a Doppler-staged disease: serial growth plus fluid plus umbilical (and middle-cerebral and ductal) studies plus CTG decide the week, not the centile.
- Praevia-accreta and abruption are the haemorrhage conditions: the management is planning, blood-bank relationships and simulation, not improvisation.
- Preterm birth has four evidence tools - steroids, selective tocolysis, magnesium neuroprotection, and the right neonatal unit - and PPROM trades infection against prematurity with erythromycin buying the middle.
- Every pregnant collapse is haemorrhage, thromboembolism, amniotic-fluid embolism or pre-existing disease unmasked; resuscitate the mother on standard algorithms with the tilt, and call obstetrics, anaesthetics and neonatology at once.
:::

::: exercise Run the severe-hypertension protocol {level=1}
A 34-week woman has BP 178/116 confirmed over 15 minutes, headache and a platelet count of 96. Give the first hour: drugs, doses as a principle, monitoring, and the two conversations that start before the pressure is down.
::: solution
**The-first-hour ([[#def-hypertension]]): the drugs - 'IV labetalol 20-40-80 incrementing or hydralazine per protocol or oral modified-release nifedipine per the acute chart, aiming for a controlled fall to the 140s-150s/90s-100s within an hour rather than normalisation; start magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis because of the headache and the low platelets (severe features); fluid-restrict and catheterise and watch the output; and crossmatch, FBC and coagulation and renal and liver panel sent now'; the monitoring - 'quarter-hourly blood pressure and maternal observations per the one-to-one high-dependency standard, hourly urine, the neurological and visual and pain questions asked as observations not chat, and continuous CTG where gestation and plans make it meaningful'; and the two conversations before the numbers are pretty - 'with anaesthetics (platelets trending down now means the spinal decision may expire during labour) and with neonatology (a 34-week growth-restricted baby plus magnesium exposure plus pre-eclamptic placenta is not a routine nursery admission), and with her, in the same first hour: "you are very ill, we know why, the medicine is delivery soon, and your baby has a team" - because the blood pressure is the easy part and the waiting is not' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]], [[cardiovascular/hypertension]].**

::: exercise Choose the cerclage {level=2}
Two women: (a) 14+2, one painless second-trimester loss at 20 weeks, no scans done; (b) 18+6 twins with cervix 12 mm on transvaginal scan, no losses, no symptoms. Advise both.
::: solution
**Two-cervices ([[#def-preterm]]): (a) history-indicated cerclage discussion - the single painless mid-trimester loss with a normal-nothing-else history is exactly the pattern the cerclage evidence (the combined history-plus-short-cervix trials) supports when combined with early cervical-length surveillance: offer TVS screening from 15-16 weeks with cerclage for shortening (or the pragmatic history-only placement at 12-14 weeks per unit culture), after exclusion of anomalies and infection and with the written plan for the second twin of progesterone-or-cerclage logic; and the counselling honesty - 'the number-needed-to-treat is not small and the operation is not the risk, but the anxiety it creates is real, so the alternative - watch with scans and accept the 20-weeks risk - must be presented as a legitimate choice, not as failure'; (b) do not operate - 'a 12 mm cervix in twins at 19 weeks with no history does not meet the evidence for cerclage in twins (the trials say no benefit, possibly harm), and this is where the specialty's discipline shows: resist the visible-action instinct, offer the scan follow-up and the symptom advice, and explain to the referring unit why you are declining - because the most-common safeguarding-of-the-fetus here is protecting the mother from a well-intentioned stitch'; and the meta-rule: the cerclage decision is made on three data points - indication type, gestation, and whether membranes are exposed or shortened - and every guideline agrees on the first two and argues about the third, which is what you tell them both at 2am - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[epidemiology-ebm/critical-appraisal]].**
:::

::: exercise Diagnose the atypical appendicitis {level=2}
A 26-week woman, 18 hours of right-flank pain, nausea, WCC 16 (pregnancy baseline ~12), afebrile, urinalysis with 1+ blood, and the surgical team says 'renal colic, we have seen worse'. Your reasoning and escalation plan.
::: solution
**Flank-pain-at-26-weeks ([[#def-preterm]]): the reasoning - 'the appendix migrates, so appendicitis in late pregnancy is flank pain with a modest leucocytosis everyone explains away; the microscopic haematuria fits both stone and an inflamed appendix kissing the ureter, and the dangerous move is anchoring on the common diagnosis when the dangerous one is treatable only by looking; pain duration plus localised-peritonism-on-repalpation-plus-the-trend-is-what-decides-and-the-symptom-seed here is the 18-hour-localised-flank-pain that is getting worse, not the white count'; the investigation - 'graded-compression ultrasound first (often non-diagnostic), MRI without gadolinium as the next step in units that can do it the same day, and CT when the picture or the trend forces it - the radiation question is answered by the perforation rate, which is the thing that actually kills these pregnancies, and a negative MRI is worth a late one rather than a missed perforation'; and the escalation - 'the surgeon's "we have seen worse" is answered with the obstetric-medicine version: "I have seen the perforation at 30 weeks - I would like the MRI and I would like us both to review her again in four hours with the fever chart", and if the answer stays no, the consultant-to-consultant call, because the delay pattern in every appendicitis-in-pregnancy audit is the same one sentence: someone competent declined to look'; and the outcome numbers to justify the looking: perforation raises fetal loss several-fold and the maternal morbidity is a laparotomy-and ICU story when it could be a day-case laparoscopy - [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

::: exercise Plan the accreta pathway {level=3}
A 31-year-old with two previous caesareans has an anterior major praevia with loss of the myometrial interface and lacunae at 30 weeks, rhesus-negative, partner available, 90 minutes from your tertiary unit. Write the delivery plan and the two contingency plans that must be pre-scripted.
::: solution
**The-accreta-plan ([[#def-growth]]): the shape of the delivery - 'planned caesarean hysterectomy-or-conservative-attempt per findings at 34-36 weeks in a centre with a specialist accreta team, interventional radiology availability (pre-op internal-iliac-or-aortic-balloon strategies where the local data support them rather than ritual), the MDT pre-brief: two consultant obstetricians, a gynaecologic-oncology-adjacent surgeon or the local pelvic-surgery expert, anaesthetics with arterial-line-and-large-bore-and-rapid-infuser planning, the cell-salvage machine tested, 6-10 units of blood and plasma and platelets and fibrinogen or cryo matched and thaw-ready, the HDU bed booked with the same phone number that books the theatre, the neonatal team for a 35-week caesarean baby, and the patient consented on the day for the hysterectomy probability in numbers she repeats back'; contingency one - 'the massive-haemorrhage pathway with the obstetric-dICP mindset: the point-of-care fibrinogen and viscoelastic testing if available, the tranexamic-acid early decision, the re-warming-and-calcium vigilance, the damage-control abdominal packing and ICU transfer with a defined second-look, and the rhesus immunoglobulin given to the quantified dose if the bleed or the version sensitises her'; contingency two - 'the bladder-and-ureter question answered before the first incision (the pre-op MRI where the plane is uncertain, the cystoscopy-and-stent plan, and the interventional-radiology-embolisation option for the bleeding she may still have after the uterus is out), and the human contingencies: the partner in the theatre policy decided in advance, the fertility conversation with her documented before the emergency when she is not bleeding, and the debrief plus the memory-preserving letter afterwards, because the women who lose uteruses and ovaries in the same night they almost died are the patients the pathway forgets and the ones who need it to remember'; and the transfer - 'in utero and stable, she comes by road with a midwife and a transfusion plan and a theatre booked, not by ambulance at 3am with an unplanned bleeding - which is the entire pathway's point' - [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]], [[blood/transfusion]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/postnatal-period]].**
