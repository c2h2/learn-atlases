Menopause is a normal transition with abnormal consequences when untreated: vasomotor symptoms that wreck sleep, a genitourinary syndrome mistaken for infections for decades, bone and cardiovascular risk accruing silently, and a treatment debate (hormone replacement therapy) whose evidence base was mangled in public for twenty years. **This chapter covers the physiology and diagnosis of menopause and perimenopause, premature ovarian insufficiency as its own urgent disease, the menopause-hormone-therapy evidence (formulations, routes, risks by type and duration, the breast-and-ovarian-and-VTE-and-endometrial numbers), non-hormological options, and the long tail - urogenital syndrome, osteoporosis interfaces, cardio-menopause, and the mood-sleep-cognition conversations. The clinical culture the NICE menopause guideline tried to restore: menopause is not a disorder, but its symptoms are treatable, age is not a reason to suffer, and the woman who says 'this is affecting my life' has made the key diagnostic statement.**

## Diagnosis and the early end

::: definition The menopause transition and premature ovarian insufficiency {#def-diagnosis}
**Definitions: the menopause is a point in time (12 months of amenorrhoea) reached retrospectively; the perimenopause (menopause transition) is the variable-length, often-cycle-having, sometimes-contraception-requiring phase of hormonal chaos - the vasomotor symptoms, sleep disruption, mood lability, arthralgia and brain-fog and cycle change that span months to many years, with the average UK age at final menstrual period around 51; and surgical menopause (bilateral oophorectomy) is an abrupt, often more symptomatic, version with the bone-and-cardiovascular consequences of sudden oestrogen withdrawal that make HRT-until-the-natural-age-a-default-recommendation-not-an-option. Diagnosis is clinical above 45 (FSH-and-oestradiol-not-required-for-the-symptomatic-woman-over-50-with-changing-cycles-and-contraception-and-medical-history-questions-answered) and biochemical under 45 where the diagnosis matters for benefits and treatment: the two-sample-or-repeatedly-elevated-FSH-and-low-oestradiol-of-premature-ovarian-insufficiency, with the androgen-and-thyroid-and-prolactin-and-hCG-exclusions and the cause-search (the Turner-mosaic-and-Fragile-X-premutation-and-autoimmune-polyglandular-and-ictal-chemotherapy-and-radiation-and-surgery-associations with the honest note that the majority remain unexplained), the DEXA-and-cardiovascular-risk-and-nutrition-and-vitamin-D-baseline-that-should-start-at-diagnosis-not-at-60, the bone-and-heart-and-neurological-and-sexual-and-psychological-consequences-of-decades-of-oestrogen-deficiency-that-are-why-POI-is-a-serious-diagnosis-treated-urgently-with-HRT-not-a-fertility-question-only (the fertility-conversation-belonging-simultaneously-with-egg-donation-options-and-the-natural-conception-5-percent-adjacent-oddity-counselled-accurately), and the contraception-contraception-point-for-POI-women-who-do-not-want-pregnancy-because-ovulation-precedes-the-first-post-treatment-period. ::: history
**The menopause is among the oldest medically described states - the Hippocratic mention of the menses ceasing at forty-one and Aristotle's account of the sterile years - and among the most recently taken seriously: the nineteenth-century 'uterine storm' diagnoses and the surgical-menopause vogue of the 1880s, then the Zondek-and-Aschheim urine-extraction work and the Allen-and-Doisy purification era that produced commercial oestrogens in the 1930s-40s, and above all Robert A. Wilson's 1966 'Feminine Forever' which promised oestrogen as youth-preservation and sold it past its evidence, the menarche-to-menopause-ovulation-arithmetic-of-1970s-risk-theory-and-the-1975-endometrial-cancer-reports-that-ended-unopposed-oestrogen-in-seconds-and-invented-the-oestrogen-plus-progestogen-pairing. The prophetic era followed: the 1980s-1990s bone-loss epidemiology that made oestrogen a fracture treatment in trials and observational cohorts, and Premarin's 2024 withdrawal amid litigation as a pharmaceutical-history lesson, and the WHI trial launched in 1991 whose combined-arm stop was announced in 2002 which was a prevention trial stopped early on a breast-risk signal in the combined arm and communicated as a verdict on all hormones, causing the prescription-collapse-studies-documented-across-Europe-and-North-America-with-the-decade-later-reanalyses-the-timing-hypothesis-and-the-Danish-follow-ups-and-the-NAMS-2022-position-and-the-BSMS-and-NICE-2015-2019-menopause-guideline-era-arriving-at-the-current-consensus: the drugs were better than the panic and worse than the promises, and the honest absolute numbers took twenty years to reach the women. The non-hormonal pharmacopoeia's own arc: the SSRIs-and-gabapentin-and-clonidine-serendipities-into-evidence, the neurokinin-3-receptor antagonism of fezolinetant, born from the KNDy-neuron thermoregulation science of the 2010s, into 2022-2024 licensing, and the CBT-and-hypnosis-and-weighted-NICE-symptom-pathways-which-are-the-quiet-rehabilitation-of-psychological-treatment-in-this-field. The genitourinary-syndrome's renaming the 2014 renaming of 'vulvovaginal atrophy' to the genitourinary syndrome of menopause precisely so clinicians would hear 'sex and urine' in the words and the laser-and-radiofrequency-marketing-then-FDA-caution-then-evidence-waiting saga are the recent case-studies in how a symptom gets dismissed, commercialised and then re-medicalised. The culture history is the liveliest: the menopause-as-medicalisation-versus-feminist-reclamation-debates-of-the-1980s-90s (the social-histories of Ann-Louise Irwin-adjacent advocacy aside, the Australian and British advocacy of the 1990s that reframed menopause as a life stage, not a deficit), the-2010s-2020s workplace and political moment (the sports-coaching disclosures of the 2021 Olympics cycle, the UK parliamentary menopause debates and the workplace-guidance campaigns of the 2020s), the menopause-in-film-and-soaps-and-sport-and-the-'I-told-my-manager'-campaigns, and the equity-layer: the diagnosis-and-treatment-gaps-by-race-and-class-and-learning-disability-and-the-under-researched-menopause-in-women-who-had-chemotherapy-or-early-ovariectomy-or-POI-because-most-research-questions-were-about-51-year-olds-with-flushes-and-most-services-still-are-too. The lesson every menopause-clinician-repeats: this chapter's greatest side-effect was never thrombosis - it was twenty-five years of unasked questions'.**

::: summary
- Menopause is a clinical diagnosis above 45; below 45 it needs biochemistry, and premature ovarian insufficiency is a whole-body disease treated with HRT until the natural age, not a fertility footnote.
- HRT is the most effective treatment for vasomotor symptoms and GSM; give the absolute numbers (small breast-risk differences by type, no VTE signal for transdermal oestrogen) and choose by her priorities.
- Oestrogen-only for hysterectomised women; the progestogen choice (or LNG-IUS) protects the endometrium and can treat bleeding at once; review annually, no forced stop-date by clock.
- The genitourinary syndrome is common, treatable, and missed unless asked about; vaginal oestrogen is low-risk and long-term; lichen sclerosus is a different disease with a surveillance duty.
- Bone and cardiovascular risk are the menopause's long tail: count the history (POI, surgical menopause, pre-eclampsia) in risk tools and treat osteoporosis with the specialist ladder.
- Mood, sleep and cognitive fog are perimenopausal presentations with real treatments and an even more real communication need; the 2002 media legacy, not pharmacology, is the largest remaining barrier.
:::
