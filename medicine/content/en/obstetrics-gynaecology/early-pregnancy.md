Early pregnancy is the emergency clinic of obstetrics: miscarriage, ectopic pregnancy, hyperemesis, the threatened pregnancy with bleeding, and the pregnancy nobody wanted yet, arriving with a decision attached. **This chapter is the medicine of the first trimester: diagnosis by hCG kinetics and ultrasound with their honest grey zones, the management of miscarriage including the medical-surgical-expectant evidence that reshaped it, ectopic pregnancy with its methotrexate-versus-surgery arithmetic and its lethal presentations, hyperemesis with its refeeding-and-thiamine discipline, and the gestational trophoblastic disease that hides behind a 'missed miscarriage' with a theca-lutein cyst. The governing skill is parallel vigilance: in every early pregnancy, the questions 'is it intrauterine, is it viable, is the mother safe, and what does she want' are asked simultaneously, and the answer to the first one is what prevents the deaths.**

## Diagnosing the early pregnancy

::: definition Pregnancy of unknown location and the ectopic question {#def-diagnosis}
**The tools and their limits: transvaginal ultrasound establishes an intrauterine pregnancy when a yolk sac or fetal pole is seen - the discriminatory hCG zone (historically 1,000-2,000 IU/L for the gestational sac in a normal-appearing uterus) is a population rule that individual units calibrate around the 4th-5th week, and nothing on a single scan proves a pregnancy is failing or normal at the margins; serial 48-hour beta-hCG trends (a normal early rise of at least 35-53 percent over 48 hours depending on starting level, and a fall of at least 21-35 percent favouring loss or resolution) are probability language, not proof, and the cross-over of normal, failing and ectopic curves is the reason pregnancy-of-unknown-location (PUL) protocols exist: with hCG below the decision threshold and no intrauterine seen, repeat hCG at 48 hours and act on the trajectory; with pain or haemodynamic threat or a rising hCG past threshold without an intrauterine pregnancy, the ectopic pathway activates; adnexal mass-with-empty-uterus is ectopic until disproved, and the 'line-like endometrial collection' pseudosac is the classic misread. Ectopic pregnancy: the tubal ampullary majority plus the dangerous minorities - interstitial (the cornual pregnancy that bleeds catastrophically late), caesarean-scar (the placenta-accreta-adjacent predecessor), cervical, ovarian, abdominal, and the heterotopic pair after fertility treatment where an intrauterine pregnancy does not exclude a second ectopic; risk factors (previous ectopic, tubal and pelvic surgery, PID and chlamydia, smoking, endometriosis, IUD-in-place pregnancies being disproportionately ectopic, and the honest note that half of ectopic pregnancies occur in women with no identified risk factor); the presentation that must not be reassuring (the 'she looked well with a collapsed tube' pattern - shoulder-tip pain, syncope, PR pain and rectal pressure and 'a bowel infection I treated last week' histories all belong in every ectopic's chart, and the vital signs can stay near-normal while litres accumulate). Management is a three-way evidence-based decision - surgery (laparoscopic salpingectomy standard, salpingotomy selected for the contralateral-tube-damage-and-desired-future-fertility-with-follow-up-capacity patient), medical methotrexate (the single-dose regimen with hCG-day-4-and-7 rules and the anti-folate counselling - no alcohol, no folic acid, the sunburn-and-stomatitis warning signs and the 'scream clinic' pain review that distinguishes the separation-tubal-abortion-crash when in doubt), and the expectant pathway for selected low-hCG-resolving-cases - with anti-D immunoglobulin for the rhesus-negative per local dose-rules, and the 'ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise' triage rule that any early pregnancy with pain, bleeding and haemodynamic oddity gets a group-and-save, a senior review and a scan that day - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]].**
:::

## Miscarriage

::: definition Early and late pregnancy loss {#def-miscarriage}
**Definition and diagnosis: miscarriage is pregnancy loss before 24 weeks (the legal and clinical line moves by nation; the 12-week cliff divides the early majority - around 10-15 percent of recognised pregnancies end in loss, far more end before they are recognised, and the reassuring corollary is that a visible fetal heartbeat at 8 weeks with normal findings carries a few-percent subsequent-loss rate in low-risk women), and the terminology carries management: threatened (bleeding, cervical os closed, viable), inevitable (open os with products at the os), incomplete (partial expulsion with bleeding risk), complete, and missed/silent (demised pregnancy retained, diagnosed by the absence of a heartbeat at defined crown-rump-length-and-mean-sac-diameter thresholds - the diagnostic criteria must be strictly met because misdiagnosis is the specialty's most consequential error, and the 'scan in 7-14 days when measurements are borderline' rule exists for exactly that reason). Management by situation, the evidence being unambiguous: expectant care is first-line for incomplete and missed miscarriage when clinically stable and chosen, with success rates around 60-80 percent for missed miscarriage at earlier gestations and the misoprostol-assisted medical pathway (vaginal or buccal with repeat dosing) raising completion; surgical evacuation (manual vacuum aspiration or suction curettage under local or general) is indicated for haemodynamic instability, infection, failed medical-or-expectant management, or the woman's clear preference after counselling - and the MVA-early-pregnancy units that moved the work from general-anaesthesia theatres to outpatient local-anaesthesia practice are one of the great de-implementation successes (less operative time, equal or better outcomes, patient acceptance). The care standards that separate good services: the dedicated early-pregnancy-assessment-unit model (same-day scan-plus-clinician-plus-named-phone-line-continuity contact, which trials show shortens the ordeal and reduces attendances), the bereavement care (a private room, the naming of the baby if wanted, the memory box and memory-making policies the midwifery teams have normalised after the 'miscarriage is managed like a haemorrhage' criticism), the pathology-and-genetic-testing policy (products-for-karyotype where recurrent or requested and it changes counselling - the balanced-translocation discovery that matters for the next pregnancy), the anti-D question for rhesus-negative women (surgical always; medical-or-expectant per the current guideline era's selective approach with the local protocol quoted), and the follow-up sentence: bleeding-and-pain red flags, the hCG-or-contraception-after-miscarriage facts (fertility returns within days-to-two-weeks and the 'wait one cycle' folklore is only about date-keeping convenience), and the next-pregnancy prognosis counselling that the majority conceive again and the majority of those have live births - and the aspirin-and-progesterone-adjacent-evidence-for-the-recurrent-loss-cohort belongs to the next paragraph. Recurrent miscarriage (three losses - two-and-investigate is now widely accepted practice): the workup is antiphospholipid-antibodies (the treatable one - the low-dose-aspirin-plus-LMWH-live-birth-improving evidence that makes aPL testing a duty not a ritual), parental karyotypes for structural-rearrangement-recognition, thyroid-and-prolactin-and-diabetic-control-optimisation, uterine-cavity-assessment (the septum-resection-controversy-having-hysteroscopy-or-shallow-saline-infusion-sonography), and the honest remainder - more than half have no identified cause, and the empirical progesterone-in-early-pregnancy-for-bleeding-with-past-miscarriages trial evidence (the PRISM-adjacent-into-NICE adoption of micronised progesterone 400 mg twice daily for eligible women) now sits alongside the reassurance-of-early-reassurance-scan-service which is itself an intervention worth measuring - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[endocrine/reproductive-endocrinology]].**
:::

## Vomiting, molar pregnancy and the wanted decision

::: definition Hyperemesis, gestational trophoblastic disease and abortion care {#def-hyperemesis}
**Hyperemesis gravidarum is vomiting with weight loss, ketonuria, dehydration and electrolyte-and-thyroid-derangement - a diagnosis of exclusion applied to the right woman, not a label for any first-trimester nausea: the management is protocol and it is early - thiamine before any glucose-containing fluid (the Wernicke's case reports are all the same sentence about dextrose-first), a stepwise antiemetic ladder (cyclizine-or-promethazine-adjacent antihistamines-and-prochlorperazine-and-metoclopramide-and-domperidone-adjacent options, the ondansetron-advantage-shift-with-its-cleft-and-jaundice-hedge-notes-settling-into-reassuring, the steroid-rescue-after-10-weeks-per-guidance-because-of-the-oral-cleft-signal-earlier), the ambulatory-day-unit-and-home-infusion-pathways-that-keep-women-out-of-isolation-rooms-and-away-from-terminations-for-social-exhaustion-and-the-entitlement-to-say-this-is-a-medical-condition-not-morning-sickness-sickness-which-is-how-psychiatric-mislabelling-ends-a-pregnancy-by-accident; plus the reflux-and-helicobacter-not-routine-thoughts, the VTE-risk-with-immobility-and-dehydration (the calf pain in hyperemesis is a scan not a shrug), and the admission for the refractory with the electrolyte-correction-speed-respect-hyponatraemia-and-the-central-line-caution. Gestational trophoblastic disease: the complete mole (the 'snowstorm'-and-cystic-theca-lutein-cysts-and-hCG-way-above-dates picture, uterus large for dates, early pre-eclampsia before 20 weeks being the stigmatising clue) and the partial (often diagnosed only after evacuation of a 'missed miscarriage' on histology), managed by suction evacuation (never sharp curettage of a molar uterus - the bleeding-and-perforation-and-trophoblastic-embolisation-avoidance logic, the consultant-gynaecologist-and-holistic-team decision about the hysterectomy-with-completed-family-possibility), the follow-up hCG-regimen (weekly until normal then monthly for months, per the national gestational-trophoblastic-disease-centre protocols) whose entire purpose is catching the post-molar neoplasia that cure-rate approaches 100 percent when registered-and-treated-early, the reliable-contraception-during-surveillance-not-'avoid-pregnancy'-hand-waving, the second-chemotherapy-protocol-for-the-low-versus-high-risk-metastatic-ratio-truths, and the honest counselling that the next pregnancy is fine, the recurrence risk is small, and the surveillance must not be abandoned to attend a wanted pregnancy (the registered-centre's 'unplanned pregnancy during surveillance - ring us first' rule). Abortion care: the lawful framework (in England and Wales two doctors' opinions and the ground-based approval under the 1967 Act as amended, the telemedicine-and-home-pharmacological-era lessons of 2020-2022 and the ongoing decriminalisation debates elsewhere, and the conscience-clause architecture that obliges services to ensure someone else can provide lawful care), the pharmacological regimen (mifepristone-then-misoprostol at home for the medical pathway with the analgesia-and-antiemetic-and-bleeding-volume-and-danger-signs-information and the safety-net phone line staffed by clinicians who answer, the vacuum-aspiration pathway for the surgical route with the same respect for informed choice and anaesthesia decisions by gestation and preference), the same-day-contraception-implant-and-coil-and-the-ovulation-returns-in-2-3-weeks-counselling, the Rhesus-immunoglobulin-per-gestation-and-local-guideline, the infection-screening-opportunity, and the counselling reality: the emotional outcome most predicted by whether the decision was hers and whether she was treated respectfully, which is why the abuse-and-coercion-and-'was-this-pregnancy-pressed-on-you' question belongs to safeguarding not to judgement. Every one of these three conditions - hyperemesis, molar pregnancy, the abortion - is a condition in which the woman's account of her own body and intentions is the primary clinical data; the chapter's skill is listening to that before the scan - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/gynaecological-cancer]], [[paediatrics/safeguarding]].**
:::

::: example The six-week pain {#ex-ectopic}
A 29-year-old, 6+2 by dates, positive test 4 days ago, presents with 8 hours of right iliac fossa pain and shoulder-tip pain; BP 104/66, HR 96, hCG 3,400, TVS shows no intrauterine pregnancy and free fluid in the pouch of Douglas with a 4 cm right adnexal ring.
::: solution
**This is a ruptured-or-rupturing-ectopic ([[#def-diagnosis]]): the arithmetic - 'hCG far above any unit's discriminatory zone, no intrauterine pregnancy, an adnexal ring, free fluid, and referred shoulder-tip pain from diaphragmatic blood - the diagnosis is made; the decision is theatre, not methotrexate, because the free fluid plus referred pain plus the tachycardia-and-narrowing-pulse pattern is the surgical indication and every minute of "we will observe the hCG" in this picture is the sentence in the claim'; the first hour - 'group-and-save-plus-fbc-plus-coagulation-plus-biochem-with-the-crossmatch-thrown-in-because-this-is-a-quantified-blood-loss-disease-not-a-vaginal-bleed-disease (the visible loss is a fraction of the real loss), the anaesthetist-and-consultant-gynaecologist-notified, resuscitation-with-blood-products-not-saline-flooding, the laparoscopy-or-laparotomy-per-senior-judgment-and-stability-with-salpingectomy-most-likely-while-the-second-lav-and-the-cell-salvage-thinking-exist-for-the-collapsed-patient; the rhesus-negative anti-D dose with the Kleihauer-for-massive-transfusion-rules; the second-thinking-list - the heterotopic pregnancy if she had any fertility treatment and the corpus-luteum-that-looks-like-a-ring mimic - which are why the surgeon looks at both tubes and the uterus before closing'; and the after - 'the histology-confirmed diagnosis, the recovery-and-driving-and-shower advice, the hCG-falls-to-zero-safety-net (persistent-trophoblast-is-rare-but-real), the next-pregnancy-early-scan-plan-that-is-her-right-not-favour, the psychological-support-offer-because-ectopic-is-a-death-bereavement-and-injury-triple-hit, and the de-brief-with-numbers: how much blood, was the tube saved, what does it mean for fertility - asked by the clinician who operated, because the midwife at 9pm cannot answer those and should not have to pretend' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]], [[emergency-critical-care/resuscitation]].**
:::

::: example The missed miscarriage with a fibroid uterus {#ex-missed}
A 36-year-old, 11+4, no symptoms, scan by protocol shows no heartbeat with crown-rump length 31 mm; her fibroid-distorted uterus and 9 cm intramural fibroid make the options unequal; she asks 'what would you do'.
::: solution
**The-options-made-personal ([[#def-miscarriage]]): the diagnosis first - 'no-heartbeat-at-CRL-31 mm is definitive pregnancy loss by the strict criteria - and she is being told by a doctor who read the number not a screen-report-word, which is the whole of dignity in this conversation'; the three routes with this uterus in view - 'expectant (possible, less predictable with the fibroid-adjacent cavity and the bleeding-and-anaemia-thoughts), medical (misoprostol, the heavier-pain-and-bleeding-with-a-fibroid-uterus-counselling, the haemoglobin-baseline-and-tranexamic-acid-on-hand practicalities, and the honest "may need surgery anyway" percentage), and surgical (MVA-adjacent-for-11-weeks-is-boundary-territory so likely a suction-evacuation-per-specification with the fibroid-and-perforation-and-incomplete-evacuation-and-second-procedure-risk-told-in-numbers-and-the-consultant-doing-it-offered-not-assumed)'; the answer to 'what would you do' - 'the honest version: "if this were me with a fibroid like yours and my haemoglobin at 105, I would choose the operation with a consultant and an anaesthetist who know the scan beforehand - and if I were stable and wanted to avoid surgery, the tablets with you here at this unit tonight would also be reasonable; here is what makes each one wrong for some women"'; and the care wrap - 'the private room, the naming-and-memory-options, the partner-included-when-she-chooses, the pathology-consent question explained in one sentence about rhesus-and-histology-and-why-we-do-not-judge-it, the anti-D decision quoted from protocol, the 'you can come back to this unit by phone for the next scan in this next pregnancy, not the GP queue' promise that is the difference between a service and a corridor, and the note that she will need the same conversation about how she is in six weeks when everyone at work has stopped asking' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[clinical-skills/communication]].**

::: example The 15-week 'missed miscarriage' that is not {#ex-borderline}
A scan at 15+2 reports 'no fetal heart, sac present'; the sonographer asks you to confirm; the BPD is small for dates and the mother's dates were uncertain with late-but-regular cycles.
::: solution
**The-diagnosis-you-must-not-make-on-one-scan ([[#def-miscarriage]]): the criteria discipline - 'pregnancy-loss criteria are strict for a reason: at uncertain dates a biometry-small fetus with no visible heart on a single suboptimal view can be a 11-weeker, and the diagnostic rule set (CRL thresholds, mean sac diameter gaps, and the "when in doubt, rescan in 7-14 days" clause) exists precisely for this; the right act is the re-scan with an expert operator and the interval, and the courage to tell the parents "I am not certain yet - and being wrong once is bearable, being wrong by termination is not"'; the process - 'the second opinion documented by name, the dates-reconciliation (the early scan if one exists, the cycle length, the fundal height), the repeat scan with the same machine and a printed image set for the notes, and the counselling that continues through the wait with the named contact who does the scans - the "two-weeks-of-hell-at-home" alternative being why day-unit or 48-72-hour-admission options exist'; and the after-confirmation path - 'when loss is confirmed by criteria, the management conversation of the previous example begins, and the same rigour that protected her from a wrong diagnosis protects her now from a wrong "everything will be fine"; this chapter's single most medicolegally-quoted lesson: misdating is the mother of the misdiagnosis, and the sonographer-and-doctor-who-say-I-do-not-know-yet are the safest people in the room' - [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]].**

::: example The positive test, the IUD and no visible pregnancy {#ex-iud}
A 31-year-old with a copper IUD in situ, 6 weeks 4 days by dates, positive urine test, hCG 2,100, TVS: empty uterus, IUD in place, no adnexal mass seen, minimal free fluid, mild twinge she calls 'period-like'.
::: solution
**The-IUD-pregnancy-workup ([[#def-diagnosis]]): the rule - 'an IUD does not cause ectopic pregnancy but a pregnancy that occurs with an IUD is disproportionately likely to be ectopic, and hCG above the discriminatory zone with an empty uterus is an ectopic-or-abnormal-pregnancy-until-proven-otherwise regardless of how "period-like" the pain adjective is; the empty-uterus-plus-IUD scan also needs the "is the IUD where it looks" check because a migrated device and a cornual pregnancy can photograph similarly to a tired eye'; the hour - 'the senior-review-now protocol, group-and-save and the repeat-hCG-at-48-hours decision made in person with the clinic appointment named (never "ring your GP Monday"), the strict written ectopic advice with the collapse-and-999 instruction, the she-is-not-alone-tonight question (who is at home), and the anti-D decision if rhesus-negative; the next day or two - the trajectory decides between the PUL-expectant path, methotrexate-for-unruptured-ectopic-with-follow-up-capacity, or theatre; the IUD is removed if an intrauterine pregnancy is wanted and possible and is removed-with-antibiotics-and-counselling-in-the-ectopic-or-loss-paths'; and the counselling that follows - 'the contraception-after-resolution conversation because her IUD clearly "failed" in her mind when it mostly did its job and hers was the unlucky minority, the honest numbers on failure rates and ectopic proportion, and the method-choice offer including the LARC she nearly died of anxiety about - because the next pregnancy with the same device-in-situ fear is either a wanted one with early-scan-planning or an avoided one, and both are successes' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]], [[emergency-critical-care/acute-assessment]].**

::: exercise Manage the hyperemesis with hyponatraemia {level=2}
Day 3 of a hyperemesis admission: sodium has fallen from 131 to 127 after 4 litres of 0.9 percent saline, she is confused-adjacent ("strange, isn't it"), and the team suggests 3 percent saline "to fix the number". Your reasoning and orders.
::: solution
**Hyponatraemia-with-a-story ([[#def-hyperemesis]]): the reasoning - 'vomiting plus volume-depleted-then-appropriately-Antidiuretic-hormone-plus-a-tea-and-toast-or-hypotonic-fluid-intake picture: the sodium is low because she is retaining water and the admission-fluid choice can make either contribution worse; 3 percent saline is reserved for severe symptomatic hyponatraemia (seizures, marked obtundation) given as a controlled small-volume infusion, not as a fix for a number and a pun - and her "strange" must be examined for the other causes too, including the Wernicke's she has been at risk of since admission, which is the same differential the junior should think of before any hypertonic order'; the orders - 'thiamine-now-and-documented-if-not-already, stop the hypotonic contributions, give the isotonic-or-slightly-hypertonic-salt strategy guided by repeat sodiums at 4-6 hours and by her fluid-balance chart, the strict-input-output-and-obs, and the correction-ceiling-rule written on the drug chart so the night team cannot "help" (max 8-10 mmol/L per 24 hours; overcorrection-with-osmotic-demyelination-is-the-avoidable-injury-with-an-unforgettable-name)'; and the context - 'the antiemetic-optimisation-continues-because-vomiting-is-the-driver, the cortisol-and-thyroid-and-pregnancy-uterus-molar-twins-thoughts-if-refractory, and the conversation with the team out loud: "we treat her sodium slowly or we trade one catastrophe for another" - which is how a ward learns this lesson without a patient paying for it twice' - [[renal/water-sodium]], [[endocrine/adrenal]].**

## Where this leads

The antenatal booking that follows a loss is in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], the surgical-safety context for evacuation in [[surgery/perioperative-care]], trophoblastic oncology in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/gynaecological-cancer]], and the fertility investigation in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]].

::: history
**Early pregnancy medicine is the youngest part of obstetrics because its diseases were invisible for most of history: pregnancy loss was 'the months', ectopic pregnancy was 18th-century's near-certain death (the abdominal and advanced cases that 'turned septic' and the first successful operations being salpingectomy-era 1880s-1900s surgeries), and the molar pregnancy whose high hCG midwives and physicians recorded as the 'hydatid mole with the ovarian cysts' had its tumour-behaviour recognition only when the 1950s-60s choriogonadotropin assays made the persistence measurable - Li, Hertz and Spencer's methotrexate-cures-of-metastatic-choriocarcinoma-in-the-early-1950s becoming oncology's first solid-tumour-cure-by-chemotherapy story, with the chorioepithelioma centres of Charing Cross and the 'register every mole' model being the world's first example of tumour-marker-driven surveillance done as national public policy. The modern diagnostic revolution was twofold and quick: the sensitivity of radioimmunoassay-and-then-immunometric hCG testing from the 1970s-80s, and the transvaginal probe of the late 1980s, which together created the 'pregnancy of unknown location' - a diagnosis that could not exist before you could measure and see separately - and the discriminatory-zone-and-48-hour-trend reasoning the previous chapter's ectopic decisions rest on; the ectopic medical-treatment era (the 1980s-90s methotrexate series into the randomised trials of the 2000s-2010s showing surgery and methotrexate equivalent in the selected unruptured patient with the fertility-outcome data still thin) is the rare case of a non-surgical treatment entering the guidelines on patient-preference-as-good-medicine grounds as much as on superiority. Miscarriage care was transformed twice: first by the misoprostol era of the 1990s-2000s (the off-label-use-into-guideline-adoption-into-home-use-studies arc with the mifepristone-priming trials) which gave women a medical route, and then by the MVA-and-ambulatory-early-pregnancy-assessment-unit movement (the French-and-UK-and-Swedish experience showing equal safety with less anaesthesia and less hospital) which took the operating theatre out of most first-trimester evacuation - the de-implementation of routine general anaesthesia for miscarriage being one of the specialty's most cost-effective-ever reforms. The bereavement-advocacy history is slower and truer: from the 1970s-80s 'it is only a miscarriage' medicine through the SANDS-and-miscarriage-association-era campaigns, the stillbirth-and-neonatal-death memory-practice spillover, the 'name the baby' policies and the 2010s-2020s era of home-miscarriage-care-with-clinician-phone-support and the 'missed miscarriage diagnosis-by-strict-criteria' safety campaigns following the documented cases of premature-diagnosis terminations; and the current frontier is the intersection with genetics - products-karyotype-and-SNP-array-calling-embryonic-cause, the PGT-A-and-RPL-panel debates, and the immunological-treatments-empire-of-hope (the IVF-adjacent intralipid-and-paternal-lymphocyte-and-NK-cell-therapies that systematic reviews keep finding unproven, and the specialty's discipline of saying so). The through-line: this chapter's diseases became survivable and then human, and both turns depended on technologies - the probe, the assay, the tablet - and on women's advocacy insisting that survivability was not the finish line - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]], [[clinical-skills/communication]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]].**
:::

::: summary
- In any first-trimester pain-and-bleeding case the three questions run in parallel: where is the pregnancy, is it developing, and is the mother haemodynamically safe; hCG trends and TVS answer the first two, and shoulder-tip pain answers the third.
- A PUL is a protocol, not a diagnosis: serial hCG to threshold or resolution, with ectopic advice and a phone number that rings.
- Ectopic with free fluid and referred pain is theatre; unruptured ectopic is a three-way decision (surgery, methotrexate, expectant) made with her, not around her.
- Miscarriage management is patient-chosen among evidence-equivalent-ish routes made unequal by her anatomy, her blood count and her preferences; MVA and home medical pathways moved the care where it belongs.
- Hyperemesis gets thiamine before dextrose and stepwise antiemetics early; a mole gets suction, registration and hCG follow-up that never gets skipped for a wanted pregnancy.
- Every early-pregnancy contact is a contraception, safeguarding and mental-health opportunity, because fertility returns before the follow-up appointment.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Interpret the PUL numbers {level=1}
hCG rises 1,100 to 1,600 over 48 hours; no intrauterine pregnancy; pain-free, stable. What are the three possibilities ranked and what do you do?
::: solution
**Rising-but-slow ([[#def-diagnosis]]): the possibilities - 'an abnormal intrauterine pregnancy (failing or slow-rising), a continuing ectopic, and a normally-rising very-early intrauterine pregnancy below the scan threshold - in that order of action-importance, because two of the three become dangerous if unwatched'; the plan - 'repeat hCG at 48 hours with the unit's decision-threshold rule (a level above threshold without an intrauterine pregnancy moves her to the ectopic-workup: MRI-or-expert-scan-opinion-where-used, the surgical-option-keeping conversation, and the day-unit-or-daily-contact surveillance rules), strict ectopic advice written as a card not a sentence, and the 24-hour-contact-name given; the two errors: assuming 'probably early, ring if worse' with hCG already past threshold, and operating on a first rising level - the trajectory is the test' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/contraception-fertility]].**
:::
:::

::: exercise Prescribe the hyperemesis admission {level=2}
A 9-week primigravida, 7 kg lost from 62 kg, ketones 4+, sodium 131, potassium 3.1, hungry and thirsty but vomiting everything. Write the first 24 hours with the fluid-and-electrolyte details and the antiemetic ladder.
::: solution
**The-hyperemesis-day ([[#def-hyperemesis]]): the rehydration discipline - 'thiamine 100-200 mg IV (plus vitamin B co-strong-or-equivalent-multivitamin-with-thiamine) BEFORE and during the glucose-containing-fluids; 0.9 percent saline as the initial volume (the hyponatraemia correction kept slow - aim under 8-10 mmol/L per 24 hours, recheck sodium at 4-6 hours), potassium replacement in a monitored-per-rate-and-total-peripheral-access-friendly-concentration with the magnesium-and-phosphate-recheck-because-the-starving-vomiting-patient-depletes-everything-except-the-ketones-and-then-refeeding-risk-appears'; the ladder - 'scheduled-not-PRN-dosing: cyclizine-or-promethazine-IV/IM-with-the-anticholinergic-tachycardia-note, plus a dopamine-antagonist (prochlorperazine-or-metoclopramide-with-the-young-woman-dystonia-and-akathisia-warning-which-is-the-reason-for-the-co-prescription-of-the-antihistamine-that-is-not-just-sedation), add or switch to ondansetron (the QT-and-constipation-notes) as the often-most-effective, and the steroid-rescue (hydrocortisone-or-methylprednisolone-short-course-after-10-weeks-per-BNf-and-local-guidance-with-her-decision-recorded) if refractory; plus the acid-suppression-and-dental-enamel advice, the gastroparesis-thought (erythromycin-rarely-used), and the 'what about the thyroid - this is gestational transient thyrotoxicosis not Graves usually' explanation that stops a beta-blocker-and-antithyroid-drug misadventure'; and the human - 'the isolation-room question answered as a medical decision (she is ill, not infectious, and the other vomiters' corridor is not care), the 'this peaks and ends - and if it does not we escalate the plan, not blame you' sentence, the VTE risk thought with immobility and dehydration, and the 'who minds the older children tonight so you can sleep 4 hours in a bed' social-prescription that determines whether she leaves tomorrow or stays'.**
:::

::: exercise Advise after one early loss {level=2}
A 33-year-old, 9-week missed miscarriage evacuated uneventfully, asks when she can 'try again' and whether she should have 'all the tests'. Give the answers and the two that change them.
::: solution
**Post-loss-counselling ([[#def-miscarriage]]): the standard answers - 'physically she can try as soon as she wishes - fertility returns within 2-3 weeks, the "wait three cycles" advice is for date-keeping not safety, and the evidence does not require delay and may favour not delaying for age-related reasons; and no, the full investigation after one loss is not indicated - the background loss rate makes single-loss testing a source of false reassurance and false findings; one loss with a normal karyotype-resulted-products-optional and a straightforward evacuation needs the contraception-or-conception-plan conversation and the early-scan-offer-for-the-next-pregnancy as peace-of-mind-medicine'; the two that change them - '(1) the loss was molar-or-the-histology-said-something (then the follow-up and the surveillance-contraception-period answers replace these), and (2) her history is richer than "one loss" (previous VTE, thyroid disease, or family-history-of-thrombophilia-and-recurrent-loss-patterns - the aPL-and-thyroid-testing-triggering-thoughts); and the counselling sentence that makes the plan stick: "you have one-in-seven risk next time because that is everyone's risk, not your new risk" - the statistic most women have never heard and most services never say' - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]].**
:::

::: exercise Design the early-pregnancy service {level=3}
Your EPU has: two consultants across 1.5 WTE, PUL patients tracked on a spreadsheet, 'routine' 2-week post-miscarriage phone calls that nobody makes, ectopic readmissions, and a misdiagnosed-interstitial-ectopic incident. Build the service redesign and the risk register.
::: solution
**EPU-redesign ([[#def-diagnosis]], [[#def-miscarriage]]): the safety spine - 'the PUL register becomes a tracked-system-with-two-names-per-case (a named clinician owns each PUL to hCG-negativity with automatic-48-hour-lab-flags-not-inbox-alerts-and-the-protocol-says-what-each-trajectory-triggers) because spreadsheet medicine fails exactly when the hCG plateaus over a weekend; the ectopic protocol gets a mandatory senior-review-and-a-second-opinion-before-methotrexate-discharge rule and the interstitial-and-scar-ectopic rare-but-lethal section in the guideline with the radiology-and-MRI-where-available-and-the-embolisation-option-because-those-women-bleed-into-ICUs-not-into-clinics; the "did-not-attend-and-did-not-call" rule: no PUL is closed without hCG-negativity or a documented clinical resolution with the same-day-re-entry-right-explained'; the patient-experience spine - 'home-miscarriage-care-with-misoprostol-and-a-clinician-phone-line-and-24-hour-re-access-and-the-privacy-of-her-own-bathroom-when-she-wants-it-with-the-safety-net-said-in-numbers-not-words, the bereavement-and-memory-policy-trained-everyone-and-the-naming-letters-printed-not-photocopied, the 'we cannot find a cause' language replaced with the baseline-risk-sentences-and-the-recurrent-loss-route-map, and the EPDS-and-safeguarding-question-asked-when-everyone-is-quiet-because-miscarriage-in-a-coercive-relationship-is-a-clinical-entity'; the workforce spine - 'the 1.5-WTE-consultant-and-PUL-and-ectopic-and-termination-and-molar-and-hyperemesis-admission-caseload-is-the-risk-register-first-line: the MVA-training-pipeline-for-registrars, the nurse-led-hyperemesis-and-post-op-review-clinics-with-protocol-coverage, the ODP-and-band-7-coordinator-who-owns-the-follow-up-calls-because-consultants-do-not-close-loops-by-magic; and the metrics that get reported to the board monthly: time-to-diagnosis-for-ectopic-from-first-contact, PUL-to-resolution-days-with-the-95th-percentile-named, readmission-for-PPH-and-ectopic-within-14-days, percentage-of-molar-patients-registered-and-followed-to-hCG-normalisation, and the patient-reported "did anyone tell me what was happening and what comes next" score - because the incident report on the interstitial ectopic will use the same three sentences the risk register wrote tonight: late senior review, absent tracking, and a woman told to wait' - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]].**
