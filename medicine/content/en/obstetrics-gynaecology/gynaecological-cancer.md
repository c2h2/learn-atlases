Gynaecological cancer is the domain of two of medicine's great preventive achievements - cervical screening and the postmenopausal-bleeding fast-track - and of one continuing failure: ovarian cancer still kills mostly by presenting late and vaguely. **This chapter covers the three common pelvic cancers (endometrial, ovarian, cervical) plus the vulval and trophoblastic minorities: presentation and the safety-net pathways, the screening programmes and their epidemiological scoreboard, surgical staging and the oncology MDT, chemotherapy and the PARP-inhibitor and immunotherapy era, the hereditary syndromes (BRCA1/2 and Lynch) that have converted family history into genetics-and-surgery decisions, and the palliative and sexual-and-body-image medicine that determines what surviving feels like. The clinical spine is simple: bleeding after the menopause is a phone call to a clinic within two weeks, a woman with ovarian symptoms that will not settle gets a CA125 and an ultrasound, and an abnormal cervical screen is a colposcopy question with a governance trail behind it.**

::: summary
- PMB is a two-week-wait symptom; endometrial thickness and sampling are the pathway, and chasing the missed appointment is as important as the scan.
- Ovarian cancer is diagnosed by persistence: new, frequent, non-specific symptoms over weeks in an over-50 woman get CA125 and ultrasound, and every epithelial case gets germline and tumour BRCA/HRD testing.
- Cervical cancer should not be a cause of death in screened populations; HPV-primary screening with test-of-cure is the technology and the register and follow-up are the medicine.
- Surgery at the right centre is a prognostic drug in ovarian cancer; molecular classification drives adjuvant decisions in endometrial; chemoradiation and brachytherapy plus rehabilitation define cervical care.
- Hereditary syndromes turn one patient into a family cohort - BRCA, Lynch - with surveillance and risk-reducing surgery decisions that must be counselled by time, not by leaflets.
- Prevention is the specialty's highest yield: HPV vaccination, obesity risk communication, and screening-uptake equity are the interventions with the largest numbers behind them.
:::
