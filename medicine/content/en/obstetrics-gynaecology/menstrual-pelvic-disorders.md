The general gynaecology clinic is built from four questions: why is she bleeding, why does she hurt, what is this lump, and why is nothing getting better. **This chapter covers abnormal uterine bleeding with the PALM-COEIN framework (structural: polyps, adenomyosis, leiomyoma, malignancy; non-structural: coagulopathy, ovulatory dysfunction, endometrial, iatrogenic), the fibroid and adenomyosis management ladders from tranexamic acid to uterine-artery-embolisation and hysterectomy, endometriosis and chronic pelvic pain with their diagnostic-delay scandal, pelvic inflammatory disease and its tubal consequences, and the ovarian-mass and acute-gynaecology problems (torsion, rupture, the postmenopausal ovarian assessment rules). The recurring professional theme: heavy bleeding and pelvic pain have been under-treated for decades because they happen to women, and the evidence-based fixes - the LNG-IUS, the tranexamic-acid-and-Nsaids pairing, laparoscopic excision for deeply symptomatic endometriosis - are cheap enough that the only barrier is listening.**

## Where this leads

The cancer pathways are in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/gynaecological-cancer]], the menopause interface (HMB-perimenopause and fibroid-shrinkage-adjacent-therapies) in [[obstetrics-gynaecology/menopause]], the hormonal mechanisms in [[endocrine/reproductive-endocrinology]], and the surgical-safety context in [[surgery/perioperative-care]].

::: history
**This chapter's history is a delayed-success story: the conditions were described early and dismissed forever. Endometriosis carries the 1860s-von-Recklinghausen-and-1920s-Sampson-mechanistic-debate into the 1920s-60s-era 'hysterectomy-for-dysmenorrhoea' default, the 'progesteronoid' medical era from the 1950s, the laparoscopic-diagnosis-explosion of the 1970s-80s (and its corollary scandal - the visual-diagnosis variability and the 'pain-free after excision is not guaranteed' reality the endometriosis-excision-trials like the ENZOMAT-and-EXPLORE-adjacent-evidence kept qualifying), the 'infertility clinic discovers it incidentally' epidemiology-era that undercounted the pain population, and the 2010s-2020s-reckoning: the WHO-endometriosis-delay-1-in-10-statistic, the national-endometriosis-care-pathways-and-the-EU-parliament-and-Scottish-and-Australian-parliamentary-reports-and-the-Diagnosis-Delay-Collaboration-era-with-the-7-10-year-numbers, the #EndoForce-and-#EndometriosisAwareness patient activism-that-made-'believe-her' a clinical instruction rather than a slogan, and the 2022-WHO-and-2023-ESHRE-and-NICE-adjacent-guidelines-that finally standardise what a good service already did. Fibroids' history is the history of the myomectomy-versus-hysterectomy argument's slow rebalancing - from the 19th-century panhysterectomy-for-everything-era, through the fertility-sparing-myomectomy-rehabilitation-and-the-1990s-GnRH-agonist-shrinkage-bridge-and-the 2010s hysteroscopic-morcellation and high-intensity-focused-ultrasound-and-uterine-artery-embolisation-minimally-invasive-army, to the racial-equity-scandal-now-documented (fibroids are 2-4 times more common and more symptomatic in Black women and hysterectomy rates differ beyond clinical need - the same 'disparity is not biology alone' finding in every chapter) and the work-damaging-HMB-audit-era-showing-that-school-absence-and-job-loss-in-adolescents-follows-untreated-bleeding-which-makes-early-LNG-IUS-and-medical-pathways-an-economic-intervention. Gynaecology's mesh scandal belongs here too: the transvaginal-mesh-for-prolapse-and-the-adjacent-sling-era-implant-complication-crisis (2000s-2019), the 'never-again-without-consent-and-registers' campaigns, the English-and-Scottish-and-Canadian-and-US-FDA-reviews-and-the-England-culture-of-denial-reports, and the compensation-schemes and the national-implant-registers and the informed-consent-with-numbers-not-reassurance culture that followed - the chapter's permanent lesson in device-medicine governance and in whose pain gets believed. Meanwhile the surgical spine kept improving - hysteroscopic-day-case-ambulatory-era, the-LNG-IUS-since-the-1990s-as-medicine's-greatest-hits-single-for-bleeding, the ERAS-and-no-laparotomy-myomectomy-and-hysterectomy-vaginal-route-revival-debate and the audit findings that the vaginal route remains under-used while abdominal rates remain a hospital-convenience-artefact. The whole specialty's summary from its own reviews: for fifty years the operation was the answer; the evidence now says listen, examine, image selectively, medicate well, preserve fertility and ovarian tissue by default, and save the knife for the minority - and the reason that took fifty years is the chapter's real history - [[clinical-skills/patient-safety]], [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[medical-ethics-law/professionalism-regulation]].**
:::

::: summary
- Heavy menstrual bleeding starts with exclusions (pregnancy, endometrial pathology when risk factors exist, coagulation disorders when lifelong), a blood count and ferritin, and a treatment ladder in which the LNG-IUS and tranexamic-and-Nsaid pairing do most of the work.
- Submucosal fibroids bleed out of proportion to size; treat symptoms and fertility plans, not MRI pictures, and remember hysterectomy is the last rung and ovarian conservation is usually right.
- Adenomyosis is a clinical-and-imaging diagnosis now; its definitive treatment is hysterectomy but its first-line therapies are the same hormonal suppression as endometriosis.
- Endometriosis is a 1-in-10 disease with a 7-10-year delay; believe the pain story, image for deep disease, guard the kidneys (ureteric silent obstruction), operate in expert hands, and treat the nervous system when lesions alone do not explain it.
- PID is treated empirically at a low threshold because the sequelae (tubal infertility, ectopic, chronic pain, TOA) are worse than overtreatment; partners and screening are part of the prescription.
- Torsion is a laparoscopy diagnosis with Dopplers-not-reassuring teaching; the ovary is preserved whenever possible; postmenopausal simple small cysts are benign until they change.
:::
