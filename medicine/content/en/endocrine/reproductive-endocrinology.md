Reproductive endocrinology asks where a hormone loop has broken - brain, gland, gonad, or target organ - and answers with a four-number logic: gonadotropins, sex steroid, anatomy, and time. **The same low testosterone or absent period means different diseases depending on whether LH is high or low, and the difference changes everything from the MRI list to the fertility conversation.**

This chapter covers the gonadal axis and its testing, amenorrhoea and PCOS, male hypogonadism and infertility, pubertal disorders, and gender-incongruence care.

## The axis

::: definition Gonadal physiology and the testing logic {#def-axis}
**The loop (pulsatile gonadotropin-releasing hormone from the hypothalamus drives LH and FSH from gonadotrophs; LH and FSH drive gonadal steroidogenesis and gametogenesis; oestradiol, progesterone and testosterone feed back on pituitary and hypothalamus, with oestradiol's switch from negative to positive feedback generating the mid-cycle LH surge - read with [[physiology/hormones]], [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]], and the cell signalling of steroid receptors in [[cell-biochemistry/signalling-cell-cycle]]); the female cycle (follicular recruitment and selection, dominant-follicle oestradiol rise, the surge, ovulation, luteal progesterone, and menstruation on luteal withdrawal - the [[#def-axis]] figure; the ovarian reserve decline and the menopausal transition - [[obstetrics-gynaecology/menopause]]); the male axis (tonic LH-testosterone with Sertoli-cell FSH support and inhibin B feedback, the high intratesticular steroid gradient that makes spermatogenesis possible, and why exogenous testosterone is a male contraceptive-adjacent fertility-hazard - [[psychiatry/substance-use]]-adjacent anabolic use); the shared embryological background (gonadal differentiation, Mullerian and Wolffian duct fate and androgen action - the disorders-of-sex-development map - [[anatomy/embryology]], [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[paediatrics/congenital-genetic]]). The testing logic (a low sex steroid with low-or-low-normal gonadotropins is central - brain or pituitary; with high gonadotropins it is gonadal failure; the prolactin and thyroid and cortisol exclusions before any gonadal label - [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]], [[endocrine/thyroid]], [[endocrine/adrenal]]; assay realities - testosterone timing-and-fraction and SHBG states and the oestradiol-assay limits in women and men alike - [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]; and imaging discipline - sellar MRI for central patterns, pelvic anatomy for amenorrhoea with secondary sexual characteristics present).)**
:::

::: summary
- Two numbers, testosterone or oestradiol with LH and FSH, localise almost every gonadal complaint; the third number, prolactin, clears the impostor.
- Amenorrhoea is an algorithm, not a hormone prescription: pregnancy, anatomy, gonadotropins, then the named diagnosis with its own fertility and bone consequences.
- PCOS is a lifetime metabolic-and-endometrial risk condition with a reproductive phase, not just a fertility obstacle.
- Male hypogonadism treatment before a fertility conversation is a foreseeable harm; exogenous testosterone suppresses spermatogenesis reliably.
- Pubertal timing disorders are mostly benign variants, but boys, under-eights, and virilised children deserve the tumour hunt.
- Gender-incongruence care is evidence-calibrated, fertility-aware, capacity-respecting medicine with lifelong monitoring responsibilities, not a one-prescription encounter.
:::
