Transplant and tumour immunology are one subject seen from two ends: **both are about whether the immune system decides a mass of cells belongs - rejection is immunity working as designed against an allograft, cancer immune escape is immunity failing against the tumour, and the therapies of one field (checkpoints, tolerance drugs, cell depletion) are the poisons and cures of the other.** This chapter covers rejection biology and management, transplantation immunogenetics, tumour immune editing and immunotherapy, and the iatrogenic syndromes (PTLD, GVHD, irAEs) where the two meet.

This chapter covers allograft rejection and immunosuppression, transplantation across the lifespan and organs, tumour immunology and immunotherapy, and the overlap syndromes.

::: summary
- Rejection is T-cell and antibody pathology against alloantigen; its timing (hyperacute, acute, chronic) tells you the mechanism and the therapy.
- Matching, crossmatching and antibody monitoring are the preventative medicine of transplantation; desensitisation makes previously impossible transplants possible with infection and rejection costs.
- Immunosuppression is a risk-trading exercise: rejection, opportunistic infection, malignancy, metabolic and organ toxicity - with BK, CMV and PTLD as its immunological signature lesions.
- Tumour immunotherapy works by releasing or redirecting immunity; its toxicities (irAEs, CRS, ICANS, B-cell aplasia) are tolerance and inflammation syndromes that other specialties must manage confidently.
- Checkpoint-inhibitor colitis, hepatitis, pneumonitis, myocarditis and endocrinopathies follow the same grading-and-steroid logic and must be documented permanently.
- The overlap states (GVHD, PTLD, rejection after HCT, chronic allograft injury) are the curriculum's integration test: one patient, two diseases, three teams.
