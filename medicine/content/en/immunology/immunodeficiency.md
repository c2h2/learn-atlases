Immunodeficiency is the clinical discipline of the unexpected infection: **the wrong organism, the wrong site, the wrong severity, the wrong frequency or the wrong outcome - and increasingly it is found in adults on immunomodulatory drugs rather than in children with rare genes, which means every physician needs the screening reflex, not just the immunologist.** This chapter covers the inborn errors, the acquired syndromes, the diagnostic algorithm and the replacement-and-definitive therapies.

This chapter covers antibody deficiency, combined and syndromic immunodeficiencies, phagocyte and complement defects, secondary immunodeficiency, and the diagnosis-to-treatment-to-transplant arc.

::: summary
- Suspect immunodeficiency from pattern, not single infections: unusual organism, site, severity, frequency, outcome or the need for IV antibiotics.
- Antibody deficiency presents with sinopulmonary bacterial infection and poor vaccine responses; measure immunoglobulins AND function, interpret against age ranges.
- Combined and T-cell defects present early with failure to thrive, opportunists, and vaccine complications; SCID is a transplant candidate found before infection.
- Secondary immunodeficiency from drugs and malignancy now outnumbers inborn error in adults: know your steroid, anti-CD20, JAK-inhibitor and bispecific-antibody risk tables.
- Asplenia and complement defects are meningococcal and pneumococcal emergencies waiting; vaccinate and educate before the fever.
- Treatment has three legs: infection prevention (vaccines, prophylaxis, immunoglobulin), immune reconstitution (HSCT, gene therapy, enzyme) and complication surveillance (lung, autoimmune, malignancy).
:::
