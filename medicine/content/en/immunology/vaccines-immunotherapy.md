Vaccines are the most cost-effective medical technology ever deployed and immunotherapy is the newest half of the same science: **both manipulate which immune responses the body prioritises - vaccines teach protective memory against chosen targets, allergen and autoimmune therapies teach restraint, and cancer immunotherapies release restraint - so this chapter teaches adjuvants and correlates of protection, programme design and hesitancy, and the therapeutic immune-modulation toolbox.**

This chapter covers vaccine immunology and platforms, programme delivery and hesitancy, adverse-event reasoning, allergen and autoimmune tolerance therapies, and cancer immunotherapy's interface with general medicine.

::: summary
- Vaccines work by buying germinal-centre time: adjuvants, platforms and schedules are all ways of controlling the quality and duration of that education.
- Platform choice is immunology plus logistics - live vaccines need permissiveness, subunits need adjuvants, mRNA needs cold chains and vectors need naive immune systems.
- Correlates of protection let programmes measure what matters; without them, efficacy is proven in trials and defended in surveillance.
- Adverse-event reasoning requires simultaneity-aware epidemiology: report signals, test them with registry designs, and communicate absolute risks honestly, including the risk of the disease.
- Hesitancy is a trust-and-invisible-risk phenomenon; the withdrawn fraudulent MMR-autism paper remains the field's cautionary tale on how fast harm from misinformation outlives retraction.
- Immune modulation now runs both directions clinically - tolerance therapy for allergy and autoimmunity, blockade of tolerance for cancer - and internists will manage the side effects of both.
:::
