Tolerance is the immune system's hardest problem: **it must never react to itself and to the harmless outside world (food, commensals, fetal antigens, inhaled antigen) while staying ready for the dangerous one, and the machinery that achieves this - central deletion, peripheral anergy and regulation, immune-privilege sites and tolerogenic tissues - is the same machinery whose failure produces autoimmunity, allergy and graft rejection, and whose exploitation now treats cancer.**

This chapter covers central and peripheral tolerance, regulatory cells and molecules, maternal-fetal and mucosal tolerance, and the clinical tolerance failures and therapies.

**Tolerance is the concept immunology needed before it could explain anything: the founding experiments - Owen's 1945 cattle-twin blood-group chimerism observation and Medawar-and-Billingham-and-Gibson's skin-graft-tolerant-mice demonstration (the wartime Edinburgh bomb-injury-grafting programme that made transplant immunology a discipline and the Nobel that followed) and Burnet-and-Fenner's textbook formulation of self-recognition clonally, with their self/non-self model and its modern critics noting that the body spends most of its immunology tolerating its own microbiome; the thymic-era - the Miller's thymectomy experiments proving the thymus educates lymphocytes and the Steinman-and-Cohn dendritic-cell discovery and Kaplan's and Lokk and the medullary-mapping era; the molecular era - the autoimmune-regulator gene (AIRE) positional-cloning-of-APECED-financially-backed-by-the-Finnish-family-registries - the 1997 international collaboration and the NOD-mouse-autoimmunity-loci (Idd) dissection and the CTLA-4-and-PD-1-and-FOXP3-biology-from-mice (the Sakaguchi, Bluestone and Rudensky Treg lines) to the human IPEX-and-CTLA4-haploinsufficiency-diagnoses - [[immunology/adaptive-responses]], [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]; the regulation-to-medicine arc - the CTLA4-Ig-abatacept-in-rheumatoid-arthritis (the "give back the missing brake" concept from the shared-epitope and mouse-arthritis work of the Winchester and Strik and the Worcester-and-Stanford groups), the low-dose-IL-2-Treg trials, the antigen-specific-immunotherapy tradition from Noon and Freeman's pollen-extracts of 1911 (the "inject what kills to cure" desensitisation whose mechanism - blocking-antibody-and-Treg-and-depletion-of-Th2 - was only understood decades later) and the oral-tolerance lineage from the 1911? Chase's-hapten-fed-guinea-pigs-work to the LEAP-peanut-early-introduction-trials of the 2010s that flipped a generation of advice - [[immunology/hypersensitivity]], [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]]; and the checkpoint paradox - Allison's-CTLA-4-tumour-rejection-experiments-and-Honjo's-PD-1-ligand-discovery and the instant-translation into oncology, whose autoimmune side effects (the diabetes and hypophysitis above) taught the decade that tolerance and tumour immunity are one dial, and the ongoing attempt to turn it down selectively (the engineered-tolerogenic-cell-therapies and the selective-costimulatory-blockade trials) - [[immunology/transplant-tumour-immunology]], [[pharmacology/drug-development-prescribing]].**
:::

::: summary
- Self-tolerance is built in two factories: the thymus deletes and diverts (AIRE, Treg generation), and the periphery maintains (anergy, Tregs, checkpoints, privileged sites).
- The human diseases name the mechanism: APECED for AIRE, IPEX for FOXP3, ALPS for Fas-pathway death, APECED-spectrum for cytokine autoantibodies.
- Pregnancy, mucosa and microbiota are not accidents but engineered tolerogenic interfaces; most of your immunology happens there.
- Co-stimulatory and checkpoint molecules are the same drugs' targets in three diseases: transplants (abatacept), tumours (ipilimumab, pembrolizumab) and autoimmunity (low-dose IL-2).
- Checkpoint inhibitors convert tolerance biology into clinical endocrinology, gastroenterology, pneumonology and neurology - every organ's immunology comes for consultation.
- Tolerance therapies for autoimmunity remain partly hope: antigen-specific and cell-based approaches are promising; blanket immunosuppression is the blunt current tool.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Explain allergy out of the family {level=1}
Parents of a 2-year-old with eczema ask whether they should avoid peanuts, eggs and dairy "like the old advice said". Advise with evidence.
::: solution
**From-avoidance-to-controlled-exposure ([[#def-interfaces]], [[immunology/hypersensitivity]], [[paediatrics/immunisation]]): the history - the LEAP randomised trial and the follow-up showing early sustained peanut consumption reduced allergy in high-risk infants and the ecological and interventional data for eggs and the general "no evidence for maternal exclusion diets and no benefit from prolonged avoidance" conclusion, with the honest limits (the population-generalisability and the different-food extrapolations and the existing-allergy-child-needs-different-advice) - [[epidemiology-ebm/randomised-trials]]; the practical prescription - the age-and-texture-and-frequency-and-safety instructions (the thinned-not-choked peanut butter and the egg-cooking and the start-small-watch-wait and the continue-regularly rule) and when to seek allergy assessment first (the existing severe eczema or IgE-mediated reactions needing supervised feeding or oral-immunotherapy protocols and the skin-prick-and-component-testing-selection criteria and the hospital-vs-home-first-feed debate) - [[paediatrics/growth-development]]; the risk framing - why untreated eczema itself drives allergic-progression and why barrier treatment and emollients and the food-introduction conversation belong in one consultation and the breastfeeding-and-hygiene-and-pets-and-daycare-and-antibiotic topics defused with the strength of each evidence line; and the equity and access layer - the food-cost-and-culture-and-allergy-service-wait-times and the supervised-feeding-access-for-the-high-risk-infants and the nursery-and-grandparent-education that keeps the tolerance going - [[public-health/determinants-inequalities]], [[public-health/prevention-promotion]].**

::: exercise Manage the rituximab-treated pregnant {level=2}
A 31-year-old with severe lupus nephritis received rituximab at 10 weeks' gestation (unplanned); she is now 24 weeks, in remission, and terrified about the baby.
::: solution
**B-cell-depletion-across-the-placenta ([[#def-peripheral]], [[#def-interfaces]], [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]): the physiology - IgG is actively transported via FcRn mainly in the third trimester, so rituximab (anti-CD20 IgG1) exposure falls on the fetus if given early but causes neonatal B-cell depletion when given late, and the B-cell-recovery-in-the-infant timeline and the no-live-vaccines-for-the-infant-for-6-months rule (the rotavirus and BCG and varicella implications) and the infection-surveillance plan for the neonate - [[paediatrics/newborn]], [[public-health/vaccination-programmes]]; the pregnancy medicine - the hydroxychloroquine-continue rule (it protects against flares) and the pregnancy-compatible agents list (azathioprine and ciclosporin and tacrolimus and the mycophenolate-and-methotrexate-and-cyclophosphamide-teratogen-withdrawal-rules) and the pre-eclampsia-and-FGR and preterm surveillance schedule and the anti-Ro-La-and-antiphospholipid testing that changes the neonatal and placental management - [[musculoskeletal/connective-tissue-vasculitis]], [[immunology/autoimmunity]] and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-complications]]; the delivery and lactation - the B-cell-count-and-immunoglobulin-in-cord-or-neonatal-blood-and-the-infection-threshold-and-IVIG-if-symptomatic-hypogammaglobulinaemia and the breastfeeding-safe-agent-table (rituximab-transfer-in-milk-is-minimal-and-oral-degradation-argues-low-risk) and the maternal-flare-post-partum-risk-management; and the documents - the shared-care plan (obstetric-medicine-and-rheumatology and neonatology and pharmacy) and the infant-card saying "rituximab-exposed: no live vaccines until X month" that prevents harm at the child-health clinic in a different town from the one that treated the mother - [[clinical-skills/records-handover]].**
