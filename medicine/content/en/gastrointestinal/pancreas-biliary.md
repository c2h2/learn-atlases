The pancreas and biliary tree present the surgical abdomen's most polite disease (biliary colic) and its most savage (severe acute pancreatitis), plus the most lethal common cancer. The anatomy does the teaching: **a gland that digests itself when its duct obstructs, and a bile river whose three tributaries (gallbladder, cystic duct, common bile duct) deliver stones into the same ampulla that drains the pancreatic duct — Opie's "common channel" of 1901 still explains half the admissions.**

This chapter covers gallstone disease and its complications, acute and chronic pancreatitis, autoimmune IgG4 disease, pancreatic and biliary cancers, and cystic lesions.

## Stones

::: definition Gallstones and cholecystitis {#def-stones}
**Stone formation (cholesterol stones from supersaturated, nucleating, stasis-prone bile — the lithogenic triad — with the oestrogen-and-fibrate-and-obesity-and-rapid-weight-loss-and-ileal-resection risk stack, pigment stones from bilirubin load in haemolysis and cirrhosis, and the enteric-bacteria-deconjugating-brown-stone of stasis — [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]]'s bile-acid circulation and [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]'s bilirubin fractions; [[blood/haemoglobin-disorders]]'s pigment logic and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]]'s hormonal slowdown and [[endocrine/diabetes]]'s fibrates-and-weight-loss-clinics [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]). Biliary colic (a stone temporarily impacts the cystic duct — a rising, non-colicky, steady right-upper-quadrant-or-epigastric pain over 30 minutes to hours with nausea and a fatty-meal association that is more folklore than physics; normal examination between attacks, normal bloods — the "don't-call-it-cholecystitis" rule — [[clinical-skills/history-taking]]); work-up (ultrasound first — the shadowing-stone-and-non-filling-cystic-duct reading with its sensitivity for stones but not for the complications — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]); management (elective laparoscopic cholecystectomy counselled against the natural history of recurrence-and-complications; the "wait-and-see-only-if-asymptomatic-incidental-stones" corollary — [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]]). Acute cholecystitis (the impacted stone plus inflammation plus secondary infection: sustained pain, fever, Murphy sign, guarding, raised CRP and neutrophils; the severity grading of the Tokyo guidelines; ultrasound with the wall-thickening, fluid, sonographic-Murphy and non-perfusing-wall features; HIDA when the diagnosis matters-and-remains-uncertain — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]; the organisms — enteric and anaerobic cover per local resistance — [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[microbiology/laboratory-diagnosis]]); management (antibiotics-and-analgesia-and-early-laparoscopic-cholecystectomy within the admission — the trial evidence for early-versus-delayed — [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]]; the percutaneous-or-endoscopic-cholecystostomy gallbladder-drainage option for the unfit-with-severe-disease and the drain-removal-and-recurrence arithmetic; the gangrene-perforation-emphysematous-cholecystitis escalation [[surgery/acute-abdomen]] and the Mirizzi-stricture trap — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]-own [[#def-stones]] row). **Choledocholithiasis and its presentations (the stone passing into the common bile duct — pain, then jaundice with the conjugated pattern and pale stools — the conjugated pattern of [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]; the risk stratification combining bilirubin, duct diameter, liver enzymes, and the stone-on-scan; investigation by probability: MRCP or EUS for intermediate, ERCP-with-clearance for high-probability and therapeutic intent — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]]; the ERCP risks — pancreatitis, perforation, bleeding and cholangitis, and the consent arithmetic — [[medical-ethics-law/consent-capacity]]); ascending cholangitis (the obstructed-infected-bile emergency: Charcot's triad and the Reynolds pentad with shock-and-confusion; blood-cultures-and-antibiotics-and-urgent-biliary-drainage-ERCP-or-PTBD-or-surgical; the sepsis-bundle resuscitation — [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]], [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]], [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]'s jaundice section); and the post-cholecystectomy syndromes (retained-and-recurrent-duct-stones, the cystic-duct-stump, bile-acid-diarrhoea — [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]]'s colonic-bile-acid-mechanism — sphincter-of-Odder-disorder and the functional-pain-overlap — [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]'s gut-brain framing).**
:::

## The self-digesting gland

::: definition Acute pancreatitis {#def-pancreatitis}
**Aetiology (gallstones and alcohol dominate; then hypertriglyceridaemia (usually above ~20 mmol/L — the chylomicronaemia syndromes and the insulin-and-fibrate-and-alcohol drivers — [[cell-biochemistry/lipid-amino-acid-metabolism]], [[endocrine/diabetes]]), post-ERCP, drugs (azathioprine-and-thiazide-and-valproate-and-metronidazole lists — [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]), hypercalcaemia, autoimmune IgG4 disease ([[#def-other-pancreas]]), trauma-and-venom-and-infections ([[infectious-diseases/tropical-infections]]), hereditary-and-cystic-fibrosis-and-anatomical-variants (pancreas divisum, choledochal cyst — [[anatomy/embryology]]), and the recurrent-idiopathic work-up (genetics, IgG4, triglycerides, occult stones on EUS) — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]). Pathophysiology (premature intracellular trypsinogen activation — the enterokinase-analog switch that should only happen in the lumen — then acinar injury, inflammatory cascades and a capillary-leak SIRS that can progress to multi-organ failure; the abdominal-compartment-syndrome consequence of third-spacing — [[physiology/homeostasis-body-fluids]], [[immunology/innate-immunity]], [[cell-biochemistry/proteins-enzymes]]'s zymogen chemistry, [[renal/acute-kidney-injury]]'s hypovolaemic hit, and the pleural-effusion-and-ARDS respiratory sequel ([[respiratory/gas-exchange]], [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]])). Diagnosis (two-of-three: characteristic pain, lipase above three-fold upper-normal (amylase's narrower window and non-pancreatic causes — [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]), imaging features; the day-one question is "why" — the gallstone-cause hunt (LFTs-and-ultrasound) because it changes the admission plan; the early CT question is answered "not routinely" — contrast CT is for diagnostic doubt and for the week-two-plus necrosis read, revised-Atlanta logic). Severity (the score families — the Ranson-and-Balthazar-and-APACHE-legacy versus the bedside BISAP and CRP-slope approaches — and the one definition that matters: persistent organ failure beyond 48 hours equals severe disease — [[#def-pancreatitis]]; the fluid-responsiveness-and-creatinine-and-haematocrit trajectory as the real-time read — [[renal/water-sodium]]). Management of the first 48 hours (analgesia-and-oxygen-and-monitored-volume-replacement with moderate-targeted crystalloid — the aggressive-fluidization-harms lesson of the WATERFALL era, balanced against hypovolaemia — [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]], [[cardiovascular/heart-failure]]; early oral feeding as tolerated — nil-by-mouth-dogma-reversed-by-trials, [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]; antibiotics NOT prophylactic — only for proven-and-suspected-infection and infected necrosis — [[microbiology/antimicrobials]], [[infectious-diseases/stewardship-outbreaks]]; the ERCP-induction-rule (emergency ERCP only for concomitant cholangitis or persistent biliary obstruction — the negative-trial-honesty on routine early ERCP — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]-own [[#def-stones]]); and the ICU triggers (persistent organ failure, abdominal-compartment-syndrome monitoring and the laparotomy-for-compartment-syndrome-not-for-the-pancreas principle — [[emergency-critical-care/critical-care]], [[surgery/acute-abdomen]]). Local complications and their calendar (the peripancreatic-fluid-collection-then-acute-necrosis-then-walled-off-necrosis timeline; the pseudocyst-versus-WON-versus-abscess distinction with the duct-disruption question; splenic-and-portal-vein thrombosis with its varices-and-splenomegaly sequelae (left-sided portal hypertension with gastric varices — [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]], [[blood/thrombosis-anticoagulation]]); the pseudoaneurysm bleed (the sentinel-haemorrhage rule and angio-embolisation — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]); the pancreatic-ascites and fistula; and the step-up management of infected necrosis (defer-and-drain-then-endoscopic-or-minimally-invasive-necrosectomy — the PANTER-and-TENSION-trial architecture, the drain-versus-surgery sequencing and the antifungal-versus-bacterial-debate — [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]], [[microbiology/fungi-parasites]]). The long tail (exocrine insufficiency with steatorrhoea and the faecal-elastase confirm — [[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]]'s lipid-digestion rule; pancreatogenic type-3c diabetes with its brittle-microangiopathic-paradox and enzyme-coordinated-insulin problem — [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]]; chronic-pain-and-narcrosis-and-quality-of-life-burden — [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]]; and the cancer-association vigilance for new-onset-diabetes-and-weight-loss-in-an-older-smoker — [[#def-cancers]]).**
:::

::: definition Chronic pancreatitis and IgG4 disease {#def-other-pancreas}
**Chronic pancreatitis (the recurrent-injury-fibrosis-ductal-disease endpoint: alcohol-and-smoking as drivers with the smoking-dose-response-and-cessation-payoff, genetic (hereditary trypsinogen and SPINK and CFTR-related disease), tropical nutrition-associated and obstructive-and-autoimmune causes — [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[public-health/global-health]], [[psychiatry/substance-use]]); the pain syndrome (ductal hypertension, intrapancreatic-nerve inflammation and central sensitisation — the reason nociceptive analgesia fails and the multimodal-chronic-pain-framing applies ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]-adjacent-nutrition-and-alcohol-programmes, [[psychiatry/anxiety-disorders]]-adjacent-pain-psychology, and the [[nervous-system/headache-pain]]-style-neuromodulator logic)); structural consequences (calcification and ductal stones and strictures, the pseudocyst-and-biliary-and-portal-vein compression syndromes, and the exocrine-endocrine failure of [[#def-pancreatitis]]-long-tail); investigation (the plain-film-and-CT-calcification reading, MRCP-secretin ductal morphology, EUS for early disease with its observer-dependence, and the function tests-faecal-elastase-serum-enzyme-late-pattern — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]); treatment ladder (alcohol-and-smoking-cessation as the only disease-modifying steps; diet-and-enzyme-and-antacid-co-therapy and fat-soluble-vitamin-and-bone surveillance ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[endocrine/bone-calcium]]); analgesic-and-neuromodulator strategy with opioid-harm-awareness ([[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]], [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]); endotherapy for ductal stones-and-strictures (ESWL lithotripsy and dilation and stenting — [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]]); surgical options for the intractable (drainage — the lateral pancreaticojejunostomy of Puestow and the Frey-and-Beger-coring-procedures — versus resection (distal and head-focused), and the total-pancreatectomy-with-islet-autotransplant last-resort with its diabetes certainty — [[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]], [[endocrine/diabetes]]); and the cancer-risk context (chronic-pancreatitis as risk, hereditary-trypsinogen-variants-among-highest — [[genetics/cancer-genetics]]). **Autoimmune pancreatitis (IgG4-related: the painless-jaundice-sausage-shaped-swollen-pancreas-with-capsular-rim and the "cancer-mimic" that generates unnecessary Whipple procedures when unrecognised; raised serum IgG4 but not perfectly, the other-organ membership (sclerosing cholangitis — [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]'s PSC-mimic point, salivary-and-lacrimal, retroperitoneal fibrosis — [[renal/urinary-tract]], renal-and-pulmonary-and-lymph-node lesions — [[immunology/autoimmunity]], [[pathology/diagnostic-pathology]]'s storiform-fibrosis-and-IgG4-plasma-cell histology); the dramatic steroid response with relapse-on-taper-and-steroid-sparing-rituximab-and-azathioprine options — [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]; and the mandatory-cancer-exclusion-before-that-label — [[#def-cancers]]).**
:::

::: example The swollen sausage {#ex-ig4}
A 67-year-old man has 6 weeks of painless jaundice and 6 kg weight loss; CT shows a diffusely enlarged "sausage-shaped" pancreas with a capsule-like rim, irregular intra- and extra-hepatic duct strictures, and enlarged salivary glands; bilirubin 128, IgG4 elevated at 5x, CA19-9 mildly raised, no mass on EUS.
::: solution
**IgG4-related autoimmune pancreatitis with associated sclerosing cholangitis — the classic cancer-mimic ([[#def-other-pancreas]], [[#def-cancers]]): the discriminators (diffuse rather than mass-forming disease, the capsule rim, the salivary and biliary multi-organ membership, elevated IgG4 with its imperfect specificity, and the absence of a cytology-positive lesion — [[pathology/cancer-diagnosis]], [[immunology/autoimmunity]]); the mandatory-cancer-exclusion-before-steroids (EUS-sampling-and-brush-cytology-and-follow-up-imaging discipline — [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]], [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]'s cholestatic-workup [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]); the steroid-induction-and-taper protocol with the relapse-on-withdrawal statistics and the rituximab-and-azathioprine-steroid-sparing tier ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]-adjacent); the biliary-drainage-question for cholangitis-or-deep-jaundice ([[#def-stones]]); the other-organ screen (retroperitoneal-fibrosis-with-ureteric-hydronephrosis — [[renal/urinary-tract]], renal-and-pulmonary-and-pituitary-lesions — [[endocrine/hypothalamus-pituitary]], [[respiratory/interstitial-vascular-disease]]); and the re-bleep rule (failure-to-melt-in-2-weeks-inverts-the-diagnosis-back-to-cancer — [[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]]).**
:::

::: summary
- Symptomatic gallstones mean cholecystectomy; duct stones mean risk-stratified MRCP/EUS/ERCP; cholangitis means antibiotics plus urgent drainage, and sepsis care.
- Acute pancreatitis is managed by hour-by-hour physiology: moderate fluids, early feeding, no prophylactic antibiotics, ERCP only for cholangitis or obstruction, CT for the complications calendar.
- Infected necrosis is treated by the step-up route at the latest feasible moment; drainage first, necrosectomy only if needed.
- Chronic pancreatitis management starts with alcohol-and-smoking cessation (the only disease-modifying drugs in the regimen), then pain-multimodal, endotherapy for ducts, and surgery for the intractable.
- Painless jaundice in an older adult is pancreatic cancer until the pancreas-protocol CT says otherwise; IgG4 disease is the mimic that imaging, serology and steroid-response logic must exclude before resection.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Sort the pain {level=1}
Give the one distinguishing feature and the key test for: biliary colic, acute cholecystitis, ascending cholangitis, gallstone pancreatitis.
::: solution
**Colic: transient self-relieving pain, normal bloods and exam between attacks, ultrasound for stones ([[#def-stones]]); cholecystitis: sustained pain with fever-and-Murphy-and-CRP, ultrasound wall-and-sonographic-Murphy findings and Tokyo-grading ([[#def-stones]]); cholangitis: jaundice-fever-shock with the conjugated pattern and duct dilation, blood cultures and emergency-decompression ([[#def-stones]], [[infectious-diseases/fever-sepsis]]); gallstone pancreatitis: pain with lipase above 3x normal and the LFT-and-ultrasound cause-hunt plus the cholangitis-ERCP decision ([[#def-pancreatitis]]).**
:::
:::

::: exercise Choose fluids, wisely {level=1}
"Aggressive fluids save pancreatitis patients" — update the slogan with the modern evidence and the physiology.
::: solution
**Moderate, targeted, reassessed ([[#def-pancreatitis]]): the capillary-leak-and-third-spacing physiology demands volume early ([[physiology/homeostasis-body-fluids]], [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]]), but over-resuscitation drives pulmonary oedema, abdominal compartment pressure and worse outcomes (the aggressive-versus-moderate randomised evidence); the titration targets (heart-rate-and-lactate-and-creatinine-and-urine-output-with-regular-reassessment-not-fixed-litre-prescriptions); and the heart-and-kidney comorbidity adjustments ([[cardiovascular/heart-failure]], [[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]]).**
:::
