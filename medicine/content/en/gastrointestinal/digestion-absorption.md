The gut's job, stated as engineering: **move 9 L of water and about 1 kg of substrate daily through a seven-metre bioreactor, digest polymers to monomers at the right address, absorb them across one-cell-thick epithelium, and exclude the 10¹⁴ microbes riding the outside of that epithelium — all under dual neural and hormonal control.** Nearly every clinical GI syndrome — diarrhoea, malabsorption, acid disease — is one of these machines failing; this chapter is the physiology that makes the rest of the course readable.

This chapter covers motility and secretion control, digestion and absorption, and the diarrhoea-malabsorption syndromes.

## Moving and secreting

::: definition Motility: the wall, the wiring and the patterns {#def-motility}
**The gut wall (mucosa, submucosa, muscularis and serosa — [[anatomy/abdomen]]) carries the enteric nervous system: the myenteric (Auerbach) plexus driving motility and the submucosal (Meissner) plexus driving secretion and local blood flow, with 200-600 million neurons operating semi-autonomously under parasympathetic (vagal and pelvic, excitatory — the [[physiology/autonomic-nervous-system]] "rest-and-digest" amplification) and sympathetic (inhibitory, sphincter-tonic) modulation, and depending on the interstitial cells of Cajal as pacemakers (the slow-wave generator — and the tumour of the same lineage, the GIST, in [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]]) and on the syncytial smooth muscle ([[physiology/muscle]]).** The patterns: **peristalsis (the enteric "law of the gut" reflex — contraction above, relaxation below a bolus — and the swallowing cascade with its sphincter coordination — [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]); segmentation for mixing; the migrating motor complex (the fasting housekeeper, motilin-linked — the pre-breakfast ripples, and the gastroparesis of diabetic autonomic neuropathy — [[endocrine/diabetes-complications]] and [[nervous-system/neuromuscular]]); receptive relaxation and accommodation (the fundus as reservoir — the pressure-volume curve behind early satiety — [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]); and the gastrocolic reflex, ileal brake and defecation reflex (the rectal sampling reflex distinguishing gas from content, and cortically trained continence — [[paediatrics/growth-development]]'s toileting physiology and [[anatomy/pelvis-perineum]]). The sphincters (upper and lower oesophageal, pyloric, ileocaecal, and the paired internal-involuntary and external-voluntary anal sphincters — [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]-adjacent pudendal control) gate each compartment, and each has its disease: the lower oesophageal sphincter of achalasia, the pylorus of infantile stenosis ([[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]]), and the anal sphincters of obstetric injury ([[obstetrics-gynaecology/labour-birth]] and [[obstetrics-gynaecology/menstrual-pelvic-disorders]]).**
:::

::: definition Secretion: saliva, acid, bile, pancreatic juice and the gut hormones {#def-secretion}
**Saliva (1-1.5 L/day — serous and mucous acini under autonomic control (the sympathetic thick-dry versus parasympathetic watery paradox), carrying amylase, lipase, IgA, lysozyme and the enamel-protection proteins — [[immunology/adaptive-responses]] and [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]).** **Gastric juice (1.5-2 L/day of acid at a 160 mmol/L lumen: the parietal cell H⁺/K⁺-ATPase with its carbonic anhydrase chemistry, canalicular amplification and mitochondrial load ([[cell-biochemistry/membranes-transport]], and the distal nephron's proton pump as cousin — [[renal/tubular-function]]); three converging inputs — acetylcholine at M3, histamine from the ECL cell at H2, gastrin from the antral G cell at CCK2 with its pH feedback — which is why pump-blockers alone close all three ([[pharmacology/drug-receptor]]'s final-common-pathway example, and the meal-timed PPI design of [[#def-secretion]]); pepsinogen autoactivation below pH 5; and the mucus-bicarbonate-epithelial-prostaglandin defence (COX-1-derived PGE₂ and PGI₂ sustaining mucus, bicarbonate and perfusion — the NSAID ulcer in one sentence: [[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]], [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]], and the same prostaglandin logic as [[renal/filtration]]'s afferent tone)).** **Pancreatic juice (1-2 L/day: the acinar cells secrete enzyme (zymogen granules; trypsinogen-cholecystokinin-vagal control — [[cell-biochemistry/proteins-enzymes]]), while the duct cells secrete the bicarbonate-rich fluid through CFTR-dependent mechanisms under secretin — the single channel whose failure produces the cystic fibrosis pancreas, lung, sweat and meconium-ileus phenotype at once ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]], [[respiratory/respiratory-infections]], [[paediatrics/congenital-genetic]]); the enteropeptidase-trypsin cascade and its premature-activation question sit in [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]).** **Bile (hepatic cholesterol-to-bile-acid synthesis at the CYP7A1 step with FXR-FGF19 feedback — [[gastrointestinal/liver-function]]; canalicular transporter secretion whose defects are the familial cholestases — [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]; gallbladder concentration and CCK-driven ejection ([[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]); and the enterohepatic circulation reabsorbing ~95 % of the pool at the terminal ileum ([[renal/tubular-function]]'s transporter kinetics again) — the basis of ursodeoxycholic acid therapy and of the bile-acid diarrhoea and gallstone risk after ileal resection ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]] and [[#ex-stones-ileum]]).)** **The gut hormones (the diffuse endocrine-cell system — gastrin, CCK, secretin, GIP, motilin, GLP-1, PYY — from Starling and Bayliss' 1902 secretin that coined "hormone" ([[physiology/hormones]]); the incretin effect now therapeutics ([[endocrine/diabetes]]); the hormone-secreting tumour syndromes — gastrinoma, glucagonoma, VIPoma, carcinoid ([[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]] and [[pathology/cancer-diagnosis]]); and the gut-brain signalling of satiety and emesis (the vagal afferents and the chemoreceptor trigger zone — [[geriatrics-palliative/symptom-control]]-adjacent antiemetic pharmacology).)**
:::

## Digesting and absorbing

::: definition The chemistry and transport of macronutrients {#def-digest}
**The fluid budget: about 9 L enters the small bowel daily (1-2 L ingested, ~7 L secreted), ~99 % is reabsorbed — bulk jejunal osmotic and solvent-drag absorption (the Starling pairing of [[renal/filtration]]), ileal and colonic salvage down to ~1.5 L ([[renal/water-sodium]]) — so a few litres mis-handled is clinical diarrhoea ([[#def-diarrhoea]]).** **Carbohydrate: amylase to disaccharides, then brush-border maltase-glucoamylase, sucrase-isomaltase and lactase (whose developmental down-regulation is the world's commonest normal variant, and lactase persistence the regulatory-variant success — [[genetics/common-disease-genetics]]); glucose and galactose exit via SGLT1 then basolateral GLUT2 ([[cell-biochemistry/membranes-transport]]), fructose via GLUT5; unabsorbed sugar holds osmotic water and ferments — the lactose-breath-test principle ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]).** **Protein: pepsin plus the pancreatic protease cascade (enterokinase activating trypsinogen — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]) to di- and tripeptides absorbed by PepT1 plus free amino acids by sodium- and hydrogen-coupled systems; the rare transport diseases read across organs — cystinuria as both stone and transport lesson ([[renal/urinary-tract]]) and Hartnup disease as pellagra-with-tryptophan-loss ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]] and [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]).** **Lipid: the most secretion-dependent macronutrient — bile-acid micelles plus pancreatic lipase and colipase, enterocyte re-esterification and chylomicron assembly on apoB-48 ([[cell-biochemistry/lipid-amino-acid-metabolism]] and [[cardiovascular/vascular-disease]]), lymphatic lacteal transport (hence chylous ascites and protein-losing enteropathy when it obstructs — [[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]]); medium-chain triglycerides bypass the micelle step — the short-bowel formula lesson ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]); and pancreatic insufficiency therefore causes steatorrhoea first ([[#def-diarrhoea]]).** **Micronutrient addresses (the deficiency-map of [[#def-diarrhoea]]): iron at the duodenum (DMT1, ferroportin, and hepcidin as master switch — [[blood/anaemia]] and [[immunology/innate-immunity]]), calcium with vitamin D in duodenum-jejunum ([[endocrine/bone-calcium]]), folate in jejunum, and B12-intrinsic-factor at the terminal ileum ([[blood/anaemia]] and [[#ex-b12]]).** **And the barrier: tight junctions, mucus layers, secretory IgA and Peyer's patches ([[immunology/adaptive-responses]]), and the microbiota-mucosa interface — short-chain fatty acids from fermented fibre as colonocyte fuel, dysbiosis after antibiotics, and the gut-lung and gut-brain signalling literature ([[microbiology/microbiota]]).**
:::

::: example Osmotic versus secretory {#ex-diarrhoea-types}
Two watery diarrhoeas. (a) A 24-year-old after large milk servings: stool osmolality 290 mOsm/kg, Na 40, K 40; symptoms stop with fasting. (b) A returning traveller: stool osmolality 295, Na 140, K 20, output ~1 L/hour, persisting fasting.
::: solution
**The arithmetic is the diagnosis ([[#def-diarrhoea]]): (a) osmotic gap = 290 − 2×(40+40) = 90 — high: unmeasured osmotically active sugar; lactase deficiency, stops on the fasting test, acidic fermenting stool ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]). (b) gap = 295 − 2×160 = 15 — near zero, sodium-dominated, fasting-persistent: secretory. Cholera's ADP-ribosylation locks Gs-cAMP onto open CFTR ([[microbiology/bacterial-pathogenesis]] and [[cell-biochemistry/signalling-cell-cycle]]) — the "toxin-paralyses-absorption" story is half-myth: SGLT1 stays workable, which is exactly what oral rehydration exploits (below, in [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]). The same channel forms the bookend with cystic fibrosis ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]). Consequences to manage: the potassium and bicarbonate losses ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]] and [[renal/acid-base-disorders]]), the shock risk ([[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]]), stool-culture-and-notification obligations ([[microbiology/laboratory-diagnosis]] and [[public-health/communicable-disease-control]]), and the travel-medicine prevention conversation ([[public-health/global-health]]).**
:::
:::

::: definition Diarrhoea and malabsorption as syndromes {#def-diarrhoea}
**Diarrhoea (≥3 loose stools or >200 g/day): acute (<14 days — infectious, drug-induced ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]'s metformin-PPI-laxative-magnesium-chemotherapy list)), persistent, or chronic (≥4 weeks). The four-mechanism taxonomy: osmotic (stool gap high, fasting-responsive — sugars, malabsorption, magnesium); secretory (gap low, fasting-persistent — toxins, bile-acid malabsorption ([[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]), neuroendocrine tumours (VIP, gastrin, carcinoid — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]), and laxative abuse ([[psychiatry/eating-personality-disorders]])); inflammatory (blood-and-pus, fever, calprotectin-lactoferrin screening, histology — the IBD work-up of [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]] and [[pathology/diagnostic-pathology]]); and disordered motility (diabetic and post-surgical and post-infectious, thyrotoxic — [[endocrine/thyroid]]), with the nocturnal pattern as organic red flag.** Red flags routing to urgent investigation (blood, weight loss, nocturnal symptoms, new onset over 50, family history of cancer, immunosuppression, fever, recent antibiotics — the [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]] pathways and the diagnostic-delay audits of [[public-health/screening]]) versus IBS-pattern management ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]'s Rome criteria and the therapeutic trials of [[#ex-stones-ileum]] and [[#def-diarrhoea]]). Investigations ladder ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]): **stool (culture, C difficile, parasites, ova, microsporidia in immunosuppression — [[microbiology/laboratory-diagnosis]], [[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]]), blood (coeliac serology with total IgA, FBC, ferritin, B12-folate, TFTs, HIV — [[infectious-diseases/hiv]]), faecal elastase, breath hydrogen-methane (SIBO and carbohydrate malabsorption — [[microbiology/microbiota]]), and endoscopy with targeted biopsies.** Treatment by mechanism, with rehydration the mortality-reducer everywhere ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]], [[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]]'s ORS-and-zinc plan, and [[public-health/global-health]]'s cholera and rotavirus programmes).** Malabsorption (steatorrhoea, weight loss, fat-soluble-vitamin deficiency — bleeding from K ([[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]]), osteomalacia ([[endocrine/bone-calcium]]), night-blindness ([[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]) — plus the anaemias of iron, folate and B12 ([[blood/anaemia]])): the three-tier cause-hunt — **luminal (pancreatic insufficiency with low faecal elastase ([[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]); bile-acid deficiency from obstruction or ileal disease ([[gastrointestinal/liver-disease]] and [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]); small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth ([[microbiology/microbiota]]); rare enzyme and trafficking diseases ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]])); mucosal (coeliac disease — tTG with total IgA, HLA-DQ2/DQ8, villous atrophy, dermatitis herpetiformis ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]] and [[musculoskeletal/skin]]); HIV enteropathy and giardiasis and tropical sprue ([[infectious-diseases/hiv]] and [[microbiology/fungi-parasites]] and [[public-health/global-health]]); drug enteropathies including the olmesartan mimic ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]); radiation, amyloid, Whipple, mast-cell and eosinophilic disorders ([[pathology/chronic-inflammation-repair]] and [[microbiology/bacteria]])); and surgical/anatomical (short bowel with intestinal failure, TPN and the adaptation phase ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]); blind loops and bypass anatomy with the bariatric deficiency ledgers ([[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]] and [[endocrine/diabetes]]'s dumping physiology); fistulas and adhesions ([[surgery/gastrointestinal-surgery]])). The deficiency-map idiom: iron-only says duodenum-or-blood-loss; B12-only says ileum-or-stomach; pan-deficiency says jejunum-wide mucosal disease or pancreatic juice missing ([[#def-digest]]'s addresses).**
:::

::: example The anaemia with a sore tongue {#ex-b12}
A 68-year-old long-standing vegetarian on metformin for 15 years: Hb 88, MCV 112, hypersegmented neutrophils, low B12, borderline folate, glossitis, numb toes; anti-parietal-cell antibodies positive.
::: solution
**Macrocytic anaemia with neuropathy is B12 deficiency until proven otherwise ([[blood/anaemia]] and the subacute-combined-degeneration pattern of dorsal-column and corticospinal involvement — [[nervous-system/motor-systems]]). The causes stack ([[#def-digest]]'s B12 address): pernicious autoimmune atrophic gastritis (the parietal and intrinsic-factor loss — [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]'s cancer-surveillance corollary, the polyglandular autoimmune associations to screen — [[endocrine/thyroid]], [[endocrine/adrenal]], [[immunology/autoimmunity]]; the antibody pair plus gastrin adjuncts — [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]); metformin's dose-and-duration-related interference with ileal B12 absorption ([[endocrine/diabetes-complications]] and the annual-monitoring argument); and diet. Two classic hazards: **folate monotherapy correcting the blood count while the neuropathy advances, and the methylmalate-and-homocysteine biochemistry ([[cell-biochemistry/lipid-amino-acid-metabolism]]) with its cardiovascular-risk hypothesis and its epidemiological debunking ([[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]). Repletion: **the intramuscular hydroxocobalamin loading-and-maintenance schedule (the high-dose-oral option for non-neurological non-pernicious cases — [[blood/anaemia]]), anticipating the repletion hypokalaemia and iron-deficiency unmasking ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]), and treating the cause terminally (lifelong injections for pernicious anaemia versus metformin risk-benefit versus supplements; the folate-fortification context of neural-tube prevention — [[obstetrics-gynaecology/pregnancy-antenatal]] and [[public-health/prevention-promotion]]); and the refractory-case loop (check adherence, folate, myelodysplasia and copper — [[blood/haematopoiesis]]).**
:::
:::

::: example The ileal resection, three years on {#ex-stones-ileum}
A 34-year-old with Crohn's disease and a 60 cm terminal-ileal resection has recurring loose stools, a 4 mm ureteric stone (calcium oxalate on analysis), and B12 of 90 pmol/L.
::: solution
**One resection, three physiology lessons ([[#def-secretion]] and [[#def-digest]]): (i) the bile-acid pool is interrupted — with a short resection, unabsorbed bile acid drives colonic secretion (the choleretic diarrhoea, cholestyramine-responsive — [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]); with a long one, the pool depletes and fat itself is maldigested (steatorrhoea — [[#def-diarrhoea]]); (ii) depleted luminal calcium (busy saponifying unabsorbed fat) leaves oxalate free for colonic absorption — enteric hyperoxaluria and calcium-oxalate stones, the exact mechanism of [[renal/urinary-tract]]'s stone case ([[renal/urinary-tract]]'s own urological sequel); and (iii) the terminal ileum is gone — B12 malabsorption with its anaemia-neuropathy trajectory ([[#ex-b12]]) and bile-acid loss compounding diarrhoea. Management: **the B12 replacement (parenteral given the site), the trial of bile-acid sequestrant versus its opposite (stop it if steatorrhoea-dominant — the 100 cm rule), stone-prevention arithmetic (fluids, calcium-with-meals as oxalate binder, citrate — [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]), the fat-soluble-vitamin and folate-and-iron sweep ([[endocrine/bone-calcium]] and [[blood/anaemia]]), the IBD-relapse surveillance ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]) and the TPN-referencing severity line ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]).**
:::
:::

::: example The toddler who will not grow {#ex-cf}
An 11-month-old (failure to thrive, weight below the 0.4th centile) has bulky, greasy, foul-smelling stools, abdominal distension, vitamin D 28 nmol/L with raised alkaline phosphatase, and two chest infections this winter; sweat chloride 96 mmol/L.
::: solution
**Cystic fibrosis exocrine-pancreatic-insufficiency presenting as malabsorption ([[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]-and-[[paediatrics/congenital-genetic]]-and-[[respiratory/respiratory-infections]]-adjacent): the ductal CFTR failure thickens secretions, obstructs pancreatic ducts, and destroys the acini — lipase and protease delivery fail first (the [[#def-digest]] rule: fat is the most secretion-dependent macro-nutrient), so steatorrhoea, calorie-loss, fat-soluble-vitamin deficiency (the vitamin-D-and-bone picture here — [[endocrine/bone-calcium]]; the vitamin-K-bleed risk — [[blood/haemostasis-bleeding]]; vitamin A — [[nervous-system/sensory-systems]]) and essential-fatty-acid deficit follow, while the lung and sweat and hepatobiliary and intestinal complications run in parallel (meconium-ileus-equivalent distal intestinal obstruction syndrome, biliary disease, and the neonatal-screening story — [[paediatrics/growth-development]] and [[public-health/screening]]).** Management: **pancreatin replacement dosed by weight-and-fat-symptoms-with-meals (the [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]] enzyme-adequacy markers — faecal elastase stays low so it cannot monitor therapy here — [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]); the high-calorie-high-protein diet with vitamin-and-mineral supplements and the enzyme-coordination (the [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]] team); growth-chart-and-dietary-recall surveillance ([[paediatrics/growth-development]]); the chest physiotherapy-and-microbiology programme and modulator-therapy era ([[genetics/genomic-medicine]]'s mutation-specific CFTR modulators changing the nutritional trajectory itself); the genetic counselling for the family and the newborn-screening cascade for siblings ([[genetics/genetic-testing]]); and the transition-planning habit from the start ([[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]]'s paediatric-to-adult clinic handover literature and [[clinical-skills/records-handover]]).**
:::

::: quiz
Oral rehydration solution works during cholera because:
- [ ] Glucose kills vibrios in the lumen
- [x] SGLT1 glucose-sodium cotransport remains functional while CFTR-driven secretion is cAMP-locked
- [ ] The solution lowers stool sodium by binding toxin
- [ ] Zinc reverses the enterocyte channel defect
::: solution
**The cholera-toxin-keeps-cAMP-CFTR-secretion-on but leaves the SGLT1 electrogenic Na⁺-glucose symport ([[#def-digest]], [[#ex-diarrhoea-types]]) intact, driving isosmolar fluid absorption — the physiological basis of the low-osmolarity WHO formula (Na 75, glucose 75 mmol/L — [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]) and one of medicine's simplest life-savers ([[public-health/global-health]] and [[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]]). Zinc shortens episodes; it does not touch the channel ([[microbiology/bacterial-pathogenesis]] is the toxin's story, not the cure's).**

::: history
**The cephalic-phase sham-feeding dogs of Pavlov's school; Bayliss and Starling's secretin (1902) — the discovery that coined "hormone" ([[physiology/hormones]]); Ivy's gastrin extracts (1937) and the later purifications of CCK, GIP and motilin;** **Castle's intrinsic-factor (1929) with the Minot-Murphy-Whipple liver-cure era that first made pernicious anaemia a treatable disease (Nobel 1934 — [[blood/anaemia]]);** **the proton-pump biochemistry (the Sachs and Ganser schools, 1970s) and omeprazole's 1979 arrival ([[pharmacology/drug-receptor]]);** **the cholera-toxin-cAMP cascade (1970s) meeting the CFTR cloning (1989) — toxin and disease as mirror-image channel physiology ([[microbiology/bacterial-pathogenesis]] and [[genetics/mendelian-inheritance]]);** **the oral-rehydration revolution (the 1960s-70s NDDRI-Cholera-Research-Laboratory physiology-to-public-health translation — [[public-health/global-health]]);** **the incretin journey from the 1906 "extract" episode and 1932 observation to GLP-1 biology and its drugs ([[endocrine/diabetes]]);** and **the diagnostic modernisation of coeliac serology and faecal calprotectin ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]] and [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]])** complete the lineage from digestive physiology to the modern GI clinic.
:::

Long-term PPI therapy: explain reduced absorption of iron, vitamin B12, itraconazole and magnesium by physiology, and name the compensations.

::: summary
- Motility is pacemaker-driven and reflex-organised (peristalsis, segmentation, the fasting MMC, sphincter gates); its failure produces obstruction-like, reflux and dysmotility syndromes by address.
- Acid output is the convergence point of vagus, histamine and gastrin onto one pump — hence PPI breadth and NSAID ulcer specificity via prostaglandin-trimmed defence.
- Absorption is address-specific and transporter-defined (SGLT1, PepT1, micelle-then-chylomicron, DMT1, IF-ileum); the deficiency map localises mucosal lesions like a dermatome chart.
- Diarrhoea sorts by the fasting test, stool osmotic gap, calprotectin and story into osmotic, secretory, inflammatory and motility types — each with its own treatment.
- Malabsorption separates luminal, mucosal and surgical causes; pancreatic juice, bile, a working mucosa and gut length are the four prerequisites for absorption.
:::

## Exercises

::: exercise Diagram the pump {level=1}
Trace HCl secretion from carbonic anhydrase to canaliculus and mark the three receptor targets and the final common pathway.
::: solution
**CO₂ hydration gives intracellular H⁺ (with Cl⁻ following electrically via CIC-2 and K⁺ recycled) extruded by the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase ([[cell-biochemistry/membranes-transport]], [[renal/tubular-function]]); targets M3 (ACh), H2 (histamine), CCK2 (gastrin) — and the pump itself, downstream of all three ([[pharmacology/drug-receptor]], [[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]]); the defence side (prostaglandins, mucus, bicarbonate, perfusion) is misoprostol's rationale ([[pharmacology/adverse-reactions]]).**
:::

::: exercise Explain the stones {level=1}
Why does ileal resection cause diarrhoea (short resection) and gallstones and kidney stones (long resection)?
::: solution
**Bile-acid economics ([[#def-secretion]]): small losses leave unabsorbed bile acid irritating the colon (secretory-type choleretic diarrhoea, cholestyramine-responsive — [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]); large losses deplete the pool → fat maldigestion and steatorrhoea ([[#def-diarrhoea]]), calcium bound to fat instead of oxalate → enteric hyperoxaluria and calcium-oxalate stones ([[renal/urinary-tract]]), and lithogenic bile from the shrunken acid pool → cholesterol gallstones ([[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]); plus B12 loss at its address ([[#ex-b12]]). See [[#ex-stones-ileum]] — one resection, four lesions.**
:::
:::

::: exercise Use the gap {level=2}
Suspected laxative-abuse diarrhoea: stool Na 60, K 50, osmolality 295. Compute the gap, and explain why the numbers alone won't settle it.
::: solution
**Gap = 295 − 2×(60+50) = 75 — a middling value: stool osmolality is near-plasma by construction, and gaps mislead when the specimen sits (bacterial consumption) or lipids and unmeasured anions (laxative salts, magnesium) shift things ([[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]). The settle-it tests are the stool screen for stimulants and magnesium, the melanosis-coli clue at endoscopy ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]), the hypokalaemia-with-alkalosis-versus-acidosis pattern ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]] and [[renal/acid-base-disorders]] — VIPoma gives acidic, watery, chloride-rich loss), and the psychiatric framing done non-confrontationally ([[psychiatry/eating-personality-disorders]], [[clinical-skills/communication]]).**
:::
:::

::: exercise Choose the ORS {level=2}
Why does glucose appear in an oral rehydration solution, and why must it not be added to IV fluids at the same osmolality?
::: solution
**Glucose is the co-substrate that makes SGLT1 (2 Na⁺ per glucose) electroneutral-and-osmotically-productive water absorption continue despite toxin-driven secretion ([[#def-digest]] and the cholera quiz); the low-osmolarity formula balances sodium-glucose-coupling maximum against luminal tonicity ([[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]] and WHO-programme evidence — [[public-health/global-health]]). Intravenously the same glucose-water combination is the wrong route (the IV route bypasses the transporter, and hypotonic sugar-water infusions cause hyperglycaemia-osmotic-diuresis and hyponatraemia — [[renal/water-sodium]]); ORS is a gut technology ([[paediatrics/acute-childhood-illness]]'s ORS-and-zinc, and [[emergency-critical-care/shock-fluids]] for the IV-need cases).**

::: exercise Localise the deficiency {level=2}

::: exercise The PPI question {level=2}
A patient on omeprazole for six years asks to come off. Give the review, the taper and the rebound physiology.
::: solution
**Review the indication (erosive oesophagitis, Barrett, ulcer-bleed prevention → continue ([[gastrointestinal/oesophagus-stomach]], [[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]]); unindicated dyspepsia → trial off). Risks named honestly: the fracture-and-magnesium and B12-iron signals ([[endocrine/bone-calcium]], [[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]], [[#ex-b12]]), the C-difficult-and-nosocomial-pneumonia associations ([[infectious-diseases/healthcare-infections]]), the CKD association with its confounding-by-indication caveat ([[renal/chronic-kidney-disease]], [[epidemiology-ebm/observational-studies]]). Rebound mechanism: **chronic acid-suppression raises gastrin and pump expression, so abrupt stopping hyper-secretes for ~1-3 weeks ([[#def-secretion]], [[#def-secretion]]-caption's nocturnal point) — hence taper-or-alternate-day with on-demand alginate-antacid cover and a 4-week review ([[clinical-skills/records-handover]], [[geriatrics-palliative/polypharmacy]]'s deprescribing framework, and the functional-symptom coping plan — [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]'s gut-brain modulation).**

::: exercise Design the diarrhoea clinic {level=3}
Design a chronic-diarrhoea service: triage, panel architecture, empirical-trial ladder and follow-up dataset.
::: solution
**Triage: the red-flag gate to urgent endoscopy-and-cross-sectional imaging ([[gastrointestinal/gi-bleeding-cancer]], [[clinical-skills/medical-imaging]]) with faecal calprotectin as the inflammatory-versus-functional divider ([[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]); immunosuppression-and-travel history routing ([[infectious-diseases/immunocompromised-host]], [[public-health/global-health]]). Panels: the stool battery (culture, C difficile, parasites, electrolytes-and-gap, calprotectin — [[microbiology/laboratory-diagnosis]], [[clinical-skills/laboratory-tests]]), the serum battery (coeliac-duo-with-total-IgA, FBC-ferritin-B12-folate, TFTs, HIV, elastase-pairing — [[endocrine/thyroid]], [[infectious-diseases/hiv]], [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]), and breath hydrogen-methane with its methane-producer caveats ([[microbiology/microbiota]]). Trial ladder ordered by yield: **the diet trials (lactose, fructose, FODMAP with dietitian — [[gastrointestinal/clinical-nutrition]]), the sequestrant trial (bile-acid diarrhoea — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]]), the enzyme trial (pancreatic insufficiency — dose-by-weight), the anti-inflammatory pathway (IBD step-up — [[gastrointestinal/bowel-disease]]) and the secretory hunt (NET imaging and calcitonin and catecholamines — [[gastrointestinal/pancreas-biliary]], [[endocrine/thyroid]], [[endocrine/adrenal]]) with the factitious screen ([[psychiatry/eating-personality-disorders]]). Follow-up dataset: stool diary, weight, hydration and electrolyte safety monitoring ([[renal/potassium-calcium-phosphate]]), quality-of-life scores, escalation criteria, and the referral-precision KPI ([[clinical-skills/clinical-reasoning]], [[public-health/health-systems-economics]]) — the clinic's first-visit rule restated: classify by mechanism before adding another antidiarrhoeal ([[gastrointestinal/digestion-absorption]]).**
